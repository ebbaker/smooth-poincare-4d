**Finding.** One equatorial sheet and zero axial sheets at every regular point leave two exceptional mechanisms in the exact Taubes model. An isolated cone gives an explicit equivariant counterexample to an inference from the regular sheet counts. On the portion of the tube after removing isolated cone germs, an exceptional point has a positive axial tangent multiplicity compensated by an increased equatorial tangent multiplicity. The primary proof bounds the number of such points. A pointwise energy-density bound given below would exclude both mechanisms on the lifted marked current.

## What the regular counts imply

- **All tangent cones.** Proposition 4.1 assigns nonnegative total equatorial multiplicity \(p\), axial multiplicities \(q_+,q_-\), and cone counts \(n_+,n_-\) from Example 1.5. The counts include repeated tangent sheets. With \(q=q_++q_-\), the signed linking count is \(p-q\) at every point, including exceptional points. The Example 1.5 cones contribute zero to this pairing. [Proposition 4.1 and its proof, pp. 251–255](https://msp.org/gt/1998/2-1/gt-v2-n1-p10-p.pdf)
- **Isolated cone germs.** Proposition 5.1 bounds the set where \(n_++n_->0\) by a finite set. At the start of §6, Taubes writes the restriction to a smaller tube as a disjoint union \(C'\cup C''\). On \(C'\), \(n_+=n_-=0\) everywhere. On \(C''\), \(p=q=0\) everywhere. Thus the sole global irreducible component in LINE does not remove \(C''\) by component counting: the decomposition is within the tube, and local pieces can belong to the same normalization through its exterior. [Proposition 5.1 and §6, pp. 256–259](https://msp.org/gt/1998/2-1/gt-v2-n1-p10-p.pdf)
- **Axial tangent multiplicity at an isolated point.** On \(C'\), Proposition 6.1 gives upper semicontinuity of \(p,q\), with \(p-q=1\) fixed. Section 7 defines regular points of \(C'\) as the local minima of \(p\). If \(p=1,q=0\) on that open dense locus, any remaining nonregular point must have \(q\ge1\) and \(p=1+q\). Conversely, if \(q=0\) at *every* point, \(p=1\) is constant and Proposition 7.1 gives the one-sheet analytic collar everywhere for \(C'\). Section 9 starts with \(q_\pm=0\) on the regular locus after the tube reductions. Proposition 9.2 gives a positive lower bound of order \(s^3\) on the local \(d\phi\wedge dh\) integral for a singular point with \(q_+>0\), with the reflected argument for \(q_->0\). Proposition 5.2 gives a tube upper bound \(O(s^3)\) for that integral, which proves finiteness in Proposition 9.1. These estimates supply no vanishing conclusion for \(p=1\) on the regular locus. No explicit realization of a compensated jump with these regular counts was obtained in this reading. [Proposition 6.1, pp. 259–260; §7, pp. 261–262; §9, pp. 285–287](https://msp.org/gt/1998/2-1/gt-v2-n1-p10-p.pdf)

## An equivariant local example and a sufficient bound

**Project calculation.** On the model cover with \(t\) of period \(2L\), take the deck map \(D(t,x,y,z)=(t+L,x,-y,-z)\). It preserves the model form and metric, fixes \(f\), and negates \(h\) and \(\phi\). The equatorial sheet \(S=\{y=z=0,\ x>0\}\) is invariant. Adjoin the extreme Example 1.5 cone \(\Sigma_+\) at \(t=t_0\) and its image \(\Sigma_-\) at \(t=t_0+L\). On the first cone \(z=s/\sqrt3\), \(\rho=\sqrt{2/3}\,s\), \(f=0\), and \(h=c_*s^3\), where \(s=\sqrt{\rho^2+z^2}\) and \(c_*=2/(3\sqrt3)\). Example 1.6 identifies \(t=t_0,f=0\) as a holomorphic slice away from the tip. The form integral of one cone within \(s<R\) is \(2\pi c_*R^3\) using the positive orientation of \(d\phi\wedge dh\). The sheet's energy there is \(LR^2\). The three pieces are disjoint in the punctured product, each has weight one, and the union is closed relative to the open punctured tube and descends. It has \(p=1,q=0\) on the regular locus and signed degree \(+1\) everywhere. Each cone is supported at one meridional time, so a nearby meridian avoids it. Its tip persists as an isolated exception downstairs. This model tests a finite-energy subvariety in the tube. Its normalization in the tube has a separate cone component, and no realization as the global marked Seiberg–Witten limit is established. [Examples 1.3, 1.5 and 1.6, pp. 223–227](https://msp.org/gt/1998/2-1/gt-v2-n1-p10-p.pdf)

**Project deduction.** For the exact model set \(\Theta(t)=\lim_{r\downarrow0}r^{-3}\int_{C\cap B_r(t)}\omega\), where \(B_r(t)\) is the flat four-dimensional ball at the lifted zero. An equatorial ray has density \(\int_0^1 2\sqrt{1-s^2}\,s\,ds=2/3\). An axial ray has twice that density with its positive orientation. Lemma 1.10 gives a uniform \(\delta_*>0\) such that the density \(\delta_c\) of any Example 1.5 cone is at least \(\delta_*\). Thus the tangent-cone expression at link \(+1\) is
\[
\Theta(t)=\frac23p+\frac43q+\sum_{i=1}^{n_++n_-}\delta_{c_i}
         =\frac23+2q+\sum_{i=1}^{n_++n_-}\delta_{c_i}.
\]
For the equality, the rescaled integral converges along every geometrically convergent subsequence off the lifted zero set by Proposition 3.2. Proposition 2.1 bounds any rescaled ball of radius \(2\varepsilon\) centered on that set by a fixed multiple of \(\varepsilon^3\). Covering the part of the rescaled unit ball within distance \(\varepsilon\) of the zero set by \(O(\varepsilon^{-1})\) such balls makes its normalized energy \(O(\varepsilon^2)\). The cone has zero two-dimensional measure along the unit sphere, so ball integrals converge; Proposition 4.1 makes the sum independent of subsequence. In particular the sufficient bound is
\[
\Theta(t)<\frac23+\min(2,\delta_*)\qquad\text{at every lifted }t.
\]
It forces \(q=n_+=n_-=0\) everywhere and hence gives one equatorial half-disk at each point by §§7–8. The lifted collar is deck invariant because it is the closure of the inverse image of the given current. It descends to a smooth embedded collar with one boundary over the original circle. The LINE period computes the total area \(\left|\int_Z\alpha\right|\); no estimate in the packet compares that value to the required local densities or their excess. [Lemma 1.10, p. 233; Propositions 2.1, 3.2 and 4.1](https://msp.org/gt/1998/2-1/gt-v2-n1-p10-p.pdf)

**Return to B07.** On the prepared tube the two missing controls are pointwise absence of Example 1.5 cones and pointwise absence of axial tangent multiplicity, including at exceptional points. The density bound is a single sufficient estimate for both. The conditional collar conclusion leaves the global spanning surface and cap-relative deformation as separate problems.
