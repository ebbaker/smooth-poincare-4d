## Descent and local cone

At a regular point with $p=1$ and $q_\pm=0$ the graph in $(t,\rho)$ is real analytic up to $\rho=0$. If these graphs occur everywhere, uniqueness glues them, and compactness gives a common collar width. The deck action in the graph parameters is translation by $\ell$ in $t$ with $\rho$ fixed. On the image it conjugates $e^{i\phi}$ and negates $z$. The quotient is an embedded annulus with one boundary mapping diffeomorphically and positively to $Z$. [Taubes, Theorem 1.8, Proposition 7.1 and §8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9901142)

Adjoin to the fixed positive $x$ equatorial ray the cone $t=t_0$, $\rho^2=2z^2$, $z>0$ and its deck image. The cone is holomorphic because the normal plane to its $t,f$-level tangent is spanned by $\partial_t,\nabla f$ and is $J$-invariant. On the positive cone $h=2N^3/(3\sqrt3)$, giving energy $4\pi b^3/(3\sqrt3)$ and flat area $\pi\sqrt{2/3}\,b^2$ inside $N<b$. Its shrinking section projects constantly along $Z$ and has length $O(b)$, so the tip adds zero signed degree and zero boundary current. The same holds on the mate. Each cone maps diffeomorphically to one weight-one annulus in the punctured quotient. The two tips become one exceptional basepoint and the union has degree $+1$. The cone normalization is connected, and the normalization of its union with the ray has two components in the tube. These pieces continue to the outer radius. Their appearance as parts of one global marked current is unresolved. [Taubes, Examples 1.3, 1.5–1.6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9901142)

## Density and finite samples

For the smooth nonincreasing radial cutoff $\chi$ of the structure paper, equal to one through radius $1$ and zero from radius $2$, let $b_\chi=3\int\chi(a)a^2\,da$ and $k=\|\chi'\|_\infty$. For one fixed marked current $T$ put
\[
D_s(v)=\frac{1}{b_\chi s^3}\int_T \chi(r_v/s)\bar\omega
\]
with $r_v$ the flat four-dimensional distance about the lifted center $(v,0)$. The support is in an injectively projected exact ball, for instance with $2s<\min(R_{\rm exact},\ell/2)$. Either lift gives the same integral. A factor of two pertains to the full lifted tube, whereas this integral uses one projected ball and the measure downstairs has mass $P$.

Rescaling by $3s$ puts the weighted integrand inside the classified unit ball. The model form scales cubically, and Proposition 2.1 bounds the rescaled area there. The rescaled form has comass at most $2\epsilon$ at normal distance $\le\epsilon$, so excision of the axis costs $O(\epsilon)$ uniformly in normalized energy. Propositions 3.2 and 4.1 thus give convergence of the smooth weighted integrals to those of the tangent terms. Different possible rotational limits have the same energy. Homogeneity gives the sharp unit-ball energy after division by $b_\chi$. For the equatorial half-plane this is $\int_0^1 2r\sqrt{1-r^2}\,dr=2/3$, and for either axial half-plane it is $4/3$. With $q=q_++q_-$ the identity is
\[
D_0(v)=\frac23p+\frac43q+\sum_i\delta_i
      =\frac23+2q+\sum_i\delta_i .
\]

For a remaining cone the normalized link $w=(\pm3u'/2,\sqrt{c/u}\cos\tau,\sqrt{c/u}\sin\tau,\pm u)$ has $|w|=1$, $w\perp w'$, and its angular interval completes at least one full turn. The area element of $(r,\tau)\mapsto rw(\tau)$ is $r|w'|\,dr\,d\tau$, giving $\delta=\frac13\int\sqrt{c/u+4u^2}\,|w'|\,d\tau$. If $c\ge1/100$, both factors are at least $\sqrt{c/u}$ and $u\le1$, so $\delta\ge2\pi/300>1/50$. If $c<1/100$, $u_{\max}\ge1/\sqrt3$ and $|u'|\le2/3$. On the interval of length $1/2$ centered at a maximum, $u\ge1/\sqrt3-1/6>2/5$. There $3|u''|/2=2u/3-c/(3u^2)>1/5$ by the cone equation. The first factor is $>4/5$, yielding $\delta>2/75>1/50$. The estimates hold for either nappe, any allowed winding and multiplicities by additivity. [Taubes, Example 1.5, Lemma 1.10, Propositions 2.1, 3.2 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9901142)

If $D_0(v)<2/3+1/50$ everywhere, all axial and isolated cone terms vanish. In the §6 splitting the piece supported on isolated cone approaches then has no approach to $Z$. For the piece with no cone terms, $p\equiv1$ gives the §7 local-minimum criterion everywhere. The resulting analytic graphs descend. Conversely, the full embedded collar of the weight-one current gives one positive equatorial tangent at every point, hence $D_0=2/3$. [Taubes, §§6–8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9901142)

The deck-invariant primitive is $\beta=-f\,dt-z(x\,dy-y\,dx)$, with $|\beta|^2=\rho^4/4+z^4\le N^4$. Write $\chi_s=\chi(r_v/s)$. Stokes with an axis cutoff $\eta_\epsilon(N)$ gives $\int_T \chi_s\bar\omega=-\int_T d\chi_s\wedge\beta$. The derivative term with $d\eta_\epsilon$ is bounded by a constant times the energy in $\epsilon<N<2\epsilon$, using $|\beta|\le N^2$ and $\bar\omega|_T=\lambda\,dA$ with $\lambda\ge N$. Using $\mu=-\eta_\epsilon d\chi_s\wedge\beta$ as a smooth compactly supported test form off $Z$ changes the sample numerator by at most $2k\epsilon P/s$. Normalizing adds an error allowance of $2k\epsilon P/(b_\chi s^4)$ beside any independently bounded pairing error.

For all admitted scales, with $R=(t-t_v)\partial_t+x\partial_x+y\partial_y+z\partial_z$ and $\theta=\iota_R\bar\omega/3$, direct contraction gives $\theta-\beta=dF$ for $F=(t-t_v)f/3$. In replacing $\beta$ with $\theta$, the Stokes error is a pairing with $F d\eta_\epsilon\wedge d\chi_s$ up to sign. It is controlled by the same annular energy since $|F|\le 2sN^2/3$ on the support and $\|d\chi_s\|\le k/s$. The bound $|\theta|\le r_v\lambda/3$ then gives $\sigma(s)=-\int_T d\chi_s\wedge\theta\le s\sigma'(s)/3$ for $\sigma(s)=\int_T\chi_s\bar\omega$, by differentiation under the finite-energy integral. Hence $D_s$ is nondecreasing throughout the admissible range. The smooth radial cutoff factor $b_\chi$ is essential to retaining the sharp energy normalization.
