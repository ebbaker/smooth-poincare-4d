## Fixed-pole incidence and the conditional period

**Project theorem, replayed.** For fixed $c,p$, let $A(q)$ be normalized evaluation, and $D$ the projected image of the compact incidence $\{(q,[u]):A(q)u=0\}$. The common leading-frame isometry at infinity bounds its spatial support. At each point of degeneracy the incidence retains the entire projectivized kernel, including the projective line at rank one. To show that $D$ omits a direction, the rank-one and rank-zero fibres must be included along with the rank-two kernel map. A2 on an exotic sphere would force $D$ to be the whole projective direction plane for every $c,p$. If $D$ has boundary, a zero of a boundary direction has spatial derivative of rank at most two by persistence of a rank-three zero. Rank one is impossible since closedness would give $\alpha\wedge\beta=0$ for a nonzero self-dual $\beta$ in a rank-one derivative $\alpha\otimes\beta$. The nonzero square of $\beta$ excludes that identity. Thus the possible boundary ranks are zero and two. At rank-two evaluation with transverse determinant, only rank two remains at those boundary zeros, and the differential of the kernel-line map has rank one there. These conclusions are fixed-marking statements. Generic transversality in the pole family and an obstruction to surjectivity remain separate inputs.
