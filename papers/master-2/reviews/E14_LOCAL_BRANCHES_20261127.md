## Cone and unit-degree ray in the quotient

**Project computation.** Use time $t\in\mathbb R/(2\ell\mathbb Z)$ and normal coordinates $(x,y,z)$ on the cover. Write $\rho=(x^2+y^2)^{1/2}$, $r=(\rho^2+z^2)^{1/2}$, and $\phi=\arg(x+iy)$. Set
\[
 f=\tfrac12\rho^2-z^2,\qquad h=z\rho^2,\qquad
 \omega=dt\wedge df+d\phi\wedge dh.
\]
The orientation is $dt\wedge dx\wedge dy\wedge dz$. The deck transformation $D(t,x,y,z)=(t+\ell,x,-y,-z)$ preserves $\omega$ and the flat metric, hence the adapted $J$. It sends $(f,h,\phi)$ to $(f,-h,-\phi)$. The negative eigenline reverses. The quotient has one twisted zero circle, oriented by increasing time. Use the outward normal convention on each meridian sphere.

Take the constant equatorial sheet $E=\{x>0,\ y=z=0\}$, which is $D$-invariant. Its tangent plane is spanned by $\partial_t$ and $\nabla f$ and is therefore $J$-invariant. Take the circular cone $K_+=\{t=t_0,\ \rho=\sqrt2 z,\ z>0\}$ and its deck image $K_-=D K_+$. The cone is the constant-$u$ case of Example 1.5. The intersection of $t=\mathrm{const}$ and $f=0$ is $J$-invariant since its orthogonal complement, spanned by $\partial_t$ and $\nabla f$, is $J$-invariant in the exact model. The two cones are disjoint on the cover. Their closures meet the lifted zero circle at $t_0$ and $t_0+\ell$. Downstairs their common image $K$ is one embedded annulus off the zero circle, with one puncture approaching one point of $Z$. The closure has a nonplanar tangent cone there. The restriction of the quotient map to $K_+$ is a diffeomorphism onto $K$, so $K$ is assigned weight one. The full lifted sum $E+K_++K_-$ pushes forward to twice the downstairs current.

On $K_+$, set $z=s>0$, so $r=\sqrt3s$ and $h=2s^3$. Then $\omega|_{K_+}=6s^2\,d\phi\wedge ds$, which fixes its positive orientation. For normal tube radius $R$, the downstairs cone has energy and flat area
\[
 \int_{K\cap\{r<R\}}\omega=\frac{4\pi R^3}{3\sqrt3},\qquad
 \operatorname{Area}_{\rm flat}(K\cap\{r<R\})=\pi\sqrt{\frac23}R^2.
\]
Every small normal-radius slice of $K$ is a circle projected to the single time $t_0$ modulo $\ell$. Its projected boundary degree is zero. The boundary of the truncated cone also tends to zero as a current because the slice has flat length $O(r)$. Generic meridians miss $K$. The ray descends with orientation $dt\wedge dr$, with $\omega=r\,dt\wedge dr$ and with quotient energy $\ell R^2/2$. The small-radius boundary of its exterior truncation is oriented by $+dt$, giving both boundary degree and signed meridional intersection $+1$. Thus $E/D+K$ has boundary $[Z]$ as a current in the open tube. Its regular sheet counts are $p=1,\ q_+=q_-=0$. At the exceptional point its lifted tangent current adds one cone to the equatorial ray, with the sign of the cone exchanged at the deck mate.

The deck pairing also works for the nonconstant rational-period cones of Example 1.5 and Lemma 1.9. Writing their angular parameter as $\tau$, the deck action is absorbed by $\tau\mapsto-\tau$, replacement of $u(\tau)$ by $u(-\tau)$, change to the negative cone, and translation of $t_0$ by $\ell$. After restriction to a sufficiently small normal tube each truncated loop projects into a contractible time arc, so its degree is zero. Finite energy follows from cubic scaling and compactness of the rational-period link. [Taubes, Example 1.5 and Lemmas 1.9–1.10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9901142)

## Connected winding collars and the remaining condition

**Project computation.** Taubes's Example 1.7 on the cover has a connected annular normalization parametrized by $\theta\bmod 2\pi$ and small $s>0$. For coprime integers $m,q$ with $q>0$, it has $t=a\theta$ with $a=2\ell q/(2\pi)$, $\rho=s$, $\phi=m\theta+\alpha$, and $z=\kappa s^2/4+O(s^4)$ with $\kappa=m/a$. Indeed, tangency to $J(a\partial_t+m\partial_\phi)$ is equivalent to $a h'=m s^2 f'$, where prime denotes the derivative in $s$. This gives $(a+2mz)s z'+2az=ms^2$ and the stated quadratic coefficient. At fixed $s$ coprimality makes the angular map embedded. Its energy density in $d\theta\wedge ds$ is $as+O(s^3)>0$. It has $q$ equatorial germs over a regular lifted time and lifted degree $q$. If $m\ne0$, its deck image reverses $\kappa$ and the sign of $z$ near the zero circle. The original annulus and its mate are disjoint there. Together they descend to one connected annulus of degree $2q$. If $m=0$, coprimality forces $q=1$ and $z=0$. The single annulus is invariant precisely for $\alpha=0$ or $\pi$. Either invariant example descends with degree $1$ and is regular along the whole zero circle. In this family every connected descended annulus of degree one has a single regular boundary sheet. [Taubes, Example 1.7, (1.12)–(1.13)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9901142)

If one assumes the normalization restricted to the full punctured tube is connected, a regular axial half-sheet forces that normalization to be the axial annulus. Unique continuation gives the full corresponding leaf on the cover, then the deck action supplies its mate. The embedded axial image downstairs is a whole local irreducible component. Its small-radius slice wraps twice with negative sign, giving degree $-2$. Hence a connected normalization on the whole tube with degree $+1$ excludes regular axial half-sheets. Global connectedness in the marked core permits disconnection upon restriction to the tube. The cone and ray above have disjoint images off $Z$, giving separate components of the tube normalization. A genuinely connected tube normalization with degree $+1$ and an exceptional point remains unverified.

For the global marked current the unresolved issue is whether the exterior data forbid such locally disconnected branches or, more directly, whether they force the cone counts $n_++n_-$ to vanish. The tube primitive $b=-f\,dt-h\,d\phi$ is smooth, deck invariant and satisfies $db=\omega$. The degree-zero cone has positive energy supplied by its outer-radius boundary integral of $b$. This energy tends to zero cubically as the tube shrinks, so finiteness alone cannot exclude the cone. The core period identity applies separately to components proper without boundary in the whole core. Applied to either truncated local component, Stokes retains an outer-radius boundary integral. Exterior pseudoholomorphic joining and cap-relative exclusion remain open, as does realization of the configuration by a Seiberg–Witten limit.

## Gerig's birth disk

**Import and project computation.** Gerig's Question 1 asks about uniqueness. Example 1.7 produces one disk in a specified birth model of a single twisted circle after changing the form outside a containing ball. On the displayed disk $w=0$, $|z|\le\sqrt\varepsilon$, the model form restricts to $(|z|^2-\varepsilon)\,dx\wedge dy$ for $z=x+iy$. The tangent complex line is invariant under the flat adapted structure, with intrinsic complex orientation opposite to the standard complex coordinate $z$ on the interior. It has clockwise boundary orientation in that coordinate and energy $\pi\varepsilon^2/2$ before any positive rescaling. This provides a connected regular boundary of absolute degree one for that birth form and metric. The current question would require a corresponding result in the prepared tube with the marked cap. [Gerig, arXiv:1905.10938v3, p. 3, Question 1 and Example 1.7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.10938)
