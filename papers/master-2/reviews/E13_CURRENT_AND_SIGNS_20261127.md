## Marked current and signs

**Import and replay.** On the closed oriented smooth Riemannian \(Y=M\#\mathbb{CP}^2\), the capped \(ar\omega\) is closed, self-dual and transverse with one twisted zero circle \(Z\). It is Kähler near the actual line \(L_\infty\) of square \(+1\). Use \(h=\operatorname{PD}[L_\infty]\) and the spin\(^{c}\) determinant \(\mathcal L\) with \(c_1(\mathcal L)=3h\). LINE's replayed chamber calculation supplies a nonzero invariant on the large negative imaginary ray of \(ar\omega\), hence the unbounded sequence of solutions to (2.9) for the chosen metric. Taubes's Theorem 2.2 gives a finite-energy subvariety in \(Y\setminus Z\) with proper normalization off \(Z\) and positive integral weights \(m_a\). The weighted relative class \(A\) satisfies \(2A=\operatorname{PD}(c_1(\mathcal L\otimes K))\) and \(A\cdot L_\infty=0\) since \(c_1(K)[L_\infty]=-3\). Positivity against holomorphic lines in the marked integrable cap gives disjointness from the actual line, including exclusion of a component equal to that line by displacing it. Properness and compactness of \(Y\) give compact support closure in \(V=Y\setminus L_\infty\). This test is at the marked line. The current can enter the affine part of the cap. [Taubes, existence paper, Definition 1.1, (2.4), (2.9), Theorem 2.2, (6.1) and §7(f), pp. 168–169, 172, 175–177, 199, 209](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9907199) · [LINE chamber and cap proof](../../investigations-2/03_lines_and_broken_fibrations/notes/CAP_DEGREE_AND_CHAMBERS.md)

**Signed computation.** Taubes orients the normal bundle so that \(\nabla\bar\omega:N_Z\to\Lambda^+\) reverses its orientation, and \(TY|_Z=TZ\oplus N_Z\) in this order. The oriented coordinates in his proof of Lemma 2.1 have \(dt>0\) along \(Z\), normal volume \(dx\wedge dy\wedge dz\), and the model derivative \(\mathrm{diag}(1,1,-2)\) in the ordered self-dual frame obtained from those coordinates. The meridian \(S\) is the outward boundary of the normal ball, and the curve orientation is the one defined by \(ar\omega|_{TC}>0\) in existence §2(d). Lemma 2.1 gives \(c_1(K)[S]=+2\). The determinant extends across the ball, so \(c_1(\mathcal L)[S]=0\). Section 7(f) therefore gives \(T\cdot S=+1\) for the weighted current.

Let \(r\) be a tubular radius and \(\pi\) the projection to the oriented \(Z\). At a transverse intersection with \(S=\{t=t_0,r=\epsilon\}\), an oriented tangent basis of an exterior truncation of the normalization is \((a,v)\), where \(dr(a)<0\) and \(v\) is the oriented boundary tangent with \(dr(v)=0\). The ambient orientation orders \(dt,dr\) before the positively oriented meridian. The intersection sign is the sign of \(-dt(v)dr(a)\), which is also the sign of \(d\pi(v)\). Thus the signed degree used by the period lemma and Taubes's meridional sign agree.

Since \(H^1(V;\mathbb R)=H^2(V;\mathbb R)=0\), \(P=\int_Z\alpha\) with \(d\alpha=\bar\omega\) is well-defined. The period identity of LINE-E06 gives \(\int_{C_a}\bar\omega=n_aP>0\) for every nonconstant irreducible component, where \(n_a\) is the degree just oriented. All the nonzero \(n_a\) have the same sign, and \(\sum_a m_a n_a=1\). Hence \(P>0\), and the current has one irreducible component with \(m=n=1\) and energy \(P\). Reversing the orientation of \(Z\) reverses both \(P\) and \(n\). In the model, Taubes's local primitive \(-f\,dt-h_{\mathrm{loc}}\,d\phi\), where \(f=(x^2+y^2)/2-z^2\) and \(h_{\mathrm{loc}}=(x^2+y^2)z\), has zero period on \(Z\). The positive period comes from the global primitive on \(V\). ## Sheet counts in a prescribed metric

**Import and signed constraint.** In the prepared tube (on its normal double cover in the twisted case), Taubes's local Proposition 4.1 counts equatorial rays by \(p\), axial rays by \(q_+,q_-\), and isolated cone terms by \(n_+,n_-\). The first step of its proof identifies \(p-q_+-q_-\) with the signed linking number, including at exceptional points as an identity for tangent-current coefficients. An equatorial strip in outward parameter \(s>0\) has \(\omega|_{C}=s\,dt\wedge ds\). On both axial strips \(z=\pm s\), \(\omega|_{C}=-2s\,dt\wedge ds\). These give the signs \(+1,-1,-1\) in the exterior boundary convention. Isolated cones have zero net meridional degree. Each lifted meridian maps diffeomorphically and orientation-preservingly to the base meridian. The **full preimage** therefore satisfies \(p-q_+-q_-=1\) at each lifted fiber. Summing the two meridians gives two. This computation uses the normal double cover of the tube and counts its lifted sheets regardless of connectivity. At regular points, Proposition 7.1 gives \(q_+,q_-\in\{0,1\}\) and constancy on the lifted regular set. The twisted deck map swaps the two axial labels. Consequently the regular counts on the lifted circle can be \((p,q_+,q_-)=(1,0,0)\) or \((3,1,1)\) by these constraints. At an exceptional point the deck map relates the counts at the two different lifts. [Taubes, local paper (1.1), Theorem 1.8, §1(c), Proposition 4.1 and proof, Proposition 7.1, pp. 222, 228–233, 251–253, 261–262](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9901142)

Neither the cap intersection nor the period separates positive and negative meridional contributions on different parts of one global normalization. A separate global component tending only to one point of \(Z\) would have degree zero and is ruled out by the period identity. A local approach with degree zero can be part of the same global component. The local energy estimates bound exceptional contributions at small scales and give a finite exceptional set. They supply neither a calibrated minimum energy charge per germ relative to \(P\) nor a vanishing bound on the number of exceptions.

## Cap-relative choice that removes regular axial germs

**Import.** Taubes's moduli Proposition 2.11 gives residual vanishing of both \(q_+\) and \(q_-\) **as defined in (1.4)** for pairs obeying the exact model (1.1), which explicitly admits the double-cover case. These are the counts of actual axial half-disks at regular points. The proof uses Lemma 6.1: in a symplectic ball, arbitrarily small compatible perturbations supported in a smaller ball can preclude any finite-energy local curve containing the prescribed annular part of a fixed embedded holomorphic disk outside the smaller ball. Section 6(a) uses the axial plane for that disk. Section 6(b) explicitly notes the localization to arbitrarily small balls on the axial plane near \(Z\). [Taubes, moduli paper (1.1), (1.4), Definition 1.2, Prop. 2.11, Lemma 6.1 and §§6(a–b), pp. 277–279, 286, 319–324](https://intlpress.com/api/bgcloud-front/resource/pdf/volume/1806254042319917058-1806254042319917058-068beefee00cf9e5190a3ef49dcaba51.pdf)

**Project deduction, working.** To preserve the marked cap and \(ar\omega\), start with the prepared exact-model tube and retain a strictly smaller whole tube. In one local chart about \(Z\) choose disjoint nested ball pairs \(B_1^\pm\Subset B_4^\pm\), centered on the two axial sides, in the original exact region outside the retained tube and away from the marked cap. The annular axial patch in each \(B_4^\pm\setminus B_1^\pm\) can be joined to an open part of the axial strip in the retained tube through the exact region without meeting either support. Choose the two Lemma 6.1 variations independently in the disjoint smaller balls and extend by the original \(J\). Any regular axial germ continues, by the unique-continuation argument in the proof of the local Proposition 7.2, along a connected exact axial strip to one of the chosen annular patches. The strip within the retained twisted tube can be followed over the circle in the orientation cover of the axial line. The choice of two patches removes dependence on which label reaches the chart. Restriction of a global finite-energy curve to \(B_4^\pm\) would contradict Lemma 6.1. Local variations in base balls lift equivariantly to the normal double cover.

On the supports where \(ar\omega\ne0\), a compatible \(J'\) gives a smooth adapted metric \(g'=(\sqrt2/|\bar\omega|_g)\bar\omega(\cdot,J'\cdot)\) in the positive compatibility convention. It glues to \(g\), with \(ar\omega\) still closed and self-dual and with the original cap and smaller exact tube. The harmonic ray, chamber argument, cap intersection, orientation and period then apply to the Taubes current obtained for the new metric. It has \(q_+=q_-=0\) and \(p=1\) at every regular point. This deduces existence for a cap-preserving choice of adapted metric. For a fixed metric the signed constraint remains the conclusion of the degree argument. **Remaining condition.** The moduli definition of boundary regularity also requires an empty exceptional set. The local §9 starts after setting the regular axial counts to zero and still analyzes exceptional points with axial tangent multiplicity. Local Proposition 5.1 gives finiteness of the isolated cone contributions and Example 1.5 shows their finite-energy type. Lemma 6.1 concerns a prescribed annular patch of an exact disk. The inspected passages give no such patch for a curve known only to have an exceptional tangent cone. The missing global input is exclusion of every residual exceptional point for some allowed cap-relative choice, including any isolated zero-degree approach of the same irreducible component. The Fredholm theory for boundary-regular curves already assumes the exceptional set is empty.
