## The question and the frame

Three statements make up the frame.

- **A1 (replayed project theorem).** For every smooth Riemannian conformal class $c$ on an oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere $M$ and every $p\in M$, the space $\mathcal H_p(c)$ defined below has dimension three. Prescribing the leading coefficient gives exactly one global pole form. The growth bound in the definition includes the zero form. [Proof and non-author reading](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/research-progress/handoffs/TO_MASTER2_FRAME_20261127.md)
- **A2 (replayed project theorem).** If one member of $\mathcal H_p(c)$ is nowhere zero on $X=M\setminus\{p\}$, then $M$ is diffeomorphic to $S^4$. The project argument makes the symplectic end exactly constant, caps it, and uses the prescribed splitting at infinity to extend the diffeomorphism across $p$. The import is Gromov's relative product splitting, §2.4.A′₁–A′₂, p. 337. [Proof and source inspection](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/references/A2_ORIGINAL.md)
- **Z (open).** For each such $M$, do there exist $c,p$ and a fixed unit vector $u$ in the three-dimensional space of coefficients such that $\omega_u(q)\ne0$ for every $q\in X$? A basis may be chosen arbitrarily. One constant linear combination must work throughout $X$.

**Project theorem (reformulation).** A2 gives one implication. In the reverse direction a diffeomorphism pulls back the round sphere's zero-free constant forms in stereographic coordinates. Thus Z is equivalent to smooth Poincaré. A1 supplies the entire family on every class. Neither A1 nor A2 has a Lean proof. [Master acceptance](../research-progress/handoffs/TO_POLE_FRAME_ACCEPTANCE_20261127.md)

## Fixed setting and conventions

- *Category and signature.* $M$ is a closed, connected, oriented smooth four-manifold homotopy equivalent to $S^4$, in the smooth category. All metrics are smooth and Riemannian, and $c$ is a conformal class of such metrics. No complex, neutral or Lorentzian structure is used anywhere in this series.
- *Orientation and chirality.* $\Lambda^\pm$ are the $\pm1$ eigenbundles of the Hodge star on two-forms, which depends only on $c$. Self-dual means $\Lambda^+$ for the given orientation of $M$. Reversing the orientation exchanges the two bundles; every statement is made for one orientation and holds for the other with the roles exchanged. For a one-form $A$, $d^\pm A$ are the two components of $dA$.
- *Norms.* The norm on two-forms has $|e^1\wedge e^2|=1$ for an orthonormal coframe, so $\omega\wedge\omega=|\omega|^2dV$ for a self-dual $\omega$. The $L^2$ norm of a two-form depends only on $c$.
- *Curvature and energies.* As in the [first master](../../master/manuscript/chiral_weyl_energy_and_smooth_poincare.tex): the curvature operator of the unit sphere is the identity, $W^\pm$ are trace-free symmetric endomorphisms of $\Lambda^\pm$ with Hilbert–Schmidt norms, $e=E_+=E_-$ on $M$, $T=4\pi^2$, and $Y(c)$ is the Yamabe constant of the conformal Laplacian $-6\Delta+R$. These enter only in the bridge question and in calibrations.
- *The puncture.* $p\in M$, $X=M\setminus p$, which is contractible. For $g\in c$, $y$ are positively oriented orthonormal normal coordinates at $p$ and $x=y/|y|^2$ are the orientation-reversing inverted coordinates, with $r=|x|$. Near infinity means near $p$. The metric $|y|^{-4}g$ is asymptotically Euclidean of order two in $x$. A form is standard at infinity when it has constant coefficients in $x$ outside a compact set.
- *The transform.* $T_c:L^2(\Lambda^-)\to L^2(\Lambda^+)$, $d^-A\mapsto d^+A$. It is a well-defined isometry because $b_1(M)=b_2(M)=0$.
- *Pole forms.* $\mathcal H_p(c)$ is the space of closed $c$-self-dual two-forms $\omega$ on $X$ with $|\omega|_g=O(d_g(p,\cdot)^{-4})$. Its elements are the pole forms of $(M,c,p)$. In the chart $y$ a pole form has leading term $|y|^{-4}R_y^*\eta$, with $R_y$ the reflection in the hyperplane orthogonal to $y$ and $\eta$ a constant anti-self-dual form, its leading coefficient. Writing $P_g(q,p)\eta=\omega_\eta(q)$ gives the unit-leading solution; the remainder at the puncture is $O_\infty(|y|^{-2})$. With source volume $dV_g(p)$ the off-diagonal transfer kernel is $K_g=(2/\pi^2)P_g$. For a fixed solution and $\widehat g=e^{2f}g$ the covariant coefficient transforms as $\eta_{\widehat g}=e^{4f(p)}\eta_g$, so the density-normalized coefficient $\eta_g/dV_g(p)$ is invariant. Reversal of orientation exchanges the two points and takes the fibre adjoint, $K_g^{-o}(p,q)=K_g^o(q,p)^*$. **Replayed project theorem.** [Normalization and reading](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/research-progress/handoffs/TO_MASTER2_NORMALIZATION_20261127.md)
- *The triple.* For a basis $\eta_1,\eta_2,\eta_3$ of $\Lambda^-_p$ the forms $\omega_1,\omega_2,\omega_3$ are a pole triple, and $\omega_u=\sum u_i\omega_i$. The evaluation map is $\mathrm{ev}_q:\Lambda^-_p\to\Lambda^+_q$. The Gram matrix is $Q_{ij}\,dV=\omega_i\wedge\omega_j$. The triple is definite at $q$ when $\mathrm{ev}_q$ is invertible. The degeneracy set is $H=\{q:\det\mathrm{ev}_q=0\}$, and the kernel map $\kappa$ sends a point of $H$ where the rank is two to the line of $u$ with $\omega_u(q)=0$. At a transverse zero of a fixed $\omega_u$, the intrinsic spatial derivative has rank three and the zero set is locally a smooth curve. Singular zeros can occur. A full normalized sphere-of-directions chart at $q$ requires invertible evaluation there.
- *Weights.* Weight $\delta$ means decay $O(r^\delta)$ at infinity; the operator $d^*\oplus d^-$ lowers the weight by one.
- *Named objects.* The calibration family $c_\varepsilon$, $\varepsilon>-4$, of section 3.5 of the [proposing note](../../master/brainstorm/notes/claude/ALTERNATIVE_FRAMEWORKS_AND_INVESTIGATIONS_2_20261127.md). The prism closings $\Sigma_{\rm ev}$ and $\Sigma_{\rm odd}$ of the [first master's prism section](../../master/manuscript/claude/even_prism_closing.tex). The presentation sphere $\Sigma_P$ of [investigation 07](../../investigations-1/07_positive_subcritical_entry/notes/PRESENTATION_SOURCES.md).
- *Labels.* Import, project theorem (working, replayed, established), certificate and heuristic, as defined in the [working rules](../../investigations-2/README.md#working-rules).

## The marked end and the zeros

**Replayed project theorems.** A1 gives an asymptotically constant closed form on the inverted end. A2 uses a primitive bound and annular cutoff to obtain an exactly constant symplectic end. The resulting diffeomorphism agrees far out with a similarity in the specified chart, so it extends smoothly over the original compactification. Zero-freeness persists under smooth variation of the class, pole and nonzero coefficient. On the standard sphere there is also a path of classes, with one fixed pole and fixed nonzero coefficient, along which every zero at the first parameter with a zero has derivative of rank at most two. This leaves the other directions and the energy of that class undetermined. [Marked-end and first-zero return](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/research-progress/handoffs/TO_02_03_MARKED_END_20261127.md)

Evaluation is a real linear map on the coefficient space. Normalizing its nonzero values gives a map of directions into the twistor sphere; at rank three it gives a chart on the whole fibre. No holomorphicity is needed for A1 or A2. [Pointwise computation](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E05_DRAFTS.md) · [Independent reading](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/reviews/E05_READING.md)

## Recognition on a handle atlas

**Replayed project theorem.** For fixed marked data $(M,c,p)$, A1 gives global evaluations $L_q u=\omega_u(q)$ on $X=M\setminus\{p\}$. A1 controls every unit coefficient sufficiently far out on the inverted end. Cover the remaining compact core by finitely many compact pieces in handle interiors and attaching collars. For this class and pole, a zero-free member exists exactly when **one coefficient line** has a positive minimum on every piece. A2 then recognizes the original smooth manifold. This finite-cover criterion has an independent mathematical reading. [Investigation 01, manuscript §6](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/manuscript/chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms.tex) · [Independent reading](../../investigations-2/01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/reviews/MANUSCRIPT_READING_20261127.md)

**Proposed strategy for Z.** Start with a presentation in the given smooth structure, choose a conformal class and marked pole, and use the attaching maps to estimate the same pole form in different frames. The first proposed case is the minus Miller–Schupp sphere $\Sigma_P$, whose two marked cap maps and inverses are available. Investigation 01 owns the construction and bounds. The master will assess which estimates depend on those attachments and whether any give a rule for choosing data on an arbitrary smooth type. A positive result on $\Sigma_P$ would recognize that sphere. Universal Z still requires a choice on every smooth type. Generalized isothermal charts may help obtain the local bounds if their transitions preserve the needed data. A complete twistor-coordinate construction is a separate goal.
