## Normalization and quantified generic-flow import

Write $\lambda_j=\lambda(S^j)$ for $1\le j\le4$. For a smooth $M^4\subset\mathbb R^5$, CMS Definition 1.6 gives

$$
\lambda(M)=\sup_{x_0\in\mathbb R^5,\,t_0>0}(4\pi t_0)^{-2}
\int_M e^{-|x-x_0|^2/(4t_0)}\,d\mathcal H^4(x).
$$

The ambient metric is Euclidean, all graph and isotopy statements are smooth, and no orientation is stipulated. The normalized multiplicity-one shrinkers are $S^j(\sqrt{2j})\times\mathbb R^{4-j}$ up to an ambient orthogonal transformation. Their entropies are the same as those of the corresponding spheres, with $1<\lambda_4<\lambda_3<\lambda_2<\lambda_1<2$. CMS Definition 1.11 calls a singular point generic when some tangent flow is one of the self-similar flows associated with these shrinkers with multiplicity one, which then holds for every tangent flow by the cited uniqueness. Entropy bounds along each admissible weak flow follow from Huisken monotonicity. [CMS Defs. 1.6, 1.8, 1.11 and Remarks 1.7, 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03856v2).

**Import candidate · CMS Corollary 1.19, the precise version of Theorem 1.3.** For every smooth closed embedded hypersurface $M^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ with $\lambda(M)\le2$ and every $C^\infty$ neighborhood of $M$ there is a graph $M'$ over $M$ in that neighborhood such that every $\mathcal M'\in\mathfrak F(M')$ has only generic singularities. Thus each singularity of these flows is modeled with multiplicity one on $S^4$, $S^3\times\mathbb R$, $S^2\times\mathbb R^2$ or $S^1\times\mathbb R^3$. CMS does not impose connectedness for this corollary. The flow class $\widehat{\mathfrak F}(M')$ consists of unit-regular cyclic integral Brakke flows initialized by $\mathcal H^4\lfloor M'$. The class $\mathfrak F(M')$ consists of members of $\widehat{\mathfrak F}(M')$ admitting a weak Brakke-flow approximation from closed embedded initial hypersurfaces, each disjoint from and converging smoothly to $M'$. CMS expressly records that $\mathfrak F(M')$ is nonempty. The quantifier is over all members of this class for each suitably selected graph. Theorem 1.13 assumes $(\diamondsuit_{n,\Lambda})$ and $(\heartsuit_{n,\Lambda})$ for $\Lambda\in(0,2]$. At $n=4$ the respective ranges $7\le k\le n$ and $2\le k\le n-3$ are empty. [CMS Def. 1.12, Thm. 1.13, Remarks 1.16–1.17 and Cor. 1.19](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03856v2).

## Isotopy imports in dimension four

Both statements quantify over **every smooth closed connected embedded** $M^4\subset\mathbb R^5$. The isotopy is an existence statement about the original hypersurface as an embedded submanifold of $\mathbb R^5$. If needed, a sufficiently small smooth normal graph is joined to the original by interpolation in a tubular neighborhood, and the subsequent isotopy is composed with it.

| Candidate | Hypothesis | Conclusion and scope |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Import · CMS Cor. 1.5(a)** | $\lambda(M)\le\lambda_3$ | There is a smooth isotopy in $\mathbb R^5$ to a round $S^4$. After a possible initial perturbation the flow part is smooth up to its spherical extinction. The isotopy uses smooth slices and a rounded endpoint before the hypersurface vanishes. |
| **Import with dimension correction · CMS Cor. 1.5(b)** | $\lambda(M)\le\lambda_2$ | There is a smooth isotopy in $\mathbb R^5$ to the boundary of a standard handlebody. The intended solid model in this dimension is $B^5$ or a boundary connected sum of $k\ge1$ copies of $B^4\times S^1$. The boundary is $S^4$ or $\#_k(S^3\times S^1)$, respectively. The construction uses the DH surgery flow of a suitable small perturbation. |

**Version and endpoint.** Corollary 1.5 gives inclusive inequalities in arXiv v2 and the Warwick accepted manuscript. In Corollary 1.22 the general hypotheses are $\Lambda\in(0,2]$, the two main conditions, and $\lambda(M)\le\Lambda$. The arXiv v2 HTML and PDF agree that part (a) uses $\Lambda\le\lambda(S^{n-1})$. Two text extractions from the same Warwick PDF URL disagree on the sign in Corollary 1.22(a). The page-indexed extraction also gives $\le$ on manuscript p. 7, and a separate transcription gives $<$. This is an extraction discrepancy, with no verified change of version. The extractions agree on $\Lambda\le\lambda(S^{n-2})$ in (b). Use Corollary 1.5(a) for the dimension-four endpoint. In the proof of Corollary 1.22 the strict entropy inequality has $M$ in the first occurrence after introducing $M'$. Section 6.1 supplies the intended input $\lambda(M')<\lambda(M)\le\Lambda$ after a short smooth flow or entropy-lowering perturbation unless $M$ is already round. The subsequent local foliation is chosen with $\lambda(M_s)\le\Lambda-\varepsilon$ for some $\varepsilon>0$ after that strict lowering. This is slack for the selected perturbation and local family. The inspected statements supply no uniform positive slack above the initial recognition threshold $\lambda_2$. The bound $2$ applies to generic tangent-model classification. [CMS arXiv v2, Cor. 1.5, Cor. 1.22 and §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03856v2) · [Warwick accepted manuscript, Cor. 1.5 and Cor. 1.22](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/194149/2/WRAP-Mean-curvature-flow-low-entropy-generic-initial-data-II-25.pdf).

**Handlebody dimension.** Both primary texts say the target is the boundary of a standard handlebody and print $B^n$ and $B^{n-1}\times S^1$ for the solids in both corollaries. Their boundaries are $(n-1)$-dimensional, whereas the target here is an $n$-dimensional hypersurface in $\mathbb R^{n+1}$. The solid model has one index-zero handle and $k$ index-one handles in dimension $n+1$, so the corrected notation is $B^{n+1}$ and $\natural_k(B^n\times S^1)$ where $\natural$ is boundary connected sum. DH Thm. 5.9 lists $S^n$ and $S^{n-1}\times S^1$ as discarded hypersurface types. HK Cors. 1.25–1.26 give the solid types $B^N$ and $B^{N-1}\times S^1$ in ambient dimension $N$. This corroborates the dimension correction. The full embedded isotopy conclusion is the assertion of CMS. [CMS Cors. 1.5(b), 1.22(b)](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03856v2) · [DH Thm. 5.9](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/170485/1/WRAP-Approximation-of-mean-curvature-flow-with-generic-singularities-by-smooth-flows-with-surgery-Daniels-Holgate-2022.pdf) · [HK p. 8](https://www.math.toronto.edu/roberth/papers/HaslhoferKleiner_MCFsurgery.pdf).

**Use of the import for an individual embedding.** If the initial embedded hypersurface in Corollary 1.5(b) is a homotopy four-sphere, it has $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=0$. The model boundary has $H_1=\mathbb Z^k$ by the connected-sum formula. Hence $k=0$ and the isotopy ends at the boundary of a standard $B^5$. This is an elementary consequence of the corrected import, conditional on the specified smooth embedding meeting its entropy hypothesis.

## Surgery import and the time trace

**Import candidate · DH Theorems 1.2 and 5.5.** Let $M^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ be closed and smoothly embedded and let a unit-regular, cyclic mod 2, integral Brakke flow from $M$ have only multiplicity-one spherical and neck-pinch singularities. The relevant backward tangent models are a shrinking $S^4$ and a shrinking $S^3\times\mathbb R$. DH Def. 1.1(a) describes a shrinking sphere but has a spurious $\times\mathbb R$ after the spherical factor in print, which would give the wrong hypersurface dimension. For the admissible weak $(\alpha,\delta,\mathbb H)$ surgery flows of Def. 3.17, DH provides a finite lower scale $H_{\min}(M)$ and finite ratio threshold $\Theta(M)$ such that every such flow with $H_{\mathrm{th}}>H_{\min}$ and $\mathbb H>\Theta$ is a smooth mean curvature flow with surgery, with $|H|\le H_{\mathrm{trig}}$ and finite extinction. Here $\mathbb H=(H_{\mathrm{th}},H_{\mathrm{neck}},H_{\mathrm{trig}})$ and the ratio condition concerns $H_{\mathrm{trig}}/H_{\mathrm{neck}}$ and $H_{\mathrm{neck}}/H_{\mathrm{th}}$. The surgery is localized to neighborhoods in which the reference flow is noncollapsed and uniformly two-convex. With the further canonical-neighborhood scale bounds in Thm. 5.9, the discarded hypersurface components are diffeomorphic to $S^4$ or $S^3\times S^1$. [DH Defs. 1.1, 3.17 and Thms. 1.2, 5.5, 5.9](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/170485/1/WRAP-Approximation-of-mean-curvature-flow-with-generic-singularities-by-smooth-flows-with-surgery-Daniels-Holgate-2022.pdf).

At the inclusive $\lambda_2$ endpoint, CMS §6.1 and Corollary 1.22 permit selecting perturbed data with strict entropy $<\lambda_2$, leaving only the sphere and the one-axis neck among generic models. The strict inequality excludes the two-axis cylinder $S^2\times\mathbb R^2$ at its own threshold. DH Lemma 6.2(i) gives an isotopy on each smooth time interval between surgeries. At surgery times caps replace necks and components can be discarded, so topology can change across surgery times. The isotopy asserted by CMS is reconstructed using surgery information. In DH §6 one sees a reverse-induction example of this distinction for the separate, dimension-three Schoenflies application (Thm. 6.4, $\Sigma^3\subset\mathbb R^4$ homeomorphic to $S^3$, $\lambda(\Sigma)\le\lambda(S^1\times\mathbb R^2)$). The dimension-four source of the isotopy conclusion is CMS Corollary 1.5. [CMS Cor. 1.22 and §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03856v2) · [DH Lemma 6.2 and Thm. 6.4](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/170485/1/WRAP-Approximation-of-mean-curvature-flow-with-generic-singularities-by-smooth-flows-with-surgery-Daniels-Holgate-2022.pdf).
