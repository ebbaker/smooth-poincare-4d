## Statement

Let $M$ be a closed smooth homotopy four-sphere. There exists a smooth embedding $f\colon M\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^5$. For every smooth embedding $f\colon M\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^5$, let $C_f$ be the closure of the unique bounded component of $\mathbb R^5\setminus f(M)$. Then

$$
C_f\cong_{\mathrm{diff}}D^5\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad M\cong_{\mathrm{diff}}S^4.
$$

Consequently, the existence of one embedding with bounded side diffeomorphic to $D^5$ and the same condition for every embedding are equivalent. Here $D^k$ and $S^{k-1}=\partial D^k$ carry their standard smooth structures.

## Embedding

**Import.** Kervaire's Theorem 3, p. 70, gives a smooth compact contractible five-manifold $V$ with boundary diffeomorphic to any given smooth integral homology four-sphere. The first part of his proof constructs this smooth filling by framed surgery on a parallelizable five-manifold with that boundary. A homotopy four-sphere satisfies the integral homology hypothesis. Fix a boundary identification $M\cong\partial V$. [Kervaire 1969, Theorem 3](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/kervsmoo.pdf#page=5)

Use a smooth collar of $\partial V$ to round the codimension-two corners of $V\times[-1,1]$ near the end faces, leaving $\partial V\times\{0\}$ intact. The resulting smooth compact six-manifold $W$ is contractible, since rounding in the collar leaves its homeomorphism type unchanged and $V\times[-1,1]$ is contractible. Its boundary is the smooth double $DV=V\cup_M(-V)$ up to diffeomorphism. The midsection $M$ is the gluing hypersurface. Small open enlargements of the two halves of $DV$ retract onto copies of $V$, with intersection retracting onto the connected $M$. Van Kampen gives $\pi_1(DV)=0$. Mayer–Vietoris gives $\widetilde H_j(DV;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H_{j-1}(M;\mathbb Z)$ for $j\ge1$. In particular $DV$ is a homology five-sphere, and simple connectivity gives the homotopy type of $S^5$ by Hurewicz and Whitehead.

**Import.** Smale's Theorem (1.1), specialized to $n=6$, says that a compact smooth contractible six-manifold with simply connected boundary is diffeomorphic to $D^6$. Apply it to $W$ and restrict the diffeomorphism to get $DV\cong S^5$ smoothly. The dimension-six case also appears in Smale's earlier Theorem M, p. 394, with $2m=6$. Choose a point in the interior of one half of $DV$ and project from its image under the boundary diffeomorphism to obtain a smooth embedding in $\mathbb R^5$. Kervaire's 1965 proof of Theorem I explicitly uses this double in the $n=4$ case. [Smale 1961, Theorem (1.1), p. 498](https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/Smale-diff-comb.pdf#page=2) · [Smale 1961, Theorem M, p. 394](https://www.math.uchicago.edu/~shmuel/tom-readings/Smale%2C%20PC.pdf#page=5) · [Kervaire 1965, p. 112](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/kervhknt.pdf#page=8)

The cobordism dimensions can also be checked directly. Puncturing $W$ in a smooth interior six-ball gives a six-dimensional cobordism from $DV$ to $S^5$. Van Kampen gives simple connectivity of the puncture, and Mayer–Vietoris for gluing the ball back in makes the $S^5$ inclusion a homology equivalence. The relative fundamental class identifies the fundamental classes of the two boundary components up to sign in the puncture, so the inclusion of $DV$ is a homology equivalence too. Both inclusions are homotopy equivalences by the simply connected Whitehead theorem. Smale's smooth $h$-cobordism theorem applies to boundary dimension $5$ and cobordism dimension $6$. Puncturing $V$ instead gives boundary dimension $4$ and cobordism dimension $5$, outside the hypotheses. That puncturing argument gives the zero $h$-cobordism class in Kervaire–Milnor's $\Theta_4$, whose definition and table are on p. 504. The filling $V$ is obtained here directly from Kervaire's Theorem 3. [Smale 1962, Theorem 1.1, p. 387](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/structur.pdf#page=2) · [Kervaire–Milnor 1963, pp. 504–505](https://www.sas.rochester.edu/mth/sites/doug-ravenel/otherpapers/kervaire-milnor.pdf#page=2)

## Recognition for each embedding

For any $f$ as above, compactify the ambient space to $S^5$ and use Alexander duality with $A=f(M)$ to get $\widetilde H_0(S^5\setminus A;\mathbb Z)\cong\widetilde H^4(A;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z$. Thus there are two components. The connected orientable $A$ has a two-sided collar. Each component meets a different side of the collar, since each component has boundary in $A$ and the two open collar halves are connected. Both closures are smooth compact manifolds with boundary $A$. The component avoiding the point at infinity gives $C_f$ in $\mathbb R^5$. The oriented boundary convention on $C_f$ is induced by the ambient orientation. The equivalence concerns unmarked diffeomorphism types, so an orientation choice on $M$ does not enter it.

If $C_f\cong D^5$ smoothly, restriction to the boundary and $f$ give $M\cong S^4$ smoothly. Conversely, if $M\cong S^4$ smoothly, choose a diffeomorphism $u\colon S^4\to M$ and use Smale's differentiable Schönflies result for the smooth embedding $f\circ u\colon S^4\hookrightarrow\mathbb R^5$. **Import.** Corollary (1.3) at $n=5$ gives $C_f\cong D^5$ smoothly for every such embedding. This corollary includes ambient dimension $5$, whereas the contractible-manifold Theorem (1.1) quoted above excludes that dimension. [Smale 1961, Corollary (1.3), p. 498](https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/Smale-diff-comb.pdf#page=2)

The appropriate codimension-zero disk statement is Palais's Theorem B, p. 275. In a connected orientable smooth $n$-manifold, two smooth embedded $n$-disks parametrized with the same orientation are matched by an ambient diffeomorphism, and Corollary 1 on p. 276 puts it in the identity component in Palais's sense. Its premise already provides two disk embeddings. Smooth Schönflies starts with a codimension-one embedding of the *standard* $S^4$, supplies the disk on the bounded side, and requires no disk parametrization as input. In particular, mere separation and contractibility of a side cannot supply the smooth boundary identification used in the forward application of Corollary (1.3). [Palais 1960, §3, Theorem B and Corollary 1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1960-0117741-0)
