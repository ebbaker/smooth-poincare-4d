## Gaussian lower tests

**Project theorem, replayed.** Work with a smooth embedded four-dimensional hypersurface \(\Sigma\subset\mathbb R^5\), Euclidean metric, multiplicity-one four-area and arbitrary centres \(x\in\mathbb R^5\), times \(t>0\). Orientation plays no role. In an ambient orthogonal splitting let \(P=B_R^2(0)\times S_\epsilon^2(0)\) be a subset of \(\Sigma\). At \((0,t)\) the disk contributes \(4\pi t(1-e^{-R^2/(4t)})\) and the sphere contributes \(4\pi\epsilon^2e^{-\epsilon^2/(4t)}\) before normalization. Hence
\[
F_{0,t}(P)=\frac{\epsilon^2}{t}e^{-\epsilon^2/(4t)}(1-e^{-R^2/(4t)}),
\qquad
\lambda(\Sigma)\ge F_{0,\epsilon^2/4}(P)=\frac4e(1-e^{-R^2/\epsilon^2}).
\]
These estimates use the Euclidean placement in the displayed splitting. An intrinsic product metric by itself supplies neither the ambient distances to the centre nor this subset of the image.

For a complete \(\mathbb R^k\times S_\epsilon^{m}\) with \(m=4-k\) and \(0\le k\le2\), the full \(k\)-plane integral cancels its own Gaussian normalization. The centred spherical factor is proportional to \(t^{-m/2}e^{-\epsilon^2/(4t)}\) and is maximized at \(t_* = \epsilon^2/(2m)\). It also gives the full cylinder entropy. Tangential translations leave the plane integral fixed, and the compact shrinking sphere, scaled to radius \(\sqrt{2m}\), achieves its full entropy at \((0,1)\) by Colding–Minicozzi Lemma 7.10 and its proof. When \(k>0\) and the disk is finite, the truncation factor is the Gaussian probability of the disk under covariance \(2tI_k\), which strictly decreases with \(t\). The derivative of the centred finite-patch expression at \(t_*\) is then negative. This clarifies the scope of the phrase “optimal time” in B01. [Colding–Minicozzi, Lemma 7.10 and its proof, pp. 785–788](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v175-n2-p07-p.pdf)

Similarities preserve entropy, with \(t\) transforming by the square of the dilation. When the prescribed dilations and rigid motions contain smooth multiplicity-one patches converging to the two-axis cylinder on every compact piece, fix a compact cutoff of that cylinder and test the dilated images at \(t=1/4\). The contribution on the corresponding patches converges, giving a lower bound for the lower limit of the whole entropies. Increase the cutoff to exhaust the cylinder to obtain \(\liminf_j\lambda(\Sigma_j)\ge4/e\). Extra sheets contribute nonnegative Gaussian area. The lower-limit inequality alone leaves open the side of the threshold from which the entropies might approach \(4/e\). At the specific test of a single exact finite patch, the total contribution allowed from the rest of an entering image is at most \((4/e)e^{-R^2/\epsilon^2}\).

**Project theorem, replayed.** Suppose two such patches with the same tangential disk have normal centres separated by \(3\epsilon\). They are disjoint. At the first centre and \(t=\epsilon^2/4\), every point of the second normal sphere is within \(4\epsilon\) in the normal factor. Its weight is at least \(e^{-16}\), whereas the first normal weight is \(e^{-1}\). The areas and tangential factors agree, so for \(R/\epsilon\ge4\) the sum is at least
\[
\frac4e(1-e^{-16})(1+e^{-15})>\frac4e.
\]
Indeed, \((1-e^{-16})(1+e^{-15})-1=e^{-16}(e-1-e^{-15})>0\). This excludes precisely a sufficient configuration for failure of entry on any image containing it. Its positive gap persists if perturbations of the two compact patch integrals together cost less than the gap.

## Area criterion and scope

**Project theorem, replayed.** For \(A_x(r)=\mathcal H^4(\Sigma\cap B_r(x))\), write the Gaussian with radial distance \(d\) as \(\int_d^\infty (r/(2t))e^{-r^2/(4t)}\,dr\) and use Tonelli. This gives
\[
 F_{x,t}(\Sigma)=(4\pi t)^{-2}\int_0^\infty \frac{r}{2t}e^{-r^2/(4t)}A_x(r)\,dr.
\]
Replacing \(A_x(r)\) by \(c\omega_4r^4\), with \(\omega_4=\pi^2/2\) and \(c=4/e\), bounds the result by \(c\). The unit Gaussian integral on a four-plane, with centre in the plane, supplies the normalization. The condition requires every ambient \(x\) and every \(r>0\).

**Project calculation.** This sufficient criterion fails even for the round closed sphere. Centring at its centre and taking the ball radius down to the sphere radius from above gives a normalized area tending to \(16/3\), though the sphere's entropy is \(32/(3e^2)\). It also fails for any exact two-cell patch of radius \(R\ge\epsilon\): the axial ball of radius \(\sqrt2\epsilon\) captures a patch of area \(4\pi^2\epsilon^4\), giving \(A_0(r)/(\omega_4r^4)\ge2\). The criterion therefore cannot certify entry of a competitor retaining such a patch even when the Gaussian estimate itself might do so.

## Recognition and the equality case

**Import, checked passage.** CMS Corollary 1.5(b) applies to one smooth closed connected embedded hypersurface with entropy at most \(\lambda(S^2)\) in dimension four. Its standard handlebody conclusion yields \(S^4\) for a homotopy four-sphere: the nonball model boundary has first integral homology \(\mathbb Z^k\), \(k\ge1\). The displayed solid dimensions in the source are one too low for a boundary hypersurface in \(\mathbb R^5\). The [E01 flow reading](../research-progress/handoffs/E01_FLOW_RETURN.md) checks the intended \(B^5\) and boundary connected sums of \(B^4\times S^1\) against Daniels-Holgate's surgery models. CMS §6.1 supplies a strict entropy decrease for a suitable perturbation of a given nonround initial image in the inclusive band. [CMS arXiv:2309.03856v2, Corollaries 1.5 and 1.22 and §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03856v2) · [Daniels-Holgate, Theorem 5.9 and Lemma 6.2](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/170485/1/WRAP-Approximation-of-mean-curvature-flow-with-generic-singularities-by-smooth-flows-with-surgery-Daniels-Holgate-2022.pdf)

**Project theorem, replayed.** For a smooth homotopy four-sphere \(M\), \(I(M)<c\) gives a single embedding with entropy below \(c\) by the infimum property. A single embedding with entropy at most \(c\) gives standardness by the import. Standardness gives a round embedding with entropy \(32/(3e^2)<c\), since \(e>8/3\). These three statements are equivalent. If \(I(M)=c\) then every embedding has entropy strictly greater than \(c\), since an equal-entropy embedding would give standardness and hence \(I(M)<c\). The equality case itself remains open. Even a hypothetical sequence with \(\lambda(\Sigma_j)\le c+o(1)\) would give only \(I(M)\le c\). Using the CMS perturbation above the threshold would require proving that some perturbation decreases entropy by at least that embedding's excess. The qualitative strictly decreasing perturbation and the endpoint import supply no such comparison. [Colding–Minicozzi, Lemma 7.10](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v175-n2-p07-p.pdf) · [CMS §6.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03856v2#S6.SS1)

**Import and project corollary.** Bernstein–Wang, Theorem 1.1, gives \(\lambda(\Sigma)\ge\lambda(S^n)\) for a smooth, compact, boundaryless embedded hypersurface \(\Sigma^n\subset\mathbb R^{n+1}\) with \(2\le n\le6\). Equality holds exactly for translates and positive dilates of the round sphere. Their translation and dilation normalization is equivalent to the unrestricted centre and scale above. In dimension four this gives \(I(S^4)=32/(3e^2)\), attained by a round embedding. Combined with the recognition equivalence, the least entropies of smooth homotopy four-spheres belong to \(\{32/(3e^2)\}\cup[4/e,\infty)\). [Bernstein–Wang, arXiv:1406.2966v2, p. 1, Eqs. (1.1)–(1.3) and Theorem 1.1, checked 27 November 2026](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2966) · [Invent. Math. 206 (2016), 601–627](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-016-0659-3)

## Marked geometry and possible upper bounds

**Heuristic assessment.** The marked construction has an explicit Euclidean starting region \(V=F\times D^3\subset\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^3\) with rounded corners. The attaching maps and their buffers have disjoint latitude bands in its boundary, and the cap maps fix the smooth gluing of \(D^2\times S^2\) to the retained exterior. The band separation of the attaching tubes gives no placement for the interiors of the two new caps as disjoint subsets of \(\mathbb R^5\). The controlled intrinsic cap metric and the Weyl estimates on the closed marked assembly do not estimate the Euclidean Gaussian contributions. The qualitative double construction can supply an embedding of the thickening with this smooth boundary, but gives no distortion control or guarantee of retaining the given ambient placement of the exterior. A finite list of local models would still require a realization with seam and inter-sheet control for the combined contributions. A verified identification of the boundary with standard \(S^4\), for example via genuine cancellation of the marked handles, would supply the round competitor directly. The launch group check certifies no such cancellation. [Marked assembly](../../../investigations-1/07_positive_subcritical_entry/notes/MARKED_CONNECTION_ASSEMBLY.md#the-attaching-maps-exterior-and-inverse) · [Embedding return](../research-progress/handoffs/E02_EMBEDDING_RETURN.md) · [Launch check](../../../master/brainstorm/notes/claude/ALTERNATIVE_FRAMEWORKS_AND_INVESTIGATIONS_2_20261127_checks/check_07_entropy_and_presentation.py)

**Project corollary.** Replacing round normal spheres with a different fixed closed normal profile while retaining a complete product limit leaves the cylindrical lower bound in force. For a smooth compact embedded surface \(\Gamma\subset\mathbb R^3\), Fubini gives \(\lambda(\mathbb R^2\times\Gamma)=\lambda(\Gamma)\ge4/e\), strictly if \(\Gamma\) is nonround, by Bernstein–Wang in dimension two. If dilated patches converge smoothly with multiplicity one to this product on compact sets, test each fixed centre and scale on compact pieces before exhausting the product and then take the supremum. This gives \(\liminf_j\lambda(\Sigma_j)\ge\lambda(\Gamma)\). Geometries without such a product limit lie beyond this test. [Bernstein–Wang, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1406.2966)

For a fixed candidate with a quantitative smooth Euclidean parametrization, there is a less restrictive way to organize the upper test than a pointwise area ratio bound. A maximum over centres for each fixed \(t\) lies in the convex hull by the weighted barycentre identity. Large scales are uniformly bounded by \(\mathcal H^4(\Sigma)/(4\pi t)^2\), and sufficiently small scales approach at most one uniformly in the centres for a fixed smooth compact embedded image. This leaves a compact set of centres and an intermediate compact time interval for a potential upper certification. Having actual bounds on the scale cutoffs and a strictly subcritical maximum would make uniform errors usable. If the maximum is at the endpoint, unsigned errors alone yield no endpoint certificate. These cutoffs need not be uniform along a degenerating sequence of embeddings. [Colding–Minicozzi, Lemma 7.7 and its proof, pp. 784–785](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v175-n2-p07-p.pdf)

The open statement for the minus Miller–Schupp boundary is the existence of one smooth Euclidean embedding for which the Gaussian bound holds at every centre and positive scale. The replay supports declining a finite junction packet on the supplied data. It yields no obstruction to entry for the marked type.
