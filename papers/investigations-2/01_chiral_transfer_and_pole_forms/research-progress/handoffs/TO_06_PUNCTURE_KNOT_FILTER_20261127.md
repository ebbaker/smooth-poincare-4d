# To 06 and the Gate 3 owner · a filter on knot witnesses

Source clarification in response to the author's feasibility question. [INV-B01 opening conditions](../../../06_invariants_and_candidates/brainstorm/notes/B01_CANDIDATE_DECISION_20261127.md). This concerns one proposed witness and does not change the B04/E08 marked pole results.

**Deduction from primary imports.** Let W be a smooth compact oriented integral homology four-ball with oriented boundary identified with the standard three-sphere. If an oriented knot J bounds a smooth properly embedded orientable genus-g surface F in W, then τ(J)≤g. Indeed b₁(W)=b₂⁺(W)=0 and H₂(W,∂W)=0. The homology-class terms in the relative genus inequality of Ozsváth–Szabó, Theorem 1.1, vanish. Apply the argument in the reverse orientation with a reflected boundary identification to bound the mirror and obtain |τ(J)|≤g. The mirror sign follows from τ being a concordance homomorphism (Theorem 1.2, using the usual reversed mirror as concordance inverse). For the following exclusion the upper bound alone suffices. Plamenevskaya, Theorem 1, gives tb(Λ)+|rot(Λ)|≤2τ(J)−1 for every Legendrian representative Λ in the standard tight boundary three-sphere. For a disk one obtains tb(Λ)≤−1. A coordinate puncture in a smooth homotopy four-sphere has precisely this homology, including for an exotic smoothing. Thus the nonnegative-tb disk condition suggested for the minus presentation in INV-B01 is impossible even if its closed sphere is exotic. The conditional lasagna theorem used by 06 is unaffected. [Ozsváth–Szabó, original paper, pp. 616–617](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0301149) · [Plamenevskaya, journal paper, Theorem 1, p. 399](https://msp.org/agt/2004/4-1/agt-v4-n1-p20-p.pdf)

**Surviving sufficient witness.** For the rational Rasmussen test and a disk, retain only knots with τ(J)=0 and s(J)≠0. The disk must be embedded in the actual coordinate puncture, with its knot diagram in the marked boundary. More generally a genus-g surface there and |s(J)|>2g would distinguish the sphere: a diffeomorphism with the standard sphere would take a coordinate puncture to a standard ball complement and reproduce the same surface genus in B⁴. The existing 05 marking specifies the candidate, with no certified surface/knot pair of this kind. MMSW, Corollary 1.13, excludes this disk test on the known Gluck-twist comparison candidates. Their §9.3 explicitly distinguishes balanced-presentation doubles in Question 9.12. [Rasmussen, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402131) · [Manolescu–Marengon–Sarkar–Willis, Corollary 1.13 and §9.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08195)

**Separate test case.** Dunfield–Gong, Theorem 5.14, prove in particular that a smooth slice disk for the knot 16n68278 in the standard four-ball would imply the existence of an exotic four-sphere, via a pair of knots with diffeomorphic zero surgeries. A verified ribbon presentation would supply such a disk. This target has no identification with the project's minus presentation. The paper describes its prior computational searches; no witness or complexity bound is supplied here. [Primary preprint, §5.13 and Theorem 5.14, p. 47](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.21825)
