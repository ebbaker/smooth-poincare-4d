## Source

I independently read the [primary Gromov journal scan](https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/945.pdf)
of *Pseudo holomorphic curves in symplectic manifolds*, Invent. Math. 82
(1985), 307–347. Section 0.3.C, printed p. 311, gives global
symplectomorphism for an open symplectic four-manifold symplectomorphic
to standard \(\mathbb R^4\) outside compact sets and with zero real
Hurewicz map in degree two. The theorem there states global
symplectomorphism. It refers to §2.4.A₂ for the argument.

I inspected the original page image and text of printed p. 337
(PDF p. 31). Section §2.4.A′₁ is the splitting criterion for a closed
symplectic four-manifold. It requires two embedded symplectic
spheres with trivial normal bundles, one transverse intersection
of sign \(+1\) in their symplectic orientations, and the periods of
all smooth sphere maps to be integer multiples of the common
area of the two spheres. The neighboring §2.4.A₁ treats the
already split product. The §2.4.A₂ remark states that if the form
already splits near the union of the spheres, the argument extends
that splitting to the whole manifold. On removal of the spheres
the target is a product of disks. This gives agreement on a
neighborhood of the divisor, whose size the remark does not
specify. That is the relative assertion used here.

For McDuff–Salamon, 2nd ed. (AMS, 2012), the AMS catalog and
Google Books bibliographic records were accessible, but searches
of their available previews and the authors' publication pages

## Capping and relative agreement

Given a standard symplectic chart \(e\) on a co-compact tail of \(X\),
truncate it along a round sphere in that chart. Its compact core
deformation retracts \(X\), by radial retraction in the chart.
In the equal-area split product
\((Y_0,\Omega_0)=(S^2\times S^2,\sigma\oplus\sigma)\) let \(D\) be the
union of two factor spheres meeting at one point. The complement
of \(D\) has the coordinates of an open polydisk \(P_L\), with
factor area \(\pi L^2\). To check the coordinates explicitly,
stereographic \(u\mapsto Lu/\sqrt{1+|u|^2}\) pulls planar area back
to \(L^2(1+|u|^2)^{-2}d\operatorname{Re}u\wedge d\operatorname{Im}u\),
since the planar radial Jacobian is \(s(r)s'(r)/r\).

Take \(L\) large enough that the truncating ball and a collar
lie inside \(P_L\). Replace the ball in \(Y_0\) with the core of
\(X\), using \(e\) on the collar. The two pieces fill opposite
sides of the seam in the common collar and the forms coincide
there, so the resulting manifold \(Y\) is closed and the glued
form is smooth and symplectic. The cap retains its prescribed
splitting near \(D\). The two factor spheres have trivial normal
bundles, their unique intersection is transverse and positive,
and both have area \(\pi L^2\). The core has \(H_2=0\) by excision
for \((M,M\setminus\{p\})\). Mayer–Vietoris with the gluing
\(S^3\), which has \(H_2=H_1=0\), identifies \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)\)
with \(H_2(Y_0;\mathbb Z)\), generated by the two factor classes.
Each smooth sphere consequently has period in \(\pi L^2\mathbb Z\).

Apply §2.4.A′₁ and §2.4.A₂ with the cap's fixed identification near
\(D\). Its extension agrees with the cap chart near \(D\), so after
removing \(D\), it sends the core and its surrounding portion of
the end to the open polydisk. The complement of the fixed
neighborhood is compact in the polydisk. A smaller concentric
polydisk can contain this complement and the truncating ball.
The relative map agrees with \(e\) on the exterior of the smaller
polydisk inside \(P_L\). Pasting with \(e\) outside the smaller
polydisk gives a global symplectomorphism. The inverse pieces
agree on the same open overlap. The agreement holds on a full
Euclidean tail since \(P_L\) is bounded.

## Cutoff and smooth compactification

The original zero-free form is symplectic because it is closed
and \(\omega\wedge\omega=|\omega|_g^2\,dV_g>0\) in the chosen
orientation. In the A1 end chart it is \(\omega^0+da\),
\(\omega^0\ne0\), \(a=O(r^{-1})\), \(da=O(r^{-2})\). With
\(\chi_R=0\) for \(r\le R\), \(\chi_R=1\) for \(r\ge2R\), and
\(|d\chi_R|\le C/R\), set \(\omega_R=\omega-d(\chi_R a)\), extending
\(\chi_R a\) by zero off the end. On the annulus it differs from
\(\omega^0\) by
\((1-\chi_R)da-d\chi_R\wedge a=O(R^{-2})\). This is uniformly
smaller than the fixed nondegeneracy margin of \(\omega^0\) when
\(R\) is large. The form is closed, equals \(\omega\) inside and
is constant outside. Its symplectic orientation is the original
one on each region.

Choose a geometrically oriented Euclidean orthonormal coframe
for \(\omega^0=\lambda(e^{12}+e^{34})\), with
\(\lambda=|\omega^0|/\sqrt2>0\). This normal form follows by
skew-orthogonal block reduction, since self-duality and the
orientation equate the two block coefficients. The orthogonal
coordinate transformation \(O\) to this coframe has determinant
\(-1\) relative to the usual \(x\)-coordinate orientation because
the \(x\)-chart is orientation reversing. Thus
\(z=Lx\), \(L=\sqrt{\lambda}O\), is a similarity, and pulls the
standard symplectic form back to \(\omega^0\). In particular a
round exterior maps to a round exterior. The relative splitting
argument above now gives a symplectomorphism \(F:X\to\mathbb R^4\)
with \(F=Lx\) for \(r\) large.

At the point at infinity of the target standard compactification,
use the smooth chart \(z\mapsto z/|z|^2\).
If \(x=y/|y|^2\), then
\[
\frac{Lx}{|Lx|^2}
   =\lambda^{-1/2}O\,y
\]
exactly. This is smooth with invertible derivative at \(y=0\)
and has a smooth linear inverse. Its restriction is the known
symplectomorphism near the puncture, so the global extended
map is a smooth diffeomorphism \(M\to S^4\). This calculation
uses that \(L\) is a similarity. For an arbitrary invertible
linear \(L\), the target expression would instead be
\(|y|^2 L y/|L y|^2\), which need not have a derivative at zero.
