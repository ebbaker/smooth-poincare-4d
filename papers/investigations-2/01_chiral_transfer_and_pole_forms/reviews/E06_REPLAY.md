## Primary source and compatibility at the newborn zero

**Import.** Clifford Henry Taubes, *A proof of a theorem of Luttinger and Simpson about the number of vanishing circles of a near-symplectic form on a 4-dimensional manifold*, Math. Res. Lett. 13 (2006), no. 4, pp. 557–570, DOI 10.4310/MRL.2006.v13.n4.a6. I read the publisher PDF's text for §2, pp. 558–560, including Proposition 2.1 and its five-part proof. Part 1 uses the standard form (2.1) and the local model (2.2)–(2.5). Parts 2–5 glue through annuli with positive square. The last part, (2.16) and the sentence following it, gives equality to the original standard form for \(r\ge32\delta\). Thus the case \(n=0\) on the standard oriented \(\mathbb R^4\) supplies a smooth closed two-form that is symplectic off its one zero circle and has exactly the original constant end. [Publisher PDF](https://intlpress.com/api/bgcloud-front/resource/pdf/volume/1806605523900329985-1806605523900329985-9fa5106dca972fdc95f65c34eac2bf03.pdf)

For the use of E04, the image of the intrinsic derivative must be wedge-positive as well as three-dimensional. In the complex coordinates \(z,w\) of (2.2), let \(\Omega=dz\wedge dw\), \(a=-\varepsilon+|z|^2-|w|^2\) and \(B=zw-R\overline w\). The local model has coefficient vector \((a,-\operatorname{Im}B,\operatorname{Re}B)\) in the Euclidean self-dual basis \((\omega_{\mathrm{std}},\operatorname{Re}\Omega,\operatorname{Im}\Omega)\) for the complex orientation. At \(|z|^2=\varepsilon,\ w=0\), \(da=2\operatorname{Re}(\overline z\,dz)\) is nonzero in the radial \(z\) direction. The other two coefficients have derivative on the real \(w\)-plane given, up to an invertible change of real basis, by \(w'\mapsto zw'-R\overline{w'}\). Its determinant is \(|z|^2-R^2=\varepsilon-R^2\ne0\). Therefore the local derivative is onto the Euclidean self-dual plane, which is wedge-positive, and the positive scalar \(T^{-1}\) of (2.5) preserves this property.

## Fixed marking and the first-zero step

In one compactifying chart \(y\) positively oriented on the standard \(S^4\), write \(x=y/|y|^2\). The numerical \(x\)-orientation is opposite to the inherited orientation on \(X=S^4\setminus\{p\}\). One may choose the two usual stereographic charts with a fixed reflection in one so that \(x\) extends globally and has that orientation. Pull Taubes's endpoint pair back from its positive complex coordinates by a fixed orthogonal identification from the \(x\) coordinates that reverses the numerical orientation. This map preserves the **geometric** orientation, so the common constant coefficient \(\beta\) is self-dual for the inherited orientation in \(x\). The two forms are positive in that same orientation wherever nonzero. Their leading coefficient \(\eta\) in the \(y\)-expansion is the constant coefficient tensor of \(\beta\), numerically anti-self-dual in the ordinary orientation. The coefficient is nonzero and the two endpoints have exactly the same \(\beta\) on the same exterior.

Apply E04 to both endpoints and choose smooth compatible compact metrics \(g_0,g_1\) agreeing with \(\delta_y\) on a single neighbourhood of \(p\). The constant Euclidean form at the first endpoint and the form with one circle at the second are the A1 pole forms for the fixed \(\eta\), by A1 uniqueness. The affine metric path \(g_t=(1-t)g_0+tg_1\) is smooth and positive definite for \(t\in[0,1]\), and the exact metric germ, normal chart, inversion, chirality and source tensor remain fixed. If the volume-normalized version of B02 is used, choose the fixed reference density to be Euclidean near \(p\), so the normalization also preserves the germ.

B02 gives joint smoothness of the A1 pole forms in the parameter and in spatial coordinates on every compact subset of \(X\). Its annular estimate with \(\delta=-1/2\) gives \(\omega_t=\beta+O(r^{-3/2})\) on a parameter neighbourhood about every \(t\). A finite subcover of \([0,1]\) makes the constant and initial radius uniform. Since \(\beta\ne0\), one fixed compact set \(K\subset X\) contains every zero. The zero locus in \([0,1]\times K\) is closed and compact, so its nonempty parameter projection has a minimum \(t_*>0\), using zero-freeness at the first endpoint.

Near a zero at \(t_*\), a smooth local frame of the varying self-dual bundle turns the family into a map \(F(t,q)\) with three real components. The spatial derivative at a zero represents the intrinsic derivative, as all terms from differentiating the frame are multiplied by zero coefficients there. If the spatial derivative were onto, one could choose three spatial coordinates with an invertible \(3\times3\) minor and hold the fourth fixed at the zero. The implicit function theorem would solve those three coordinates as smooth functions of \(t\), giving zeros for \(t<t_*\) arbitrarily close to \(t_*\). This remains valid when \(t_*=1\) by extending the affine metric path slightly past its endpoint. Compactness of \(S^4\) gives a uniform positive-definiteness margin, and the extended path still has the same flat germ. The resulting contradiction proves a nontransverse zero for a pole form with the fixed marking. The argument makes no assertion about the number, exact rank, or geometry of the first zeros, or the existence of zero-free poles in other directions of the resulting class.
