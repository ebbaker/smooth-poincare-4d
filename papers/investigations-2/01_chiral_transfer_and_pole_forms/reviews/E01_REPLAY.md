**Import.** The relevant case has a smooth boundaryless manifold with a cylindrical end, equal-rank smooth bundles, a square elliptic order-one operator \(A\), and a smooth elliptic comparison operator \(A_\infty\) on the same bundles with translation-invariant coefficients on the end. For \(p_{\mathrm{Sob}}=2\) and target order zero, (6.5) requires uniform convergence of the coefficients in the cylindrical frames. With the factor \(e^{\sigma z}\) in the norms, Theorem 6.2 gives Fredholmness for the order-one to order-zero map precisely off the imaginary parts of pencil eigenvalues in (1.10)–(1.11). On each interval missing these weights, the index and kernel are constant by Lemma 7.1. Generalized pencil solutions can have polynomial factors in \(z\), and each eigenvalue arises from an ordinary pencil eigenvector.

In this application \(A=rD\), \(z=\log(r/R_0)\) and \(S^3\) is the compact cross-section. The Euclidean density \(r^{-4}d^4x\) becomes the cylinder density, and \(\sigma=-\delta\). Multiplication by \(r\) gives the target weight shift. A global \(A_\infty\) is obtained by using \(A\) on the compact region, \(rD_0\) for large \(r\), and a cutoff interpolation on a distant annulus in the common end frames. The limit symbol is uniformly invertible on the unit cylindrical cotangent sphere. The \(O(r^{-2})\) coefficient convergence makes the interpolating principal symbols uniformly close to it on the annulus, so the continuation is elliptic. This gives the global comparison operator presumed in §6 of the source.

## Independent checks

| End and ellipticity | Differentiation of inversion gives \(r^{-2}R_x\) and cancels the blow-up factors in the metric. The zeroth and first normal-coordinate conditions give \(h-\mathrm{Id}=O_\infty(r^{-2})\). The orientation of the inverted chart is opposite to that of the original chart. For \(D=(d_h^*,\sqrt2 P_h^-d)\), \(D^*D=\Delta_h\) on one-forms because \(d_h^**_hd=0\). On anti-self-dual \(\beta\), \(d_h^*\beta=*_h d\beta\) and \(d_h^*d\beta=-*_h dd_h^*\beta\), which yields \(DD^*=\Delta_h\) on the target. These identities imply square ellipticity. |
| Exceptional weights | The flat identities reduce the coefficients of a homogeneous kernel element \(r^qv(\theta)\) to scalar harmonic functions. The polar Laplacian yields \(q(q+2)=\ell(\ell+2)\), so the only candidates in the given interval are \(0,-2,-3\), all real. At \(-2\), \(r^{-2}v\) has a constant coefficient vector and divergence proportional to \(r^{-4}x\cdot v\), so vanishes in the kernel only for \(v=0\). At \(0\) constants work, and at \(-3\) the gradient of \(r^{-2}\) works. If a generalized mode has powers of \(z\), its highest coefficient is an ordinary eigenvector and adds no exceptional weight. |
| Annular regularity | On fixed annuli, Cartesian rescaling of coefficients gives a first-order operator \(R P\) with uniformly bounded coefficients in every \(C^j\) and a uniformly invertible principal symbol. The interior estimate gains one derivative, and the source term after rescaling is \(O(R^{\gamma+1})\) when \(Pu=O_\infty(r^\gamma)\). Uniformity also follows by absorbing the small \(C^j\) error in the constant-coefficient Fourier Sobolev estimate and using nested cutoffs. A weighted \(L^2_\delta\) bound on an annulus gives the needed rescaled \(L^2\) norm \(O(R^\delta)\). |
| Weighted inverse | At \(\delta=-3/2\), the Stokes boundary term has exponent \(3-3/2-5/2=-1\). Thus a kernel element is closed, exact because \(H^1(X;\mathbb R)=0\), and its primitive is harmonic with a single finite limit since both the radial tail and sphere oscillation are \(O(r^{-1/2})\). The maximum principle kills it. The \(h\)-pairing dual of \(L^2_{\delta-1}\) is \(L^2_{-3-\delta}\). The formal adjoint equation first kills the decaying harmonic scalar, then leaves a closed anti-self-dual two-form. Its compact-metric norm is \(O(\rho^{-5/2})\). Removability and \(H^2(M;\mathbb R)=0\) kill it. This gives index zero and trivial kernel at the midpoint and hence an inverse throughout \((-3,0)\). |
| Higher Sobolev order | For \(P=D\), write \(u_R(z)=u(Rz)\) on a fixed annulus and \(f_R(z)=f(Rz)\). The rescaled order-one equation has right-hand side \(Rf_R\). The interior estimate bounds \(R^{-\delta}\|u_R\|_{H^{k+1}}\) by the sum of \(R^{1-\delta}\|f_R\|_{H^k}\) and \(R^{-\delta}\|u_R\|_{L^2}\) on an enlarged annulus. These are the local weights of \(W^{k,2}_{\delta-1}\) and \(L^2_\delta\) respectively. Summing squares on dyadic annuli of finite overlap and using compact interior estimates gives the proposed higher-order estimate. The order-one inverse bounds the \(L^2_\delta\) term and gives uniqueness. |
| Endpoint estimate | With the positive scalar Laplacian the flux normalization is \(\Gamma=(4\pi^2|x|^2)^{-1}\). For either of the flat operators, \(P_0P_0^*\Gamma\) is the identity times the Dirac distribution. Convolution with \(P_0^*\Gamma=O(|x|^{-3})\) and data of order \(-k\) for \(k=2,3\) has order \(1-k\). The inner, comparable and outer radii contribute, respectively, \(r^{-3}O(r^{4-k})\), \(r^{-k}O(r)\), and \(O(\int_{2r}^\infty s^{-k}ds)\). All give \(O(r^{1-k})\) with no logarithm. The kernel is locally integrable and absolute tail convergence permits the distributional identity. The bounded homogeneous residual is an entire coefficientwise harmonic function and hence a constant. |
| Existence | The source \(P_h^-dA^0=O_\infty(r^{-2})\) is in \(L^2_{\delta-1}\) for \(-1<\delta<0\). The weighted solve and annular estimate give \(a=O_\infty(r^\delta)\). The error \((D_0-D)a\) is \(O_\infty(r^{\delta-3})\). Cutting off in Cartesian frames and applying the flat endpoint estimate with \(k=2\) gives \(a=O(r^{-1})\), since both the cutoff solution and convolution tend to zero. Rescaling upgrades the bound to \(O_\infty(r^{-1})\). Thus \(da=O_\infty(r^{-2})\). |
| Exhaustion | A form of compact-metric order at most four is bounded in the end metric. Chiral closedness makes it a solution of \(Q=d+d_h^*\), so its derivatives have the bounded-end estimates. Hence \(Q_0\omega=O_\infty(r^{-3})\). The cutoff argument with \(k=3\) yields a constant residual and \(O(r^{-2})\) difference. Taking the limit in the chiral projection makes the residual geometrically self-dual. Applying the annular estimate to the difference gives the differentiated remainder. |
| Removal and uniqueness | In the compact chart the cutoff error in the exterior derivative at exponent \(b\) is \(O(\rho^{3-b})\). It tends to zero for both \(b=5/2\) in the adjoint problem and \(b=2\) in the difference of two pole forms with the same leading term. Conformal invariance of the star retains chirality. Thus closure gives coclosedness as a distribution on the compact manifold. Elliptic regularity gives smoothness, and exactness and Stokes give zero squared norm. |
| Sharpness and scale | In the product test metric on the radial ray the reversed-orientation star gives \((*_h-1)(dx^{12}-dx^{34})=(b-1)dx^{12}+(1-b^{-1})dx^{34}\), where \(b=\sin\rho/\rho\). For a fixed form its constant numerical coefficients scale by \(e^{2\phi(p)}\); the two dual coframe factors supply another \(e^{2\phi(p)}\) as tensors at \(p\). The growth condition and chirality of the space itself are conformally invariant. |

For an additional check of the two endpoint spaces, degree \(-3\) has coefficient vector \(r^{-4}Bx\) for a constant matrix \(B\). Its divergence condition forces the symmetric part of \(B\) to be scalar. The resulting radial solution is a multiple of \(d(r^{-2})\). For the skew part \(K\), the primitive \(\alpha=(Kx)\cdot dx\) pulls back under inversion to \(r^{-4}\alpha\), so \(d(r^{-4}\alpha)\) has the opposite flat chirality from \(d\alpha\). The required chirality then imposes a three-dimensional linear condition on the skew part, giving three skew directions and one radial direction. The degree-zero space has dimension four from the constant one-forms. The endpoint enumeration uses Cartesian coefficient degree, as in the weighted spaces of E01.
