# Moving the pole and the conformal class

Inputs are the second master conventions, [B01](B01_KERNEL.md) and the weighted isomorphism in [E01](../../notes/E01_A1.md), at the working tree based on 6a422ce. **Project theorem.** Give smooth conformal classes on the fixed compact oriented smooth homotopy sphere the $C^\infty$ topology, using the unique representative of any fixed positive volume density. Over $(c,p)$ take the three-dimensional bundle of B01 source tensors (equivalently its projective lines for a zero test). The triples $(c,p,\eta)$ with a nowhere-zero pole form on $M\setminus p$ constitute an open set in the nonzero coefficient bundle. In particular the set of classes admitting some such $p,\eta$ is open. Pole forms depend continuously with all spatial derivatives on compact sets away from a moving pole. Along any smooth finite-dimensional family of classes, poles and coefficients they depend smoothly in moving coordinates, locally in every spatial derivative. No fixed energy hypothesis enters this statement.

**Decision.** Use smooth convergence, for which geodesic charts and all coefficients vary continuously with their derivatives. A bound solely on the pointwise distance between Hodge stars would not by itself give the estimates used below. Openness for the stronger topology is sufficient for making smooth variations; no claim about the weakest possible topology is needed.

**Project theorem (fixed end for comparison).** Choose a coordinate neighborhood of $p_0$, and a smooth oriented orthonormal frame at each nearby marked point (Gram–Schmidt for each varying representative). For each nearby datum $t$ let $F_t$ agree close to $p_0$ with the composition of the reference normal coordinates and the new framed exponential at $p_t$. A uniform small normal ball exists by the geodesic ODE and the inverse function theorem. Extend this germ to a diffeomorphism of $M$ by cutting off its coordinate displacement from the identity inside the original chart. Its derivative is uniformly close to the identity there; extending that local formula by identity to coordinate Euclidean space gives a bijection by the contraction estimate for its displacement. The cutoffs are fixed. Pull back to $X_0=M\setminus p_0$ and use the same inversion of the reference normal variable for all $t$. In this trivialization the new representative has normal components $\delta_{ij}+O(|y|^2)$ with all required differentiated estimates uniform, and their coefficients converge with the metrics. The end blow-up multiplier is the same function $|y|^{-4}$ extended positively in the interior. Thus $g'_t$ have the same Euclidean model and their end remainders converge in every finite order with the $O(r^{-2})$ symbol bounds. This follows directly from Taylor's integral formula at $y=0$ and the differentiated inversion. The corresponding statements hold with parameter derivatives along smooth finite-dimensional families.

**Project theorem (weighted inverse).** Fix $\delta=-1/2$. Use the coefficient weighted Sobolev convention of E01 and an integer $k\ge4$. The reference map $D_0:H^{k+1}_\delta\to H^k_{\delta-1}$ is an isomorphism by E01 (constant rescaling of its two-form row is immaterial). Use the projection between the nearby target anti-self-dual bundle and the reference target; its inverse exists since the projectors are uniformly close. The weighted domain of covariant one-forms is fixed. On a dyadic annulus rescale coordinates and coefficients to a unit annulus as in E01. Closeness of the coefficients with their first $k+1$ derivatives bounds the difference $D_t-D_0$ in the displayed operator norm, uniformly over annuli, and the finite interior charts give the same conclusion. In a sufficiently small neighborhood the inverse is $D_0^{-1}(1+(D_t-D_0)D_0^{-1})^{-1}$ by the convergent geometric series. This proves norm continuity, and termwise differentiation or the inverse identity gives smooth parameter dependence for a smooth family.

Let $A^0_t$ be a fixed cutoff times $\frac12\iota_x\eta_t$ in the common end coordinates. Solve $D_t a_t=(0,-d^-_t A^0_t)$, with matching scalar factor if the two-form row is rescaled. The constant end form $dA^0_t$ is self-dual for the limiting metric with the manifold orientation; its wrong projection decays as $O(r^{-2})$. The error and its derivatives vary continuously with the same bounds; hence the right side belongs continuously to $H^k_{\delta-1}$ (the strict margin is $1/2$). The corrected form is $d(A_t^0+a_t)$; the gauge equation applies only to $a_t$. This is the A1 solution by uniqueness. The correction varies continuously in $H^{k+1}_\delta$. After scaling to a unit annulus the usual Sobolev sup bound gives a pointwise bound on $da_t$ of $C r^{-3/2}$ uniformly for nearby data. For example the compactly supported unit-chart estimate follows by Fourier inversion and Cauchy–Schwarz against $(1+|\xi|^2)^{-k/2}$, square integrable in dimension four for $k>2$. Finite overlaps and fixed cutoffs give the annular estimate. The same argument with larger $k$ gives any fixed number of spatial derivatives on compact sets. Increasing $k$ gives the claimed smooth dependence along smooth parameter families; estimates may use a smaller local parameter neighborhood for each finite order. This proof only requires the weaker uniform $O(r^{-3/2})$ bound. The sharp $O(r^{-2})$ for each fixed datum is separately supplied by E01.

**Project theorem (openness).** If the base pole form is zero-free then its coefficient is nonzero. Shrink the parameter neighborhood so the Euclidean norm of the coefficient stays above half its initial norm. Choose a single radius so the uniform correction on the exterior is smaller than half that lower bound. On the compact core the reference form has a positive minimum and the pulled-back forms converge in $C^0$. They remain nonzero on both regions. Projection of an open subset of the locally trivial coefficient-and-point parameter space onto conformal classes is open. This proves the claim also with the point or coefficient line held in a chosen local trivialization. It gives no new conformal class with a zero-free pole on an unknown smooth sphere.
