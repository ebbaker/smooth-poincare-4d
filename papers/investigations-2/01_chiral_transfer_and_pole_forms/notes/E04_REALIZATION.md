## Fixed-end criterion

*Project theorem, working.* Let \(M\) be a smooth closed oriented four-manifold, \(p\in M\), and \(X=M\setminus\{p\}\). Fix a smooth oriented chart \(y\) at \(p\) with \(y(p)=0\). On its punctured domain set \(x=I(y)=y/|y|^2\) using the numerical Euclidean norm. Give the \(x\)-end the orientation inherited from \(X\), which is opposite to the numerical coordinate orientation because \(I\) reverses orientation. Use \(\delta\) for the numerical Euclidean metric, with the inherited orientation for its star. Suppose \(\omega\) is a smooth closed two-form, positive where nonzero in the sense \(\omega\wedge\omega>0\), and at every zero \(z\) its intrinsic derivative has a three-dimensional wedge-positive image in \(\Lambda^2T_z^*X\). The everywhere positive symplectic case is included. Suppose \(\omega=\beta\) for all sufficiently large \(|x|\), where \(\beta\) is a nonzero positive constant two-form in \(x\).

There exists a smooth oriented Riemannian conformal class on \(M\) making this same \(\omega\) self-dual on \(X\) if and only if \(*_{\delta}\beta=\beta\) in the inherited \(x\)-orientation. In that case a representative can be chosen equal to \(\delta_y\) near \(p\), and the associated complete end representative on \(X\) can be chosen equal to \(\delta_x\) near infinity. The compactified value of the compatible conformal class at \(p\) is necessarily \([\delta_y]\).

### Necessity

Write \(y=\rho\nu\) with \(|\nu|=1\). With \(R_\nu=\mathrm{Id}-2\nu\otimes\nu\), the end formula in the \(y\)-chart is \(\omega(y)=\rho^{-4}R_\nu^*\beta\). If the compatible conformal class extends even continuously with positive definite value at \(p\), then \(R_\nu^*\beta\in P=\Lambda^+_{c(p)}\) for every \(\nu\), by taking the limit on each ray. The three-plane \(P\) is positive for the wedge pairing in the \(y\)-orientation.

In a Euclidean orthonormal basis positively oriented for the **inherited** \(x\)-orientation, write the skew normal form \(\beta=a e^{12}+b e^{34}\), where \(a,b>0\) and \(e^{ij}=e^i\wedge e^j\). Reflections in the hyperplanes normal to \((e_1+e_3)/\sqrt2\) and \((e_2+e_4)/\sqrt2\) give respectively
\[
 a e^{23}-b e^{14},\qquad b e^{23}-a e^{14}.
\]
If \(a\ne b\), those vectors span \(e^{23},e^{14}\), each of square zero for the wedge pairing. This contradicts positivity of \(P\), so \(a=b\), which is Euclidean self-duality in the inherited orientation. When \(a=b\), use also the reflections normal to \(e_1\) and \((e_1+e_4)/\sqrt2\). Together with the first reflection above they give the three independent vectors proportional to \(-e^{12}+e^{34}\), \(e^{23}-e^{14}\), and \(e^{24}+e^{13}\). All are Euclidean self-dual in the \(y\)-orientation because each reflection exchanges the two orientations. Therefore \(P=\Lambda^+_{\delta_y}\), which fixes the conformal class at \(p\).

### Sufficiency with relative interpolation

For any background Riemannian metric \(g_0\), write \(\Lambda^2=E_+\oplus E_-\) for its star eigenspaces. A maximal wedge-positive three-plane is the graph of a unique map \(B:E_+\to E_-\) with \(\|B\|<1\), because projection to \(E_+\) is injective and the wedge form in this splitting is the difference of squared norms. To contain \(\omega=\omega_++\omega_-\) is the affine constraint \(B\omega_+=\omega_-\). Both the strict norm bound and the constraint are preserved under convex combinations. Smooth positive three-plane fields determine smooth conformal classes. This is the fibrewise correspondence and convexity used by Auroux, Donaldson and Katzarkov in the proof of Proposition 1 and by Perutz in Proposition 1.1.4. [Auroux–Donaldson–Katzarkov, §7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0410332) · [Perutz, §1.1](https://web.ma.utexas.edu/users/perutz/PDF_files/PerutzThesis.pdf)

Construct local planes containing \(\omega\). At a nonzero point the rank-one formula \(Bv=\langle v,\omega_+\rangle\omega_-/|\omega_+|^2\) is smooth and has norm less than one by positivity. At a zero \(z\), extend the positive image of \(D\omega(z)\) to a smooth positive plane field on a neighbourhood of \(z\), and use its splitting for this local calculation. In local frames of the splitting write the two parts of the form as \(a(t,u)\) and \(b(t,u)\) in \(\mathbb R^3\). The derivative of \(a\) is onto and \(Db(z)=0\). By the submersion theorem use \((t,u)\) as local coordinates with \(a=u\). If \(u=0\), positivity or vanishing of \(\omega\) forces \(b(t,0)=0\), since a nonzero negative-plane vector has negative wedge square. Then
\[
 b(t,u)=B(t,u)u,\qquad B(t,u)=\int_0^1\partial_u b(t,su)\,ds.
\]
At \(z\), \(B=0\). Shrink the neighbourhood so that the graph remains positive. This also gives the local zero curve \(u=0\). The zero set is compact because the end form is symplectic. Thus a nonempty zero set is a finite disjoint union of smooth embedded circles.

Use the Euclidean positive plane on the end, where it contains \(\beta\), and extend the background metric to all of \(X\) with this end value. Express every local plane from above as a graph in the splitting of that one background metric. Choose a smooth partition of unity subordinate to the interior neighbourhoods and the end, with the end term equal to one on a smaller end. Averaging the graph maps there and on the core produces a smooth compatible conformal class with the prescribed Euclidean end. The same construction agrees with any smooth compatible class prescribed on a neighbourhood of a closed region, after choosing the partition to equal one on a smaller neighbourhood within the prescription domain. Select a representative \(h\) on \(X\) with \(h=\delta_x\) near infinity, for example by prescribing the background volume. On the end \(I^*h=\rho^{-4}\delta_y\). Multiplying by a positive function equal to \(\rho^4\) there gives a representative extending as \(\delta_y\) across \(p\). Its star on two-forms agrees with that of \(h\) on \(X\).

### Constant end metrics and linear normalization

In the specified \(x\)-chart a constant coefficient metric \(h\) gives the angular compactified matrix \(R_\nu^{\mathsf T}hR_\nu\) after multiplication by \(\rho^4\). These matrices all have the same trace. Hence a continuous positive conformal limit requires that they all coincide. A reflection in one eigenvector of \(h\) gives \(h\) itself. A reflection interchanging two eigenvectors forces their eigenvalues to agree. Thus the compactifiable constant coefficient end metrics are exactly \(h=\lambda\delta_x\) with \(\lambda>0\). In coordinates \(x'=Lx\) this means \(h'=\lambda(L^{-1})^*\delta_x\), expressed as a constant metric in \(x'\). Choosing Euclidean coefficients in \(x'\) is allowed exactly when \(L\) is a similarity. Also, the compactifying transition \(y'=ILI(y)=|y|^2Ly/|Ly|^2\) is differentiable at \(0\) exactly in that case. It is homogeneous of degree one, so differentiability would make it linear. Composing this linear map with \(L^{-1}\) would then give a linear map preserving every line, hence a scalar, which requires \(|Ly|/|y|\) to be constant. For a compatible positive Euclidean self-dual \(\beta\), a positive similarity suffices to put it in standard symplectic form, by choosing an oriented orthonormal skew frame and scaling its common symplectic coefficient.

## The forms that the result identifies

*Project theorem, working, with the indicated A1 premise.* When \(M\) is a homotopy sphere and the fixed-end criterion holds, the constructed compact metric is Euclidean in \(y\) near \(p\), so \(y\) is a normal chart there. The exact end formula gives \(|\omega|_g=O(\rho^{-4})\). Thus \(\omega\in\mathcal H_p([g])\) by the definition, and A1 identifies it as the unique pole form for its nonzero leading coefficient. This applies to an unchanged input only under the criterion above.

For a general constant positive \(\beta\), choose an orientation-preserving linear map \(L\) such that \(L^*\beta\) is Euclidean self-dual. One may change the **form** in the distant end while keeping the chart and the compactification fixed. Choose a smooth path \(L_t\) in \(GL^+(4,\mathbb R)\) from the identity to \(L\). In the end chart multiply the time-dependent vector field \(\dot L_tL_t^{-1}x\) by a cutoff equal to zero near an arbitrarily prescribed compact core and one past a larger radius. Extend the vector field by zero on the core. It has at most linear growth and its time-one flow \(\Psi\) on \(X\) is a diffeomorphism. For sufficiently large initial radius the whole path \(L_t x\) stays past the outer cutoff because \(\min_{t\in[0,1]}s_{\min}(L_t)>0\). Thus \(\Psi=L\) there. Take the core to include the zeros. Then \(\Psi^*\omega\) agrees on the core, has the same zeros and positivity, and has Euclidean self-dual constant end \(L^*\beta\), so the theorem applies to this modified form on the original \(M\). The intrinsic derivative condition is preserved by pullback.

Conversely, a nonzero pole form is closed and symplectic off its zero set, and its intrinsic derivative at a zero has image in \(\Lambda^+\). It is near-symplectic exactly when it has rank three at every zero, equivalently it is transverse there as a section of \(\Lambda^+\). A1 supplies the nonzero Euclidean self-dual limit \(\omega^0\) and \(\omega=\omega^0+da\) with \(a=O(r^{-1})\), \(da=O(r^{-2})\) in the inverted normal chart. The remainder can be nonzero, as the fixed-normal-chart example in [E01 §4](E01_A1.md#4-optimal-order-and-independence-of-the-representative) shows. A pole form with transverse zeros, or none, can be converted into a positive near-symplectic or symplectic form with an exactly constant end. On a sufficiently far end replace it by \(\omega^0+d(\chi_R a)\), using a radial function equal to one up to \(R\), zero from \(2R\) on, and with \(|d\chi_R|\le C/R\). The error from \(\omega^0\) on the transition annulus is \(O(R^{-2})\), so it is positive there for large \(R\). The construction fixes the core and the zero set. Consequently the exact-form equality asserted broadly in proposal §3.4 is replaced by the fixed-end criterion and these modification statements. The transversality of an arbitrary pole form at its zeros remains a separate condition.
