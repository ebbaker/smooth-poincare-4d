## 1. Statement and the asymptotically Euclidean end

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere, \(c\) a smooth
Riemannian conformal class, \(g\in c\), and \(p\in M\). Use oriented
\(g\)-normal coordinates \(y\) at \(p\), set \(\rho=|y|\), and write
\(x=y/\rho^2\), \(r=|x|\). Let \(O_\infty(r^\gamma)\) mean that the Cartesian
coefficients have derivatives of order \(j\) bounded by \(C_jr^{\gamma-j}\)
on the end. Near \(p\) use the same convention for \(O_\infty(\rho^\gamma)\).

For every \(\eta\in\Lambda_p^-\), there is a unique smooth closed self-dual
two-form \(\omega_\eta\) on \(X=M\setminus\{p\}\) with
\[
\omega_\eta=\rho^{-4}R_y^*\eta+O_\infty(\rho^{-2}).
\]
Here \(\eta\) is identified with its constant coefficients in the oriented
normal chart and \(R_y=\mathrm{Id}-2\hat y\otimes\hat y\).
Every smooth closed self-dual \(\omega\) with \(|\omega|_g=O(\rho^{-4})\)
occurs this way. In \(x\) the expansion is
\(\omega_\eta=\omega^0+da\) on the end, where the constant form \(\omega^0\)
has the coefficients of \(\eta\), \(a=O_\infty(r^{-1})\) and
\(da=O_\infty(r^{-2})\). In particular the pole space has dimension three.

Choose \(h\) globally on \(X\) by multiplying \(g\) by a smooth positive
function equal to \(\rho^{-4}\) near \(p\).
Differentiating the inverse inversion \(y=x/r^2\) gives
\(D_xy=r^{-2}R_x\). In these \(x\) coordinates,
\[
h(x)=R_x^{\mathsf T}g(x/r^2)R_x
   =\mathrm{Id}+O_\infty(r^{-2}).
\]
The normal-coordinate Taylor conditions are \(g(0)=\mathrm{Id}\) and
\(Dg(0)=0\). Since each derivative of the inversion costs one power of
\(r^{-1}\) and \(R_x\) is smooth homogeneous of degree zero, they give the
stated differentiated bounds. This end is complete and is the only end of
\(X\). Its \(x\)-chart reverses the given orientation. Thus Euclidean
self-duality *for the orientation of \(X\)* corresponds to anti-self-duality
for the usual coordinate orientation of \(\mathbb R^4_x\). The middle-degree
Hodge star on \(X\) is the same for \(g\) and \(h\), and
\(|\omega|_h=\rho^4|\omega|_g\) near \(p\).

## 2. Weighted invertibility

Use \(d_h^*\) for the metric formal adjoint of \(d\), \(P_h^-\) for
anti-self-dual projection, and \(\Delta_h=dd_h^*+d_h^*d\). Normalize the
second component of the sketch's operator by a constant:
\[
D=(d_h^*,\sqrt2 P_h^-d),\qquad D^*(f,\beta)=df+\sqrt2 d_h^*\beta .
\]
The identities
\[
D^*D=\Delta_h\big|_{\Lambda^1},\qquad
DD^*=\Delta_h\big|_{\Lambda^0\oplus\Lambda_h^-}
\]
hold for any smooth \(h\) in this dimension. Indeed \(d_h^**_h d=0\) on
one-forms. For an anti-self-dual two-form \(\beta\),
\(d_h^*\beta=*_h d\beta\) and \(d_h^*d\beta=-*_h dd_h^*\beta\), so that
\(2P_h^-dd_h^*\beta=\Delta_h\beta\). The off-diagonal entries vanish by
\(d^2=(d_h^*)^2=0\). In particular, the symbol of \(D\) is invertible at
every real nonzero covector.

Use \(W^{k,2}_\delta\) with squared norm equivalent on the end to
\[
\sum_{j=0}^k\int r^{2(j-\delta)}|\partial^j u|^2\,r^{-4}d^4x,
\qquad L^2_\delta=W^{0,2}_\delta.
\]
On the compact part use the usual Sobolev norm. On the end identify
\(\Lambda_h^-\) with the flat \(\Lambda_0^-\) by the restriction of
\(P_h^-\) to \(\Lambda_0^-\), an isomorphism for \(r\) large. In these
frames the flat operator is \(D_0\), and the first- and zero-order
coefficients of \(D-D_0\) are \(O_\infty(r^{-2})\) and
\(O_\infty(r^{-3})\), respectively. Differentiating the metric, the frames
and the volume gives the same orders for \(D^*-D_0^*\).

**Import (weighted Fredholm and stability).** Robert B. Lockhart and
Robert C. McOwen, *Elliptic differential operators on noncompact
manifolds*, Annali della Scuola Normale Superiore di Pisa, Classe di
Scienze, Série 4, **12** (1985), no. 3, 409–447
(bibliography key proposed: `LockhartMcOwen1985Elliptic`).
The [primary journal scan](https://www.numdam.org/item/ASNSP_1985_4_12_3_409_0/)
was inspected on 27 November 2026 at §1, pp. 414–417, §2, estimate (2.4)
p. 420, §3, parametrix discussion pp. 425–426, §6, pp. 432–434 and §7,
pp. 434–435. For the order-one case of Theorem 6.2, let \(A\) be a smooth
square elliptic system on a smooth boundaryless manifold with one end
\(\Omega\times(0,\infty)\), where \(\Omega\) is compact and smooth.
In product end frames suppose the coefficients converge uniformly to
those of a translation-invariant elliptic system \(A_\infty\), with their
derivatives through order \(s\) for a target of Sobolev order \(s\), as in
(6.5). For \(1<p_{\mathrm{Sob}}<\infty\), the map of Sobolev orders
\(s+1\) to \(s\), both with exponential weight \(e^{\sigma z}\) in the
norm, is Fredholm exactly off the weights \(\sigma=\operatorname{Im}\lambda\)
for which there is a nonzero solution \(e^{i\lambda z}v(\theta)\) of the
model homogeneous equation. Theorem 6.2 also gives index constancy in a
root-free interval, and Lemma 7.1 gives constancy of the kernel and
adjoint kernel there. This uses their determinant definition of
ellipticity in §1, which applies here since the determinant has degree
four and is nonzero away from zero.

Apply the import with \(z=\log(r/R_0)\) and \(A=rD\), using a positive
smooth extension of \(r\) on the compact part. For the global comparison
operator take \(A_\infty=A+\chi(r)(rD_0-A)\), where \(\chi\) is zero
before a large annulus and one past it. The two principal symbols are
uniformly close on unit cylindrical covectors in the annulus, and the
flat one is uniformly invertible there. Choosing the annulus far out
makes the interpolation elliptic. Take \(R_0\) beyond this annulus, so
\(A_\infty=rD_0\) on the selected cylindrical end. In \((\theta,z)\)
and Cartesian coefficient
frames, \(rD_0\) is translation invariant and \(rD-rD_0\) has coefficients
and all their cylindrical derivatives \(O(r^{-2})\).
The end measure \(r^{-4}d^4x\) is \(dz\,d\theta\), the cylindrical weight
is \(\sigma=-\delta\), and multiplication by \(r\) identifies the target
\(L^2_{\delta-1}\) of \(D\) with the target \(L^2_\delta\) of \(rD\).
The indicial exponents \(q=i\lambda\) are those of
\(D_0(r^qv(\theta))=0\), with the exceptional Euclidean weights
\(\operatorname{Re}q=-\operatorname{Im}\lambda\).

Each Cartesian component of a homogeneous flat kernel element is harmonic
by \(D_0^*D_0=\Delta_0\). Separation on \(S^3\) gives
\(q(q+2)=\ell(\ell+2)\), \(\ell=0,1,\ldots\). The spherical spectrum here
follows by restricting homogeneous harmonic polynomials: their
eigenvalues follow from the polar Laplacian and their restrictions span a
dense subspace by harmonic decomposition of polynomials.
In \([-3,0]\) the sole candidates are \(-3,-2,0\). At \(-2\) the
one-form has constant coefficient vector times \(r^{-2}\), and its
codifferential is \(2r^{-4}\sum_j v_jx_j\), which vanishes only if all
\(v_j\) vanish. The mode \(d(r^{-2})\) realizes \(-3\), and constant
one-forms realize \(0\). The only exceptional weights in the requested
interval are therefore \(-3,0\).

For the kernel and cokernel calculation we use the local elliptic
parametrix for a smooth elliptic system: a distributional solution with
smooth right-hand side is smooth. Its interior estimate on nested
relatively compact subdomains bounds \(H^{m+1}\) by the \(H^m\) norm of
the right-hand side and the \(L^2\) norm of the solution on the larger
subdomain. These are the regularity and local estimates used in the
Lockhart–McOwen parametrix arguments at (2.4) and pp. 425–426. On fixed
rescaled annuli \(x=Rz\), for \(D,D^*\), and \(Q=d+d_h^*\) on all forms,
the operator \(R P\) in coefficients converges smoothly to its elliptic
flat model, so these interior bounds are uniform for \(R\gg1\). In
particular a homogeneous solution \(u\in L^2_\delta\) is
\(O_\infty(r^\delta)\). To see the scaling, its \(L^2(dz)\) norm on the
larger annulus is bounded by a constant times \(R^\delta\|u\|_{L^2_\delta}\).
Iterated interior estimates followed by Sobolev embedding give
\(\sup R^j|\partial^j u|\le C_j R^\delta\) on the smaller annulus.
With a right-hand side \(P u=O_\infty(r^\gamma)\) the same bounds have
the additional term \(C_j R^{\gamma+1}\).

At \(\delta=-3/2\), \(Da=0\) implies \(a=O_\infty(r^{-3/2})\) and
\(da\) is self-dual. If \(X_R\) is \(X\) cut off at radius \(R\),
\[
\int_{X_R}|da|_h^2\,dV_h
 =\int_{X_R}da\wedge da=\int_{\partial X_R}a\wedge da=O(R^{-1}).
\]
Hence \(da=0\). Since \(H^1(X;\mathbb R)=0\), write \(a=dF\).
This vanishing follows from the relative cohomology sequence of
\((M,X)\) and excision at \(p\). Then \(\Delta_hF=0\). On the end
\(dF=O(r^{-3/2})\) gives uniform convergence of \(F\) along rays and
angular oscillation \(O(r^{-1/2})\) by integrating along spheres.
Thus all rays have the same limit \(C\). The maximum principle on
\(X_R\) applied to \(F-C\) and its negative gives \(a=0\).

By the \(h\)-volume pairing, the dual of \(L^2_{\delta-1}\) is
\(L^2_{-3-\delta}\), as is seen by multiplying the weights of the two
norms in the integrals. A cokernel representative therefore satisfies
\(D^*(f,\beta)=0\) distributionally with \((f,\beta)\in L^2_{-3/2}\) at
the midpoint. It is smooth and \(O_\infty(r^{-3/2})\). Applying \(d_h^*\)
gives \(\Delta_h f=0\), and the maximum principle with \(f\to0\) gives
\(f=0\). Since \(*_h\beta=-\beta\), \(d_h^*\beta=0\) implies \(d\beta=0\).
In the compact metric \(g\), \(|\beta|_g=O(\rho^{-5/2})\).

A closed \(g\)-chiral two-form on \(X\) with compact norm
\(O(\rho^{-b})\), \(b<3\), extends as a smooth closed \(g\)-chiral form on
\(M\). It is locally \(L^1\). Test its exterior derivative after
multiplying by a cutoff vanishing for \(\rho<\epsilon\), equal to one for
\(\rho>2\epsilon\), and having derivative \(O(\epsilon^{-1})\). The
error is \(O(\epsilon^{3-b})\), which tends to zero. The algebraic
chirality then gives \(d_g^*\beta=0\) distributionally, and elliptic
regularity for \(d+d_g^*\) gives smoothness. Every such form vanishes on
\(M\): \(H^2(M;\mathbb R)=0\) makes the form exact, so Stokes and
\(\beta\wedge\beta=\pm|\beta|_g^2dV_g\) give zero \(L^2\) norm.
This argument applies to the above \(\beta\) with \(b=5/2\).

Thus \(D:W^{1,2}_\delta\to L^2_{\delta-1}\) is an isomorphism at
\(\delta=-3/2\) by Fredholmness. Lemma 7.1 applied between the midpoint
and any other weight in the same root-free interval transfers zero
kernel and index zero. This proves invertibility throughout
\((-3,0)\).

For each integer \(k\ge0\), the realization
\(D:W^{k+1,2}_\delta\to W^{k,2}_{\delta-1}\) is also an isomorphism.
Apply the uniform rescaled interior estimates to the \(W^{1,2}_\delta\)
solution and sum their weighted squares over finite-overlap dyadic
annuli, using ordinary interior estimates on the compact part. This
bounds the \(W^{k+1,2}_\delta\) norm by the \(W^{k,2}_{\delta-1}\) norm
of the data and the \(L^2_\delta\) norm of the solution. The latter is
controlled by the inverse at \(k=0\), which also gives uniqueness.

## 3. The endpoint estimate and exhaustion

The elementary flat estimate used twice below is as follows. Let \(P_0\)
be \(D_0\) or \(d+d_0^*\), the latter on all degrees. Their products with
their adjoints in either order are the coefficientwise positive
Laplacian. Its fundamental solution in four dimensions is
\(\Gamma(x)=(4\pi^2|x|^2)^{-1}\), with the normalization obtained by flux
over a small sphere. Set \(E=P_0^*\Gamma\) as a matrix kernel. Then
\(P_0E=\delta_0\mathrm{Id}\), \(|E(x)|\le C|x|^{-3}\).
If \(F\) is smooth on \(\mathbb R^4\) with \(F=O(r^{-k})\), \(k=2\)
or \(k=3\), the convolution \(E*F\) converges absolutely and is
\(O(r^{1-k})\). On \(|z|\le r/2\) the integral is bounded by
\(C r^{-3}\int_{|z|\le r/2}(1+|z|)^{-k}dz=O(r^{1-k})\).
On \(r/2<|z|\le 2r\), use
\(C r^{-k}\int_{|x-z|\le 3r}|x-z|^{-3}dz=O(r^{1-k})\).
On \(|z|>2r\), integrate \(C|z|^{-3-k}\). In the distributional identity
\(P_0(E*F)=F\), Fubini against compactly supported tests is justified by
the local integrability and the same tail bound. If \(P_0 u=F\) on all
of \(\mathbb R^4\) and \(u\) is bounded, then \(u-E*F\) is entire
coefficientwise harmonic and bounded, hence constant. It is zero if
\(u\to0\).

For existence, choose a constant \(\omega^0\) that is Euclidean
self-dual for the orientation of \(X\). Set
\(A^0=\tfrac12\iota_{\sum x^j\partial_{x^j}}\omega^0\) on the end, so
\(dA^0=\omega^0\), and multiply by a cutoff to extend \(A^0\) smoothly
by zero on the compact part. The conformal end projection gives
\(P_h^-dA^0=O_\infty(r^{-2})\). For any \(\delta\in(-1,0)\) solve
\[
Da=(0,-\sqrt 2 P_h^-dA^0)
\]
in \(W^{1,2}_\delta\). The right-hand side is in \(L^2_{\delta-1}\)
because \(-2<\delta-1\). Regularity and the annular estimates first give
\(a=O_\infty(r^\delta)\). In the flat end frames \(D_0 a=O_\infty(r^{-2})\),
since the difference of first-order operators on this \(a\) is
\(O_\infty(r^{\delta-3})\). Extend \(a\) with another cutoff to all of
\(\mathbb R^4\) and apply the flat estimate with \(k=2\). The cutoff
section tends to zero, so \(a=O(r^{-1})\). The annular estimates for
\(D_0\) give \(a=O_\infty(r^{-1})\). Therefore
\(\omega=d(A^0+a)\) is closed and \(h\)-self-dual, with the asserted
end primitive and \(O_\infty(r^{-2})\) remainder. The weighted solve on
\((-1,0)\) alone gives only \(da=O_\infty(r^{\delta-1})\). The flat
estimate supplies the endpoint in the same solving weight.

For exhaustion, any \(\omega\) in the statement is bounded in the
Euclidean end frames by the norm comparison of §1. Since \(d\omega=0\)
and \(*_h\omega=\omega\), \(Q\omega=(d+d_h^*)\omega=0\). The annular
estimates give \(\omega=O_\infty(1)\), so
\(Q_0\omega=(d_0^*-d_h^*)\omega=O_\infty(r^{-3})\). After a cutoff
extend this section to \(\mathbb R^4\) and apply the flat estimate with
\(k=3\). It follows that \(\omega=\omega^0+O(r^{-2})\) for a constant
two-form \(\omega^0\). Since \(P_h^-\omega=0\) and
\(P_h^-\to P_0^-\), \(P_0^-\omega^0=0\). The annular estimates for \(Q_0\)
applied to \(\omega-\omega^0\) upgrade the remainder to
\(O_\infty(r^{-2})\). Subtract the solution constructed with this
\(\omega^0\). The difference has compact norm \(O(\rho^{-2})\);
the removability argument of §2 with \(b=2\) makes it zero. This gives
uniqueness for every leading coefficient and exhausts the space.
Uniqueness and linearity of the defining equations make the
correspondence a linear isomorphism.

Finally \(D_yx=\rho^{-2}R_y\), so the pullback of the constant \(\omega^0\)
has coefficients \(\rho^{-4}R_y^*\eta\) as in §1, and pulling back an
\(O_\infty(r^{-2})\) two-form yields \(O_\infty(\rho^{-2})\).

## 4. Optimal order and independence of the representative

The remainder can attain order \(r^{-2}\) in the stipulated normal
coordinates. On \(S^4\) take a metric equal near \(p\) to a product of
a unit-curvature spherical surface and a flat plane, extended by
interpolating with any smooth metric away from \(p\).
In product normal coordinates, on the ray \(y=(\rho,0,0,0)\), the
surface radial metric \(d\rho^2+\sin^2(\rho)d\theta^2\) gives
\(g=\mathrm{diag}(1,b^2,1,1)\), \(b=\sin(\rho)/\rho\).
The same diagonal matrix is \(h\) on the inverted ray.
For \(\omega^0=dx^1\wedge dx^2-dx^3\wedge dx^4\), the reversed
orientation gives on that ray
\[
(*_h-\mathrm{Id})\omega^0=(b-1)dx^1\wedge dx^2
        +(1-b^{-1})dx^3\wedge dx^4
 =-\tfrac{1}{6}r^{-2}(dx^1\wedge dx^2+dx^3\wedge dx^4)+O(r^{-4}).
\]
If its pole form differed from \(\omega^0\) by \(o(r^{-2})\) there,
boundedness of \(*_h-\mathrm{Id}\) and self-duality would contradict
this expansion.

Self-duality and the growth condition on \(M\setminus\{p\}\) depend only
on \(c\): if \(g_1=e^{2\phi}g\), the two-form norm scales by
\(e^{-2\phi}\), the star stays fixed and the two distance functions at
\(p\) are comparable. For leading coefficients viewed as tensors at
\(p\), choose the \(g_1\)-orthonormal frame to be the \(g\)-orthonormal
frame scaled by \(e^{-\phi(p)}\). Then
\(y_1=e^{\phi(p)}y+O(\rho^2)\),
\(x_1=e^{-\phi(p)}x+O(1)\) and
\(D_xx_1=e^{-\phi(p)}\mathrm{Id}+O(r^{-1})\).
Thus the constant leading coefficients of a fixed form, expressed in
the new numerical chart, are multiplied by \(e^{2\phi(p)}\). The
dual frame at \(p\) adds another \(e^{2\phi(p)}\) for a two-form,
so the leading tensor transforms as
\(\eta_{g_1}=e^{4\phi(p)}\eta_g\). Rotating the oriented orthonormal
frame merely rotates its components. This comparison is for fixed
\(\omega\); the kernel normalization is a separate B01 decision.
