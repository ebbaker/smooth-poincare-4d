## Definition and standard comparator

**Import.** Let \(X\) be a compact smooth oriented four-manifold and \(L\) a
framed oriented link in \(\partial X\). A filling has finitely many disjoint
interior input four-balls, a properly embedded framed oriented surface \(F\)
in their complement, and a homogeneous \(\mathfrak{gl}_N\)
Khovanov–Rozansky label on each input link in its boundary three-sphere.
The surface meets the outer boundary in \(L\). The integral skein lasagna
module \(\mathcal S^N_0(X;L;\mathbb Z)\) is generated by these fillings
modulo multilinearity, isotopy relative to the outer boundary, and the
relation replacing a labelled ball by any filling of that ball whose
link-cobordism evaluation gives the label. Link homology in abstract
three-spheres, including the sweep-around property, makes evaluation
intrinsic. The homological and quantum bidegree of a filling is the sum of
the label bidegrees plus \((0,(1-N)\chi(F))\). Capping the input links inside
their balls gives the additional integral skein degree
\(\alpha\in H_2(X,L;\mathbb Z)\) with boundary \([L]\). For \(L=\varnothing\)
this becomes \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)\). The subscript \(0\) denotes the zeroth
blob-homological part and allows all link-homological degrees. Field
coefficients can be used in the link-homology input.
[Morrison–Walker–Wedrich, §§4.2, 5.1–5.4, Defs. 5.3–5.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.12194v5).
[Manolescu–Neithalath, §§2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.08520v2).

**Import.** Evaluation in the standard ball is an isomorphism
\(\mathcal S^N_0(B^4;L)\cong\operatorname{KhR}_N(S^3,L)\). Ordinary cap
attachment of a four-handle preserves the module. Thus for the oriented
standard sphere the full empty-link module is
\(\mathcal S^N_0(S^4;\varnothing;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\), or the ground
field with field coefficients, generated by the empty filling in
\((h,q,\alpha)=(0,0,0)\). This is an exact evaluation in all degrees. Every
homotopy four-sphere also has only skein degree \(0\), but its other two
degrees and multiplicities require a theorem or computation.
[Morrison–Walker–Wedrich, Ex. 5.6](https://arxiv.org/html/1907.12194v5).
[Manolescu–Neithalath, Prop. 1.6 and §2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.08520v2).

## Handle formulas and the input they consume

**Import.** For a \(0,2\)-handlebody, the manifold part of the input is a
framed attaching link \(K\subset S^3\). With an empty outer link,
Manolescu–Neithalath identify each skein-degree piece with cabled
\(\operatorname{KhR}_N\). Manolescu–Walker–Wedrich extend this to attaching
two-handles to any starting \(W\) along a framed \(K\) disjoint from the
chosen outer link. For a prescribed two-handle intersection vector
\(\alpha\) and the compatible class in \(W\), sum over all cables with
\(r_i\) additional pairs of oppositely oriented parallels on the \(i\)-th
component, \(r_i\ge 0\). The summand is the lasagna module of the cable and
outer link in \(W\), with quantum shift
\((1-N)(2|r|+|\alpha|)\). Identify the cable-braid action with the identity.
For the insertion of an oppositely oriented pair by an annulus, identify
the \(N-1\)-dot map with the original element and impose zero for each
insertion with fewer dots. These are exact relations over the original
integral input, with field versions obtained by working with field
coefficients. For \(W=B^4\) the summands are ordinary link homology.
[Manolescu–Neithalath, Thm. 1.1, §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.08520v2).
[Manolescu–Walker–Wedrich, Def. 3.1 and Thm. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04616v2).

**Import.** After adding the one-handles, the starting four-manifold is
\(\natural^m(S^1\times B^3)\) and the attaching link is now in
\(\#^m(S^1\times S^2)\). Over a field, for a null-homologous link in this
boundary, cut along each belt sphere met in \(2p_i\) points. Close the cut
tangle by all admissible pairs of tangles, one paired with its mirror at
the opposite three-ball. Take the direct sum of the link homologies, with
quantum shift \((N-1)\sum_i p_i\), and quotient by the differences of the
two actions of the corresponding three-ball tangle categories. Cables and
cobordism maps enter this presentation. Non-null-homologous links in the
one-handlebody have an empty filling set.
[Manolescu–Walker–Wedrich, Thm. 4.7 and following functoriality discussion, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04616v2).

**Import.** For a three-handle attached along \(S^2\), choose an equator
\(J\) with its two hemisphere disks, each regarded as a cobordism removing
\(J\) from the link. The three-handle map is surjective with kernel the
image of the difference of the two capping maps. For each target skein
degree, the source of the resulting quotient sums over all classes that
map to that degree. Apply this for every three-handle, then cap off with
the four-handle. Thus even when \(H_2(X;\mathbb Z)=0\), intermediate
classes killed by the three-handles and the associated relations can
matter. The integrated exact presentation is Thm. 3.10.
[Manolescu–Walker–Wedrich, Prop. 3.4, Thms. 3.7, 3.10](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04616v2).

**Import.** The one-handle sums may have infinitely many terms of the same
grading shift. For \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\), \(S^1\times B^3\) with at least two
pairs of oppositely oriented parallel longitudinal circles has an
infinite-dimensional \((0,0)\) piece, in particular over \(\mathbb Q\). A finite
handle diagram and a selected degree do not supply a cutoff. A
distinguishing finite calculation must justify the fate of its classes in
the full quotient or prove an applicable stabilization or vanishing
criterion.
[Manolescu–Walker–Wedrich, Thms. 1.5(c), 4.23 and Remark 4.8](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04616v2).

**Import, subsequent presentations.** Martin–Stelow–Wattal give exact
gluing via a tensor product over the intervening skein algebra and derive
separate one-, two-, and three-handle formulas. Their input theory must
be strictly monoidal and functorial in abstract \(S^3\) and
\(S^3\times I\). Blackwell–Krushkal–Luo give an alternative gluing
presentation using trisection data and a Hochschild quotient for the
central-surface complement. Their self-gluing formulation then uses
relations for filling the central-surface neighborhood. These are
presentations with additional input, not bounds on what a truncation can
decide. The new Rozansky–Willis-based module with one-dimensional inputs
is a distinct construction. Montague–Sullivan's subsequent cabling and
lasso formulas are for that rational variant. The abstract was accessible,
and the full paper was not retrieved in this reading.
[Martin–Stelow–Wattal, Thms. 1.1–1.4, §2, Thm. 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.17825v1).
[Blackwell–Krushkal–Luo, Thms. 3.14–3.15 and Cor. 3.19](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.05861v1).
[Ren–Sullivan–Wedrich–Willis–Zhang, Def. 3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05273).
[Montague–Sullivan, abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29452).

## Computed examples and limits

**Import.** Ren–Willis distinguish
\(X_1=X_{-1}(-5_2)\) and \(X_2=X_{-1}(P(3,-3,-8))\), the compact oriented
knot traces formed by one \(-1\)-framed two-handle on the standard
four-ball. In the authors' framed \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) normalization at
skein class \(1\in H_2(X_i;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\) and \(h=0\),
\(X_1\) has \(\mathbb Q\) in quantum degrees \(1,3\) and zero in all
other quantum degrees. For \(X_2\), the Lee calculation and rank inequality
give dimension at least one in quantum degree \(-1\), sufficient for the
distinction without a full calculation of either trace. The conclusion
including interiors is Theorem 1.1. The traces have boundary and
\(b_2=1\). The source also gives satellites under its slice-disk and
framing hypotheses in Theorem 1.3.
[Ren–Willis, Thms. 1.1, 1.3, 1.11, 1.13 and §1.5.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10452v3).

**Imports, closed values and examples.** Besides the standard sphere,
the exact field-coefficient value of
\(\mathcal S_0^N(S^1\times S^3;\varnothing)\) is the ground field generated
by the empty filling. Sullivan–Zhang give the exact zero
\(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) module for \(S^2\times S^2\) over characteristic-zero
fields in all skein degrees. Ren–Willis independently deduce its
vanishing and that of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) using their positive
self-intersection sphere criterion. Their Proposition 1.14 computes
specified integral tridegrees for \(\overline{\mathbb{CP}^2}\). Their
Conjecture 6.15 proposes a full rational formula and has that status in
the version checked. The underlying \(S^2\times D^2\) example has a full
integral Laurent-polynomial presentation, with quantum generators of
degrees \(-2k\) and homological degree zero. These closed examples and
the knot traces have different input from a general closed homotopy
four-sphere.
[Manolescu–Walker–Wedrich, Cor. 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.04616v2).
[Sullivan–Zhang, Cor. 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.01081v3).
[Ren–Willis, Thm. 1.4, Prop. 1.14, Ex. 3.2, §6.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10452v3).
[Manolescu–Neithalath, Thm. 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2009.08520v2).

**Import, blow-up-stably-standard homotopy spheres.** Ren–Willis
Proposition 6.17 computes the full rational \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\) module of
every oriented smooth homotopy sphere \(X\) for which
\(X\# k\overline{\mathbb{CP}^2}\cong k\overline{\mathbb{CP}^2}\)
by an oriented diffeomorphism for some \(k\). It is \(\mathbb Q\) in
\((0,0,0)\). Their proof uses the tensor product for connected sums and
Proposition 6.16's finiteness control to cancel the nonzero
negative-projective-plane factor. For spheres from Gluck twists of
\(S^4\), Section 6.10 records that a single negative blow-up suffices.
The vanishing \(S^2\times S^2\) factor gives no such cancellation.
[Ren–Willis, Prop. 2.4, §6.10 and Props. 6.16–6.17](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.10452v3).

**Import, Gluck twist theorem.** Ren–Sullivan–Wedrich–Willis–Zhang
Theorem 7.1 concerns the **classical** rational \(\mathfrak{gl}_2\)
module. For compact smooth oriented \(X\), framed oriented
\(L\subset\partial X\), and a smoothly embedded interior two-sphere \(S\)
with trivial normal bundle, write \(X_S\) for the Gluck twist. The theorem
gives two \(\mathbb Q\)-linear isomorphisms, with skein-degree transport
\(H_2(\tau_S)_\pm\). In class \(\alpha\) their \((h,q)\) shifts are
\((\mp(\alpha\cdot[S])^2/2,\ \pm(\alpha\cdot[S])^2/2)\).
One can choose either orientation of \(S\) in this square. Corollary 7.2
gives grading-preserving equality when \(S\) is null-homologous. Taking
\(X=S^4\) and \(L=\varnothing\) proves the exact full value
\(\mathcal S_0^2((S^4)_S;\varnothing;\mathbb Q)=\mathbb Q\) in
\((0,0,0)\) for **every** such twist. The theorem's coefficient and rank
hypotheses leave integral, finite-characteristic and higher-rank
comparisons beyond this import.
[Ren–Sullivan–Wedrich–Willis–Zhang, Thm. 7.1 and Cor. 7.2, pp. 49–50](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05273).
