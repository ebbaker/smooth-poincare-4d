## The exact inputs

Here $X$ is a smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere and $X^\circ=X\setminus\operatorname{int} B^4$ uses a small smooth coordinate ball. We identify its outgoing boundary with oriented $S^3$. The standard comparator is $B^4$. All witnessing surfaces are smoothly and properly embedded and oriented, with their induced boundary orientation. The classical boundary knot $K\subset S^3$ in the slice test and the embedded two-sphere $J\subset S^4$ used in the twist are different inputs.

**Import — the original test.** For a knot $K\subset S^3$ Rasmussen’s original invariant is $s_{\mathbb Q}(K)$ from Lee’s rational deformation, normalized by $s_{\mathbb Q}(U)=0$. If $g_4(K)$ is the minimum genus of a connected surface in the standard $B^4$ with boundary $K$, then $|s_{\mathbb Q}(K)|\le 2g_4(K)$. Freedman–Gompf–Morrison–Walker (FGMW) require a surface of known genus $g$ in $X^\circ$ and $|s_{\mathbb Q}(K)|>2g$. For a disk the second condition is $s_{\mathbb Q}(K)\ne0$. In §2 they construct witnesses from co-core boundary knots joined by bands where the handle presentation gives them, and explicitly allow higher-genus witnesses when three-handles are present. Their §2 also points to the extension of $s$ to multicomponent links and to other theories as separate possibilities. [Rasmussen, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402131#page=1) · [FGMW, §§1–2, pp. 173, 175–178, especially (2.1)](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/36744?nt=1)

**Import — Gluck stabilization and strong sliceness.** Let $J\subset S^4$ be any smoothly embedded $S^2$ with product tubular neighborhood $S^2\times D^2$. Form $X=G_J(S^4)$ by regluing the neighborhood via $(x,e^{i\theta})\mapsto(R_\theta x,e^{i\theta})$, where $R_\theta$ rotates $S^2$ through angle $\theta$. The proof of MMSW Corollary 1.13 uses the oriented diffeomorphisms

$$
X\mathbin{\#}\mathbb{CP}^2\cong\mathbb{CP}^2,\qquad
X\mathbin{\#}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\cong\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}.
$$

The proof cites Gompf–Stipsicz, *4-Manifolds and Kirby Calculus*, Exercise 5.2.7(b). If an oriented $m$-component link $L\subset S^3=\partial X^\circ$ bounds $m$ disjoint disks in $X^\circ$, MMSW Corollary 1.13 gives $s_{\mathbb F}(L)=1-m$. Their Definition 6.2 calls the analogous condition in a general closed four-manifold *strong H-sliceness* when the total relative class of the disks vanishes in $H_2(X^\circ,\partial X^\circ;\mathbb Z)$. The relative group vanishes for a punctured homotopy sphere. Theorem 6.12 gives the same value for the standard comparator. Definition 3.1 uses $\mathbb Q$ in the abbreviated notation and explicitly extends the results to all fields of characteristic different from two. The proof bounds $s(L)$ and $s(-L)$ from above using the two stabilization signs, and uses the link-mirror lower bound on their sum. Here $-L$ means the mirror with orientation reversed on every component. [MMSW, arXiv v2, Definition 3.1, p. 23, Definition 6.2 and Corollaries 1.9 and 1.13, with proofs on pp. 30–33](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1910.08195v2)

**Import — the direction in the genus estimate.** MMSW Corollary 1.9 concerns $W=(\#^k\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2})^\circ$, $k\ge 0$, with negative-definite summands when $k>0$. If $F\subset W$ has boundary $L\subset S^3$, no closed components, and $[F]=0\in H_2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z)$, it gives $s_{\mathbb F}(L)\le 1-\chi(F)$. For a connected orientable knot surface the right side is $2g(F)$. Reversing the ambient and surface orientations in the **positive** stabilization gives the second upper bound for $-L$. For knots $s_{\mathbb F}(-K)=-s_{\mathbb F}(K)$, giving the absolute-value bound on the punctured Gluck twist. For links one may use the two upper bounds $s_{\mathbb F}(L),s_{\mathbb F}(-L)\le 1-\chi(F)$. The knot mirror identity applies to the knot case. [MMSW, Corollary 1.9, its proof through Theorem 6.10, and Remark 6.13](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.08195v2#S6)

**Import — the coefficient extension.** Ren’s Corollary 1.4 for the case of $S^3$ boundary links gives the following over *every* coefficient field $\mathbb F$. Let $C$ be a smooth properly embedded oriented cobordism in $Z=(I\times S^3)\mathbin{\#}(\#^{k}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2})$ from $L_0$ to $L_1$, each null-homologous in its copy of $S^3$. Every component of $C$ must meet $L_1$. Then

$$
s_{\mathbb F}(L_1)\le s_{\mathbb F}(L_0)-\chi(C)-[C]^2-
\sum_{i=1}^k |[C]\cdot z_i|,
$$

where $z_i$ is the generator of the corresponding negative projective-plane summand and $[C]\in H_2(Z,\partial Z;\mathbb Z)$. Corollary 1.5 specializes to a connected surface bounding a knot, taking $L_0=\varnothing$ with $s(\varnothing)=1$. The characteristic-two boundary invariant uses Bar–Natan’s deformation. Ren’s allowance of characteristic two in this theorem is restricted to the $S^3$ boundary case. The proof uses Theorem 1.1 for the relevant $T(n,n)$ links over arbitrary fields and the cobordism estimate. [Ren, published Corollaries 1.4–1.5, p. 3937, and §§2.2–2.3](https://msp.org/gt/2024/28-8/gt-v28-n8-p09-s.pdf)

## Consequence over every field

**Working project deduction from the imports.** For any field and any oriented surface $F\subset X^\circ$ without closed components, let $m=|\partial F|>0$. Select $m$ small disjoint coordinate balls around interior points of $F$, with at least one on every component. Each ball meets $F$ in one flat disk. Join these balls by a tree of thin tubes away from $F$. Its union after rounding is a standard interior four-ball meeting $F$ in $m$ disjoint disks, whose boundaries form the split unlink $U_m$ in its boundary. Remove its interior. The resulting surface $C$ is a cobordism $U_m\to L$ with $\chi(C)=\chi(F)-m$, and every component meets both ends. Its integral relative class vanishes before stabilization and still vanishes after taking the connected sum away from the surface. The normalized value on the unlink is $s_{\mathbb F}(U_m)=1-m$, including in characteristic two, from the filtered homology of crossingless unknots and the split-union formula. [Schütz, arXiv v1, Definition 2.1 and Lemma 2.3(1)–(2), pp. 5–6, primary preprint cross-check](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.11373v1#S2)

Apply Ren’s negative-definite inequality to $C$ after negative stabilization. The two punctures can be carried to standard balls in the closed stabilized manifold by choosing the diffeomorphism up to isotopy. In the positive stabilization reverse the orientation of the ambient cobordism and of $C$, and interchange the incoming and outgoing boundary labels. This presents the same pairs of oriented links in the reverse direction in the negative model, with the component condition satisfied again. The resulting bounds are

$$
1-2m+\chi(F)\le s_{\mathbb F}(L)\le 1-\chi(F).
$$

For $m=1$ and connected $F$ this is $|s_{\mathbb F}(K)|\le 2g(F)$. For a collection of $m$ disks it gives $s_{\mathbb F}(L)=1-m$. The same proof with $k=0$ gives the stated bounds for the standard $B^4$. For any homotopy sphere with separately standard stabilizations by nonnegative numbers of copies of each sign of $\mathbb{CP}^2$, Ren’s inequality gives the same deductions using those numbers of summands. MMSW §9.3 identifies the two stabilization diffeomorphisms as a sufficient condition in its coefficient range and records the general strong-sliceness question as Question 9.11 in the inspected version. [MMSW, §9.3, p. 50](https://arxiv.org/html/1910.08195v2#S9.SS3)

The excluded witness is a violation of the ordinary boundary $s_{\mathbb F}$ genus bound or strong-sliceness normalization. These results leave the full link filtration, integral and cohomology-operation refinements, higher-rank link invariants and closed four-manifold skein modules outside the exclusion. They supply no diffeomorphism of the unstabilized Gluck closing. For $\Sigma_P$, the available framed construction supplies neither the Gluck hypothesis nor both definite stabilizations in this packet.
