## Actual section and retained old orbit

**Project proof replay.** Put \(B=(1+i+j+k)/2\), \(z=e^{2\pi i/11}\)
and \(\Gamma=Q_8\times\langle z\rangle\), acting on the unit quaternions
by \(q\mapsto u q z^{-r}\). On \(Y=S^3/\Gamma\) the isometry induced
by \(J(q)=Bqj^{-1}\) has period six. Indeed its coset is the identity
precisely when the left factor belongs to \(Q_8\) and the right factor,
after a possible simultaneous sign change, belongs to \(\langle z\rangle\).
This forces the power of \(J\) to be a multiple of both three and two.
Conjugation cycles \(i,j,k\) and inverts \(z\). With
\(n=(i+j+k)/\sqrt3\) and \(p_0jp_0^{-1}=n\), the actual degree-one
section is represented by \([p_0e^{j\pi t/6},t]\) in the convention
\((y,1)\sim(Jy,0)\). The relation \(J(p_0e^{j\pi/6})=p_0\)
shows that its lift gives the stable letter, with the stated
conjugation or its inverse according to the path convention.

The even subgroup of the orbit deck group acts freely. For a fixed
point its two quaternion real parts must agree. For a nontrivial
right \(z^r\) the real part is none of \(0,\pm1/2,\pm1\), since
a root of unity with such real part has order dividing \(4\) or \(6\).
If the right factor is \(1\), the left factor must be \(1\).
Consequently every nontrivial orbit stabilizer has order two.
The class of \((i,j)\), acting as \(q\mapsto i q j^{-1}\), fixes the
great circle defined by \(qj q^{-1}=i\), and lies in the \(J^3\)
coset. It supplies a genuine old exceptional orbit.

For \(C=p_0e^{j\mathbb R}\), an intersection with a translate by
\((A,z^rj^c)\) forces
\(\cos(4\pi r/11)=n\cdot\operatorname{Ad}_A n\), which is \(1\)
or \(-1/3\). The latter would give the nonintegral rational
algebraic integer \(2\cos(4\pi r/11)=-2/3\). The former forces the
setwise stabilizer, whose action on \(C\) is generated by the free
shift \(-\pi/6\) of \(J\). The projected loop is therefore embedded

a swap supported over a sufficiently small tube of this loop.

## Both transported inverse framings

**Project proof replay.** For an invariant metric the effective
\(C_2\) slice action is an orientation-preserving nontrivial
involution on the normal three-disk. It is a half-turn \(R_\pi\)
about an axis. It cannot act trivially on the slice because the
stabilizer would then fix an open set of the connected effective
circle manifold. If \(u\in S^1\), the slice bundle can be written
\((S^1\times D^3)/((z,y)\sim(-z,R_\pi y))\).
The product coordinates \([z,y]\mapsto(z^2,R_{\arg z}y)\)
give the local action
\[
u\cdot(w,y)=(u^2w,R_{\arg u}y).
\]
Twisting the frame once along the core by the axial rotation
\(R_{\arg w}\) represents the other class in \(\pi_1SO(3)=C_2\).
It changes the normal rotation weight from \(1\) to \(3\);
using the opposite twist gives weight \(-1\) instead. Thus for
an odd weight \(k\) representing the transported framing, the
same boundary action extends across the inverse surgery cap,
\[
u\cdot(\zeta,x)=(u^2\zeta,R_{k\arg u}x)
\qquad (\zeta,x)\in D^2\times S^2.
\]
The product collar in these coordinates makes the gluing smooth.
Reversing the orbit coordinate replaces the core weight by \(-2\)
with the same odd-weight extension after orienting its normal frame.
A homotopy of the transported framing to the chosen weight
representative induces an isotopy of the boundary attaching map,
which extends over its collar. A retained principal orbit makes the
closed action effective. This compares the two inverse framings
around \(e\) for this intermediate. The original section surgery
throughout uses \(a\).
