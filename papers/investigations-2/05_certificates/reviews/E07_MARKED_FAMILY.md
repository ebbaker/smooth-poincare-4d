## The actual section and its lift

Put \(n=(i+j+k)/\sqrt3\), \(b_q=e^{n\pi/3}\), \(z=e^{2\pi i/d}\), and \(J(q)=b_q q j^{-1}\). The full deck group is \(T^*\times_{\{\pm1\}}\langle z,j\rangle\) with action \((A,B)q=AqB^{-1}\), of order \(48d\). Indeed \(J\) normalizes \(Q_8\times C_d\), induces order six on its quotient, and enlarges it to the displayed group of that order. A fixed point requires equality of the real parts of \(A,B\). The even right factors have real parts \(\pm\cos(2\pi r/d)\). The only matches with tetrahedral real parts \(0,\pm1/2,\pm1\) are the trivial effective pairs, because \(d\) is odd and prime to three. The odd right factors have real part zero. A nonidentity stabilizer thus has order two and its generator lies over \(J^3\) in the suspension group.

Choose \(p_0jp_0^{-1}=n\) and \(p_0ip_0^{-1}=x_*=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2\). In the suspension convention \([u+1,q]=[u,Jq]\), the actual section is \(\gamma(u)=[u,p_0e^{j\pi u/6}]\). Its projection is the image of \(C=p_0e^{j\mathbb R}\). If \(C\) meets a translate by \((A,B)\), preservation of the inner products of the marked axes gives \(\cos(4\pi r/d)=n\cdot\operatorname{Ad}_A n\), where \(r\) is the right-axis index and the right side is \(1\) or \(-1/3\). Twice the cosine is an algebraic integer, which excludes \(-2/3\). Equality at \(1\) forces \(A\in\langle b_q\rangle,B\in\langle j\rangle\). The resulting order-twelve stabilizer is generated by \(J\) and acts on \(C\) by translations. Hence \(\gamma\) maps diffeomorphically to the embedded regular knot \(\ell=C/\langle J\rangle\). Its lift in the even quotient \(M_d^+\) is \(K=C/\langle J^2\rangle\), with angular period \(\pi/3\). The odd involution \(\tau\) makes a half-period shift on \(K\).

## Fractions and unwrapped heights

The Hopf base of \(M_d^+\) is \(S^2/T\) with the round angular unit-sphere metric. The positive generic fibre period is \(P=\pi/d\), generated by shifts \(\pi,2\pi/d\). The positive base rotations at \(n,-n,i\) lift to \(b_q,b_q^{-1},i\) with positive shifts \(\pi/3,\pi/3,\pi/2\). The fractional fibre turns are \(2/3,2/3,1/2\) at both orders. The odd involution reverses fibre angle, exchanges the two order-three cones and on the base is induced by the antipodal map.

The connection \(\alpha=\langle q^{-1}dq,i\rangle\) has curvature \(-\omega/2\), since \(d\alpha(j,k)=-2\) and the Hopf base images of \(j,k\) at \(1\) have oriented area \(4\). Its quotient integral in fibre turns is \(-d/6\). Delete invariant disks about the three cones. Extend a fixed section of the mirror interval equivariantly across its collar by horizontal transport, then extend on the annulus on the \(n\) side and reflect. The prescribed subinterval on the outer boundary is contractible, so it permits an extending section and annulus gauges of arbitrary degree trivial there. Set the integer correction at \(n\) to zero. Reflection sets the one at \(-n\) to zero because it reverses both base loop and fibre. Stokes' theorem with positive orientations about the deleted disks gives
\[
\tfrac12+n_2+\tfrac23+\tfrac23=d/6,\qquad
n_2=(d-11)/6,\qquad k=2n_2+1=(d-8)/3 .
\]

The Hopf image of \(p_0e^{jv}\) runs round the equator normal to \(n\) at angular speed two. The cyclic permutations give its generic identifications. An even sign change returns to this equator only at a zero coordinate, giving one double point in the quotient, with preimages \(v=0,\pi/6\) in one knot period. Transport by \(\operatorname{Ad}_{kb_q}\), with \(k\) here the quaternion unit, gives ordered tangents \((1,1,-2)/\sqrt6,(1,1,2)/\sqrt6\) at \(x_*\). Their ordered determinant against \(x_*\) is positive. For \(0<v<\pi/6\), conjugating by \(i\) puts the arc in the positive octant, where its tangent-left normal has negative dot product with \(n\). Thus the \(n\) lobe is clockwise. Reflection gives the other, counterclockwise lobe. Each bounds one third of the medial triangle of side \(\pi/3\), hence has unsigned angular area \(\Omega=\arccos(1/3)-\pi/3\).

The section integral in the positive orientation about \(n\) is \(2/3-\Omega/(2P)\). Horizontality gives the **positive real increment** \(h=2/3-\Omega/(2P)\) on the clockwise lobe, unwrapped by the prescribed integer zero. Direct quaternion multiplication gives \(-kb_q p_0e^{j\pi/6}=p_0e^{i\vartheta}\) with \(\vartheta=\arccos(1/\sqrt3)=\pi/3-\Omega/2\). Thus \(h=d\vartheta/\pi-m\), with \(m=(d-2)/3\), namely three at \(11\) and five at \(17\).

**Exact bound.** The alternating cosine polynomials give
\[
\cos(15/16)\ \ge\ \frac{158857531}{268435456}\ >\ \frac1{\sqrt3},
\qquad
\cos(31/32)\ \le\ \frac{14280577}{25165824}\ <\ \frac1{\sqrt3}.
\]
The inner comparisons follow by squaring positive numbers. Since \(31/10<\pi<22/7\), at both orders \(m\pi/d<15/16<\vartheta<31/32<(m+1/2)\pi/d\). For the first bound on \(\pi\) use the inscribed dodecagon and \(3(\sqrt6-\sqrt2)>31/10\). The second follows by integrating \(x^4(1-x)^4/(1+x^2)>0\) on \((0,1)\). This gives \(0<h<1/2\) at \(11\) and \(17\).

Choose the fixed section at the short-arc midpoint, so the clockwise lobe starts at \(-h/2\) and ends at \(+h/2\). Equivariance determines the real height on the other lobe by sign. On the right half of a symmetric plane model the lobe bounds a disk around the marked cone. Matching the mirror tangents at the crossing, this disk and its complement gives \(x=2\sin t,\ v=\sin 2t\) with that cone inside the right lobe. Reflect to the other side. The two ordered crossing tangents in this model have positive determinant. Interpolate the real antiperiodic height to \(\theta=-H\cos t\) for \(0<H<1/4\). The two crossing heights maintain their order and remain less than half a turn apart. This is an equivariant isotopy avoiding the fixed strands and cone tori, since the crossing is the only double point and the only contact with the fixed base interval. The positive vertical push stays transverse.

## Quotient diagram and ball framing

Temporarily put product tori back at the exchanged cone disks. The involution is \((x,v,\theta)\mapsto(-x,v,-\theta)\), and at fixed \(v\) the annular quotient is a disk with \(w=X+iY=\cosh(cx+2\pi i\theta)\), \(c>0\). The two old strands are \(X=+1,-1,\ Y=0\). The plus branch is \(\theta=0\) at \(x=0\). The core of the exchanged product tori becomes \(F\), the ellipse \(x=x_F\) at \(v=0\), \(0<x_F<2\), oriented by positive fibre. Its meridian is positive about the cone disk. Its product fibre longitude is a zero push, since the projected ellipse is simple and a constant base push in the \(v\) direction gives its product framing. The order-three filling slope is \(3\mu_F-2\lambda_F\). The regular knot \(L=K/\tau\) projects to the \(w\)-disk via \(\cosh(2c\sin t-2\pi iH\cos t)\), \(0\le t\le\pi\). The oval in the strip \(|\operatorname{Im}(cx+2\pi i\theta)|<\pi/2\) maps injectively modulo sign, so the projected circle is simple. Flattening it in this projection gives the ball's zero longitude from \(\partial_v\).

In the raw native tube use
\[
q=p_0(\cos r\,e^{ju}+\sin r\,e^{j\xi}i),\qquad
a:(\Delta u,\Delta\xi)=(\pi/6,-5\pi/6),\quad \mu:(0,2\pi).
\]
The tangent-normal basis at the reference point is \((j,i,-k)\), positive in the ambient orientation. The longitude \(a\) comes from \(J^{-1}\), which shifts \(u\) and the constant normal direction by these two angles. It is independent of \(d\). The right Hopf push \(q e^{i\epsilon}\) has \(\xi=u\), so over a period of \(K\) it gives \(\lambda_H=2a+\mu\). In the model \((x,v,\theta)\) put \(g^2=x'^2+v'^2\). The positive normal frame modulo tangent is \((\partial_\theta,(v',-x',0)/g)\). In this frame the ball push has coordinates \((-v'\theta'/g^2,-x'/g)\). Its imaginary component vanishes at \(\pi/2,3\pi/2\). At the first point its real part is positive and the imaginary part changes from negative to positive. At the second the real part is negative and the imaginary part changes from positive to negative. Its winding is \(+1\). The ball longitude lifts to \(\lambda_H+\mu=2a+2\mu\), hence \(\lambda_L=a+\mu\) and \(\mu_L=\mu\) downstairs.

The planar \(w\)-projections intersect twice at \(x=x_F\). On the real axis their endpoints and the two fixed values occur as \(F_{\rm left},-1,L_{\rm left},+1,F_{\rm right},L_{\rm right}\). These data identify the complementary planar regions with those for the circles of radius \(2,3/2\) centered at \(0,+1\). Deform to those circles with the two fixed strands and the crossing heights retained. The sign of \(v\) on \(L\) is opposite to \(Y\), so take \(v=Y\) on \(F\) and \(v=-Y\) on \(L\). Project to \((X,v)\) from \(+Y\), with ambient orientation \((X,v,Y)\). The tangents on \(F,L\) follow \((-v,X),(v,1-X)\), and greater \(Y\) is over. Both mutual crossings have negative sign. Erasing the two old strands gives the negative Hopf link.

The corner quotient with singularities forgotten is a smooth ball. Use a square disk and round corners in the simultaneous conjugation model of the next section. The quotient becomes an interval times the \(\cosh\) disk in the two reflected coordinates. The boundary identification of the two smooth balls can be absorbed into the corner ball by extending its sphere diffeomorphism through a collar using the connectedness in Smale's theorem. [Smale, *Diffeomorphisms of the 2-sphere*, Theorem A, p. 621](https://doi.org/10.2307/2033664) · [Original opening-page facsimile in the slide PDF](https://math.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/seminars/download.php?fid=2693#page=7)

The negative Hopf product exterior identifies \(\mu_F=-\lambda_L\), \(\lambda_F=-\mu_L\). The two attached meridians in the same basis therefore are \(2\mu_L-3\lambda_L\) and \((s-1)\mu_L+\lambda_L\). Their signed intersection is \(3s-1\). At \(s=0\) they form a unimodular basis, giving the standard two solid tori of \(S^3\). At \(s=1\), a meridian-preserving boundary twist on the \(L\) solid torus changes the coefficient \(-3\) in \(2\mu_L-3\lambda_L\) by two, giving the standard gluing of \(L(2,1)=\mathbb{RP}^3\). Since \(\lvert3s-1\rvert=1\) has only one integer solution, the raw \(a\) is intrinsically the unique \(S^3\) longitude.

## The outer pillowcase and endpoint words

\big((k+1)x_0/2+\theta,\ kx_0+2\theta\big).

## Signed word check

Use positive meridians \(F,L\) on arcs just after the lower mutual crossing along \(F\) and upper mutual crossing along \(L\), respectively. The old strands are oriented upwards. At a crossing, the sign is \(\epsilon=\operatorname{sgn}\det(\text{over tangent},\text{under tangent})\) in the displayed plane. From the front, a path following the understrand contributes the over-meridian to power \(\epsilon\) to its longitudinal transport. For the over tangent pointing right and under tangent pointing up, the front paths follow a positive loop in the \((v,Y)\) plane. Consequently the outgoing under-meridian is conjugated by the inverse of that contribution. Greater \(Y\) and the displayed tangent directions give \(\epsilon=-1\) at all eight crossings. For example the old plus arc is in front of \(L\) at \(v>0\), and behind at \(v<0\). Its tangent is \((0,1)\).

| Strand | Ordered over-meridians | Result |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(F\) | \(c,b,L\) | \(\lambda_F=c^{-1}b^{-1}L^{-1}\) |
| \(L\) | \(b_1,F\) | \(\lambda_L=b_1^{-1}F^{-1}\) |
| Old plus | \(L,F\) | \(b_1=LbL^{-1}\), \(b_t=Fb_1F^{-1}\) |
| Old minus | \(F\) | \(c_t=FcF^{-1}\) |

The surgery components have no self crossings. The first two rows impose the corresponding longitude commutators. Together with \(b^2=c^2=1\), the pillowcase relation and \(F^3=\lambda_F^2\), the relation on \(L\) is \(L^{s-1}\lambda_L=1\). An ambient sign control comes from restoring the regular tube by \(L=1\). Write \(F'=bFb\) and \(r=cb\). The even subgroup is generated by \(F,F',r\). Its abelianized laws are \(3F=2r,\ 3F'=-2r,\ 2(F'-F)=kr\). Conjugation by \(b\) gives the same laws with signs reversed or \(F,F'\) exchanged. Their determinant has magnitude \(9k+24=3d\), the order of \((T^*\times C_d)^{\rm ab}\cong C_3\times C_d\). Reversing just one pillowcase translation sign would give magnitude \(15\) at eleven and \(3\) at seventeen.

For \(s=0\), \(L=\lambda_L\) gives \(L=F^{-1}b\) and \(\lambda_F=cF\). The commutator \([F,\lambda_F]=1\) gives \([F,c]=1\), and \(F^3=(cF)^2\) gives \(F=1\). Thus \(d_2=1,\ f=cb\) and the remaining relation is \((cb)^k=1\). The substitutions in reverse satisfy every preceding relation. At \(k=1\) the involutions are equal. At \(k=3\) the two involutions generate the dihedral group of order six.

For \(s=1\), \(\lambda_L=1\) gives \(F=b_1\) and \(F^2=1\). With \(q=\lambda_F\) this gives \(F=q^2,\ q^4=1\). The longitude words give \(L=qc,\ b=cq^2c,\ b_t=q^2,\ c_t=q^2cq^2\). Hence \(d_2=(cq^2)^2,\ f=(cq^2)^{-1}\), with remaining relations \(c^2=q^4=(cq^2)^{4+k}=1\). Conversely these relations give all the earlier ones under the stated substitutions. Identifying \(q^2\) with the involution \(u\) of \(D_{2(4+k)}\) gives the displayed amalgam. These are symbolic checks for the two requested slopes.

## Section framing and surgery

Extend the actual section of the suspension action over the regular tube by \([u,q(u,w)]\), with \(u\) of period one, \(r=|w|\), and native angles \(u_{\rm angle}=\pi u/6\), \(\xi=(-5\pi/6+2\pi s)u+\arg w\). The map is smooth at \(w=0\) by the native formula, and the \(J^{-1}\) relation makes it smooth under \(u\mapsto u+1\). The suspension gives the equivariant product \(S^1_a\times S^1_f\times D^2_w\). The meridian phase is positive, the coordinate circle \(S^1_a\) at fixed \(w\) represents \(a+s\mu\), and \(\gamma\) is \(f=0,w=0\). The relative frame rotation changes by a positive full turn about the orbit direction when \(s\) increases by one. Its lift to the unit quaternions ends at \(-1\), so \(s=0,1\) specify the two distinct normal three-framings of the actual section.

For direct comparison with the orbit filling, take an interval \(I\subset S^1_f\) about zero, and let \(I'\) be the complementary interval. Work with corners and remove \(S^1_a\times I\times D^2_w\) for the framed circle surgery, gluing \(D^2_a\times\partial(I\times D^2_w)\). In the resulting section closing, \(S^1_a\times I'\times D^2_w\) together with \(D^2_a\times\partial I\times D^2_w\) is \(S^2\times D^2_w\), with the compressed torus as core. The remaining local piece is \(D^2_a\times I\times S^1_w\). Sphere surgery replaces \(S^2\times D^2_w\) by \((D^2_a\times I')\times S^1_w\) with \(D^2_a\times I'\) the chosen \(D^3\). Product gluing at the two interval ends, followed by rounding, gives \(S^1_f\times D^2_a\times S^1_w\), with the exterior boundary marking inherited from the tube. Rotation in \(f\) extends the exterior action. Its base meridian is \(a+s\mu\), and the attached core is regular. The inverse framed circle surgery is on the core of \(D^3\times S^1_w\), at fixed interior \(f\) in \(I'\) and the centre of \(D^2_a\). This circle maps diffeomorphically to the base dual core, and its normal framing is the product \(D^3\) framing. The old exceptional orbits are disjoint from the construction.
