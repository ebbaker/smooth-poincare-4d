## 1. Quaternionic boundary and a homology check

Write \(n=(i+j+k)/\sqrt3\), \(b=e^{n\pi/3}\), \(z=e^{2\pi i/17}\),
\(\Lambda=(T^*\times\langle z,j\rangle)/\langle(-1,-1)\rangle\).
The action is \((A,B)q=AqB^{-1}\), and the chosen regular great circle is
\(C=p_0 e^{j\mathbb R}\) with \(p_0 j p_0^{-1}=n\). The stabilizer acts
freely on \(C\) and is generated by \(J=(b,j)\) of order twelve. In a small
tube write
\[
q=p_0(\cos r\,e^{js}+\sin r\, e^{j\theta} i).
\]
The positive meridian \(\mu\) advances \(\theta\) by \(2\pi\). The native
longitude \(a\) advances \((s,\theta)\) by \((\pi/6,-5\pi/6)\) and ends
at \(J^{-1}\) of its starting point. In the parity-even cover
\(M^+=S^3/(T^*\times C_{17})\), the full inverse image of the knot is
\(K=C/\langle J^2\rangle\). Its primitive covering longitude is \(A=2a\).
The right Hopf push \(q\mapsto q e^{i\epsilon}\) has \(\theta=s\) on this
coordinate torus. It gives
\[
\lambda_{\mathrm H}=A+\mu .
\]
In the sphere the constant-normal longitude is
\(\lambda_C=12a+5\mu\). These are equalities of boundary classes with the
indicated projection or lift understood.

Here is an independent exact check of the whole abelian marking, including the
singular meridian. A great circle \(q u q^{-1}=v\), with unit imaginary
\(u,v\), is oriented by right multiplication by \(e^{ut}\).
Its two-plane has self-dual and anti-self-dual unit directions \(v,u\),
respectively, in the bases from left and right quaternion multiplication.
For instance \(1\wedge j\) gives the directions of
\((1\wedge j)-(i\wedge k)\) and \((1\wedge j)+(i\wedge k)\).
The determinant of two oriented unit frames, in this order, is
\(\tfrac12(v\cdot v'-u\cdot u')\). Its sign is their linking number:
straightening the two transverse planes by an orientation-preserving linear
map reduces to the coordinate Hopf link, and reversing either plane reverses
the sign.

The 68 regular circles have \(v\) one of four tetrahedral vertices and
\(u=\operatorname{Ad}_{z^r}j\) with \(0\le r<17\). In the row for
\(C\), the other 16 with the same \(v\) give sign \(+1\).
For each of three other vertices, \(v\cdot v'=-1/3\), and 11 of
the cosine values \(\cos(2\pi k/17)\) exceed \(-1/3\), while 6
are smaller. The row sum is \(16+3(6-11)=1\).

An involution of \(\Lambda\) with a fixed point has representative
\((A,z^rj)\) with \(A\in\{\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\}\). Indeed the two
factors must have the same real part, the right odd real part is zero,
and the left zero real part forces a pure unit in \(Q_8\). The parity-even
group acts freely. Every stabilizer has order at most two, since its even
subgroup is trivial. The 102 fixed circles are therefore disjoint. They are
also disjoint from the regular link. All fixed involutions are conjugate by
left tetrahedral rotations on the six axes and right conjugation of \(z^rj\)
on the seventeen choices. This choice of representative is invariant under
conjugation up to the same parametrization and gives an orientation of their
single image. The linking with this singular lift, for a fixed right axis,
is computed with \(v\cdot A=\pm1/\sqrt3\) and
\(j\cdot z^rj=\cos(2\pi r/17)\).
For each of three positive axes, 5 values of the cosine exceed
\(1/\sqrt3\), giving total \(+7\) per axis. For each of three
negative axes, 11 exceed \(-1/\sqrt3\), giving total \(-5\) per
axis. The whole row sum is \(3(7-5)=6\).

For the threshold counts it suffices to check consecutive angles by
monotonicity on \([0,\pi]\).
The values at \(k=2,3\) straddle \(1/\sqrt3\), because
\(4\pi/17<\pi/4\) and
\(\cos(6\pi/17)=\sin(5\pi/34)<1/2\).
The values at \(k=5,6\) straddle both negative thresholds. In fact
\(\sin(3\pi/34)<1/3\) and
\(\sin(7\pi/34)>21/34-(21/34)^3/6>1/\sqrt3\).
The second bound uses the increasing Taylor lower bound \(x-x^3/6\) at
\(x=7\pi/34>21/34\). The rational comparisons are included in the finite
[checker](../numerics/e02_geometry_checks.py).

Let \(E^\circ\) be the ordinary exterior of both the regular knot and
the singular knot in the underlying closed space. Let \(\sigma\) be the
positive meridian of the singular knot before adding its orbifold relation.
A meridian of a singular lift maps to \(2\sigma\), whereas a regular
meridian maps to \(\mu\). The two row sums give
\[
12a+5\mu=[\lambda_C]=\mu+12\sigma\quad\text{in }H_1(E^\circ).
\]
The ordinary closed space has \(H_1=C_3\). To see the quotient of groups
needed for this claim, remove the fixed link upstairs, use the regular
\(\Lambda\)-cover of the smooth locus, and restore the singular tube
downstairs. The upstairs kernel is normally generated by the lifted
meridians, each mapping to the square of a meridian downstairs. Restoring
the tube kills the kernel and the fixed involution classes in
\(\Lambda\). Its abelianization is therefore the quotient of
\(\Lambda^{\rm ab}=C_3\times C_2\) by the odd involution class.
The path \(a\) maps to the generator \(J^{-1}\) of the resulting
\(C_3\) abelianization. The meridian sequence of \(E^\circ\) has
kernel freely generated by \(\mu,\sigma\), since the closed
oriented space has \(H_2=0\). Apply it to \(3a\) before dividing
the displayed row-sum relation by four. This proves
\[
H_1(E^\circ)=\langle a,\mu,\sigma\mid 3a+\mu-3\sigma=0\rangle.
\]
Adding \(2\sigma=0\) and \(a+s\mu=0\) gives \(C_{2|3s-1|}\).
If the singular meridian is also killed, the order is \(|3s-1|\).
These checks use no tangle data.

## 2. Section, reflection, and the quotient model

Use the right Hopf map \(q\mapsto qi q^{-1}\). The even quotient base is
the positive and negative spherical octants modulo cyclic coordinate
permutations, joined on the edges by the even sign changes. The odd
quotient reflects across the join, interchanges the order-three cone
points, and reverses the right fibre. The join has one order-two cone.
On the seam lift \(x=(\cos u,\sin u,0)\), \(0<u<\pi/2\), use
\(s(u)=e^{ku/2}\) as Hopf section. The odd lift \(q\mapsto kqj^{-1}\)
acts by
\[
s(u)e^{i\alpha}\longmapsto s(u)e^{i(\pi/2-\alpha)}.
\]
With regular period \(P=\pi/17\), the fixed fibre positions in this
section are \(1/4,3/4\) of a turn. At \(u=\pi/4\) the first crossing lift
can be represented by \(ip_0\), with
\(p_0 i p_0^{-1}=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2\). Conjugating \(j\) by
\(s(u)^{-1}ip_0\) gives \(-\sqrt{2/3}\,j-k/\sqrt3\).
Consequently its angle is \(-\pi/4-\vartheta/2\pmod\pi\) for
\(\vartheta=\arccos(1/\sqrt3)\). By the second lift calculation
in E62 the other crossing angle adds \(\vartheta\). The reduced angles
of the two branches thus lie on the two sides of the fixed height \(1/4\),
at \(1/4\mp h_*/2\) where \(h_*=\vartheta/P-5\). For an exact bound
one can use \(15/16<\vartheta<31/32\) from the alternating cosine
bounds and \(157/50<\pi<22/7\), giving \(0<h_*<1/4\).

For the corner choose the base lift near \(i\) with local complex coordinate
\(w=x_j+\mathrm i x_k\), and take the short minimal rotation from \(i\) as
Hopf section. The even local deck generator \(r=(i,1)\) acts by
\(w\mapsto-w\), shifting the fibre by \(\pi/2\).
The odd lift \(\tau=(k,j)\) acts by \(w\mapsto\bar w\) and
\(\alpha\mapsto\pi/2-\alpha\). One checks this on the section by
conjugating the short rotation by \(i\) or \(k\); the latter uses
\(k j^{-1}=i\). After translating the origin to the selected fixed section
and writing the fibre in turns, the two formulas are
\[
r(w,\phi)=(-w,\phi+1/2),\qquad
\tau(w,\phi)=(\bar w,-\phi).
\]
In the local sector of the positive octant, the real face has fixed fibre
heights \(0,1/2\), and the imaginary face has fixed heights \(1/4,3/4\).
The two points on each face join at the corner on the same fixed arc,
as applying \(r\) identifies the two lifts there.

Delete disk neighborhoods of the three cone points. The positive half of the
remaining pair of pants is an annulus with a mirror interval on its outer
boundary. Select the fixed section by the short-arc midpoint at the crossing,
continue it on the interval, and extend it to a section of the annulus.
Existence of this extension follows by trivializing the annulus bundle and
extending the prescribed circle map from a neighborhood of an interval.
On the other half use the reflected section. In the local radial charts
at the two order-three points, the section boundary loop around a deleted
disk advances the reduced angle by \(2/3+n_\pm\) turns. The fractional
part is \((\pi/3)/P\bmod 1=2/3\) on either positive local base rotation.
At order two the corresponding advance is \(1/2+n_2\). Stokes for
the connection \(\langle q^{-1}dq,i\rangle/P\), with curvature
\(-\omega/(2P)\) in unit angular area, gives
\[
\tfrac12+n_2+\tfrac23+n_++\tfrac23+n_-=\tfrac{17}{6}.
\]
Indeed the angular area is \(4\pi/12\), and the boundaries of the complement
have orientation opposite the disk boundaries. Reflection gives \(n_-=n_+\).
A gauge on the positive annulus can change \(n_+\) by any integer while
fixed on the mirror collar. Use a degree-one circle map on the annulus
whose change is supported off that interval. Thus choose \(n_+=0\).
The resulting \(n_-=0\), \(n_2=1\) make the cone meridians
\(3d_+-2f,3d_--2f,2d_2-3f\), where \(d\) is the positively
oriented section boundary loop and \(f\) is the positive regular fibre.

Use coordinates \((x,v)\) on the disk with the order-two disk deleted and
the other two cone disks temporarily restored with the product section,
oriented by \(dx\wedge dv\), with \(x>0\) on the positive half.
The mirror is \(x=0\). The positive lobe runs clockwise and surrounds
the positive cone once, as seen by folding the arc into the positive
octant. Its horizontal lift has real height increment \(h_*\) in the
chosen section. In detail, the section connection integral on the
counterclockwise lobe is \(2/3-\Omega/(2P)\) by Stokes on the annulus
inside the lobe with the cone disk removed. Here
\(\Omega=\arccos(1/3)-\pi/3\) is the E62 lobe area. The actual
clockwise horizontal lift has the opposite of the negative section integral,
namely \(2/3-\Omega/(2P)=h_*\). Reflection gives the returning increment
\(-h_*\). This calculates the unwrapped change, which is necessary for
the height isotopy.

An oriented change of disk coordinates can now use the base curve
\((x,v)=(\sin u,\sin 2u)\), \(0\le u\le2\pi\), with
the positive cone at \((x_F,0)\) for some \(0<x_F<1\). To see this, match
the two ends in a collar of the mirror contact, preserving the oriented
crossing, and match the inside and outside disks of the positive lobe
relative to the cone label. Extend the boundary identifications across
those disks and reflect. The two distinct tangents and the absence of
further crossings follow from the equator model of E62. Interpolate the
real fibre lift to \(-H\cos u\) with \(H=h_*/2\). Its endpoint heights
agree throughout at the crossing and stay distinct. The interpolation
can be chosen anti-periodic under \(u\mapsto u+\pi\), so it is equivariant.
All other base projection points along the knot have multiplicity one
before the odd identification. The embedded knot and the positive Hopf
push therefore move through the interpolation without passing through a
cone fibre or an old fixed strand.

With fibre coordinate \(\theta\) in turns centered on the chosen section,
the involution on the temporarily filled product is
\((x,v,\theta)\mapsto(-x,v,-\theta)\).
For any \(c>0\) the Joukowsky quotient of \(e^{cx+2\pi\mathrm i\theta}\)
by inversion maps an annulus slice to a disk via
\[
W=X+\mathrm iY=\tfrac12(e^{cx+2\pi\mathrm i\theta}
                 +e^{-cx-2\pi\mathrm i\theta}).
\]
Its two branch values are \(X=1,-1\), \(Y=0\). Over the interval
of \(v\)-values the quotient is a ball. The temporary positive cone fibre
\(F\) projects to the ellipse \(x=x_F\), \(v=0\), oriented by the positive
fibre. The regular quotient knot \(L\) projects to the image of the
half-ellipse \(x=\sin u,\ \theta=-H\cos u\) for \(0\le u\le\pi\).
This projected curve is embedded with endpoints identified under inversion:
the full ellipse is inside the strip \(|2\pi\theta|<\pi/2\), where
the only branch value is \(+1\), and \(W=W'\) there requires the same
exponential or its inverse. The resulting closed projection surrounds
\(1\), leaves \(-1\) outside, and crosses \(F\) twice transversely at
\(x=x_F\). The two real intersections of the projection of \(L\) are
\(cos(2\pi H)\) and \(cosh(c)\). The six marked values on the real
line are in the order \(F_{\mathrm{left}},-1,L_{\mathrm{left}},1,
F_{\mathrm{right}},L_{\mathrm{right}}\).

## 3. Push-off and marked outer face

The positive regular meridian is the positive meridian \(\mu_L\) of
the ball diagram. Its zero framing is the push in the \(\partial_v\)
direction since its projection to \(W\) is embedded. For the doubled
parametrization write \(g=\sqrt{x'^2+v'^2}\). In the oriented normal
frame \((\partial_\theta,(v',-x',0)/g)\) along the knot, the coordinates
of \(\partial_v\) modulo the tangent are
\[
-\frac{v'\theta'}{g^2}-\mathrm i\frac{x'}g.
\]
Their winding is \(+1\). The imaginary part vanishes only at
\(u=\pi/2,3\pi/2\), with real parts positive and negative respectively.
At the positive real point the imaginary part increases. This proves
the lift of the zero longitude is \(\lambda_{\mathrm H}+\mu=2a+2\mu\).
Thus the based peripheral classes, with the same whisker, satisfy
\[
\mu=\mu_L,\qquad a=\lambda_L\mu_L^{-1}.
\]
Here the right-hand side is a product in the peripheral group. The
commutator relation makes its order immaterial in the exterior. The product
fibre \(f\) is also the zero longitude on \(F\), since the product push in
the \(v\)-direction projects to the same embedded ellipse.

For the corner attachment let \(u_2\) be a positive turn on the base
boundary of the order-two disk and \(\theta\) the product fibre angle
in turns. Put \(u_2=0\) at the upper mirror endpoint and \(u_2=1/2\) at
the lower endpoint. This order follows from the corner coordinates:
near the \(i\)-axis the positive half has transverse \(x_k>0\), so
the oriented seam coordinate \(v\) decreases along the seam leaving \(i\);
the boundary of the deleted disk runs from this upper endpoint into
the positive half. On the double angle cone chart \(w\) the angle is
\(u_2/2\). The lifted equivariant section angle in the corner chart
increases by \(3/2\) turns per positive base turn and is odd across
\(u_2=0\). It therefore has angles \(0,3/4\) at the endpoints, and
can be straightened to \(3u_2/2\) with both endpoints fixed. With the
local action \(r,\tau\) above, smooth solid-torus coordinates before
odd quotient are \((w e^{2\pi\mathrm i\phi},e^{4\pi\mathrm i\phi})\).
Their two boundary angles are
\[
(u_2,\theta)\longmapsto (2u_2+\theta,3u_2+2\theta).
\]
This is the attaching map, including the signs and endpoint labels.
The involution conjugates both smooth coordinates. Its two fixed arcs
each join the two fibre fixed heights at one base endpoint, because
the second smooth angle is \(0\) at the upper endpoint and \(1/2\)
at the lower endpoint.

On the boundary quotient the group is generated by the integral
translations \(d_2,f\) and inversion by any fixed-point half-turn,
using the universal cover \(\mathbb R^2\) of the boundary orbifold.
The corner-piece group is the corresponding infinite dihedral group
on the universal cover \(D^2\times\mathbb R\). The map on translations
is \((m,n)\mapsto3m+2n\), surjective, with kernel generated by \((2,-3)\).
In the common front face \(Y>0\) the positive half of the boundary
pillowcase is the open rectangle \(0<u_2<1/2,0<\theta<1/2\).
It lies in \(Y>0\) because the sign of \(Y\) for \(x>0\) is the sign of
\(sin(2\pi\theta)\). Choose the four whiskers from this rectangle
and connect its interior to the front of the projection along the ball
boundary. The front boundary region is connected and these tracks
stay in it away from the strands. The four half-turns based through this
one face around \((0,0),(0,1/2),(1/2,0),(1/2,1/2)\) map to
\(b_{\rm top},c_{\rm top},b,c\), the upper and lower meridians of the
\(X=1,-1\) strands. Composing half-turns gives
\(d_2=b b_{\rm top}\), \(f=c_{\rm top}b_{\rm top}\).
Consequently the outer relation is \(d_2^2 f^{-3}=1\).
The common face is also why no individual strand conjugations appear
in these two translations.

## 4. Eight crossings and group comparison

For the projection calculation choose the \(W\)-plane circles of radii
\(R_F=5/2\) about \(0\), \(R_L=7/4\) about \(1\), with heights
\(v_F=Y\), \(v_L=-Y\). This deformation preserves the marked arrangement.
The original \(v_L=\sin2u\) has sign opposite to \(Y\) off the real
line, so the displayed choices preserve the relative \(v\)-heights at
the two intersections. The original and deformed loops meet the real
line in the same order, and there is exactly one mutual crossing in
each open half-plane. Cutting each half-plane along the arcs gives
disks with matching cyclic boundary order. Extending the marked
boundary maps over the disks gives a disk map fixed at \(\pm1\) and
near the outer boundary. For the isotopy, first interpolate the monotone
map of the real segment to the identity there, then use the relative
disk isotopy in each half-disk. The arc maps can be smoothed in crossing
charts before extension. The old strands stay at \(X=\pm1,Y=0\).

Project onto \((X,v)\) viewed from \(+Y\); the ambient orientation is
\((X,v,Y)\). The oriented tangents on the circles are proportional to
\((-v,X)\) for \(F\) and \((v,1-X)\) for \(L\), and the old strands
are locally oriented upwards. The \(F,L\) crossing pairs have
\(X=67/32\), \(v^2=1911/1024>0\). The \(F\) crossings with the
old strands have \(v^2=21/4\), and those of \(L\) with the \(+\) strand
have \(v^2=49/16\); \(L\) misses the \(-\) strand. At each of the eight
crossings the determinant of the ordered projected over and under
tangents is \(-|v|\). For the signs in the associated words, in local
coordinates with the over-arc pointing right, an under-path from
bottom to top and its two front whiskers give a counterclockwise turn
in the oriented normal plane of the over-arc. Thus the longitudinal
word takes the over-meridian to the determinant sign, and the outgoing
under-meridian is conjugated by its inverse.

Let \(F\) be the meridian immediately after the lower mutual crossing
on \(F\), and \(L\) the one immediately after the upper mutual crossing
on \(L\). Order the undercrossings by traversal. The \(F\) list is
\(c,b,L\), the \(L\) list is \(b_1,F\), the old \(+\) list is \(L,F\),
and the old \(-\) list is \(F\). Here \(b_1\) denotes the \(+\)
strand meridian between its two undercrossings. These lists and signs
follow from the rational crossing model and are checked in
[`e02_geometry_checks.py`](../numerics/e02_geometry_checks.py). There are
no self crossings on either ordinary knot. Following the transported
meridians and joining the end of each closed strand to its start gives
\[
\begin{gathered}
b_1=LbL^{-1},\quad b_{\rm top}=F b_1 F^{-1},\quad
c_{\rm top}=Fc F^{-1},\\
\lambda_F=c^{-1}b^{-1}L^{-1},\qquad
\lambda_L=b_1^{-1}F^{-1}.
\end{gathered}
\]
The crossing relations are the usual van Kampen relations from small
crossing balls and the front-face meridians, with
\([F,\lambda_F]=[L,\lambda_L]=1\) accounting for the two closed
ordinary strands. The two \(F\) cone disks in the even model are exchanged,
so downstairs one restores a single ordinary solid torus with meridian
\(3\mu_F-2\lambda_F\). The resulting presentation for the marked
orbifold exterior is
\[
\left\langle F,L,b,c\ \middle|\quad
 b^2=c^2=1,\ [F,\lambda_F]=[L,\lambda_L]=1,\
 (b b_{\rm top})^2(c_{\rm top}b_{\rm top})^{-3}=1,\
 F^3\lambda_F^{-2}=1\right\rangle.
\]
This is the **working project derivation**. It uses local cone gluing,
the explicit embedded quotient model, the normal winding, and a
same-face van Kampen calculation. Its words agree with E70 under the
displayed generator correspondence. The native slope adds
\(L^{s-1}\lambda_L=1\). In particular this is a presentation
identification before running any filling enumeration.

The independent ordinary and orbifold abelianizations above agree with
these words: in the orbifold exterior the latter give \(c=b\), \(2b=0\)
and \(3F+2L=0\) additively. Write \(L=3u,F=-2u\). Then
\(\mu=3u\), \(a=-b-u\) and \(\sigma=b\).
The parity-even peripheral lattice and the full covering longitude
are consistent with that abelianization. No general-\(d\) identification
or four-dimensional recognition theorem is used in this derivation.
