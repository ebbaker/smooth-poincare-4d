## Setup and perturbation sign

**Project theorem (working, setting and conventions).** Let $M$ be a closed, connected, oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere. Take $X=M\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ with the positive orientation on the projective-plane summand and the assumed smooth line $L_\infty$ oriented by the capped form $\bar\omega$. Set $H=\operatorname{PD}[L_\infty]$. Then $H^2[X]=1$, $H^2(X;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb ZH$, $b_1=0$, $b_2^+=1$, $b_2^-=0$, $\chi=3$, and $\sigma=1$. In particular $[\bar\omega]=aH$ with $a=\int_{L_\infty}\bar\omega>0$. Choose the homology orientation given by $H$ on $H^2_+$, and let $h_g$ be the self-dual harmonic representative of $H$ for any smooth Riemannian metric $g$. Thus $\|h_g\|_{L^2}=1$ in the [second master's normalization](../../../../master-2/research-overview/OVERVIEW.md#fixed-setting-and-conventions).

**Project theorem (working, wall sign check).** With $f_A=iF_A/(2\pi)$ representing $c_1(\det S^+)=kH$ and the closed perturbation $+i\mu$, a reducible has $f_A^+=-\mu/(2\pi)$. Testing against $h_g$ gives the wall equation $W_k(g,\mu)=0$, where

$$
W_k(g,\mu)=2\pi k+\int_X h_g\wedge\mu .
$$

The harmonic self-dual obstruction is the only obstruction to solving the reducible curvature equation. Denote the sides $W_k>0$ and $W_k<0$ by $C_+(k)$ and $C_-(k)$. A smooth self-dual perturbation on the closed manifold realizing the large-perturbation side is $\mu=-r(\bar\omega)^+_g+\mu_0$, where $\mu_0$ is fixed and $r\to+\infty$. The superscript can be dropped for a metric making $\bar\omega$ self-dual. The coefficient of $r$ in $W_k$ is $-\int_X h_g\wedge\bar\omega=-a<0$. This gives $C_-(k)$, in agreement with Gerig's (2.4). For a fixed class, a sufficiently small perturbation has the sign of $k$. The wall labels depend on the determinant convention and the chosen positive ray.

**Project theorem (working, long-neck vanishing).** For each odd $k$, the $c_1=kH$ unperturbed moduli space on $X$ is empty for all sufficiently long round necks in a family of metrics chosen as follows. Consequently the invariant with any permitted insertion vanishes in $C_{\operatorname{sgn}k}(k)$.

Take the compact truncation $P$ of the punctured positive-scalar-curvature projective plane from the preceding import. Use the fixed metric on $P$ and the round neck $[0,T]\times S^3_\rho$, $t=0$ on the $P$ end. On $M\setminus\operatorname{int}(B^4)$ use an arbitrary fixed metric with the matching product collar, prescribed through the gluing parametrization. Its scalar curvature has a finite lower bound that is uniform in $T$. The marked-line version uses a tubular disk bundle of the $+1$ sphere as $P$, since its oriented normal bundle has Euler number $+1$ and hence it is the standard disk bundle of a projective line. Only $P$ and the round neck are required to have positive scalar curvature.

In Kronheimer–Mrowka's spinor normalization set $u=|\Psi|^2$ and use $\Delta=d^*d$. Their Lemma 2 identity gives

$$
\Delta u+\tfrac12 s u+\tfrac12 u^2\le0 .
$$

The uniform lower bound on $s$ gives $0\le u\le B$ for a constant independent of $T$. With $s_\mathrm{neck}=6/\rho^2$ and $\lambda=\sqrt{s_\mathrm{neck}/2}$, the comparison function $B(e^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)})$ solves $(\Delta+s_\mathrm{neck}/2)v=0$ on the neck and is at least $B$ on both ends. The maximum principle bounds $u$ by this function, giving a uniform bound on the integral of $u^2$ over the neck. At $t=T/2$, $u\le2Be^{-\lambda T/2}$. The maximum principle on $P\cup([0,T/2]\times S^3_\rho)$, which has positive scalar curvature throughout, gives this bound on the whole truncated cap. In particular, $u\to0$ uniformly on each fixed compact subset of the complete cap $P_\infty$ as $T\to\infty$.

For $f_T=iF_{A_T}/(2\pi)$ the unperturbed curvature equation gives $|f_T^+|\le C u$. The fixed volumes of the two compact pieces and the neck estimate bound $\|f_T^+\|_{L^2}$ uniformly. Since

$$
\int_X f_T\wedge f_T=k^2=\|f_T^+\|_{L^2}^2-\|f_T^-\|_{L^2}^2 ,
$$

the full curvatures are uniformly $L^2$-bounded. If solutions existed for an unbounded sequence of $T$, restrict $f_T$ to the truncated cap and extend it by zero on the unused part of $P_\infty$. A subsequence converges weakly in $L^2(P_\infty)$ to a form $f_\infty$. On each fixed compact subset the curvatures are closed for large $T$, and their self-dual parts converge to zero there by the spinor estimate. Thus $df_\infty=0$ and $f_\infty^+=0$ distributionally. For a closed, compactly supported Thom form $\theta$ of the line in $P$, each $\int f_T\wedge\theta=k$, so $\int f_\infty\wedge\theta=k$.

The round cylindrical metric is conformal under $r=e^{-t/\rho}$ to the Euclidean metric on a punctured ball, since $r^2\rho^{-2}(dt^2+\rho^2g_{S^3})=dr^2+r^2g_{S^3}$. Take this conformal rescaling near infinity and a smooth positive interpolation on the interior to compactify $P_\infty$ to $\mathbb{CP}^2$. Both anti-self-duality and the middle-degree $L^2$ norm persist. For the closure equation across the added point, a cutoff across a radius-$\epsilon$ annulus has $\|d\chi_\epsilon\|_{L^2}=O(\epsilon)$, so its error paired with an $L^2$ two-form and any bounded smooth test form tends to zero. Anti-self-duality then implies coclosedness distributionally, and elliptic regularity gives a smooth closed anti-self-dual form on the closed compactification. Since that compactification has $b_2^-=0$, the form is zero, contradicting the pairing $k\ne0$. This excludes an unbounded sequence of neck lengths with solutions. At any sufficiently long fixed $T$, properness in Kronheimer–Mrowka's Lemma 5 gives emptiness for perturbations in a neighborhood of zero. Taking a generic perturbation in that neighborhood computes the invariant of the stated chamber. The argument allows $T$ and the perturbation scale to depend on $k$.

## Both chambers

**Project theorem (working, chamber calculation).** The Wu relation gives $w_2(X)=H\bmod 2$, and the absence of 2-torsion makes the odd integers $k$ parametrize the $\mathrm{Spin}^{c}$ structures uniquely. Their expected real dimensions are $d(k)=(k^2-9)/4$. When $d(k)\ge0$, the invariant is evaluated on $U^{m}$, $m=d(k)/2=(k^2-9)/8$, in degree $d(k)$. There are no degree-one insertions since $H_1(X;\mathbb Z)=0$. Integer multiples of $U^m$ act by linearity, and $U^0=1$ for the zero-dimensional count. For $d(k)<0$, the generic moduli space is empty. The following values are mod two, with homology orientation and wall labels as fixed above.

| Determinant $kH$ | Permitted top-degree insertion | $C_+(k)$ | $C_-(k)$ (Taubes) |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| Odd $k\ge3$ | $U^{(k^2-9)/8}$ | 0 | 1 |
| $k=\pm1$ | Negative expected dimension | 0 | 0 |
| Odd $k\le-3$ | $U^{(k^2-9)/8}$ | 1 | 0 |

The vanishing entries also vanish over $\mathbb Z$, and before mod-two reduction each nonzero entry has magnitude one by Li–Liu. On the standard $\mathbb{CP}^2$, Fubini–Study positive scalar curvature and Lemma 2 give small-perturbation vanishing, so the same wall-crossing computation gives the same table. The chamber agreement expected in proposal §3.6 follows at this evidence level. For $k=3$ the Taubes entry is the zero-dimensional count. For $k=5$ it uses $U^2$, two point insertions.
