## Relative application to the punctured sphere

**Project theorem (working, conditional on the cap for the parity assertion).** Let $X=M\setminus\{p\}$ for a closed connected oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere $M$. Suppose $\omega$ is smooth, near-symplectic, and equal to a standard constant positive symplectic form on the end. If it has zeros, a finite concatenation of Perutz cobordisms, supported in a compact subset of $X$, changes its zero set to one circle. Assume the cap adds a symplectic neighborhood of a line $L_\infty$ of square $+1$, giving $Y=M\#\mathbb{CP}^2$. The final circle is odd, and the same cobordism can be taken fixed near $L_\infty$.

Indeed, the zero set is a closed one-manifold contained in a compact core, hence is a finite union of circles. In the interior of a connected compact truncation containing the zeros, choose two short zero arcs on distinct circles as long as at least two remain. A connecting embedded path with interior disjoint from the zero set exists by general position in dimension four. A ball neighborhood of the letter H can be taken with compact closure in that interior. Apply Theorem 1.4(2) there with the local choices just recorded. The path is stationary off the ball, and successive stages can be reparametrized to be stationary near their times of joining. The support of the concatenation is in a finite union of compact balls. If the cap is already fixed, use the truncation in its complement, with a collar between the balls and the cut. The path then extends by the original cap form. [Perutz, Theorem 1.4 and §§3–4](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0601320v2)

The capped manifold has $b_1=0$ and intersection form $(+1)$, so $b_2^+=1$ and $b_2^-=0$. The endpoint is near-symplectic on the cap because it agrees there with the fixed symplectic form. The number of even circles is $0$ modulo two by Theorem 1.8. Since only one circle remains, its $L^-$ is nonorientable. The same reasoning applies to any single transverse zero circle of a standard-ended form with this positive cap, independently of how the circle was obtained. Theorem 1.8 is applied on the capped closed four-manifold. [Perutz, Theorem 1.8 and Proposition 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0601320v2)

**Project theorem (working, endpoint variant).** Theorem 1.4 reaches any prescribed positive count from a form with a nonempty zero set by splitting or merging. Taubes's Proposition 2.1 reaches a positive count from a zero-free initial form by finitely many supported births, with the end and cap fixed, and can also be used to increase a positive count. Each ball-supported difference of closed endpoint forms is exact by $H_c^2(\mathbb R^4)=0$, so the cap cohomology class stays fixed. The moves act on closed forms on the fixed smooth manifold. Perutz's Lemma 2.1 supplies a realizing conformal structure for a near-symplectic endpoint. A constraint prescribing the same conformal class throughout would require a separate argument. [Perutz, Theorem 1.4 and Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0601320v2) · [Taubes, Proposition 2.1](https://intlpress.com/api/bgcloud-front/resource/pdf/volume/1806605523900329985-1806605523900329985-9fa5106dca972fdc95f65c34eac2bf03.pdf)

**Boundary of the conclusion.** Perutz's index-one move needs two arcs and has positive zero count at both endpoints. Its decrease starts at $n\ge2$. Reversing the local birth model gives an index-two death on its domain and requires a matching exterior to implement it for a given form. Reversing a specifically constructed relative birth at the level of endpoints recovers its original symplectic local form. For a given twisted circle the required extension across a death remains open in this packet. Closed parity permits both one odd circle and zero circles. Perutz states the remaining question explicitly after Remark 1.9, p. 241. The cap-relative $1\to0$ problem goes to LINE-B02. [Perutz, Theorem 1.4, §2.1 and Remark 1.9](https://arxiv.org/html/math/0601320v2)
