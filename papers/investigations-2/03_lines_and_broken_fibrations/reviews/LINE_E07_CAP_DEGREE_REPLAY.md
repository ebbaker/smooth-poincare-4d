## Fixed sign conventions and the cap argument

Set $H=[L_\infty]$, $h=\operatorname{PD}_Y H$, and $c_1(\det S^+)=kh$. The ordinary topology gives $h^2[Y]=1$, $(\chi,\sigma)=(3,1)$ and $w_2(Y)=h\bmod2$. Thus $k$ is odd, and absence of $2$-torsion gives a unique spin$^c$ structure for each $k$. With $g$ the adapted metric and $h_g$ the harmonic representative of $h$, $h_g$ is self-dual, $\|h_g\|_{L^2}=1$, and $\bar\omega=a h_g$ where $a=\bar\omega[H]>0$. For a perturbation $+i\mu$ in the determinant equation, $iF_A/(2\pi)$ represents $c_1(\det S^+)$. At a reducible, $(iF_A/(2\pi))^+=-\mu/(2\pi)$, so the wall function is $W_k=2\pi k+\int_Y h_g\wedge\mu$. Taubes's ray for $\epsilon\bar\omega$ has $\mu=-r\epsilon\bar\omega/4$, making $W_k=2\pi k-r\epsilon a/4$. It selects $C_-$ for $\epsilon=+1$ and $C_+$ for $\epsilon=-1$, with the positive generator kept fixed. A nonzero invariant gives solutions for generic nearby perturbations on the same side of the wall. Properness at fixed metric then gives a solution at the exact perturbation for each sufficiently large $r$, and rescaling the spinor gives (2.9).

Choose the metric on a marked neighborhood of $L_\infty$ to be the Kähler metric of the scaled Fubini–Study form. Its norm on the Kähler form is $\sqrt2$ in the unit-wedge convention, so Taubes's $J_\epsilon=\sqrt2g^{-1}(\epsilon\bar\omega)/|\bar\omega|_g$ is $\epsilon J_{\rm std}$ there. In complex dimension two $-J_{\rm std}$ has the same ambient orientation and reverses the complex orientations of both the tangent and the normal lines to each projective line. Over the positively oriented line $c_1(K_+)[H]=-3$, and passing to $-J_{\rm std}$ conjugates the canonical bundle. Thus $c_1(K_\epsilon)[\epsilon H]=-3$ in either case. For the weighted relative cycle $T$ from Taubes, $2[T]=\operatorname{PD}(c_1(\det S^+\otimes K_\epsilon))$ on the complement in the sense of Theorem 2.2, since $E^{\otimes2}=(\det S^+)\otimes K_\epsilon$ there. Pairing with the oriented line gives $2T\cdot(\epsilon L_\infty)=\epsilon k-3$.

The pairing can be computed on a small projective-line displacement. A whole closed neighborhood of the line lies in the integrable cap and misses $Z$, so a compact set there contains every sufficiently small displacement. Properness makes its inverse image compact in the normalization, with only finitely many components meeting the compact set. If a current branch is shared with a displaced line, the identity theorem extends that branch along its normalization into that line, since the line is closed and the intersection condition is analytic near every point on it. The shared branch therefore comes from a line component. A displacement avoiding those finitely many components has isolated intersections, with positive multiplicities computed by the order of the defining equation along each branch. For $-J_{\rm std}$ this same argument uses conjugate complex coordinates, with the conjugate orientation on both curves. The relative pairing follows from a Thom class supported in the complement of $Z$. This establishes nonnegativity including when the original line is itself a current component. Ends that tend to $Z$ stay out of this compact intersection calculation.

It follows that $C_-$ vanishes for $k<3$ and $C_+$ vanishes for $k>-3$. The formal dimension is $(k^2-9)/4$, with the absence of degree-one insertions since $b_1=0$. For $|k|\ge3$, Li–Liu gives the other entry magnitude one on $U^{(k^2-9)/8}$. For $k=\pm1$ the dimension is negative, and both entries vanish.

## Independent neck comparison

Use a fixed positive-scalar-curvature punctured projective-plane metric with a round product end, a fixed metric on the other punctured summand with matching collar, and a neck of length $T$. The core metrics and cylinder radius $\rho$ are independent of $T$. For $u=|\Psi|^2$ and $\Delta=d^*d$, the unperturbed identity of Kronheimer–Mrowka gives $\Delta u+su/2+u^2/2\le0$ pointwise. At a global maximum, $u\le B$ for a constant from the uniform scalar lower bound. On the round neck the comparison function $B(e^{-\lambda t}+e^{-\lambda(T-t)})$, $\lambda=\sqrt{3}/\rho$, solves the linear equation $(\Delta+3/\rho^2)v=0$. It dominates the two boundary values of $u$ and gives a uniform bound for $\int_{\rm neck}u^2$. Applying the maximum principle on the cap truncated at the midpoint bounds $u$ there by $2Be^{-\lambda T/2}$, using positive scalar curvature on the entire truncated cap.

For $f_T=iF_{A_T}/(2\pi)$ the curvature equation bounds $|f_T^+|$ by a constant times $u$. The global curvature $L^2$ bound follows from the bound on $\|f_T^+\|_2$ and $k^2=\|f_T^+\|_2^2-\|f_T^-\|_2^2$. On the complete projective side use a weak $L^2$ limit along a hypothetical unbounded sequence of solution lengths. Extending the restrictions by zero beyond the truncated interval only defines them in a common $L^2$ space. For each compactly supported test form, the moving boundary is eventually outside its support, so the limit is distributionally closed. The cap spinor estimate makes the limit anti-self-dual. A radius-$\delta$ cutoff has derivative with $L^2$ norm $O(\delta)$, which proves distributional closedness over the added point when tested against bounded smooth forms. ## Degree and tube consequences

- **Project theorem, conditional on the period lemma for the component and area assertions.** At $k=3$ for $\bar\omega$, the relative degree is zero. A component equal to $L_\infty$ would contribute positively against a displacement, so it is absent. Without a shared component, an intersection with the original line would also contribute positively. Properness implies the support is closed off $Z$, and $L_\infty$ is compact and disjoint from $Z$. Thus the closure of the support in $Y$ misses an open neighborhood of $L_\infty$ and lies in a compact subset of $V=Y\setminus L_\infty$. Ends may still accumulate at $Z$. For one zero circle, the period lemma gives $\int_S\bar\omega=n_S P>0$ for each component, $P=\int_Z\alpha$ with $d\alpha=\bar\omega$ on $V$. Total meridional degree one then forces one component of weight one and $|n_S|=1$, with area $|P|$. This is algebraic boundary degree and allows several geometric ends, singularities and positive genus.
- **Project theorem, class comparison.** At $k=5$ the degree is one. The relative boundary for $k=3$ and $k=5$ is the same generator for the same sign of $\bar\omega$. The sequence $0\to\mathbb Z H\to H_2(Y,Z;\mathbb Z)\to H_1(Z;\mathbb Z)\to0$ shows the classes differ by $H$, since $H$ pairs to $1$ with the line. For the same fixed $J$, the degree-zero current and $L_\infty$ have disjoint supports and their sum is a positive weighted degree-one representative. This verifies precisely the relative class and meridional constraints. The two $U$ insertions establish nonvanishing of a closed count. The checked Taubes theorem yields a current and its relative class, with no assertion that it is this disconnected representative or that it meets two prescribed points. The original circle is contractible and twisted, outside the Gerig incidence theorem scope identified by E01.
- **Project theorem, small tube.** Let $D^3$ be one sufficiently small transverse disk, trivialize $\Lambda_g^+$ over it and keep the full ambient form fixed along $\partial D^3$. The Taylor expansion of $\bar\omega$ restricted in this trivialization is an invertible linear section plus $O(\rho^2)$. Its unit direction on the boundary has degree $\pm1$ by a straight-line homotopy through nonzero maps there. A smooth positive nondegenerate replacement $\eta$ has $|\eta^+|> |\eta^-|$ pointwise in the fixed metric, so its normalized self-dual projection extends the same boundary direction over $D^3$. This contradicts the nonzero degree even if the replacement is adapted to another metric. Equality of only the pulled-back two-forms on the boundary sphere would lack the needed ambient data.
