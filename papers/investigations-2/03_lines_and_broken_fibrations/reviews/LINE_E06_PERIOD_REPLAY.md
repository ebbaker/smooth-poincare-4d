**Project theorem, replayed.** In the oriented smooth Riemannian setting of the note, let \(V=Y\setminus L_\infty\) and let \(Z\subset V\) be the one transverse zero circle of \(\bar\omega\). For a nonconstant, boundaryless \(J\)-holomorphic normalization \(u:S\to V\setminus Z\) with the stated properness, compact image closure in \(V\), and finite \(\bar\omega\)-area, the area is \(n_S P>0\), where \(P=\int_Z\alpha\) for \(d\alpha=\bar\omega\), and \(n_S\) is the signed meridional degree. A positive integer weighted current of this type, with the same support condition and total meridional degree of absolute value one, has one irreducible component of weight one and area \(|P|\).

## Compact truncations, degree and sign

The cap identifies \(V\) diffeomorphically with the homotopy sphere minus a point. Excision for a small coordinate ball and the pair sequence give \(H^1(V;\mathbb R)=H^2(V;\mathbb R)=0\). A primitive \(\alpha\) exists and any two differ by an exact form, so \(P\) depends only on the oriented circle and \(\bar\omega\). Choose a tube \(N\to Z\), radius \(\rho\), and a positive angular form \(dt\) of period one on \(Z\).

If \(K\subset V\) is compact and contains the image closure, the exterior truncation at radius \(r>0\) has preimage contained in the preimage of \(K\) minus the open radius-\(r\) tube. That target set is compact in \(V\setminus Z\). Properness makes the truncation compact. At a regular level its boundary is a compact smooth one-manifold, hence a finite union of circles. Orient it as the boundary of the exterior truncation. The integer \(n_S=\int_{\partial S_{\ge r}}u^*\pi^*dt\) is the degree of its projection to \(Z\). Stokes on the compact inverse image between two regular radii, applied to the closed angular form, proves independence of the level. The empty boundary gives degree zero by the same definition.

Orient the rank-three normal bundle by \(TZ\oplus\nu=TV|_Z\) and the meridian by the outward boundary orientation in a normal fiber. In ambient oriented coordinates the order is \((t,\rho,\text{oriented meridian})\). At a transverse intersection take a positive tangent basis \((a,v)\) on \(S\), with \(a\) pointing out of the exterior truncation and \(v\) tangent to its oriented boundary. Then \(d\rho(du(a))<0\) and \(d\rho(du(v))=0\). The intersection sign against the oriented meridian is the sign of \(dt(du(v))\), agreeing with the projection degree at a regular base point. Thus \(n_S\) is the signed intersection for this convention. Reversing the orientation of \(Z\) changes both \(n_S\) and \(P\) by a sign. Properness also gives finite intersections on a transverse compact meridian.

## Cutoff and limit

The normal contraction \(H(s,t,y)=(t,sy)\) gives the smooth primitive \(b=\int_0^1\iota_{\partial_s}H^*\bar\omega\,ds\) on the tube. The homotopy formula gives \(db=\bar\omega\) since the endpoint at \(s=0\) has one-dimensional image. The contracting velocity has size \(O(\rho)\), the form at the contracted point has norm \(O(s\rho)\), and the other derivative is bounded. Hence \(|b|_g\le C\rho^2\). The rank-three normal derivative at the transverse zero has a uniformly positive least singular value on the compact circle, which yields \(c\rho\le |\bar\omega|_g\le C\rho\) on a smaller tube. The retraction to \(Z\) now gives \(\alpha=b+P\pi^*dt+df\) there.

Let \(\chi_\epsilon\) be the increasing radial cutoff of the note. The pullback of \(\chi_\epsilon\alpha\) has compact support by the same compact-set argument as for truncations. Consequently,

\[
\int_S u^*(\chi_\epsilon\bar\omega)
=-\int_S u^*(d\chi_\epsilon\wedge b)
 -P\int_S u^*(d\chi_\epsilon\wedge\pi^*dt).
\]

The \(df\) term vanishes as the derivative of \(-f\,d\chi_\epsilon\), supported on the compact inverse image of the cutoff annulus. On that annulus the comass of \(d\chi_\epsilon\wedge b\) is bounded by \(C\rho^2/\epsilon\le C'\rho\). The adapted-metric identity \(u^*\bar\omega=(|\bar\omega|_g\circ u)/\sqrt2\,dA_{u^*g}\) gives an error bounded by a constant times the \(\bar\omega\)-area of the annulus. The identity and bound extend through critical points using the pullback area density. Finite area makes this error tend to zero. The left side tends to the total area.

For the angular term, choose regular levels \(r_-<\epsilon<2\epsilon<r_+\) in the tube. On the compact inverse image of \([r_-,r_+]\), apply Stokes to \(\chi_\epsilon\pi^*dt\). The inner boundary contributes zero. The outer boundary of this annulus has the opposite orientation to the boundary of \(S_{\ge r_+}\), so the integral of \(d\chi_\epsilon\wedge\pi^*dt\) is \(-n_S\). This proves the formula with no information about individual ends at \(Z\). Area is positive at an immersion point of the nonconstant map, and therefore \(P\) and \(n_S\) are nonzero.

## Local control of the component count

Fix a single cutoff and consider the closed compactly supported two-form \(\eta=-d\chi\wedge\pi^*dt\) on \(V\setminus Z\), extended by zero outside the tube. It represents a Thom form of an oriented meridian in compactly supported cohomology. Indeed, replace \(dt\) by a form of integral one supported in a short base interval. The change is exact on \(Z\) and changes \(\eta\) by a compactly supported exact form. In the resulting local product the normal-coordinate form is \(dt\wedge d\chi\) with integral one in the \((t,\rho)\) orientation. The annular argument computes \(\int_Su^*\eta=n_S\) for every normalization, independently of whether a fixed radius is regular for all of them.

For \(T=\sum_S m_S u_*[S]\), with positive integer weights and locally finite mass, the sum of evaluations on \(\eta\) is absolutely convergent. Alternatively, on its fixed compact support choose a nonnegative smooth compactly supported bump \(\lambda\) equal to one there. Since \(|\bar\omega|_g\) has a positive lower bound there, \(\lvert \int_S u^*\eta\rvert\le C\int_S u^*(\lambda\bar\omega)\). The sum of the latter with weights is finite by local mass and positivity. Every term \(m_S n_S\) has the sign of \(P\) and absolute value at least one, so only finitely many components can occur. If \(\lvert T(\eta)\rvert=1\), there is one term, with \(m_S=|n_S|=1\). This verifies the count without assuming a finite weighted total area. The count gives no bound on the number of geometric ends or the genus.
