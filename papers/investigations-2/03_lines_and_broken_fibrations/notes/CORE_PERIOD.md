# A period test for components in the punctured core

Input is the [marked cap](LINE_B01_MARKED_CAP.md). **Project theorem, replayed.** The [independent reading](../reviews/LINE_E06_PERIOD_REPLAY.md) verifies the sign with the exterior orientation, uses a fixed annular test form for local finiteness, and compares the shrinking cutoff with Taubes, *Moduli spaces and Fredholm theory*, proof of Proposition 2.2, §3(c), p. 289. This calculation is conditional on a given geometric current; no curve-existence statement is used in its proof.

Let \(V=Y\setminus L_\infty\) in the marked construction. The open radial collar identifies \(V\), as a smooth manifold, with the original punctured sphere (stretch its finite radial interval to an infinite one). The form on \(V\) need not be standard at Euclidean infinity under this identification. \(H^1(V;\mathbb R)=H^2(V;\mathbb R)=0\), so choose \(d\alpha=\bar\omega\); its period around a fixed oriented loop is independent of the primitive. Assume there is precisely one transverse zero circle \(Z\). Use an adapted smooth Riemannian metric and its compatible almost complex structure \(J\) off \(Z\), so that on every \(J\)-complex line, \(\bar\omega=(|\bar\omega|/\sqrt2)\,dA_g\) with the complex orientation.

Let \(S\) be the nonconstant normalization of one irreducible component, properly parametrized without boundary in \(V\setminus Z\), and with finite \(\bar\omega\)-area. Assume its closure is contained in a compact subset of \(V\) (ends can tend only to \(Z\)). Fix a tube projection \(\pi:N\to Z\), an angular one-form \(dt\) with integral \(1\) on the chosen orientation of \(Z\), and a smooth tubular radius \(\rho\) off \(Z\). For small regular values \(r\) of \(\rho|_S\), the boundary of the truncated normalization \(S_{\rho\ge r}\), counted with induced orientation and pushed down to \(Z\), has an integer degree \(n_S\). This degree is constant between any two regular levels by Stokes on the compact inverse image of their annulus, since \(\pi^*dt\) is closed. This works with any finite number of branches at a regular level and requires no boundary regularity at \(Z\). It is the boundary coefficient of the component, equivalently the signed intersection with a meridian sphere, up to one common orientation sign. To see this last identification, cut along \(\rho=r\): the normal sphere bundle over a point of \(Z\) meets the boundary loops with total local degree of their projection. Tangencies may be avoided by choosing a regular base point, without changing the degree.

**Project theorem.** Put \(P=\int_Z\alpha\). Then
\[
                         \int_S\bar\omega = n_S P>0.
\]
Indeed, shrink normal fibers by the maps \(h_u(t,y)=(t,uy)\). The radial homotopy primitive \(b=\int_0^1 h_u^*(\iota_{\partial_u h_u}\bar\omega)\,du\), with contraction understood along \(h_u\), satisfies \(db=\bar\omega\) on \(N\) since the pullback from \(Z\) vanishes. In a bounded tube \(|b|\le C\rho^2\): the velocity is \(O(\rho)\), \(\bar\omega(t,uy)=O(u\rho)\), and the remaining tangent factor is bounded. Transversality gives \(c\rho\le|\bar\omega|\le C\rho\). Since \(N\) retracts to \(Z\), on \(N\) one has \(\alpha=b+P\,\pi^*dt+df\) for a smooth real \(f\).

Let \(\chi_\epsilon\) increase from zero for \(\rho\le\epsilon\) to one for \(\rho\ge2\epsilon\), with \(|d\chi_\epsilon|\le C/\epsilon\). Extend it by one outside \(N\). On the normalization \(\chi_\epsilon\alpha\) has compact support by the closure and properness assumptions, and integration gives \(\int_S\chi_\epsilon\bar\omega=-\int_S d\chi_\epsilon\wedge\alpha\). The \(df\) term integrates to zero (the derivative of a compactly supported form on this annulus). The \(b\) term is bounded by \(C\int_{S\cap\{\epsilon<\rho<2\epsilon\}}\bar\omega\), which tends to zero by finite energy. The remaining term is \(n_S P\): integrate the derivative of the radial cutoff over its regular slices, using the boundary orientation of the *exterior* truncation. The coarea identity ignores the critical levels; equivalently use a primitive of the cutoff derivative on compact annuli and ordinary Stokes. The left side converges to the area. Its strict positivity follows from the nonconstant \(J\)-holomorphic parametrization.

**Project theorem, consequence.** Suppose a positive integral current of this kind has total meridional intersection \(1\) and support closure compact in \(V\). Each of its irreducible components has area \(>0\), hence \(P\ne0\), and all \(n_S\) are nonzero integers of the same sign. With the common choice of boundary orientation their weighted sum is \(1\) or \(-1\). This forces exactly one component, with multiplicity one and \(|n_S|=1\). The sum may also be evaluated on a fixed transverse meridian in the complement of \(Z\), where the proper normalization gives a compact, finite intersection. Alternatively apply the cutoff identity to the full current; a finite total weighted area bounds the number of components by its ratio to \(|P|\). Even without an a priori bound on the weighted total, the fixed meridional class and local finiteness suffice. Its area is \(|P|\). These conclusions bound total algebraic degree and count global components; several geometric ends, singularities, genus and distinct possible currents for the same data are still allowed.
