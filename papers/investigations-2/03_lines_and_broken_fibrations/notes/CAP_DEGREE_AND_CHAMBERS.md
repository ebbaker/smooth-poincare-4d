A nonzero SW invariant in the negative-imaginary large-perturbation chamber supplies the solutions. **Project theorem.** Suppose B01 and choose the adapted metric equal to the scaled Fubini–Study metric on an open neighborhood of \(L_\infty\). Let \(h={\rm PD}[L_\infty]\), \(c_1(\det S^+)=kh\), \(k\) odd. For \(\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\) apply the curve implication to \(\epsilon\bar\omega\), with the same metric. Its \(J_\epsilon\) near the cap is \(J_{\rm std}\) or \(-J_{\rm std}\); both are integrable and preserve the family of projective lines. Give the line the orientation of \(\epsilon\bar\omega\); it represents \(\epsilon[L_\infty]\), has square \(+1\), and \(K_\epsilon\) evaluates there to \(-(2+1)=-3\) by its tangent and normal complex lines. Thus the meridionally prescribed positive current \(T\) must satisfy
\[
  T\cdot (\epsilon L_\infty)=(\epsilon k-3)/2 .
\]
The left side is nonnegative. Indeed all sufficiently small projective line displacements lie entirely in the marked integrable cap. Their union is contained in a compact subset off the zeros. The preimage of that compact set is compact in the proper normalization and meets only finitely many connected components. A branch shared with a line analytically continues as a line component since its image stays in that compact line. Choose a displacement different from those finitely many lines. Its intersections, confined to a compact set off the zeros, have positive complex multiplicity. This follows in a holomorphic chart from the vanishing order of the defining linear equation pulled back to a nonconstant branch. For \(-J_{\rm std}\) use conjugate coordinates on both factors. The intersection agrees with the relative pairing because the displaced line and its isotopy miss the zero set.

Write \(C_-\) for the chamber of the large negative-imaginary perturbation in the ray of \(\bar\omega\), \(C_+\) for the opposite side, with the positive harmonic generator always fixed by \(h\). The two choices of sign in the preceding test yield \(SW_{C_-}(k)=0\) for \(k<3\), and \(SW_{C_+}(k)=0\) for \(k>-3\). They refer to the same smooth oriented four-manifold; changing \(J\) to \(-J\) in complex dimension two preserves the ambient orientation. A change of homology orientation only changes the overall integer sign and is immaterial to vanishing and mod-two values. The other entry has magnitude one for \(|k|\ge3\) by wall crossing, since \(d=(k^2-9)/4\). Both entries vanish for \(k=\pm1\). In particular \(k=3\) and \(k=5\) give nonzero negative-chamber invariants, evaluated on \(1\) and \(U^2\), respectively. This proof uses the marked transverse capped form; the long-neck proof of E02 does not need it for the chamber table.

**Project theorem, degree zero.** At \(k=3\) Taubes supplies a current \(T_0\) with intersection zero with \(L_\infty\). A component equal to \(L_\infty\) would contribute positive intersection with a displaced line. Once that case is excluded, any intersection with \(L_\infty\) would have positive local multiplicity. Hence the support misses \(L_\infty\). Properness off the zeros and compactness of both \(Y\) and the line give a full neighborhood of the line disjoint from the support. For one transverse zero circle the [period calculation](CORE_PERIOD.md) therefore applies to every irreducible component. The conclusion is a single global irreducible component, with weight one, unit *algebraic* boundary degree, and energy \(\big|\int_Z\alpha\big|\) for \(d\alpha=\bar\omega\) on \(Y\setminus L_\infty\). This conclusion only controls the neighborhood of \(L_\infty\); it allows the curve to enter the outer part of the retained core or the affine part of the cap.

**Project theorem, degree one.** At \(k=5\) the relative degree of the entire current is one. In relative homology the prescribed class can already be represented by \(T_0+L_\infty\), a disconnected positive \(J\)-holomorphic current with the required meridional degree. To check the class, the exact sequence of \((Y,Z)\) has kernel \(\mathbb Z[L_\infty]\) for the boundary map, and addition of \([L_\infty]\) raises its cap intersection by one. This construction tests only class, linking, and positivity. It does not assert that the disconnected current is an SW limit or obeys specified incidence constraints. The two insertions in the closed \(k=5\) invariant supply no two-point incidence theorem for this original contractible twisted zero under the checked imports.

**Project theorem, obstruction to support in a normal tube.** Let \(D^3\) be a sufficiently small transverse normal disk at a zero, with \(S^2=\partial D^3\). Trivialize the original \(\Lambda^+\) over \(D^3\). The unit direction of \(\bar\omega\) on \(S^2\) has degree \(\pm1\), by homotopy to the invertible normal derivative. A positive nondegenerate replacement agreeing on this sphere as an ambient two-form would project to a nowhere-zero section of that same \(\Lambda^+\) on \(D^3\), since positive square means \(|\eta^+|>|\eta^-|\). Its unit boundary direction would extend over the disk, forcing degree zero. This excludes removal by a modification confined to a small normal tube with its boundary germ fixed, even allowing a changed metric. It allows a modification on a larger region containing a spanning surface and changing the former meridians. Agreement of the pullbacks as tangential two-forms on \(S^2\) alone is weaker and is not used.
