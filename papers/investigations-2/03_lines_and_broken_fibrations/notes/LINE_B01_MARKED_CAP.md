## Statement and end straightening — project theorem

Let \(M\) be an oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere, \(X=M-\{p\}\), and let \(\omega\) be a smooth closed positive symplectic form off a compact transverse near-symplectic zero locus. Here transverse means the normal differential maps isomorphically onto a positive three-plane for the wedge pairing at each zero. In particular this applies to a closed self-dual form with transverse zeros for a smooth Riemannian metric. Assume a marked inverted smooth coordinate ball at \(p\) in which \(\omega\) is a nonzero constant form \(\omega_0\) near infinity, positive for the orientation of \(X\). Alternatively assume \(\omega=\omega_0+\beta\) there with \(|\beta|_{\mathrm{Eucl}}=O(r^{-2})\) and with \(\omega_0\) a nonzero self-dual form for the limiting oriented Euclidean metric. These are data of the statement. The growth bound in the definition of a pole and transversality for arbitrary pole forms have separate owners.

In the decaying case one can straighten the end, leaving the zero germs intact. Here is a primitive estimate that only needs zeroth-order decay. Write \(\beta=dr\wedge\gamma_r+\delta_r\) on \((a,\infty)\times S^3\), with angular forms measured on the unit sphere. Then \(|\gamma_r|=O(1/r)\), \(\partial_r\delta_r=d_{S^3}\gamma_r\), \(d_{S^3}\delta_r=0\). Fix a smooth primitive \(u\) of \(\delta_{r_0}\) on one slice (\(H^2_{\rm dR}(S^3)=0\)). The tangential form
\[
 b(r)=u+\int_{r_0}^r\gamma_s\,ds
\]
has \(db=\beta\) and Euclidean norm \(O((1+\log r)/r)\). With a radial cutoff equal to one before \(R\) and zero after \(2R\), use \(\omega_0+d(\chi b)\) on the end. On the annulus its distance from \(\omega_0\) is \(O((1+\log R)/R^2)\); the cutoff derivative is \(O(R^{-1})\). Positivity holds for large \(R\), also along the linear interpolation of cutoffs. This operation can change the self-duality equation for the original conformal class. Nothing in the cap argument needs that equation on the altered end.

## Smooth cut and its marking — project theorem

Choose a linear isometry and scale of the end sending the constant self-dual form to \(\omega_{\rm st}=\sum du_j\wedge dv_j\) in the resulting \(z_j=u_j+iv_j\) coordinates. For a general constant positive symplectic form a real linear symplectic coordinate identification works, with ellipsoids in the original chart. In the pole case the raw inversion \(y\mapsto y/|y|^2\) reverses orientation. With \(y\) positive on \(M\), the raw coefficient is anti-self-dual for the coordinate orientation; the change to complex coordinates includes a reflection. The complex orientation is then the orientation of \(X\).

Pick \(s\) with the end already standard on a collar of and beyond \(|z|=s\), and a cut radius \(R_c>s\). Retain the interior. On the model ball of radius \(R_c\), the map
\[
 z\longmapsto [z_1:z_2:\sqrt{R_c^2-|z|^2}]
\]
identifies the interior with the affine chart of \(\mathbb{CP}^2\). At \(|z|=R_c\) it collapses precisely the Hopf circles to the line \(L_\infty=\{Z_3=0\}\). Smoothness at the line is defined by the projective charts, not by differentiating the square root as a function on the closed ball. Equivalently the cut is the diagonal circle quotient of \(\{|z|^2+|w|^2=R_c^2\}\subset\mathbb C^3\). Where \(w\ne0\), the section \(w>0\) pulls the product form back to \(\omega_{\rm st}\). This is the interior choice in Lerman §1.1 (import below).

In affine coordinates \(v=z/\sqrt{R_c^2-|z|^2}\), the potential one-form for the cut form is
\[
 \frac{R_c^2}{2}\frac{\operatorname{Im}\sum\bar v_j\,dv_j}{1+|v|^2}.
\]
Its derivative is \(R_c^2(i/2)\partial\bar\partial\log(1+|v|^2)\), a positive form extending over the line. The potentials in different projective charts differ at the level of their logarithms by a log squared modulus of a nowhere-zero holomorphic coordinate, whose mixed derivative is zero. On a projective line the area is \(\int_{\mathbb C} R_c^2(1+|v|^2)^{-2}\,du\,dv=\pi R_c^2\). Intersecting \(Z_3=0\) with the nearby complex line \(Z_3=\epsilon Z_1\) gives one transverse positive intersection. Thus \(L_\infty^2=+1\).

Glue the part of this projective model with \(|z|\ge s\) to the original core along a common open collar where the forms coincide. Both omitted open balls are coordinate balls: one contains \(p\) in \(M\), the other is \(|v|<s/\sqrt{R_c^2-s^2}\) in the affine chart. In the pole normalization the sphere identification from the positive \(y\) coordinates to affine \(v\) is a reflection composed with an orthogonal rotation and positive constant rescalings; the radial parameter reverses across the two punctures. For a general linear normalization the linking surfaces are linear images followed by inversion and are star-shaped, hence have radial coordinate collars; the induced angular map is the normalized linear map and its orientation reversal extends over a coordinate ball (interpolate the positive symmetric part to the identity on a collar). Explicitly, writing the normalization as A, the omitted ball in y has radial boundary |Aθ|/s. Interpolate its radius from a small constant near the center to this function near the boundary. For A=QP in polar decomposition, interpolation of P to the identity gives a smooth extension which equals the orthogonal reflection Q near the center. The [replay](../reviews/LINE_E05_FLAT_REPLAY.md) gives the maps and collar parameters. These are the ordinary oriented connected-sum markings. In particular the resulting triple is on \(Y=M\#\mathbb{CP}^2\); one does not assume any smooth identification of the core of \(M\) with a ball. The standard collar and the chosen charts are part of the marking. Changing \(R_c\) changes the line area, and no symplectic uniqueness across choices is claimed.

## What passes across the cut — project theorem

* The pair is closed and connected, with smooth form \(\bar\omega\), the same zero circles, their transverse first jets and splitting types, and a positive symplectic line with a specified neighborhood. The line has its standard complex structure in the displayed cap. The two coordinate-ball decompositions, glued along a simply connected homology three-sphere \(S^3\), give \(\pi_1(Y)=0\), \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z[L_\infty]\), intersection matrix \((1)\), and \(b_1=0,\ b^+=1,\ b^-=0\). Thus \([\bar\omega]=\pi R_c^2\,{\rm PD}[L_\infty]\). There is no integral class of square \(-1\).
* To use a self-dual harmonic hypothesis choose the original positive rank-three bundle near the original self-dual zeros, the standard one near the line, and positive three-planes containing \(\bar\omega\) elsewhere. For clarity choose any reference Riemannian splitting of \(\Lambda^2\). Positive three-planes are graphs of strict contractions \(\Lambda^+\to\Lambda^-\); containing a specified form is the affine condition \(A\bar\omega^+=\bar\omega^-\). Local choices on the symplectic part exist by a local symplectic frame, and a partition of unity averages their graphs, preserving both conditions and the prescribed germs. A positive three-plane specifies an oriented conformal metric in dimension four (equivalently, the \(GL^+(4)\) orbit of the standard positive plane has stabilizer \( \mathbb R_{>0}SO(4)\)). With a smooth choice of volume form, closed self-duality gives coclosedness. For an input given just by the intrinsic near-symplectic condition, the metric existence is the separate Perutz source statement in E03; the pole input already has the local metric.
* All conclusions on \(Y\) that require further hypotheses are itemized separately in the E returns. In particular the homology generator called a line, a current with ends at the zero locus, and an embedded sphere through two prescribed points are three distinct assertions. A change of form supported away from the marked collar can be unglued by attaching the same standard exterior. A conclusion only up to unmarked diffeomorphism of \(Y\), or only about a blown-up pencil, has no such relative conclusion built into it.
