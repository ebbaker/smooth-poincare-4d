## One route through the adjoint mass

1. Start with the pure spacetime gauge $N=4$, $SU(2)$ theory. Give its
   adjoint hypermultiplet a supersymmetry-preserving mass $m$. At finite
   $m$ the resulting $N=2$ theory retains the massive adjoint matter and
   has the Coulomb-branch curve (16.24). Use the decoupling limit
   $q=e^{2\pi i\tau}\to0$, $m\to\infty$,
   $\Lambda_0^4=4m^4q$ fixed. Equation (16.22) gives
   $y^2=(x-u)(x^2-\Lambda_0^4)$, the pure $N=2$ curve in that normalization.
   The elementary singularity escapes to infinity and the two finite strong
   coupling singularities remain. [Seiberg–Witten, §16.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9408099)
2. Take the monopole singularity from the two finite points
   $u=\pm\Lambda_0^2$ and use its magnetic dual frame. The light charged field and dual photon give a weakly coupled
   local infrared description. The spacetime twist yields
   $D_A M=0$, $F_A^+=q(M)$, where $q(M)$ abbreviates the quadratic
   term with the conventions of (2.1). On a spin homotopy sphere, the branch
   takes the unique spin structure and its induced spinᶜ structure, with
   trivial $L$. The holomorphic Coulomb coordinate $u$ is a vacuum
   parameter. The branch's marked residue direction $[u]\in\mathbb{P}(\Lambda_p^-)$
   is a separate geometric input. [Witten, §§2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9411102) · [Branch domain](../../research-overview/OVERVIEW.md)
3. Witten's operator expansion estimates the correlation function under
   $g_t=t g$ at large $t$. Section 5 gives the suppression argument away
   from the two monopole points under $b_2^+>1$, flags a gap for the
   non-Kähler $b_2^+=3$ case, and qualifies the neglect of higher operators.
   The sphere has $b_2^+=0$, a reducible in the ordinary unperturbed
   spinᶜ sector, and virtual dimension $-1$ by (2.5). The local infrared
   equations therefore supply a baseline on the sphere. The argument for a
   closed correlation determined by a signed zero-dimensional count lies
   outside this application. [Witten, §§2 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/9411102)

## One off-shell precedent

Mason lets $J$ vary and pairs its Nijenhuis tensor with $k$ in the chiral
action. Lemma 4.1 derives invariance under $k\mapsto k+\bar\partial_J c$ for
compactly supported $c$ by projecting $d^2=0$ and integrating an exact form.
This identity works before imposing $N_J=0$. The chiral $k$-equation then
imposes integrability, and the full action in Proposition 4.1 uses the
two-fibre term on $PT\times_M PT$. Gauge-shift invariance of this action is
a classical statement. A nilpotent scalar cohomological symmetry on the
field quotient, along with $Q$-exact metric variation of an insertion,
would require further data. [Mason, §§4–4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/hep-th/0507269)
