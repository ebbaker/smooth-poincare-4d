## Cycle and differential

Fix \(N\ge 0\), \(M\in GL^+(N,\mathbb R)\), and \(m\ge 0\) longitudinal
pairs. The observable algebra is the polynomial superalgebra over
\(\mathbb C\) with even coordinates \(x,H,b,c\) and odd coordinates
\(\theta,\chi,\zeta\). The auxiliary polynomial depends holomorphically
on \(H\). The even coordinates \(x,b,c\) are real on the original real
fields. Use left odd derivatives, so for homogeneous \(F\) an odd
derivation acts on \(FG\) as \((QF)G+(-1)^{|F|}FQG\).

On the prescribed cycle \(H=Mx+iy\), with \(x,y\) real, the pulled-back
differential satisfies \(Dx=\theta\) and \(0=DH=M\theta+iDy\). Hence
\(Dy=iM\theta\) and the differential on the real coordinates with
complex coefficients is
\[
D=\sum_{j=1}^N\theta_j
   \left(\partial_{x_j}+i\sum_{a=1}^N M_{aj}\partial_{y_a}\right)
 +\sum_{a=1}^N(Mx+iy)_a\partial_{\chi_a}
 -\sum_{k=1}^m b_k\partial_{\zeta_k}.
\]
Together with \(D\theta=Db=Dc=0\), these rules give \(D^2=0\)
and intertwine \(Q\) with pullback. The \(\partial_y\) coefficient follows
from holomorphic dependence on \(H\), with no \(\overline H\) derivatives.

Writing the cycle's weight as
\[
W=\exp\!\left(-\tfrac12|Mx|^2-\tfrac12|y|^2+\chi^TM\theta\right)
\]
uses the holomorphic transpose in \(H^TH\) in the original action.
The bosonic part of the exponent has derivative
\(-(Mx+iy)^TM\theta\), and \(D(\chi^TM\theta)=(Mx+iy)^TM\theta\)
by the odd product rule. The exponent is even and \(DW=0\).

## Convergence and Ward identity

Let \(R\) evaluate \(b=\zeta=0\) and let
\(d\gamma_t(c)=\sqrt{t/(2\pi)}e^{-tc^2/2}\,dc\).
Writing \(\widetilde F\) for the pullback of an observable, the functional
fixed by B05 is
\[
\mathcal L_{M,N,t}(F)=
(2\pi)^{-N}\int_{\mathbb R}d\gamma_t(c)
  \int_{\mathbb R^{2N}}dx\,dy\,\int_{\mathrm B}R(\widetilde F W),
\qquad
\int_{\mathrm B}e^{\chi^TM\theta}=\det M.
\]
Integration over the charge-one gauge stabilizer contributes its Haar
mass \(1\). The constant \(U(1)\) acts trivially on these adjoint fields
and translates the connection by zero. Its factor is unchanged in a
Ward identity on the quotient.

The longitudinal operator \(-\sum_k b_k\partial_{\zeta_k}\) maps every
polynomial to the ideal generated by the \(b_k\), which \(R\) annihilates.
The remaining operator induces a differential \(D_0\) on the restricted
variables, and \(RD=D_0R\) on all superpolynomials. In particular the
current pairs a longitudinal \(D\)-derivative to zero without integrating
any undamped \(b\) directions. All coefficients after restriction and
fermion expansion are polynomials in \(x,y,c\) times the combined
Gaussian. The positive definiteness of \(M^TM\) and \(t>0\) give absolute
integrability for every coefficient and for its differentiated terms.

Since \(DW=0\), the Ward integral for \(QF\) is the integral of
\(D_0R(\widetilde F W)\). The \(x_j\) and \(y_a\) terms have coefficients
independent of \(x,y\). They integrate to zero by ordinary real
integration by parts, coefficientwise, against the full decaying
product. Each boundary term is a polynomial times a Gaussian tending
to zero at the corresponding two real ends. The argument applies also
to the complex coefficient \(iM_{aj}\theta_j\). For an odd derivative
\(\partial_{\chi_a}\), its coefficient \((Mx+iy)_a\) is even and
independent of \(\chi_a\), and the derivative removes that odd variable
from every surviving monomial. The resulting Berezin top coefficient
vanishes. The \(c\) measure is untouched since \(Dc=0\). Absolute
integrability justifies taking these operations term by term and
integrating the remaining coordinates. Thus
\(\mathcal L_{M,N,t}(QF)=0\) for every polynomial \(F\), of either parity.

For an explicit mixed identity with \(N>0\), take the allowed
polynomial \(F=(H-Mx)_a\chi_b/i\), which pulls back to \(y_a\chi_b\).
Here \(a,b\) are indices in the auxiliary block. Its derivative is
\[

\]
The second term has expectation \(i\delta_{ab}\) because \(Mx\) and
\(y\) are independent standard Gaussians under the normalized even

\(-(M^{-1})_{jb}\), giving \(-i\delta_{ab}\) for the first term.
For \(a=b\) the two terms are separately nonzero.

reproduced the odd and even contributions in all four entries.

## Normalization, point class and limits

The \(x\) and \(y\) integrals without an observable contribute
\((2\pi)^N/|\det M|\). The prefactor, the oriented Berezin value

stabilizer give \(\mathcal L_{M,N,t}(1)=1\). At \(N=0\) the transverse

More explicitly, \(z=Mx\) and \(\eta=M\theta\) express the functional as
\[
\int_{\mathbb R}d\gamma_t(c)\,
\mathbf E_{z,y}\!\left[
 \int_{\mathrm B}^{\mathrm{std}} e^{\chi^T\eta}\,
 F(M^{-1}z,z+iy,0,c;M^{-1}\eta,\chi,0)
\right],
\]
where the slots in \(F\) are \(x,H,b,c;\theta,\chi,\zeta\).
Here \(z,y\) are independent standard real Gaussians in dimension
\(N\), and the standard Berezin functional has
\(\int_{\mathrm B}^{\mathrm{std}}e^{\chi^T\eta}=1\).
The odd substitution contributes \(\det M\), canceling the even
Jacobian from \(x\). All \(t\)-dependence enters through
\(c=t^{-1/2}C\) for a standard real Gaussian \(C\).

\[
