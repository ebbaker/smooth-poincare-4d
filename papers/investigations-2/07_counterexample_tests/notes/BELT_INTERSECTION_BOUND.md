# Ordinary slice genus and the cost of meeting the two marked belts

Working project theorem at issue for non-author replay. All maps below are smooth; knots and surfaces are oriented. No metric is involved. [CERT model and inverses](../../05_certificates/notes/E06_PRESENTATION_HANDLES.md) · [05 reading](../../05_certificates/reviews/E09_FORMAT_HANDLE_REPLAY.md) · [POLE charts](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E07_MARKED_ATLAS.md).

Let W be the original minus presentation sphere with the open radius-1/16 coordinate ball at the POLE mark removed. Use the interior orientation and the outward boundary mark y=(1/16)R(u), where R flips the first coordinate. In each cap retain the actual belt sphere S_i={0}×S² in D²×S². For any connected oriented compact proper smooth surface F with the single boundary K in this mark, transverse to the belts, put N_i=#(F ∩ S_i). Then
```math
g_4(K)\ \le\ g(F)+(N_1+N_2)/2,\qquad
g_4(K)=\min_{\partial F=K}\left(g(F)+(N_1+N_2)/2\right).
```
In particular a native disk for a knot of ordinary smooth four-ball genus d needs at least 2d geometric belt intersections in every transverse representative. An actual saving of k units of genus requires at least 2k belt intersections. This is an obstruction to proposed geometric witnesses with insufficient intersections.

**Removing intersections.** The algebraic intersection with each S_i vanishes: F determines a class in H₂(W,∂W;ℤ)=0 by the homology sphere and the coordinate puncture. In an oriented product normal disk of the belt, straighten F at its finitely many transverse intersections to small normal disks, with the prescribed signs. Pair positive and negative points on each S² by embedded disjoint arcs whose interiors avoid the other points. For instance connect one pair at a time in the complement of the existing arcs; removing finitely many disjoint embedded intervals from a sphere leaves it path connected. In S_i×D² take the circle of a small normal radius over each arc as an annulus. Replace the two transverse disks at its ends by this annulus, rounding in disjoint endpoint collars. Compactness and transverse straightening let the radii be chosen so that the annuli meet the rest of F only at the designated boundaries; along arc interiors away from the endpoints F is a positive distance away. Opposite intersection signs give exactly the opposite induced orientations of the two meridians required by the annulus. This adds one orientable handle to the connected surface per pair, preserves its outer collar, and gives an embedded surface F' disjoint from both belts. No Whitney disks are assumed.

**Moving a belt-disjoint surface to the standard ball.** Let Y be the original rounded boundary of the five-dimensional zero- and two one-handle stage (before the two two-handles). In the marked gluing the cap minus its belt has coordinates (ρm(t),n), 0<ρ≤1. The map to Y sends this point to Φ_i(t,ρn). Its image is the original attaching tube with core circle removed. It is a diffeomorphism there, with inverse obtained from w by ρ=|w| and n=w/|w|. Together with the identity on the exterior it is smooth across each seam by the very signed collar, and fixes our puncture. Thus F' lies with unchanged boundary in punctured Y.

For completeness, Y is the oriented connected sum of two S¹×S³'s: the 5-dimensional ball with one ordinary orientable one-handle is S¹×D⁴ and boundary connected sum of the two one-handle blocks gives the displayed boundary. In each connected-sum factor take S¹×{pt}, with its product framing. Start away from the coordinate ball (a small ball in the retained exterior) and choose the connecting balls away from these circles. A small smooth ambient isotopy, fixed near the puncture, puts the two framed circles off F': arcs of dimension one and surfaces of dimension two have sum of dimensions three in a four-manifold. Equivalently perturb the loops transversely relative to the fixed ball and use sufficiently small transported tubes. This operation concerns new cancelling surgeries in Y; it does not alter either original cap marking. Replace the two disjoint S¹×D³'s by D²×S² with the transported product identification. On an unperturbed factor the other half is S¹×D³, and its union with D²×S² is ∂(D²×D³)=S⁴ after rounding. Ambient isotopy transports this calculation to the perturbed circles. The connected-sum neck and puncture survive. The resulting closed manifold is S⁴, containing F' in the complement of a parametrized coordinate ball. Small parametrized oriented balls can be shrunk, transported through oriented charts, and expanded by ambient isotopy; equivalently choose a standard hemisphere as comparator. This identifies the marked complement with B⁴ with the stated reflection. The boundary genus comparison follows. None of these steps recognizes W.

**Sharpness.** Choose a least-genus standard surface for K in B⁴. A small round interior ball disjoint from it can be moved to a concentric ball by a diffeomorphism fixed near ∂B⁴ (shrink, move and rescale). The surface now lies in a spherical shell. Embed this shell by product collar coordinates into W, respecting the boundary parametrization. Both belts miss such a collar, proving the reverse inequality for the minimum. This also proves g_Σ(K)≤g₄(K) for every marked coordinate puncture of a homotopy sphere. The ordinary genus on the left is a classical comparator. The intersection penalty uses these two fixed presentation belts. Optimizing the penalized genus recovers that comparator; the unpenalized genus in W can still be smaller.

**Limits.** Tubing supplies an upper bound for the cost of eliminating intersections. It supplies neither a lower bound for the unpenalized native genus on arbitrary surfaces nor a way of finding a surface attaining a strict saving. The proof uses the specified orientable one-handle stage, both inverse cradle maps and the native puncture. A projection of the two relators to a plane alone would not give this identification.
