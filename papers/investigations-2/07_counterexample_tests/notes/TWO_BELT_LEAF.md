# A removable opposite-sign pair at the minimum belt budget

27 November 2026 (America/New_York). For Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI Codex assistance. WIT-B04, lead derivation, base e171829 plus the original marked [transfer](ONE_BELT_TRANSFER.md), [coordinate puncture](../01_examples/notes/E02_PUNCTURE_INPUT.md) and [first-cap construction](../01_examples/notes/E04_NATIVE_SURFACE.md). Actual lead Codex, GPT-6 family; exact variant and effort unavailable. Smooth oriented category throughout.

Let F be a compact connected oriented properly embedded surface with product collar and one signed boundary knot K in the original minus puncture W. Call H a replacement disk if there is an embedded three-ball P in the interior of W, with boundary the union of two smooth disks D,H with common rim (corners allowed), such that F meets P precisely in D, and H and the rim are disjoint from both belts. The rim and the continuation of F have ordinary smooth collars, with the rest of F disjoint from the local replacement away from that rim. Suppose D contains one + and one − transverse intersection with belt i, and meets the other belt nowhere. These conditions include the embedded filling region; two opposite algebraic signs alone do not furnish it.

Replace D by H and smooth along the rim. A collar of the rim in a sufficiently small neighborhood is disjoint from the belts by compactness. Retain a narrow collar of the old disk at its rim. In the cross section of P there, interpolate from this collar into the interior of the sector and then to a small inward push-off of H. This local smoothing avoids the continuing part of F even if its tangent plane agrees with that of H. The resulting disk joins F smoothly. Equivalently, after identifying the creased ball with an ordinary round ball with hemispheres, parallel disks sweep from one hemisphere to a push-off of the other, rel rim and then smoothed in its collar. Transversality to the belts is needed only for the initial and final surfaces. The rest of F, in particular its boundary collar, stays fixed. This yields F' with the same genus, N'_i=N_i−2 and N'_j=N_j.

The marked transfer applied to F' gives

    g₄(K) ≤ g(F) + min(N_i−2,N_j)/2.

In particular when N_i=2 such a pair already precludes any saving for this F. With multiple pairwise disjoint replacement balls the same argument subtracts twice their respective counts before taking the minimum. Sequential replacements also work when the next ball meets the new surface in precisely its designated disk. No general existence of a replacement ball for a Whitney pair is used.

In the meridian assembly of WEX-E07 one can cut the middle exterior three-dimensional handle at any regular product cross section outside both enlarged tubes, say halfway between cap 1's south and cap 2's north ports. The cap-2 thickened ball together with this terminal half-handle is P. Its exposed boundary is D and its cross section is H. All embeddedness and separation hypotheses reduce to E07's handle inequalities and smoothing supports. The collar knot summand sits by the pole and misses P. Leaf removal clears the second belt for both the disk and the genus-one surface. The truncated first assembly has just one attachment leading toward the pole. A transverse slice of the original pole-to-cap-1 tether in the exterior cuts off the remaining thickened first cap with its short sealed handle stub as a three-ball. This second replacement clears the first belt. Neither operation changes the signed boundary knot in the fixed chart. It follows that even a different collar knot tied into this tree cannot produce a genus saving from these belt pairs. This exclusion applies as well to any other surface carrying a clean removable pair on a belt it hits exactly twice. A general surface with a nonlocal or knotted exterior may fail the three-ball hypothesis.

For a minimal-budget disk search with g₄(K)=1 and N₁=N₂=2, the complement in the disk of four small transverse disk germs is planar with five boundary circles. In a template made of exactly four full original cap sections and an exterior, that exterior is the five-boundary surface. Counts alone give no full-cap assertion. A cap tree with a clean terminal three-ball fails this test for a genus saving; both pairs would have to resist the above replacements. This condition is additional to passing the homology-ball eligibility tests for the same signed knot.
