**Theorem.** For the minus Miller–Schupp puncture W, each of the two separate belt counts satisfies
\[
       g_4(K)\le g(F)+N_i(F)/2\qquad (i=1,2).
\]
Consequently \(g_4(K)=\min_F[g(F)+\min(N_1(F),N_2(F))/2]\). Each belt must meet any transverse disk for a knot of positive ordinary *four-ball* genus d in at least 2d points. Every strict saving requires intersections of both signs at both belts. The result applies to every compact connected oriented proper smooth surface with that marked boundary, including arbitrary complexity in the cap that is left in place.

**One inverse surgery.** Suppose F misses belt i. Use the cap-minus-belt map \((\rho m(t),n)\mapsto\Phi_i(t,\rho n)\), extended by the identity as in the preceding note. This places the same F in the puncture of \(Y[r_j]\), the result of the other originally framed circle surgery on \(Y=\#^2(S^1\times S^3)\), with \(j\ne i\). Retain the entire cap j. Only the removed belt must miss F. The diagram and the coordinate ball at the north pole are fixed throughout this map.

**An auxiliary circle.** When \(j=1\), take a circle representing the first factor generator x in Y. When \(j=2\), take the second generator y. It can be placed in the common open exterior of the original tubes, away from the puncture: for instance use the corresponding original planar circle \(L_x\) or \(L_y\) at fixed polar colatitude \(\phi=1/2\) radians (measured from the north pole) and longitude 0. The two attaching latitude bands and the north chart ball of radius 1/16 are disjoint from this loop, including at the repeated planar basepoint. The loop is homotopic in Y to the appropriate standard summand core by the handle marking. In an oriented 4-manifold freely homotopic embedded oriented loops are ambient isotopic: perturb a smooth homotopy relative to embedded end collars, keeping time as a parameter. The locus of fiberwise double points has expected dimension \(1+1+1-4=-1\), for distinct circle parameters, and vanishing of the spatial derivative has codimension four on a two-dimensional domain. Relative transversality therefore gives a path of embeddings. Near the diagonal an immersion is locally embedded; isotopy extends by extending its velocity to a tubular neighborhood. The same argument works away from a coordinate ball. One may first move a homotopy off the ball center by general position and push off the closed ball by a collar. This argument is about curves, independently of any homotopy of surfaces in dimension four.

Take the auxiliary loop's framing transported from the product surgery in the standard summand. Perturb the loop in the common exterior to be disjoint from F, since \(1+2<4\). This local isotopy can stay away from the puncture and the other attaching tube; shrink the framed tube after perturbation to miss F. These framed isotopies need only preserve the isotopy class of the unadorned auxiliary loop in Y. They are not changes of either original handle framing. The two surgeries, the auxiliary one and the original jth one, have disjoint supports in Y and give the same manifold in either order with the same untouched boundary chart. Thus the surface, which can enter the jth cap, survives when the auxiliary surgery is performed on \(Y[r_j]\).

**Identify the filled ambient manifold.** To identify the resulting closed manifold do the auxiliary surgery first. Surgery on one product core in the connected sum gives a single \(S^1\times S^3\), by the explicit product calculation in the preceding note. The surviving fundamental group is the infinite cyclic group on the other generator. More explicitly, deleting a circle does not change \(\pi_1\) in dimension four (general position for loops and homotopy disks). Van Kampen across \(S^1\times S^2\) then kills exactly the auxiliary generator. The still-framed attaching loop projects as follows.

| Retained attaching word | Auxiliary generator killed | Class in the cyclic group |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(r_1=x^{-1}y^2xy^{-3}\) | \(x\) | \(y^{-1}\) |
| \(r_2=x^{-1}yx^2y^{-1}x^{-2}\) | \(y\) | \(x^{-1}\) |

The surviving loop in \(S^1\times S^3\) is freely homotopic, hence ambient isotopic, to a product core with reversed direction. Its actual tubular framing is carried by the isotopy. Both framing choices give the same closed result here: after transport to a product core, a change of normal framing acts on the boundary by \((t,n)\mapsto(t,A(t)n)\) in a product trivialization, and extends over the complementary \(S^1\times D^3\) by the same orthogonal action on its disk factor. Reversing the core coordinate likewise extends over that complement. To see the two product hemispheres fit these formulas, identify them by reflection in the equator of \(S^3\), pointwise fixed on their common \(S^2\). Isotope tubular coordinates to their normal framing using radial rescaling. The glued manifold is consequently \((S^1\times D^3)\cup(D^2\times S^2)=\partial(D^2\times D^3)\), smoothly the standard sphere after rounding. This identification is only used to recognize the auxiliary filled closed ambient manifold. No step of it requires simultaneous control of F under a further surgery. The marked coordinate-ball argument identifies the puncture of this sphere with the standard ball with the signed boundary K.

**Count and sharpness.** If F meets belt i transversely in \(N_i\) points, its algebraic count there vanishes since \(H_2(W,\partial W)=0\). The disjoint meridional annuli in the [tubing proof](BELT_INTERSECTION_BOUND.md) remove these intersections at genus cost \(N_i/2\). They are chosen near belt i, disjoint from the other belt. Apply the one-belt transfer just proved. Do this separately for i=1 and i=2. An ordinary least-genus surface can be placed in the marked outer collar missing both belts, which proves the minimum formula. This gives exact genus for each of the two restricted problems in which surfaces must miss a designated belt. It does not identify which knot, if any, has a smaller unpenalized genus in W.
