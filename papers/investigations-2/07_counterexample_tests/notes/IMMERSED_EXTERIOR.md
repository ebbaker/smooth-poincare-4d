**Claim.** Keep the signed coordinate knot K and its product collar. On each of the two original seams keep two disjoint full sections of opposite orientations, with their original collar parametrizations. Fix the homotopy classes of the four rooted cuts, including their order, as in the based boundary calculation. Among proper, connected, orientable *immersed* exterior surfaces with these data and only transverse interior double points, the minimum genus is precisely cl(W). This formulation prescribes no global normal framing, number of double points or signs of double points. The exterior genus equals the domain genus after the four cap disks are attached.

Let X be the rounded exterior between the three boundary components (the coordinate sphere and the two tube boundaries). Projection to the three-holed planar base F induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups. To check this, first fill the two original tubes by their cradles and the coordinate sphere by its coordinate ball, returning to the rounded boundary Y of F × D³. In each cradle gluing the inclusion from S¹ × S² to S¹ × D³ induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups. Both S³ and its ball are simply connected. Applying van Kampen to successive gluings along connected collared boundaries shows that X → Y induces an isomorphism. To compute the projection for Y, before corner rounding start with F × S². Along each boundary component attach S¹ × D³ on S¹ × S². These identifications induce the identity on the circle generators and add no relation to π₁(F × S²). Corner rounding replaces a collar quadrant boundary by its rounded arc and preserves this calculation. More explicitly, the two planar generator loops on the sphere face well away from the rounding still lift the original basis; using an inward collar of ∂F in this decomposition just moves the three gluing circles by isotopy. Thus the isomorphism identifies the actual projected words, and all specified rooted path classes can be lifted with their prescribed endpoints. The same assertion holds with narrow collars trimmed.

For the lower bound, compose any connector with projection and use the surface polygon relation. For the converse, reserve disjoint product collars of the four sections and K in X. On the surface remaining after collars, put copies of the five given loops on the inner faces. Realize the rooted paths with the required classes and the 2g handle loops in the trimmed exterior, using a commutator factorization of W⁻¹ with g=cl(W). The attaching loop for the polygon is nullhomotopic there by the isomorphism. Fill it by a continuous disk. The collar of the target allows the interior of the extension to be pushed inward, leaving embedded product collars at every boundary and keeping the original ends. Smooth the map relative to smaller collars. All choices fix the homotopy classes of the cuts.

For clarity, relative general position here only produces an immersion. In the interior the rank-one locus of first derivatives from dimension 2 to 4 has codimension 3, and the rank-zero locus has codimension 8. A generic perturbation misses both. On ordered pairs of distinct domain points transversality to the diagonal in X² leaves dimension 4−4=0. On ordered triples transversality to the total diagonal leaves dimension 6−8<0. These tests can be made relative to the reserved collars: put the remaining map into the trimmed target with a product transition, and use coordinate bumps in the interior. At disjoint source neighborhoods the constant and linear perturbations provide independent evaluations and first jets; apply relative approximation and parameter transversality on a countable exhaustion off the diagonals. On fixed smaller collars the map is already an embedding, and more distant parts of the interior can be kept outside the corresponding target collars. Compactness and the local embedding property of an immersion exclude accumulation of double points along the domain diagonal. Attach the original cap sections to the resulting smooth product seams. The four transverse belt intersections and signed chart knot have been preserved. [Hirsch, ch. 2 §2 and ch. 3 §2, Theorems 2.7–2.8](https://people.dm.unipi.it/benedett/HIRSCH.pdf)

**A planar test that passes.** Order the seam words r,r⁻¹,s,s⁻¹ and prescribe paths (1,s,1,r). The product is [r,s][s,r]=1. Thus every signed knot K in the given puncture chart admits an *immersed* disk with these four marked cap sections. This statement includes the signed Whitehead probe whose filling obstruction excludes embedded disks. It follows that these group data alone cannot make a distinction among boundary knots. One can also see why following the other full relator does not in itself force a geometric interaction. In the ordered tuple of based loops apply the product-preserving change (a,b) ↦ (b,b⁻¹ab) first at places 2,3 and then at places 2,3 again. Starting with (r,sr⁻¹s⁻¹,s,rs⁻¹r⁻¹), the new tuple is (r,r⁻¹,rsr⁻¹,rs⁻¹r⁻¹), which cancels pairwise. This is a change in a factorization; no embedded replacements, Whitney disks or isotopies in X are supplied.

An embedded disk for an eligible signed knot must realize a genus-zero word and control the interior double points. The minimum formula explains exactly which of these two tasks can be tested at the level of the exterior fundamental group.
