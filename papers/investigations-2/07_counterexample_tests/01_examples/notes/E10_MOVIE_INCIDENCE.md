## Events and directed trees

Let \(D\) be a smooth oriented disk properly embedded in the standard oriented \(B^4\), with oriented boundary knot \(K\) and a terminal product collar. Use outward radial time \(t\), or squared radius if the disk meets the origin. Assume a Morse restriction with distinct critical values and exactly two births \(B\), two saddles and one death \(X\). Events at a common height can be considered after separating their critical values. A regular level \(L_t\) is a link in the sphere of radius \(t\) (or radius \(\sqrt t\) for squared radius). Its components are boundary circles of the compact sublevel surface \(D_t\). The radial identifications of regular spheres with standard \(S^3\) preserve orientation.

The two saddle feet lie on either different circles, giving an oriented merge \(M\), or one circle, giving an oriented split \(S\). In the first case an oriented band joins two punctured circles. In the second case removing the two feet gives two arcs, and the orientation-compatible side pairings close the two arcs separately. The other pairing would make the band attachment nonorientable. If \(m\) is the number of merges then the net change in level count is \(2-m+(2-m)-1=1\), so \(m=1\) and there is exactly one split. Only a merge can join components of \(D_t\). Births increase the sublevel component count by one and a maximum fills a boundary circle without deleting any part of the sublevel surface. Connectedness of \(D\) therefore forces the sole merge to join the two sublevel components. The death cannot cap a sublevel component's last boundary circle. This would produce a closed surface that would also be a component of the final disk, since no further handle can attach to it. The changes are

| Event | Number of level circles | Number of sublevel surface components |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Birth \(B\) | \(+1\) | \(+1\) |
| Merge \(M\) | \(-1\) | \(-1\) |
| Split \(S\) | \(+1\) | \(0\) |
| Death \(X\) | \(-1\) | \(0\) |

Collapse every connected component of every level of \(D\) to a point. The regular cylinders are the edges. There are four leaves (the two births, the death and the boundary level) and two trivalent vertices (the saddles), so there are six vertices and five edges. The quotient is connected because \(D\) is, and consequently is a tree. Its two trivalent vertices share an edge. Orient each edge in increasing time, and let \(B_s\) denote the birth feeding the split directly when the split comes first. The other birth is then \(B_m\).

| Directed saddle edge | Inlets and outlets at the split and merge | All possible strict event words, with the births both written \(B\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(M\to S\) | Both births feed \(M\). The two outputs of \(S\) end at \(X\) and \(K\). | \(BBMSX\) |
| \(S\to M\), with \(X\) off \(S\) | \(B_s\) feeds \(S\). One output of \(S\) ends at \(X\), and the other merges with \(B_m\). The output of \(M\) ends at \(K\). | \(BBSMX,\ BBSXM,\ BSBMX,\ BSBXM,\ BSXBM\) |
| \(S\to M\), with \(X\) off \(M\) | \(B_s\) feeds \(S\). One output of \(S\) ends at \(K\), and the other merges with \(B_m\). The output of \(M\) ends at \(X\). | \(BBSMX,\ BSBMX\) |

These words are the linear orders permitted by the directions of the edges, with \(K\) terminal. Whenever both births precede \(S\), either may be \(B_s\) in a split-first tree. If both births are below both saddles and the death is above both, the only saddle orders are merge then split, with level counts \(0,1,2,1,2,1\), and split then merge, with counts \(0,1,2,3,2,1\). In the latter order both decorations occur. In particular the death can cap the merged output while the other output of the split carries the final knot.

Each row and each permitted event order is realizable by a planar graph movie ending at an unknot. Use planar sublevels in a fixed oriented chart of the spherical shell, with small disks at births, untwisted exterior strips at saddles and a shrinking hole at the death. For merge first, join two planar disks by a strip, then attach a strip between two arcs of the new disk's boundary to create a hole and close it by a cap. For split first, attach the self-band on the first disk to create a hole. If the death is directly off the split, place the second disk outside the first disk and its hole, and join across their outer boundaries. If the death is off the merge, place the second disk inside the hole (or in its future position) and run the joining strip through the hole from the new disk to the hole's boundary. The inner boundary after this merge bounds the remaining hole to be filled. The disk and strip footprints can be chosen disjoint except at their indicated attachments. The local graphs of a quadratic minimum, saddle and maximum in the planar coordinates, joined by planar collars, realize these changes in their assigned shells. Changing the heights of events on separate branches gives the indicated linear orders. The total smooth surface is orientable and connected, with one boundary circle and Euler characteristic \(2-2+1=1\), hence is a disk.

## Intrinsic cancellation and embedded cancellation

In the abstract disk, cap a pair of pants on the side of an adjacent birth or death leaf. Including the intervening cylinder gives an annulus, with one incoming and one outgoing regular circle. Take the other two cut circles on the edges immediately adjacent to the saddle, before any other graph vertex on those edges. Near each cut the level function gives a collar coordinate. Replace the function on this annulus with the projection of a product annulus to the interval between the cut levels, preserving those two collar germs. Thus an adjacent \(B\)–\(M\) pair or \(S\)–\(X\) pair can be pruned by a change of function on the abstract surface. In the last row the path from \(S\) to \(X\) goes through \(M\); the leaf at \(X\) is available for this particular pruning after pruning \(B_m\)–\(M\). On every row this procedure reduces the abstract disk to a one-birth function.

An embedded cancellation relative to unaffected tracks needs an explicit local model or ambient isotopy relative to the indicated collars. For \(B\)–\(M\) it must carry the birth disk, the framed band and its attaching arcs on the continuing track into a standard cancelling pair with support clear of the rest of the movie. For \(S\)–\(X\) it must do the same for the band, both outgoing tracks up to the chosen cuts and the actual death cap. This is the time reverse of the birth–merge model. A dying circle is a split unknot sufficiently close to its maximum by the local Morse graph, but a ball separating that circle near the maximum need not give a supported cancellation with the earlier band. If another move intervenes, verify clearance of the cancellation across its track or exhibit transported framed supports and collars for a reordering. In particular, applying a rule for adjacent split–death supports to a death reached through the merge requires an additional geometric move. Preserving the boundary knot while replacing the whole disk requires an actual embedded replacement movie. The abstract annulus argument supplies only a new function on \(D\).

There is a limited automatic replacement for two of the interleaved words. In \(BSXBM\) and \(BSBXM\), choose a cut strictly between \(X\) and \(M\), and before the second birth in the first word. The sublevel component that underwent the split and death is a one-minimum disk, so its cut knot is an unknot by E03. In \(BSBXM\), the other circle was born split from all existing circles after the split, and remains split at the cut because the only intervening critical point deletes a split circle. Thus the cut is respectively an unknot or a two-component unlink. Replace the entire prefix by respectively one or two disjoint ordinary birth disks with the same cut and collar, using an isotopy of the cut unlink to round circles and back in the radial shell. This gives a max-free disk with the same boundary by replacement. It makes no assertion about isotoping the original disk, and neither word belongs to the ordered births–saddles–death class. The E03 implication uses the reverse ribbon concordance from its cut knot to the small unknotted birth circle. [E03](E03_DISK_MOVIES.md) · [Zemke, *Knot Floer homology obstructs ribbon concordance*, Ann. Math. 190 (2019), §1 and Theorem 1.5, pp. 931 and 934, rechecked 28 November UTC](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v190-n3-p05-s.pdf)

In the ordered class, reversing the shell above a cut just above both births gives a cobordism from \(K\) to the two-component unlink, with one minimum and two saddles. The original disk with its two bottom disks chopped off is a pair of pants. The reversed surface therefore has three boundary circles and cannot serve as the annulus in E03.

## An embedded counterexample to automatic max removal

Use the banded-unlink ribbon disk \(R\) of Friedl and Teichner with untied band. In a radial shell, realize the two unlink components by disjoint smooth birth caps, the single oriented band by a smooth saddle in a regular product slab, and continue the resulting knot by a product collar. This gives a smooth radial movie ending at \(J=6_1\), with no appeal to their topological slicing theorem. Their exterior group calculation gives \(G=\langle a,c\mid aca^{-1}=c^2\rangle\). It is nonabelian, as seen by mapping \(a\) to \(x\mapsto 2x\) and \(c\) to \(x\mapsto x+1\) in the affine group of the line. They also give the surjection from the boundary knot group of a ribbon disk onto its exterior group. In particular \(J\) is nontrivial since the unknot group is cyclic. [Friedl–Teichner, *New topologically slice knots*, Geom. Topol. 9 (2005), introduction and §2, Figs. 1.1, 2.3, pp. 2130–2131, 2135–2136, and the meridian map in §7, pp. 2154–2155, rechecked 28 November UTC](https://math.berkeley.edu/~teichner/Papers/topslice.pdf)

Double \((B^4,R)\) by the identity on its sphere and knot, reversing the time and surface orientation of the second half. The ambient sphere is the standard double of the ball. The exterior of the doubled sphere is the union of two copies of the disk exterior along the boundary knot exterior. With basepaths identified at the gluing, the fundamental group is the pushout \(G*_{H}G\) for the same surjection \(H=\pi_1(S^3\setminus J)\twoheadrightarrow G\) on both sides. In the pushout the copies of every element of \(G\) are identified and the folding map onto \(G\) is an inverse. The double has exterior group \(G\).

Place the two halves in a single outward movie by first running \(R\) and then running its exact reverse in an upper spherical shell with the same level identifications. The reversed merge splits \(J\) back into the unlink. Retain the first of the two reversed birth caps, stop the movie before the second, and use a collar of the remaining circle \(U\) as the terminal end. The omitted part is a single ordinary cap and its regular track in the other standard ball. The resulting proper disk \(P\) in the standard \(B^4\) has word \(BBMSX\), boundary \(U\) an unknot, and the merge–split tree in the first row. The exterior of the omitted cap has cyclic group generated by its meridian. The inclusion of the unknot exterior at the cut induces an isomorphism onto that group, as it does for the product model \((D^2\times D^2,D^2\times\{0\})\) after rounding. Van Kampen therefore gives \(\pi_1(B^4\setminus P)\cong G\), by gluing the cap back to recover the double.

Any outward ribbon disk for \(U\) has cyclic exterior group by the boundary-group surjection. Hence there is no ambient isotopy of \(P\) relative to its boundary into an outward ribbon disk, and in particular no ambient split–death cancellation. With the intermediate \(J\) level fixed, neither pair can be deleted even by a different movie in its own slab. Deleting a birth and the merge below \(J\) would give a disk for \(J\) with a single birth and no saddles. Deleting the split and death above \(J\) would give an annulus with no critical points from \(J\) to \(U\), so the level knots would be isotopic. The same directed tree with the same unknot boundary has the planar realization above, where a small flat self-band and its cap can be supported in the standard cancelling model.

There are also examples where cancelling a birth–merge anywhere, with the terminal boundary fixed, is impossible. Start with the same \(R\), and insert a local split–death pair in its terminal product cylinder, leaving its terminal knot \(J\) unchanged. One explicit insertion uses planar graph coordinates \((x,y)\) on a patch of the cylinder, chosen by a level-preserving coordinate change so that the original height is \(x\) up to translation and scaling. Replace the height by \(x+A\psi(x^2+y^2)\), where \(\psi(u)=e^{-1/(1-u)}\) for \(0\leq u<1\) and is zero for \(u\geq1\). Choose \(A>0\) large enough that \(2Ax e^{-1/(1-x^2)}/(1-x^2)^2=1\) has two solutions in \(0<x<1\). The left side starts and ends at zero, and its derivative there has the sign of \(1-3x^4\). These are the only critical points (both at \(y=0\)). The smaller is a maximum, the larger a lower saddle since the height decreases between them and the second derivative in \(y\) is negative there. A scaled copy fits in an arbitrarily small product patch away from all other tracks. Graphs with amplitude from \(A\) to zero give the supported isotopy deleting this pair. The padded movie has word \(BBMSX\), but any birth–merge reduction would leave one birth and force \(J\) to be an unknot by E03.

Thus the ordered incidence data allow both saddle orders and formal pruning, but cannot guarantee an embedded cancellation or an ambient ribbon reduction. The counterexample for isotopy still has a different ribbon disk for its boundary unknot. The classification and controls do not decide the existence of a ribbon disk with the same boundary for an arbitrary ordered movie, and make no sliceness assertion for the named candidate.
