## Incidence and embedded prefixes

An oriented band between separate level circles merges them. With two feet on one circle the compatible orientation reconnects its two cut arcs into separate circles, so the band splits. Writing \(m\) for the number of merges, the terminal circle count is \(2-1-m+(2-m)=1\), giving \(m=1\). The two births start separate sublevel components. Only the merge can connect them, and it must do so. A cap of the last circle of a component would leave a closed component that could never attach to the terminal disk.

The connected level quotient has four univalent and two trivalent vertices, so it has five edges and is a tree. Its two trivalent vertices share an edge. If the merge precedes the split, both leaves below the merge are births, and the split carries the two upward leaves. If the split precedes the merge, one of its outputs feeds the merge and the other either dies or survives to the boundary. The merge's free input is the other birth. Independently enumerating the permutations of the two labeled births and \(M,S,X\) subject to these directed edges gives the following table. The labels of the births are forgotten after taking the linear extensions.

| Directed edge | Death attached directly to | Strict words |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(M\to S\) | \(S\) | `BBMSX` |
| \(S\to M\) | \(S\) | `BBSMX`, `BBSXM`, `BSBMX`, `BSBXM`, `BSXBM` |
| \(S\to M\) | \(M\) | `BBSMX`, `BSBMX` |

The union has six words. With both births below both saddles and the death above them, the rows reduce to `BBMSX` and `BBSMX`, with two decorations of the latter. All three trees admit the described planar realizations. For death off \(M\) in the split-first tree, the second birth disk lies in the future hole. It can be disjoint from the first disk and the closing strip even if born before the strip finishes enclosing that hole. The later strip joins its outer boundary to the first disk's inner boundary. Its remaining planar hole takes the death.

For intrinsic pruning, the saddle pair of pants together with a cap along its leaf is an annulus between the other two edge cuts. On these cuts, one points into, and one out of, the annulus in increasing time. The product height can agree with the original height in both collars. If the death occurs on the output of \(M\) with the split feeding \(M\), first prune the free birth against \(M\). This makes the path from \(S\) to \(X\) a regular cylinder. These operations use only the abstract disk. A supported embedded move also has to carry the framed band, cap, and continuing collar to a cancellation model with clearance from the other tracks.

In `BSXBM` the cut between \(X\) and the second birth meets a one-birth disk. In `BSBXM` the cut between \(X\) and \(M\) meets the boundary of that one-birth component and the separate second birth component. The latter was born split from the two circles already present. Up to \(X\) the regular levels are link isotopies. After discarding the dying circle from the link just below \(X\), the other two circles continue smoothly through its critical height and remain split by isotopy extension. E03 makes the first cut component an unknot by reversing its one-minimum disk above the minimum. The resulting annulus goes from the cut knot to the unknot with only indices zero and one, in the direction required for the genus inequality. The second component in `BSBXM` is also an unknot, so the cut is a split unlink. One or two ordinary caps with a regular isotopy to the cut link and the original top collar replace these prefixes. The replacement keeps the terminal boundary and supplies a disk with zero interior maxima. It need not preserve the isotopy class of the original disk. For `BBSXM`, the second circle was already present at the split, and this splitting argument gives no such replacement. ## The double, its cap and the fixed endpoints

Friedl–Teichner's Figure 1.1 depicts a single band on two disks, with a local knot \(C\) tied in it. In §2 their construction has \(s\) minima and \(s-1\) saddles, and the paragraph at Figure 2.3 explicitly takes \(s=2\) for this disk. The p. 2136 remark identifies the boundary as \(J=6_1\) when \(C\) is the unknot. This choice retains the drawn band. Taking the unlink at two distinct birth heights and placing the band in a regular spherical slab gives the word `BBM` for a smooth proper disk. The complement handle diagram and p. 2136 calculation give \(G=\langle a,c\mid aca^{-1}=c^2\rangle\). The meridians in Figure 7.5 give \(a,b\) with \(c=ab^{-1}\), so the boundary inclusion surjects, as also explained in the introduction. The affine images \(a(x)=2x\) and \(c(x)=x+1\) satisfy the relation and do not commute. Consequently \(G\) is nonabelian and \(J\) is nontrivial. These facts use the smooth banded-unlink disk, independent of the paper's topological slicing theorem. [Friedl–Teichner, Figure 1.1 and introduction, p. 2130](https://math.berkeley.edu/~teichner/Papers/topslice.pdf#page=2) · [§2, pp. 2135–2136](https://math.berkeley.edu/~teichner/Papers/topslice.pdf#page=7) · [Figure 7.5, p. 2154](https://math.berkeley.edu/~teichner/Papers/topslice.pdf#page=26)

For the double, choose a basepoint in the common knot exterior and use the identical marks on the two copies of the disk exterior. Both inclusions effect the same quotient \(q\colon\pi_1(S^3\setminus J)\twoheadrightarrow G\). Reversing surface orientation and height in the second half changes its orientation marks and leaves the underlying inclusion unchanged under the copy identification. Van Kampen imposes \(q(h)_{\rm first}=q(h)_{\rm second}\) for all \(h\). Surjectivity identifies every element in the two copies, and folding the copies gives an inverse to the inclusion of either one. Thus the double has group \(G\).

The ambient double is the standard smooth \(S^4\), with the two spherical height coordinates joined through a product equatorial collar. Reverse the upper disk's `BBM` movie, and cut at a regular standard sphere after the first reversed birth dies and just before the last death. The discarded cap is then a local standard cap, with its boundary in a small three-ball at the cut and the cap in the nearby Morse chart. It is boundary parallel there. Its disk exterior has group \(\mathbb Z\), and gluing along the cut unknot exterior induces an isomorphism to this group, generated by the meridian. The resulting punctured double is a proper disk \(P\) in the lower standard ball with word `BBMSX` and unknot boundary \(U\). The pushout for gluing back the cap leaves \(\pi_1(B^4\setminus P)\) unchanged. It equals \(G\).

An outward ribbon disk exterior has one zero-handle and handles of indices one and two, from surface births and saddles. Turning the decomposition over shows that its rounded boundary surjects on its fundamental group, since the relative handles have indices at least two. The boundary's knot exterior surjects onto the rounded boundary group, since the torus inclusion into the normal solid torus surjects there. Thus every outward ribbon disk for \(U\) has cyclic exterior group. An ambient isotopy rel boundary taking \(P\) to an outward ribbon disk would preserve its exterior group and is impossible. Deletion below the marked \(J\) level would instead require a disk for \(J\) with a single minimum and no saddles. Deletion above the marked \(J\) level would require a regular annulus from \(J\) to \(U\), an isotopy of the level knots. These slab statements fix \(J\) as well as the surviving collar. The boundary \(U\) itself admits the ordinary ribbon disk. [Friedl–Teichner, introduction and §2](https://math.berkeley.edu/~teichner/Papers/topslice.pdf#page=7)

The additional example with terminal knot \(J\) also checks. For the E10 height perturbation \(x+A\psi(x^2+y^2)\), the derivative in \(y\) forces \(y=0\) at a critical point inside its support. On \(0<x<1\) the positive function \(2Ax\exp[-1/(1-x^2)]/(1-x^2)^2\) increases and then decreases because the sign of its derivative is the sign of \(1-3x^4\). For sufficiently large \(A\) the two crossings of one give a maximum and a lower saddle, and scaling the patch preserves the other tracks. Interpolating the amplitude is an isotopy of the graphs. Removing a birth and merge from the resulting movie with boundary \(J\) would violate the one-birth conclusion in E03.
