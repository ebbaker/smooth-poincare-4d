## Reconstructing the controls

The stereographic parametrization at \((x,y,z)=(0,0,0)\) has spatial columns \(2e_1,2e_2,2e_3\), with outward normal \(-e_4\). This is positive for \(S^3\) as the boundary of the oriented Euclidean ball. The map \(\Xi(\rho,x,y)=\rho\Psi(x,y,0)\) is embedded for \(\rho>0\), since its norm recovers \(\rho\) and its direction recovers \((x,y)\). Thus the two control movies can be checked in one plane times the physical radius.

For the exact polynomial \(F=(x^2+y^2-1)^2+x/2\), write \(r=\sqrt{x^2+y^2}\). On \(r\leq1\), the two separate upper bounds on the squared term and on \(x/2\) cannot both be saturated, giving \(F<3/2\). For \(r\geq2\), \((r^2-1)^2-r/2\) is increasing from 8. On a ray with angle \(\theta\), the first radial derivative for \(r\geq1\) is \(4r(r^2-1)+\tfrac12\cos\theta\), with strictly positive derivative \(4(3r^2-1)\). Every level \(3/2\leq h\leq2\) therefore has a unique, transverse crossing of each ray, in \(1<r<2\). The level is a single smooth circle, and its planar sublevel is a disk. The terminal circle \(C=\{F=2\}\) has the chosen negative-axis basepoint.

The ordinary control has local equation \(\rho=1/4+x^2+y^2\) at its one birth. At any radius greater than \(1/4\) the angular interpolation in the note uses a strictly positive radius function, and near 1 it is the terminal loop \(C\) at every radial level. Distinct physical radii are disjoint. The one birth and terminal collar give the prescribed proper disk with counts \((1,0,0)\).

For the padded control, the height graph on \(F\leq3/2\) has radial height \((1+F)/4\in(0,5/8]\), since there \(r<2\) and \(F\geq-r/2>-1\). At \(\rho=5/8\), the interpolated end agrees locally with the graph, with planar levels \(F=4\rho-1\) near the join. All following interpolated values used before the terminal collar lie in \([3/2,2]\), so the end is smooth and has exactly one regular circle on every radial level. Radius is itself a coordinate on the end, with no new critical point.

Solving \(F_y=0\) excludes \(r=1\) as a critical point because \(F_x=1/2\) there. The axis equation \(4x(x^2-1)+1/2=0\) changes sign in each one of \((-2,-1)\), \((0,1/\sqrt3)\), and \((1/\sqrt3,1)\), hence a cubic has exactly one root in each. The diagonal entries of the Hessian there are \(4(3x^2-1)\) and \(4(x^2-1)\). In the order of increasing \(x\) these give indices \(0,2,1\). The negative root is a minimum of negative value and the positive saddle value is positive. The axis derivative is negative between the two positive roots, so the saddle height is strictly below the maximum height, which is strictly below \(3/2\). Thus in outward radius the event order is index \(0,1,2\). The planar sublevel after the sole minimum is a disk. The saddle attaches a planar one-handle with both feet on its boundary, yielding an annulus. The remaining maximum fills its hole. The graph is embedded because planar projection is its inverse on this part, and the end's distinct radii give the extension.

In the oriented saddle chart \(F=S-u^2+v^2\), the strip between \(u=\pm\sqrt{\delta+v^2}\) for \(|v|\leq h\) lies in the empty part of the lower level except at its feet. The sublevel is locally on the outer sides of these two arcs. Thus the left foot has increasing \(v\), the right foot has decreasing \(v\), and the order given in the note is compatible with an oriented untwisted band. The two exposed sides pair the corresponding ends at the same sign of \(v\), as do the two arcs in the upper-level model. For the cap, a sufficiently small planar maximum chart has \(F=M-u^2-v^2\). The level segment \(F=M-\delta'\) meets this chart exactly in the shrinking circle within its marked disk. Projection of the cap and the other regular component at each of these levels gives disjoint planar sets, even when the circles are nested. Distinct physical radii also keep different levels disjoint. This realizes a split death and gives counts \((1,1,1)\), Euler characteristic one and one boundary component. Both controls orient their planar disk portion by \(dx\wedge dy\), so its outgoing level is counterclockwise. That orientation propagates along the regular tail to the same positive based \(C\) in each case. The control moves fit the original planar-band, edge, component, crossing and isotopy budgets. ## Reversal and the Seifert genus test

Let a smooth proper disk have a radial Morse movie with exactly one interior minimum and a terminal product collar. Compactness and uniqueness isolate a whole bottom sublevel in a neighborhood of the minimum. At a nonzero minimum the tangent plane of the surface is spatial in an ambient product chart for the spheres. Projecting the small disk to that plane supplies a graph, and the planar Morse chart on the graph makes the regular bottom boundary an unknot in the local three-ball in the level. If using squared radius with the minimum at the origin, write the surface as a graph of \(f=O(r^2)\) over its tangent plane. Scaling \(f\) by \(u\in[0,1]\) preserves strict increase along small planar rays of \(r^2+u^2|f|^2\), whose derivative is \(2r+O(r^3)\) uniformly. The level circles then give an isotopy to a round unknot in the fixed small sphere under the same radial identification. A collar straightening at the regular cut introduces no critical levels.

The remainder of the disk is an annulus with outward oriented boundary \(-U\sqcup K\), with only critical indices 1 and 2 under outward height. The normalized direction \(x/|x|\) identifies the spherical levels without reflecting their spatial markings. Reverse the interval and reverse the surface orientation. The resulting annulus has \(-K\) at the new incoming end and \(U\) at the outgoing end, with indices 1 and 0, respectively, for old indices 1 and 2. This satisfies the ribbon hypothesis in Zemke's article, where a concordance is oriented from its negative boundary end to its positive one and ribbon means indices 0 and 1. His journal Theorem 1.5 then gives \(g_3(K)\leq g_3(U)=0\). Zero Seifert genus forces the unknot. The publication's convention is on p. 931 and Theorem 1.5 is on p. 934, as now cited in the frozen input. [Zemke, *Knot Floer homology obstructs ribbon concordance*, Ann. of Math. **190** (2019), 931–947](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v190-n3-p05-s.pdf)

The external identity needed for the exclusion is nontriviality. Dunfield–Gong Theorem 1.1 and §1.8 define the census and \(\mathrm{PS}_{19}\) from nontrivial prime knots up to homeomorphism of \(S^3\). Table 11 gives `16n68278` among those knots. Either mirror is therefore nontrivial. This import uses the census identification without recovering the signed PD row. [Dunfield–Gong, arXiv:2512.21825v1, Theorem 1.1, §1.8 and Table 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.21825)

For the finite language fixed for E03, every geometrically valid target movie would have one birth, so the annulus argument excludes the full class. That implication comes from the geometric hypothesis and the published genus inequality, independently of finite enumeration. The same proof excludes movies with one nondegenerate outward minimum and arbitrary finite saddle and maximum counts, with the stated radial and terminal conditions. Movies with more than one minimum lie outside this exclusion.
