## What can the twistor obstruction retain before integration?

The scalar \(J_g(\psi)\) discards the direction of the tensor \(P_g\psi\) and where the defect occurs. The first series has already computed one higher derivative. Away from spinor zeros, the squared norm of the projected covariant curl of \(P_g\psi\), divided by \(\lvert\psi\rvert^2\), is exactly \(\lvert W^+\rvert^2/2\). Integrating that contraction recovers the chiral Weyl energy. One elementary alternative is to retain parallel transport around two marked loops. The trace of the commutator of their chiral holonomies records whether their spin rotations commute and is independent of the frame at their common basepoint. The loops and the connection still matter. This gives a marked comparison to test alongside the two constructions below. [Projected defect identity](../../../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/notes/DIR_E12_20261124.md)

**Characteristic classes on a family of fields.** The positive half-spin bundle has second Chern number \(-1\) on every oriented homotopy four-sphere in our conventions. A family asks a different question. Suppose twistor fields modulo their symmetries define a moduli space \(\mathcal M\) with a universal bundle \(\mathcal E\) over \(\Sigma\times\mathcal M\). At a point \(x\in\Sigma\), restricting \(c_2(\mathcal E)\) to \(\{x\}\times\mathcal M\) would give a degree-four class on \(\mathcal M\). Could a product of such classes evaluate nontrivially on a compactified, oriented family of the matching dimension? If \(\mathcal E\) is just the pullback of the fixed half-spin bundle, its restriction at a point is trivial and this proposal gives zero. Identifying what varies after taking the quotient is therefore the first test. [Half-spin normalization and relative Chern numbers](../../../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/01_dirac_collar_matching/notes/E12_RELATIVE_CHIRAL_CHARGE_20261125.md)

**Operator insertions and gluing.** Witten's four-dimensional gauge theory provides the model for this question. Floer homology supplies the space of ground states on a three-manifold. Correlation functions of operator insertions give Donaldson invariants in four dimensions. A twistor analogue would need its own equations and observables, defined on an arbitrary smooth candidate. One could cut \(\Sigma=X_1\cup_Y X_2\), insert a point observable in \(X_1\), and ask whether pairing its resulting state with that of \(X_2\) gives a number independent of the point, metric and cut. The existing Chern–Simons calculation gives a control: a boundary framing changes the relative charge on each side, and matching the framings recovers the fixed closed Chern number. What additional information could an insertion preserve through this gluing? Perry and Teo's deformation theory of conformally self-dual metrics is a precedent for comparison. Assuming such a metric on the candidate would already settle its standardness. ## The punctured genus profile

Fix a closed connected oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere Σ. Remove the interior of a small parametrized coordinate ball and use the outward-normal-first boundary of the complement. Compose the positively oriented coordinate chart with the fixed reflection of the model ball on its boundary. Use this convention for every chart, and track mirroring and knot orientation. Write \(g_\Sigma(K)\) for the minimum genus of a connected orientable compact smooth properly embedded surface with the single specified boundary K in this puncture. Chart independence permits any such coordinate chart. At threshold h, the set of knots with \(g_\Sigma(K)\le h\) records which knots they span within the genus budget. On homotopy spheres the relative second homology of the puncture vanishes. Extending the comparison to a manifold with second homology requires specifying the surface class.

With the reflected boundary convention, the chart comparison gives an invariant of the oriented smooth sphere and \(g_{S^4}(K)=g_4(K)\). The desired certificate on a fixed probe is

\[
g_\Sigma(K)\le U < L\le g_4(K).
\]

An embedded surface gives the upper bound \(U\). A way to obtain that bound directly from a presentation would simplify the candidate side of the test. Rasmussen's rational invariant supplies the possible standard-ball bound \(L=|s(K)|/2\). A lower bound valid in *every* integral homology ball constrains the same surface in the candidate. A failure on one knot leaves the rest of the profile open. Manolescu–Marengon–Piccirillo's Corollary 1.4 distinguishes two specified homeomorphic indefinite four-manifolds by null-homologous sliceness of the trefoil. Cochran–Harvey's Definition 3.8 allows all smooth manifolds homeomorphic to B⁴ in its minimization. ## Two ways of retaining pole data

For a smooth Riemannian conformal class c and pole p, use A1's rank-three residue space \(U=\Lambda^-_p\) with its conformal inner product, and the normalized closed self-dual form \(\omega_{p,u}\) on \(\Sigma\setminus\{p\}\). The inverted end has the manifold orientation. The leading form is nonzero for \(u\ne0\). A2 identifies a sphere carrying a zero-free pole form with the standard sphere. These statements enter at the evidence levels of investigation 01. [A1](../../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E01_A1.md) · [A2](../../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E02_A2.md)

* Vary \((c,p,[u])\) over all triples with transverse pole form and minimize the number of connected components of its zero locus. The replayed comparison gives zero on the standard sphere and one on any hypothetical exotic sphere. It uses Gerig's fixed-end existence input, relative circle mergers and realization on the original compactification. A practical exoticity test would need a computable positive lower bound valid across all admissible triples. Counting circles in one triple gives an upper bound. [Count and hypotheses](../notes/E03_COUNT.md) · [Independent reading](../reviews/E04_COUNT.md)
* Fix \((\Sigma,c,p)\) and set \(D=\{[u]\in\mathbb P(U):\omega_{p,u}(q)=0\ \text{for some }q\ne p\}\). Branch 02 obtains D as a compact projection including directions contributed by lower-rank fibres. Measure it with the rotation-invariant projective probability measure from the conformal inner product. The set itself and its normalized area are candidates for comparison up to conformal equivalence of marked objects. Check naturality under change of residue frame and marked pullback. The round class gives D empty. A certified subset of D or its complement with positive area supplies a one-sided bound even when the actual area is unknown. A signed trace estimate for a single component on a cap supplies neither inclusion. Restriction to a chosen metric can retain information erased by minimization. To descend such a statistic to smooth type, specify an intrinsic family of markings, establish its existence and naturality, and control the statistic throughout that family or prove a choice-independence theorem. ## The centered twistor gap

A smooth generating pair of positive spinors determines one vanishing parameter line at each point. The logarithmic coefficient in the angular mean of their critical twistor error records the projected derivative at that line. Centering the associated map to the unit four-sphere against compact volume gives a fixed-metric infimum whose vanishing characterizes a conformally round class. One nonzero Weyl value certifies positivity, and the first positive scalar Laplace eigenvalue sometimes gives an explicit lower bound. The charge of a point insertion is constant on the unconstrained pencil family. Variation of the conformal volume can drive the centered infimum to zero on every smooth type.
