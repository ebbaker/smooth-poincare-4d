For a chosen numerical representative of the residue in an oriented inverted chart define \(A(\omega)=|\int_Z\alpha|\), \(d\alpha=\omega\). Excision gives \(H^2=H^1=0\), so the primitive exists and its period is independent of the choice. Changing the residue by \(t\ne0\) scales \(A\) by \(|t|\). We fix its numerical representative and end chart in the comparison. On LINE's positively capped, one-circle input the area of the marked current is \(A>0\). That conclusion uses its cap, actual line and current hypotheses. Algebraic degree one gives neither an embedded spanning disk nor a relative removal. The proposed lower bound would require an obstruction for every admissible triple, including triples with empty zero set. The period is originally defined only when the circle is present. **Two controls.** Write \(q=(z,w)\in\mathbb C^2\) with its complex orientation and constant form \(\beta=\tfrac i2(dz\wedge d\bar z+dw\wedge d\bar w)\). Compactify using \(y=Qq/|q|^2\) with \(Q\) an orthogonal reflection so that \(y\) is positive. In the numerical frame \(x=y/|y|^2=Qq\) the residue is represented by the same constant coefficients on both ends, anti-self-dual in the positive \(y\) frame. The round class has the zero-free pole \(\beta\). Gerig's Example 1.7 agrees with \(\beta\) outside its compact modification; the *completed* model has a single transverse twisted zero circle \(w=0,\ |z|^2=\epsilon\). On \(w=0,\ |z|^2\le\epsilon\) its form restricts to \(\kappa(|z|^2-\epsilon)\,d\Re z\wedge d\Im z\) for the positive matching multiple \(\kappa\). Stokes with the counterclockwise boundary yields \(-\kappa\pi\epsilon^2/2\). The absolute period is strictly positive. The exact tail and strict jets realize a smooth compact class by POLE E04, with the original \(p\) and identical residue. A1 uniqueness distinguishes that class from the round class for these marked data. The zero-free form has no last-circle period. Assigning it value zero would be an extension by definition. This already rules out using positivity at one marked circle to infer that the underlying smooth sphere has \(m=1\). **No positive period threshold at fixed pole and numerical residue.** Take any realized one-circle input equal to a constant \(\beta\) outside a compact set in an inverted flat-end chart. Take its compact metric representative \(g\) Euclidean near \(p\), as supplied by E04. Let \(F_L\) be identity on a compact core containing \(Z\), and equal to \(x\mapsto Lx\) on the far end for \(L\ge1\). On the intervening annulus the radius can be \(r[1+(L-1)h(r)]\) with \(h\) a smooth nondecreasing cutoff from 0 to 1. This is a proper orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. It extends across \(p\) as \(y\mapsto y/L\). Put
\[
g_L=L^2\bar F_L^*g,\qquad \omega_L=L^{-2}F_L^*\omega.
\]
The compact representative again equals the Euclidean metric near \(p\). The self-duality, closedness, transverse zero circle and twist transport, and on the far end the two powers of \(L\) cancel exactly in the coefficients of \(\beta\). A1 gives the normalized pole in \(c_L=\bar F_L^*c\) with the same numerical residue. The primitive \(L^{-2}F_L^*\alpha\) gives \(A(\omega_L)=L^{-2}A(\omega)\). The circle itself stays in place. Thus the infimum along this **one-circle family** is zero, including when each member has strictly positive period. No limiting triple or zero-free endpoint is required. Even the unmarked conformal structures are diffeomorphic here. The numerical period depends on how the scale at the pole is fixed under that diffeomorphism. An arbitrary projective residue alone does not set this scale.

**Quantifiers.** A new unmarked obstruction would have to rule out zero-free triples as the class, point and residue direction vary. In testing their *existence* there is a useful reduction. Fix one pole \(p_0\), one positive coordinate germ and a nonzero numerical anti-self-dual residue in that germ. A zero-free triple anywhere can be straightened to an exact constant tail and realized flat near its pole by the cutoff in E03 and POLE E04. Transport its pole and compact chart germ to the chosen ones by an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. A smooth connected oriented manifold allows a path moving a point, and positive chart germs at a point are isotopic: scale a germ \(f\) fixing the origin to \(t^{-1}f(t\,\cdot)\), then join its positive derivative to the identity in \(GL^+(4)\). After shrinking the ball each local isotopy extends by a cutoff time-dependent vector field. An oriented orthogonal rotation acts transitively on the unit sphere of anti-self-dual directions (the \(SO(3)\) factor on \(\Lambda^-\)); a nonzero scalar fixes the desired magnitude. The resulting form is exact constant on the new end, has the chosen numerical residue and remains zero-free for a compatible class. Conversely every such fixed-germ zero-free triple is an admissible triple in the original domain. This reduces the markings for the zero-existence question; it leaves the search over conformal classes intact. The period calculation fails even with this stronger common marking.

A fixed negative-eigenline twist or the obstruction to extending a boundary germ across its *small fixed tube* is compatible with both controls. The comparison does not construct a homotopy of transverse poles from one to the other. The count theorem \(m=0\) on standard \(S^4\), \(m=1\) on any exotic type, retains precisely the relative imports in E03/E04. Its candidate-side universal premise remains open.
