**Use a knot to probe a fixed puncture.** For an oriented smooth homotopy sphere Σ, assign to each oriented knot K in S³ the least genus of a compact, connected, oriented, smoothly and properly embedded surface spanning K after removal of a small coordinate ball. Identify every boundary through its parametrized chart with the same orientation convention. The proposal ranges over all such compatible markings. Its standard comparator is the ordinary smooth four-ball genus. Fixing K before trying surface presentations makes the comparison reproducible. One certified surface of genus g and an independent standard-ball lower bound strictly larger than g would distinguish the profiles, even if both exact minima are unknown. The genus-zero threshold recovers the set of slice knots of the puncture. Compare Manolescu–Marengon–Piccirillo's null-homologous slice knots and Cochran–Harvey's exotic slice genus, which varies the smooth filling over all manifolds homeomorphic to B⁴, before attributing novelty. Their choices of ambient manifold and relative homology class matter. **Keep the conformal class and pole as markings.** The compact incidence set of branch 02 projects to the closed set D of directions in the residue projective plane whose pole forms have a spatial zero. A candidate marked statistic is the fraction of that plane covered by D, measured with its conformal Euclidean probability measure. One transverse zero with uniform continuation over a certified patch of directions could give a positive lower bound; one patch with a uniform zero-free estimate could give an upper bound below one. A calculation on two marked classes could distinguish their pole families. A smooth-sphere comparison needs a rule that survives changing the markings and a computable bound on that rule.
