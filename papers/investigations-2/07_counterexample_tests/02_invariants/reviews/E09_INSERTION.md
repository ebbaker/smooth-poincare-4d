## Round family

In the positively oriented spatial frame \((1,i,j,k)\), the positive half-spin summand is the one on which the action of \(1\wedge i+j\wedge k\) is left multiplication by \(2i\). Its complex orientation from right \(i\) is \((1,i,j,-k)\), since \(ji=-k\). In the flat model write \(\partial_j\psi=c_j\eta\), where \(\eta=-D\psi/4\). Commuting derivatives and tracing with the Clifford action gives \(2\partial_j\eta=c_jD\eta\), whose trace forces \(D\eta=0\). Thus the positive solution is \(u+xv\) with constant \(u,v\in\mathbb H\).

For the round conformal factor \(\Omega(x)=2/(1+|x|^2)\), the twistor weight gives \(\Omega(x)^{1/2}(u+xv)\). With \(x=-y^{-1}\) the change of oriented frames is the composition of reflections \(R_1R_{y/|y|}\). The inverse spin lift on positive coefficients is left multiplication by \(-y/|y|\). Multiplying this by \(|y|\) from \(\Omega(x)^{1/2}=|y|\Omega(y)^{1/2}\) gives the second expression \(\Omega(y)^{1/2}(v-yu)\). It extends smoothly through \(y=0\). The only zeros are \(x=-uv^{-1}\) for \(v\ne0\) and \(y=0\) for \(v=0,u\ne0\). Their linear parts, up to positive factors, are right multiplication by \(v\) or \(-u\). Both preserve the real quaternionic coordinate orientation and reverse orientation into the complex positive spinor fibre. [Friedrich, §1 and Example 2](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/701462/WSGP_09-1989-1_7.pdf) · [DIR-E07, conformal covariance][e07]

The odd mixed term integrates to zero. Round radial inversion interchanges \(\Omega\) and \(|x|^2\Omega\) and preserves the measure, so both integrals are \(V=\operatorname{Vol}(S^4,g_{\rm rd})\) by \(\Omega(1+|x|^2)=2\). This gives the \(L^2\) unit \(S^7\) in the scaled coefficient coordinates and the right \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) quotient \(P=\mathbb{HP}^1\). At a spatial point evaluation is a surjective quaternionic row of singular value \(\sqrt{2/V}\) in these coordinates. The unit sphere projects onto the ball of that radius. Prescribing an interior value leaves a sphere of positive radius in the four real kernel coordinates, and prescribing a boundary value leaves one vector. The right action is free on all nonzero fields including fields with zero evaluation. A nonzero target value has trivial right stabilizer, while zero has the whole right group. The combined action \((a,b)\cdot h=ahb^{-1}\) has kernel the diagonal central pair and at \(h\ne0\) stabilizer \(a=hbh^{-1}\).

For the Hopf map \((u,v)\mapsto(2u\bar v,|u|^2-|v|^2)\), write \(z=uv^{-1}\) near the south pole. The tangent chart has negative quaternionic orientation there, from the outward normal \(-\partial_t\) in ambient order \((1,i,j,k,t)\). Near the north pole, \(w=vu^{-1}\) also has negative quaternionic orientation because the target quaternion coordinate is \(2\bar w/(1+|w|^2)\). Thus the round kernel map \(\kappa=[-x:1]=[1:y]\) has degree \(-1\). The signed Hopf number in the outward orientation of \(S^7\) is \(+1\). The normal derivative at the north coordinate sphere conjugates the normal quaternion, while at the south coordinate sphere it preserves the quaternion coordinates and the target chart orientation reverses. Both fibre orientations are therefore opposite to their coordinate sphere orientations. In the coordinate orientations the complement projection \((u,v)\mapsto v/|v|\) has degree \(+1\) on the positive normal meridian of the first sphere and on the second sphere. The two orientation reversals leave the linking sign positive.

## Characteristic classes and incidence

Use \(u_P\) for the positive generator in this parameter orientation and \(u_M\) for the positive generator of the spatial base. The tautological right line \(T\to P\) has the complex orientation from right \(i\). Projecting \((1,0)\) onto \(T\) gives coefficient proportional to \(\bar z\) in the local frame \((z,1)\), so its one zero has sign \(-1\). Hence \(c_2(T)=-u_P\), and the quaternionic quotient \(Q\) has \(c_2(Q)=u_P\) from the trivial sum. The same signs follow from the first-coordinate projection section of \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb H}(T,\mathbb H)\), with coefficient \(z\) and sign \(+1\), since taking adjoints reverses the coefficient orientation by conjugation. Here the top Chern class equals the Euler class in the complex orientation, and the transverse zero gives its Euler pairing. [Milnor–Stasheff, §§9, 11.1, 14.1–14.2 and Whitney formula (14.7)][ms]

Point evaluation on an equivariantly framed pair descends using \((\psi,w)\sim(\psi b,wb)\), which represents the same map \(\psi h\mapsto wh\). On the quotient it is a section of \(E=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb H}(\pi_P^*T,\pi_M^*S^+)\). The coefficient orientation \((1,i,j,-k)\) is preserved by both frame changes. At fixed parameter the bundle identifies with \(S^+\) preserving orientation. At fixed point it identifies with \(T\) by adjoint after choosing the target frame, reversing orientation. By the degree-four Künneth decomposition,
\[
 e(E)=\pi_M^*c_2(S^+)-\pi_P^*c_2(T)=-\pi_M^*u_M+\pi_P^*u_P.
\]
The parameter Euler pairing is \(+1\). The parameter slice also admits left \(i\) as complex structure on its coefficient in the chosen target frame. Its complex orientation is \((1,i,j,k)\), giving Chern pairing \(-1\). On the full product, an endomorphism commuting with left \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) is right multiplication by a quaternion. Commuting with the right factor as well forces that quaternion to be real, so the full coefficient representation admits no invariant complex structure. The unrestricted real Hom bundle has rank 16, with Euler class in a vanishing cohomology group. The pullback of the fixed spin bundle restricted at the spatial point is constant, with zero parameter Chern pairing. Ordinary evaluation changes by right multiplication and descends with the associated quaternionic Hom bundle above. The closed spin number remains \(-1\), measured by restriction at fixed parameter.

For any smooth generating pair on an oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere, the kernel \(\kappa(x)\) varies smoothly, as in the local formula \([-a:1]\) when \(s_1=s_0a\). The quotient isomorphism \(S^+\cong\kappa^*Q\) forces \(\deg\kappa=-1\). In a graph chart for the line \(\ell\), the parameter derivative of the incidence section at a zero is composition of \(\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathbb H}(\ell,\mathbb H^2/\ell)\) with the induced quotient isomorphism \(\overline F_x\). It is an orientation-preserving real isomorphism. Thus the zero set is the transverse graph with its projection orientation over \(M\), and its dual and normal Euler class are \(e(E)\) and \(-2u_M\), respectively. The latter also follows from the oriented graph normal bundle \(\kappa^*TP\). The tangent Euler class pairs to \(2\). [Milnor–Stasheff, §§11.1 and 11.5][ms]

## Fixed-base existence and the nonround control

A finite family of bump-supported smooth local frame sections spans each real fibre. The linear combination map is a submersion in section values at every point, so regular parameters for projection from its zero locus give a transverse section by Sard. Its zeros are finite. A second section can take nonzero values at those points by using disjoint bump-supported frames. This constructs a smooth generating pencil with no differential constraint for any fixed metric. [Milnor, §§2–3][milnor]

For a regular parameter, differentiating the incidence relation gives \(D_x\sigma=-D_\ell\sigma\,d\kappa_x\). The minus sign in real dimension four preserves orientation, so the local indices are the degree signs, with sum \(-1\). Every smooth degree \(-1\) map to \(P\) is smoothly homotopic to \(\kappa\) by the Hopf degree theorem for connected closed oriented four-manifolds. Quaternionic parallel transport in the pullback of \(Q\) along such a homotopy identifies its pullbacks at the endpoints. The quotient of the trivial rank-two quaternionic bundle then realizes the desired map as a pencil kernel. In the collapse example, identify the oriented ball coordinates with \(y=y_0+y_1i+y_2j-y_3k\). A real reflection \(R\) now has negative sign from this oriented ball to the oriented \(q\)-chart. The derivative at its only inverse image of \([0:1]\) is \(eR\), with sign \(-1\). In the other chart the factor \(\exp(-1/(1-|y|^2))\) and all derivatives vanish at the boundary, where \((Ry)^{-1}\) is smooth. If the initial ball were identified with \((1,i,j,k)\) instead, the same reflection formula would give degree \(+1\) in \(P\). This fixes the orientation reading needed for the example. An everywhere invertible differential gives a diffeomorphism by the regular-value count and local inversion. Conversely a diffeomorphism of the required sign yields such a pencil by realization. The everywhere-invertible condition is equivalent to smooth standardness. The single regular fibre and the incidence numbers occur on every allowed smooth base. [Milnor, §§5–7][milnor]

For the fixed nonround E33 control, the centered metric jet gives curvature entries \(-\epsilon,+\epsilon\) in the \(12,13\) coordinate planes and scalar curvature zero at the center. Its positive Weyl matrix there is \(\operatorname{diag}(-\epsilon/2,\epsilon/2,0)\). For \(P\psi=0\), projecting \((d^\nabla P\psi)^+\) to the kernel of the Clifford trace leaves the \(W^+\) term, since the scalar and \(D^2\psi\) contributions are trace terms and the Weyl matrix is symmetric and traceless. On the open set where \(W^+\ne0\), this gives a nonzero real self-dual Clifford action annihilating \(\psi\) at each point. Such action is invertible on the positive half-spin line by \(c(\nu)^*c(\nu)=2|\nu|^2I\), so \(\psi\) vanishes there. For \(\nabla_i\psi=c_i\eta\), let \(T_i=\sum_jc_jR^S(e_i,e_j)\psi\). The trace gives \(T_i=-2\nabla_i\eta+c_iD\eta\) and \(\sum_i c_iT_i=-6D\eta\). Substitution gives a homogeneous smooth linear system for the pair along paths. The pair vanishes on the open set and therefore everywhere. This control has an empty normalized exact-solution space on the same smooth standard sphere. Arbitrary generating pencils still exist there and give the same incidence computation as above.
