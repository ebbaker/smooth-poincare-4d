## Fourfold covering of a hypothetical disk

Let \(D\subset H\) be a smooth proper disk bounding the prescribed \(C\). Use a product tube respecting the boundary collar, possible since \(D\) is contractible. Let \(E\) be its closed exterior. Using collars to thicken the two sets, the relevant part of integral Mayer–Vietoris is
\[
0=H_2(H)\longrightarrow H_1(D\times S^1)\longrightarrow H_1(E)\oplus H_1(D\times D^2)\longrightarrow H_1(H)=0.
\]
It identifies \(H_1(E)\) with \(\mathbb Z\) on the normal meridian. The exterior is connected since paths can avoid the codimension-two disk. The mod-four meridian character is surjective, and its restriction to the tube wall gives the connected degree-four cyclic cover on the circle factor. Fill by \(D\times D^2\\) upstairs with normal map \(z\mapsto z^4\). The tube-wall identification can be chosen as the product covering and extended in a radial collar. This makes a smooth compact branched cover \(X_4\to H\). On the outer boundary the meridian character restricts to the knot meridian, so \(\partial X_4=\Sigma_4(C)\) with its induced orientation. The order-two subgroup acts in the tube by \(z\mapsto-z\). Its quotient \(X_2\\) has disk coordinate \(w=z^2\), with the smooth structure glued to the intermediate exterior cover using that coordinate. Thus both \(X_4\to X_2\) and \(X_2\to H\) are smooth double branched covers, with the ramification disk in \(X_2\) serving as the branch disk for the upper map.

For either double cover \(q:B'\to B\), write \(A\subset B\) for the branch disk. Use finite cells adapted to \(A\). For example, take the product of disk cells on \(A\) with a normal disk decomposed as a cone on a subdivided meridian circle, lift the sectors upstairs and use lifted cells on the exterior. The exterior and its wall can be subdivided compatibly before lifting. Branch cells have one lift and every other open cell has two. At the chain level over \(\mathbb F_2\), on a nonbranch \(k\)-cell \(e\) put \(T[e]=e_++e_-\), where brackets denote the relative chain class. Then
\[
0\longrightarrow C_*(B,A;\mathbb F_2)\xrightarrow{T} C_*(B';\mathbb F_2)
\xrightarrow{q_*} C_*(B;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow 0
\]
is exact. Each branch cell pushes forward once, while the free pair has kernel generated by its sum. For the chain property of \(T\), each coefficient on a branch face doubles to zero and on a free pair the coefficients are equal by the deck involution. Their common value is the downstairs incidence coefficient, since each open cell projects with multiplicity one. Thus \(T\partial=\partial T\), using the relative differential on the left. If \(B\) is mod-two acyclic then \(C_*(B,A;\mathbb F_2)\) is acyclic by the disk's homology and the isomorphism on \(H_0\). The long exact sequence gives \(H_*(B';\mathbb F_2)\cong H_*(B;\mathbb F_2)\). Apply this first to \(H\) and \(X_2\), then to \(X_2\) and \(X_4\).
