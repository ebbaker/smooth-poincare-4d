## Completed model and disk period

I read Gerig, *No homotopy 4-sphere invariants using ECH = SWF*, arXiv `1905.10938v3`, p. 3, Example 1.7, and Taubes, *A proof of a theorem of Luttinger and Simpson about the number of vanishing circles of a near-symplectic form on a 4-dimensional manifold*, Math. Res. Lett. **13** (2006), 557–570, §2, pp. 558–561. In complex-oriented coordinates \(q=(z,w)\) on \(\mathbb C^2\), put \(\Omega_0=\tfrac i2(dz\wedge d\bar z+dw\wedge d\bar w)\). In Taubes's (2.2) the model is
\[
\mu=(-\epsilon+|z|^2-|w|^2)\Omega_0
    +\frac i2\bigl(C\,dz\wedge dw-\overline C\,d\bar z\wedge d\bar w\bigr),
\qquad C=Rw-\bar z\bar w.
\]
Use the completed form \(\Omega\) from Gerig's example, equal to \(\kappa\mu\) for \(|q|\le\delta=\sqrt{2\epsilon}\) and to \(\Omega_0\) for \(|q|\ge32\delta\). The core coefficient is \(\kappa=T^{-1}>0\) in Taubes (2.5), with \(\epsilon>0\) sufficiently small, \(R\) sufficiently large after \(\epsilon\), and \(T\) sufficiently large after \(R\). The exterior coefficient is one in (2.16). The large unmodified zero circle from (2.3) is outside the retained core. [Gerig, Example 1.7][gerig] · [Taubes, (2.1)–(2.7), (2.16)][taubes]

The intermediate Taubes displays (2.11)–(2.12) have literal matching slips. Once the first cutoff equals one, (2.2) and (2.7) give \(T^{-1}(1-\epsilon)\Omega_0\) for the constant term, and the entire next annular expression must retain the common factor \(T^{-1}\). The core and exterior coefficients can be matched by the following direct calculation. Decompose \(\mu=-\epsilon\Omega_0+B+K\), where \(B\) is the closed self-dual part linear in \(w,\bar w\) with coefficient \(R\), and \(K\) is the closed quadratic remainder. Use the radial primitive \(\alpha_0=\tfrac12\iota_E\Omega_0\), where \(E=q\cdot\partial_q\). [Taubes, §2][taubes]

On the first annulus add \(d(b\alpha_0)\) to \(\mu\) before multiplication by \(T^{-1}\), for \(b=b_*(r^2/\delta^2)\) with \(b_*\) smooth nondecreasing from zero to one on \([1,2]\). The decomposable part \(db\wedge\alpha_0\) has type \((1,1)\) and wedges trivially with the real \((2,0)+(0,2)\) terms. Thus the square divided by twice the Euclidean volume is \(\theta^2+|C|^2+\theta b_*'r^2/\delta^2\), where \(\theta=b-\epsilon+|z|^2-|w|^2\). If \(w=0\), then \(\theta\ge\epsilon\). If \(w\ne0\), any negative contribution is bounded in magnitude by \(32\|b_*'\|_\infty |w|^2\) since \(\theta\ge-2|w|^2\) and \(r\le4\delta\). The inequality \(|C|\ge(R-4\delta)|w|\) gives strict positivity for large \(R\).

On the second annulus cut off the primitive \(\tfrac14\iota_E K\). This contributes \(O(\delta^2)\), a small perturbation of the self-dual form \((1-\epsilon)\Omega_0+B\) whose norm is bounded below independently of \(R\). On the third annulus increase the coefficient of \(\alpha_0\) monotonically from \(T^{-1}(1-\epsilon)\) to one, and add \(T^{-1}B\) to its differential. The differential is a positive \((1,1)\)-form and its square adds to the square of the self-dual term. Finally cut off the primitive \(\tfrac13\iota_E B\) on the fourth annulus. The perturbation of \(\Omega_0\) is \(O(T^{-1}R\delta)\), so choosing \(T\) large gives positivity. Smooth cutoffs constant on each side give equality across the annular joins. [Taubes, §2][taubes]

Orient \(D=\{w=0,\ |z|^2\le\epsilon\}\) by \(d\Re z\wedge d\Im z\). Its counterclockwise boundary is the surviving circle \(Z\), and the whole disk lies within the unchanged core. Only the \(z\)-plane summand survives restriction. Since \(\mathbb R^4\) has vanishing \(H^1\) and \(H^2\), a smooth primitive exists and its period on \(Z\) is independent of that choice. Stokes in polar coordinates \((s,\theta)\) on \(D\) gives
\[
P=\int_Z\alpha=\int_D\Omega=2\pi\kappa\int_0^{\sqrt\epsilon}(s^2-\epsilon)s\,ds
=-\frac{\kappa\pi\epsilon^2}{2},\qquad A=|P|.
\]
The orientation induced by the restricted form on the interior of the disk reverses its complex orientation and gives the positive sign. Gerig's ordinary \(i\)-holomorphic disk by itself provides no positively calibrated current for LINE's adapted complex structure. [Gerig, Example 1.7][gerig] · [LINE replay][line]

At \((z,w)=(a e^{i\theta},0)\) with \(a=\sqrt\epsilon\), the radial \(z\)-derivative of the real coefficient is \(2a\), and the \(w\)-derivative of \(C\) is \(u\mapsto Ru-\bar z\bar u\), invertible for \(R>a\). The three derivatives therefore span the Euclidean self-dual three-plane and are strictly wedge-positive. The twist can also be read off in the flat compatible metric on the core. For the clockwise unit tangent to \(Z\), contraction of the derivative with that tangent gives the symmetric normal pairing. Apart from the factor \(\kappa\), its radial entry is \(2a\) and its \(w\)-plane block is \(-a\operatorname{Re}(\bar u v)+R\operatorname{Re}(e^{i\theta}uv)\). The negative eigenline is \(\mathbb R i e^{-i\theta/2}\). A generator reverses after a circuit, giving the twisted type. [Gerig, Example 1.7][gerig] · [POLE E04 relative realization][e04]

## Both controls on the same smooth compactification

Set \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1)\), \(I(v)=v/|v|^2\), and use \(y=QI(q)\) as the chart at \(p=\infty\) in the standard smooth \(S^4\). Two orientation reversals make \(y\) positive. Then \(x=I(y)=Qq\) has reversed coordinate orientation relative to the manifold. The exterior value of each control is
\[
\beta=dx_1\wedge dx_2-dx_3\wedge dx_4.
\]
It is Euclidean self-dual with the inherited orientation in \(x\). In the positive \(y\)-frame the same numerical tensor is the anti-self-dual residue \(\eta=dy_1\wedge dy_2-dy_3\wedge dy_4\), of norm \(\sqrt2\). In fact the singular form in that chart is exactly \(|y|^{-4}R_y^*\eta\), with \(R_y\) the reflection in the hyperplane perpendicular to \(y\). The growth and chirality match A1. For the modified form the exact tail, positive square off \(Z\) and positive spatial jet give a smooth compatible compact class by E04, with flat representative near \(p\). The unmodified constant \(\Omega_0\) is compatible with the round class by Euclidean self-duality in stereographic coordinates. A1 identifies each as the normalized pole with the same \(p,y,\eta\). Their different zero sets force the two pointwise conformal classes to differ. If the desired convention has \(|\eta|=1\), divide both forms and the computed period by \(\sqrt2\). Only the modified control has a zero circle to which the period applies. [A1][a1] · [POLE E04][e04]

## Dilation at a fixed numerical residue

On a smooth oriented homotopy sphere punctured at \(p\), the relative cohomology of the pair is concentrated in degree four by excision around the point. Thus \(H^1=H^2=0\) on the puncture, and for every supplied exact-ended, one-circle input \(\omega\) the period is defined up to the chosen orientation of \(Z\), with \(A\) orientation-independent. Choose a nondecreasing smooth radial cutoff \(h\) supported beyond a core containing \(Z\), equal to one sufficiently far out. The end map \(F_L(x)=[1+(L-1)h(r)]x\), \(L\ge1\), has strictly increasing radius with derivative \(1+(L-1)(h+rh')\ge1\). It is the identity on the core and \(x\mapsto Lx\) on the far end, so it is a proper oriented diffeomorphism extending as \(y\mapsto y/L\) near \(p\).

For a compatible compact metric \(g\) in whose normal chart \(y\) the tail is exact, set \(g_L=L^2\bar F_L^*g\) and \(\omega_L=L^{-2}F_L^*\omega\). The metric matrix in the marked chart is \((g_{ij}(y/L))\), so it is Euclidean near \(p\) when \(g\) is. In general the same chart is normal because the normal chart for \(L^2g\) is \(Ly\), which precomposes with \(\bar F_L\) to give \(y\). Closedness and self-duality follow by naturality and four-dimensional conformal invariance. The circle and its jet type persist because the map fixes a neighborhood of \(Z\) and the form is multiplied there by a positive constant. On the far end \(F_L^*\beta=L^2\beta\), so the compensating factor keeps the full residue tensor and its unit-norm convention fixed. A1 identifies the result in \(c_L=\bar F_L^*[g]\). [A1][a1]

The primitive \(L^{-2}F_L^*\alpha\) gives \(P_L=L^{-2}P\), hence \(\inf_{L\ge1}A_L=0\). This holds on every type admitting such an input and even in one unmarked diffeomorphism orbit of conformal classes. If the initial period is positive in absolute value, every finite member still has positive absolute period. Projective scaling alone leaves an amplitude freedom. At fixed chart scale the quotient \(|P|/|\beta|\) removes that freedom and also scales by \(L^{-2}\) along the exhibited family. The compact diffeomorphism fixes the pole with derivative \(L^{-1}\mathrm{Id}\) there. Thus diffeomorphism equivalence without a chart marking leaves out the normalization needed for this comparison.

## Fixed-germ existence and scope

Given any zero-free pole on the oriented smooth homotopy sphere, A1 writes its end as \(\beta+da\) with \(a=O(r^{-1})\), \(da=O(r^{-2})\). Replacing it by \(\beta+d(\chi_S a)\), with cutoff from one to zero on \([S,2S]\) and \(|d\chi_S|=O(S^{-1})\), changes only the far end. Its deviation from \(\beta\) on the transition is \(O(S^{-2})\) and the square remains positive for sufficiently large \(S\). E04 realizes the resulting zero-free form with a smooth compact metric flat in that chart. Move the prescribed pole to the old pole by an ambient isotopy along a path, then correct the chart germ at that point. A positive local diffeomorphism germ \(f\) fixing the origin is connected to \(Df(0)\) by \(t^{-1}f(t\,\cdot)\), smoothly extended to \(t=0\). A path in \(GL^+(4)\) joins the derivative to the identity. On a common sufficiently small neighborhood, the resulting isotopy yields a time-dependent vector field. Cut it off on a larger chart to obtain a global ambient isotopy with the desired germ. Pullback transports the flat chart and exact tail. An additional rotation in the prescribed chart and a positive scalar put the constant coefficient in any prescribed nonzero numerical anti-self-dual direction and magnitude, by the \(SO(4)\) action on the unit sphere in \(\Lambda^-\). Rotations commute with inversion and preserve the flat germ. This construction preserves zero-free existence, and a fixed-germ example is itself an admissible triple in the original problem. The conformal class is allowed to change. [A1][a1] · [POLE E04][e04]
