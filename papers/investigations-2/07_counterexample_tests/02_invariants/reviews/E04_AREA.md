## Disk calculation and compatible marking

The vanishing equations are \(f=F=0\). In \(r<\delta\), take \(R>\delta\). For \(w\ne0\), \(F=0\) would give \(|z|=R\). Hence the zero set there is exactly \(w=0, |z|^2=\epsilon\). At each such point the \(w\)-plane derivative is \(v\mapsto zv-R\bar v\) with determinant \(\epsilon-R^2\), and the radial \(z\) derivative of \(f\) is nonzero. The intrinsic derivative image is the full flat self-dual three-plane, which is wedge-positive. Thus POLE E04 applies with the reflected orientation-compatible stereographic end, and its relative positive-plane patching can use the flat plane on an open neighborhood of the disk as well as on the far end. The volume scale can be made Euclidean near the disk and the compact representative Euclidean near \(p\), because those prescriptions are on disjoint regions. At infinity, reflection compensates the orientation reversal of inversion. Hence the numerical coefficients in the original positive compactifying chart are anti-self-dual at \(p\), and E04 and A1 identify the given form with the prescribed pole under the original compactification. It has least singular value \(s\) and determinant \(2z_0(\epsilon-R^2)<0\). Both scalar normalizations of the map are positive, so the local degree is \(-1\) with these ordered orientations. ## Measure, naturality and quantitative scope

The three A1 end expansions yield a common injective tail by uniform closeness of the evaluation map to its injective constant coefficient map. The projective incidence therefore lies in a compact truncation times the projective plane and is closed there. Its projection \(D\) is compact and measurable. In the coefficient inner product use the pushforward of normalized spherical area. With \(\theta=\arccos|\langle u,u_0\rangle|\) for unit \(u\), any direction with \(\theta<\pi/2\) has a representative \(u_0+\tan\theta\,v\), \(v\) orthogonal unit. The homotopy on the disk boundary stays away from zero if \(\tan\theta\,M<m\), where \(M\) is the supremum of the perpendicular evaluation operator norm there. Brouwer degree then forces an interior zero for every such direction. Two antipodal spherical caps of radius \(\alpha=\arctan(m/M)\) give probability \(1-\cos\alpha\), since each cap has spherical area \(2\pi(1-\cos\alpha)\). Hence
\[
 \mu(D(c_1,p))\ge 1-\frac{M}{\sqrt{M^2+m^2}}>0.
\]
When \(M=0\) the open projective disk of radius \(\pi/2\) has full measure and its closure is the whole projective plane. If the perpendicular unit basis evaluations have certified boundary norm bounds \(M_1,M_2\), the operator norm is at most \((M_1^2+M_2^2)^{1/2}\) by Cauchy–Schwarz, which gives the conditional numeric estimate when used with the computed lower bound for \(m\). The construction and compactness alone make \(M\) finite. Neither perpendicular pole field is explicitly specified on the disk by the inserted field, so the bound here is a formula in boundary inputs and a strict qualitative comparison.

Under \(g\mapsto e^{2\phi}g\) the coefficient tensor of a fixed field scales by \(e^{4\phi(p)}\) by A1. The two-form inner product scales by \(e^{-4\phi(p)}\). These uniform factor changes preserve all projective angles. An orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphism sending the pole to the pole is an isometry when the representative on the domain is chosen as the pullback. The normal coordinates then transport through the diffeomorphism with their orthonormal bases. A1 uniqueness intertwines all pole fields with pullback of their coefficient tensors, and the induced projective orthogonal identification preserves both the set \(D\) and its normalized measure. For an arbitrary linear change of coefficient frame \(S\), the coordinate description uses the transformed inner product matrix \(S^{\mathsf T}G S\). Using the Euclidean unit sphere in general nonorthonormal coordinates would generally change the angle and area. The conformal pullback result is for the intrinsic projective probability.

At the same \(p\) the round conformal class is the Euclidean one in the oriented stereographic chart on the puncture. A1 and the common compactifying coordinates give a nonzero constant pole form for every nonzero coefficient, so \(D(c_{\mathrm{rd}},p)=\varnothing\) and \(\mu=0\). The inequivalence detected is between these two marked standard-sphere conformal classes. The construction leaves the area at the native marked sphere and the exact area of the inserted class undetermined.
