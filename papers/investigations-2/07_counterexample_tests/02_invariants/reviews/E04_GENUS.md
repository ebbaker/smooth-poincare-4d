## Chart, hemisphere and reflection

Let \(W_b=\Sigma\setminus b(\operatorname{int} B^4)\) for a positive coordinate embedding \(b\) defined on a neighborhood of the closed ball. The negative outward normal on the complement changes the orientation sign of \(b|_{S^3}\). Thus \(c_b=b\circ R|_{S^3}\), for \(R=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)\), is positive as a map into \(\partial W_b\). The same sign applies in the original chart of the minus presentation, with \(b(u)=\chi(u/16)\) on and near the unit ball. [Original puncture](../../01_examples/notes/E02_PUNCTURE_INPUT.md)

For two such embeddings \(b_i\), first shrink each parametrically by \(x\mapsto b_i(rx)\), using \(r\) uniformly small. In exponential coordinates centered at \(p_i=b_i(0)\), write \(h_i=\exp_{p_i}^{-1}\circ b_i\). For \(s>0\) the map \(x\mapsto \exp_{p_i}(h_i(srx)/s)\) is an embedding by scaling and composition on a common neighborhood of \(B^4\). Indeed the exponential argument extends as \(r\int_0^1 dh_i(tsrx)x\,dt\), so a derivative bound makes its image lie in the injective exponential chart for all \(s\in[0,1]\). At \(s=0\) it is the embedding given by the invertible positive frame \(r\,dh_i(0)\). Join the centers by a smooth path with a path of positive frames over it, using connectedness of \(GL^+(4,\mathbb R)\) in a trivialization over the path. Compactness lets the same \(r\) be chosen below the exponential-chart bounds for the frame path. Concatenation with the other chart's inverse path gives a smooth isotopy \(e_t\) of the two parametrizations, after smoothing the time parametrization at each join.

The cutoff velocity \(V_t(e_t(x))=\beta(x)\partial_t e_t(x)\) is supported in the image of a fixed compact subset of the shared coordinate domain, with \(\beta=1\) on \(B^4\). It extends smoothly by zero, including as a function of time. Its flow has \(\Phi_t(b_0(x))=e_t(x)\) for each \(x\in B^4\) by uniqueness, so \(\Phi_1\circ c_{b_0}=c_{b_1}\) pointwise. This preserves orientations and the actual marked boundary knots. An isotopy of an oriented knot within the model sphere extends by the same velocity argument over a short collar in \(W_b\). Consequently the genus is independent of both the chosen parametrized positive ball and the representative of the oriented knot type. Naturality follows by transporting \(b\) along an orientation-preserving ambient diffeomorphism.

For the unit \(S^4\subset\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R\), use the north chart \(b_N(x)=(2x,1-|x|^2)/(1+|x|^2)\) and the south chart \(q_S(x)=(2Rx,|x|^2-1)/(1+|x|^2)\). At the centers take the ordered frames \((e_5,2e_1,\ldots,2e_4)\) and \((-e_5,2Re_1,\ldots,2Re_4)\), so the south normal and tangent determinant each contribute a minus sign. The restrictions identify the unit \(B^4\) with the respective hemispheres and \(q_S|_{S^3}=c_{b_N}\) at the equator. Hence \(g_{S^4}(K)=g_4(K)\) with no ambiguity about the knot handedness. In \(\bar\Sigma\) reverse the ambient orientation and use the chart \(b\circ R\). Its complement marking is \(c_b\circ R\) on the same underlying boundary, yielding \(g_{\bar\Sigma}(K)=g_\Sigma(mK)\) with the knot orientation carried by \(R\). Reorienting the spanning surface yields reversal invariance of the knot orientation at fixed \(\Sigma\).

## Upper and lower bounds for the positive braid

The oriented smoothing of \(T=\widehat{\sigma_1^3}\) gives the closures of the two separate strands, and thus two Seifert disks. The positive half-twisted bands at the three original crossings give a connected oriented surface with the braid closure as its single boundary, since the permutation of strands of \(\sigma_1^3\) is a transposition. Its Euler characteristic is \(-1\) and hence it has genus one. A graph of a smooth function on this Seifert surface in an inward boundary collar fixes the boundary and pushes the interior inside. Choose the function nonnegative and equal to a small multiple of the inward coordinate near the surface boundary so the graph is transverse to the ambient boundary. This gives the upper bound in any marked filling of \(S^3\). More generally this construction gives the finite upper bound by Seifert genus for any knot, and the least attainable genus exists by well-ordering.

The puncture \(W_b\) is an integral homology ball by excision in the pair \((\Sigma,W_b)\). The only nonzero relative homology group is \(H_4\cong\mathbb Z\), and the orientation class maps to a generator. The pair sequence then gives the required absolute homology. In any smooth integral homology ball \(W\) with oriented boundary identified with \(S^3\), the two Betti numbers \(b_1(W)\) and \(b_2^+(W)\) vanish, as does the relative class \([F]\in H_2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z)\). Ozsváth–Szabó Theorem 1.1 therefore specializes to \(\tau(K)\leq g(F)\). Apply it to the opposite orientation on \(W\) with the boundary marking precomposed by \(R\). The same surface has boundary identified with \(mK\), and their Lemma 3.3 gives \(\tau(mK)=-\tau(K)\). This gives the absolute bound. Their Corollary 1.7 evaluates the positive torus-knot normalization, giving \(\tau(T_{2,3})=1\). [Ozsváth–Szabó, *Knot Floer homology and the four-ball genus*, Thm 1.1, Cor. 1.7, Lem. 3.3 and §3.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0301149)

The positive-genus displayed conclusion in the copy on p. 631 does drop the factor of two on the right. The immediately preceding adjunction inequality gives \(2m+\|[F]\|_1+[F]^2\leq 2g(F)-2\) for \(m<\tau(K)\). Taking \(m=\tau(K)-1\) recovers the stated theorem. In the disk case §3.4 boundary-connect-sums two copies of the disk and ambient manifold, then adds a trivial handle to give a genus-one surface bounding \(K\# K\). For a homology ball the genus-one inequality is \(2\tau(K\#K)\leq2\). By additivity and integrality this gives \(\tau(K)\leq0\). The reversed-orientation application gives \(\tau(K)\geq0\). Thus the claimed lower bound actually excludes genus zero whenever \(|\tau(K)|>0\). For the trefoil and its mirror the bounds coincide at one. [Ozsváth–Szabó, §3.4, pp. 630–631 and Prop. 3.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0301149)

Rasmussen's positive diagram has three crossings and two oriented-resolution circles, so his normalization gives \(s(T)=3-2+1=2\). With his Theorem 1 this supplies the independent standard-ball lower bound \(g_4(T)\geq1\). The two-sided \(\tau\) argument gives the lower bound in the general fillings above. [Rasmussen, *Khovanov homology and the slice genus*, arXiv:math/0402131v1, Thm 1 and §§4.4, 5.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402131v1)

## Literature comparisons and closed-to-punctured correspondence

Manolescu–Marengon–Piccirillo use the fixed ambient puncture problem in a specified relative homology class. Their Definition 2.1(b) calls the zero-class disk version H-sliceness. Corollary 1.4 gives a right-handed trefoil in the zero class of \(\#^3\mathbb{CP}^2\#^{20}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\) using the disk in \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) of Example 2.3, where incoming LHT becomes outward-boundary RHT. The opposite side uses the nonzero square-zero left-handed trefoil disk of Example 2.5 in K3. K3 and its blow-up are symplectic and satisfy \(b_2^+=3\), so Theorem 1.3 excludes an H-slice right-handed trefoil disk in \(K3\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\). The usual trefoil Seifert surface pushed into the latter puncture's collar is zero in relative homology, since the collar relative to its boundary retracts onto that boundary. The minimum in the zero class of their second puncture is therefore one. These are relative genus-zero distinctions on an indefinite pair. In a homotopy-sphere puncture the zero class condition holds for every admissible surface. The paragraph following their Corollary 1.4 already states the homotopy-sphere sliceness strategy. [Manolescu–Marengon–Piccirillo, arXiv:2012.12270v2, introduction, Def. 2.1, Examples 2.3, 2.5 and §4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.12270v2)

Cochran–Harvey's Definition 3.8 minimizes the genus of smooth compact oriented surfaces over smooth manifolds homeomorphic to \(B^4\), and Proposition 3.9 uses this exact genus-zero condition. Every marked \(W_b\) above is simply connected by van Kampen across the collared cap sphere, because the added cap and the common sphere are simply connected. Acyclicity and simple connectivity give contractibility by Hurewicz and Whitehead. Freedman's note following Theorem 1.6 identifies compact contractible topological four-manifolds with standard sphere boundary as topological balls. Thus every \(W_b\) is allowed in the exotic slice genus minimum. Conversely for any such smooth \(W\) with identification \(c:S^3\to\partial W\), attach a positively oriented standard cap using \(c\circ R\), an orientation-reversing boundary map. With product collar gluing the cap embedding extends across the seam to a neighborhood of \(B^4\). Its reflected complement marking is \(c\). Mayer–Vietoris gives the capped manifold the homology of \(S^4\), and van Kampen gives simple connectivity. Hurewicz supplies a fundamental class representative from \(S^4\), which is a homotopy equivalence by the simply connected homology form of Whitehead's theorem. These two constructions prove \(g_{\mathrm{ex}}(K)=\min_\Sigma g_\Sigma(K)\) over oriented smooth homotopy spheres. [Cochran–Harvey, *The geometry of the knot concordance space*, §3.2, Def. 3.8, Prop. 3.9](https://msp.org/agt/2018/18-5/agt-v18-n5-p01-p.pdf) · [Freedman, *The topology of four-dimensional manifolds*, JDG 17 (1982), p. 371, note after Theorem 1.6](https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/1982_The%20topology%20of%20four-dimensional%20manifolds.pdf#page=15)

Cochran–Harvey also use the ordinary and exotic signature lower bounds and a left-handed trefoil signature of absolute value two in their §6. Their mirror equality from Proposition 3.9 and the genus-one collar give the same value on their exotic slice genus minimum. The checked result for this project is the exact knot and chart calibration in each fixed filling. Its equality with the standard-ball value rules out a strict genus gap for this one signed knot on the named minus sphere. [Cochran–Harvey, §6, pp. 2536–2537](https://msp.org/agt/2018/18-5/agt-v18-n5-p01-p.pdf)
