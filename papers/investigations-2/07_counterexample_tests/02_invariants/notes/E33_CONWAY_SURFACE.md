Let \(W\) be the original marked minus Miller–Schupp puncture and \(C\) the oriented Conway knot of Piccirillo’s Figure 1 left. There is a connected smooth proper surface of genus one for \(C\) in \(W\), with one negative and one positive transverse intersection at each original belt. A hypothetical disk with these counts must resist a clean three-ball replacement on each belt. The freely cancelling four-return construction supplies an immersed disk with its own specified paths.

## The marked collar and cobordism

Use the outward coordinate mark and radial collar from WEX-E07,
\[
\iota(u)=\chi(rR(u)),\qquad
\Gamma(u,s)=\chi((r+s)R(u)),\qquad
r=1/16,\quad R(u)=(-u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4).
\]
The complement outward direction on its boundary is \(-\partial_\rho\). Radial coordinates in the positively oriented chart \(\chi\) therefore induce the negative of the original ball boundary orientation. The reflection \(R\) compensates that sign, so \(\iota\) preserves the outward model \(S^3\) orientation. Realize the over- and under-crossings of Figure 1 left in this oriented model before using \(\Gamma\). The actual disk \(D_{12}\) has a radial product collar over the oriented unknot
\[
U(\alpha)=(-k\cos\alpha,k\sin\alpha,\ell,0),\qquad
k=\sqrt{55}/8,\quad \ell=3/8,\quad \alpha\text{ increasing}.
\]

In the model \(S^3\times[0,a]\), choose \(a>0\) smaller than the radial product collar width of \(D_{12}\), with \(a<r/2\). Make the initial isotopy, the single crossing change and the final isotopy in disjoint open height intervals. The final isotopy takes the changed unknot to the exact \(U\) above with its increasing-\(\alpha\) orientation. In stereographic coordinates \((x,y,Z)=(u_1,u_2,u_3)/(1-u_4)\), the half-turn \((x,y,Z)\mapsto(x,-y,2\ell-Z)\) preserves the ambient orientation and reverses the orientation on \(U\). It extends across the projection point and lies in the isotopy of rotations about the same axis. Use it if the initial isotopy of the underlying unknots gives the opposite orientation. Take the family constant near both ends. For the crossing change move one arc through the other in a small crossing ball. The tangents of the two strands are independent in the projection plane and the relative velocity at their meeting has nonzero component normal to it. Including the height direction gives two transverse trace sheets in four dimensions. Each time slice away from the single crossing time is embedded, and height separates different time slices. The trace is an immersed annulus with one interior transverse double point.

Resolve this point locally as follows. The two transverse sheets can be straightened simultaneously in a small chart to coordinate planes, using defining submersions for the two sheets. Choose complex coordinates \((z,w)\) on their two factors with complex orientation agreeing with the oriented trace on each plane. In a small bidisk meeting only these sheets, replace the pieces of the axes by the neck \(zw=\epsilon\) in \(\{|z|,|w|\le b\}\), with \(0<\epsilon<b^2\). On \(b\le|z|<2b\), attach the graph \(w=\epsilon\eta(|z|)/z\), where \(0\le\eta\le1\) equals one near \(b\) and zero near \(2b\). Attach the symmetric graph \(z=\epsilon\eta(|w|)/w\) on the other end. The two graphs have disjoint interiors, since their other coordinate has modulus below \(b\). The joins are smooth by the agreement near \(b\). The oriented neck matches each graph via its complex coordinate projection, and the outer graphs match the original oriented axes. The whole replacement is an embedded annulus agreeing with the original trace near the bidisk boundary. It removes two open disks from the domain and glues in the annulus, so the connected cobordism has Euler characteristic \(-2\), two boundary circles and genus one.

Orient the trace at regular points by \(d\theta\wedge ds\), with \(\theta\) increasing along the oriented slice knot and \(s\) increasing into \(W\). At \(s=0\), the outward direction of the trace is \(-\partial_s\) and the boundary orientation is \(C\). At \(s=a\), the outward direction is \(+\partial_s\) and the boundary orientation is \(-U\). Under \(\Gamma\), the cobordism then glues to the part of \(D_{12}\) retained at \(s\ge a\), which has boundary \(+U\) at that join. The product collars agree and the interiors lie on opposite sides of the join. The retained disk contributes Euler characteristic one, giving \(\chi=-1\) and one boundary for the resulting genus-one surface. The new pieces are all within \(r\le\rho<3r/2\) and miss both caps.

For each original belt \(Q_i=\{0\}\times S^2\subset C_i=D^2\times S^2\), the WEX endpoints are unchanged. The ambient orientation is oriented disk followed by sphere, and the thickened cap piece has orientation disk followed by \(dv\). Outward-first at its lower \(v\) end gives the negative disk orientation, and at the upper \(v\) end gives the positive one. Consequently the ordered signs are \((-1,+1)\) at \((-1/32,\pi+1/32)\) for \(Q_1\), and at \((-1/32,1/32)\) for \(Q_2\). These are the transverse intersections of the original \(D_{12}\) with its actual belts. ## What the two disk mechanisms would require

- **Four prescribed returns.** In exterior boundary orientation, use the seam words \((r_1,r_1^{-1},r_2,r_2^{-1})\) and basings \((1,r_2,1,r_1)\), where \(r_1=x^{-1}y^2xy^{-3}\) and \(r_2=x^{-1}yx^2y^{-1}x^{-2}\). The product is \([r_1,r_2][r_2,r_1]=1\) in the free group. The immersed-exterior result supplies a product-collared immersed connector with planar domain and five boundary circles, for this signed \(C\) and the four full cap sections with those basings. Capping the four seam circles gives an immersed disk with two opposite signs at each belt. The cuts belong to that prescribed connector. Identifying them on \(D_{12}\) would require a separate cut calculation. To get an embedded disk from the immersed connector would require geometric control of its interior double points. [Based exterior](../../notes/BASED_EXTERIOR.md#what-the-paths-record) · [Immersed exterior](../../notes/IMMERSED_EXTERIOR.md)
- **Retaining a clean removable pair.** Suppose an embedded proper disk for this \(C\) meets each belt twice and carries a clean replacement on either belt in the sense of the leaf lemma. This means an embedded interior three-ball meets the disk precisely in the exposed disk face containing the specified opposite pair, the face misses the other belt, and the replacement face and rim miss both belts. The ball and rim conditions let the replacement be smoothed without changing the outer collar, genus or the other belt’s intersections. It would produce a smooth proper disk for the same marked \(C\) missing the chosen belt. The corresponding one-belt transfer would give a disk in the standard \(B^4\), contradicting Piccirillo’s Theorem 1.1. In the WEX tree the \(Q_2\) replacement is available first and the exhibited \(Q_1\) replacement is available on the truncated tree. Retaining that sequence in a disk would already fail at \(Q_2\). A clean \(Q_1\) replacement independently available on a hypothetical minimal disk would fail by its own transfer. With higher counts, retaining one such replacement on belt \(i\) forces \(N_i\ge4\) by applying the transfer inequality and \(g_4(C)\ge1\) after \(N_i\) drops by two. Both concrete leaf removals remain valid on the genus-one surface because the new cobordism lies in the untouched polar collar. The obstruction concerns disks with these geometric replacement conditions.
