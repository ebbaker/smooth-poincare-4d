## Ordinary four-ball genus

Let \(C\) be the oriented knot on the **left** of Lisa Piccirillo's Figure 1, in the standard oriented \(S^3=\partial B^4\). The right-hand picture is its Kinoshita–Teresaka mutant. For a positively oriented puncture chart \(b\), the boundary input is \(c_b(C)\) with the transported orientation, using the outward-boundary identification \(c_b=b\circ R|_{S^3}\) of E01. [Piccirillo (2020), Figure 1, p. 582](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v191-n2-p05-s.pdf#page=2) · [E01 boundary convention and standard calibration](E01_GENUS_CONTROL.md)

Piccirillo's Proposition 2.2 starts from one unknotting crossing and the proof of Proposition 1.4 applies that construction to \(C\) in Figure 4, starting in panel (a). Mackaay–Vaz give an explicit signed check in their own Figure 4. Switching the encircled negative crossing to positive gives the unknot, and the oriented smoothing gives the two-component link \(P(3,-2,-3,2)\). The crossing sign quoted here refers to their diagram. The one-crossing property is preserved under oriented isotopy, mirroring and reversal. The oriented two-saddle cobordism to the unknot and its standard cap give the ordinary genus-one upper bound. Piccirillo's Theorem 1.1 excludes a smooth proper disk in the standard \(B^4\), giving \(1\leq g_4(C)\leq1\) and hence \(g_{S^4}(C)=1\) in the reflected marking. [Piccirillo, Theorem 1.1, Proposition 2.2 and Figure 4(a)](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v191-n2-p05-s.pdf) · [Mackaay–Vaz, arXiv:0812.1957v1, §3.2 and Figures 4–5](https://arxiv.org/abs/0812.1957v1)

The Conway knot has \(\Delta_C=1\) and is topologically slice. Piccirillo records the resulting vanishing of the abelian and metabelian sliceness obstructions. On \(C\) itself the rational Rasmussen invariant is \(s_{\mathbb Q}(C)=0\), and Piccirillo's trace-wide statement on p. 584 gives \(\epsilon=\tau=0\) on \(C\), on the Figure 2 partner \(K'\), and on every knot with that same zero trace. The numerical calculation in §3 gives \(s_{\mathbb Q}(K')=2\). Proposition 1.4 identifies the two zero traces, and Lemma 1.3 uses trace embedding in the *standard* \(S^4\) to transfer ordinary sliceness between their attaching knots. In that lemma the complement of a small coordinate ball at the cone point is the standard \(B^4\), the ambient manifold for the Rasmussen bound used here. [Piccirillo, pp. 582–584, Proposition 1.4, Lemma 1.3 and §3](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/wp-content/uploads/annals-v191-n2-p05-s.pdf) · [Manolescu, *Four-dimensional topology*, discussion of trace embeddings and \(s(C)\)](https://web.stanford.edu/~cm5/4D.pdf)

## Four-fold branched-cover obstruction

The targeted source search found a stronger obstruction on the Conway knot itself. In [Golla–Pinzón-Caicedo, arXiv:2609.22378v1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.22378v1), Theorem 1.2 asserts that the four-fold cyclic branched cover \(Y_4(C)\) is an integral homology sphere of infinite order in smooth rational homology cobordism. Its proof gives \(d(Y_4(C))>0\) in the authors' orientation. The nonzero correction term excludes a smooth rational homology ball bounding \(Y_4(C)\) by their Theorem 3.1. Reversal of knot orientation preserves the covering manifold by changing the choice of generator of the cyclic group. Mirroring reverses its orientation, which reverses the sign of \(d\) and preserves nonvanishing. The argument therefore applies to the frozen signed knot regardless of diagram conventions between the papers. The checked source is the 2026 preprint. Its Heegaard Floer computation is used at the strength of the stated theorem. [Primary preprint text, §§3–4 and proof of Theorem 1.2](https://borowiecki.dev/pdf/2609.22378)

To apply this to a filling, let \(W\) be any smooth oriented integral homology four-ball with a marked \(S^3\) boundary. Suppose a smooth proper disk \(D\subset W\) bounds the marked \(C\). The integral homology sequence of \((W,W\setminus D)\), with the normal Thom isomorphism, gives \(H_1(W\setminus D;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\) generated by a meridian, and a boundary knot meridian maps to this generator up to sign. Reduction modulo four defines a cyclic cover of the disk exterior, which fills by the normal model \(z\mapsto z^4\) along the disk to give a smooth four-manifold with boundary \(Y_4(C)\).

The cover factors into two double branched covers, each along a copy of \(D\). For either double cover \(X'\to X\), use cellular chains over \(\mathbb F_2\) relative to the fixed disk. An equivariant triangulation with the disk a subcomplex gives two free lifts of every quotient cell off the disk. Summing those lifts and projecting give the short exact sequence
\[
0\longrightarrow C_*(X,D;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow C_*(X',D;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow C_*(X,D;\mathbb F_2)\longrightarrow0.
\]
Since \(D\) and the base of the first cover are mod-two acyclic, \(H_*(X,D;\mathbb F_2)=0\). The sequence gives the same result upstairs, so the first cover is mod-two acyclic and the argument applies again. The four-fold cover is thus a mod-two homology ball and, by finite generation and the universal coefficient theorem, a rational homology ball. This contradicts the nonzero correction term.

Every coordinate puncture of a smooth homotopy four-sphere is an integral homology ball by Mayer–Vietoris. The preprint and this filling argument give \(g_\Sigma(C)\geq1=g_4(C)\) for every such \(\Sigma\), including the native puncture. A strict saving for this probe would require genus zero, so the obstruction rules out that saving throughout the domain.
