For each oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere \(\Sigma\), the reflected chart marking gives a well-defined genus function \(g_\Sigma\) on oriented knots. It agrees with ordinary smooth four-ball genus on the standard sphere. For the positive right-handed trefoil \(T=\widehat{\sigma_1^3}\), the value is \(g_\Sigma(T)=1\) on every such sphere. The upper and lower bounds also give genus one in every smooth oriented integral homology four-ball with boundary identified with the standard oriented \(S^3\).

## A puncture with an exact boundary marking

Let \(b\) be a positively oriented smooth coordinate embedding defined on a neighborhood of the closed unit \(B^4\subset\mathbb R^4\), and set \(W_b=\Sigma\setminus b(\operatorname{int}B^4)\). Use outward-normal-first boundary orientations. Fix the linear reflection \(R(x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)=(-x_1,x_2,x_3,x_4)\). The marking \(c_b=b\circ R|_{S^3}\colon S^3\to\partial W_b\) preserves orientation because the two sides of the removed sphere have opposite outward normals. For an oriented knot \(K\) in the model \(S^3\), minimize the genus of compact connected oriented smoothly properly embedded \(F\subset W_b\) with oriented boundary \(c_b(K)\). An oriented isotopy of \(K\) extends over a collar, and a boundary Seifert surface pushed into the collar gives a finite upper bound. In particular the minimum is attained among the nonnegative integer genera that occur.

**Chart comparison.** In any connected oriented smooth closed four-manifold, let \(b_0,b_1\) be two such embeddings. The maps \(x\mapsto b_i(t x)\) for \(r\leq t\leq1\) shrink the two balls in their respective charts. Fix an auxiliary Riemannian metric, put \(p_i=b_i(0)\), and write \(h_i=\exp_{p_i}^{-1}\circ b_i\) near \(0\), so \(A_i=dh_i(0)\) is a positive frame. For \(s>0\) use

\[
 x\longmapsto \exp_{p_i}\bigl(h_i(sr x)/s\bigr),
 \qquad
 h_i(sr x)/s = r\int_0^1 dh_i(u s r x)\,x\,du.
\]

The integral extends smoothly to \(s=0\), giving \(x\mapsto\exp_{p_i}(r A_i x)\); at \(s=1\) the map is \(b_i(r x)\). All maps are embeddings on a fixed neighborhood of the closed ball for small \(r>0\). Indeed \(h_i(sr x)/s\) is a scaled coordinate embedding for \(s>0\), is linear and invertible at \(s=0\), and stays in an injective exponential neighborhood by the uniform derivative bound in the integral. Now join the two centers by a smooth path \(p(t)\) and the frames by a positive frame path \(A(t)\) over it. Such a path exists by trivializing the oriented tangent bundle along the interval and using the connectedness of \(GL^+(4,\mathbb R)\). For all small \(r\) the maps \(x\mapsto\exp_{p(t)}(r A(t)x)\) are embeddings along the path, by a uniform injectivity radius on the compact path. A common small \(r\) makes all these steps valid.

Concatenate the paths, using smooth reparametrizations constant near their join times, to get \(e_t\) from \(b_0\) to \(b_1\) on a common neighborhood of \(B^4\). To extend this path, choose a smooth function \(\chi\) equal to one on \(B^4\) and supported inside that neighborhood. The time-dependent vector field

\[
 V_t(e_t(x))=\chi(x)\,\partial_t e_t(x)
\]

extends smoothly by zero and has a flow on the full time interval. Uniqueness of the flow gives \(\Phi_t\circ b_0=e_t\) on \(B^4\). The time-one orientation-preserving diffeomorphism sends \(W_{b_0}\) to \(W_{b_1}\) and satisfies \(\Phi_1\circ c_{b_0}=c_{b_1}\). Thus it transports the admissible surfaces in both directions with the same boundary orientations and genus. The minimum over charts equals the minimum for each chart. The proof uses paths of parametrized coordinate embeddings throughout.

**Standard sphere and naturality.** Give \(S^4\subset\mathbb R^4\times\mathbb R\) its boundary orientation. The maps

\[
 b_N(x)=\left(\frac{2x}{1+|x|^2},\frac{1-|x|^2}{1+|x|^2}\right),\qquad
 q_S(x)=\left(\frac{2Rx}{1+|x|^2},\frac{|x|^2-1}{1+|x|^2}\right)
\]

are positive coordinate embeddings about the north and south poles. Their orientation signs follow at the centers from the normals \(+e_5\) and \(-e_5\) and the tangent maps \(2I\) and \(2R\). The first takes \(B^4\) to the closed northern hemisphere. The second identifies \(B^4\) with the complement \(W_{b_N}\) of the open northern hemisphere and is exactly \(c_{b_N}\) on \(S^3\). Thus \(g_{S^4}(K)=g_4(K)\) for the specified signed knot. For an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(f\colon\Sigma\to\Sigma'\), the chart \(f\circ b\) gives the identical marking after applying \(f\), so \(g_\Sigma=g_{\Sigma'}\). A diffeomorphism onto the standard sphere can always be made orientation-preserving by composing it with a reflection of \(S^4\).

**Mirror and reversal.** Write \(\bar\Sigma\) for \(\Sigma\) with reversed ambient orientation and \(mK=R(K)\) with transported knot orientation. The chart \(b\circ R\) is positive on \(\bar\Sigma\), and its complement marking on the underlying boundary is \(b|_{S^3}=c_b\circ R|_{S^3}\). It follows that \(g_{\bar\Sigma}(K)=g_\Sigma(mK)\). Reversing the orientation of a spanning surface gives \(g_\Sigma(-K)=g_\Sigma(K)\), where \(-K\) here denotes reversal of the knot orientation alone. For \(S^4\), reflection and naturality identify the two ambient orientations, giving the standard mirror equality. The formula for a general sphere relates the two oriented profiles.

## The positive two-braid control

The oriented Seifert smoothing of the closure of \(\sigma_1^3\) has two circles, one for each strand. Span those circles by the two Seifert disks and attach a positive half-twisted band at each of the three crossings, in their order along the braid. The attachments join the two disks, and the resulting oriented boundary is the original positive braid closure. It has one component because the braid induces the transposition on two strands. The surface has Euler characteristic \(2-3=-1\) and one boundary component, hence genus one. To place it in an inward collar, embed it as the graph of a small nonnegative boundary-defining function on this Seifert surface, zero exactly at its boundary and with nonzero inward derivative there. This gives a smooth proper embedding with the same oriented boundary and genus, in the standard ball or in any marked puncture. The mirror surface is obtained by reflecting the boundary construction before pushing it into the collar.

The lower bound uses Ozsváth–Szabó, *Knot Floer homology and the four-ball genus*, Geom. Topol. **7** (2003), 615–639. Theorem 1.1 on p. 617 applies to a smooth oriented \(W\) with \(\partial W=S^3\), \(b_1(W)=0\) and \(b_2^+(W)=0\), and a smooth embedded surface \(F\) with boundary the knot \(J\) there. It gives \(2\tau(J)+\lVert[F]\rVert_1+[F]^2\leq 2g(F)\), with the norm defined just before the theorem using the negative diagonal form. Section 3.4 (pp. 630–631) treats both positive genus and disks. For an integral homology ball, the relative homology class is zero because \(H_2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z)=0\). Both homology terms vanish and \(\tau(J)\leq g(F)\). Apply the same theorem to \(-W\) with the boundary marking precomposed by \(R\). Its boundary knot in the model sphere is \(mJ\) and its two required Betti numbers are still zero. Lemma 3.3 (p. 627) gives \(\tau(mJ)=-\tau(J)\), so \(|\tau(J)|\leq g(F)\). Corollaries 1.3 and 1.7 (pp. 617–618) give the standard-ball bound and \(\tau(T_{p,q})=(p-1)(q-1)/2\) for the positive torus knot. The frozen braid is \(T_{2,3}\), so \(\tau(T)=1\). [Published text, arXiv:math/0301149](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0301149) · [Earlier project specialization](../../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/research-progress/handoffs/TO_06_PUNCTURE_KNOT_FILTER_20261127.md)

The inspected copy has a dropped factor of two on the right of the positive-genus conclusion displayed on p. 631. The immediately preceding lines give \(\lVert[F]\rVert_1+[F]^2+2m\leq 2g(F)-2\) for the integer \(m<\tau(J)\). Taking \(m=\tau(J)-1\) yields the theorem as stated. [Printed proof and theorem statement](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0301149)

For a homotopy sphere puncture, excision identifies \(H_i(\Sigma,W_b;\mathbb Z)\) with \(H_i(B^4,S^3;\mathbb Z)\). The map in degree four from the fundamental class of \(\Sigma\) sends it to a generator of this relative group. The long exact sequence and the homology of \(\Sigma\) then give \(H_*(W_b;\mathbb Z)\cong H_*(B^4;\mathbb Z)\). This verifies the ambient hypothesis for the frozen probe. Combining the inequalities yields

\[
 g_4(T)=g_\Sigma(T)=1,
 \qquad g_\Sigma(mT)=1.
\]

Rasmussen gives a second standard-ball comparison. Theorem 1 on p. 1 of arXiv:math/0402131v1 gives \(|s(J)|\leq 2g_4(J)\), with the bounding surface in \(B^4\) in the proof in §4.4. His positive-diagram computation in §5.2, p. 14, evaluates \(s(T)=3-2+1=2\). The bound on an arbitrary puncture above uses the verified Floer theorem. [Rasmussen, *Khovanov homology and the slice genus*, arXiv:math/0402131v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402131v1)

## Scope of the two predecessor comparisons

**Fixed ambient relative genus.** Manolescu–Marengon–Piccirillo study proper smooth surfaces in a punctured closed oriented four-manifold in a prescribed class in \(H_2(X^\circ,\partial X^\circ;\mathbb Z)\). Their introduction states this relative genus problem and Definition 2.1 calls the genus-zero condition in the zero class *H-sliceness*. That class restriction is automatic in a homotopy sphere puncture. Corollary 1.4 and its proof in §4.5 give H-sliceness of this right-handed trefoil in \(X=\#^3\mathbb{CP}^2\#^{20}\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\) and exclude H-sliceness in \(X'=K3\#\overline{\mathbb{CP}}^{\,2}\). The first side uses their null-homologous disk in \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) in Example 2.3, with the change from incoming to outward boundary orientation made there. The second uses the left-handed trefoil disk in K3 in Example 2.5 in a nonzero class of square zero, and Theorem 1.3 with the two closed symplectic manifolds K3 and its blow-up, both with \(b_2^+=3\). This comparison concerns the null-homologous genus-zero threshold on the stated indefinite pair. Their paragraph following Corollary 1.4 explicitly identifies the homotopy-sphere sliceness strategy. [Checked arXiv:2012.12270v2, 7 December 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2012.12270v2) · [Journal record, Math. Ann. **390** (2024), 1481–1506](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00208-023-02787-4)

**Minimizing over ambient balls.** Cochran–Harvey define \(g_{ex}(K)\) by minimizing the smooth genus over all smooth manifolds homeomorphic to \(B^4\) in §3.2, Definition 3.8, p. 2518. Their Proposition 3.9 identifies the genus-zero condition with sliceness in such a ball. The fixed-sphere \(g_\Sigma(K)\) uses one marked diffeomorphism type of puncture. The precise relationship is \(g_{ex}(K)=\min_\Sigma g_\Sigma(K)\), over oriented smooth homotopy spheres. Indeed van Kampen gives \(\pi_1(W_b)=0\) by adjoining the coordinate ball across its collared \(S^3\), and the preceding homology calculation and Whitehead's theorem give contractibility. Freedman's relative topological statement then identifies \(W_b\) topologically with \(B^4\). Conversely, given such a smooth ball \(W\) with an oriented boundary identification \(c:S^3\to\partial W\), glue on a smooth cap by \(c\circ R\). The capped manifold is a smooth closed simply connected integral homology four-sphere, hence a homotopy sphere by Hurewicz and Whitehead. Its cap parametrization extends across the seam using the gluing collar and is a positive coordinate chart whose complement marking is exactly \(c\). This gives both inequalities for the minimum. Topol. **18** (2018), 2509–2540, journal text](https://msp.org/agt/2018/18-5/agt-v18-n5-p01-p.pdf) · [Freedman, *The topology of four-dimensional manifolds*, J. The trefoil entries calibrate this specific probe. Their equality on the entire homotopy-sphere domain precludes a strict genus gap for this knot in the marked candidate.
