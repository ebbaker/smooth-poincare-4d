## Actual holonomy on the punctured base

The open set \(X\) is path connected: paths may be perturbed off finitely
many points, keeping their endpoints fixed, by transversality of a curve to
points in dimension four. Add pairwise disjoint open coordinate balls about
the deleted points. Each ball is simply connected and intersects the previous
union in a punctured ball, which retracts onto \(S^3\). Van Kampen gives
\(\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(M)=0\).

Fix a spin frame at \(x\) and let \(H\subset\mathrm{SU}(2)\) be the group
of actual holonomies. Every \(h\in H\) is joined to \(1\) by a smooth
path in \(H\). To see this, first reparametrize the smooth pieces of a
representative loop so that derivatives of every positive order vanish at
each join. This gives a smooth loop with the same transport. It admits a
continuous disk filling because \(X\) is simply connected. Make the filling
equal to the boundary loop along a radial collar by reparametrizing the
radius, then smooth the filling relative to a smaller collar by relative
smooth approximation. For the resulting smooth disk map \(D\), with
\(D(1)=x\), the loops
\[
 \gamma_s(t)=D\bigl(s+(1-s)e^{2\pi i t}\bigr),\qquad 0\le s,t\le 1,
\]
give the based contraction. Smooth dependence for the transport equation
gives the claimed path from \(h\) to \(1\).

For the group argument, let \(V\subset T_1\mathrm{SU}(2)=\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H\)
be the real span of velocities at \(1\) of smooth curves in \(H\) through
\(1\). Conjugation by elements of \(H\) preserves \(V\). If \(V=0\),
translation to the identity shows that all smooth paths in \(H\) are
constant, so \(H=\{1\}\). If \(\dim V=1\) or \(2\), the orthogonal
adjoint action preserves a line, namely \(V\) or its orthogonal complement.
Path connectedness forces it to fix that line pointwise. The centralizer
of a unit imaginary quaternion \(u\) is the circle
\(\{\cos t+u\sin t:t\in\mathbb R\}\), since the imaginary part of any
quaternion commuting with \(u\) is parallel to \(u\). Thus in each of
these dimensions \(H\) lies in a maximal torus. Finally, if \(\dim V=3\),
choose three curves through \(1\) with independent velocities. Their product,
with independent parameters, has invertible differential at the identity,
so the inverse function theorem puts an open identity neighborhood in
\(H\) itself. An open subgroup of the connected \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) is the
whole group. This argument applies to loop products in \(X\) and yields
actual holonomies without a closure step.

twice the cross product, hence no two-plane is a Lie subalgebra. Inspected

## The Chern number excludes a torus reduction

If \(H\) is proper, the group argument puts it in a torus, including the
trivial case. In the defining complex representation such a torus preserves
a complex line \(L_x\subset E_x\). Transport \(L_x\) along paths from
\(x\) to give \(L_y\subset E_y\) for every \(y\in X\). Any two paths differ
by a based holonomy preserving \(L_x\), so \(L\) is well defined. In a
small coordinate ball in \(X\), transport along straight segments from a
fixed interior point shows smoothness. The path definition makes \(L\)
parallel.

For a deleted point \(p\), fix a convex coordinate ball \(B\) containing
no other deleted point and fix \(y\in B\setminus\{p\}\). For every \(z\in B\)
transport \(L_y\) along the straight coordinate segment from \(y\) to \(z\)
using \(\nabla^+\) on \(M\). This defines a smooth line even at \(z=p\).
It agrees with \(L_z\) whenever the segment misses \(p\). Those endpoints
are dense in the punctured ball, so smoothness gives agreement throughout
\(B\setminus\{p\}\). The extension is parallel, since the covariant
derivative of its orthogonal projector vanishes off \(p\) and is continuous.
Extending at each deleted point gives a complex line \(L\) on the closed
\(M\). The unitary splitting and trivial determinant give
\(E\cong L\oplus L^{-1}\), so
\[
 c_2(E)=-c_1(L)^2=0\qquad\text{since }H^2(M;\mathbb Z)=0.
\]
This contradicts the frozen Chern number, and \(H=\mathrm{SU}(2)\).
The Chern-class obstruction uses the extended line on \(M\). Smooth
extension of the connection was used precisely at this step.

## Unmarked ordered-pair trace

In the chosen spin frame, take the unit quaternions \(a=i\) and
\(b_t=\cos t+j\sin t\), \(0\le t\le\pi/2\). They are independently
realized by based loops in \(X\) by the group result. Since
\(iji^{-1}=-j\),
\[
 a b_t a^{-1}b_t^{-1}=b_t^{-2}=\cos(2t)-j\sin(2t),\qquad
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C^2}(a b_t a^{-1}b_t^{-1})=2\cos(2t).
\]
The endpoints and the midpoint of the interval are attained at
\(t=0,\pi/2,\pi/4\), respectively. Every element of \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) has
trace twice its real quaternionic part, which gives the opposite range
inclusion. A change of spin frame conjugates both holonomies simultaneously
and preserves the trace. The freely varied range is therefore identical for
the unit round control and every smooth metric on every homotopy four-sphere,
including after finite deletion with the same smooth connection. The result
is scoped to unrestricted based loops in these domains and smooth metric
scales across the deleted points.
