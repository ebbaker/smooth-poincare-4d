**Disposition.** Working project deduction using the replayed A1, A2 and E04, Perutz's relative saddle, and Gerig's prior existence theorem. Every oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere admits a transverse pole triple for a suitable smooth Riemannian conformal class. The all-class minimum is zero exactly on the standard smooth sphere and one otherwise. The reduction can keep the pole, inverted chart and numerical leading coefficient of an input. The existence and minimum range over conformal classes, so transversality or zero removal for a prescribed class remains a separate question.

## 1. Domain and end cutoff

Let \(\Sigma\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere and \(p\in\Sigma\). For a smooth Riemannian conformal class \(c\), let \(U_{c,p}=\Lambda^-_{c,p}\). For \([u]\in\mathbb P(U_{c,p})\) choose any nonzero representative and use the normalization of A1 with a fixed representative metric \(g\in c\). The domain \(\mathcal T(\Sigma)\) consists of the triples \((c,p,[u])\) for which the resulting pole section of \(\Lambda^+_c\) is transverse to the zero section on \(X=\Sigma\setminus\{p\}\), including a section with empty zero set. Scaling \(u\) by a nonzero real number scales the section by the same number and preserves transversality and the zero set. The metric-rescaling rule in A1 §4 preserves the direction. An orientation-preserving diffeomorphism takes the star, differentiated end expansion and vanishing locus to those for the pulled-back data, by using the pullback representative metric. A1 uniqueness then gives naturality of the counts.

For any triple in \(\mathcal T(\Sigma)\), choose the oriented \(g\)-normal chart \(y\) at \(p\), set \(x=I(y)=y/|y|^2\), and let \(r=|x|\). Inversion reverses numerical orientation. In \(x\) use the orientation inherited from \(X\), for which the nonzero limiting constant \(\beta\) of A1 is Euclidean self-dual. Its coefficients in the numerical \(x\)-frame are precisely those of the anti-self-dual residue in \(y\). A1 gives \(\omega=\beta+da\), \(a=O_\infty(r^{-1})\), \(da=O_\infty(r^{-2})\). Thus all zeros are in a compact core. At each zero, differentiating the self-duality equation shows \(\operatorname{im}D\omega=\Lambda^+_c\) there by transversality. This is a wedge-positive three-plane. Off the zeros, \(\omega\wedge\omega=|\omega|_g^2\,dV_g>0\). Compactness and the regular zero-section theorem give a finite number \(n\) of embedded circles.

Take \(R\) so large that the end formula holds on a neighborhood of \(r\ge R\) and the zeros lie in \(r<R\). Choose \(\chi_R=1\) through \(r=R\), zero from \(2R\) on, and \(|d\chi_R|\le C/R\). Use \(\omega\) on the core and \(\beta+d(\chi_Ra)\) on the end. Both formulas agree where \(\chi_R=1\). The resulting \(\theta\) is smooth and closed. Its distance from \(\beta\) in flat norm on \(r\ge R\) is \(O(R^{-2})\), including the term \(d\chi_R\wedge a\). A fixed small flat-norm neighborhood of \(\beta\) lies in the positive wedge-square cone. For sufficiently large \(R\), the new form stays within it on the whole modified tail and retains the original zeros and first jets. The form is exactly \(\beta\) for \(r\ge 2R\). ## 2. Compactly supported merger

**Import.** Tim Perutz, *Zero-sets of near-symplectic forms*, J. Symplectic Geom. **4**(3), 237–257, DOI `10.4310/JSG.2006.v4.n3.a1`. The publisher PDF labels the volume 2007. I inspected arXiv `math/0601320v2` (15 January 2007), Definition 1.3, Theorem 1.4 and its §4 proof including Lemmas 4.2–4.3, formula (8), Proposition 4.4 and Remark 4.5. On a connected oriented four-manifold, the theorem gives a ball-supported smooth cobordism of closed forms joining two components of a near-symplectic zero set when \(n\ge2\). The endpoint has \(n-1\) components. Its proof uses an arc joining points on two different zero circles, with interior avoiding the zeros, and an embedded local model along that arc and short pieces of the two circles. The contact stability construction puts the varying model on a collar stationary near the boundary of the insertion region so it glues into the original manifold. Apply the support bound for the larger square in the proof of Lemma 4.3 and in §4.2, as recorded in LINE E03. The critical slice itself can have spatial derivative rank two. The endpoint is taken on the far side of the saddle. The relative support can be taken inside the interior of a connected compact truncation containing all zeros. Work on that open interior, with its inherited orientation, and choose an embedded joining arc with normal tangent at the endpoints. The complement of the zero circles in the interior is path connected by general position. The compact graph consisting of the arc and short pieces of the zero circles has a sufficiently small neighborhood whose closure is in this interior. The arc normalizations in Lemmas 3.1 and 4.2 and the stationary-boundary insertion in §4 give a deformation supported there. Consequently it extends to \(X\) by its initial form on the original outer region. Each step takes place on the same oriented smooth manifold and inside the same truncation. This proves preservation of the tail even when successive support regions meet.

The endpoints satisfy the strict jet condition of E04. In the new part of the refined model (8) at a zero, \(x_2=x_4=f=0\) with \(f=f_t^\delta(x_1,x_3)\). Write \(c_0=\delta\epsilon/2\), and \(f_i=\partial_i f\). At an endpoint \(df\ne0\) by Lemma 4.3. The spatial image of the first derivative is spanned by
\[
 b_1=\delta\,dx^{12}+dx^{34},\qquad
 b_2=\delta\,dx^{13}+dx^{42},\qquad
 b_3=-\delta\,dx^{14}-dx^{23}+c_0(f_1\,dx^{12}+f_3\,dx^{32}).
\]
Dividing their wedge matrix by \(2\delta\,dx^{1234}\) gives diagonal \((1,1,1+c_0 f_3)\) and only one off-diagonal pair, \((1,3)=(3,1)=\epsilon f_1/4\). The uniform \(C^1\) bound of Lemma 4.3 allows the small \(\epsilon\) in the model to be chosen with both corrections of magnitude at most \(1/4\). The matrix is then positive definite. The coordinate embeddings are orientation-preserving since they match positive forms along the symplectic portion. The collar adjustment acts near the cylindrical lateral boundary away from the model zeros. The zero-arc normalization uses the positive standard model (2), positive local interpolation of the first jets and a supported pullback. On unchanged portions the original jet is wedge-positive. This verifies the strict condition in the actual construction, where strictness is needed for realization. For \(n\ge2\), repeat the endpoint operation \(n-1\) times. For \(n=1\) take \(\theta\), and for \(n=0\) take the symplectic \(\theta\). These operations return a smooth closed form \(\theta_*\) equal to \(\beta\) on the distant end, with one circle if \(n>0\) and no zeros if \(n=0\). The zero-free case remains vacuously transverse. The theorem's merger hypothesis requires two distinct circles and leaves last-circle removal open.

## 3. Realization on the original compactification

Apply POLE E04 to \(\theta_*\) in the original \(y\)-chart. The closedness, positive square away from the zeros, wedge-positive rank-three jets and exact Euclidean self-dual tail have all been verified. E04 patches compatible positive three-planes by convex graph interpolation and takes an end representative equal to \(\delta_x\). The compact representative is \(\delta_y\) near \(p\), since \(I^*\delta_x=|y|^{-4}\delta_y\). Thus \(y\) is still an oriented normal chart, and A1 identifies this same form as the normalized pole form for the same numerical leading coefficient \(\beta\) and the new class \(c_*\). Rank three gives transversality in \(\Lambda^+_{c_*}\). This procedure takes place on the original smooth \(\Sigma\), including the original point \(p\). It uses the freedom to vary the class on the compactification. ## 5. Value and scope of the minimum

Define \(m(\Sigma)=\min_{(c,p,[u])\in\mathcal T(\Sigma)}\#\pi_0 Z(\omega_{c,p,u})\). The preceding existence argument makes the domain nonempty for every oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere. Section 2 bounds the minimum by one. If \(m(\Sigma)=0\), the A2 recognition theorem applies to the resulting zero-free pole form on the original smooth compactification. Conversely a diffeomorphism with the standard sphere pulls the round pole family back, with a standard reflection composed first if the orientations differ. In an oriented stereographic end the round conformal class is Euclidean, so the nonzero flat self-dual constants are its pole forms by A1 and are zero-free. Thus
\[
 m(\Sigma)=
 \begin{cases}
 0,&\Sigma\cong_{\mathrm{diff}} S^4,\\
 1,&\Sigma\text{ is a smooth homotopy four-sphere of any other smooth type}.
 \end{cases}
\]
The formula also gives the same value for the opposite orientation. The second case is conditional on such a smooth type. This identifies the minimum as a reformulation of A2's existential recognition test. It gives no distinguishing estimate for the candidate's actual marked class or any path that removes its last circle.
