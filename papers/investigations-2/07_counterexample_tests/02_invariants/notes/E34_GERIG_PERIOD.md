## The exact form on the disk

On \(\mathbb C^2\) write \(z=q_1+iq_2,\ w=q_3+iq_4\), and use the complex orientation. Gerig’s final form \(\Omega_\epsilon\) has a single twisted zero circle \(Z_\epsilon=\{w=0,\ |z|^2=\epsilon\}\). It equals \(\kappa\omega_\epsilon\) on the radius-\(\sqrt{2\epsilon}\) core and equals \(\Omega_0=dq_1\wedge dq_2+dq_3\wedge dq_4\) outside the radius-\(32\sqrt{2\epsilon}\) ball. In Taubes’s construction \(\kappa=T^{-1}>0\), with \(T\) sufficiently large after choosing small positive \(\epsilon\) and large \(R\) with \(R\sqrt{\epsilon}\gg1\). The source specifies the exact exterior coefficient \(1\) and leaves the core matching factor adjustable. [Gerig, Example 1.7, arXiv v3, p. 3][gerig] · [Taubes, §2, (2.1), (2.2), (2.5), (2.16)][taubes]

Set \(D_\epsilon=\{w=0,\ |z|^2\le\epsilon\}\), orient the disk by \(dq_1\wedge dq_2\), and orient \(Z_\epsilon\) counterclockwise as its boundary. The whole disk is inside the unchanged core. In the displayed form of the source the coefficient of \(\Omega_0\) is \(-\epsilon+|z|^2-|w|^2\). Every other summand contains a differential in the \(w\)-plane and pulls back to zero. Therefore

\[
\left.\Omega_\epsilon\right|_{D_\epsilon}
   =\kappa(|z|^2-\epsilon)\,dq_1\wedge dq_2,
\qquad
\int_{D_\epsilon}\Omega_\epsilon
   =2\pi\kappa\int_0^{\sqrt\epsilon}(s^2-\epsilon)s\,ds
   =-\frac{\kappa\pi\epsilon^2}{2}.
\]

The smooth closed form has a primitive \(\alpha_\epsilon\) on \(\mathbb R^4\), and two primitives differ by an exact one-form. Stokes gives the primitive period \(P(Z_\epsilon)=\int_{Z_\epsilon}\alpha_\epsilon=-\kappa\pi\epsilon^2/2\) with the specified orientation. The form is negative on the complex-oriented disk interior. Giving that interior the orientation induced by the restricted form yields clockwise boundary and period \(+\kappa\pi\epsilon^2/2\). Gerig’s assertion that the coordinate disk is \(i\)-holomorphic refers to the ordinary complex structure. Its complex orientation accounts for the negative sign. The \(R\)-dependent terms drop out of the integral. Matching can still affect its size through \(\kappa\). [Gerig, Example 1.7][gerig] · [Taubes, (2.2), (2.5)][taubes]

## Realization and the round control with the same residue

Use the standard smooth one-point compactification \(S^4\) of the oriented \(q\)-space, with pole \(p=\infty\). Let \(Q=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,1,-1)\) and \(I(v)=v/|v|^2\). The compactifying chart \(y=QI(q)\) is oriented because both factors reverse orientation. A1’s inverted chart \(x=I(y)=Qq\) consequently has inherited orientation opposite to its coordinate orientation. Both end forms have the identical constant coefficient

\[
\beta=dx_1\wedge dx_2-dx_3\wedge dx_4.
\]

This form is Euclidean self-dual in the inherited orientation. For the realization of \(\Omega_\epsilon\), the matching construction supplies smooth closedness, exterior equality, positivity off \(Z_\epsilon\), and absence of other zeros. The derivative condition at \(Z_\epsilon\) can be checked in the original core. The real coefficient \(-\epsilon+|z|^2-|w|^2\) has radial derivative \(2\sqrt{\epsilon}\) in the \(z\)-plane. The complex coefficient \(Rw-\bar z\bar w\) has \(w\)-derivative \(\delta w\mapsto R\delta w-\bar z\overline{\delta w}\), invertible over \(\mathbb R\) for \(R>\sqrt{\epsilon}\). These three real derivatives span the Euclidean positive three-plane generated by the Kähler form and the real and imaginary parts of \(dz\wedge dw\). Thus [POLE E04][e04] gives a smooth Riemannian class \(c_\epsilon\) on the specified compactification for which \(\Omega_\epsilon\) is self-dual and transverse. Choose its representative equal to \(\delta_y\) near \(p\). The exact end gives growth \(O(|y|^{-4})\) in this representative. [A1][a1] identifies \(\Omega_\epsilon\) as the pole form for the full residue

\[
\eta=dy_1\wedge dy_2-dy_3\wedge dy_4\in\Lambda_p^-,
\qquad |\eta|_{\delta_y}=\sqrt{2}.
\]

The unmodified \(\Omega_0\) is closed, zero-free, and self-dual for the Euclidean metric in \(q\) and hence for the round conformal class \(c_{\mathrm{rd}}\). This class also has a smooth representative equal to \(\delta_y\) near \(p\), since the round metric is conformally Euclidean in the same \(y\)-chart. Its exact tail gives the same \(O(|y|^{-4})\) growth and the same numerical residue \(\eta\). A1 identifies it as the round pole form. For a unit-residue convention, divide both forms by \(\sqrt2\) and the period magnitude becomes \(\kappa\pi\epsilon^2/(2\sqrt2)\). The zero-free control has no zero-circle period. If one keeps the coordinate disk as an auxiliary marking in the round example, its complex-oriented flux with tail \(\Omega_0\) is \(\pi\epsilon\). Its boundary is then an ordinary loop.

These are two controls at the same smooth compactification, pole, oriented compact chart, inverted chart and numerical residue. A1 uniqueness forces \(c_\epsilon\) and \(c_{\mathrm{rd}}\) to differ because their pole forms have different zero sets. The comparison establishes a nonzero period on a standard-sphere pole triple with a single twisted circle. It supplies two endpoints for the fixed-end comparison. A deformation through admissible forms connecting them would require a separate argument.
