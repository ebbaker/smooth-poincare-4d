A useful test should name a manifold, a piece of smooth geometry someone could actually exhibit, and a comparison theorem whose hypotheses a second person can check. There are two different prizes. One is an exotic sphere constructed from a pair of knots. The other is a proof that the particular minus Miller–Schupp sphere already marked in this repository is exotic. The first has a smaller missing geometric input.

## 1. Build a disk for a named knot in the ordinary four-ball

Dunfield and Gong, Theorem 5.14, give an unusually sharp target. A smooth properly embedded disk in the standard four-ball with boundary the knot `16n68278` would imply an exotic smooth four-sphere. Their Table 11 identifies a second knot with the same zero-surgery and a smooth sliceness obstruction. The table's diagram code describes the three-component surgery link. The knot diagram for the disk comes from their named-knot data. Keeping the objects separate matters even for a successful disk movie. The authors already tried diagrammatic bands, perturbed diagrams, cached ribbon links and connected sums with ribbon knots. [Dunfield–Gong, §§1.19, 2.2–2.6, 2.10, 5.13–5.14 and Table 11](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.21825v1)

One different search space would allow one interior local maximum of the radial movie. Start at the empty link, end at the exact target, and record an isotopy or elementary oriented birth, band saddle or death at each step. For a connected orientable surface with one boundary component, the disk condition is births minus saddles plus deaths equal to one. Each move must be embedded in its own slab, with genuine disjoint capping disks at the extrema. A local cancelling saddle–death pair can pad a ribbon movie, so count the move class honestly. First test whether it adds any actual presentations beyond previously covered ones. The first-series two-face and framed-band calculations suggest how to record the band feet, twist, intervening isotopies and gluing. They are worked examples on other diagrams. A replayable ribbon disk would suffice too. Failure with one maximum and finite diagram bounds would speak only to that list of movies. ## 2. Extract a low-genus surface in the repository's presentation sphere

Take the original marked minus Miller–Schupp boundary sphere ΣP and remove a small, parametrized coordinate ball. Look for a knot J in its boundary and a compact, smooth, connected, properly embedded orientable surface of genus g spanning J in the puncture. If the ordinary rational Rasmussen invariant satisfies |s(J)| > 2g, standardness of ΣP would contradict the standard-ball genus bound. The coordinate chart identifies the small ball and its boundary knot convention. Arbitrary embedded separating three-spheres would introduce a separate filling question. Branch 05 supplies the marked five-dimensional presentation and its four-dimensional boundary. Branch 01 supplies charts and both directions of its marked seams. Neither return contains the required surface. [05 marking](../../../05_certificates/notes/E06_PRESENTATION_HANDLES.md) · [01 atlas](../../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E07_MARKED_ATLAS.md) · [Rasmussen, Theorem 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0402131)

Prefer a small surface that the handle data actually display. Record transitions on every piece, framing, interior disjointness, genus and the signed boundary diagram before seeking an invariant value. There is a cheap exclusion. Every such surface already satisfies |τ(J)| ≤ g by the homology-ball genus bound. For a disk, τ must vanish, and a Legendrian representative with nonnegative Thurston–Bennequin number is impossible in the standard tight boundary sphere. Thus τ = 0 and s ≠ 0 is an eligibility test for a knot, with the geometric disk still to be found. For a control, a standard chart ball reproduces the ordinary genus inequality. Branch 06 also documents known Gluck cases in which this approach is blind. ## 3. Obstruct a disk that must live in the standard ball

The other sign of Dunfield–Gong's theorem singles out `18nh00000601`. Proving that this knot bounds no smooth disk in the standard four-ball would produce an exotic sphere. The same theorem already gives it a smooth disk in a homotopy four-ball. This rules out every proposed obstruction that also forbids a disk in an arbitrary smooth integral homology ball with this boundary. A sensible first experiment on paper would take one precisely stated sliceness obstruction, identify the step that uses standardness of the filling, and check whether its hypotheses hold for this knot. Without such a step or a new applicable obstruction, reserve this route. Failure to find a ribbon or one-maximum disk would leave the required nonsliceness open. [Dunfield–Gong, Theorem 5.14 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.21825v1)

## 4. Detect a class after closing the actual handle presentation

A closed invariant could distinguish ΣP without choosing a boundary knot. A concrete version would give a graded class and a functional detecting it in a degree where the standard sphere's closed module vanishes. The functional has to descend through all the relations in that version of the invariant, including those from one- and three-handles and all relevant cables. A truncated relation matrix can suggest a class that becomes zero in the full quotient. Branch 06 owns the choice of invariant and has declined a computation without control of that quotient. Reopen here only for a named class, a specified comparator and a finite certificate or uniform proof of descent. ## 5. Develop an invariant from the constructions themselves

**Minimum genus as a function of the boundary knot.** Fix an oriented homotopy sphere Σ and an oriented knot K in the standard S³. Consider the smallest genus of a connected orientable surface spanning K in a small, parametrized coordinate puncture of Σ. Write it as \(g_\Sigma(K)\), allowing all compatible coordinate charts in the definition. On S⁴ the comparator is the ordinary smooth four-ball genus \(g_4(K)\). A marked surface of genus g gives an upper bound on the candidate value. A standard-ball lower bound strictly greater than g would already distinguish them, even if neither minimum were known exactly. For example, record the lower bound \(\lvert s_{\mathbb Q}(K)\rvert/2-g\) on the gap \(g_4(K)-g_\Sigma(K)\). A fixed finite list of knots chosen before comparison makes a reusable set of probes. A failure on that list leaves all other knots open.

Relative genus and sets of slice knots in punctured four-manifolds have an established history. Manolescu, Marengon and Piccirillo give a homeomorphic pair of closed four-manifolds distinguished at genus zero by a specified knot. Their example has nonzero second homology. Cochran and Harvey's exotic slice genus instead minimizes over all smooth four-balls homeomorphic to B⁴, which forgets which sphere supplied a witness. [Cochran–Harvey, Definition 3.8](https://msp.org/agt/2018/18-5/agt-v18-n5-p01-p.pdf)

The possible contribution here is a way to certify entries from the repository's marked handles, together with a sensitive probe for a homotopy sphere. Check the literature, invariance under smooth diffeomorphisms, dependence on orientation or chart, the standard calibration and computable one-sided bounds before proposing any new invariant. [Manolescu–Marengon–Piccirillo, introduction and Corollary 1.4](https://web.stanford.edu/~cm5/slice4D.pdf)

**The least number of circles among transverse pole forms.** On an oriented smooth homotopy sphere consider, provisionally, the minimum number of zero circles among all transverse members of its normalized pole families as the smooth Riemannian conformal class, pole and nonzero residue direction vary. With a transverse form and a zero-free punctured end the zero set is a compact one-manifold. The round sphere supplies the control value zero. Record whether the domain is nonempty before assigning any value. A marked atlas could certify an upper bound from one transverse member. The difficult and more interesting question is whether the marked analysis can yield a lower bound stable under *all* admissible changes. Counting the last circle for one metric or capping construction cannot do this. There is an immediate threat to this proposal. Perutz's merging operation can reduce a transverse near-symplectic zero set with at least two circles, while 01 gives a route back to a normalized pole after changing conformal class with a prescribed inverted end. Check the relative-end and realization hypotheses first. If they compose on this domain, the all-class minimum can only be zero or one wherever defined. The closed set of bad residue directions at a fixed conformal class and pole retains more marked information and has its own independent replay. As a first investment, prioritize the genus profile for a finite witness and investigate the pole count for a genuinely analytic invariant. Either could be valuable without a full answer to smooth Poincaré if it gives a reproducible comparison on another smooth pair. Keep smooth invariance, nontriviality on a control, computability and power to distinguish a homotopy sphere as four separate questions.
