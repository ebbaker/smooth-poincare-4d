## Smooth transmission at the whole seam

Use the fixed oriented Riemannian product \(M=[-1,1]\times Y\), \(Y=\mathbb R^3/\mathbb Z^3\), with the periodic spin lift and common parallel spin frame at \(t=0\). The supplied positive half-spinors, with the chirality acted on by self-dual forms, are smooth up to their closed pieces. They have matching nowhere-zero seam trace \(s\) and fixed outer traces. Prescribed transverse zeros with fixed Clifford-compatible first jets, if present, are away from the seam. A collar of \(Y\) on the two sides is zero-free. The first variation uses the real spinor pairing, \(S=J_g^4\), \(P_X=\nabla_X+\tfrac14X\cdot D\), and \(X\cdot X=-|X|^2\). For supplied stationary pieces, the shrinking-sphere terms at the prescribed zeros vanish by E03, and the seam term is the sum of the outward densities \(4W_\pm(\nu_\pm)\,dA\), \(W=|\psi|^{-4}|P\psi|^2P\psi\). Arbitrary smooth seam tests extend with support in the zero-free collar, so the joined field is weakly stationary in the fixed-jet class exactly when those outward covectors cancel in the real trace dual. [Fixed-jet excision](../notes/E03_FIXED_JETS.md) · [E04 transmission](../notes/E04_GLUING.md)

On the seam the outward normals are \(+\partial_t\) on the minus piece and \(-\partial_t\) on the plus piece, with common positive area. Common tangential first derivatives give \(q_i=Az_i+C\), where \(q_i=P\psi_i\), \(z_i=\partial_t\psi_i\), \(A=\Pi(dt\otimes\cdot)\), and \(C\) is the common tangential contribution. The orthogonal Clifford projection gives \(A^*A=\tfrac34 I\) and \(A^*W=W(\partial_t)\) on the projection range. Thus cancellation equates \(N(z_i)=4|s|^{-4}A^*(|q_i|^2q_i)\). The real-pairing identity

\[
(|q_1|^2q_1-|q_2|^2q_2,q_1-q_2)
=\tfrac12(|q_1|^2+|q_2|^2)|q_1-q_2|^2
+\tfrac12(|q_1|^2-|q_2|^2)^2
\]

is at least \(\tfrac14|q_1-q_2|^4\). Substituting \(q_1-q_2=A(z_1-z_2)\) gives the formalization handoff's \(\tfrac9{16}|s|^{-4}|z_1-z_2|^4\) bound for \((N(z_1)-N(z_2),z_1-z_2)\). The first jets therefore match even at zero defect. Where the common \(q=P\psi\) is nonzero, the double-normal Hessian block is bounded below by \(3|s|^{-4}|q|^2I\). Solving the bulk equation for \(\partial_t^2\psi\) and differentiating in \(t\) gives all higher normal derivatives from the traces of lower normal derivatives and their tangential derivatives. Induction works on the open nonzero-defect set and continuity extends matching to its closure. [E04 normal jet calculation](../notes/E04_GLUING.md#first-jets-and-regularity-across-the-seam)

On a connected coordinate patch \(U\) in the interior of the zero-defect set, the common twistor first jet gives \(\partial_i s=e_i\cdot a\) and \(\partial_t\psi_\pm=e_0\cdot a\) on \(U\). Commuting tangential derivatives of \(s\) in the common parallel frame gives \(e_i\cdot b_j=e_j\cdot b_i\), \(b_j=\partial_j a\). For distinct indices this gives \(b_j=e_j\cdot e_i\cdot b_i\). The chains \(1\to3\) and \(1\to2\to3\) give opposite values of \(b_3\), so \(b_1=b_2=b_3=0\). The resulting constant \(a_0\) defines a local affine twistor spinor \(T=s(x_*)+(te_0+\sum_i(x^i-x_*^i)e_i)\cdot a_0\), which has the common trace and normal first derivative and satisfies \(PT=0\).

If \(w=\psi_\pm-T\) has all normal derivatives through order \(m-1\) zero as functions on \(U\), \(m\ge2\), its smooth one-sided Taylor expansion with integral remainder is \(w=t^m(h+tR)\), where \(h=\partial_t^m w(0,\cdot)/m!\). The expansion differentiates tangentially on each compact subpatch. Thus \(Pw=t^{m-1}(mAh+O(t))\), and \(W=t^{3m-3}(|s|^{-4}m^3|Ah|^2Ah+O(t))\), with the bracketed errors smooth after division by \(t\). These factorizations use the same signed \(t\) on both sides, since the norm square in \(W\) contributes the even factor \(t^{2m-2}\). The normal divergence in the Euler equation can have order \(t^{3m-4}\), tangential divergence starts at \(t^{3m-3}\), and the denominator force starts at \(t^{4m-4}\). Dividing the normalized bulk equation \(4(P^*W-|\psi|^{-6}|P\psi|^4\psi)=0\) by the signed power \(t^{3m-4}\), its limit on either side is

\[
-4(3m-3)|s|^{-4}m^3|Ah|^2 A^*Ah
=-\tfrac94(3m-3)m^3|s|^{-4}|h|^2h.
\]

This forces \(h=0\) at each point of \(U\). Induction applies to each one-sided field, and tangential differentiation of the normal trace identities gives every mixed derivative. The closure of the nonzero-defect set and the interior of the zero-defect set cover \(Y\). The matching derivatives give a smooth join by induction, using the common trace for tangential derivatives and matching one-sided difference quotients for normal derivatives. Continuity then gives the classical Euler equation at the seam. The Taylor comparison determines all seam jets. Uniqueness of a smooth field with a flat remainder relative to \(T\) is outside this result. [E10 proof](../notes/E10_DEGENERATE_SEAM.md)

## Sobolev composition at \(b=1/200\)

On the same cylinder and its two closed halves, set \(w=e^{-bt}\psi-\sigma\) for the fixed unit parallel \(\sigma\). With \(a=bA\sigma\), \(k=|a|\), the density is \(F(w,dw)=|a+Pw+bAw|^4|\sigma+w|^{-4}\). The base Hessian is bounded and has the E08 pointwise lower bound \(k^2(|Pv|^2-134b^2|v|^2)\). On any of the three intervals of length \(\ell\le2\), zero Dirichlet trace gives \(\|Pv\|_2^2=\tfrac34\|\nabla v\|_2^2\) by zero extension and the flat symbol identity, and \(\|v\|_2^2\le\ell^2\|\partial_t v\|_2^2\). These bounds yield coercivity with \(c_b=7k^2/50>0\) in the \(H^1\) norm on all three products. The base coefficients remain constant after restriction. The normal principal matrix remains \(3k^2I+6k^2\sigma\otimes\sigma\). Riesz representation gives the weak inverse, tangential Fourier truncation controls derivatives of tangential order at most four in \(H^1\), and the invertible normal block recovers the remaining derivatives through total order five for \(H^3\) forcing. At order four in the tangential step, the forcing is controlled in the dual of \(H^1_0\) by moving one tangential derivative onto the test. The endpoint trace lift by cutoff exponential Fourier modes fits each half with disjoint end collars. The same \(H^5\) bulk and two-end trace inverse, and hence the same local inverse-function argument in the \(H^5\) and \(H^3\) orders, hold on each half. [E08 inverse](../notes/E08_LOCAL_DIRICHLET.md#homogeneous-dirichlet-inverse)

Write \(X=H^{9/2}(Y;S^+_{\mathbb R})\) for one trace and \(X'=H^{-9/2}\) for its dual through the real \(L^2\) pairing. For \(q=(q_-,q_+)\) sufficiently close to the base pair in \(X^2\), restriction and trace of the smooth full-cylinder solution map provide a smooth middle trace \(r(q)\). Shrink the outer trace neighborhood so both restrictions lie in the chosen half-cylinder solution neighborhoods. They are the corresponding half solutions by uniqueness and have cancelling outward momenta. Conversely, take a nearby middle trace \(r\) with cancellation. The half fields have common trace, and integrating weak first derivatives separately cancels their seam terms. The join belongs to \(H^1\) with a bounded first jet almost everywhere, using \(H^5\) on each half. The half Euler equations and opposite fluxes annihilate the joined first variation on smooth fixed-outer-trace tests, since the smooth common seam trace of each test pairs with the sum of the two covectors in \(X'\). Since the denominator is uniformly positive and both first jets are bounded, this integral first variation is a continuous linear functional of an \(H^1_0(M)\) test. Density extends the vanishing to all such tests. In conjugated variables the same conclusion follows by using \(e^{bt}\eta\) as the original variation for \(\eta\in H^1_0(M)\).

In particular test the difference of the joined and full solutions in conjugated variables, \(z=w_{\mathrm{join}}-w_{\mathrm{full}}\in H^1_0(M)\). The affine segment \(w_\theta=w_{\mathrm{full}}+\theta z\) has uniformly small first jet almost everywhere on the two halves. For every test \(v\in H^1_0\), the difference between its integrated Hessian and the base quadratic form is bounded by \(\varepsilon\|v\|_{H^1}^2\). This follows from the uniform operator norm of the Hessian coefficient difference as a function of finite-dimensional first jets, with the same bound on both pieces. After shrinking, take \(\varepsilon<c_b/2\). The first-variation difference on \(z\) is the integral of this Hessian on \((z,z)\) over the segment, by the pointwise fundamental theorem of calculus and the integrable bound \(C(|z|^2+|dz|^2)\). This uses an integral identity on the bounded segment and the two weak first-variation functionals. It yields \(0\ge (c_b/2)\|z\|_{H^1}^2\). The join therefore equals the full solution, recovers its global \(H^5\) representative, and has middle trace \(r(q)\). [First-jet Hessian estimate](../notes/LOCAL_MINIMUM_AND_SMOOTH_DATA.md) · [Composition proof](../notes/LOCAL_FULL_TRACE_COMPOSITION.md)

Let \(F_\pm\) be the half-cylinder on-shell actions in the original trace variables. The middle derivative of \(G(q,r)=F_-(q_-,r)+F_+(r,q_+)\) is exactly the sum of the outward covectors. Additivity gives the full on-shell action \(\mathcal A(q)=G(q,r(q))\), and the chain rule drops the middle term because \(D_rG(q,r(q))=0\). Thus the outer covectors equal \(d\mathcal A\) with incoming sign reversed in the signed relation. The actual matching locus is a smooth graph over \(q\). Each half solution map is smooth into \(H^5\), its flux is smooth into \(H^4\), and its momentum density is smooth into \(H^{7/2}(Y)\) by trace. Consequently the derivative of the momentum residual factors into \(H^{7/2}\) before inclusion into \(X'\). A nonzero constant parallel spinor times a point delta on the torus lies in \(H^{-9/2}\) by the Fourier weights and lies outside \(H^{7/2}\). This gives a direct failure of surjectivity in the real Hilbert dual used by the statement.
