## Signed determinant and coercivity

Under the identification with ordered frame \((1,I_1,I_2,I_3)\) and \(\sigma=1\), \(c(X)\colon S^-\to S^+\) acts by left multiplication by \(X\). The adjoint of Clifford contraction has the same range as \(X\mapsto Xq\), so these matrices form the kernel of the real orthogonal twistor projection. Right multiplication commutes with that projection. For a unit quaternion \(q/|q|\), right multiplication is orthogonal and orientation preserving by continuity on the connected unit sphere and the value at \(1\). Hence for \(A_q(X)=Xq\), \(|A_q|=2|q|\) in Frobenius norm and \(\det A_q=|q|^4\), also at \(q=0\). A compatible transverse jet has this form with \(q\ne0\), so its normalized field has degree \(+1\) on the outward small link. Stokes on the complement of small balls gives the sum of the link degrees as the degree integral on the outer boundary, which vanishes by the constant angular traces. Thus the smooth admissible class is zero-free. [E14 quaternion convention](../notes/E14_FIRST_NEGATIVE_MODE.md#clifford-sign-and-stationarity) · [B09 zero argument](../notes/B09_GLOBAL_AMPLITUDE.md#the-smooth-trace-class-and-its-zeros)

For \(\psi=e^v g\), \(m_i=\partial_i v+(\partial_i g)\bar g\) has real row \(dv\) and imaginary rows the right-translated differential of \(g\). At \(1\in S^3\), the outward-normal-first boundary orientation is \((I_1,I_2,I_3)\). Orienting at \(g\) by right translation gives the same sphere orientation since right multiplication preserves the ambient orientation and sends the normal \(1\) to \(g\). With the unnormalized sphere volume \(\Omega\), this proves \(\det m\,dV=dv\wedge g^*\Omega\) in the specified product orientation. Its integral is \(\int_{\partial M}v g^*\Omega=0\). The same right translation gives \(|m|^2=|dv|^2+|dg|^2\) and \(S=\int|\Pi m|^4\).

For an arbitrary matrix \(m=A_q+R\), \(R=\Pi m\), the column expansion and Hadamard's bound give \(|\det m-|q|^4|\le\sum_{j=1}^4\binom4j|q|^{4-j}|R|^j\). Absorption by Young's inequality gives \(\det m\ge |q|^4/2-C_0|R|^4\). Combining with \(|m|^4\le128|q|^4+8|R|^4\) proves \(|m|^4\le256\det m+(256C_0+8)|\Pi m|^4\) for every real matrix. The absence of an absolute value on the determinant permits integration. With fixed traces this controls both polar derivatives by the action.

## Critical density and strong compactness

Extend \((v,g)\) by the constants \((\pm b,\sigma)\) past the respective ends and periodically in space. Matching traces cancel the boundary terms in the weak derivative. Let \(g_\epsilon\) be its convolution using a nonnegative smooth kernel supported on a radius-\(\epsilon\) ball. The mean oscillation about the ball average \(a\) is bounded by \(C(\int_{B_\epsilon}|dg|^4)^{1/4}\), by Poincaré and the dimension-four scaling. The supremum of this quantity goes to zero by absolute continuity of the \(L^1\) integral of \(|dg|^4\) on a fixed periodic enlargement. Since \(|g|=1\), \(\operatorname{dist}(a,S^3)\le\fint |g-a|\). The convolution differs from \(a\) by at most a constant times the same mean oscillation. Thus \(g_\epsilon\) stays uniformly in a shrinking neighborhood of \(S^3\). The radial normalization map there has uniformly bounded differential. The mollified differential converges strongly in \(L^4\), and the differential of the normalization tends in measure to its value at \(g\), which acts as the identity on \(dg\). These facts give strong \(W^{1,4}\) convergence after normalization. For the smoothed \(v\) and the normalized \(g\), composing by \(t\mapsto(1+2\epsilon)t\) restores the exact traces since the two preimages of the end faces sample constant regions of the extension. The pullbacks are uniformly bounded on \(W^{1,4}\) on the enlargement and converge strongly to restriction for each fixed extended map, by smooth approximation. This verifies density with the traces. In a strongly converging sequence of polar pairs, the formula for \(m\) gives strong \(L^4\) convergence by boundedness of \(g\) and convergence in measure. The action and integrated determinant are therefore continuous. In particular the integral vanishes also for every relaxed pair.

The derivative estimate, Poincaré with the fixed traces, and compact \(W^{1,4}\) embedding into \(L^4\) give a subsequence with a weak \(W^{1,4}\) and strong \(L^4\) limit, with almost everywhere convergence and the same traces. The angular limit stays unit. The multiplier \(\bar g_n\) is bounded and converges almost everywhere. Applied to any fixed \(L^{4/3}\) test coefficient it converges strongly, giving the weak \(L^4\) limit \(m\) of \(m_n\). Weak lower semicontinuity and the strong smooth recovery just proved identify the minimum with the smooth infimum. Convergence of \(\|\Pi m_n\|_4\) and uniform convexity yield strong \(L^4\) convergence of \(\Pi m_n\). This argument also applies to minimizing sequences in the relaxed class.

For \(U_n=(v_n,g_n)\in W^{1,4}(M;\mathbb R^5)\), every order-\(j\) minor of \(dU_n\), \(1\le j\le3\), converges weakly in \(L^{4/j}\). For the induction, testing \(d(U_n^{i_1}\,dU_n^{i_2}\wedge\cdots\wedge dU_n^{i_j})\) uses strong \(L^4\) convergence of \(U_n^{i_1}\) and the inductive weak \(L^{4/(j-1)}\) convergence of the remaining wedge. On each compactly supported smooth test form, the first factor converges in the needed dual \(L^{4/(5-j)}\). The identity follows for Sobolev maps by ordinary strong approximation in Euclidean components. Uniform \(L^{4/j}\) bounds and reflexivity identify the weak limits on all of \(M\). The angular right-multipliers and coefficients in the row minors of \(m_n\) are bounded and converge almost everywhere. The Cauchy–Binet formula and the test multiplier argument transfer the same weak convergence to the minors of \(m_n\).

Expanding \(\det(m_n-m)\), the two pure terms integrate to zero by the signed identity and density. Each mixed term integrates a \(j\)-minor of \(m_n\) times a complementary \((4-j)\)-minor of \(m\) in \(L^{4/(4-j)}\), its Hölder dual. Its limit equals the corresponding term with \(m_n=m\). The full sum therefore tends to the integral of \(\det(m-m)=0\). The matrix inequality applied to \(m_n-m\), together with strong convergence of \(\Pi m_n\), now gives \(m_n\to m\) in \(L^4\). The real part recovers \(dv_n\), and \(\operatorname{Im}(m_n)g_n\) recovers \(dg_n\). The same multiplier argument in the strong topology yields strong \(W^{1,4}\) convergence for the pairs. The proof uses only weak convergence of the lower minors and the prescribed integrals of the pure fourth minors.

## Physical convergence

Fix a smooth \(v_0\) with the same end traces and arbitrarily close to \(v\) in \(W^{1,4}\). For \(a>0\) take it close enough that \(24a\|d(v_n-v_0)\|_4<1/2\) for large \(n\). With \(f_n=e^{a(v_n-v_0)}\), the trace of \(f_n-1\) vanishes. E12's scalar bound and Hölder give

\[
\|f_n\|_4\le2^{1/4}+24a\|d(v_n-v_0)\|_4\|f_n\|_4 .
\]

For relaxed pairs one first truncates \(v_n-v_0\) on both sides before exponentiating, applies the same inequality, and uses Fatou to remove the truncation. Taking \(a=2\) gives the asserted uniform \(L^8\) bound for \(e^{v_n}\), hence strong \(L^4\) convergence to \(e^v\) by convergence in measure. Hölder with \(m_n g_n\to mg\) in \(L^4\) proves strong \(L^2\) convergence of \(d\psi_n=e^{v_n}m_n g_n\). The fields converge strongly in \(L^2\) as well. The derivative identity for the relaxed exponential follows from truncation and the same integrability. Choosing larger \(a\) gives the corresponding strong \(W^{1,p}\) physical convergence for every \(1\le p<4\), using \(1/p=1/4+1/r\) and strong \(L^r\) convergence of \(e^{v_n}\). At a strict interior slope E25 applied to smooth recovery sequences gives radial uniqueness in the relaxed space, and the full sequence converges to it. Compactness alone leaves the endpoint limits in the compact set of relaxed minimizers. [Interior argument](../notes/B08_INTERIOR_COMPACTNESS.md)

Physical \(W^{1,4}\) is already unbounded on a smooth minimizing sequence at \(b=0\). In an interior ball of fixed radius \(r_0\), write \(s=\log(r_0/r)\). Let \(w_n=0\) for \(s\le0\), let \(d w_n/ds=1/n\) on \([1,n^2-1]\), and use smooth cutoffs between \(0\) and \(1/n\) in the adjacent unit intervals. Extend constantly toward the center and by zero outside the ball. Then \(\|dw_n\|_4^4\le |S^3|n^{-2}\), and for \(\psi_n=e^{w_n}\sigma\) at \(b=0\), \(S(\psi_n)=(9/16)\|dw_n\|_4^4\to0\). Yet \(\|d\psi_n\|_4^4\) is at least \(|S^3|n^{-4}\int_1^{n^2-1}e^{4(s-1)/n}\,ds\), which diverges.

## Coexistence and the unrestricted endpoint

Assume \(b_{\rm G}<b_{\rm H}\). Choose \(b_n\downarrow b_{\rm G}\) and smooth strict competitors at \(b_n\). Subtract \((b_n-b_{\rm G})t\) from each \(v_n\), keeping \(g_n\) fixed. The \(L^4\) norms of the projected matrices are bounded by the radial actions. The change in those matrices is a constant array tending to zero in \(L^4\), so the actions differ by \(o(1)\). Because the radial field minimizes at the closed endpoint, the transferred sequence is minimizing there. It has a strongly converging polar subsequence.

Put \(u=v-bt\), \(Q=|\Pi m|^2\), and \(C=3/4\). In E17's \(K_i=\sum_{j\ne i}(g,c_ic_j\partial_jg)\), the term formed with \(\sigma\) in the first slot is divergence-free, since \(c_ic_j=-c_jc_i\) and mixed derivatives commute. Its pairing with \(du\) vanishes by the trace. The real Clifford vectors formed with \(g-\sigma\) in the corresponding slots are orthogonal with common length \(|g-\sigma|\) for each fixed \(j\), so the remaining current has length at most \(\sqrt3|g-\sigma||dg|\). E12 gives \(\|g-\sigma\|_4\le24\|dg\|_2\). E17's helicity estimate, with \(b_{\rm H}=2C(17\pi/8)\), now gives

\[
\int_M Q-2Cb^2
\ge C\|du\|_2^2+C(1-b/b_{\rm H})\|dg\|_2^2
-12\sqrt3\,\|du\|_2\|dg\|_2\|dg\|_4 .
\]

All terms and this estimate extend to the relaxed space by the established density and continuity. If \(0<b<b_{\rm H}\) and \(\|dg\|_4<\sqrt{1-b/b_{\rm H}}/(8\sqrt3)\), the right side is positive unless \(du=dg=0\), by the discriminant of the quadratic form. Jensen gives \(S>9b^4/8\) in the positive case. This verifies uniform exclusion of strict competitors approaching the radial pair when the slopes stay in a compact subinterval of \((0,b_{\rm H})\). The minimizing subsequence at \(b_{\rm G}\) therefore has a nonradial limit. Its angular part is nonconstant, since \(g=\sigma\) leaves an action strictly convex in \(dv\) and Jensen forces \(u=0\) at equality. In particular any other relaxed minimizer at \(b_{\rm G}<b_{\rm H}\) must have \(\|dg\|_4\ge\sqrt{1-b_{\rm G}/b_{\rm H}}/(8\sqrt3)\). [E17](../notes/E17_POLAR_LOWER_BOUND.md) · [Closed-interval comparison](../notes/B08_GLOBAL_TRANSITION.md)

The slope comparison also gives a necessary first-derivative consequence. Set \(F(b)=b^{-4}\inf S_b\) for \(b>0\). In normalized coordinates \(v=bt+u\), a fixed pair gives \(\Phi_{u,g}(s)=\int|X+sY|^4\), with \(s=1/b\), \(X=\Pi(dt\otimes 1)\), \(Y=\Pi m(u,g)\). If \(Y=0\), coercivity at zero traces for \(u\) gives the radial pair. Any other pair that coexists at \(b_{\rm G}\) yields a strictly convex \(\Phi\) with \(\Phi(0)=\Phi(1/b_{\rm G})=9/8\), hence \(\Phi'(1/b_{\rm G})>0\). Testing this fixed pair gives a strictly negative upper right difference quotient for \(F\), whose left derivative at \(b_{\rm G}\) is zero. In fact \(F'_+(b_{\rm G})\) equals the minimum of \(-b_{\rm G}^{-2}\Phi'_{u,g}(1/b_{\rm G})\) over relaxed minimizers there. For the reverse bound choose minimizers at \(b_{\rm G}+h\) as \(h\downarrow0\), which exist by the compactness just proved. Their \(Y\) are uniformly bounded in \(L^4\) by the radial upper bounds and the fixed \(X\). Transferring to \(b_{\rm G}\) changes the normalized action by \(O(h)\) and thus gives a minimizing sequence at \(b_{\rm G}\). Strong polar convergence along a subsequence makes \(Y\) converge strongly, and the polynomial difference quotients converge to the derivative of a limiting minimizer. Its value bounds the lower right difference quotient from below because \(\Phi_{u,g}(1/b_{\rm G})\ge F(b_{\rm G})\) on all pairs.

Compactness would convert relaxed radial uniqueness throughout \(0<b<b_{\rm H}\) into the unrestricted comparison at \(b_{\rm H}\). The separation estimate only excludes nearby competitors and its radius tends to zero at \(b_{\rm H}\). Subcrossing coexistence and its slope condition are consistent with the fixed-pair convexity, and crossing-height attainment also allows a distinct relaxed minimizer there. These tests supply neither subcrossing uniqueness nor an unrestricted strict competitor at the crossing.
