**Local minimality.** The coefficients of the real second variation are smooth functions of the zero-free first jet. The embedding \(H^5\hookrightarrow C^1\) therefore makes the integrated Hessian continuous in the norm of bounded bilinear forms on \(H^1_0\): bound every coefficient difference in \(L^\infty\) and use Cauchy–Schwarz on the derivatives and values of the test fields. Shrink a convex \(H^5\) ball about the background until its Hessians on \(H^1_0\) retain half of E08's positive coercivity constant \(c_b\). For a stationary field \(w\) of the local branch and any other field \(w+z\) in that ball with the same trace, Taylor's integral remainder gives
\[
 S(e^{bt}(\sigma+w+z))-S(e^{bt}(\sigma+w))\ \ge\ (c_b/4)\|z\|_{H^1}^2 .
\]
The local critical field is consequently a strict minimum among its same-trace competitors in the ball. The size of the ball and the lower bound depend on this background.

**Smooth data.** Shrink the neighborhoods so \(|P\psi|\) stays positive on the whole cylinder, using continuity from \(H^5\) to \(C^1\). For smooth endpoint traces in the neighborhood, tangential translations are a smooth finite-dimensional family in the endpoint \(H^{9/2}\) norm. The translated solution solves the translated problem and remains near the original solution for small translations by strong continuity in \(H^5\). Local uniqueness and smooth dependence identify its translations with this smooth \(H^5\)-valued family. Difference quotients therefore give \(\partial_x^\alpha\psi\in H^5\) for every purely tangential multi-index. In particular all these tangential derivatives are \(C^2\) up to the faces by four-dimensional Sobolev embedding. In the pointwise Euler equation the coefficient of \(\partial_t^2\psi\) is invertible where \(|P\psi|>0\), by the positive symbol and \(\sigma(P)(dt)^*\sigma(P)(dt)=3I/4\). Thus \(\partial_t^2\psi\) is a smooth function of its first jet and of its second derivatives with at most one \(t\) derivative. Differentiating this identity, inductively in the number of normal derivatives, yields continuous derivatives of every order up to both ends. This proves smoothness for smooth traces on this local branch. It leaves the stated Sobolev regularity of the solution map for general \(H^{9/2}\) traces intact.
