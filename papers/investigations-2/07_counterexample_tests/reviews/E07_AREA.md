## Normalized field and \(L^2\) estimate

The oriented stereographic coordinates use the orthogonal reflection after inversion to match the orientation of the punctured sphere. The flat chiral splitting in these coordinates is fixed. For a smooth marked class with graph \(B:E_+\to E_-\) supported in a compact set, write the A1 pole form as \(\eta+u+v\) with \(u\in E_+\), \(v\in E_-\), \(v=B(\eta+u)\). The coefficient \(\eta\) is the constant leading form in these same coordinates. E04 chooses the compact metric Euclidean near the pole, so the orthogonal reflection identifies the anti-self-dual residue plane at the pole isometrically with the flat positive plane at infinity. The space of perpendicular unit residues in the disk estimate is computed with this identification. [POLE A1](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E01_A1.md) · [POLE E04](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E04_REALIZATION.md)

Both corrections are tempered distributions because A1 gives \(u=O(r^{-2})\) and \(v\) is smooth compactly supported. Off Fourier frequency zero, closure implies the symbol lies in \(n^\flat\wedge n^\perp\), by contracting the exterior multiplication equation with \(n=\xi/|\xi|\). The pullback by the reflection \(R_n=I-2n\otimes n\) has eigenvalue \(-1\) on this subspace and exchanges chiralities. Its other eigenspace is \(\Lambda^2n^\perp\). Consequently \(\widehat u=-R_n^*\widehat v\) off zero, even in the distributional equation there, since its coefficients are smooth away from zero. The multiplier \(\mathscr T=-R_n^*\) on the negative component is orthogonal onto the positive component at every nonzero frequency. Plancherel gives the exact \(L^2\) isometry. For every negative \(L^2\) input \(w\), \(w+\mathscr T w\) is distributionally closed, by the same reflection eigenspace description. [ZERO E01 flat symbol](../../02_zero_circles/notes/E01_FLAT.md)

The Fourier transform of \(u-\mathscr T v\) is supported at the origin, so its inverse transform is a polynomial. If a vector polynomial of degree \(d\ge0\) is nonzero, one component has a nonzero leading homogeneous term and magnitude at least \(c r^d\) on a sector of positive solid angle for large \(r\). Since \(u\to0\), that would force \(\mathscr T v\) to fail square integrability on the sector. Thus the polynomial vanishes and \(u=\mathscr T v\) in \(L^2\). This is needed because the A1 estimate alone is at the critical decay exponent. Substituting the actual A1 field into the graph equation yields \((I-B\mathscr T)v=B\eta\). With \(b\ge\|B\|_\infty\), \(b<1\), the Neumann series gives \(\|v\|_2=\|u\|_2\le\|B\eta\|_2/(1-b)\). This proves the estimate for the original normalized pole family.

On a flat four-ball of radius \(\ell\) centered at a disk boundary point, \(v=0\) and \(u\) is closed and flat self-dual, hence distributionally harmonic. Its componentwise harmonic mean value and Cauchy–Schwarz bound its center norm by \(\sqrt2\|u\|_2/(\pi\ell^2)\), from the volume \(\pi^2\ell^4/2\). For every perpendicular unit \(\eta\), the estimate is \(|\omega_\eta|\le1+b(L/\ell)^2/(1-b)\) when the graph is supported in the ball of radius \(L\), since \(\|B\eta\|_2\le b\pi L^2/\sqrt2\). The optional trial residual has the same estimate with \(\|v-v_*\|_2\le\|v_*-B(\eta+\mathscr T v_*)\|_2/(1-b)\) by the same inverse. On the flat balls \(v-v_*=0\) almost everywhere by the support hypothesis, so its Fourier partner is smooth harmonic there and has the stated pointwise estimate. The condition-number comparison also holds: for complementary coordinate planes in an oriented orthonormal flat eigenframe, the compatible positive line is spanned by \(e^{ij}+t\,\mathrm{sgn}(i,j,k,l)e^{kl}\), with \(t=\sqrt{\lambda_k\lambda_l/(\lambda_i\lambda_j)}\). Its flat graph singular value is \(|1-t|/(1+t)\), bounded by \((\kappa-1)/(\kappa+1)\) when \(t\in[1/\kappa,\kappa]\).
