Let \(b(x)=\chi(x/16)\) on the closed unit four-ball, where \(\chi\) is the original positive POLE chart. Write \(W=\Sigma_P\setminus\operatorname{int}b(B^4)\) and \(\iota(u)=b(\mathcal R u)\) for its outward boundary mark, with \(\mathcal R(u)=(-u_1,u_2,u_3,u_4)\). Let \(F\subset W\) be smooth, compact, connected, properly embedded and oriented, with \(\partial F=\iota(K)\) as oriented knots. Suppose \(F\) is transverse to the original cap belts \(S_i=\{0\}\times S^2\), and let \(N_i=|F\cap S_i|\). Then \(N_i\) is even for each \(i\), and
\[
 g_4(K)\le g(F)+(N_1+N_2)/2,\qquad
 g_4(K)=\min_F \bigl(g(F)+(N_1+N_2)/2\bigr).
\]
The minimum is over these transverse surfaces in the fixed coordinate puncture. For any \(F\) with \(g_4(K)-g(F)\ge k>0\) the inequality forces \(N_1+N_2\ge 2k\). In particular a disk requires at least \(2g_4(K)\) intersections. [Actual puncture and outward mark](../01_examples/notes/E02_PUNCTURE_INPUT.md#1-puncture-and-knot-orientation)

## Intersection signs and tubes

The relative class of \(F\) is zero in \(H_2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z)=0\). Intersecting with the closed oriented \(S_i\) therefore gives signed count zero separately for each belt. Its normal \(D^2\) is oriented as the cap disk because the cap orientation is the disk orientation followed by the outward sphere orientation. Interchanging the two dimension-two factors has positive sign. Near an intersection use \((z,n)\in D^2\times S^2\), with \(z=0\) on the belt. Transversality makes \(F\) a graph of \(n\) over \(z\) in a neighborhood of the intersection. Cutting off the graph in local sphere coordinates straightens it to the constant \(n\) graph on a smaller disk, with induced disk orientation \(\sigma\,d\Re z\wedge d\Im z\), where \(\sigma\) is the intersection sign. These changes have disjoint support.

Pair negative to positive intersection points on \(S_i\) by embedded arcs. Given previously drawn arcs, choose disjoint disk neighborhoods of those arcs missing every unused endpoint. The complement of the neighborhoods is connected, so the next arc can be chosen there, avoiding all unused points. For such an arc \(\gamma(s)\), \(0\le s\le1\), the tube is \((z,n)=(\epsilon e^{i\theta},\gamma(s))\) with orientation \(ds\wedge d\theta\). Its start circle is oriented by \(-d\theta\) and its end circle by \(+d\theta\). At a removed disk of sign \(\sigma\), the remaining surface induces \(-\sigma\,d\theta\). The tube thus glues with opposite boundary orientations at both ends. Smaller common radii fit the straightened disk germs. The part of each arc outside the endpoint neighborhoods is compact and disjoint from \(F\), so a small radius keeps its tube clear there. The endpoint corner is the product of the meridian circle with two incident arcs in the radial and arc directions. Rounding this local corner away from \(z=0\) preserves embeddedness and orientation. Disjoint arcs and disjoint caps give disjoint modifications.

Each pair deletes two disks and inserts an annulus. The connected surface remains connected and its single outer boundary is unchanged, so decreasing the Euler characteristic by two increases its genus by one. The resulting surface \(F'\) is still proper, has genus \(g(F)+(N_1+N_2)/2\), and is disjoint from both belts. The construction works with normal circle bundles over the prescribed arcs, without a Whitney move.

## Undoing the original surgeries and canceling the one-handle stage

For each \(i\) the map \(((1+s)m(t),n)\mapsto\Phi_i(t,(1+s)n)\) on the cap side of the signed seam coincides with the original exterior coordinates on the other side. The normal variable \(s\) is increasing on both representations, and both top-dimensional forms have the sign of \(ds\wedge dt\wedge dA\). At \(s=0\) the induced signs on cap and exterior are \(+dt\wedge dA\) and \(-dt\wedge dA\). The global cap-minus-belt map uses \(1+s=|\zeta|>0\) on all of \(D^2\setminus\{0\}\), whose image is exactly the attaching tube minus its core. Inverse longitude and fiber coordinates give \(|\zeta|=|w|\), \(n=w/|w|\), and \(\zeta=|w|m(t)\). Both maps are smooth across the seam and preserve the original coordinate-ball mark by the identity on the exterior. This gives the claimed identification with the one-handle boundary \(Y\) minus the two original attaching cores and transfers \(F'\) as a surface in punctured \(Y\). In the original planar pair of pants, a proper arc on \(X=1/2\) separates the two holes and misses the pole ball. Its product with \(D^3\) exhibits the boundary connected sum of the two annulus-times-\(D^3\) pieces. With rounded corners each annulus product is \(S^1\times D^4\) before taking the four-dimensional boundary. Thus \(Y\) is a connected sum of two \(S^1\times S^3\) factors, with the puncture away from the neck. In a product factor the capped neck ball and, if present, the puncture ball can be shrunk in their own parametrized charts by ambient isotopies. In these coordinates the shrunken balls can be avoided by a product circle \(S^1\times\{p_i\}\). This gives product-framed circles outside the actual neck and puncture, by transporting back through the isotopies. In each factor one can then perturb the circle in the open complement of the neck and puncture, relative to collars of those sets, to miss \(F'\). Transversality there gives empty intersection because \(1+2<4\). The perturbation can be a small isotopy of embeddings, extended with support in the same complement. The transported framing tubes can be chosen small after the perturbation since the circles and \(F'\) are compact and disjoint. Transport the framed surgery calculation back to an unperturbed product circle. A small round product \(D^3\) at \(p_i\) leaves a complementary \(D^3\) in \(S^3\). Filling \(S^1\times D^3\) on its resulting seam by \(D^2\times S^2\) with the product identification is the rounded \(\partial(D^2\times D^3)\). The orientation check is the same product one: in retained coordinates \(dt\wedge d\rho\wedge dA\), the seam has sign \(-dt\wedge dA\) under \(+\partial_\rho\), and the disk cap has sign \(+dt\wedge dA\) under the outward disk radial normal. The complementary disk coordinates are matched with a constant sphere identification, which extends on the cap. The calculation uses the transported product framing without a longitudinal twist. Each capped factor becomes \(S^4\). The neck is glued with the coordinate-ball identification of the connected sum. The disk-isotopy argument below can standardize both neck marks in the two spheres, so this connected sum is the standard \(S^4\) with the original puncture still a parametrized disk. The surgeries leave \(F'\) and a neighborhood of its boundary in the common exterior.

## Marked ball and equality

In the resulting oriented \(S^4\), choose positive stereographic coordinates \(h:\mathbb R^4\to S^4\setminus\{p_\infty\}\) with \(h(0)=b(0)\). Temporarily write \(f=h^{-1}\circ b\) near zero. First shrink \(b\) by the parametrized isotopy \(x\mapsto b(r x)\), with \(r\) decreasing from 1 to a small \(\delta>0\). Then use \(x\mapsto h(\delta f(t x)/t)\) for \(t\) decreasing from \(\delta\) to zero. At \(t=0\) the formula extends smoothly to \(h(\delta Df(0)x)\) by the integral formula for \(f(tx)/t\). Each map is an embedding defined on a neighborhood of the unit ball. The derivative is in \(GL^+(4,\mathbb R)\), so a path to the identity followed by radial expansion yields \(x\mapsto h(x)\). Extend the isotopy to the ambient sphere by isotopy extension. For the complement of \(h(B^4)\) the map \(x\mapsto h(\mathcal R x/|x|^2)\) extends at zero in the opposite stereographic chart. It is oriented and agrees on the unit sphere with \(h(\mathcal R u)\), precisely the transported \(\iota\). This identifies the complement with the standard ball retaining the oriented knot and works on each of the connected-sum neck disks as well. The argument applies to parametrized extended disk embeddings, so it requires no recognition theorem for arbitrary embedded three-spheres. [Hirsch, Chapter 8 §1, Theorems 1.3–1.4](https://virtualmath1.stanford.edu/~ralph/math215b/Hirsch.pdf)

For equality choose a least-genus standard surface in \(B^4\). Move an interior round ball disjoint from that surface to the concentric radius-\(1/2\) ball by an ambient isotopy fixed near the boundary. One can shrink along the initial radial coordinates, transport its center in the interior, and expand after centering. The transported surface misses the new ball. For \(1/2\le r\le1\) the shell embeds by
\[
 ru\longmapsto \chi\bigl((1/16+(1-r)/8)\mathcal R u\bigr).
\]
Both the radial direction and the sphere orientation reverse, so the four-dimensional orientation is preserved. At \(r=1\) this is exactly \(\iota\), and the image lies in the pole chart away from both belts. This realizes the upper bound for the minimum with \(N_1=N_2=0\). The lower bound is the tubing and cancellation argument.

The replay also checks the stated upper bound \(g_\Sigma(K)\le g_4(K)\) using just the collar transfer in an arbitrary coordinate puncture. The penalty and its lower bound use the original two belt spheres and the identified one-handle stage. They constrain any proposed genus saving by its geometric intersection count.
