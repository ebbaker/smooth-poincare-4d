## The measured set and naturality

Fix an oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere \(M\), a smooth Riemannian conformal class \(c\) and \(p\in M\). Choose \(g\in c\), put \(U=\Lambda^-_{c,p}\), and let \(A(q)u=\omega_{p,u}(q)\in\Lambda^+_{c,q}\) on \(X=M\setminus\{p\}\) for the unit-leading-coefficient normalization of [POLE A1](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E01_A1.md). The map is linear by uniqueness. In the flat coefficient norm on the inverted chart its leading term on \(U\) is an isometric inclusion. Applying the remainder estimate to three orthonormal basis vectors bounds the operator error by \(C r^{-2}\) for fixed \(c,p\). With \(R\) large this gives \(|A(q)u|_{\rm flat}\ge |u|/2\) for \(r\ge R\) and all \(u\). Therefore every zero lies in one compact truncation. The incidence on its product with \(\mathbb P(U)\) is closed, since local unit lifts of each ray can be used in the equation \(A(q)u=0\). Its projection
\[
 D(c,p)=\{[u]\mid \omega_{p,u}\ \text{vanishes somewhere on }X\}
\]
is compact. [ZERO B02 compact incidence](../../02_zero_circles/brainstorm/notes/B02_INCIDENCE.md)

The measure \(\mu\) is the pushforward of normalized area on the Euclidean unit sphere of \(U\) by the antipodal quotient. A projective angular disk of radius \(0<\alpha\le\pi/2\) has mass \(1-\cos\alpha\), from its two antipodal cap lifts. Every nonempty open subset has positive mass. If \(D\) is proper, its open complement contains such a disk, so \(a(c,p)=\mu(D)<1\). If \(a(c,p)<1\), that complement is nonempty. Thus \(a<1\) is equivalent to existence of a zero-free direction. The round computation below proves \(D=\varnothing\) directly, since zero measure alone is compatible with nonempty closed subsets.

For \(g_1=e^{2\phi}g\), the fixed pole field's leading tensor of A1 §4 changes by the positive factor \(e^{4\phi(p)}\), while the inner product on \(\Lambda^-\) changes by \(e^{-4\phi(p)}\). The metric comparison therefore preserves the ray and the measure. For an orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphism carrying \(p\) to \(p'\), take on the domain the pullback of the target metric. The resulting isometry transports the oriented orthonormal normal frames, inversion and the leading tensor by pullback at \(p\). It preserves closedness and Hodge chirality, so A1 uniqueness identifies the pulled-back pole field. Hence it transports \(D\) by an isometric projective identification for these representatives and preserves \(a\). A general frame change uses the transported inner product matrix. ## Same pole, two signed standard-sphere markings

Choose \(y\) positive at the pole on the round sphere, with \(x=I(y)=y/|y|^2\) extending to a stereographic chart on \(X\). The numerical orientation of \(x\) is reversed because the differential of \(I\) has one negative eigenvalue and three positive eigenvalues, up to positive scale. A fixed orthogonal reflection of \(x\) gives positive Euclidean coordinates on \(X\). A flat positive self-dual constant in those coordinates is therefore self-dual for the inherited orientation in \(x\), with the numerical coefficients of an anti-self-dual residue in positive \(y\). In the round class the pullbacks of all three flat constants are closed and self-dual. A round representative can be chosen Euclidean near \(p\) in \(y\), so the exact end coefficients are the A1 residues. Uniqueness identifies every nonzero pole direction with a nonzero constant in the positive stereographic coordinates. Hence \(D_{\rm rd}=\varnothing\) and \(a_{\rm rd}=0\).

Taubes's Proposition 2.1 includes \(n=0\) in the smooth oriented category. Its §2 construction starts with the positive standard symplectic form \(\beta\) in complex coordinates \((z,w)\) and finishes with the identical form on the distant region. Use the same positive stereographic coordinates just identified to pull back the insertion. The fixed-end realization uses the closed, positive-off-the-zero-set endpoint, the wedge-positive rank-three jet checked below, and the exact flat self-dual constant at infinity. Project E04 then supplies the compact compatible class \(c_1\) with representative Euclidean in \(y\) near \(p\). Its relative patch agrees with the flat compatible plane near the transverse disk. A1 identifies the very same endpoint as the pole field for its end coefficient. Taubes supplies the relative birth theorem and inner model. The corrected annular formulas and positivity bounds are the local project calculations in the child reports. In particular the saturated coefficient is \(1-\epsilon\), (2.12) carries \(T^{-1}\), and the radial cutoff term has type \((1,1)\), with zero mixed wedge against the off-diagonal complex basis terms. [Taubes, Proposition 2.1, (2.1)–(2.16)](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/mrl/2006/0013/0004/MRL-2006-0013-0004-a006.pdf) · [WIN E03 area](../02_invariants/notes/E03_AREA.md#matching-check-for-the-relative-insertion) · [WIN E04 area checks](../02_invariants/reviews/E04_AREA.md#primary-insertion-and-matching)

For the signed local check set \(z_0=\sqrt\epsilon\), and restrict the inner coefficient map to \(z=z_0+t,\ w=u+iv\). In the displayed self-dual basis \((\beta,\operatorname{Re}\Theta,\operatorname{Im}\Theta)\), with \(\Theta=dz\wedge dw\), the off-diagonal part from (2.2) is \((-\operatorname{Im}F,\operatorname{Re}F)\), with \(F=zw-R\bar w\). The derivative matrix in the disk coordinates \((t,u,v)\) is
\[
 \begin{pmatrix}
 2z_0&0&0\\
 0&0&-(z_0+R)\\
 0&z_0-R&0
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Its determinant \(2z_0(\epsilon-R^2)\) is negative in that order and its least singular value is \(s=\min(2z_0,R-z_0)>0\). The full image is the wedge-positive flat self-dual plane, as required by E04. In a disk \(K\) of radius \(\sigma<\min(\sqrt{2\epsilon}-z_0,s)\) about the zero the quadratic coefficient remainder is bounded by \(\sigma^2\) on the boundary, since its squared norm is \((t^2-|w|^2)^2+t^2|w|^2\). The boundary minimum \(m\) for the normalized coefficient \(u_0\) is at least \((s\sigma-\sigma^2)/T\). The \(\sqrt2\) norms of the basis two-forms cancel the norm of the unnormalized end coefficient. The disk degree is \(-1\) in the indicated frames.

Write \(B=\max_{\partial K}\|A(q)|_{u_0^\perp}\|_{\rm op}\). A ray at angle \(\theta<\pi/2\) has representative \(u_0+\tan(\theta)v\) for a perpendicular unit vector \(v\) when \(\theta>0\). The homotopy from \(A(q)u_0\) to its perturbed evaluation changes the boundary map by at most \(B\tan\theta\). For \(B\tan\theta<m\) it preserves degree and forces an interior zero. The resulting angular disk is contained in \(D(c_1,p)\), so
\[
 a(c_1,p)\ \ge\ 1-\frac{B}{\sqrt{B^2+m^2}}>0.
\]
For \(B=0\) take radius \(\pi/2\). The field for \(u_0\) supplies \(m>0\), and smoothness gives \(B<\infty\). A numerical value or numerical lower bound would require quantitative input for the perpendicular fields on this disk. The qualitative result is \(a(c_1,p)>a(c_{\rm rd},p)=0\).
