## Annulus reversal and the Morse counts

For a smooth proper disk in the oriented standard ball, use the increasing radial Morse parameter on the disk and its terminal product collar. The same argument applies to squared radius at a nondegenerate minimum at the origin. With exactly one minimum, a sufficiently close regular level cuts off a single small Morse disk. In a level chart away from the origin its boundary is a graph over a planar simple closed curve in a three-ball, so it is an unknot \(U\). At the origin the disk is locally a graph over its tangent plane, and shrinking that graph's normal component to zero gives round small spherical intersections up to isotopy. In both cases compactness gives a positive radial gap from the minimum on the complement of the local piece, so no other component meets this small level.

The rest is a proper annulus in a concentric shell. Identify each spatial level by \(x/|x|\). In outward time its oriented boundary is \(-U\sqcup K\), and only Morse indices 1 and 2 occur. Reversing the interval exchanges indices \(0\) and \(2\) and preserves index \(1\). Reversing the annulus orientation then gives boundary \(-K\sqcup U\). The spatial spheres retain their identification, so the knot \(K\) undergoes no mirroring. This is a smooth ribbon concordance from \(K\) to \(U\) in Zemke's convention. By his Theorem 1.5, \(g_3(K)\le g_3(U)=0\). The census knot is nontrivial by Dunfield–Gong's Theorem 1.1, §1.8 and Table 11, so any such disk has \(b\ge2\) outward minima. For a disk, \(b-s+d=1\) for the counts of births, saddles and deaths. With precisely one death this gives \(s=b\ge2\). With zero deaths it gives \(s=b-1\ge1\). These are necessary counts. The \((2,2,1)\) class has neither a movie nor an exclusion in the checked inputs.
