## Both replacement balls and scope

The cap-2 end of either reviewed surface is exactly \(D_2\), and \(\operatorname{int}P_2\) and \(\operatorname{int}H_2\) are disjoint from that surface. The regular product slice gives an ordinary right-angle corner of the complementary truncated handle. Keeping its new face \(H_2\) and rounding the corner in a small neighborhood yields the replacement, entirely outside the two original tubes there. It removes the two cap-2 intersections and retains the cap-1 pair.

Next cut the original pole-to-cap-1 tether \(T_{01}\) at \(H_1=\{h=1/2\}\) in its own embedded three-handle chart. This cut is in the sphere-face exterior, away from the original tubes, the end roundings and the pole collar. After the first replacement the cap-1 thickened ball, its sealed middle-handle stub and the terminal half of \(T_{01}\) form a three-ball \(P_1\) with a single cut face \(H_1\). Its exposed disk includes the already rounded closing from \(H_2\). It is exactly the part met by the new surface. The same local product-corner rounding at \(H_1\) removes the cap-1 pair. The polar collar pieces lie strictly below both cuts. This proves the two replacements can be performed sequentially with the same marked boundary, genus, smooth embedding and orientations. The construction above allows any signed knot carried in that untouched collar, subject to the same gluing assumptions there.
