## The three starting surfaces

Write \(X\) for the rounded exterior with its coordinate ball and both open attaching tubes removed. A standard ball surface of genus \(g_4(K)\) can be arranged to miss a sufficiently small concentric ball. Choose a small closed ball about an interior point of its complement and move a smaller ball about that point to the center by a smooth isotopy supported in the interior of the standard ball, carrying the surface by the same isotopy. The support misses the boundary collar. The remaining shell is a product. Transfer it into the native pole collar by the orientation-preserving \(\iota\) and an increasing change of inward collar depth, using \(1-\rho\) on the standard side and \(\rho-1/16\) on the native side. The product collar and boundary orientation of \(K\) then agree under the marked identification. Call the transferred surface \(S\). [WEX-E02, puncture orientation](../01_examples/notes/E02_PUNCTURE_INPUT.md#1-puncture-and-knot-orientation)

In each native tube collar, fix an arc in \([0,\eta)\times S^2\) from \((0,n_-)\) to \((0,n_+)\), where the subscripts refer to the cap disk signs. Choose its interior in positive depth and give it radial end segments. The endpoints have distinct sphere coordinates, so two disjoint end segments can be joined at a common positive depth by an embedded spherical arc and rounded at the two junctions away from depth zero. Its product with \(S^1_t\) is embedded in the actual signed exterior chart \(\Phi_i(t,(1+u)n)\) for \(u\ge0\). If \(\sigma\) increases along the arc from \(n_-\) to \(n_+\), the orientation \(dt\wedge d\sigma\) gives \(+dt\) on the \(n_-\) rim and \(-dt\) on the \(n_+\) rim. These are opposite to the corresponding disk orientations at the cap seams. The two annuli \(A_i\) occupy disjoint tube collars away from \(S\). Each of the five boundary curves retains its product collar. [WEX-E02, both ambient seams](../01_examples/notes/E02_PUNCTURE_INPUT.md#2-both-ambient-seams-and-a-surface-through-each)

## Realization of the path classes

Projection induces \(\pi_1(X)\cong\pi_1(F)\) with the actual \(x,y\) basis. Before rounding, the boundary of \(F\times D^3\) is obtained by adjoining one \(S^1\times D^3\) for each boundary circle to \(F\times S^2\). Each common \(S^1\times S^2\) maps by an isomorphism on \(\pi_1\) to the adjoining piece, so van Kampen leaves the free group of the planar factor. Corner rounding changes the product collar by an isotopy and preserves the planar generator loops lifted far from the rounded corners. Similarly, filling \(X\) by the original cradles along its two \(S^1\times S^2\) boundaries, and by the pole ball along its \(S^3\) boundary, induces an isomorphism on \(\pi_1\). Trimming smaller collars is a homotopy equivalence. For fixed endpoints in the trimmed exterior, the same isomorphism gives a bijection of relative path classes by comparing with a reference connecting path. [Immersed exterior, ambient path calculation](../notes/IMMERSED_EXTERIOR.md)

Choose four foot centers on \(S\) along four distinct spokes in an interior half-disk based at the knot root. Place the spokes in the order needed when traversing the boundary of the cut neighborhood. On each \(A_i\), choose a foot center near each of its two \(t=0\) rim points, connected to that point by disjoint short paths. The centers can be outside the smaller collars reserved at all boundaries. Concatenate the prescribed class with the inverses of the chosen projected access pieces, including the short basepoint identifications in the central disk, to obtain a class for each core with fixed endpoints. Lift these classes to \(X\) using the preceding isomorphism. Fixed short normal germs can be included by transporting the endpoints to the inner ends of the germs and applying the same path-class bijection there.

Smooth and perturb the four arcs in the interior while fixing smaller endpoint germs. For maps of intervals into an ambient four-manifold, the zero-velocity locus has codimension four in the first jet. For pairs of distinct arc parameters, the diagonal of \(X\times X\) has codimension four, exceeding the dimension two of the parameter pairs. The three surface interiors have dimension two, so transversality also gives avoidance for the one-dimensional arc interiors away from the protected endpoints. These applications are relative to the fixed germs, which are embedded, have distinct foot neighborhoods and leave their surfaces normally. Coordinate bump perturbations supported away from such germs provide arbitrary evaluations and first jets there, and independent evaluations at two distinct free parameters. Pair tests with one fixed parameter then use the perturbation at the free parameter. Apply the tests on the open interiors and on an exhaustion off the parameter diagonal. Small perturbations preserve the relative homotopy classes and stay away from the reserved boundary collars. This yields four embedded, pairwise disjoint cores with interiors disjoint from the three surfaces. [Hirsch, *Differential Topology*, chapter 3 §2, theorems 2.7–2.8](https://ebooks.karbust.me/Mathematics/Morris%20W.%20Hirsch%20-%20Differential%20Topology%20%281976%29%20%5B978-1-4684-9449-5%5D.pdf)

## Oriented thickening and the cuts

At each end choose a local chart flattening the surface to its disk plane and the normal germ to a coordinate ray. Give the core parameter \(\lambda\) the direction from \(S\) to the annulus. Trivialize its oriented normal three-plane bundle over the interval. Choose the ordered two-frame at \(\lambda=0\) to agree with the source surface orientation and at \(\lambda=1\) to give the opposite of the destination surface orientation. An orthonormal two-frame in an oriented Euclidean three-plane determines a positively oriented third vector and hence an element of \(SO(3)\). Connectedness gives an extension of the endpoint frames along the core, stationary in the fixed coordinate charts at both ends. One can use a tubular map from a metric equal to the flat endpoint metrics so that its two-plane disks at the ends lie exactly in the given surface disks. Compactness and separation of the cores from each other and from the surfaces off the endpoint charts give an embedded solid \(D^2\times I\) of sufficiently small radius along each core, with disjoint interiors. Inside the endpoint charts the straight transverse germs give the same separation up to the endpoint disks.

In oriented disk polar coordinates let \(\vartheta\) be the positive frame angle. The side orientation \(-d\lambda\wedge d\vartheta\) induces \(+d\vartheta\) at the source and \(-d\vartheta\) at the destination. The two original punctured surfaces induce \(-d\vartheta\) at the source and \(+d\vartheta\) at the destination by the frame choices. The orientations therefore glue. Around each rim the two joined sheets are a right angle in a normal slice, with one sheet in the original disk plane outside the rim and the other along the core parameter at constant disk radius. Rounding the angle by a smooth arc matching the straight collars in a sufficiently small neighborhood gives a smooth embedded join. The rim neighborhoods can be chosen disjoint and away from all five reserved collars.

Take each cut along its spoke, a tube longitude, and its own destination access path. Given the approach directions of the two access paths, an angle function along the associated cylinder can connect their endpoint angles. In the solid tube the longitude is homotopic to its core with end offsets supplied by radii in the foot disks. Rounding the corners and the cuts themselves changes the paths only in the same local chart neighborhoods. The four cuts are disjoint except at the common root and retain the source order. Their neighborhood together with the boundary circles is obtained from five annular collars by attaching four strips connecting distinct components. This gives a planar surface with the original five boundaries plus one interior boundary circle. The complement attaches along that one circle and carries the handles, so the polygon relation uses precisely the chosen boundary order and basings. ## Genus and scope

The starting Euler characteristic is \(1-2g_4(K)\), since each seam annulus has Euler characteristic zero. Removing eight interior disks decreases it by eight. The four cylinders each have Euler characteristic zero and attach along circles. The resulting surface is connected, oriented and has five boundary circles. Writing \(2-2g-5=1-2g_4(K)-8\) gives \(g=g_4(K)+2\). Gluing the four prescribed cap disks recovers one boundary component and leaves genus unchanged. The cap charts identify their centers with four transverse intersections with the original belt spheres, one point of each sign on each sphere. Every modification in the construction is away from the native product ends.

For the fixed tuple with \(\operatorname{cl}(W)=2\) and a signed standard slice knot, the polygon lower bound and the constructed upper bound give embedded minimum two. The WEX-E07 disk for the signed latitude has its own meridian cut paths. The comparison concerns the minimum with that path prescription and the minimum when paths may vary on the same marked exterior. For an annulus with both basings trivial, put a small circular destination foot centered at \(t=0\) at an interior \(\sigma\) in the annular strip. The two access arcs to the rim points travel in opposite \(\sigma\) directions at \(t=0\), starting at antipodal points of the foot. The strip projects into the central disk because \(\lvert c_i(t)\rvert\) is uniformly small near zero and the entire seam displacement is bounded by \(5/64\). At the source, send two neighboring spokes from the root along opposite sides of a small foot, using two antipodal rim points. These spokes can be chosen within one sector in the root half-disk, alongside the other spokes for the other cap. Once the orientation sign of each end frame is set, an in-plane frame rotation matches the chosen opposite offsets. A half-turn interchanges their assignments while preserving the end frame orientation. Constant antipodal angles give disjoint longitudes in the framed cylinder. The relative homotopy from each longitude to the core uses end radii in the two feet. All the source spokes, access arcs, and end offsets project into the contractible central disk. One choice of core relative class therefore makes both completed projected paths trivial. The resulting two cuts run independently from the root to the two seam basepoints, and their consecutive order at the root can realize the specified order of the two rim words. Their small neighborhoods again supply separate strips in the planar cut neighborhood.

If \(T\) tubes are used, the Euler characteristic is \(1-2g_4(K)-2T\). At least one tube joins \(S\) to each annulus, so the result is connected with five boundaries and genus \(g_4(K)+T-2\). The tuple \((0,0)\) uses \(T=2\). A nonzero coordinate-axis tuple uses \(T=3\), sharing one tube at its cap with two trivial basings. For a standard slice knot these genera are zero and one, respectively. The one nontrivial commutator on either axis gives the matching lower bound by the all-integer free reduction. At \((1,1)\) the four-tube bound gives the matching genus two for a standard slice knot when combined with the separate certificate \(\operatorname{cl}([r,x][s,y])=2\).
