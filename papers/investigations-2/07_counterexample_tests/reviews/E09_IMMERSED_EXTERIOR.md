**Disposition.** The minimum replays for the genus of the domain of a proper smooth connected oriented immersion into the native exterior, with the signed chart knot, four product-collared sections and ordered rooted path classes fixed. The immersion has finitely many transverse interior double points and no triple points. The same genus is attained by its domain after the four prescribed cap disks are attached. The genus formula concerns domains of immersions and imposes no normal framing or prescribed double-point data.

## Projection and the two kinds of filling

Write \(Y=\partial (F\times D^3)_{\rm round}\), \(X=Y\setminus(\operatorname{int}B_p\cup\bigcup_i\operatorname{int}\Phi_i(S^1\times D^3))\), and let \(\pi\) be actual planar projection. With \(\delta=1/128\), the sphere face above the inward truncation \(F_\delta=\{b\ge\delta\}\) is exactly \(F_\delta\times S^2\). Over each boundary collar of \(F\), the CERT-E06 rounding profile attaches a product of that boundary circle with a three-ball along its \(S^1\times S^2\) boundary. The ball consists of the old radius-\(1-\delta\) disk at \(b=0\) and the spherical shell from the rounded arc. Indeed the profile gives increasing radius as \(b\) increases to \(\delta\), with \(N\in S^2\) unchanged. The seam inclusion into this piece gives an isomorphism on \(\pi_1\), since both fundamental groups are the boundary circle group. Hence van Kampen makes \(F_\delta\times S^2\to Y\) an isomorphism on \(\pi_1\). Its composition with \(\pi\) induces the collar homotopy equivalence \(F_\delta\to F\). In particular the original based planar loops \(L_x,L_y\), away from the rounding, give the intended basis. [CERT-E06 rounding and marked generators](../../05_certificates/notes/E06_PRESENTATION_HANDLES.md)

For each of the two removed tubes the restoring piece is the original cradle \(\Phi_i(S^1\times D^3)\). Its boundary is attached by \(a_i\) with the same longitude \(t\) and with exterior radial coordinate \(1+s\). The seam map on \(\pi_1\) into the cradle is the identity in the longitude basis. The coordinate puncture is \(B_p=\chi(\{|y|\le 1/16\})\) inside the retained sphere face. Its restoring map glues this very ball to its own \(S^3\), so both piece and seam are simply connected. The three restoring pieces each meet \(X\) in a connected boundary and \(Y\) is connected, which also gives connectedness of \(X\). Using collared open thickenings in van Kampen, each restoring operation preserves \(\pi_1\). Therefore \(\pi|_X\) induces an isomorphism. Trimming disjoint boundary collars preserves it by a deformation retraction. The signed knot uses \(u\mapsto\chi(\tfrac1{16}\mathcal R u)\) because the exterior outward normal on the puncture points toward decreasing \(\rho\). The reversed first coordinate compensates for the reversed boundary orientation. The knot projects into the central disk. Each oriented seam section projects, via the linear tube-clearance homotopy and the short basepoint identification at \(t=0\), to \(r^{\pm1}\) or \(s^{\pm1}\) with its exterior boundary sign. ## The marked domain and its extension

Let \(Q_g\) be the abstract connected oriented surface of genus \(g\) with the five ordered boundary components. The geometric rooted cuts belong to this domain. For the extension, transport their endpoints along the fixed product collars to the inner faces. To realize a projected path class with those prescribed endpoints, take any path between the endpoints in the trimmed exterior. The desired projected class differs from its projection by a based loop at the projected starting point, after adjoining the prescribed short basepoint paths. Lift the loop using surjectivity on \(\pi_1\) and prepend it. Injectivity identifies the resulting endpoint-fixed homotopy class in the exterior. The tracks used in trimming carry the prescribed class back to the original boundary endpoints.

The genus-\(g\) surface polygon has one relation, the product of the \(g\) handle commutators and the ordered based boundary factors. Projection kills the knot factor. Thus every map with the marked data satisfies \(\operatorname{cl}(W)\le g\). The abelianization of \(W\) is zero because each seam contributes opposite signs, so its commutator length is finite. Conversely for \(g=\operatorname{cl}(W)\) lift each handle generator of a factorization of \(W^{-1}\) into \(g\) commutators. Use the actual boundary loops on the inner faces and the chosen rooted paths to map the remaining polygon edges. The attaching loop projects to the identity and is nullhomotopic in the trimmed exterior by injectivity. This gives a continuous extension across the polygon. Use the same product formulas a short way past the collars to be held fixed and trim at that greater depth. The continuous extension is smooth near the protected collars and its remaining image is separated from \(\partial X\). One may smooth relative to the protected collars by local smoothing of the \(\mathbb R^5\) coordinates followed by a tubular retraction to \(Y\), sufficiently close to keep the remaining image in \(\operatorname{int}X\). Close interpolation through the same retraction preserves the relative homotopy classes. [Based polygon conventions](../notes/BASED_EXTERIOR.md)

## Relative general position

First-jet variations by constant and linear coordinate bumps at a movable point give all derivative matrix variations. In dimension \(2\to4\), the strata of rank one and zero have codimensions \((2-1)(4-1)=3\) and \(8\). The fixed collars already have full rank. Localized parametric transversality then gives an immersion. At a possible coincidence of distinct points, at most one point is fixed because the union of the fixed collars is embedded. Independent constant bumps at the movable points give all normal directions to the diagonal. For ordered pairs the normal space has dimension four and the configuration space dimension four. For ordered triples these dimensions are eight and six. Applying Sard's theorem to the local parameter families and taking a countable compact exhaustion of the movable jet and distinct-point configuration sets gives transverse double points and eliminates triples, relative to the protected collars. The perturbations may be taken in a small smooth neighborhood of the smoothed map and supported on the complement of the fixed collars. The positive distance from the ambient boundary there and the retained product collars give \(f^{-1}(\partial X)=\partial Q_g\). Compactness and local injectivity of an immersion exclude limits of coincidence pairs on the domain diagonal, so the isolated coincidence pairs are finite. These checks give the relative argument directly. The [Hirsch scan cited by the note](https://people.dm.unipi.it/benedett/HIRSCH.pdf) opened without readable text.

On the cap sides the original section disks attach by the same product collar. With the outward-first convention the exterior surface orientation \(ds\wedge dt\) induces \(-dt\) on its \(s=0\) boundary, while the corresponding cap disk induces \(+dt\). Reversing the disk orientation reverses both signs. The joins are smooth, the interiors of the cap disks are separated from the exterior, and they give two opposite transverse intersections on each original belt. The domain Euler characteristic increases by four and the number of boundary components falls from five to one, preserving genus. The signed knot and its collar are unchanged. ## The planar case and the two exchanges

At order \((r,r^{-1},s,s^{-1})\) and \((h_1,h_2,h_3,h_4)=(1,s,1,r)\), the product is \([r,s][s,r]=1\) for \([a,b]=aba^{-1}b^{-1}\). In the ordered tuple \((r,sr^{-1}s^{-1},s,rs^{-1}r^{-1})\), the first exchange at positions 2 and 3 sends \((sr^{-1}s^{-1},s)\) to \((s,r^{-1})\). The second sends this pair to \((r^{-1},rsr^{-1})\). The resulting tuple \((r,r^{-1},rsr^{-1},rs^{-1}r^{-1})\) cancels in adjacent pairs. Each identity is an exchange of based group factors and preserves their product.

With these paths the genus-zero domain becomes a disk after the four caps are attached, for every smooth signed knot in the same coordinate puncture. In particular the immersed construction applies to the marked positive-clasp, two-twisted Whitehead double of the right-handed trefoil. Its embedded genus is one by the separate filling comparison. The algebraic exchanges supply no geometric change to the fixed paths or a method to remove double points. [Independent Whitehead filling replay](E07_FILLING.md)
