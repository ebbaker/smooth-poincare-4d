## The retained cap and the auxiliary circle

Let \(b(x)=\chi(x/16)\) be the positive marked disk and \(\iota(u)=b(\mathcal R u)\) its outward complement mark, with \(\mathcal R\) the first-coordinate reflection. Use \(T_i=\Phi_i(S^1\times D^3)\) for the original attaching tubes and \(C_i=D^2\times S^2\) for their replacements. If the proper oriented \(F\) misses \(S_i=\{0\}\times S^2\), transfer \(C_i\setminus S_i\) to \(T_i\) minus its core by \((\rho m(t),n)\mapsto\Phi_i(t,\rho n)\). This is invertible for \(\rho>0\), and \(\rho-1\) is the signed radial coordinate across the seam, so extension by the identity is smooth there. This transfers \(F\) with its marked boundary to punctured \(Y[r_j]\), \(j\ne i\), with all of \(C_j\) in place.

The auxiliary circle in the common exterior is \((L_a(v),P(1/2,0))\), where \(a=x\) for \(j=1\) and \(a=y\) for \(j=2\). The planar loop lies at least \(3/4\) from either hole boundary and at least \(2\) from the outer boundary, so it stays off the rounding. Its colatitude is below \(\pi/3-5/64\), the bottom of the first enlarged tube, and above \(1/4\), the maximum colatitude in the pole chart. This clearance holds at every planar point and longitude, including the based point. In the original boundary \(\partial(F_{\rm planar}\times D^3)\), projection to the planar pair of pants induces an isomorphism on \(\pi_1\). Indeed the face \(F_{\rm planar}\times S^2\) has this group, and each side face \(S^1\times D^3\) is glued along a distinct \(S^1\times S^2\) with the same \(\pi_1\) as the side face. The rounding leaves this calculation unchanged. In the boundary connected sum decomposition the two hole loops are the positive product generators. Thus the displayed auxiliary circle has the requisite class in \(Y\). For the loop-isotopy step, a smooth homotopy between homotopic parametrized embeddings can be made constant in time near the endpoints. Perturb its spatial derivative off zero relative to these endpoint collars. The rank-four zero condition is imposed on the two-dimensional circle-times-interval domain. After this perturbation, all time slices are immersions and compactness gives a uniform neighborhood of the parameter diagonal with no fiberwise double points. On pairs outside that neighborhood the evaluations at distinct spatial parameters can be varied independently by local perturbations of the homotopy. The same-time pair domain has dimension three and the target diagonal has codimension four. A further sufficiently small perturbation therefore removes all double points, with the local separation and endpoint collars preserved. The resulting family is a smooth isotopy, and ambient isotopy extension transports the product framing. Compactly supported small perturbations of the auxiliary loop in the common open exterior then avoid \(F\), since a transverse \(1\)-manifold and \(2\)-manifold in dimension four have empty intersection. Shrinking the transported tube around this loop makes the whole tube disjoint from \(F\), the original tubes, and the marked ball. This perturbation keeps the product-surgery identification of the auxiliary framed loop. ## Commutation and the filled ambient manifold

Denote the auxiliary tube by \(T_a\), and its filling by \(C_a\). The closed result in both surgery orders is the common complement of \(\operatorname{int}T_a\cup\operatorname{int}T_j\) in \(Y\), with \(C_a\) and \(C_j\) attached at their separate boundary components by the same collars. Hence \(F\) survives the auxiliary surgery on \(Y[r_j]\) even if \(F\) enters \(C_j\). Its part in \(C_j\) is carried by the identity in the order comparison. The common complement contains the marked ball and an open neighborhood of its boundary on which the comparison is the identity.

To identify the closed result, do the product-framed auxiliary surgery first. On its summand it leaves \(S^1\times D^3\), glued to \(D^2\times S^2\) as the two faces of the rounded \(\partial(D^2\times D^3)\). The other summand remains \(S^1\times S^3\). The neck disk in the surgered summand is still parametrized and can be straightened to a round disk in this standard \(S^4\) by a smooth isotopy of disk embeddings. The resulting connected sum is thus \(S^1\times S^3\). Deleting a circle leaves \(\pi_1\) unchanged by general position for curves and their two-dimensional homotopies. Removing the tube gives the same group as deleting its core by radial retraction. Van Kampen with the simply connected \(D^2\times S^2\) kills the longitudinal generator from \(S^1\times S^2\). In the ordered original basis the retained loops become
\[
 \begin{array}{c|c|c}
 \text{retained word} & \text{auxiliary generator killed} & \text{surviving class}\\ \hline
 x^{-1}y^2xy^{-3} & x & y^2y^{-3}=y^{-1}\\
 x^{-1}yx^2y^{-1}x^{-2} & y & x^{-1}x^2x^{-2}=x^{-1}.
 \end{array}
\]
Each surviving loop is therefore freely homotopic to an oppositely oriented product core in \(S^1\times S^3\). Apply the circle-isotopy argument to that parametrized loop and use the resulting ambient map to carry its actual tubular coordinates.

The framing check uses the full disk factor of the complementary hemisphere. In local product coordinates a shrunk transported tube around the standard core has the form \((T(t,z),V(t,z))\), with \(T(t,0)=\pm t\) after fixing the phase and \(V(t,0)=0\). The coordinate homotopy \((T(t,\lambda z),cV(t,\lambda z)/\lambda)\) for \(0<\lambda\le c\) starts at the shrunk tube and ends smoothly at \((\pm t,cD_z V(t,0)z)\). For positive \(\lambda\) it is an embedding by rescaling the original tube. The limiting normal derivative is invertible, and \(c\) can be chosen small enough for the entire isotopy to lie in the product chart. Its polar decomposition deforms the normal matrices to \(A(t)\in O(3)\), preserving invertibility. This straightens the framed tube with its parameter retained. The two hemispheres of the sphere factor are related by reflection fixing their common \(S^2\). On the resulting boundary, the frame and direction change is \((t,n)\mapsto(\pm t,A(t)n)\), which extends over the complementary \(S^1\times D^3\) by \((t,z)\mapsto(\pm t,A(t)z)\). Thus every transported framing gives the product-glued \(\partial(D^2\times D^3)\), smoothly \(S^4\). This step identifies the filled closed manifold by a diffeomorphism. It imposes no avoidance constraint on the retained attaching circle relative to \(F\) in the reversed calculation, since preservation of \(F\) took place in the other surgery order.

## Marked bounds and scope

The marked ball survives as a parametrized disk. In the filled standard sphere it can be shrunk in its own coordinates, rescaled to its derivative in a positive stereographic chart, and expanded to a standard hemisphere by ambient isotopy of the parametrized disk. If the final disk map is \(h(x)\), the complementary ball parametrization \(x\mapsto h(\mathcal R x/|x|^2)\) agrees with the transported mark at \(|x|=1\), and extends at zero in the opposite stereographic chart. The two reversals make it positively oriented. This verifies that a surface missing the designated belt bounds the same \(K\) in the standard ball with its genus preserved. The ambient recognition uses circle isotopy and isotopy of extended disk embeddings. It presupposes neither the smooth type of the candidate sphere nor unknotting for an arbitrary embedded \(S^2\) or \(S^3\).

For the stated count inequalities use connected, compact, proper smooth oriented surfaces transverse to both belts, with oriented boundary \(\iota(K)\). The result for each individual count only needs transversality to that belt. The signed count on belt \(i\) is zero because \(H_2(W,\partial W;\mathbb Z)=0\). Pair opposite points along disjoint arcs on that belt and attach the normal-meridian annuli with opposite induced boundary orientations. This is confined to its product neighborhood. Each pair replaces two disks by an annulus and adds one genus, without touching the other belt or the boundary collar. Applying the single-belt transfer to the new surface gives
\[
       g_4(K)\ \le\ g(F)+N_i(F)/2 \quad (i=1,2).
\]
An ordinary least-genus surface transfers into the pole collar with the same \(\iota\) and misses both belts, so
\[
 g_4(K)=\min_F\left[g(F)+\tfrac12\min\{N_1(F),N_2(F)\}\right].
\]
The minimum genus with either designated belt omitted is also \(g_4(K)\). A saving of \(k>0\) requires \(N_i(F)\ge 2k\) for **each** belt, with both signs represented at each. This refines the count requirement on a potential witness in the fixed marked sphere. The tube modifications use embedded meridional annuli over arcs, so the count proof uses no Whitney disks.
