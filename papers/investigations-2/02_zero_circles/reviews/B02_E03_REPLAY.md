## B02 — Incidence and the spatial derivative

**Project theorems replayed, conditional on A1 and, for the exotic-type implication, A2.** Fix one smooth oriented Riemannian conformal class and pole \(p\) on a smooth homotopy four-sphere. In the inverted end chart A1 identifies \(U\) with the flat leading self-dual space for the inherited orientation. Choose the coefficient norm using that identification. Write \(A(q)=A_\infty+R(q)\) in the ambient two-form coefficients. The three basis asymptotics imply \(\sup_{|u|=1}|R(q)u|=O(r^{-2})\). Thus \(A(q)\) is injective on a whole end tail for all directions at once. The zero pairs then form a closed subset of the product of a compact truncation and \(\mathbb{RP}^2\). Its image \(D\) is compact, and the fibre over any degeneracy point is its full projective kernel. A2 forces \(D=\mathbb{RP}^2\) at **each** marked pair on a hypothetical exotic sphere. At rank two everywhere on \(H\) this is surjectivity of \(\kappa\). At lower ranks the projective fibres already contribute multiple directions. On the stated diagonal calibration at the fixed pole the three positive Gram entries and the independent limiting values at \(r=0\) give \(D=\varnothing\) for every \(\varepsilon>-4\). This uses A1 to identify that triple as a basis. [A1 and A2 handoff](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/research-progress/handoffs/TO_MASTER2_FRAME_20261127.md) · [Calibration, §4](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E03_ALGEBRA.md)

If \(D\) is nonempty and proper, connectedness of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) gives \(\partial D\ne\varnothing\), and closedness gives \(\partial D\subset D\). Given **any** zero of a boundary direction, choose an affine chart of projective coefficients and a three-dimensional spatial disk tangent to a complement of the derivative's kernel if that derivative is onto. The implicit function theorem solves the three evaluation equations on that disk for every nearby direction. This puts the original direction in \(\operatorname{int}D\). Hence every zero over \(\partial D\) has spatial rank below three. If a derivative of a closed self-dual form at a zero had rank one, \(L(v)=\alpha(v)\beta\) would give \(0=d\omega=\alpha\wedge\beta\). The map \(\alpha\mapsto\alpha\wedge\beta\) is injective since a nonzero self-dual \(\beta\) has positive square and is nondegenerate. This excludes rank one and leaves rank zero or two on the boundary. Here rank is that of the **spatial jet of one form**. The rank of the evaluation triple at the same point is a separate condition.

At a rank-two evaluation, fix \(u\in\ker A_q\setminus\{0\}\) and let \(P:E_q\to E_q/\operatorname{im}A_q\). In adapted bases the adjugate of \(A_q\) has a single nonzero entry, so \(d(\det A)_q\) is \(P\,L\) under a nonzero identification of the cokernel with \(\mathbb R\). A frame change multiplies that derivative at a zero of the determinant by a nonzero scalar. When \(P L\ne0\), \(T_qH=\ker(P L)\) and \(L(T_qH)=\operatorname{im}L\cap\operatorname{im}A_q\) has dimension \(\operatorname{rank}L-1\). Normalize a local lift of \(\kappa\) using the nonzero minor. Modulo \(\ker A_q\), differentiation along \(H\) gives \(A_q\,du=-L\). Since \(A_q\) identifies \(U/\ker A_q\) with its image, \(\operatorname{rank}d\kappa_q=\operatorname{rank}L-1\). This is one at every such boundary point and two at a preimage of a regular value of \(\kappa\) **on the rank-two stratum with simple determinant zero**. For a fixed direction with all its zeros transverse the zero set is a compact smooth one-manifold without boundary and has finitely many components, each a circle.

At a rank-one evaluation, write the local matrix as \(\left(\begin{smallmatrix}a&b\\c&F\end{smallmatrix}\right)\) with scalar \(a\ne0\). The two equations for a kernel vector give \(u_1=-a^{-1}bu'\) and \(B u'=0\), for \(B=F-ca^{-1}b\). Hence \(\det A=a\det B\) and \(\operatorname{rank}A=1+\operatorname{rank}B\). If \(dB_q\) is invertible, the four entries give smooth coordinates, the determinantal hypersurface is the claimed cone, and the incidence is locally an open subset of the total space of \(\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbb R^2/\ell',\mathbb R^2)\) over \(\mathbb{RP}^1\). The actual line is \([(-a(q)^{-1}b(q)u',u')]\), with the specified spatial dependence. For example, at an apex incidence point the spatial derivative \(L\) surjects onto the two-dimensional cokernel, since its projected derivative is \(dB_q(\cdot)u'\). The tangent equation for direction variation \(\dot u\) in an affine lift of \(\mathbb P(U)\) is \(Lv+A_q\dot u=0\). It exhibits directly why the image of the direction differential depends on whether \(\operatorname{im}A_q\) lies in \(\operatorname{im}L\). The Schur coordinates by themselves only resolve the incidence space. No transversality of the pole triple to either matrix stratum is used.

## E03 — Local models and source qualifications

**First jet replayed.** At a transverse zero of a closed self-dual form the derivative kills the tangent to the zero set. In an oriented orthonormal normal frame and tangent coordinate \(s\), alternating the first jet \(\sum_{i,j} L_{ij}x_j\beta_i\), with \(\beta_i=ds\wedge dx_i+dx_j\wedge dx_k\) for cyclic \((i,j,k)\), gives the trace and three antisymmetric coefficients. Thus \(L\) is symmetric, traceless and invertible. Choosing the tangent direction with \(\det L<0\) gives the canonical rank-one negative eigenbundle. The normal bundle is oriented and trivial over each circle. In Honda's two quotients of the same flat model the \(x_3\)-axis is the negative eigenline. It is fixed by pure translation and reversed in the twisted quotient. Honda's Theorem 6 realizes both by flat metrics, and Theorem 7 starts with a generic self-dual harmonic form on the closed manifold of that paper. It keeps the same zero set and absolute class through a supported deformation by closed forms symplectic off the zero set, and gives the models up to sign at the endpoint. Its proof uses a homeomorphism smooth off the zero set. Perutz's Lemma 3.1 specifies a smooth family of near-symplectic forms supported as a deformation in any prescribed neighborhood of a closed embedded zero arc, with a smooth coordinate embedding for the endpoint model. Perutz's Lemma 2.1 supplies a compatible smooth conformal structure for a near-symplectic input. [Honda, author revision of 13 January 2004, eq. (3), Lemma 4 and Theorems 6–7](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~honda/moser.pdf#page=4) · [Taubes, published §2(c), pp. 171–173](https://msp.org/gt/1999/3-1/gt-v3-n1-p08-p.pdf#page=6) · [Perutz, published Lemmas 2.1 and 3.1, §2.3](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/jsg/2006/0004/0003/JSG-2006-0004-0003-a001.pdf#page=5)

For an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(F\) of two transverse-zero germs the chain rule is \(D(F^*\omega)(v)=(DF)^*D\omega(DFv)\). Derivatives of \(DF\) multiply the zero value of \(\omega\). The maximal positive planes in the images fix the conformal classes at the two zeros, so in orthonormal frames \(DF=cQ\) and \(Q\) carries the canonical oriented tangent to the corresponding tangent. The two factors on the two-form pullback and the one on the argument give \(L_{F^*\omega}=c^3Q_N^{-1}L_\omega Q_N\). The unordered positive eigenvalue ratio survives oriented smooth changes of coordinates. The diagonal jets \((1,2,-3)\) and \((1,1,-2)\) give distinct ratios and are both realized by flat transverse closed self-dual germs. This verifies E03's obstruction to replacing the deformation by smooth equivalence of the initial germs.

**Birth and saddle replayed.** In Perutz's §2.1–2.2 take \(0<\epsilon\le1/2\). The three spanning two-forms in the displayed birth formula have wedge matrix, after division by twice the oriented Euclidean volume, with diagonal \(1,1+\epsilon x_3,1\) and off-diagonal entries zero except for the first two, where the entry is \(-\epsilon x_1/2\). Its leading \(2\times2\) minor is at least \(7/16\) on the open unit ball. The saddle formula reverses the sign of \(\epsilon x_3\) and has the same bound. In both cases the closedness check cancels the derivatives of the quadratic coefficient against the \(x_4\)-dependent terms. The equations for the zero surface are \(x_2=x_4=0\) and \(t=x_1^2+x_3^2\) for the birth or \(t=x_1^2-x_3^2\) for the saddle. The parameter derivative supplies the third spatially missing direction at the origin. The projection to \(t\) has index 0 for the birth and index 1 for the saddle. The bare birth formula varies on the boundary of the ball. Theorem 1.4 separately proves a relative saddle cobordism on a connected oriented four-manifold, a split from \(n\ge1\) or a merge from \(n\ge2\), by attaching a one-handle to arcs in the same or different zero circles. Proposition 1.5 states a relative **two-circle** endpoint insertion. Its displayed product expression on \(\mathbb R^3\times\mathbb R\) has zero lines and persists along the real factor, so that expression and the stated Darboux-ball restriction alone do not establish the claimed compactly supported endpoint. No later argument here needs Proposition 1.5. [Perutz, published pp. 238–239 and 242, Def. 1.3, Thm. 1.4, Prop. 1.5 and eqs. (3)–(5)](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/jsg/2006/0004/0003/JSG-2006-0004-0003-a001.pdf#page=2)

**Taubes's relative one-circle endpoint checked.** Proposition 2.1 allows \(n=0\) and the proof chooses a Darboux chart disjoint from all preceding zeros. Equation (2.2) gives \(\mu=-\epsilon\Omega+Q+RB\), where \(\Omega\) is Taubes's standard form, \(Q\) is the closed homogeneous quadratic part, and \(B\) the closed homogeneous linear part obtained by taking the \(R\)-coefficient. The only zero inside \(\delta=\sqrt{2\epsilon}\ll R\) has \(w=0,\ |z|^2=\epsilon\). Here \(d(|z|^2)\ne0\) and \(w\mapsto zw-R\bar w\) is invertible as a real-linear map, which gives rank three. Taubes's (2.11) prints \(1+\epsilon\), whereas (2.2) and (2.7) yield \(1-\epsilon\) once the first cutoff is one. The expression for \(\omega'\) in (2.12) requires the common \(T^{-1}\) to meet the two adjacent formulas. The cutoff construction can be checked with the following compatible expressions, using the real forms defined by (2.2). [Taubes, published Proposition 2.1 and proof, pp. 558–560](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/mrl/2006/0013/0004/MRL-2006-0013-0004-a006.pdf#page=2)

1. On \(\delta\le r\le4\delta\) use \(T^{-1}(\mu+d(\beta_\delta\alpha))\), where \(d\alpha=\Omega\) is (2.6) and \(\beta_\delta=\beta(r^2/\delta^2)\) increases from 0 to 1. The square before multiplying by \(T^{-2}\), divided by twice Euclidean volume, is \(\theta^2+\delta^{-2}\beta' r^2\theta+|zw-R\bar w|^2\), with \(\theta=\beta_\delta-\epsilon+|z|^2-|w|^2\). Indeed \(d(r^2)\wedge\alpha\) is decomposable of type \((1,1)\) and wedges to zero with the off-diagonal \((2,0)+(0,2)\) component. On the annulus \(\theta\ge\epsilon-2|w|^2\). The negative part of the middle term is bounded by \(32\|\beta'\|_\infty |w|^2\). For \(w\ne0\) large \(R\) makes the last term dominate it, while \(w=0\) gives \(\theta\ge\epsilon>0\).
2. On \(4\delta\le r\le8\delta\) use \(T^{-1}[(1-\epsilon)\Omega+RB+d((1-\beta_{4\delta})\tau)]\), with \(\tau=\iota_{\mathcal E}Q/4\) and \(\mathcal E\) the Euler vector field. The cutoff term is \(O(\delta^2)\). The other terms are self-dual for the flat metric, and their smallest skew singular value is at least \(1-\epsilon\) because \(B\) is pointwise orthogonal to \(\Omega\). For small \(\delta\) the cutoff preserves positive square.
3. On \(8\delta\le r\le16\delta\) use \(d(\chi_{8\delta}\alpha)+T^{-1}RB\), increasing \(\chi_{8\delta}\) from \(T^{-1}(1-\epsilon)>0\) to 1. The first summand is a positive \((1,1)\) form, so its square is positive, its wedge with \(B\) vanishes and the square of \(B\) is nonnegative.
4. On \(r\ge16\delta\) use \(\Omega+(R/T)d((1-\beta_{16\delta})\nu)\), with \(\nu=\iota_{\mathcal E}B/3\). Its difference from \(\Omega\) is \(O(R\delta/T)\) on the last annulus and vanishes for \(r\ge32\delta\). Taking \(T\) large gives positive square and equality with the original form near the boundary of the chosen ball. The difference is a compactly supported closed two-form in a ball and has vanishing compactly supported cohomology class in degree two.

**Cap and parity replayed.** By A1 use an orientation-compatible end coordinate after a reflection of the orientation-reversing inverted chart. Write \(\omega=\omega^0+da\) in this chart with \(|a|=O(r^{-1})\), \(|da|=O(r^{-2})\) and \(\omega^0\) constant nonzero and self-dual for the Euclidean limit and the inherited orientation. On \(r\ge R\) the cutoff replacement differs from \(\omega^0\) by \(d\chi_R\wedge a+\chi_R da=O(R^{-2})\). It is symplectic there for sufficiently large \(R\). Keep the original form on the core, so the zeros and their first jets agree. Normalize \(\omega^0\) to the standard symplectic form by an oriented orthogonal change and dilation. The same gluing starts directly from any orientation-compatible co-compact end chart with an exact standard collar beyond all the zeros. At a level sphere in the exact region, the core occupies the inside of that collar and the product cap occupies the outside. The induced boundary orientations are opposite, and the two forms coincide on the collar. A round ball with its collar fits inside a product of area disks in \(S^2\times S^2\), so the capped form is smooth, near-symplectic and symplectic on the cap. The radial end retract and excision for the puncture give \(H^1(W;\mathbb R)=H^2(W;\mathbb R)=0\) for the core \(W\). Mayer–Vietoris across the three-sphere gives \(b_1(Y)=0\) and rank two in \(H_2(Y;\mathbb R)\). The two product factors outside the ball have square zero and intersection \(+1\), giving \(b_2^+(Y)=1\). Perutz's Theorem 1.8 is for a closed connected four-manifold with a near-symplectic form and counts the components whose negative line is trivial. On \(Y\) it gives \(N_{\mathrm{ev}}\equiv1-0+1\equiv0\pmod2\). Thus a pole form with a single transverse zero circle has an odd circle. This argument uses the parity theorem on the capped manifold with its preserved transverse jets.
