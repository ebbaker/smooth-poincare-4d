# Directions with zeros and the derivative of the kernel line

Uses the [A1 normalized expansion and A2 implication](../../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/research-progress/handoffs/TO_MASTER2_FRAME_20261127.md) at the evidence status of their owner, the [local source reading](../../notes/E03_LOCAL_SOURCES.md) and the [master conventions](../../../../master-2/research-overview/OVERVIEW.md). All deductions below are project theorems, working at issue. See the [register](../../research-progress/REGISTER.md) for the independent reading. No genericity theorem is a premise.

Let \(p\) be fixed in a smooth oriented Riemannian homotopy four-sphere. Write \(X=M\setminus p\). Fix a scale for the three-dimensional coefficient space \(U\), write \(A(q):U\to E_q=\Lambda^+_q\) for normalized evaluation, and set \(H=\{q:\operatorname{rank} A(q)<3\}\).

**Project theorem.** The compact incidence space
\[
 {\cal I}=\{(q,\ell)\in X\times\mathbb P(U):A(q)\ell=0\}
\]
has closed image \(D\subset\mathbb P(U)\). Its fibre over \(q\in H\) under the other projection is \(\mathbb P(\ker A(q))\), respectively a point, a projective line, or a projective plane in ranks two, one, or zero. Indeed the three A1 expansions, expressed in the flat leading frame, give an injective triple outside a single compact set by uniform closeness to its limiting isometry. Incidence is closed there and projective directions are compact. Its complement in the direction plane consists exactly of zero-free directions. On an exotic sphere A2 would force \(D=\mathbb P(U)\) for every \(c,p\). It would force surjectivity of the single-valued kernel-line map on \(H\) if all its ranks are two. Lower ranks contribute all their kernel lines to \(D\).

**Project theorem (direction boundary).** If \(D\) is nonempty and proper, its boundary is nonempty. For any direction on that boundary there is a zero, and *every* zero of that direction has spatial derivative rank at most two. In an affine chart on directions, choose a smooth nonzero coefficient representative. A zero with surjective spatial derivative persists at nearby directions by solving on a transverse three-disk, contradicting the defining boundary property. This argument needs neither smoothness of \(H\) nor transversality of the triple. It also shows that if all zeros of all directions are transverse at this marked pair, \(D\) is empty or the entire projective plane.

**Project theorem (closedness excludes rank one).** At any zero of a single smooth closed self-dual form, the intrinsic derivative \(L:T_qX\to E_q\) cannot have rank one. Such a derivative would be \(\alpha\otimes\beta\), with nonzero covector \(\alpha\) and nonzero self-dual \(\beta\). Closedness gives \(\alpha\wedge\beta=0\) by alternation, since the form vanishes. But \(\beta\wedge\beta>0\). In a basis beginning with \(\alpha\), the first identity makes \(\beta=\alpha\wedge\gamma\), which squares to zero. Thus a boundary zero has rank zero or two.

**Project theorem (the rank-two locus).** Where \(A(q)\) has rank two, its kernel and cokernel are lines. If \(d(\det A)_q\ne0\) in any local frames, \(H\) is a smooth hypersurface near \(q\). Its kernel map \(\kappa\) is smooth and satisfies
\[
 \operatorname{rank}d\kappa_q=\operatorname{rank}D_q\omega_u-1\ \in\{1,2\},\qquad 0\ne u\in\ker A(q).
\]
Indeed trivialize \(E\), choose bases at \(q\) putting \(A\) into \(\mathrm{diag}(1,1,0)\) and take \(u\) the last vector, up to scale. The derivative of the determinant is the cokernel component of \(L(v)=D_v(Au)\), up to nonzero scale. It is nonzero, so \(T_qH\) is its kernel and the image \(L(T_qH)\) has codimension one in \(\mathrm{im}(L)\). Choose a local generator \(u(q')\) for the kernel by solving on the invertible minor and keeping the last component fixed. Differentiating \(A(q')u(q')=0\) along \(H\) gives \(A\,du=-L|_{T_qH}\). The induced map \(U/\ker A\to\mathrm{im}(A)\) is an isomorphism, proving the rank identity. Closedness excludes rank one for \(L\), and the determinant excludes rank zero. At boundary directions every such rank-two point of \(H\) is therefore a rank-one critical point of \(\kappa\); at regular directions the associated zeros there are transverse. When all zeros are transverse their compact zero set is a finite union of circles. Compactness follows as above, and a compact manifold has finitely many open connected components.

**Project theorem (conditional rank-one model).** Near a rank-one point use local invertible row and column operations to split off a nonzero \(1\times1\) minor. The remaining \(2\times2\) Schur complement \(B\) vanishes at the point; its four entries are transverse coordinates precisely when \(dB\) is invertible. Under this extra condition rank one is isolated and \(H\) is the cone \(\det B=0\) in these coordinates. The incidence is smooth here: locally it is \(\{(B,\ell'):\ell'\in\mathbb{RP}^1,\ B\ell'=0\}\), the total space near its zero section of the rank-two vector bundle with fibre \(\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb R^2/\ell',\mathbb R^2)\). Column changes transport the lines back to the actual coefficient space and generally depend on \(q\). Thus a whole projective line lies over the apex. No assertion about the differential of the direction projection there follows just by freezing those column changes.

**Decision / heuristic.** The first invariant to compute is the actual image \(D\), retaining the rank of the spatial derivative over its boundary. A determinant sign or the topology of \(H\) alone discards which directions have zeros. On the complete diagonal calibration at the fixed pole, \(H={\cal I}=D=\varnothing\) for every \(\varepsilon>-4\), by the [two-pole calculation](../../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E03_ALGEBRA.md). For a class in the gate table a strictly positive continuum lower bound gives the same answer at its tested pole. A nonempty image on an untreated pair, automatic transversality to rank strata, and a restriction that would exclude a surjective direction projection for all marked points are different questions. The rank restriction above uses closedness at the zero; smooth maps between rank-three bundles need not satisfy it. No last-circle removal follows from these results.
