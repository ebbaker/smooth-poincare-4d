## First jet and smooth models

**Import.** Let $X$ be a smooth oriented Riemannian four-manifold and let $\omega$ be a smooth closed two-form with $*_g\omega=\omega$ for the given orientation. At a zero $q$, transversality is surjectivity of the intrinsic derivative $D\omega_q:T_qX\to\Lambda^+_q$. The implicit function theorem gives a smooth one-manifold $Z=\omega^{-1}(0)$ with tangent $\ker D\omega_q$, and $D\omega_q$ identifies $N_qZ$ with $\Lambda^+_q$. Wherever $\omega\ne0$, the equality $\omega\wedge\omega=|\omega|_g^2\,dV_g$ gives positive symplecticity. Conversely, Perutz's Lemma 2.1 gives a smooth conformal class making a near-symplectic form self-dual and transverse. The near-symplectic hypotheses are closedness, strictly positive square off the zero set and maximal positive three-plane image of the derivative at each zero. [Perutz, Definition 1.1 and Lemma 1.2, pp. 237–238](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/jsg/2006/0004/0003/JSG-2006-0004-0003-a001.pdf#page=1) · [Lemma 2.1, p. 241](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/jsg/2006/0004/0003/JSG-2006-0004-0003-a001.pdf#page=5)

**Import, with local calculation.** On a tube along an arc of $Z$, choose positively oriented coordinates $(s,x_1,x_2,x_3)$ orthonormal along $x=0$, with $s$ arclength on $Z$ and the $x_i$ normal. Set $\beta_i=ds\wedge dx_i+dx_j\wedge dx_k$ for cyclic $(i,j,k)$. These basis forms have norm $\sqrt2$ along $Z$. Then
$$
\omega(s,x)=\sum_{i,j=1}^3 L_{ij}(s)x_j\beta_i+O(|x|^2).
$$
In $d\omega$ along $x=0$, the coefficient of $dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3$ is $\operatorname{tr}L$. The three coefficients containing $ds$ are, up to signs, $L_{ij}-L_{ji}$. Thus $L$ is symmetric and traceless, and is invertible by transversality. In invariant terms $L$ is the normal derivative followed by contraction with the unit tangent and metric duality. Perutz orients the tangent so $\det L<0$, giving two positive eigenvalues and one negative eigenvalue. The quadratic form is intrinsic up to positive scale, since $\mathrm{im}\,D\omega_q$ determines the conformal class at $q$. [Honda, author revision of 13 January 2004, eq. (3) and Lemma 4, pp. 4–5](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~honda/moser.pdf#page=4) · [Taubes 1999, published §2(c), pp. 172–173](https://msp.org/gt/1999/3-1/gt-v3-n1-p08-p.pdf#page=6) · [Perutz, §2.3 and Proposition 2.2, p. 243](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/jsg/2006/0004/0003/JSG-2006-0004-0003-a001.pdf#page=7)

**Import.** The rank-one negative eigenbundle $L^-$ is trivial for an **even** or **untwisted** circle and Möbius for an **odd** or **twisted** circle, as detected by $\langle w_1(L^-),[Z_i]\rangle$. The full normal rank-three bundle is oriented and trivial over $S^1$ in both cases. The flat representatives come from $\Theta=x_1\beta_1+x_2\beta_2-2x_3\beta_3$. Its quotient by a translation of $s$ gives an even circle. Combining translation with $(x_1,x_2,x_3)\mapsto(x_1,-x_2,-x_3)$ gives an odd circle whose negative line is the $x_3$-axis with reversed monodromy. Honda's Theorem 6 gives the realized self-dual harmonic models for the corresponding flat metrics. Theorem 7 obtains them up to sign near the zeros at the **end of a deformation** through closed forms, supported near the zeros, of constant absolute class, with common zeros and symplecticity on their complement. The local Moser argument uses a homeomorphism smooth off the zero set. Perutz's Lemma 3.1 gives a deformation through near-symplectic forms supported in any prescribed neighborhood of a closed embedded zero arc, with fixed zero set and an orientation-preserving smooth coordinate embedding pulling back the endpoint to $\Theta$. [Honda, Proposition 5 and Theorems 6–7, pp. 5–7](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~honda/moser.pdf#page=5) · [Perutz, eqs. (1)–(2), Proposition 2.2 and Lemma 3.1, pp. 241–245](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/jsg/2006/0004/0003/JSG-2006-0004-0003-a001.pdf#page=5)

**Project check.** Even at one zero an exact smooth coordinate equivalence to $\Theta$ can fail for the original form. An orientation-preserving diffeomorphism relating two germs identifies the positive three-planes that are the images of their first derivatives at the zero. Its tangent map is therefore conformal for the induced metric classes. In orthonormal frames write $DF=cQ$ with $c>0$ and $Q$ orthogonal. The canonically oriented unit tangents correspond under $Q$, and the normal operators satisfy $L_{F^*\omega}=c^3Q_N^{-1}L_\omega Q_N$. The ratio of the positive eigenvalues, up to inversion, is a first-jet invariant. The closed self-dual germ $x_1\beta_1+2x_2\beta_2-3x_3\beta_3$ is transverse with positive eigenvalue ratio 2, while $\Theta$ has ratio 1.

## Closed parity and an explicit cap

**Import.** Perutz's Theorem 1.8, attributed to Gompf, applies to a smooth near-symplectic form on a smooth closed connected oriented four-manifold $Y$. If $N_{\mathrm{ev}}$ counts the components with trivial negative line, then
$$
N_{\mathrm{ev}}\equiv 1-b_1(Y)+b_2^+(Y)\pmod2.
$$
The sign of the $b_1$ term in the abstract gives the same congruence. This constrains the number of even circles. [Perutz, Theorem 1.8, p. 240, and §5, proof at pp. 255–256](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/jsg/2006/0004/0003/JSG-2006-0004-0003-a001.pdf#page=4)

**Project deduction.** Suppose $X=\Sigma\setminus\{p\}$ for a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere and $\omega$ is near-symplectic. To transfer parity, take a compact truncation $W$ containing the zero set and an orientation-compatible end chart with an annular collar on which $\omega$ is the constant standard symplectic form. A co-compact end chart with that equality gives such a collar. An explicit zero-free cap is $S^2\times S^2$ with the product symplectic form minus a round ball in a Darboux chart. One can use two area coordinates on disks large enough for the collar, as in the [product cap construction](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/references/A2_ORIGINAL.md#passing-from-the-fixed-splitting-to-the-fixed-euclidean-end). Replace this ball by $W$ using their equal forms on the open collar. The two domains occupy opposite sides of the gluing sphere and have opposite induced boundary orientations under the collar identification. The resulting closed $Y$ has a smooth near-symplectic form equal to $\omega$ on the core and symplectic on the cap, with the same zero-set jets. Since $H^1(W;\mathbb R)=H^2(W;\mathbb R)=0$, Mayer–Vietoris across $S^3$ gives $b_1(Y)=0$ and the intersection form of $S^2\times S^2$, with $b_2^+(Y)=1$. Thus $N_{\mathrm{ev}}\equiv0\pmod2$. A single transverse zero circle is odd.

For a nonzero pole form satisfying A1, the exact collar follows by a change supported far out on the end. In an end chart positively oriented for $X$, write $\omega=\omega^0+da$, with $\omega^0\ne0$ constant self-dual, $a=O(r^{-1})$ and $da=O(r^{-2})$. Inversion from positively oriented coordinates at $p$ reverses orientation, so compose with a reflection to obtain this chart. Choose a radial cutoff $\chi_R$ equal to 1 up to $R$ and 0 past $2R$, with $|d\chi_R|=O(R^{-1})$. Replace the tail by $\omega^0+d(\chi_R a)$. The difference of the new form from $\omega^0$ is uniformly $O(R^{-2})$ on $r\ge R$, which preserves positive symplecticity for large $R$. An orthogonal change and common dilation normalize $\omega^0$ while preserving round collars. This is the end argument of [A2](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/brainstorm/notes/B01_RECOGNITION_SOURCE.md) using the [A1 asymptotics](../../01_chiral_transfer_and_pole_forms/notes/E01_A1.md#1-statement-and-the-asymptotically-euclidean-end). It preserves all the original zeros and their neighborhoods. Applying parity requires their transversality and uses the capped $Y$.

## Scope of genericity

**Import.** The primary genericity result used here is LeBrun's Proposition 1, attributed there to Taubes. It uses a closed oriented smooth four-manifold with $b_2^+\ge1$, $C^{k,\alpha}$ Riemannian metrics with $k\ge2$ and $0<\alpha<1$, and a chosen sheet of the unit hyperboloid in a fixed codimension-$(b_2^+-1)$ subspace of $H^2(M;\mathbb R)$ on which the intersection form has one positive direction. For each metric the unique self-dual harmonic representative with class in that sheet is the normalized family. The proposition describes the transverse metric set as second Baire category, with a Sard–Smale argument for the regular values. Corollary 2 gives $C^2$ metric approximation by $C^r$ metrics for each integer $r\ge2$, together with $C^1$ form approximation by transverse forms for a given nonzero closed-manifold self-dual harmonic form. [LeBrun, published §3, Proposition 1 and Corollary 2, pp. 542–544](https://archive.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/1997/0005/0003/CAG-1997-0005-0003-a006.pdf#page=8)

**Scope for poles.** The imported family is cohomologically normalized on a closed manifold. In particular on $\Sigma$ every smooth harmonic two-form vanishes, since $H^2(\Sigma;\mathbb R)=0$. A pole form uses an asymptotic datum on $\Sigma\setminus\{p\}$. Conditional on transversality, the local results apply to such a form, and a nonzero constant leading term makes the zero set compact, hence a finite union of embedded circles. Transversality for a generic prescribed-pole solution, simultaneous behavior in the direction parameter, and rank strata of the pole triple each require a result for the pole solution family. The [separate Honda transversality paper](https://www.math.ucla.edu/~honda/transverse.pdf) has unreadable text extraction in the available author copy, so the genericity import here uses LeBrun's checked passage.
