## Exact limit and finite energy

$$

## Physical curvature maximum scale

If $K_i=\sup_M|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_i}|_{\rm tens}\to\infty$, set $\lambda_i=K_i^{-1/2}$ and center at a maximum. In the scaled metrics $|\operatorname{Rm}|\le1$ everywhere. The physical Yamabe test on each ball of radius $r\le1$ gives $Y_i V_{g_i}(r/2)^{1/2}\le(24/r^2+Y_i)V_{g_i}(r)$. The same iteration yields a uniform lower bound for normalized volume, which transfers to unit and smaller balls in the scaled metrics. Together with the full curvature bound this gives a uniform injectivity estimate and pointed harmonic-coordinate compactness. At fixed compact scales one obtains strong first-order convergence and weak $W^{2,p}$ for each finite $p$ after choosing $p>4$. The latter follows from the harmonic Ricci formula and interior elliptic estimates. The scaled total volumes tend to infinity, so the diameters tend to infinity by Bishop comparison. The pointed limit is complete. [Cheeger–Gromov–Taylor, Theorem 4.7](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-differential-geometry/volume-17/issue-1/Finite-propagation-speed-kernel-estimates-for-functions-of-the-Laplace/10.4310/jdg/1214436699.pdf) · [Anderson, *Convergence and rigidity of manifolds under Ricci curvature bounds*](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01233434)

Use these approximants in the E08 replacement at a diagonal index. Their Weyl matrices converge strongly and their comparison curvature is bounded, so the same proof gives strong chiral convergence and stationarity against smooth compact variations of the rough metric. The scalar curvature is zero by the weak curvature limit, and the full curvature lies in $L^\infty$ by the scaled bound. The harmonic metric equation $h^{ab}\partial_{ab}h_{jk}=-2(\operatorname{Ric}_h)_{jk}+Q_{jk}(h,\partial h)$ and the Ricci equation bootstrap to smoothness in the limiting harmonic atlas. The equality $|\operatorname{Rm}_{\lambda_i^{-2}g_i}|(p_i)=1$ is a pointwise statement on second derivatives and can be lost in this convergence.
