## Selection and quantifiers

Let \(I=I^+(M)<2T\) be attained on a nonstandard oriented homotopy four-sphere, with \(T=4\pi^2\). The existing lower recognition gap gives \(I>0\). The positive Yamabe locus is open, so the minimizing conformal class is Bach-flat by first variation. Every positive Bach-flat candidate in the definition of the first exact value \(a_M\) is admissible for \(I^+\), and the attained minimum belongs to that exact set. Hence \(a_M=I\). Taking \(b=a_M\) and any \(0<\delta\le 2T-I\) meets \(a_M\le b\le2T-\delta\) and \(b<a_M+\kappa_\delta\), since \(\kappa_\delta>0\). The [first-value theorem] then gives smooth sequential compactness modulo oriented diffeomorphisms of the volume-one Yamabe representatives at exactly this value.

If such a sequence converges smoothly after pullback, its energies, volumes, scalar constants and Bach equations pass to the limit. In the Hilbert–Schmidt conventions the identity is

\[
 16\pi^2=2I-\tfrac12D+\tfrac1{24}Y^2,\qquad
 D=4I-\frac{Y_{\rm rd}^2-Y^2}{12},
 \qquad Y_{\rm rd}=8\sqrt6\,\pi .
\]

In particular \(Y^2\ge48(2T-I)>0\), and the limiting scalar constant is positive. For each fixed smooth test, the Yamabe inequality passes to the smooth limit, and its constant function realizes equality by volume normalization. Thus the limiting metric is itself a volume-one Yamabe representative at the exact value. The continuous invariants \(D\) and \(Y^2\) attain their extrema by taking minimizing or maximizing sequences. Since \(I\) is fixed, their minimizers coincide. If the least \(D>0\), no exact minimizing class has a positive Einstein representative. In such a class the Einstein representative would be a Yamabe metric after scaling by the Yamabe existence theorem and Obata rigidity, and hence would yield \(D=0\). If the least \(D=0\), the selected metric is positive Einstein. [Lee–Parker, §3 and the Yamabe existence theorem](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf)

At the maximizing representative, equality \(Y=Y_{\rm rd}\) would identify its conformal class with the round sphere and hence its smooth type with the standard sphere. The strict inequality for that representative gives a positive deficit for the entire exact stratum on this type. Over a fixed strict band \(I^+(M)\le2T-\delta\), conditional [fixed-band finiteness] permits the minimum of these finitely many positive deficits if there are any represented nonstandard types. All these comparisons concern exact strata. They give no additional compactness for sequences of arbitrary admissible near-minimizers. The minimum of \(D\) on this stratum also supplies no first variation of \(D\) in directions leaving it.

## One-sided Ricci separation and the fourth variation

Put \(Z=\operatorname{Ric}_g^\circ\) and \(s=Y([g])>0\) in the volume-one least-\(D\) representative. Every exact positive class has \(Y\ge s\) by minimizing \(Y^2\) and positivity. Its every representative has normalized scalar action \(\mathscr S\ge Y\) by using the constant function in the Yamabe quotient. At \(g\), differentiating \(\operatorname{Vol}^{-1/2}\int R\) gives the \(L^2(g)\) gradient \(-Z\). Specifically the derivative of \(\int R\) is represented by \(-\operatorname{Ric}+\frac12sg\), and the derivative of \(\operatorname{Vol}^{-1/2}\) subtracts \(\frac14sg\). Along \(g+tZ\) the slope is \(-D\). Thus a one-sided \(C^2\) tangent \(h\) to the exact set through \(g\) has \(\int\langle Z,h\rangle\le0\), and a two-sided exact tangent has pairing zero. Contracted Bianchi gives \(\operatorname{div} Z=0\) because \(R=s\), explaining the same conclusion modulo smooth conformal and diffeomorphism tangent terms.

On the fixed small convex \(C^2(g)\) ball of the [new tests], uniform metric and curvature bounds make the derivative of \(\mathscr S\) uniformly bounded as an \(L^2(g)\) functional. For \(D>0\), it follows that the positive Ricci line has distance at least \(Dt/(2L)\) from the exact metrics within that ball, where \(L\) is its scalar-action Lipschitz constant. The opposite inequality \(t\|Z\|_2\) uses \(g\) itself. This is a marked \(L^2\) distance with the exact set restricted to the indicated \(C^2\) neighborhood. The argument supplies neither a global distance estimate nor an estimate after enlarging the comparison set to arbitrary nearly minimizing metrics.

The openness of the positive sign lets one test both signs of \(t\) and every smooth second-order term. If \(\mathcal H(Z,Z)=0\) for \(\mathcal H=D^2 e_g\), then \(De_g=0\), \(D^3e_g[Z,Z,Z]=0\) and \(\mathcal H(Z,k)=0\) for every \(k\), by the two-sided minimum and polarization. Taylor expansion at \(g+tZ+\frac12t^2 k\) has fourth coefficient \((A+6 B_k+3\mathcal H(k,k))/24\), with \(A=D^4 e_g[Z,Z,Z,Z]\) and \(B_k=D^3e_g[Z,Z,k]\). Nonnegativity for every real multiple of \(k\) gives \(A\ge0\) and \(3B_k^2\le A\mathcal H(k,k)\), including \(B_k=0\) when the second variation on \(k\) is zero. Along the affine positive line the energy excess is strictly positive by scalar separation, while the two-sided minimum gives \(O(t^4)\). On that line one can equivalently use one half of the full curvature-operator Weyl square by closed signature zero. Its density is analytic in \(t\) in a common small complex disk by positive definiteness and compactness, using the branch of the density that is positive at \(t=0\). The first nonzero term is therefore positive and even. The distance lower bound excludes powers below its order for an energy-growth inequality with uniform positive constant along the line, including every power below four and, when the quartic is zero, every power below six. These are necessary conditions. No vanishing Ricci Hessian or failure of the fourth-variation inequality is established on a remaining class.

## Analytic propagation on a Bach-flat hypersurface

The local analytic conformal representative follows from Lassas–Liimatainen for the smooth obstruction-flat ambient metric in dimension four. With \(B(X,Y)=g(\nabla_X\nu,Y)=\beta h(X,Y)\), the Codazzi identity \(R(X,Y)\nu=(X\beta)Y-(Y\beta)X\) gives \(\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,X)=-2X\beta\) by tangential contraction. Thus an intrinsic geodesic satisfies the four equations of the [new tests], including \(\dot\beta=-\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,v)/2\) and ambient normal acceleration \(-\beta |v|^2\nu\). On a small ambient analytic coordinate chart this analytic ODE, with fixed base point, fixed initial normal and principal curvature, and varying initial tangent vector, gives the actual intrinsic exponential by uniqueness. The parametrization at time one is analytic with injective differential at zero. This proves analytic parametrizability of the smooth umbilic hypersurface in each of the local conformal gauges. Cotton vanishing is invariant under change of the conformal factor. The analytic identity theorem on connected cut charts now propagates the vanishing from any open patch to the connected umbilic cut, without a global analytic representative. Vanishing to infinite order of the Cotton tensor at an actual point has the same consequence. [Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorem 3.7(c)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08030#page=26)

If the cut is specified as a hypersurface component of a global conformal involution, averaging gives an invariant metric. The fixed component is totally geodesic. In a homotopy sphere a closed embedded connected hypersurface is two-sided and separating by mod-two intersection. Reflection interchanges its two complementary interiors. The fold retraction onto either closed side makes that side simply connected and kills its intermediate homology, while its top homology is zero because of its nonempty boundary. The side is contractible by the homological Whitehead theorem. The separating face is an integral homology sphere, with positive induced scalar in the physical CGY gauge by umbilic Gauss. The positive-scalar prime list and contractible-filling obstruction give \(S^3\), and the propagated local conformal flatness gives its round conformal parametrization by the developing map. [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574#page=10)

Codazzi also gives the proposed pointwise ambient tests. In the sign convention \(R_{abcd}=\langle R(e_a,e_b)e_d,e_c\rangle\), mixed curvature from \(d\beta=-P^g(\nu,\cdot)\) equals the mixed Schouten contribution. Thus \(W_{\nu abc}=0\). The mixed entries between \(\nu^\flat\wedge T^\ast\Sigma\) and its Hodge image vanish, so the blocks in the adapted bases of \(\Lambda^\pm\) agree. Both invariant differences \(\Phi_2,\Phi_3\) vanish on the cut. Their weights under \(g\mapsto e^{2f}g\) are \(-4,-6\), respectively, so the rank-two obstruction and regular-locus conditions are conformal. The Gauss trace gives \(P^h=P^g|_{T\Sigma}-W_{\nu\,\cdot\,\nu\,\cdot}+\frac12\beta^2 h\). In the intrinsic antisymmetric derivative, the normal-connection terms of \(P^g|_{T\Sigma}\) cancel the derivative of \(\frac12\beta^2 h\) by Codazzi. Derivatives of the normal slots in the Weyl contraction give mixed components that vanish. This verifies the displayed Cotton formula on an umbilic patch.

A supplied compact component of the regular \(\Phi_2=0\) locus is a closed embedded hypersurface. It is locally analytic in the same conformal gauges by the implicit function theorem. Therefore vanishing of the trace-free tangential Hessian on one relatively open patch propagates umbilicity on the component. The Cotton test on the patch then propagates as above. If the component is diffeomorphic to \(S^3\), this supplies the actual cut for the cap argument. As a separate observation the same reasoning applies with \(\Phi_3\) in place of \(\Phi_2\) when its regular zero component is the one supplied. The signature integral alone gives neither a regular component of the required topology nor the two geometric vanishing tests.

## Generic absence of an umbilic hypersurface

The chiral invariant constraints hold for local immersions as well as embedded cuts. In the ten-dimensional Weyl fiber, equality of the quadratic and cubic traces gives equality of characteristic polynomials for the traceless \(3\)-by-\(3\) blocks. On the simple-spectrum locus, the restrictions of the two gradients to the first block are independent: a relation would impose a polynomial of degree at most two on three different eigenvalues. The nonzero repeated-spectrum locus is parametrized by one eigenvalue \(a\ne0\) of multiplicity two and the simple eigenline in each block. The parameter \(a\) is recovered smoothly from \(A\) as \(-\operatorname{tr}(A^3)/\operatorname{tr}(A^2)\). The simple eigenline has projector \((\operatorname{Id}-A/a)/3\), and likewise for the second block. This verifies embeddedness of the five-dimensional stratum. The remaining pair of zero blocks is one point. These give the codimensions \(2,5,10\).

Varying metric second derivatives while keeping its value and first derivatives fixed realizes every algebraic Riemann tensor by the normal-coordinate quadratic formula. Projection to the Weyl blocks is a surjective linear map on this fiber variation. Thus the three inverse-image strata have the same codimensions in the metric two-jet bundle. The tensor polynomials of degree at most two, multiplied by cutoffs equal to one on a finite smaller-ball cover, span the vertical two-jet directions at each point. The base-point derivative supplies the horizontal directions, so the parameterized jet map is a submersion. For each preimage stratum, regular values of its projection onto the parameter ball give transverse slices. This follows by projecting the derivative of the submersion to the normal space of the stratum. A parameter projection is surjective on its kernel precisely when the slice derivative fills that normal space. Sard applied to the three projections simultaneously gives arbitrarily small parameters transverse to all three strata. Slices miss the strata whose codimension exceeds the dimension four of the carrier.

At the simple common zeros the differentials of \(\Phi_2,\Phi_3\) therefore have rank two, so the joint zero locus is a two-manifold or is empty. An immersed umbilic hypersurface would differentiate into that joint kernel with rank three, which is impossible. Compactness and two-jet dependence give openness in \(C^3\), and the finite-parameter perturbation proves density in \(C^\infty\). Intersecting with the strict entered band preserves the relative open-dense conclusion. These perturbations take place in the full metric space. The argument yields no transverse perturbations among exact positive minima or among Bach-flat metrics. The linked general transversality source is Boardman, who states Thom's theorem, while the finite-parameter proof here verifies the required bundle-section version directly. [Boardman, *Singularities of differentiable maps*, Theorem (1.5)](https://www.numdam.org/item/PMIHES_1967__33__21_0.pdf#page=6)

## Einstein Cauchy data on an actual patch

Let \(g\) be a smooth positive Einstein four-metric of scalar \(s\) on a connected manifold. On an actual connected embedded hypersurface patch assume the induced metric has constant sectional curvature \(k\) and \(B=\beta h\) for a fixed smooth local normal. Contracted Codazzi and \(\operatorname{Ric}_g(\nu,\cdot)|_{T\Sigma}=0\) give \(2\,d\beta=0\). Scalar Gauss gives \(6k=s/2+6\beta^2\), so \(k=K+\beta^2\), with \(K=s/12\). On the round four-sphere of curvature \(K\), the geodesic sphere of radius \(\rho\in(0,\pi/\sqrt K)\) has induced sectional curvature \(K+K\cot^2(\sqrt K\rho)\) and principal curvature \(\sqrt K\cot(\sqrt K\rho)\) in its radial normal convention. Pick \(\rho\) realizing the signed \(\beta\). Local space-form coordinates on the patch identify both marked Cauchy tensors \(h\) and \(B\). Pick the round side so its inward normal has the matched sign relative to that of the local physical side. Both collar metrics satisfy \(\operatorname{Ric}=3K g\), are smooth up to the patch and can be restricted to a common small cylinder. Anderson–Herzlich's local uniqueness theorem therefore gives a boundary-fixing isometry to the round collar on a one-sided neighborhood. [Anderson–Herzlich, Theorem 3.1, Cauchy data on the horizontal face](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0710.1305#page=6)

There is a direct curvature check of the same implication. Full Gauss gives the round ambient curvature pattern on the four tangential slots, and Codazzi with constant \(\beta\) makes the components with one normal slot zero. For two normal slots the tangential Einstein Ricci equations give \(R_{\nu a b\nu}=K h_{ab}\) in the convention \(R_{ijji}>0\) on positive sectional curvature. Indeed, the tangential contraction of the Gauss result supplies \(2K h_{ab}\) out of \(3K h_{ab}\). These are all independent components, so \(W=0\) along the patch. Its tangential covariant derivatives vanish there. Differential Bianchi for \(W\) in the Einstein metric gives \(\nabla_\nu W_{abij}=0\) for \(a,b\) tangent. The divergence-free identity gives \(\nabla_\nu W_{\nu aij}=0\). Applying a divergence to differential Bianchi and commuting derivatives gives a rough-Laplacian equation \(\nabla^m\nabla_m W=\mathrm{Rm}*W\), where the right side denotes a linear combination of algebraic contractions with the smooth curvature. In Gaussian coordinates the leading coefficient of the pure second normal derivative is one. With \(W\) and its first normal derivative zero along the patch, differentiating this equation in the normal variable inductively gives all normal derivatives zero there. Tangentially differentiating these identities gives zero for every Taylor coefficient at an interior patch point, a coordinate-independent property of the zero jet. In harmonic coordinates the Einstein metric and its Weyl tensor are real analytic. Thus \(W\) vanishes on an actual neighborhood. [DeTurck–Kazdan, Theorem 5.2](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.1405.pdf#page=26)

Einstein analyticity, or Bach-flat continuation, propagates that vanishing on the connected interior. In the closed simply connected case constant sectional curvature \(K\) and completeness identify the metric with the round sphere up to scale. For a **closed** round-conformal umbilic \(S^3\) in the Einstein metric, Codazzi gives constant \(\beta\), so scalar Gauss gives constant boundary scalar curvature. Obata rigidity for the round conformal class gives an intrinsically round boundary, verifying the sectional-curvature premise. On an open patch intrinsic conformal flatness and constant scalar curvature alone are insufficient. A great circle in one sphere times the other sphere is a totally geodesic hypersurface in the equally scaled positive Einstein product \(S^2\times S^2\). Its induced cylinder metric is locally conformal to Euclidean three-space and has constant scalar curvature, with sectional curvatures zero and positive in different planes. The Einstein product is nowhere of constant sectional curvature. [Obata's round-conformal rigidity, Lee–Parker Proposition 3.1](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf#page=12)

## The separate closed-cut hypothesis at a minimum

The [weighted cap comparison] supplies positive smooth caps approaching the raw signed energies for a **supplied** closed round-conformal umbilic \(S^3\) in any positive subcritical class. The original sides are connected compact domains with the given common boundary. Van Kampen and Mayer–Vietoris give simply connected capped homology spheres. Signature on the smooth capped sides and passage to the cap limit give equal raw masses in both chiralities on each side. One mass \(m\) is strictly below \(T\), since the two sum to \(e<2T\). Sufficiently close positive smooth caps recognize this side, and the [marked-cut proof] uses its actual embedded cap ball and the original gluing map to identify the other capped type with \(M\). In the attaining class each positive approximation on the surviving type has energy at least \(I^+(M)=e\); its limit is \(e-m\). Hence \(m=0\), which by nonnegativity, smoothness and equality in both chiralities gives \(W=0\) on the interior of the discarded side in the original metric. Smooth Bach-flatness on the connected closed original carrier supplies the obstruction-flatness and regularity in the continuation theorem. Global local conformal flatness and the developing map on the compact simply connected carrier then give conformal roundness. This final implication requires neither the Einstein hypothesis nor least-\(D\) selection. [Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorem 3.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1912.08030#page=27)
