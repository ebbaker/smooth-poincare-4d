## Actual local cut at a rotational axis

The separate local-axis proof checks before attainment. The input is a smooth conformal $SO(3)$ action on an invariant open tube about an interior segment of a fixed curve, with the standard three-dimensional normal action. Averaging the representative as a tensor over the action stays in the conformal class. In invariant Fermi coordinates the fixed curve is geodesic and Gauss's lemma gives $g_*=dr^2+a^2dx^2+b^2\sigma_2$, with $a=1+O(r^2)$ and $b=r(1+O(r^2))$. The coefficients $a$ and $b/r$ are smooth in $r^2$. The isotropy eliminates cross terms with sphere directions, and radial arclength fixes the leading orbit coefficient. The optical quotient $(dr^2+a^2dx^2)/b^2$ determines the umbilic ring since its geodesic times the orbit sphere is totally geodesic in $b^{-2}g_*$. [Local-axis construction, input blob `bec695978a2d`](../notes/LOCAL_AXIS_MARKED_CUT_20261127.md)

Scale $(x,r)=\epsilon (u,v)$ and $b$ by $\epsilon^{-1}$, so on compact sets in $v>0$ the optical metric is $O(\epsilon^2)$ from the hyperbolic upper half-plane. The horizontal initial geodesic at $(0,1)$ has a transverse hit of $v=1/2$ on each side near the corresponding unit semicircle hits. Parameterizing the lower branches by $v$, the momentum $p$ of the length density $b^{-1}\sqrt{1+a^2u'^2}$ gives $u'=pb/(a\sqrt{a^2-p^2b^2})$ and $p'=\partial_u(b^{-1}\sqrt{1+a^2u'^2})$ with the slope held fixed in the partial derivative. The limiting $p$ is $+1$ or $-1$ and the limiting denominator squared inside the radical is at least $3/4$. Because $a_u=O(\epsilon^2v^2)$, $b_u=O(\epsilon^2v^3)$ and $u'=O(v)$, both equations are $v$ times smooth functions of $(u,v^2,p)$. Taking $v^2$ as the independent variable gives a regular ODE and smooth dependence through both endpoints. The lift hence has smooth pole graphs $u=u(|y|^2)$, closes with distinct endpoints and is $O(\epsilon^2)$ from a sphere in scaled Cartesian coordinates. Its radial projection gives a smooth actual parametrization. The induced metric is conformally round by integrating $d\log\tan(\theta/2)=dt/d(t)$ in the meridian metric $dt^2+d(t)^2\sigma_2$. At each simple smooth pole, $d(t)$ is odd in inward distance and has slope one, so the resulting conformal factor is positive and smooth. The two actual normals on the enclosed ball and its complement are negatives. Their corresponding first jets use the same round conformal marking, with a reflection to enforce the opposite boundary orientation on each cap.

The old $L^3$ trace test also holds on these specific spheres. In the globally pinched gauge $g=w^2g_*$, the rescaled boundary metrics $k_\epsilon=(w(0)\epsilon)^{-2}h_\epsilon$ satisfy $k_\epsilon=\sigma_3+2\epsilon L\sigma_3+O_{C^2}(\epsilon^2)$. Here $L$ is the restriction of the linear term of $w/w(0)$ to the unit sphere. Pullback by the time-$\epsilon$ flow of $\nabla_{\sigma_3}L$ cancels the linear term because $\nabla^2L=-L\sigma_3$. For $J(k)=\int(R_k/6)^{3/2}\,dA_k$, the scalar variation at $\sigma_3$ is $\frac14\int\dot R=-\frac12\int\operatorname{tr}\dot k$, which cancels the volume variation. The functional is twice differentiable in the $C^2$ neighborhood with uniformly positive scalar curvature. Diffeomorphism invariance and the quadratic remainder thus give $J(k_\epsilon)=2\pi^2+O(\epsilon^4)$. The positive ambient quantity $R_g-2\operatorname{Ric}_g(\nu,\nu)$ has a fixed positive lower bound on the compact smaller tube. After the above rescaling, the Gauss expression for $(|H|/3)^2$ subtracts this quantity times $(w(0)\epsilon)^2/6$ from $R_{k_\epsilon}/6$. The mean value theorem gives $\int(|H|/3)^3\,dA_{h_\epsilon}\le 2\pi^2-c\epsilon^2+O(\epsilon^4)$ with $c>0$. Both signed norms satisfy the original bound strictly. The argument requires the local rotation action and establishes a cut in the actual entering class with that action. [Local cut and original trace test](../notes/LOCAL_AXIS_MARKED_CUT_20261127.md)

The independent curvature shortcut under the stronger Bach-flat hypothesis also checks. On the punctured tube let $q=b^{-2}(dr^2+a^2dx^2)$, $K=K_q$ and $u=K+1$. The horizontal Weyl sectional curvature is $u/3$ and the mixed one is $-u/6$. Thus the horizontal Bach equation is $(\Delta_q u\,q-\nabla_q^2u)/3+(K^2-1)q/6=0$. Its trace and trace-free parts give $\nabla_q^2u=(1-K^2)q/2$. The curvature transformation gives $u=O(r^2)$ and $|\nabla_q u|=O(r^2)$. Integrating the Hessian equation yields $|\nabla_qu|^2=K-K^3/3+2/3=u^2(1-u/3)$, with the integration constant fixed by the axis limits. For any fixed $0<\eta<1$ the resulting radial inequality $|\partial_r u|\le(1+\eta)|u|/r$ on a smaller tube gives $|u(r)|\le |u(s)|(r/s)^{1+\eta}$ for $0<s<r$. Passing to $s=0$ shows $u=0$. The Weyl tensor vanishes on this open tube, so on a connected Bach-flat closed manifold continuation applies directly.
