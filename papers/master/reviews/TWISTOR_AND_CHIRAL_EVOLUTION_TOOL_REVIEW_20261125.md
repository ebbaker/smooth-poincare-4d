## An immersion supplies explicit spinors in both chiralities

Master Theorem 3.1 starts with an actual smooth immersion $F:M\to\mathbb R^5$, available on every smooth homotopy four-sphere. Give $M$ its induced metric $g=F^*g_{\rm Euc}$ and spin structure. Restrict an orthonormal complex basis of four parallel ambient spinors to obtain a pointwise orthonormal basis $\Psi_1,\ldots,\Psi_4$ of the full spinor bundle. Their chiral projections $\psi_j^\pm$ satisfy $\sum_j|\psi_j^\pm|^2=2$ everywhere, by the rank of the chiral projection. Although an individual projection can vanish, this family has no common zero. [Master Theorem 3.1](../manuscript/immersion_entry.tex#L13) · [Bär, Gauduchon and Moroianu, §§3 and 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0303095)

Let $A$ be the shape operator, $H=\operatorname{tr}A$, and $A^\circ=A-H\,\mathrm{Id}/4$. With the intrinsic Clifford convention $\nabla_X\Psi_j=\tfrac12c(AX)\Psi_j$, the spinorial Gauss formula gives $D\Psi_j=-H\Psi_j/2$. Thus the twistor operator $P_X=\nabla_X+\tfrac14c(X)D$ satisfies

$$
 P_X\Psi_j=\tfrac12c(A^\circ X)\Psi_j,
 \qquad \sum_{j=1}^4|P\Psi_j|^2=|A^\circ|^2.
$$

The matching paper's identity $2|\Pi(d^\nabla P\psi^\pm)^\pm|^2=|W^\pm|^2|\psi^\pm|^2$ applies to arbitrary smooth fields. Summing it over the four projections recovers the chiral curvature density pointwise without dividing by a small spinor norm. There is also a first-order bound in this particular geometry. Substituting the displayed formula into the immersion paper's shape estimate gives

$$
 E_\pm(g)+\frac89\int_M(-\det A)_+\,dV_g
 \le \frac16\int_M\left(\sum_{j=1}^4|P\Psi_j|^2\right)^2 dV_g .
$$

This gives the spinor program a concrete quantity to estimate across the handles and their attaching regions on one immersion. An upper bound that brings $E_+$ to $64\pi^2/9$ already suffices for immersed recognition. The threshold and its extrinsic estimate are in the existing work. The additional observation is the identification of their input with the twistor defect of specified fields. A new gain would require independent control of that integral, or a sharper control of the projected derivatives. The restricted fields solve a Dirac equation with the displayed mean-curvature term. Any transmission estimate must include this forcing, and applying the conclusion to a separately selected Yamabe metric would require a metric comparison. [Shape estimate](../../investigations-1/04_hypersurface_immersions/manuscript/chiral_energy_of_hypersurface_immersions.tex#L188) · [Projected derivative](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/01_dirac_collar_matching/manuscript/dirac_transmission_on_marked_collars.tex#L243)

The older degree-one-map test gives a check on the same idea. For the oriented normal map $\nu$, the induced metric satisfies $|d\nu|^2=|A|^2$ and $s_g=H^2-|A|^2$. Its twisted-Dirac potential therefore has deficit $s_g-3|d\nu|^2=-4|A^\circ|^2$. A constant test gives a strictly negative modified Yamabe quotient for a nonumbilic immersion. Using the raw Gauss map in this test would demand more than the master needs. [Fixed-map quotient](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L882)

## Curvature prescription only has to beat the minimizing excess

The sections paper shows that imposing a prescribed round ball leaves the positive-class infimum unchanged. At such a reference it can make a small exterior perturbation preserving its volume form and energy exactly, and obtain an onto linearization of $W^+$. In a common isometric identification of the Weyl bundles, the new reference admits a genuine path $W^+(g_t)=(1-t)W^+(g_0)$ for a positive duration. Its energy is $(1-t)^2E_+(g_0)$. The marked ball remains anti-self-dual along the path and its metric can change. [Marking approximation](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L2885) · [Prescription theorem](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L1190)

Choose positive regular references of energy $e_i\downarrow I>0$ in the strict band and put $d_i=e_i-I$. A single path at a single index would contradict the hypothesized infimum if it reached

$$
 t>1-\sqrt{I/e_i}=\frac{d_i}{e_i+\sqrt{e_i I}}.
$$

The decreasing energy stays below the $8\pi^2$ wall, preserving positive Yamabe sign along the joined path. The required duration is asymptotic to $d_i/(2e_i)$. The sections paper supplies sufficient durations of order $C_i|a|$ or $C_i a^2$ along its chosen regularizing perturbation $a$, according to the projection of $W^+$ onto the adjoint kernel. **The missing estimate is a comparison of those source-dependent durations with $d_i/e_i$.** A bound for the inverse only in the radial direction would already matter. At a nonround Bach-flat reference $L^*W^+=-B/4=0$, so the linear equation $Lh=-W^+$ there has no solution. The perturbed reference and the second-order alternative address a real obstruction. [Step-size estimate](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L1500) · [Bach pairing](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L3671)

This gives a global alternative to cutting the E61 disk. For a supported version on that *particular* disk, the master bounds the first variation by $C\sqrt{d_i}$ on the critical unit ball of metric variations. The round-model transmission result also identifies a forced opposite-chirality response and two radial moments for compact support. A localized prescription would have to bound this response on the actual curved reference, including the cutoff widths and the unchanged complement. The round ball introduced to regularize a reference carries zero Weyl charge in its interior and cannot stand in for the selected charged disk. ## What the chiral connections say about the modified Ricci flow

In the regime of master Proposition 2.4, use its upper volume-ratio assumption, fixed positive-time markings, and rescaled smooth initial approximations $q_i$. On every compact test support the Weyl and scalar square integrals tend to zero, while $|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2dV\rightharpoonup\mu$ with $\mu\ne0$. The collar-matching paper computes the normalized chiral characteristic forms of the Levi–Civita spin connections. In its conventions,

$$
 H_\pm=\left(|W^\pm|^2+\frac{s^2}{48}-\frac{|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2}{4}\right)dV,
 \qquad H_\pm(q_i)\rightharpoonup-\tfrac14\mu .
$$

Their sum gives the master's initial Euler trace $-\mu/2$. Each chirality therefore already detects the negative charge on the approximations. On a nonnegative cutoff carrying $\mu$, the positive chiral Levi–Civita connection cannot have positive curvature wedge matrix throughout the support for all late indices. A definite connection built independently, or a different conformal representative, has its own curvature test. Even on the exact round product collar $dt^2+g_{S^3}$ the Levi–Civita curvature satisfies $F_i\wedge F_j=0$. These computations explain the real obstacle to starting Fine's flow from the geometries used by the master. [Master Proposition 2.4](../manuscript/recognition_gap.tex#L118) · [Signed spin-connection forms](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/01_dirac_collar_matching/manuscript/dirac_transmission_on_marked_collars.tex#L337) · [Product collar](../../investigations-1/01_chiral_metric_evolution/manuscript/chiral_curvature_and_metric_evolution.tex#L14133)

A specific use of the older connection calculation would be to transport the two charges separately on a marked flow region with boundary. In fixed spin bundles along a smooth time interval, $H_\pm=\sigma_\pm\operatorname{Tr}(F_\pm\wedge F_\pm)$, with $\sigma_+=-1$, $\sigma_-=1$. The Bianchi identity gives the globally defined transgression current

$$
 \partial_t H_\pm=dJ_\pm,\qquad
 J_\pm=2\sigma_\pm\operatorname{Tr}(\dot A_\pm\wedge F_\pm).
$$

The two terms distinguish boundary fluxes that can cancel in their sum. To obtain a stronger singular-end exclusion one would need a geometric estimate on at least one flux on the actual marked boundary. The initial split alone yields no such estimate. On a smooth global candidate the older static criterion recognizes the type when a round-compatible positive definite connection has sufficiently small native strain. A separate compactness theorem applies to a sequence with strictly tamed metric twistor spaces, pointwise Ricci and $W^+$ bounds, a volume lower bound, and diameter and Euler upper bounds. The general physical minimizing sequence has an integrated curvature budget. Applying either theorem requires supplying its additional geometry. ## Priorities for the manuscript and Lean

For the intrinsic proof I would first test the radial duration against the minimizing excess. For the immersion proof I would test the restricted spinors across one of the actual curved attachments. The first question could rule out a positive infimum on an entire smooth type. The second starts with fields available on every type and asks for a first-order estimate on a specified geometry.

Lean already has the three-axis matrix estimate. Its relative spinor estimate explicitly takes a regularized integrated Bochner inequality as a premise. On the immersed route the geometric inputs to formalize would be the hypersurface spin connection, its identification with the intrinsic connection, and the shape identity above. On the intrinsic route the substantive missing input is a bound for the actual radial lift. The scalar comparison with $e_i-I$ is elementary once that bound exists.
