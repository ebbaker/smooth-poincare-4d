## Variational comparison

- On the fixed smooth homotopy sphere, zero first conformal-Laplacian eigenvalue gives a smooth scalar-flat representative. Chern–Gauss–Bonnet gives $16\pi^2=2E_+-\|\operatorname{Ric}\|_2^2/2$, hence the wall is $E_+\ge8\pi^2$. The first eigenvalue is continuous along each smooth path and has the Yamabe sign. Staying strictly below the wall along a path from a positive class preserves that sign. The lower bound in each positive class then follows by taking the unit-volume Yamabe representative and dropping its nonnegative trace-free Ricci integral. [Lee–Parker, §2, scalar-curvature transformation and Yamabe form](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf) · [Draft sign test](../manuscript/variational_comparison.tex#L1)
- For the local replacement, take the all-lowered curvature in fixed coordinates. The constant linear second-order part yields distributional convergence, and the first-derivative quadratic term converges strongly in $L^2$. The coordinate curvature bound gives weak $L^2$ convergence. Uniform metric convergence permits passage through the chiral projection and density factor. With $a_s=1-s\chi$, the product rule gives an error of order $o_{L^2}(1)$ in $F_\pm(n_{i,s})-F_\pm(h)-a_s r_i$, uniform in $s\in[0,1]$. The term linear in $r_i$ in the integrated energy difference is $-2s\langle\chi F_\pm(h),r_i\rangle_{L^2}$ and tends to zero uniformly. The quadratic difference is $-(2s\chi-s^2\chi^2)|r_i|^2$. Total source energies agree by signature. The wall applies before invoking minimality. The drop estimate gives strong local convergence for both matrix densities. Repairing until the metric equals $h$ on a neighborhood of a test support makes the subsequent compact variation have exactly the energy increment of $h$, independent of the source index. Taking the sum of the two first variations gives the compactly supported full Weyl first variation, hence the Bach equation. - For the short cut, extend the logarithm of the initial chart multiplier using the unused collar margin. The metric path is smooth on the joined type and uniformly positive definite by convexity. With $0\le 1-s\psi\le1$, the all-lowered estimate for the splice gives total excess and each signed raw-side excess bounded above by $o(1)$. The endpoint is exactly product on an open subcollar. Lengthening there gives smooth joined metrics for every finite $L$, preserves energy, and preserves positive sign by the same wall. Each fixed compact test on a side completed by a half-cylinder sits in a sufficiently long joined metric. Its quotient is therefore bounded below by $\sqrt{48(8\pi^2-e_i')}$ before taking any source limit. With $r=e^{-t}$ the end compactifies by the smooth flat ball metric $r^2(dt^2+\gamma)$ near the center. The point cutoff has gradient cost $O(r^2)$ and gives the closed cap the same Yamabe lower bound. For each full side, the only possible increase of Weyl energy comes from its own splice. The cut criterion now bounds the smaller raw mass by $e_i-I^+(M)+o(1)$. The argument is for entire sides, including their portions in the splices. ## Complete physical limit

The scalar and Ricci limits use weak $L^2$ curvature on each fixed compact, with contractions and density factors converging uniformly. The rescaled scalar is $a_i^2Y_i\to0$. Weak lower semicontinuity, first on compacts and then by exhaustion, gives $d\le4e+y^2/12-32\pi^2$. Local strong Weyl convergence and the exact Bach equation are supplied by the preceding variational comparison. The Sobolev inequality transported to the smooth limit excludes compact $X$ by the constant test. With $v_x(s)=\operatorname{Vol}B_s(x)/s^4$, the cutoff inequality implies $v_x(s)\ge(y/96)v_x(s/2)^{1/2}$. Iteration to the Euclidean small-ball limit gives $v_x(s)\ge(y/96)^2$ at all centers and radii. Tian–Viaclovsky's small-energy estimate applies uniformly to balls centered far out, because their curvature energies are bounded by the tail and the volume lower bound holds at every center. The resulting $o(r^{-2})$ curvature decay, Sobolev inequality and integrable negative Ricci part give finitely many differentiated order-zero ALE ends. Ache–Viaclovsky then give order two in dimension four for scalar-flat Bach-flat metrics. [Draft budget and ALE argument](../manuscript/complete_limits.tex#L18) · [Tian–Viaclovsky, 2008 published text, (1.5) and Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43177?nt=1) · [Tian–Viaclovsky, 2005 v3, definition (1.3)–(1.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0310302) · [Ache–Viaclovsky, v2, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1106.1249v2)

On each fixed large end annulus the comparison uses the additional constant scale $R^{-2}$ after $a_i^{-2}$, in the coordinate dilation to the unit annulus. The full curvature integral is scale invariant, so the source budget bounds it uniformly there. With the fixed radial cutoff the Cartesian estimates apply on the finite cover and descend to the quotient. The whole truncation supplies the marked link. The changes of chiral energy are bounded above by $O(R^{-2})+o_i(1;R)$, where all constants in $o_i$ are used at fixed $R$. Hence the modified closed source for that link alone has $E_+<9\pi^2$. Hillman's finite list, the smooth spin obstruction for the Poincaré sphere, and the product-boundary signature formula apply. For $Q_8$, the three order-four subgroup terms give $8\eta=3\cdot4(-1/2)$ in one orientation since the common involution and identity terms are zero. The sides have zero signature. Their boundary orientations are opposite, so one side has $E_+-E_-=9\pi^2$ in the manuscript convention. Nonnegativity of $E_-$ contradicts the source $E_+<9\pi^2$. This eliminates each quotient link before applying the multi-end Euler identity. No estimate on a sum of all splices, and no Yamabe sign for the quotient diagnostic, is required.
