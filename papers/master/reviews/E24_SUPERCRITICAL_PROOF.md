## Radius floor, properness and smooth markings

Both powers in the mixed harmonic-radius definition are positive for (p>2). An admissible chart is defined on the whole intrinsic ball. Restricting it to a smaller ball at a different center and translating the coordinate origin preserves the inequalities, so the supremum is 1-Lipschitz. Positivity of the radius and compactness ensure that (h/d) attains its minimum away from (d=0), or on the whole source if the protected ball is already the whole source. Failure of a floor at a point with (dge a_i/2) makes this minimum tend to zero. With (b_i=h(z_i)) at the chosen minimum, the normalized harmonic radius is one at (z_i) and at least (1-Db_i/d(z_i)) on (B_{b_i^{-2}g_i}(z_i,D)). Such balls lie in the original (a_i)-ball eventually for every fixed (D). Scaling the native tensor norm and volume gives the displayed (L^p) estimate (L(b_i/a_i)^{2-4/p}) for trace-free Ricci there.

For the finite nets, use ellipticity on whole intrinsic chart balls with a fixed radius below the normalized floor. A Euclidean coordinate ball of a smaller fixed radius lies in each chart image, by lifting its radial segments and ruling out first exit using the intrinsic buffer. Still smaller Euclidean balls and their source images are bi-Lipschitz for the ambient distances. A shortest path joining two such points stays in the chart by the length of the comparison straight segment. This gives packing at every sufficiently small fixed mesh. Enlarge the covered radius by that mesh at each step, following ambient minimizing segments, to obtain a finite net on any fixed pointed window. Volumes of these windows are bounded above by a finite sum of chart-ball volumes. Thus the scalar term (b_i^2Y_i) also goes to zero in (L^p) on every such window. These bounds establish pointed compactness and a proper complete limit without invoking global volume comparison on the sources. ## Local replacement and the weak Bach equation

On a fixed protected chart write the pulled-back constant physical rescalings as (m_i). Their weak (W^{2,p}), uniform and strong (W^{1,2p}) convergence gives weak (L^p) convergence of the full all-lowered curvature and the chiral densities, since the curvature is linear in (D^2m_i) up to terms quadratic in (Dm_i), with continuous coefficients. In particular the Weyl matrix densities, with the volume-density weight of the owning proof, converge weakly in (L^2). For a compactly supported smooth cutoff (chi), the formal rough interpolation has the owning quadratic defect identity, with nonnegative factor (2schi-s^2chi^2) in its energy decrement. The formal metrics need not be admitted competitors. Fix a smooth (h_j) approximating the limit in (W^{2,p}) on the cutoff support and replace (h) by (h_j) in the interpolation. Its difference there is (schi(h_j-h)). Continuity of the curvature expression, uniform ellipticity and bounded (W^{2,p}) norms bound the change of full chiral energy by (C|h_j-h|_{W^{2,p}}), uniformly in (i) and (s\in[0,1]). The resulting smooth path on the actual closed source starts at (g_i) and remains strictly below (8pi^2) for a sufficiently large fixed (j) and then large (i). The zero wall keeps it positive Yamabe. Apply the minimizing inequality to the endpoint, take the source limsup at fixed (j), then send (j) to infinity. This proves strong local (L^2) convergence of both matrix densities. For the negative block use equality of the two *closed* integrals, with no such equality required on the chart. [Owning local replacement and zero wall](../manuscript/variational_comparison.tex#L1)

For a smooth tensor (H) supported within a single fixed chart, it also suffices to vary the smooth source directly by (m_i+sH), restoring the physical constant scale before gluing. For small (s) of either sign, the first two derivatives of the varied chiral matrix in (s) are uniformly bounded in (L^2), as is the matrix itself, by its formula and the local (W^{2,p}) bounds. The first derivative at zero converges weakly in (L^2) because its dependence on second metric jets is linear, with uniformly converging coefficients, and all products of first jets converge strongly in (L^p). Pair with the strongly convergent unvaried matrix. For any sufficiently small fixed (s), the uniform Taylor bound keeps the path below the wall. The minimizing inequality, followed by (i\to\infty) and then (s\to0) from both signs, proves local first variation zero for both chiral energies of the rough metric. This also follows by the smooth-comparison repair and taking the two limits in the same order. No smooth whole marking is needed at this stage. The scalar curvature identity passes by the same weak second-jet and strong first-jet convergence and gives constant scalar curvature, zero at shrinking physical scales.

For the coefficient products in the Bach pairing, let (p'=p/(p-1)), let (a\in W^{2,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}) be any contraction or density factor, and let (phi\in W^{2,p'}_0) in a fixed smaller coordinate domain. Multiplication by (a) preserves (W^{2,p'}_0). In dimension four use (a\in L^\infty), (Da\in L^{2p}), (D^2a\in L^p), and the Sobolev inclusions

\]

The reciprocal exponents are permitted since (1/(2p)<1/4) and (p>2). The two Hessian cross products (DaD\phi) and (D^2a\,\phi) both belong to (L^{p'}). These estimates apply to (h^{-1}), (sqrt{\det h}), and their needed inverses by ellipticity. The gradient formula for a smooth approximation converges by the same multiplier estimates and continuity of (h\mapsto B_h\) into (W^{-2,p}). Stationarity against smooth (H) therefore gives (B_h=0) as a (W^{-2,p}) distribution, by density and inversion on (W^{2,p'}_0). This argues locally in each harmonic chart. ## Bootstrap for all finite (p>2)

In dimension four the cited weak identity applies in a single coordinate ball to (h\in W^{2,p}) with constant scalar and (B_h=0). It gives

\[
 \Delta_h\operatorname{Ric}_h=\mathrm{Rm}_h*\operatorname{Ric}_h\in L^{p/2},
 \qquad \operatorname{Ric}_h\in L^p\subset L^{p'}.
\]

The hypotheses of Theorem 2.2(ii) of the cited preprint are (k=2), coefficient exponent (q=p>2), and target exponent (p/2\in(1,q]), so it gives (operatorname{Ric}_h\in W^{2,p/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}). For (2<p<4) this supplies (t=2p/(4-p)) for Ricci. Independently the existing (W^{2,p}) bound on the metric gives (Dh\in L^{2t}_{\mathrm{loc}}), making its quadratic first-derivative term (L^t). To raise the scalar component equation (h^{ab}\partial_{ab}h_{jk}=F_{jk}\in L^t) to (W^{2,t}), choose a cutoff on a small ball where the continuous principal coefficients have sufficiently small oscillation. The localized equation has (L^t) forcing since (Dh\in L^{2t}). On that ball solve with zero boundary using the inverse of the frozen constant-coefficient operator in (W^{2,t}) and a contraction for the coefficient difference. The (W^{2,t}) solution is also (W^{2,p}) and agrees with the localized original solution by the same uniqueness argument at exponent (p). This justifies the gain (t>p) independently of the range of the rough tensor estimate. Iterate on nested interiors. At (p=4), (W^{2,2}) Ricci and (W^{1,4}) first metric jets both have every finite local integrability exponent, so choose (t>4). The recurrence before reaching four is (1/2-1/t=2(1/2-1/p)), giving finitely many exponent steps. Once (p>4), Ricci is Hölder from (W^{2,p/2}), and the metric is (C^{1,\alpha}). The harmonic scalar equation gives (C^{2,\beta}) for some (eta>0), then the tensor Ricci equation and the harmonic scalar equation iterate via interior Schauder estimates. In the Ricci-flat radius proof the initial Ricci term is zero, so the scalar portion of the same reasoning applies immediately. ## Curvature mass, quantiles and attainment

On smaller protected windows the source (W^{2,p}) charts bound the full curvature in (L^p), not merely Ricci. After scaling (a_i) to one, a physical ball of radius (b/a_i) inside the protected central chart has volume at most (C(b/a_i)^4) by ellipticity and its intrinsic buffer. Hence Hölder bounds the squared curvature integral by (C(b/a_i)^{4-8/p}). The integral is invariant under the constant rescaling. For (u_i/a_i\to0), a bounded subsequence of (P_i(x_i,a_i)) would force vanishing full mass at radius (u_i), contradicting the lower bound (4\theta e_i) when the ball is charged. For the optional short-annulus argument it is precisely this hypothetical bounded subsequence that supplies the chart. With (s_i=\sqrt{u_i a_i}), normalizing the metric at the center and dilating by (s_i) gives the asserted (O((s_i/a_i)^{1-2/p})) uniform and (L^4) first-jet errors by Morrey and Hölder. On the fixed annulus the full curvature integral is bounded by the source budget. The radius ratios and local ellipticity put the entire inner ball inside the cut and the entire coordinate disk inside the physical median ball at the same center. The exterior of that median-radius ball has at least (e_i/2) chiral energy. The owning marked-cut proof supplies positivity on both capped sides by the original-type stretched path and the zero wall. For the shrinking-scale theorem, apply the floor on larger fixed windows, pass to a subsequential pointed limit, and use the local stationarity and bootstrap before constructing the smooth compact markings. The complete physical limit theorem then applies and makes the limit flat. In a fixed flat chart of the limit the pulled-back connection one-forms tend to zero in (L^4), while curvature remains bounded in (L^2). Testing the Euler transgression against compact cutoffs shows the localized Euler integrals tend to zero. The four-index identity (|\mathrm{Rm}|^2+\mathcal E=2|W|^2+R^2/3) makes the full curvature integral tend to zero on smaller compacts by strong Weyl convergence and the scaled constant scalar tending to zero. A finite cover and the first-exit inclusion give the whole-ball bound. Applying this extraction to any subsequence on which the conclusion fails proves the convergence on the given subsequence. With (N_i\to\infty) and (a_i=N_i^{-1/\gamma}) the assumed bound holds for all centers, since (a_i^\gamma N_i=1). If the supremum of the fixed-window curvature energy failed to vanish, choose violating centers. Hence a charged quantile cannot have bounded (r_i(\theta)/a_i). When (N_i) is bounded, use (a_i=1) throughout. The floor is uniform across the source. Packing disjoint fixed small balls along a minimizing segment and using unit physical volume bounds the diameter.
