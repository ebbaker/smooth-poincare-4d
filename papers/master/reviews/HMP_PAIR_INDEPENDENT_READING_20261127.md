## Tracking the two cancellations

Read the upper attachments \(C,A\) downwards. They are the reverse replacements on the actual \(C,A\) filling tori. In the forward lower link they are represented by zero-framed small meridians \(\mu_C,\mu_A\) of \(C,A\). On each corresponding boundary torus the old normal-circle direction becomes the new core direction. The small meridian and its disk-framed parallel push into the inserted torus as the two parallel core circles under the product-coordinate swap. This checks the reverse framing. The replacement is relative to the other filled tori, in particular \(U,D\). Although the meridian \(\mu_A\) is drawn before surgery on \(A\), it represents the actual dual after the \(A\) filling. The isotopy uses the local meridian annulus and the collar of its filling torus.

Use the order \(\mu_C,\mu_A,A,D\) for the four disjoint middle attaching tubes. First cancel the dotted \(C\) with its small meridian, which passes geometrically once over its belt. Choose the \(\mu_A\) ball on a short arc of the **unremoved** \(A\), off the dotted disks, the \(C\)-cancellation region and all other attaching tubes. This can be done by taking the arc away from their contact points before choosing the normal disk. The lower cancellation can be performed relative to this ball. The \(A\) tube is transported through the outgoing cancellation comparison everywhere else. Thus the retained \(b=\mu_A\) is still a local disk-framed unknot in the actual \(\partial H_U\), even when its spanning disk meets the retained \(A\) arc. The framed attaching tubes themselves are disjoint.

To check the upper cancellation, undo the \(C,A\) fillings of the common surgery carrier, with their inverse parametrizations. The actual \(D\) filling torus is untouched. The belt of the last forward \(D\) is its core in this same marked solid torus. In the resulting boundary \(\partial H_{U,D}\) that core crosses the belt sphere of the *dotted* \(D\) once. In the dotted-handle boundary model this sphere is the spanning disk on the exterior side capped by a meridional disk of the zero filling. The core meets the cap once. It is disjoint from the external \(U\) belt. The reverse \(D\) therefore cancels the upper dotted \(D\) while carrying the exterior \(U\) and the transported closing. The inverse is the reversed sequence of the same collar comparisons. [05, dual tube and reassembly formulas][05]

## Disposition

The remaining smooth handle presentation has counts \((1,1,2,1,1)\). The surviving pair is \(a\), the transported \(A\), and the local zero-framed \(b\). The lower row is primitive since \(H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=0\); \(b\) has winding zero, so \(a\) has winding \(\pm1\). The \(b\)-first cut in its actual mark is \(Y_2=\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\) by local zero surgery. A PSC connected-sum metric on \(Y_2\) and the identity on this cut give a degree-\(+1\) source. The \(Z_b\) clause of 05’s theorem recognizes this closing as standard. The calculation provides a marked topological witness independently of any four-dimensional collar estimate. It concerns the specified HMP Figure 3 gluing and its transported inverse. An arbitrary divergence from that marking has no comparison here. [05, Theorem 4.2][05] · [HMP, Figures 3–4][hmp]
