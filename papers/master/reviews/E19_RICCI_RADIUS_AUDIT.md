## Conditional radius floor

Let \((M_i,g_i)\) be smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian integral homology four-spheres. Assume \(Y([g_i])\ge y_0>0\) and \(\int_{M_i}|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_i}|_{\mathrm{tens}}^2\,dV_{g_i}\le\Lambda\). Fix finite \(p>2\), \(0<a_i\le1\), and write \(\widetilde g_i=a_i^{-2}g_i\). For sets \(S_i\subset\Omega_i\subset M_i\), with \(\Omega_i\) open, suppose there is a fixed \(b>0\) such that \(B_{\widetilde g_i}(x,b)\subset\Omega_i\) for every \(x\in S_i\), and

\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}_{\widetilde g_i}\|_{L^p(\Omega_i,dV_{\widetilde g_i})}\le C.
\]

The mixed \(W^{2,p}\) harmonic radius defined below satisfies \(r_H(g_i,x)\ge c a_i\) for \(x\in S_i\), for a positive \(c\) depending only on the displayed bounds and the chart thresholds. A fixed trimmed distance annulus has this buffer in a wider annulus, by the distance function's Lipschitz bound. In a complete-window hypothesis, the estimate on \(B_{\widetilde g_i}(o_i,A+1)\) gives the floor on \(B_{\widetilde g_i}(o_i,A)\) for each fixed \(A\). The constants may depend on \(A\). The argument also works with a scaled pointwise Ricci bound, using its decay directly in the fixed size normalized harmonic charts.

In the master setting the physical metrics are volume-one Yamabe minimizers in their conformal classes, and \(e_i=E_+(g_i)\) uses the operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm on \(\Lambda^+\). The closed signature and Gauss–Bonnet identities give \(Y_i^2=48(8\pi^2-e_i)+12\|\operatorname{Ric}_{0,g_i}\|_2^2\) and \(\int|\operatorname{Rm}|_{\mathrm{tens}}^2=8e_i+2\|\operatorname{Ric}_{0,g_i}\|_2^2+Y_i^2/6\). Thus \(e_i\le8\pi^2-\delta\) and \(Y_i\le8\sqrt6\pi\) supply the two global bounds. The radius argument works with these bounds supplied directly, including when the source smooth type varies. ## Radius convention, localization and scale

Fix \(m>1\), \(\varepsilon_H>0\) and \(q=2p>4\). An admissible radius \(r\) is one for which the *whole* open intrinsic ball \(B_g(x,r)\) admits centered smooth harmonic coordinates, with array \(G\) satisfying

\[
 m^{-1}I\le G\le mI,\qquad
 r^{1-4/q}\|DG\|_{L^q(dz)}+r^{2-4/p}\|D^2G\|_{L^p(dz)}\le\varepsilon_H.
\]

The integrals use coordinate Lebesgue measure on the chart image. Take the supremum over such radii. Both powers are positive, so restriction and translation of the coordinates preserve admissibility on every contained ball of smaller radius. Local smooth harmonic coordinates with an affine normalization give positivity at every center. Compactness of the closed source makes the radius finite. Restriction to \(B_g(y,r-d_g(x,y))\) for admissible \(r>d_g(x,y)\), followed by interchange of the two centers, proves the radius is 1-Lipschitz. It rescales as a length by dilating harmonic coordinates by the same factor as the metric distance. The mixed exponents matter when \(2<p<4\): the single \(L^p\) first-derivative term with power \(1-4/p\) does not give this restriction proof. [Earlier radius convention and restriction proof with \(p>4\)](../../investigations-1/01_chiral_metric_evolution/notes/165_MINIMUM_HARMONIC_RADIUS_EXTRACTION_TEST_20261122.md)

Suppose \(x_i\in S_i\) have \(r_H(\widetilde g_i,x_i)\to0\). On \(\overline B_{\widetilde g_i}(x_i,b/2)\), maximize \(d_i(z)/r_H(\widetilde g_i,z)\), where \(d_i(z)=b/2-d_{\widetilde g_i}(x_i,z)\). Write \(z_i\) for a maximizer and \(\lambda_i=r_H(\widetilde g_i,z_i)\). Then \(\lambda_i\le r_H(\widetilde g_i,x_i)\to0\) and \(d_i(z_i)/\lambda_i\to\infty\). For any fixed \(D\), the radius on \(B_{\widetilde g_i}(z_i,D\lambda_i)\) is at least \(\lambda_i(1-D\lambda_i/d_i(z_i))\) by maximality. These balls lie in \(\Omega_i\) eventually. In \(k_i=\lambda_i^{-2}\widetilde g_i\) the radius at \(z_i\) is exactly one and has a floor \(1/2\) on every fixed ball about \(z_i\) once \(i\) is large enough. The physical bubble scale \(a_i\lambda_i\) tends to zero. For fixed \(D\), tensor norm and native volume scaling give

\[
 \|\operatorname{Ric}_{k_i}\|_{L^p(B_{k_i}(z_i,D),dV_{k_i})}
 \le C\lambda_i^{2-4/p}\longrightarrow0.
\]

No regularity of the distance sphere at the outer buffer is used.

## Complete limit and whole smooth compact markings

Uniformly elliptic harmonic charts on intrinsic balls of radius \(1/4\) have a common smaller Euclidean ball in their coordinate images. To see this, lift Euclidean radial segments of length less than \(1/(4\sqrt m)\). The length bound for their lifts keeps them strictly inside the intrinsic chart ball, so completeness prevents a first exit. On a still smaller common Euclidean ball, these charts and inverses are uniformly Lipschitz for ambient distances. A shortest path between two such points stays in the intrinsic chart ball by the upper bound from the straight coordinate segment. This also gives a uniform distance margin from a compact coordinate subball to the complement of a slightly larger chart subball.

For properness, fix \(D\) and a small \(t>0\) compared with this chart margin. Every ball of radius \(2t\) centered in \(B_{k_i}(z_i,D+1)\) has uniformly bounded \(t\)-packing by the chart comparison with a Euclidean ball. Cover \(B_{k_i}(z_i,D)\) by successive finite \(t\)-nets, starting at \(z_i\) and enlarging the covered radius by \(t\) at each step. A point in the next shell lies within \(2t\) of a previous center, by following a minimizing segment from \(z_i\). Choose the next net inside the part being covered, so every center remains in the prescribed window. This gives a uniform finite bound at each fixed \(D,t\), without any Ricci lower bound or injectivity input. Diagonalize distances between finite nets with mesh tending to zero on larger balls. Their completed limit is proper, and limits of minimizing segments or repeated midpoints give a connected pointed geodesic space.

Lift a countable dense set of that space to chart centers and take chart limits on fixed smaller coordinate balls. The uniform local Lipschitz and interior margins give open bi-Lipschitz limit charts that cover the space, persistent overlaps on compacts, and identification of the distance with the limit metric's length distance locally and then globally. The matrices converge weakly in \(W^{2,p}\) and strongly in \(C^{0,\alpha}\) for \(0<\alpha<\min(1,2-4/p)\), and in \(W^{1,2p}\), by compact embedding in dimension four. The limiting metric is complete because the pointed distance is proper.

In each chart the equation is

\[
 G_i^{ab}\partial_{ab}G_i=-2\operatorname{Ric}(G_i)+Q(G_i^{-1},DG_i),
\]

with \(Q\) quadratic in the first derivatives. Ellipticity compares tensor component norms and coordinate volume with their native counterparts. The Ricci term goes to zero in \(L^p\), and strong \(W^{1,2p}\) makes the quadratic terms converge strongly in \(L^p\). The inverse metric coefficients converge uniformly and are uniformly Hölder. The limit \(G\) solves the same equation with zero Ricci. Apply the interior \(W^{2,p}\) estimate to \(G_i-G\), with right side given by the difference of quadratic terms, the vanishing Ricci term, and \((G^{ab}-G_i^{ab})\partial_{ab}G\). The right side tends to zero in \(L^p\), so the convergence is strong in \(W^{2,p}\) on smaller charts. The harmonic-coordinate Ricci identity holds for these matrices, and harmonicity passes by strong first-order convergence. To obtain smoothness, if \(p<4\) the local gain from the quadratic term takes \(p\) to \(2p/(4-p)>p\) by Sobolev and the interior estimate with Hölder coefficients. The reciprocal exponent follows \(1/p_{\rm next}=2/p-1/2\), so this reaches \(p\ge4\) in finitely many steps. At \(p=4\) choose a finite first-derivative exponent greater than eight. Once \(p>4\), apply Schauder estimates to the exact Ricci-flat equation and iterate. [Avalos–Cogo–Royo Abrego, arXiv:2610.35257v1, (4.1) and the interior step in the proof of Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.35257v1#S4)

The overlap maps solve the scalar harmonic equation with the same inverse metric coefficients. Their locally uniform convergence follows from chart approximation and the persistent overlaps. The limits solve the scalar harmonic equation for the smooth limiting coefficients, as do their inverses, so they give a smooth oriented atlas. On an overlap the difference of the transition maps \(T_i-T\) has right side \((G^{ab}-G_i^{ab})\partial_{ab}T\), tending to zero in \(L^{2p}\) and \(W^{1,p}\). Interior estimates first give strong \(W^{2,2p}\) convergence. Differentiate the equation once and use \(DG_i^{-1}\) bounded in \(L^{2p}\) and \(D^2(T_i-T)\to0\) in \(L^{2p}\) to control the commutator in \(L^p\). This gives strong \(W^{3,p}\), hence strong \(C^1\), on smaller overlaps. Uniform \(C^1\) convergence of the **metric components** themselves is not used for \(2<p\le4\).

To recover actual smooth embeddings, on a finite chart cover of a neighborhood of a fixed compact set use the Euclidean blocks \((\zeta_a,\zeta_a u_a)\) for cutoffs \(\zeta_a\) supported inside chart coordinate balls \(u_a\). Their concatenation gives a smooth embedding where at least one cutoff is positive, because that block recovers the local coordinates. Apply the same blocks to the source charts. The resulting maps converge in their chart expressions in \(C^1\) and \(W^{3,p}\), including near cutoff boundaries by the overlap margins. Project onto a smooth tubular neighborhood of the limit image and return to the limit coordinates. The resulting source-to-limit maps tend to the identity in local chart expressions. Invert on smaller chart balls by the derivative and range margins. The branches agree wherever both are defined, since candidates for the same preimage lie in a common larger approximating chart by metric convergence and the common map is locally injective there. These branches give a single smooth orientation-preserving embedding \(F_i\) on a neighborhood of the *whole* compact set. Its local expressions converge in \(C^1\) and \(W^{3,p}\). Here composition and the differentiated inverse identity use first derivatives bounded, second derivatives in \(L^{2p}\), third derivatives in \(L^p\), and uniformly bounded inverse Jacobians. Pullback of the metric then gives strong \(W^{2,p}\) on compacts: products involving one derivative of a metric and two derivatives of a map are controlled by the two \(L^{2p}\) factors. A small smooth translation in the limit chart makes the maps based. This adapts the finite-atlas construction to the point-picked charts. The abstract compactness theorem with pointwise Ricci and injectivity hypotheses is unnecessary. For each fixed \(D\), choose embeddings on a compact neighborhood of \(\overline B_h(z,D+1)\) in the resulting limit \((X,h,z)\). Their images contain \(B_{k_i}(z_i,D)\) eventually. A source path exiting the embedded limit \((D+1)\)-ball must already have length at least \((1-o(1))(D+1)\) before first exit, by uniform metric comparison on the closure. Exhaustion and a diagonal choice give markings on domains increasing to \(X\), with convergence on each fixed compact. The argument uses the limit at fixed radii first.

## The complete Ricci-flat limit is Euclidean

The source Yamabe bound, which is invariant under constant rescaling, applies to smooth compact tests transported by \(F_i\) and extended by zero. On a fixed support the scaled scalar term tends to zero by the scaled local Ricci \(L^p\) convergence and uniformly bounded volume in the marking. Therefore

\[
 6\int_X|df|_h^2\,dV_h\ge y_0\Bigl(\int_X|f|^4\,dV_h\Bigr)^{1/2},
 \qquad f\in C_c^\infty(X).
\]

This gives noncompactness and Euclidean lower volume growth at every center and radius. A slope \(2/s\) cutoff gives \(y_0 V(s/2)^{1/2}\le 24s^{-2}V(s)\). Iterating the inequality for \(V(s)/s^4\) and taking the small-ball limit gives a positive lower bound depending only on \(y_0\). Strong \(W^{2,p}\) and \(W^{1,2p}\) on fixed embedded compacts give strong curvature convergence in \(L^p\) and hence in \(L^2\) there. Scale invariance of the global source full-curvature integral and exhaustion give \(\int_X|\operatorname{Rm}_h|_{\mathrm{tens}}^2\,dV_h\le\Lambda\). ## Harmonic-radius stability and scope

Use the based marking on the closed Euclidean ball of radius five. The pullbacks \(H_i\) converge to the Euclidean matrix strongly in \(W^{2,p}\) and uniformly there. On the Euclidean radius-four ball solve \(\Delta_{H_i}(x+v_i)=0\), with \(v_i=0\) on its boundary. Write the operator \(A_i^{ab}\partial_{ab}+B_i^a\partial_a\). Then \(A_i-I\to0\) uniformly and \(B_i\to0\) in \(L^{2p}\cap W^{1,p}\), using the first and second derivatives of \(H_i\). The Dirichlet inverse for the Euclidean Laplacian in \(W^{2,2p}\) and the inclusion \(W^{2,2p}\subset C^1\) let one absorb both perturbations, giving \(v_i\to0\) in \(W^{2,2p}\). The solution is smooth, and differentiating the equation with an interior estimate gives \(v_i\to0\) in \(W^{3,p}\) on the radius-three ball. The terms involving two first and second derivative factors are controlled in \(L^p\) by their \(L^{2p}\) norms. The resulting coordinate maps and inverses tend to the identity in \(C^1\) and \(W^{3,p}\). The recentered coordinate matrices tend to the Euclidean matrix uniformly and in \(W^{2,p}\cap W^{1,2p}\). The actual source ball of radius two lies inside the image of the radius-three ball for large \(i\), by the first-exit inclusion. These harmonic coordinates therefore meet both strict admissibility margins at radius two, contradicting \(r_H(k_i,z_i)=1\). Merely weak convergence of second derivatives would fail to give this margin.

The checked result is a non-effective interior radius floor under a supplied scaled \(L^p\) Ricci bound with \(p>2\) on the physical buffer. It also gives a uniform local \(L^p\) full-curvature bound on smaller harmonic chart balls in the scaled gauge, by the chart curvature formula. Consequently a ball of scaled radius \(s\) sufficiently below the floor has full curvature energy at most \(C' s^{4-8/p}\) by Hölder and the harmonic chart volume bound. Minimizing energy, Bach estimates, and a chiral energy ceiling are independent of this argument once the two global bounds are supplied. At \(p=2\) the Ricci scaling factor loses its decay and the compact embedding into \(W^{1,2p}\) reaches its endpoint. The master program still needs a scaled Ricci bound on its selected windows, and a whole useful annular marking must also establish the arrangement and separation of the two actual source sets. A local radius floor alone leaves those steps open.
