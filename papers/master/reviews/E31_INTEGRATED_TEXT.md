## The whole critical annulus

**Critical estimate.** Work in the given Cartesian coordinates and use Euclidean component norms. For \(m=\delta+v\), set \(\eta=\|v\|_\infty\), \(G=\|\chi Dv\|_4\), \(X=\|\chi^2D^2v\|_2\). After Young's inequality this gives \(G^2\le C\eta X+C\eta^2\|D\chi\|_4^2\), including the case \(G=0\). For \(\Delta_m=\operatorname{div}_m\nabla\) the coordinate tension is \(T^a=-m^{bc}\Gamma^a_{bc}\), so the Ricci equation has the two displayed negative \(m\,DT\) terms when solved for \(m^{ab}\partial_{ab}v\). The formula is a principal-part identity, with every other first-jet term quadratic. Applying the flat \(L^2\) Hessian identity to \(\chi^2v\) adds cutoff terms bounded by \(C\|D\chi\|_4G+C\eta\|D^2(\chi^2)\|_2\). The principal error and quadratic terms cost \(C\eta X+CG^2\), and the mixed cutoff term costs an arbitrarily small multiple of \(X\) plus \(C\eta\|D\chi\|_4^2\). This gives the stated absorption at small fixed \(\eta\). In particular, vanishing full curvature and \(DT_i\to0\) yield \(G\le C\varepsilon_*+o_i(1)\) on the fixed trims. ## The induced immersion metric

For the induced metric, take the Gauss normal compatible with the orientations. The oriented identification \(TM\simeq n^*TS^4\) gives \(\deg n=1\), and the signed Jacobian is \(\det A\), also with the opposite sign convention for \(A\) in dimension four. This hypersurface specialization is derived directly from the area formula. ## The integrated supercritical corrections

For finite \(p>2\), the transitions solve a harmonic scalar equation with uniformly elliptic Hölder coefficients. Locally their derivatives are bounded in \(W^{1,2p}\) by the \(W^{2,2p}\) scalar estimate. Differentiation gives a product of two \(L^{2p}\) terms in \(L^p\), and the compact embedding \(W^{3,p}\Subset W^{2,2p}\) holds because \(2p<4p/(4-p)\) for \(2<p<4\), with the usual endpoint and higher-\(p\) finite-exponent alternatives. After regularity the transitions to the smooth limit converge strongly in \(W^{3,p}\) by the differentiated difference equation. Their use in smooth compact markings is legitimate after this regularity step. Pullback differentiations use bounded first derivatives, \(L^{2p}\) second derivatives and \(L^p\) third derivatives of the maps. This retains the required bounds and first-order strong convergence, and retains strong \(W^{2,p}\) when the local metric convergence has that order.
