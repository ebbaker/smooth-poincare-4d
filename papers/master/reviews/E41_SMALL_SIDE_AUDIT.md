## Source, chart and an ambient path barrier

Let \(g_i\) be the volume-one Yamabe minimizing representatives on the same closed oriented homotopy four-sphere in the master's positive-class subcritical minimizing sequence. Write \(Y_i=R_{g_i}\ge y_0>0\), \(e_i\to I^+(M)>0\), and suppose \(e_i\le8\pi^2-\delta\). Use the uniform physical noncollapse \(\operatorname{Vol}_{g_i}B(z,r)\ge\nu r^4\) for all \(z\) and \(0<r\le1\). At the attaining inner center \(x_i\), the complete inner ball \(D_{i,-}=B_{g_i}(x_i,a_i)\) carries \(\theta e_i\) for fixed \(0<\theta<1/2\). The entire median exterior \(D_{i,+}=M\setminus B_{g_i}(x_i,b_i)\) carries at least \(e_i/2\). Distance spheres have zero chiral mass. [Physical Yamabe bound and the two quantiles](../manuscript/complete_limits.tex)

Let \(F_i:A(1/8,L)\hookrightarrow M\) be oriented smooth embeddings, \(L>16\) fixed, with \(m_i=\rho_i^{-2}F_i^*g_i\) satisfying \(\Lambda^{-1}\delta_{\mathrm{Euc}}\le m_i\le\Lambda\delta_{\mathrm{Euc}}\) on the whole chart with fixed \(\Lambda\). Write \(S_r=F_i(\{|z|=r\})\) and \(K_i=F_i(\{1/4\le |z|\le4\})\). The notation suppresses \(i\) on the spheres. Each \(S_r\) has two connected sides. The embedded product gives a two-sided connected collar. A path between its two faces in the complement would yield a loop meeting the sphere once modulo two, which contradicts \(H_1(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0\). Every component of the complement abuts the sphere and thus meets one of the two collar components. We call the two full sides coordinate-inner and coordinate-outer according to this collar.

For \(1/8<u<v<L\) the image of \(A(u,v)\) is a connected component of the complement of \(S_u\cup S_v\). It is open by the local inverse theorem and relatively closed there because the compact closed band has precisely these two boundary spheres. Any crossing of the compact band has physical length at least \(\rho_i(v-u)/\sqrt\Lambda\). In particular a path from outside the band reaching \(S_u\) for the first time from its coordinate-outer side must enter the band at \(S_v\) and pay that lower bound, even if its route before entering was external to the chart. One takes the last visit to \(S_v\) before the first visit to \(S_u\). Separately, a path from an arbitrary point in a compact protected subannulus to the complement of the chart must first exit a larger compact band. Starting at distance \(d>0\) in radius from either boundary of that band costs at least \(d\rho_i/\sqrt\Lambda\). Both estimates use ambient paths, with ellipticity applied only on the segment before exit or during crossing.

## The full smaller-volume side

Use a function zero on one full side of \(S_1\) and increasing linearly from zero on \(S_1\) to one on \(S_{1/2}\) or \(S_2\) in the other side, as appropriate. Extend by one deeper on that side. The resulting globally Lipschitz function \(f\) has gradient supported in the corresponding compact radial strip. Ellipticity on \(A(1/2,2)\) gives \(\int|df|^2_{g_i}\,dV_{g_i}\le C_1(\Lambda)\rho_i^2\), independent of \(L\). If its supporting side \(U\) is the smaller-volume side, Hölder and the exact Yamabe inequality on \(g_i\) give

\[
 (1-2^{-1/2})y_0\|f\|_{4,g_i}^2
 \le 6\int |df|_{g_i}^2\,dV_{g_i}.
\]

Here \(\|f\|_2^2\le\operatorname{Vol}(U)^{1/2}\|f\|_4^2\), and smooth approximation justifies the Lipschitz test. The function is one on \(U\) off the transition strip. The strip has physical volume at most \(\Lambda^2 |A(1/2,2)|\rho_i^4\). Thus \(\operatorname{Vol}_{g_i}U\le C_*(y_0,\Lambda)\rho_i^4\) for a constant independent of \(L\). In particular the conclusion about off-trim volume also controls the full side.

The coordinate-outer side contains \(F_i(A(2,L/2))\), whose physical volume is at least \(\Lambda^{-2}\omega_4((L/2)^4-2^4)\rho_i^4\), with \(\omega_4\) the Euclidean unit four-ball volume. Require this coefficient to exceed \(C_*\). The small side is then the coordinate-inner side. For every \(p\in U\), the ball of radius \(\min(1,d_{g_i}(p,S_1))\) about \(p\) is in \(U\), since its minimizing segments from \(p\) miss \(S_1\). Noncollapse and \(\rho_i\to0\) imply

\[
 d_{g_i}(p,S_1)\le (C_*/\nu)^{1/4}\rho_i .
\]

In particular the **entire** small side is close to \(S_1\). The width threshold is a numerical condition on the chart actually supplied. A uniform ellipticity constant specified independently of the desired width lets one choose \(L\) first. Ellipticity on an isolated chart of arbitrary width, or constants that worsen without control as wider charts are sought, need not meet the threshold. ## Marks from the placed chart

First suppose the wide image is disjoint from both charged sets, and the central trim is in the closed physical \(t_i\)-ball about \(x_i\), where \(t_i=o(b_i)\). Then \(\rho_i=O(t_i)\) automatically. Take antipodal coordinate points on \(S_1\). A path between them either stays in the image of the Euclidean coordinate ball of radius \(1/4\) about the first point until it exits, or could never reach the second point. The first exit costs at least \(\rho_i/(4\sqrt\Lambda)\), whereas the two points are at ambient distance at most \(2t_i\). Hence \(\rho_i=o(b_i)\). The full coordinate-inner side is in the physical ball of radius \(t_i+(C_*/\nu)^{1/4}\rho_i=o(b_i)\). This puts the entire median exterior on the coordinate-outer side. Both marked sets already miss the trim.

An additional centre test puts \(x_i\) in the coordinate-inner side. If \(x_i\) were coordinate-outer, the ambient crossing estimate, together with \(x_i\) outside the chart, would give

\[
 d(x_i,S_{1/2})\ge d(x_i,S_2)+\frac{3\rho_i}{2\sqrt\Lambda}.
\]

Thus the test \(d(x_i,S_{1/2})\le d(x_i,S_2)\) suffices. Alternatively, if \(d(x_i,S_1)\le A\rho_i\), require \((L/2-1)/\sqrt\Lambda>A\). An outer \(x_i\) would then have to cross \([1,L/2]\) to reach \(S_1\), violating the distance bound. The entire inner ball follows the center, since the ball is connected and disjoint from \(S_1\). Either test has the required sign even in the presence of shorter ambient paths outside the chart.

## Marks without assumed placement

Under the revised hypotheses assume \(a_i/\rho_i\to0\), \(\rho_i/b_i\to0\), \(\int_{F_i(A(1/8,L))}|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_i}|^2dV_{g_i}\to0\), and \(d(x_i,S_1)\le A\rho_i\). Take \(L\) to meet both the volume and distance conditions above. The first-exit argument gives \(B(p,c\rho_i)\subset F_i(A(1/8,L))\) uniformly for \(p\in F_i(\{1/4\le|z|\le L/2\})\). For instance the compact comparison band \([3/16,3L/4]\) protects either radial end by a fixed positive distance. Were \(x_i\) inside the indicated buffered band, the whole inner ball would be contained in the low-curvature image, contrary to its positive chiral mass. If any \(p\in K_i\) belonged to the inner ball, the triangle inequality would give \(B(x_i,a_i)\subset B(p,2a_i)\), again inside the low-curvature image. These arguments use the whole charge rather than an assumption about the energy density at the center or at the point of intersection. Since \(x_i\) is outside the buffered band, an outer \(x_i\) would have to cross \([1,L/2]\) before reaching \(S_1\), as before. Thus \(x_i\) and the whole inner ball lie on the coordinate-inner side, and the inner ball misses \(K_i\).

Choose a nearest point of \(S_1\) to \(x_i\). A path along \(S_1\) in the chart followed by a radial path places every point of \(K_i\) within physical distance \((A+\sqrt\Lambda(\pi+3))\rho_i\) of \(x_i\). The full coordinate-inner side is also within \((A+\pi\sqrt\Lambda+(C_*/\nu)^{1/4})\rho_i\) of \(x_i\). Here the angular term accounts for the possible difference between the nearest point of \(S_1\) to \(x_i\) and the nearest point to a given point of the small side. Both bounds are \(o(b_i)\). The complete median exterior therefore lies on the coordinate-outer side and misses the trim. The wide image need not be assumed to lie between the marks.

## The conditional cap contradiction

For either set of placement hypotheses the marked conclusion supplies the two complete raw sides required by the frozen critical annulus proposition. On \(A(1/8,8)\) require the near-Euclidean bound \(\|m_i-\delta_{\mathrm{Euc}}\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_*\), the vanishing full-curvature energy, and harmonic coordinates or \(\|D(\Delta_{m_i}z)\|_2\to0\) in those same coordinates. The wide part enters the marking argument through ellipticity and, in the version without placement, through its vanishing integrated full curvature. The harmonic and small-distortion assumptions are only needed in the fixed central annulus. The developed cut in the proposition is inside \(K_i\) and separates its faces, so it preserves the full side assignments outside \(K_i\), even when its sphere differs from \(S_1\). The frozen splice and cap argument yields positive caps with combined surcharge \(o(1)\). One capped side is standard by the band, and cutting and reassembly give a competitor of the original smooth type from the retained side. The discarded raw side has at least \(\min(\theta,1/2)e_i\) chiral energy, contradicting the upper bound \(e_i-I^+(M)+o(1)\). The extra centre condition excludes an off-centre shell surrounding an unrelated small region. As a geometric illustration, a Euclidean annulus of chart scale \(\rho\) about a point at distance \((L+2)\rho\) from \(x\) can lie wholly at distances \(O(L\rho)=o(b)\) while leaving \(x\) outside. It violates both sufficient centre tests when \(L\) meets the above width requirements. This illustrates the need for positional information and is not an example of a subcritical minimizing sequence. Once the centre and width hypotheses hold, an external shortcut cannot bypass the compact crossing band, and noncollapse prevents a far point on the small side from being hidden in small volume. Extraction of a sufficiently wide, uniformly elliptic chart with its central analytic control and centre test remains a premise.
