# Why the first edition starts with a single cut

25 November 2026. SP4-MASTER-B01. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI Codex assistance. The [overview](../../research-overview/OVERVIEW.md) identifies the manuscript versions and original research.

Recognition on a positive conformal class occurs at chiral energy $4\pi^2$. Below twice this energy, closing both sides of an inexpensive cut forces at least one capped side to be standard. On a hypothetical nonstandard source, the other side retains the original smooth type. Its energy therefore bounds the fixed-type infimum from above. That argument needs one marked boundary and a scalar-sign estimate. Global energy accounting and a priori Bach estimates are unnecessary for this implication.

We chose the first-order splice because it identifies the analytic datum one would need on an actual weak cut. We included the physical-volume theorem because it constructs marked regions on the general sequence admitted by the argument. The additional one-sided inequality uses the parametrization of each ball already obtained in that theorem. If the raw chiral mass survives in the complement of almost all physical volume, the repair inequality gives a precise new target. No curvature equiintegrability or cap estimate is implicit in the volume theorem.

A full marked base, smooth ALE descent, a closed rigidity theorem, or genuine curvature control from transmitted spinors can change which proof is shortest. The [plan](../../research-plan/PLAN.md) identifies their re-entry conditions. The common introduction preserves the historical motivation for these methods. This edition develops only the arguments that enter the displayed reductions.
