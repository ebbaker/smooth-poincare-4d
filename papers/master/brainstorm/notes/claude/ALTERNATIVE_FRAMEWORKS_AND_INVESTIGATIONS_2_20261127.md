## 1. Summary

The entry framework asks for two things on an arbitrary homotopy sphere, a positive class below $8\pi^2$ and a recognition theorem for such classes, and both are open. A framework suits the new rules better when its recognition step is already a published theorem, when its basic object exists on every homotopy sphere without hypotheses, and when what remains is one statement. I found one framework with all three properties that is also the closest Riemannian form of the original idea of generalized isothermal coordinates. I recommend it as the subject of investigations-2.

The object is the kernel of a conformally invariant operator. On a conformal four-manifold $(\Sigma,c)$ with $b_2=0$, the map $d^-A\mapsto d^+A$ is an isometry $T_c$ from $L^2$ anti-self-dual forms to $L^2$ self-dual forms. It is the four-dimensional Beurling transform, the operator whose two-dimensional counterpart solves the Beltrami equation and produces isothermal coordinates. Its kernel at a point $p$ is a three-dimensional space of closed self-dual two-forms on $\Sigma\setminus p$ with a pole of order four at $p$. In the flat picture they are the three constant self-dual forms, the analogue of $dz$ on the Riemann sphere. I call them the pole forms of $(\Sigma,c,p)$. They exist for every class on every homotopy four-sphere (section 3.2, derived here). If one of them has no zeros, then it is a symplectic form on $\Sigma\setminus p$ that is standard at infinity, and a theorem of Gromov and McDuff gives $\Sigma\cong S^4$ (section 3.3). The converse holds trivially, so the conjecture is equivalent to the existence, on each homotopy sphere, of one class and one point with a zero-free pole form. No energy threshold and no Yamabe sign enters. On an explicit family of classes on $S^4$ the three pole forms are zero-free at chiral energies more than a thousand times $4\pi^2$ (section 3.5, checked case).

This is a reformulation and not a reduction in difficulty. What it changes is where the difficulty sits. Existence is linear analysis. Recognition is imported. The whole problem becomes the zero set of a closed self-dual form, which is a union of circles for generic data and is the subject of near-symplectic geometry, where Taubes's program supplies pseudoholomorphic curves and Perutz, Honda, Auroux, Donaldson and Katzarkov supply normal forms and a dictionary with broken Lefschetz fibrations. Section 3.7 lists eight tasks and says which of them Lean can referee.

A second framework is extrinsic (section 4). Every homotopy four-sphere embeds in $\mathbb R^5$, and a published theorem of Chodosh, Mantoulidis and Schulze recognizes the standard sphere from an embedding whose Colding–Minicozzi entropy is at most $4/e$. It is the natural successor of investigation 04. Its open step is as hard as entry, and nothing in it can be formalized soon, so I rank it second.

Three smaller streams are worth keeping whatever framework leads (sections 5 and 6): finite certificates that Lean can check outright, beginning with the prism result that was added to the master today and the family it belongs to; a search for Andrews–Curtis trivializations of the presentation sphere named in investigation 07; and, on the side of a possible counterexample, the Khovanov skein lasagna invariants, a family of field-theoretic invariants that the theorems quoted in section 6 do not exclude.

Section 7 proposes the folders and the manuscript. I have not created them. The request said "perhaps", and the choice of the leading framework is yours. (They were created later the same day at the author's request; see the addendum in section 7.)

## 2. What the working rules change

The earlier note recommended specialist readers for the recognition gap and the attainment proof and proposed units of publication. Under the present rules those recommendations lapse. What takes their place is a classification of every claim by how it will be certified.

A claim is an *import* when it is a published theorem. In Lean it should appear once, as a named proposition in a ledger file with its citation, and every theorem that uses it should take it as an explicit hypothesis. A claim is a *project theorem* when the repository proves it. It is not established until Lean proves it from Mathlib, the pinned differential-geometry library and the ledger. A claim is a *certificate* when it is a finite computation, such as a group order, a sequence of moves or an interval bound, and Lean can check the certificate itself. Everything else is a heuristic and should be labelled so.

By this classification the entry program is expensive. Its recognition statement beyond the gap is an open project theorem. Its attainment theorem is a long analytic working proof that would have to be formalized on top of Bour's flow. Its entry statement has no proof strategy on record: the constructions tried on 26 and 27 November ended in lower bounds or obstructions for the specific pieces. I do not suggest discarding it. The recognition gap, the immersed inequality, the first-order operators of investigation 03 and the slide enumerations of 05 remain assets, and the first-band target of the earlier note (section 4 there) is still the best question inside that framework. I suggest that it stop being the only frame, and that new effort go where the open part is smaller.

## 3. Framework A: the Beurling transform of a conformal four-sphere

### 3.1 What the initial investigations left untried

The [20 November summary](../../../../research-overview/PROJECT_SUMMARY_2026-11-20.md) states the original aim: represent the null geometry of a curved four-manifold in flat coordinates, with the curvature carried by transport data, taking isothermal coordinates and twistor theory as the models. The charts were to live on the correspondence space, the bundle of chiral directions, and to map it to the flat one. The [obstruction note](../../../../initial-investigations/01_generalized_isothermal_coordinates/notes/RESEARCH_OBSTRUCTIONS_2026-11-20.md) then proved that a single holomorphic chart over complete direction fibres which preserves the twistor distribution exists only when $W=0$, and found that weaker transfers, in which a bundle or a connection descends while the geometric inverse does not, do occur. All its examples are holomorphic wave metrics, whose real forms are neutral. Its overview ends by asking whether a weaker transfer that retains a compatibility defect has any role in the sphere problem. Investigations-1 took a different lesson from the same obstruction. It kept the obstruction tensor $W^+$, turned it into an energy and dropped the chart.

Three things were therefore never tried: a chart in Riemannian signature, a chart that is global, and a chart that is allowed to be linear on the direction fibres without being conformal there. The object below has all three features, and its compatibility defect is exactly the failure of conformality on the fibres.

### 3.2 The transform and its pole forms

In two dimensions a conformal structure on the plane is a Beltrami coefficient $\mu$, and the isothermal coordinate is the solution of $f_{\bar z}=\mu f_z$ normalized by $f(z)=z+O(1/z)$. It is found by inverting $I-\mu T$, where $T$ is the Beurling transform $f_{\bar z}\mapsto f_z$, an $L^2$ isometry with kernel $-1/\pi(z-w)^2$. The form $df$ is a closed $(1,0)$-form with a double pole at infinity. That it has no zeros, so that $f$ is a coordinate, follows from the degree count $2g-2=-2$.

In four dimensions the Hodge star on two-forms is conformally invariant, and so is the $L^2$ norm of a two-form. Let $(\Sigma,c)$ be closed and oriented with $b_2=0$. For a one-form $A$, Stokes's theorem gives $\|d^+A\|=\|d^-A\|$, and every $L^2$ anti-self-dual form is $d^-A$ for a coclosed $A$ that is unique up to harmonic one-forms. Hence
$$T_c:\ L^2(\Lambda^-)\to L^2(\Lambda^+),\qquad d^-A\mapsto d^+A,$$
is a well-defined isometry depending only on $c$. For an abelian connection it reconstructs the self-dual half of the curvature from the anti-self-dual half. It is the operator that Donaldson and Sullivan used to do gauge theory on quasiconformal four-manifolds and that Iwaniec and Martin used for quasiregular maps, and a conformal structure relative to a reference one is a bundle map $\mu:\Lambda^+\to\Lambda^-$ of norm less than one, the four-dimensional Beltrami coefficient. The equation $d^-_cA=0$ reads $d^-_0A=\mu\,d^+_0A$.

**Proposition A1 (derived here).** Let $\Sigma$ be a smooth homotopy four-sphere, $c$ a smooth conformal class and $p\in\Sigma$. The space $\mathcal H_p(c)$ of closed $c$-self-dual two-forms $\omega$ on $\Sigma\setminus p$ with $|\omega|_g=O(d(p,\cdot)^{-4})$ has dimension three. In normal coordinates $y$ at $p$ for a metric $g\in c$, each element is
$$\omega=|y|^{-4}\,R_y^*\eta+O(|y|^{-2}),$$
where $R_y$ is the reflection in the hyperplane orthogonal to $y$ and $\eta$ is a constant anti-self-dual form, and $\eta\mapsto\omega$ is a linear isomorphism from $\Lambda^-_p$ onto $\mathcal H_p(c)$. The forms are the columns of the Schwartz kernel of $T_c$ at $p$.

*Sketch.* The metric $g'=|y|^{-4}g$ near $p$ is asymptotically Euclidean of order two in the inverted coordinates $x=y/|y|^2$, and $\Sigma\setminus p$ is contractible. A constant form $\omega^0$ that is self-dual for the orientation of $\Sigma$ has a linear primitive $A^0$, and $d^-_{g'}A^0=O(|x|^{-2})$. One solves $(d^*\oplus d^-)a=(0,-d^-A^0)$ in a weighted Sobolev space of weight $\delta\in(-1,0)$. On flat space the operator $d^*\oplus d^-$ composed with its adjoint is the Laplacian, its homogeneous solutions are harmonic one-forms, and the only exceptional weights in $[-3,0]$ are $-3$ and $0$, because $c_i\,dx^i/|x|^2$ is not coclosed. For weights in $(-3,0)$ the kernel vanishes: a decaying solution is $O(|x|^{-3})$, its differential is an $L^2$ closed self-dual form, the boundary term in $\int da\wedge da$ vanishes, so $da=0$, and then $a=df$ with $f$ harmonic and decaying. The cokernel vanishes because a decaying harmonic function is zero and a decaying closed anti-self-dual form is $O(|x|^{-4})$, hence $L^2$, hence extends over $p$ as a harmonic form on $\Sigma$, and $b_2=0$. So $\omega=d(A^0+a)=\omega^0+O(|x|^{-2})$ exists and is unique. Pulling back by the inversion gives the stated expansion; that pull-back is `check_06` (a). No positivity of the Yamabe constant is used, since the factor $|y|^{-4}$ replaces the Green's function. A full proof with the Lockhart–McOwen or Bartnik theory has not been written.

### 3.3 The criterion

**Proposition A2 (derived here from a published theorem).** If some nonzero $\omega\in\mathcal H_p(c)$ has no zero on $\Sigma\setminus p$, then $\Sigma$ is diffeomorphic to $S^4$.

*Sketch.* Since $\omega\wedge\omega=|\omega|^2dV$, a zero-free $\omega$ is symplectic on $X=\Sigma\setminus p$ for the orientation of $\Sigma$. Write $\omega=\omega^0+da$ near infinity with $a=O(|x|^{-1})$ and $da=O(|x|^{-2})$. For a cut-off $\chi_R$ that vanishes on $B_R$ and equals one outside $B_{2R}$, the form $\omega'=\omega^0+d((1-\chi_R)a)$ agrees with $\omega$ on $B_R$, equals $\omega^0$ outside $B_{2R}$, and differs from $\omega^0$ by $O(R^{-2})$ in between, so it is symplectic for large $R$. After a linear change of the coordinates $x$, the form $\omega^0$ is the standard one. Now $(X,\omega')$ is a connected symplectic four-manifold which, outside a compact set, the chart identifies with the complement of a closed ellipsoid in the standard $\mathbb R^4$, and $H_2(X)=0$ excludes symplectic spheres of square $-1$. The theorem of Gromov and McDuff, in the form of McDuff and Salamon's Theorem 9.4.2, gives a symplectomorphism of $X$ onto $\mathbb R^4$ that agrees with the given chart outside a neighbourhood of the compact set. A diffeomorphism that is the inverted chart near $p$ extends smoothly over $p$ to a diffeomorphism $\Sigma\to S^4$.

The clause about agreement at infinity matters. Without it one would know only that $\Sigma\setminus p$ is diffeomorphic to $\mathbb R^4$, and the passage from there to $\Sigma\cong S^4$ is the four-dimensional Schoenflies problem. I read the theorem as quoted in two later papers and did not open the book (source record).

### 3.4 Equivalence, and what is gained

On the round sphere the pole forms are the constant forms and have no zeros, so the conjecture is equivalent to the statement that every homotopy four-sphere carries a class and a point with a zero-free pole form. The class of objects is also familiar. Every closed two-form on $\Sigma\setminus p$ that is constant near infinity and is symplectic or near-symplectic is self-dual for some metric that is flat near infinity, and by the uniqueness in A1 it is then a pole form of that class. The pole forms of all classes are therefore exactly the near-symplectic forms on the punctured sphere that are standard at infinity, and the conjecture says that one of them has no zero circle. In that bare form the equivalence is folklore.

What the conformal formulation adds is the linear structure. One class gives all three forms at once, with a pointwise evaluation map $\mathrm{ev}_q:\Lambda^-_p\to\Lambda^+_q$ for each $q\ne p$. The framework has the three properties asked for in section 1. It also inherits two features of the two-dimensional theory. The condition should be open in the class, which is an estimate to be proved in task A-1. And the defect of the chart is a tensor: where $\mathrm{ev}_q$ is invertible, the three forms are a definite triple in Donaldson's sense, their Gram matrix $Q_{ij}\,dV=\omega_i\wedge\omega_j$ is positive, and the trace-free part of $Q$ is the Beltrami coefficient of the chart. If it vanishes identically the triple is hyperkähler after a conformal change, so $W^+=0$, the signature formula on $\Sigma$ gives $W^-=0$, and Kuiper's theorem gives the round sphere. The harmonic equation $\nabla^*\nabla\omega=2W^+\omega-\tfrac R3\omega$ shows that $W^+$ is the source of the defect, which is the Riemannian form of the obstruction proved in the initial investigation.

The map $(q,u)\mapsto\omega_u(q)/|\omega_u(q)|$ from $(\Sigma\setminus p)\times S^2$ to the twistor space of $\Sigma\setminus p$ is the chart on the correspondence space that the drafts M2 and M3 asked for. It covers the identity, it is the projectivization of a linear map on each fibre, and it is defined wherever $\omega_u(q)\ne0$. It is conformal on the fibres only when $W^+=0$, that is, only for the round class.

### 3.5 Two calibrations

*Definite at any energy (checked case).* On $\mathbb R^4$ with the left-invariant coframe $\sigma_i$ of $S^3$, $d\sigma_1=2\sigma_2\wedge\sigma_3$, take $g=dr^2+a^2\sigma_1^2+b^2\sigma_2^2+c^2\sigma_3^2$. The form $d(f\sigma_1)$ is closed and self-dual exactly when $f'=2af/(bc)$, so each of the three forms $d(f_i\sigma_i)$, $f_i>0$, is nowhere zero, and when the metric is asymptotically Euclidean they are the pole forms at the point at infinity. For
$$a=r\Bigl(1+\frac{\varepsilon r^2}{(1+r^2)^2}\Bigr),\qquad b=c=r,\qquad \varepsilon>-4,$$
the class extends smoothly over infinity to a class $c_\varepsilon$ on $S^4$, and $f_1=r^2e^{-\varepsilon/(1+r^2)}$. Its chiral energy, computed from the curvature with $E_+=E_-$ confirmed to a relative error below $10^{-9}$, is $0.79\,T$ at $\varepsilon=1$, $4.2\,T$ at $\varepsilon=2$ and $1172\,T$ at $\varepsilon=12$, where $T=4\pi^2$. The pole triple is definite for all of them (`check_06`, with the curvature routine validated on the flat, round and Fubini–Study metrics). The criterion of A2 is not an energy condition.

*Zeros do occur on the standard sphere (published).* Perutz proves that a near-symplectic form, and in particular a symplectic one, can be modified inside a ball so that its zero set gains a circle. Applied to the standard form on $\mathbb R^4$, this gives forms that are standard at infinity and vanish on a circle. By section 3.4 they are pole forms of some classes on $S^4$. So zero-freeness is a property of the class, and a proof must move within the space of classes.

### 3.6 The failure set

For a generic class one expects each pole form to vanish transversally along circles, as Honda and LeBrun proved for harmonic self-dual forms on closed manifolds; for pole forms this is part of task A-5. As the direction $u$ runs over the projective plane of $\mathcal H_p$, the zero circles sweep the hypersurface $H=\{q:\det\mathrm{ev}_q=0\}$, which is compact, and the kernel line defines a map $\kappa:H\to\mathbb{RP}^2$ whose fibres are the zero circles. Proposition A2 says that $\Sigma$ is standard as soon as $\kappa$ misses a point. On an exotic sphere $\kappa$ would be onto for every class and every point, a statement of Borsuk–Ulam type that no homotopy argument on bundle maps can prove, because it depends on closedness.

Three published facts organize the zero circles. Collapsing the Hopf circles at infinity turns $\Sigma\setminus p$ into the closed manifold $\Sigma\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ with a near-symplectic form. Perutz shows that the number of circles can be changed to any positive number by modifications inside balls, and that the number of circles whose normal splitting is trivial has the parity of $1+b_1+b_2^+$, which is even here. So on a homotopy sphere a form with zeros can always be brought to one with a single circle, and a single circle is of the twisted kind: the conjecture is a last-circle problem. Taubes proved that a nonzero Seiberg–Witten invariant forces a pseudoholomorphic curve in the complement of the zero set that bounds it homologically, and Gerig turned this into an equality of invariants modulo two when all circles are untwisted. I expect the invariants of $\Sigma\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ in the chamber of a large multiple of $\omega$ to equal those of $\mathbb{CP}^2$, by the connected-sum principle and the wall-crossing formula, so that lines exist and can only break on zero circles (heuristic; I did not check either statement against a source). The lines are the coordinate curves of the generalized isothermal chart. Gromov's proof of his theorem is the case in which none of them breaks.

Finally, Auroux, Donaldson and Katzarkov, Gay and Kirby, Lekili, and Baykur and Saeki translate near-symplectic forms into broken Lefschetz fibrations and these into trisections, where a zero circle is a round singular circle and the moves that create and cancel circles are explicit. Hayano classified the simplified fibrations of genus one and determined their total spaces. This is the combinatorial shadow of the last-circle problem, and it meets the group trisections of investigation 07.

### 3.7 Tasks, and what Lean can referee

A-1. Write Proposition A1 in full, with the kernel, its reciprocity under exchange of the two points and reversal of orientation, and its conformal weights. The analysis is standard. Its Lean form is a project theorem on top of whatever elliptic theory the pinned library offers, and otherwise a named import of the weighted Fredholm theorem.

A-2. Write Proposition A2 in full. In Lean it is a frame theorem: Gromov–McDuff as a ledger import, A1, and the hypothesis of a zero-free form give a diffeomorphism to $S^4$. The cut-off estimate and the extension over $p$ are the project's part.

A-3. Formalize the pointwise algebra, which needs nothing beyond linear algebra on $\Lambda^2\mathbb R^4$: conformal invariance of the star, the inversion formula, the equivalence of a closed orthonormal self-dual triple with a hyperkähler structure, and the inequality $\tfrac13\operatorname{tr}Q\ge(\det Q)^{1/3}$ with its equality case. Formalize the family of section 3.5 as the first checked case.

A-4. Compute. On $\mathbb R^4$ the flat transform $T_0$ is a Fourier multiplier of degree zero, and the pole forms of a class with compactly supported $\mu$ are $(I-\mu T_0)^{-1}$ applied to the constants, a fixed-point iteration with fast Fourier transforms. Along one-parameter families $t\mu_0$ this finds the first $t$ at which a zero circle is born, together with the energy there. The first question is whether, on $S^4$, a positive class with $e<8\pi^2$ always has a zero-free pole form at some point. A counterexample would be cheap to look for and would show that the framework cannot serve the recognition statement of the first master. If none appears, that statement acquires a new formulation: an entered class has a zero-free pole form. At an attained Bach-flat minimizer the regularity needed to study it is available.

A-5. Local theory of the zero set for the triple: transversality for pole forms, the structure of $H$ and $\kappa$, the normal form at a twisted circle, and the behaviour of the triple when a neck is stretched.

A-6. Read Taubes's and Gerig's theorems for $b_2^+=1$ and settle the two statements marked heuristic in section 3.6, including the gap between Gerig's untwisted hypothesis and the single twisted circle.

A-7. Translate a pole form with one twisted circle into a simplified broken Lefschetz fibration of $\Sigma\#\mathbb{CP}^2$ and ask for the least genus. Diagrams of fixed genus are finite data, and a standardness proof for a given diagram is a certificate.

A-8. Record the twistor reading of section 3.4 against the drafts M2 and M3, so that the initial investigations and the new one share definitions.

Tasks A-1 to A-3 are certain to succeed and give the second master its first sections. A-4 is the first real test and costs days. A-5 to A-7 are the research.

## 4. Framework M: entropy of an embedding

Every homotopy four-sphere bounds a contractible five-manifold, because $\Theta_4=0$, and the double of that manifold is $S^5$ by the h-cobordism theorem, so $\Sigma$ embeds smoothly in $\mathbb R^5$ (published, classical). For a closed hypersurface let $\lambda$ be its Colding–Minicozzi entropy, which does not increase along mean curvature flow. The entropies of the shrinking spheres and cylinders are $\lambda(S^1)\approx1.5203$, $\lambda(S^2)=4/e\approx1.4715$, $\lambda(S^3)\approx1.4531$ and $\lambda(S^4)=32/(3e^2)\approx1.4436$ (`check_07`; the value of $\lambda(S^3)$ in my working notes had two digits transposed).

Chodosh, Mantoulidis and Schulze prove, for a closed connected embedded $M^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ (published; their Corollary 1.5 read through a summarizing reader): if $\lambda(M)\le\lambda(S^3)$, the flow of a small perturbation is an isotopy to a round sphere; if $\lambda(M)\le\lambda(S^2)$, the flow with the surgery of Daniels-Holgate is an isotopy to the boundary of a standard handlebody, which for a simply connected $M$ is the ball. Their Theorem 1.3 says that for $\lambda(M)\le2$ a generic flow has only multiplicity-one singularities modelled on $S^4$, $S^3\times\mathbb R$, $S^2\times\mathbb R^2$ and $S^1\times\mathbb R^3$. Huisken and Sinestrari recognize two-convex hypersurfaces in the same way. So with $I_{\rm ent}(\Sigma)$ the infimum of $\lambda$ over embeddings, $I_{\rm ent}(\Sigma)\le4/e$ implies $\Sigma\cong S^4$. This is a recognition gap whose band is settled, in a framework where the entropy plays the part that $e$ plays in the first master.

The open step is to lower the entropy of an embedding of a given $\Sigma$, and I see no easier route to it than to entry. Between $4/e$ and $2$ the flow meets $S^2\times\mathbb R^2$ and $S^1\times\mathbb R^3$ singularities, for which no surgery exists; these correspond to surgeries on two-spheres and circles, that is, to two-knots and their closings, the objects of the tube investigation. A heuristic count suggests that a thin neighbourhood of a spine of the contractible five-manifold has entropy near $\lambda(S^{4-k})$ along its cells of dimension $k$, so that the spheres which bound five-dimensional two-handlebodies, the presentation spheres of investigation 07, are the ones just above the settled band. None of this is formalizable soon. I would keep it as one subinvestigation with three tasks: an upper bound for $I_{\rm ent}$ of the presentation spheres, a comparison of entropy with the Chern–Lashof integral behind the immersed inequality of 04, and a watch on the bubble-sheet literature.

### 5.1 The prism family

The even prism closing is now [in the master](../../../manuscript/claude/even_prism_closing.tex) with its status. ### 5.2 Andrews–Curtis certificates

## 6. Topological field theory and the search for a counterexample

Every framework above tries to prove the conjecture. The methods of field theory bear mainly on the other possibility, and three theorems limit them. The Donaldson and Seiberg–Witten invariants of a homotopy sphere carry no information, since $b_2^+=0$. Reutter proved that no semisimple four-dimensional field theory distinguishes homotopy equivalent simply connected closed four-manifolds, which covers the state-sum theories of Crane–Yetter and Dijkgraaf–Witten type and every unitary theory. Manolescu, Marengon, Sarkar and Willis showed that the strategy of Freedman, Gompf, Morrison and Walker with the Rasmussen invariant cannot detect a sphere obtained by a Gluck twist (recalled, not reread).

One family is not excluded. The skein lasagna modules of Morrison, Walker and Wedrich, built from Khovanov–Rozansky homology, are not field theories of the kind his theorem covers, and Ren and Willis used them to distinguish an exotic pair of compact four-manifolds with boundary without gauge theory. Whether they can see a closed homotopy sphere is open. The repository now has named test objects: the odd prism closing, which is a Gluck twist along an explicit fibred knot, and $\Sigma_P$. A computation needs a handle diagram, and for a Gluck twist the result of Manolescu and coauthors is discouraging, so I rank this as a watch item with one bounded task: produce handle diagrams for the two named spheres, which the certificate streams need anyway.

The twistor actions and the holomorphic Chern–Simons theories of the physics literature need an anti-self-dual background. A homotopy sphere has none unless it is standard. Taubes's theorem gives one on $\Sigma\#n\overline{\mathbb{CP}}{}^2$ for large $n$, with a compact twistor space, but a recognition there concerns the stabilized manifold and no cancellation theorem returns to $\Sigma$. I set this aside. The transform $T_c$ of section 3 is what survives of that circle of ideas without self-duality.

Other candidates I considered and do not propose: the charge-one instanton moduli space of $(\Sigma,c)$, which is a five-manifold bounded by $\Sigma$ with no control of its topology; four-dimensional Ricci flow outside positive isotropic curvature, where no surgery theory exists; the definite-connection flow, which investigation 01 already pursued; and the quasiconformal question of whether Freedman's homeomorphism can be made quasiconformal, which is a genuine intermediate problem but has no tools beyond those of Donaldson and Sullivan.

## 8. Limits of this note

Propositions A1 and A2 are proof sketches written in one session. I believe them, and the pieces I could test by computation passed, but neither has been written in full or read by anyone else. The equivalence in section 3.4 uses the existence of a compatible metric that is flat near infinity for a near-symplectic form, which I took from the standard local construction without writing it. The two statements about Seiberg–Witten invariants in section 3.6 are expectations. Every published theorem was read as a statement, through search excerpts or a summarizing reader, and none in full; the source record gives the level for each. The entropy heuristics of section 4 have no proof. The four observations of section 5.1 include inferences of mine that the tube investigation did not make. I did not open the three draft papers in `references/unfinished_notes` and relied on the 20 November summary for them. I did not build the Lean project.
