## Where the discussion stands

Two types of starting data are available. Every smooth homotopy four-sphere $M$ admits a Euclidean hypersurface immersion. Its induced metric comes with four explicit spinor fields and a sign-free recognition threshold $E_+\le64\pi^2/9$. On the intrinsic route, assume a positive Yamabe class with $E_+<8\pi^2$ on the smooth type under consideration. A smooth descending path from that class stays positive. Reaching $4\pi^2$ recognizes $M$. On a hypothetical nonstandard type already in this band, even a single admissible saving greater than $d_i=e_i-I^+(M)$ at a minimizing reference of energy $e_i$ yields a contradiction. Constructing an initial positive subcritical class on an arbitrary type is still open. **Suggested opening question.** How much of the apparent descent simply undoes the act of making a round mark? In the conditional shrinking-mark example the energy changes by $O(\rho^4)$. The trace estimate in the brainstorm gives $-\dot e(0)\le C\rho$ and bulk extension cost at least $c/\rho$ for every radial tangent on fixed-width product collars. A lower rate of order $\rho$ persisting for a time of order $\rho^3$ would be on the same energy scale as the mark. Can one compute the leading response and distinguish relaxation of the patch from a change in the exterior? If the original class attains the infimum, recovering that class uses exactly the excess. If its energy is $E>I^+(M)$, the excess also includes $E-I^+(M)$.
