## Flow selection on the fixed smooth type

Let $M$ be a closed oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere, with positive-definite metrics. Assume a smooth positive-Yamabe class with $E_+<8\pi^2$ exists on $M$. Here $E_+=\int |W^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV$ for the Weyl operator on $\Lambda^+$. Let $q_i$ be smooth positive-Yamabe seeds with $E_+(q_i)\le 8\pi^2-\delta$ for a fixed $\delta>0$ and $d_i=E_+(q_i)-I^+(M)\to0$, where the infimum ranges over the smooth positive-Yamabe classes on $M$. On a hypothetical nonstandard type $I^+(M)\ge4\pi^2$ by the strict sphere theorem. The selection estimate itself only requires the upper band and the initial Yamabe sign. [Master conventions and entry](../research-overview/OVERVIEW.md#the-variational-problem-on-a-fixed-smooth-type)

Run independently from each $q_i$ the committed manuscript's modified Bach equation $\partial_tg=B_g+\frac1{12}(\Delta_gs_g)g$, with $\Delta=\operatorname{tr}_g\nabla^2$, on a smooth interval $0\le t<T_i$. Let $V_i=\operatorname{Vol}(q_i)$. The Bach tensor is trace-free, so $\partial_tdV=(\Delta s/6)dV$ and volume is conserved. In the manuscript's notation $E_i'(t)=-c_B\int_M|B_{g_i(t)}|^2\,dV_{g_i(t)}$. In the preprint's equation (1.1) convention $c_B=1/2$, since $DE_+=D\mathcal W/8$ on the fixed oriented manifold and $D\mathcal W[H]=-4\int\langle B,H\rangle\,dV$. The estimate below keeps the manuscript's $c_B$. [Committed Bach manuscript, `modified-bach` and `weyl-dissipation`](../../investigations-1/02_bach_flow/manuscript/bach_flow_and_smooth_poincare.tex#L169) · [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, arXiv:2508.06633v1, §1 and §2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06633v1#S1)

For every smooth time, apply Gauss–Bonnet in a unit-volume Yamabe minimizer of $[g_i(t)]$. Since $E_+=E_-$ and the Weyl integrals are conformally invariant, the square of the Yamabe constant is at least $48(8\pi^2-E_i(t))\ge48\delta$. The sign is preserved by continuity of the first conformal Laplacian eigenvalue along the smooth path and its sign agreement with the Yamabe constant. Thus $Y([g_i(t)])\ge\sqrt{48\delta}$ and $I^+(M)\le E_i(t)\le I^+(M)+d_i$. [Committed Bach manuscript, `yamabe-budget`](../../investigations-1/02_bach_flow/manuscript/bach_flow_and_smooth_poincare.tex#L207) · [Chang–Gursky–Yang, equation (0.4)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf#page=3)

Write $b_i(t)=\int_M|B_{g_i(t)}|\,dV_{g_i(t)}$. On any smooth interval $[a_i,a_i+\tau_i]\subset[0,T_i)$, Cauchy–Schwarz in space and integration of dissipation give an actual time $t_i$ in that interval with

$$
 b_i(t_i)^2\le \frac1{\tau_i}\int_{a_i}^{a_i+\tau_i}b_i(t)^2\,dt
 \le \frac{V_i}{c_B\tau_i}\bigl(E_i(a_i)-E_i(a_i+\tau_i)\bigr)
 \le \frac{d_i}{c_B(\tau_i/V_i)}.
$$

Both conclusions, $b_i(t_i)\to0$ and $E_i(t_i)\to I^+(M)$, therefore hold whenever $d_i/(\tau_i/V_i)\to0$. The actual interval drop can replace $d_i$ in that condition. Equivalently for the estimate using only the excess and maximal lifespan, finite lifespans can be halved and infinite ones can be sampled on a window of length $V_i$. It suffices that $d_i/(T_i/V_i)\to0$, with ratio zero for infinite lifespan. The $d_i=0$ case is already stationary in energy and has $B=0$ at its smooth seed by the first variation and openness of Yamabe positivity. Global existence from **each** near-minimizing seed suffices, whereas the trajectory from one fixed seed can have energy limit strictly above the infimum as far as the dissipation estimate is concerned.

For $\widehat g=u^2g$, the covariant Bach tensor is $\widehat B=u^{-2}B$ and its pointwise norm is $u^{-4}|B|_g$. Thus $|B|\,dV$ is unchanged as a measure, while $\int|B|^2\,dV$ becomes $\int u^{-4}|B|_g^2\,dV_g$. Choose any unit-volume minimizing Yamabe representative at the selected time. It has the same $b_i(t_i)$ and $E_i(t_i)$, and scalar curvature at least $\sqrt{48\delta}$. For constant $u=\lambda$, the rescaled solution is $\widehat g(t)=\lambda^2g(t/\lambda^4)$, since each term on the right of the covariant metric equation scales by $\lambda^{-2}$. Both $V_i$ and $T_i$ scale by $\lambda^4$, so $	au_i/V_i$ and the selection estimate are invariant. Nonconstant conformal changes preserve the slice integrals, but generally change the trajectory. The trace-free part of the time derivative of a conformally changed path has an $u^2$ factor, while its Bach tensor has an $u^{-2}$ factor. A scalar time change generally cannot reconcile the two. [Committed Bach manuscript, `sequence`](../../investigations-1/02_bach_flow/manuscript/bach_flow_and_smooth_poincare.tex#L238) · [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06633v1#S1)

## Lifespan available from the checked theorems

A scalar record saturates the estimate with vanishing excess and nonvanishing slice norm. Take $V_i=1$, a fixed $b_*>0$, $T_i=d_i/(c_Bb_*^2)$, $b_i(t)=b_*$, $\int|B|^2=b_*^2$, and $E_i(t)=I^+(M)+d_i-c_Bb_*^2t$ on $[0,T_i)$. It obeys the energy barriers, the exact dissipation equation and Cauchy–Schwarz. This is a test of the information in those inequalities. Producing a geometric finite-time counterexample would require a solution with those features.

Bahuaud–Helliwell, Theorem C, provides a positive time for every smooth initial metric on a compact four-manifold. In §4, Lemmas 4.2–4.3 and Theorem 4.4, the gauge-adjusted proof takes a small ball of radius $\mu_i$ with the product of the Schauder and nonlinear constants times $\mu_i$ smaller than $1/2$. The inhomogeneous linear term is bounded there by $C_{3,i}t$ and time is restricted so $C_{3,i}t\le\mu_i/2$. Consequently a family to which these parabolic inputs apply uniformly on a common time window, with $V_i=1$, would suffice. The proof uses control of the initial forcing and coefficients in differentiated Hölder norms, and supplies no comparison of its seed-dependent time with $d_i$ from the energy and Yamabe bounds. The quantifiers $T_i>0$ for each seed and $d_i\to0$ alone do not control $d_i/(T_i/V_i)$. [Bahuaud–Helliwell, arXiv:1010.4287v1, Theorem C and §§4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1010.4287#page=12)

López, Theorem 1.1, bounds curvature derivatives on an existing solution interval on which the spatial and temporal supremum of full curvature is already bounded. Theorem 1.2 implies that finite maximal time on a compact manifold forces unbounded supremum of full curvature. Neither bound yields the required lifetime from the energy budget or the initial pointwise curvature alone. The Yamabe band gives a full-curvature $L^2$ bound in the physical Yamabe gauges, which is distinct from the pointwise control in the flowing gauge in these theorems. For example, a separately proved lower bound $T_i\ge C K_i^{-2}$ with common $C>0$ and $K_i=\|\mathrm{Rm}_{q_i}\|_\infty>0$ would suffice on sequences with $d_i V_i K_i^2\to0$. This is a scale-consistent implication, without a supplied proof of that lifespan bound or of that joint estimate. [López, arXiv:1506.01979v1, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.01979#page=3)

## What the variational slope would have to control

At a smooth metric $g$ of positive Yamabe class, let $H$ range over smooth symmetric covariant tensors. With the same Bach convention,

$$
 DE_+(g)[H]=-c_B\int_M\langle B_g,H\rangle_g\,dV_g,
 \qquad
 \sup_{\sup_M|H|_g\le1}|DE_+(g)[H]|=c_B\int_M|B_g|_g\,dV_g.
$$

The upper bound is immediate and equality follows by taking $H=B_g/(|B_g|_g^2+\eta^2)^{1/2}$ and letting $\eta\downarrow0$. These tests are pointwise trace-free and integrate to curves preserving the volume form, using $g_s=g\exp(sg^{-1}H)$ in endomorphism notation. They remain positive Yamabe for sufficiently small $s$ by smooth dependence. The needed slope is thus a dual uniform-norm slope, including when the variation is volume constrained. [First variation in the marked comparison proof](../../investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/manuscript/physical_volume_and_marked_conformal_limits.tex#L51) · [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06633v1#S1)

Ekeland's Theorem 1.1 yields a small slope for a lower semicontinuous, lower bounded functional on a complete metric space. A locally uniform metric, for example the path distance with speed $\sup_M|\dot g|_g$, would yield the displayed dual norm at an interior smooth selected point. Its domain of smooth admissible metrics is incomplete. Indeed, begin at a smooth seed in the strict energy band and take uniformly positive metrics converging in $C^2$ to a nonsmooth $C^2$ metric by smoothing a small scalar coefficient of a local trace-free tensor in the volume-preserving exponential chart. These metrics are Cauchy for the path distance because the straight paths between the small chart perturbations have small uniform speed. They remain in the strict band with positive Yamabe constant by $C^2$ continuity, and the nonsmooth limit persists in this fixed volume form. Completing the domain and using an energy relaxation requires a theorem returning smooth almost stationary points with the uniform-norm slope, or regularity and slope-preserving approximation of the relaxed points. Ekeland supplies the metric variational inequality on the completed space. [Ekeland, *On the Variational Principle* (1974), Theorem 1.1, p. 324](https://www.ceremade.dauphine.fr/~ekeland/Articles/OnTheVariationalPrinciple.pdf#page=1)

Stronger topologies can give well-defined first variation on a Banach completion after the admissible domain is closed appropriately. Their dual slopes can be small even with positive total Bach norm. To test this on smooth metrics, take a smooth conformally round representative $g_*$ on standard $S^4$ that is Euclidean on a coordinate ball, by extending the local conformal factor smoothly. Take $\chi$ supported there and identically one on a smaller box, and define

$$
 g_k=g_*+k^{-4}\chi\cos(kx^1)H_0,\qquad H_0=(dx^2)^2-(dx^3)^2.
$$

The perturbation is $O(k^{-2})$ in $C^2$, so $Y([g_k])>0$ for large $k$, $W(g_k)=O(k^{-2})$ and $E_+(g_k)=O(k^{-4})$. It is a minimizing sequence on the standard type, where $I^+(S^4)=0$. The flat linear Bach operator on the transverse trace-free mode $\cos(kx^1)H_0$ is $-\frac14\Delta^2$ on that mode: $s'=0$, $P'=\frac12\operatorname{Ric}'=-\frac14\Delta H$, and the divergence term in the linearized Bach formula vanishes. Differentiating the cutoff changes the leading expression by $O(k^{-1})$. The full expression is affine in the fourth derivatives with smooth coefficients, and the lower jets of the perturbation are $O(k^{-1})$ or smaller. Since $g_*$ is flat on the support and Bach-flat off it, this gives

$$
 B_{g_k}=-\tfrac14\chi\cos(kx^1)H_0+O(k^{-1})
$$

uniformly. On the smaller box the integral of $|\cos(kx^1)|$ stays bounded below, so $\liminf_k\int|B_{g_k}|\,dV_{g_k}>0$. For a test of bounded $C^{0,\alpha}$ norm, $0<\alpha\le1$, its pairing with the leading oscillation is $O(k^{-\alpha})$. Translate the compactly supported amplitude in the $x^1$ direction by $\pi/k$, average the original and translated integrals, and use the Hölder difference. The changes in inverse metric and volume in the pairing are $O(k^{-4})$. Thus the Bach first variations tend to zero in $(C^{0,\alpha})^*$ and in $(C^m)^*$ for $m\ge1$. Replacing the metrics by their unit-volume Yamabe representatives preserves the energy and positive lower bound on the total Bach norm. The weak first-variation computation is in the displayed chart gauges. This example tests the norm implication on positive-Yamabe near-minimizers and does not supply a geometric counterexample in the hypothetical nonstandard energy band. [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo equations (1.1) and (2.16)](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06633v1#S2.SS3)

## Return to B04

Independent flow starts give the desired almost Bach-flat minimizing sequence if the normalized smooth times dominate the excess, or if one can find intervals with the smaller actual drop per normalized time tending to zero. No such interval was obtained on arbitrary seeds in the positive subcritical band. The abstract variational argument requires a complete admissible framework with smooth uniform-norm slope selection or an additional norm upgrade. Even with successful selection, the same-type ALE and whole marked-base comparisons still require their own extraction hypotheses, and the closed nonround survivor remains a separate problem.
