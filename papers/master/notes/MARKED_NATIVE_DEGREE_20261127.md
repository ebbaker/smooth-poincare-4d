## An embedded source in the round class on this same double

Choose an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(\Phi\colon M\to S^4\) from Isoshima's standardness result and pull back the unit round metric. This class has \(Y>0\) and \(e=0\). An explicit embedded copy of \(\partial(D\times D^2)\) in its stereographic chart can be given positive induced scalar curvature as follows.

In \(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2\), let \(D\) be a round closed disk with the interiors of two disjoint smaller round disks removed. Along each of its three boundary circles let \(c\) be a unit-speed parametrization, \(n\) its outward planar normal, and \(\kappa\) the signed curvature defined by \(n'=\kappa c'\). Choose a smooth nondecreasing \(\chi\) with \(\chi=0\) on \(s\le0\), \(\chi>0\) on \(0<s\le1\), and \(\chi=1\) near \(1\). Set, for \(0\le s\le1\),
\[
  \theta(s)=\frac{\pi\int_0^s\chi(v)\,dv}{2\int_0^1\chi(v)\,dv},\qquad
  u(s)=\epsilon\int_0^s\cos\theta(v)\,dv,\qquad
  r(s)=\epsilon\int_s^1\sin\theta(v)\,dv .
\]
Join the flat product \(D\times S^1_{r(0)}\) to the parametrized rims
\[
  (c+u(s)n,\ r(s)\omega),\qquad \omega\in S^1.
\]
They are disjoint for sufficiently small \(\epsilon>0\). At \(s=0\) the profile matches the constant-radius product to every order since \(\theta\) is flat. At \(s=1\) the linear germ of \(\theta\) makes the profile a circular arc meeting the rotation axis orthogonally. The rim is thus a smooth \(\partial D\times D^2\) piece after collapsing its angular circle at the axis, glued by the angular product coordinate. The resulting \(\Sigma_{\rm E}\) is smooth, closed, embedded and diffeomorphic to \(\partial(D\times D^2)\cong \#_2(S^1\times S^2)\). Denote its outward-oriented boundary identification with that model by \(i\).

The three Euclidean principal curvatures of a rim, with the displayed outward normal \((\sin\theta\,n,\cos\theta\,\omega)\), are
\[
  k_b=\theta'/\epsilon,\qquad k_c=\cos\theta/r,\qquad
  k_\ell=\kappa\sin\theta/(1+\kappa u).
\]
In fact the profile has speed \(\epsilon\) and tangent \((\cos\theta\,n,-\sin\theta\,\omega)\), and the other two directions are its orthogonal circle factors. Monotonicity of \(\chi\) gives \(\theta'\ge\theta/s\ge\sin\theta\) for \(s>0\). Moreover \(k_c\ge c_0/\epsilon\) for some \(c_0>0\) independent of \(\epsilon\). On \([0,1/2]\) use \(\cos\theta(1/2)>0\) and \(r\le\epsilon\); on \([1/2,1\)) integrate \(-\partial_s\cos\theta=\theta'\sin\theta\) using \(\theta'\ge\theta(1/2)>0\). For \(\epsilon\) small we have \(|k_\ell|\le 2\max|\kappa|\sin\theta\), so \(k_b,k_c\ge4|k_\ell|\). The Euclidean Gauss equation gives
\[
  R_{\Sigma_{\rm E}}=2(k_bk_c+k_bk_\ell+k_ck_\ell)\ge k_bk_c>0
\]
on the rims away from their flat joins, including the collapsed circles by continuity and the uniform positive limiting \(k_b,k_c\) there. Write \(h\) for the Euclidean induced metric. The zero set of \(R_h\) is the flat product with its joins. On that set \(\Delta_h|z|^2=4\) for \(z\in\mathbb R^4\), since the last two coordinates have constant Euclidean norm and the first two give the planar factor.

For \(d>0\) let \(\iota_d\) be dilation by \(d\) followed by inverse stereographic projection. The induced round metric on the model is \(d^2e^{2f_d}h\), where \(f_d=\log(2/(1+d^2|z|^2))\). Its scalar has the sign of
\[
  R_h-4\Delta_h f_d-2|\nabla_h f_d|^2
   =R_h+4d^2\Delta_h|z|^2+O(d^4).
\]
The error is uniform on the compact hypersurface. On a neighborhood of the zero set, \(\Delta_h|z|^2>2\) by continuity. On the compact complement \(R_h\) has a positive minimum. Therefore all sufficiently small \(d>0\) give strictly positive round induced scalar curvature everywhere.

For such a \(d\), orient the physical source \(\Sigma=\Phi^{-1}(\iota_d(\Sigma_{\rm E}))\) by the orientation of \(\Sigma_{\rm E}\) transported through its embedding. The map
\[
  \Sigma\xrightarrow{\ \iota_d^{-1}\circ\Phi\ }\Sigma_{\rm E}
  \xrightarrow{i}\partial(D\times D^2)\xrightarrow{j} Z_b
\]
is an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism and sends \([\Sigma]\) to \([Z_b]\). The actual induced PSC metric and this map supply exactly the premise of 05's theorem on the original marked sphere. The use of known standardness to pick \(\Phi\) makes this a calibration, with zero newly recognized smooth types. It identifies no prescribed nearly umbilic embedding of the native cut in a previously supplied entering metric. ## Sweepout check on the same double

The \(a\)-handle has product coordinates \(D^2_{\rm c}\times D^2_{\rm co}\). The doubled co-core \(K=D^2_{\rm co}\cup_{S^1}(-D^2_{\rm co})\subset M\) is an actual embedded two-sphere with the product normal framing from \(D^2_{\rm c}\). The belt is reached by pushing the original \(b\)-meridian across the outgoing solid torus, using the product coordinates of the two-handle. The tube of \(K\) closes using the original identity double. To describe its exterior, remove a small product neighborhood of the co-core from each half. The remaining annulus \((D^2_{\rm c}\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2_\epsilon)\times D^2_{\rm co}\) is a sleeve on the attaching tube of \(a\) in \(\partial H\). Collar compression identifies each trimmed half with \(H\), identifies the doubling face with \(\partial H\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu(a)\), and retains \(S^1_{\rm c}\times D^2_{\rm co}\) as the tube boundary face. Thus the exterior is the double of \(H\) along that marked complement. Each inclusion of the doubling face surjects onto \(\pi_1(H)=\mathbb Z\) since any generator on \(\partial H\) can avoid \(a\). The two identical maps give exterior fundamental group \(\mathbb Z\) by van Kampen, with the normal meridian primitive since \(a\) winds once. The calculation supplies the actual closing and the group. A relative diffeomorphism of this exterior to \(S^1\times D^3\), or a matched round metric germ at the native tube, remains to be supplied.

In fact this native tube boundary \(S^1\times S^2\) cannot be retained as a regular level of **any** Morse sweepout on a homotopy four-sphere whose induced scalar curvatures have one finite lower bound. By 08's Theorem `thm:index` the sweepout has no index-two points. With handles of indices \(0,1,3,4\) only, fundamental groups of sublevel components inject into the final fundamental group: one-handles take free products or add free generators and higher indices preserve each existing fundamental group. Hence all sublevel components are simply connected. The same argument for the negative function treats superlevels. Mayer–Vietoris across any whole regular level then gives \(H_1(\Sigma)=0\) since \(H_2(M)=0\) and the two sides have \(H_1=0\). In particular neither the native tube boundary nor the native \(Z_b\) can be held fixed as such a level.
