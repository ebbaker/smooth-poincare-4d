\subsection{Short spherical cuts and local minimization}
In the following comparisons the marked embeddings refer to the original
smooth sources. The sign along a joined path follows from Lemma~\ref{lem:wall}.
The cut accounting applies to both complete sides once their caps are positive.

\begin{proposition}[A short marked cut]\label{prop:short-cut}
On a fixed oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere let \(g_i\) be positive
classes with \(e_i\to I^+(M)<2T\). Suppose there are oriented marked
embeddings of \(-1<t=\log|x|<1\) with middle separating spheres.
On each annulus suppose some smooth positive conformal multiple \(m_i\) of
the pulled-back physical metric satisfies
\[
 \|m_i-\delta_{\rm Euc}\|_\infty+
 \|D(m_i-\delta_{\rm Euc})\|_{L^4(dx)}\longrightarrow0,\qquad
 \sup_i\|\Rm_{m_i}\|_{L^2(\mathcal T,m_i)}<\infty ,
\]
where \(\mathcal T\) contains the two fixed splice regions including
the central region to be flattened. Both full raw side energies are
computed in \(g_i\). On a hypothetical nonstandard source at the
infimum the smaller of these energies tends to zero.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Fix the indicated length before taking any source limit. Choose a
smooth cutoff \(\psi\) equal to one for \(|t|\le 1/4\), zero for
\(|t|\ge3/4\), with \(0\le\psi\le1\). Use
\(\delta_{\rm Euc}+(1-s\psi)(m_i-\delta_{\rm Euc})\),
\(0\le s\le1\), in the conformal cylindrical gauge \(|x|^{-2}\).
Extend the initial gauge by cutting off its logarithm beyond the
support of the surgery. The curvature computation of
the first-order splice estimate \cite[First-order splice]{Baker2026CollarDeformation} applies uniformly to \(1-s\psi\)
and bounds the total energy along this path by \(e_i+o(1)\).
It bounds each side's surcharge at the endpoint by \(o(1)\).
The endpoint is exactly the round product near the cut.
Lemma~\ref{lem:wall} keeps the entire path positive.

For each such \(i\), insert any length \(L\) of the exact round
product between its two product faces. This is a continuous path
on the same marked smooth type: in a fixed smaller product collar
replace \(dt^2\) by \((1+L b(t))^2dt^2\) with a smooth
nonnegative interior bump of integral one. Arclength identifies
the result with the stretched cylinder. Its Weyl tensor vanishes
and the total chiral energy \(e_i'\le e_i+o(1)\) is independent
of \(L\). The stretched metrics therefore have Yamabe constant at
least \(y_i'=\sqrt{48(8\pi^2-e_i')}>0\), uniformly in \(L\).

Attach a half-infinite product to either entire raw side after its
splice. Every compactly supported test there extends by zero to a
sufficiently long joined source, so its quotient is at least \(y_i'\).
An exponential radial coordinate compactifies that end by a smooth
marked ball: near infinity \(r=e^{-t}\) gives the flat metric
\(r^2(dt^2+\gamma)\). Extend the conformal multiplier positively
towards the fixed splice. Smooth tests avoiding the added point
are dense in \(H^1\) by cutting off in a ball of radius \(a\)
at squared gradient cost \(O(a^2)\). Conformal covariance thus gives
\(Y_{\rm cap}\ge y_i'\) on each closure. Only its original splice
contributes to the excess. The side estimates and
the finite cut criterion \cite[Finite cut criterion]{Baker2026ChiralMaster} give the assertion. The auxiliary
length \(L\) is free for each fixed source, after the two short
splices have been made.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Local replacement at the infimum]\label{thm:local-min}\label{prop:local-min}
Let \(g_i\) be the selected volume-one Yamabe sequence with
\(e_i\to I^+(M)<8\pi^2\). On an oriented open four-manifold \(U\)
suppose smooth embeddings of neighborhoods of a compact exhaustion
give \(\Phi_i^*g_i=u_i^2m_i\), \(u_i>0\), and
\[
 m_i\to h\text{ locally uniformly and strongly in }W^{1,4},
 \qquad \sup_i\|\Rm_{m_i}\|_{L^2(K,m_i)}<\infty
 \quad (K\Subset U),
\]
for a smooth metric \(h\). Then the two chiral Weyl matrix
densities converge strongly in \(L^2_{\rm loc}\), and \(h\)
locally minimizes chiral energy against sufficiently small smooth
compactly supported metric variations. In particular \(B_h=0\).
The energy on a fixed compact is measured by its full pullback,
even when its physical volume tends to zero.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Work in a relatively compact oriented coordinate domain \(D\).
Use the matrix densities \(F_\pm(p)=\mathcal P_p\Rm_{\rm low}(p)\)
of the first-order splice estimate \cite[First-order splice]{Baker2026CollarDeformation}, so that their squared Euclidean norms
integrate to \(E_\pm(p;D)\). The constant principal part
\(L(D^2p)\) and the strong convergence of \(Q(p)[Dp,Dp]\) in
\(L^2\) give distributional convergence of all-lowered curvature.
The curvature bound makes it weak \(L^2\) convergence. Thus
\(r_i=F_\pm(m_i)-F_\pm(h)\rightharpoonup0\) in \(L^2(D)\).

Choose \(0\le\chi\le1\) smooth and compactly supported in \(D\).
Transplant \(n_{i,s}=h+(1-s\chi)(m_i-h)\) with multiplier \(u_i^2\)
to the source, leaving its entire exterior fixed. The curvature
commutator involves \(D\chi\,D(m_i-h)\) and \(D^2\chi(m_i-h)\),
both small in \(L^2\). The products of first derivatives converge
strongly to those for \(h\), uniformly for \(0\le s\le1\).
Changing the algebraic projection costs at most
\(C\|m_i-h\|_\infty(1+\|\Rm_{m_i}\|_2)\). Consequently
\[
 F_\pm(n_{i,s})=F_\pm(h)+(1-s\chi)r_i+o_{L^2}(1)
\]
uniformly in \(s\). In the difference of squared norms the cross
term against \(\chi F_\pm(h)\) tends to zero by weak convergence.
The energy of the resulting closed metric \(G_{i,s}\) is
\[
 E_\pm(G_{i,s})=e_i-
 \int_D (2s\chi-s^2\chi^2)|r_i|^2\,dx+o(1).
\]
Both total initial energies equal \(e_i\) by signature. The path
stays below the wall, so \(E_\pm(G_{i,1})\ge I^+(M)\). It follows
that the integral with weight \(2\chi-\chi^2\) tends to zero.
Taking \(\chi=1\) on smaller compacts proves both strong limits.
The squared densities converge in \(L^1_{\rm loc}\).

The repaired metrics \(G_{i,1}\) also approach the infimum. On the
interior of \(\{\chi=1\}\) their comparison metric is exactly \(h\).
For a fixed symmetric tensor \(H\) supported there, replace \(h\)
by \(h+sH\) on that interior. The change in either closed chiral
energy is exactly the change for \(h\) on the support, independent
of \(i\). For all sufficiently small \(|s|\) the entire new path
remains below the wall by the fixed margin. Its endpoint is a
positive competitor. Sending \(i\to\infty\) gives a nonnegative
energy change for either sign of \(s\). The construction with a
cutoff on a larger relatively compact set works for any compact
support, using finitely many coordinates for the preceding estimates.
The sum of the two chiral first variations is one quarter of the full four-index Weyl variation. The compactly supported first variation of the latter functional is
a nonzero multiple of the Bach pairing
\cite[\S2.1]{TianViaclovsky2004Moduli}. This proves the assertion.
\end{proof}

For variable conformal factors the curvature bound belongs to
\(m_i\). A local \(L^2\) scalar bound there suffices: in the
orthonormal coordinate frame the connection forms \(\omega_i\)
are bounded in \(L^4\). The Euler form is locally the derivative
of a transgression of size
\(C(|\omega_i||\Rm_{m_i}|+|\omega_i|^3)\)
\cite[\S3]{ChernSimons1974Characteristic}. In full tensor norms
the unnormalized Euler density \(\mathcal E\) satisfies
\(|\Rm|^2+\mathcal E=2|W|^2+R^2/3\).
Test with a squared cutoff. Young's inequality absorbs the term
linear in cutoff-weighted curvature. Both Weyl energies have the
source bound by conformal invariance. This proves the full bound
on smaller compacts. Constant physical rescalings already carry
the full bound of \eqref{eq:budgets}.

\begin{corollary}[A whole marked base]\label{cor:whole-base}
On a nonstandard minimizing type, suppose the open comparison in Theorem~\ref{thm:local-min} exhausts
\(N\setminus P\), where \(N\) is a smooth closed oriented homotopy
four-sphere, \(P\) is a finite set of ordinary points, and \(h\)
extends smoothly to \(N\). Let \(e_N=E_+(h;N)\).
If \(e_N>0\), then \(N\) has the oriented smooth type of \(M\), and
\([h]\) is a positive class attaining \(I^+(M)=e_N\).
If \(e_N=0\), \(N\) is conformally round and along a slow exhaustion
and a subsequence one marked exterior carries all the limiting chiral
energy of either sign. Capping that exterior gives positive classes
of the original smooth type at the same limiting energy.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Compactly supported Yamabe tests transfer in the conformal marking.
On a fixed compact the scalar density is a divergence of an expression
linear in \(Dm_i\) plus a term quadratic in \(Dm_i\), with smooth
metric coefficients. Hence its integral against a fixed test square
converges by the assumed first-order convergence. Removing the finite
points by cutoffs gives \(Y([h])\ge\limsup_iY_i>0\).

At fixed small \(b\), take the whole core obtained by deleting
the \(h\)-normal balls of radius \(b\). Each face has one distinct
exterior, by Mayer--Vietoris in degrees zero and one on the connected
core in \(M\). The empty-face case is a closed diffeomorphism.
After rescaling normal coordinates to radius one, \(h\) is
\(O(b^2)\)-close to Euclidean through order two on a fixed short
annulus. At this fixed \(b\), \(m_i\) adds \(o_i(1;b)\) to the
first-order error. To make the estimates uniform as \(b\downarrow0\)
even for a variable conformal gauge, first install the exact \(h\)
near each face by the cutoff interpolation in
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-min}. The cost on its larger, fixed annulus
tends to zero at fixed \(b\) by strong chiral convergence.
The remaining splice now uses the curvature of \(h\).
The annuli are disjoint. The simultaneous short splices and exact
stretches therefore give positive caps with vanishing excess on a
slow diagonal. The core energy tends to \(e_N\) by the ordered
limits, and the sum on the full raw exteriors tends to \(e-e_N\).

If \(e_N>0\) and \(e-e_N>0\), one of the finitely many exterior
labels carries a fixed positive mass on a subsequence, opposite the
positive core. The one-cut comparison gives a contradiction. Hence
each capped exterior has energy tending to zero and is standard.
The marked disk identifications of the finite cut criterion \cite[Finite cut criterion]{Baker2026ChiralMaster}
give \(M\cong N\), and \([h]\) attains the infimum.
If \(e_N=0\), signature and the conformal developing map give roundness \cite{Kuiper1949}.
With finitely many labels pass to limits of each exterior mass.
Two positive limits give the same one-cut contradiction. The
unique positive exterior carries \(e\), the capped core is standard
and the other capped exteriors are standard. Apply the finite marked
reassembly to the positive cap on the surviving exterior.
On the standard type the limiting energy is zero in both signs. The same conclusion about the nonstandard survivor for the opposite chirality follows by signature
on each closure and the vanishing change in both splice integrals.
\end{proof}
