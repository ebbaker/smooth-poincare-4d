## Fixed and shrinking radii

Lee–Lee's published Theorem 1.1 has uniform constants determined by dimension, local upper volume constant and Sobolev constant, with no dependence on the domain parameter. The hypotheses concern respectively every initial ball of radius at most one, a local inhomogeneous Sobolev inequality on the unit ball, and the initial unit-ball critical curvature norm. The conclusions used are the estimates \(\Lambda_1(\epsilon+\sqrt t)/t\) for the curvature supremum and \(\Lambda_1(\epsilon+\sqrt t)\) for the \(L^2\) norm on each evolving half-ball, the positive-time injectivity lower bound, and the final two-point estimate. This is the published theorem with the \(\sqrt t\) terms. Taking the domain parameter larger than the diameter gives one smooth complete flow on each closed rescaled source. Reapplying the theorem with other centers invokes ordinary compact Ricci-flow uniqueness. Under rescaling by \(r^{-2}\), the uniform original Sobolev gradient coefficient stays fixed and its zero-order coefficient is multiplied by \(r^2\). The volume upper bound and initial curvature norm transfer to the unit radius at any fixed sufficiently small \(r\) and at the subsequently selected shrinking radius. Take \(t_*\le\min(1,\widehat T,\epsilon^2)\). Then both curvature coefficients are bounded by \(2\Lambda_1\epsilon\) after accounting for the factor \(t^{-1}\) in the pointwise estimate. [Lee–Lee, published Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0010437X26103595)

The weighted Sobolev inequality from the endpoint estimate is applicable to these closed flows. Its starting entropy test uses \(\|f\|_2=1\), the initial Sobolev coefficients and \(R\ge-1\), and uses no unit-volume hypothesis beyond that test normalization. On an evolving half-ball absorb the scalar term by the local \(L^2\) curvature bound. Cutoff iteration at radii below \(1/2\) then gives a uniform lower volume ratio, in particular on radius-\(1/16\) balls. At a fixed contrary radius \(r\), a maximal \(1/8\)-separated net covers by radius-\(1/8\) balls, and yields cutoffs supported on radius-\(1/4\) balls with squared sum one. The cardinality is bounded by the total volume and the disjoint small-ball volume floor. For each cutoff \(f_j=\chi_j f\), apply the local form estimate and sum. The cross derivatives cancel because \(\sum_j\chi_jd\chi_j=0\). The gradient coefficient remains \(C\epsilon\) and the possible radius dependence lies only in the zero-order term. Thus the choice of small fixed \(\epsilon\) precedes the contrary radius. Gronwall makes the negative isotropic pair sums vanish in the smooth positive-time limit at each fixed radius. A fixed-radius upper diameter bound follows by packing disjoint small balls along a geodesic and using the total volume upper bound \(e^{t_*}r^{-4}\). With the injectivity and curvature derivative estimates, the closed limit is diffeomorphic to the late sources. The inherited closed nonnegative-isotropic argument uses their homotopy-sphere type and rules out the contrary subsequence. [Weighted form proof](../manuscript/endpoint_smoothing.tex#L28) · [Pair evolution and closed recognition](../manuscript/isotropic_gap.tex)

For each smooth initial source, the maximum over centers of the radius-\(r\) curvature integral is continuous in \(r\). This follows by the joint continuity in \(x,r\) for \(r>0\), since distance spheres are volume null, compactness of the center set, and a uniform per-source small-ball bound tending to zero as \(r\downarrow0\). The fixed-radius exclusions therefore give a first scale \(a_i\to0\) at which the maximum equals \(\epsilon^2\). At that scale, Lee–Lee's shrinking-balls lemma places every evolving radius-\(1/2\) ball inside the initial unit ball at the same point. The scalar lower bound gives \(dV_t\le e^{t}\,dV_0\) pointwise. The upper volume of the evolving half-ball and the lower volume of the disjoint radius-\(1/16\) balls now bound the overlap of the radius-\(1/4\) supports, uniformly in the varying scale. The standard squared-cutoff calculation consequently gives
\[
 \int U f^2\,dV_t\le C\epsilon\big(\|df\|_{2,t}^2+\|f\|_{2,t}^2\big),\qquad U=|\mathrm{Rm}|_{\rm tens}.
\]
All constants are independent of \(i\). They can be held fixed while making \(\epsilon\) smaller below the absorption thresholds, because the flow constants are fixed by the initial geometric bounds and the initial step makes the scalar absorption uniformly bounded away from failure.

The tensor evolution, integrated on the closed source and with the evolving volume accounted for, gives
\[
 \frac d{dt}\int U^2\,dV_t
 \le -2\int|\nabla\mathrm{Rm}|^2\,dV_t+C\int U^3\,dV_t .
\]
Use \(f=U\) in the form bound and \(|dU|\le|\nabla\mathrm{Rm}|\); it is the tensor derivative that remains on the left. The initial uniform global energy then bounds \(\sup_t\int U^2\) and \(\int_0^{t_*}\int|\nabla\mathrm{Rm}|^2\). Both negative isotropic sums obey the same absorption inequality with a uniform Gronwall coefficient. Their initial squared integral tends to zero in the unscaled smooth modified metric and is invariant under constant rescaling. Their fixed positive-time smooth limit vanishes, giving nonnegative isotropic curvature.

## Complete noncompactness and the actual support

On closed positive-time subintervals the global curvature and injectivity bounds allow complete pointed smooth compactness, using the curvature derivative estimates and a diagonal exhaustion. The comparison embeddings are fixed as maps in time on any compared space-time compact. A compact limit at any one positive time would provide a whole compact exhaustion member without boundary. Its embedding onto the connected source at that time would be surjective, by openness and closedness. The volumes would then remain bounded on the subsequence. In fact they tend to infinity. The manuscript's \(f=1\) form test suffices, and the energy estimate gives a sharper direct check. If \(V_i(t)=\operatorname{Vol}(M_i,g_i(t))\) in the rescaled metric, then
\[
 |V_i'(t)|\le\|R(t)\|_{2,t}\sqrt{V_i(t)}
       \le C\sqrt{V_i(t)},\qquad
 |\sqrt{V_i(t)}-a_i^{-2}|\le Ct .
\]
The latter uses the initial unit volume before rescaling, the tensor bound \(R^2\le C U^2\) and the integrated energy bound. It holds on the entire common horizon. [Hamilton, compactness for Ricci flows](https://doi.org/10.2307/2375080)

The first inequality of the Lee–Lee two-point estimate gives \(d_t(x,y)\le(\Lambda_1 d_0(x,y))^{1-\gamma}\) for a controlled \(\gamma=2(n-1)\Lambda_1\epsilon<1\) on the allowed initial chart. Subdivide each initial minimizing geodesic of length at most one into a bounded number of pieces shorter than \(r_1/2\), applying that estimate centered at the beginning of each piece. This gives \(B_0(x_i,1)\subset B_t(x_i,D)\) for one fixed \(D\), after allowing a strict radius margin. A terminal cutoff \(\chi_i\) can be one on this ball and have support inside \(B_t(x_i,D+1)\), with pointwise terminal gradient at most two. Apply the shrinking-balls estimate with initial radius \(D+2\) to place the support in \(B_0(x_i,D+2)\). The curvature bound used for shrinking balls is global on the closed sources, so its use at this fixed enlarged radius is valid. On returning to the original modified scale, this ball has radius \((D+2)a_i\) and vanishing volume by the stipulated upper bound. The original scalar and Weyl square integrals there vanish. Smooth conformal approximation was chosen separately for each source with uniform relative distance error and \(o(1)\) total variation error for the squared curvature measures. Constants and the vanishing error can be taken uniformly over all terminal cutoffs constructed in this manner. [Same-gauge approximation statement](../manuscript/ricci_concentration.tex) · [Lee–Lee, final estimate of Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 3.2](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0010437X26103595)

## Euler sign, temporal transgression, and a fixed limiting cutoff

Take the Euler form \(\mathcal E=(|W^+|^2+|W^-|^2+R^2/24-|Z|^2/2)dV\), integrating to \(8\pi^2\chi(M_i)\). On the initial charge ball \(B_0(x_i,1)\), the equality \(\int U^2=\epsilon^2\) and the vanishing Weyl and scalar square integrals give \(\int|Z|^2=\epsilon^2/2+o(1)\), since \(U^2=4(|W^+|^2+|W^-|^2)+2|Z|^2+R^2/6\). The terminal cutoff equals one on this ball, and the other \(-|Z|^2/2\) contributions on its support are nonpositive. Therefore \(\int\chi_i\mathcal E(0)\le-\epsilon^2/4+o(1)\). This is a signed weighted test on the actual source.

To compare connections globally, let \(J_t\) send a fixed oriented Euclidean bundle to the \(g(t)\)-orthonormal tangent bundle by the evolution \(\dot J_t=\mathrm{Ric}_{g(t)}^\sharp J_t\). In the common bundle the connection derivative has the following tensor, in the corresponding evolving frames:
\[
 \langle \dot\omega_t(X)Y,Z\rangle
 =(\nabla_Z\mathrm{Ric})(X,Y)-(\nabla_Y\mathrm{Ric})(X,Z).
\]
Indeed differentiating the Koszul formula gives the three derivatives of \(\mathrm{Ric}\), with the term \(-(\nabla_X\mathrm{Ric})(Y,Z)\) canceled by differentiating the moving frame. The remaining expression is skew in \(Y,Z\). If \(P\) is the polarized Pfaffian with the Euler normalization here, the covariant Bianchi identity gives \(\partial_t\mathcal E=d(2P(\dot\omega_t,\Omega_t))\). The primitive has norm at most \(C|\nabla\mathrm{Ric}|U\), measured in \(g(t)\). This calculation compares fixed bundles and requires no identification of source tangent frames across different indices. The identity also applies to a fixed terminal Lipschitz cutoff by spatial smooth approximation, or in distributional integration by parts. [Chern–Simons, §3, Proposition 3.2 and (3.3)–(3.4)](https://math.mit.edu/juvitop/pastseminars/notes_2019_Fall/Chern-Simons.pdf)

For \(0<s\le\tau\), the global bound \(|\mathrm{Ric}(t)|\le b/t\) gives \(g(\tau)\le(\tau/s)^{2b}g(s)\), so \(|d\chi_i|_{g(s)}\le2(\tau/s)^b\). The exponent may be fixed with \(0<b<1/2\) by taking \(t_*\le\epsilon^2\) and choosing the initial \(\epsilon\) small. Apply Cauchy–Schwarz in space with \(\sup_t\|U(t)\|_2\) and in time with \(\int_0^{t_*}\|\nabla\mathrm{Rm}\|_2^2\). For \(0<u\le\tau\),
\[
 \left|\int\chi_i\mathcal E(u)-\int\chi_i\mathcal E(0)\right|
 \le C\,\tau^b u^{1/2-b},
\]
where \(C\) includes \((1-2b)^{-1/2}\) and the uniform energy and derivative bounds. To justify the lower time endpoint first integrate on \([\eta,u]\). For each fixed smooth source its connection and Euler form are continuous at zero, and the time integral is finite down to zero. Setting \(u=\tau\) yields the asserted \(C\sqrt\tau\). Take this error at most \(\epsilon^2/16\); the initial \(o(1)\) can independently be taken at most \(\epsilon^2/16\). Thus the terminal weighted Euler integral is at most \(-\epsilon^2/8\), so its supporting ball has \(\int|Z|^2\ge\epsilon^2/4\).

At each fixed terminal time a compact comparison containing the limiting radius-\(D+2\) ball has image containing the corresponding smaller source ball. A minimizing curve leaving the image would have to cross the mapped outer comparison boundary, at a larger distance by smooth metric convergence. The support and all its curvature densities are thus inside this comparison. This transfers the unweighted trace-free Ricci lower bound to the limiting fixed-radius ball. It also gives a weighted negative Euler integral on that ball by taking a uniformly convergent subsequence of the terminal cutoffs on a slightly larger comparison. Negativity concerns the cutoff test. The unweighted whole-ball Euler integral has no sign conclusion from this argument.

There is a stronger fixed-test formulation. Choose one terminal time \(\tau_0\) with \(C\sqrt{\tau_0}\le\epsilon^2/16\). Keep the associated \(\chi_i\) fixed for all \(0<u\le\tau_0\). Pull it back by the time-independent pointed convergence maps on one compact neighborhood of the terminal support and extract uniform convergence to a fixed compactly supported \(\chi_\infty\). After a further subsequence the bounded numbers \(J_i=\int\chi_i\mathcal E_i(0)\) converge to \(J\le-\epsilon^2/4\). For every fixed \(u>0\) smooth flow convergence on that same compact passes the weighted integrals to the limit. The displayed estimate gives
\[
 \left|\int\chi_\infty\mathcal E_\infty(u)-J\right|
 \le C\tau_0^b u^{1/2-b}.
\]
Thus the limiting flow has one fixed cutoff whose Euler test stays negative on this whole short positive-time interval and has a strictly negative limit as \(u\downarrow0\). It is a time-zero limit of a signed test, without an identification of the limiting time-zero metric in a first-order topology. The time-dependent curvature theorem cannot be combined with the original Euclidean-limit theorem unless such a first-order comparison on the actual supports is supplied.

Finally \(R\ge0\) in the limit by the initial rescaled lower bound and the scalar minimum principle. A zero at any interior positive time on the connected limit would force \(R=0\) on all earlier positive slices by the strong parabolic minimum principle, and the scalar evolution would then give \(\mathrm{Ric}=0\) there. This contradicts the definite trace-free Ricci integral. The scalar is thus positive on the positive-time limit. The fixed-radius and shrinking-radius conclusions, including nonflatness, are justified within the stated upper volume hypothesis.

## E72s · No complete smooth time-zero metric for this limit

This follow-on uses the same pointed limit and the finite source energy bounds of E71. There is no complete smooth metric \(h_0\) on its limiting manifold \(X\) such that the positive-time metrics \(h(t)\) converge smoothly on compacts to \(h_0\) as \(t\downarrow0\). In fact the proof only requires locally uniform and strong \(W^{1,4}\) convergence \(h(t)\to h_0\) on that fixed smooth manifold, with \(h_0\) still smooth and complete. The excluded object is an extension of the positive-time limit. It does not require convergence of the original rescaled source metrics in any time-zero charts.

Fix the terminal comparison at \(\tau_0\). A fixed \(f\in C_c^\infty(X)\) transported by those same time-independent flow embeddings gives smooth \(f_i\) on the sources, extended by zero past a compared neighborhood of its support. The terminal support lies in a ball \(B_{\tau_0}(x_i,D_f)\) of fixed radius, and \(\|f_i\|_\infty\) and \(|df_i|_{\tau_0}\) are uniformly bounded. One can choose the compared neighborhood with fixed compact curves joining the support to the basepoint, so the terminal radius bound follows directly from smooth metric convergence there. The shrinking-balls inclusion gives a fixed larger rescaled initial ball containing every such support. Its unrescaled radius is \(O_f(a_i)\), so the stated volume upper bound gives bounded rescaled initial volume and vanishing unrescaled modified volume. Volume at all subsequent times \(0\le s\le\tau_0\) is also uniformly bounded on the support, by \(dV_s\le e^s dV_0\). In particular the initial scalar and Weyl square integrals on that support tend to zero. These facts apply to each fixed compact test without fixing one radius for the whole exhaustion.

The signed scalar variation is sufficient. For smooth nonnegative \(\psi\in C_c^\infty(X)\) let \(\psi_i\) be its transported test. The exact flow equation gives
\[
 \frac d{ds}\int\psi_i R(s)\,dV_s
 =-\int\langle d\psi_i,dR(s)\rangle\,dV_s
     +\int\psi_i\big(2|\mathrm{Ric}(s)|^2-R(s)^2\big)\,dV_s .
\]
The initial scalar test tends to zero by Cauchy–Schwarz using the bounded support volume and the local initial scalar-square estimate. The reaction contributes \(O_\psi(t)\). In the gradient term use the bounded support volume, \(\|dR\|_2\le C\|\nabla\mathrm{Rm}\|_2\), and the earlier gradient bound \(C_\psi(\tau_0/s)^b\). Cauchy–Schwarz in time gives the uniform error \(O_\psi(\tau_0^b t^{1/2-b})\). Passing first to the positive-time limit therefore gives \(\int\psi R_{h(t)}dV_{h(t)}\to0\) as \(t\downarrow0\). For a smooth extension \(h_0\), \(R_{h_0}=0\), and the closed algebraic nonnegative isotropic inequalities at positive times pass to \(h_0\) to give \(W_{h_0}^\pm=0\). No separate evolution estimate for the chiral blocks is needed.

The homogeneous Sobolev inequality also transfers to the putative \(h_0\). On the original rescaled closed source its Yamabe inequality is
\[
 Y_i\|f_i\|_{4,0}^2\le
    6\int|df_i|_0^2dV_0+\int R(0)f_i^2dV_0 ,
\]
with the same \(Y_i\) by constant-scale invariance. The last term is \(o_i(1)\) on the fixed compact support. Since
\[
 \partial_s\big(|df_i|_s^2\,dV_s\big)
 =\big(2\operatorname{Ric}_s(\nabla f_i,\nabla f_i)
                          -R(s)|df_i|_s^2\big)dV_s ,
\]
the uniform curvature \(L^2\) bound, bounded support volume, and terminal comparison control its time-integrated absolute change by \(C_f\tau_0^{2b}t^{1-2b}\). The \(f_i^4\) volume integral changes by at most \(C_f t\), using \(\partial_s dV_s=-R(s)dV_s\) and the same bounds. Pass to the positive-time limit first and then to \(h_0\), obtaining \(y\|f\|_{4,h_0}^2\le6\int|df|_{h_0}^2dV_{h_0}\) for all compactly supported smooth \(f\).

For each compactly supported cutoff \(\psi\), \(0\le\psi\le1\), the uniform Euler transport estimate gives
\[
 \lim_{i\to\infty}\int\psi_i\mathcal E_i(0)
       =\int\psi\mathcal E_{h_0}.
\]
Indeed, compare first through any fixed \(t>0\) using the estimate \(C_\psi\tau_0^b t^{1/2-b}\) and smooth positive-time convergence, then take \(t\downarrow0\). The initial Euler test equals \(-\tfrac12\int\psi_i|Z_i(0)|^2dV_i(0)+o_i(1)\) by the shrinking support scalar and Weyl bounds. Thus after exhausting \(X\), \(d=\int_X|\mathrm{Ric}_{h_0}|^2dV_{h_0}\le4e+y^2/12-8T<y^2/12\), using the time-zero modified Gauss–Bonnet budget. The single limiting cutoff from E71 has a strictly negative weighted Euler trace at \(t=0\), so \(d>0\).

For the stated first-order strengthening, smooth convergence at \(t=0\) in the preceding argument can be replaced by uniform and strong \(W^{1,4}\) convergence. The positive-time limit has a uniform global \(L^2\) curvature bound by exhaustion. The all-lowered coordinate curvature formula therefore gives weak local \(L^2\) curvature convergence to that of the smooth \(h_0\), using distributional convergence of the linear second derivatives and strong \(L^2\) convergence of the quadratic first derivatives. The scalar trace just proved and \(R_{h(t)}\ge0\) imply local scalar \(L^1\) convergence to zero. Nonnegative isotropic curvature gives \(|W^\pm_{h(t)}|\le\sqrt6\,R_{h(t)}/6\) from the rank-three trace-free eigenvalue bound, so local \(L^1\) convergence and weak \(L^2\) curvature convergence still give \(R_{h_0}=W^\pm_{h_0}=0\). Euler tests transfer through the metric-square-root identification of orthonormal bundles, with the connection difference tending to zero in \(L^4\) and the curvature of the interpolated connection bounded in \(L^2\). The \(L^{4/3}\) transgression then pairs with the \(L^4\) derivative of a compact cutoff, including the fixed Lipschitz cutoff supplied by E71. Uniform metric convergence is sufficient for the final homogeneous Sobolev limit. [Fixed-bundle first-order transgression](../manuscript/ricci_concentration.tex)

This data contradicts the same complete-end argument as in the modified Euclidean limit theorem. The scalar-flat conformally flat \(h_0\) has harmonic curvature and finite \(\int|\mathrm{Rm}|^2=2d\). Its homogeneous Sobolev estimate gives the Tian–Viaclovsky end theorem's finite homogeneous Sobolev hypothesis. The resulting finitely many ALE ends have rational homology sphere links. For each whole fixed compact truncation of \(X\), the positive-time comparison embeds its neighborhood into an actual smooth homotopy sphere. This supplies the necessary topology in the fixed smooth manifold even without a time-zero metric comparison on the source. As in the modified limit proof, a truncation with \(k\) ends has Euler characteristic \(2-k\). Writing \(m_j\) for the group order of the \(j\)-th end, the normalized Euler boundary integrals give
\[
 d=4T\Big(k-2+\sum_j m_j^{-1}\Big).
\]
The homogeneous sharp radial tests on the \(j\)-th end give \(y^2\le96T/m_j\). For \(k\ge2\), with \(m=\max_jm_j\), one gets \(d\ge8T/m\ge y^2/12\), contradicting the strict bound. For \(k=1\), nonnegative \(d\) forces \(m_1=1\) and then \(d=0\), contradicting the signed test. An incomplete time-zero metric, or a time-zero limit without the indicated norm convergence to a smooth complete metric, is outside this exclusion.
