## Initial data from the joint deficit

Consider a sequence of possibly varying closed connected oriented smooth homotopy four-spheres \(M_i\) of hypothetical nonstandard type. Let \(g_i\) be smooth volume-one Yamabe metrics of positive constants \(Y_i\), with chiral energies \(e_i=E_+(g_i)=E_-(g_i)\le 2T-\delta\) for a fixed \(\delta>0\), where \(T=4\pi^2\). The chiral norm is Hilbert–Schmidt on the two Weyl endomorphisms. Write \(L_g=\max_\pm\lambda_{\max}(W_g^\pm)\), \(\ell_i^2=\int L_{g_i}^2\,dV_{g_i}\), \(D_{g_i}=\int |\operatorname{Ric}_{0,g_i}|^2\), and \(\Delta_i=36\ell_i^2-Y_i^2\). The check assumes \(D_{g_i}\to0\) and \(\Delta_i\to0\). It uses the ordinary Yamabe inequality in conformal gauges and the identities \(Y_i^2=48(2T-e_i)+12D_{g_i}\), \(Y_i\le S=8\sqrt6\pi\).

Chen–Zhu Lemma 2.1 supplies the actual volume-one modified minimizing representative \(q_i\in[g_i]\) with positive \(C^{2,\alpha}\) conformal factor and \(R_{q_i}=6L_{q_i}+c_i\). Their Theorem 1.7 and simple connectivity force \(c_i\le0\) on each hypothesized nonstandard smooth type. The modified quotient is at least \(Y_i-6\ell_i\) by the ordinary Yamabe inequality and Hölder. Thus \(\Delta_i\ge0\) and
\[
  -\frac{\Delta_i}{6\ell_i+Y_i}\le c_i\le0,
  \qquad Y_i\ge\sqrt{48\delta}.
\]
The conformally invariant \(\ell_i^2\) is measured equally in \(q_i\) and \(g_i\). Chen–Zhu's assumptions here are a smooth initially closed Riemannian four-manifold and the indicated conformally covariant nonnegative Weyl potential. The maximum eigenvalue is locally Lipschitz in the original smooth gauge. [Chen–Zhu, Comm. Anal. Geom. 22 (2014), (1.12), Theorem 1.7, Lemma 2.1 and §2](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2014/0022/0005/CAG-2014-0022-0005-a002.pdf)

For the next formulas suppress the index and write \(L=L_q\). The constant Yamabe test and unit volume give \(Y\le6\int L+c\), so \(\int L\ge Y/6\) and \(\|L-Y/6\|_2^2\le\ell^2-Y^2/36\). Also
\[
 \int R_q^2=36\ell^2+c(12\int L+c)\le36\ell^2,
 \quad \|R_q-Y\|_2^2\le\Delta,
 \quad D_q=D_g+\frac{\int R_q^2-Y^2}{12}\le D_g+\Delta/12.
\]
The sign in the first estimate follows from \(12\int L+c\ge2Y-c>0\). The last uses Gauss–Bonnet with the full tensor Weyl norm equal to four times the sum of the chiral operator squares. It extends to \(q\) by smooth \(C^2\) approximation. Approximate each conformal factor by a positive smooth one and then renormalize volume. At each fixed index the curvature expressions, the isotropic lower deficit, and all these integrals can be kept arbitrarily close. Denote the smooth approximants by \(h_i\). They satisfy \(R_{h_i}\ge-1\) eventually. The usual Yamabe inequality on their common conformal class gives
\[
 (Y_i-\|R_{h_i}-Y_i\|_2)\|f\|_{4,h_i}^2
 \le6\|df\|_{2,h_i}^2+Y_i\|f\|_{2,h_i}^2,
\]
so their inhomogeneous Sobolev constants are uniform. [Lee–Parker, §2, (2.7)](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/lee-parker.yamabe.pdf) · [Chang–Gursky–Yang, (0.4) and Remark 2](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf)

For any trace-free symmetric three-by-three block with largest eigenvalue \(l\), its squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm lies between \(3l^2/2\) and \(6l^2\). For the upper bound its middle eigenvalue \(b\) lies in \([-l/2,l]\), and \(l^2+b^2+(l+b)^2\) is convex in \(b\) and largest at \(b=l\). Thus in the smooth modified gauge the full tensor curvature \(U=|\operatorname{Rm}|_{\mathrm{tens}}\) satisfies
\[
 U^2\le48L^2+2|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2+R^2/6.
\]
The vector \((\sqrt{48}Y_i/6,0,Y_i/\sqrt6)\) has norm \(\sqrt{3/2}Y_i\). Comparing the two Euclidean vector norms shows that for fixed \(K_0=\sqrt{3/2}S\), \(\|(U_i-K_0)_+\|_2\to0\). This uses bounded largest eigenvalue in the modified gauge up to a vanishing \(L^2\) error. The endpoint E57 argument's stronger \(L^1\) convergence of \(U^2\) to a constant is unnecessary.

## Common time and weak cone transport

Apply the common-time part of the E57 smoothing lemma to \(h_i\). Its truncated curvatures are \(v_i=(U_i-\phi)_+\), with \(\phi'=a\phi^2\), \(\phi(0)=K_0\), and \(\phi\le2K_0\) on the common short interval. Scalar lower bounds propagate. The weighted Sobolev inequality follows from Perelman's entropy monotonicity and the fixed-slice logarithmic-to-Sobolev conversion, before any pointwise bound on the source curvature maximum. Absorbing the small \(L^2\) contribution of \(v_i\) gives the ordinary inhomogeneous Sobolev inequality with uniform constants. The E57 energy bootstrap and record curvature argument give \(\sup_{t\le t_*}\|v_i(t)\|_2=o(1)\), and \(U_i(t)\le C/t\) for \(t>0\). [Perelman, arXiv:math/0211159v1, §3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0211159) · [Ye, arXiv:0707.2424v4, Theorem 5.5(1), (5.17)–(5.18)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.2424) · [Hamilton, *The formation of singularities in the Ricci flow*, Theorems 7.1 and 8.1](https://doi.org/10.4310/SDG.1993.v2.n1.a2)

For the isotropic term use the two diagonal blocks \(A=W^++RI/12\) and \(C=W^-+RI/12\), with off-diagonal block \(B:\Lambda^-\to\Lambda^+\). The two lowest eigenvalues of either diagonal block sum to \(F_\pm=R/6-\lambda_{\max}(W^\pm)\). Nonnegative isotropic curvature is exactly \(F_\pm\ge0\). In a moving orthonormal frame the \(A\) equation is \((D_t-\Delta)A=2(A^2+2A^\#+BB^\top)\), and the other equation has \(B^\top B\). This coefficient uses the operator with unit-round curvature equal to the identity. In particular it gives the correct evolution \(K'=6K^2\) for a constant sectional curvature \(K\) in dimension four. If \(a\le b\le d\) are the eigenvalues and \(F=a+b\), taking the trace over a minimizing plane gives, in the support sense,
\[
 (\partial_t-\Delta)F\ge 2(a^2+b^2)+4dF+2\operatorname{tr}_E(BB^\top).
\]
The plane is extended by normal spatial and time-compatible orthonormal frames at the touching point. The minimum plane trace is concave as a function of the block, so this includes ties. The last term has the corresponding transpose in the other block. [Hamilton, J. Differential Geom. 24 (1986), §2, pp. 157–158 and §4](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-differential-geometry/volume-24/issue-2/Four-manifolds-with-positive-curvature-operator/10.4310/jdg/1214440433.pdf) · [Chen–Zhu, §3, (3.1)–(3.2)](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2014/0022/0005/CAG-2014-0022-0005-a002.pdf)

It follows that \(w=(-F)_+\) satisfies \((\partial_t-\Delta)w\le C U w\). One weak justification is to regularize the convex Lipschitz function \(A\mapsto(-F(A))_+\) by a rotation-invariant fibre mollifier. At differentiability points its reaction pairing is bounded by \(CUw\) by the minimum-trace calculation. Its convexity gives a nonpositive diffusion contribution for the regularized chain rule. On each individual smooth compact time slab the quadratic reaction is locally Lipschitz in the blocks, so the commutator with mollification goes to zero at that index. This gives the weak inequality and permits the \(w\)-energy test. With the evolving measure and \(-R\le1\), the estimate is
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_2^2+2\|dw\|_2^2
 \le C\|w\|_2^2+C\|v_i\|_2\|w\|_4^2.
\]
The same Sobolev and tail absorption applies. Initially \(F_\pm(h_i)\ge c_i/6-o(1)\) uniformly by the approximations. Thus \(\sup_t\|w(t)\|_2\to0\) for both signs. Equivalently one can propagate fibre distance to the closed convex cone. At the nearest-point projection the outward normal pairs nonpositively with the invariant ODE, and the quadratic reaction difference is bounded by a dimensional constant times \(U\) times the distance.

Pass to \(Y_i\to y>0\). The bounds on \(R_{h_i}\) and \(D_{h_i}\) give \(\|\operatorname{Ric}_{h_i}-(y/4)h_i\|_2\to0\). The E57 Einstein transport with \(\lambda(t)=\frac{y/4}{1-(y/2)t}\) therefore applies on a smaller interval. At a fixed interior positive time there are uniform curvature derivatives, comparison-ball noncollapse, injectivity lower bounds, and a diameter upper bound by packing and the volume upper bound from \(R\ge-1\). The resulting smooth closed limit is diffeomorphic to every sufficiently late source on the subsequence. It is positive Einstein and satisfies \(F_\pm\ge0\) by the \(L^2\) transports and smooth convergence at that positive time. All convergence used here takes place at positive times. [Shi, J. Differential Geom. 30 (1989), pp. 223–301](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214440432) · [Cheeger–Gromov–Taylor, Theorem 4.7(i)](https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/536.pdf#page=33) · [Hamilton, Amer. J. Math. 117 (1995), pp. 545–572](https://doi.org/10.2307/2375080)

## Recognition of the limit and the aggregate invariant

Let \(h\) be the smooth positive Einstein limit of volume \(V\) and scalar curvature \(R\). On a hypothetical nonstandard limit, \(Y([h])=R\sqrt V\). For a direct check, let \(\widehat h=u^2h\) be its smooth Yamabe minimizer. Since \(h=u^{-2}\widehat h\) is Einstein, the conformal traceless Ricci formula gives \(u Z_{\widehat h}=-2(\nabla^2_{\widehat h}u)_0\). Pair with \(Z_{\widehat h}\) and integrate. The constant scalar curvature of \(\widehat h\) makes \(Z_{\widehat h}\) divergence free, hence \(Z_{\widehat h}=0\) and the Hessian of \(u\) is pure trace. If \(u\) is nonconstant, taking divergence then subtracting its mean gives the Obata equation \(\nabla^2(u-\bar u)=-(\lambda_{\widehat h}/3)(u-\bar u)\widehat h\) with \(\lambda_{\widehat h}>0\). This identifies a round sphere by Obata's Theorem A and settles its type. In the remaining case \(u\) is constant, so the scalar quotient of \(h\) is the Yamabe constant. [Obata, J. Math. Soc. Japan 14 (1962), Theorem A, p. 334](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-the-mathematical-society-of-japan/volume-14/issue-3/Certain-conditions-for-a-Riemannian-manifold-to-be-isometric-with/10.2969/jmsj/01430333.pdf) · [Schoen, J. Differential Geom. 20 (1984), Theorem 2](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214439291)

Nonnegative isotropic curvature on \(h\) gives \(6 L_h\le R\) everywhere, hence \(36\int L_h^2\le R^2V=Y([h])^2\). Chen–Zhu Theorem 1.4 handles strict inequality. Theorem 1.6 handles equality and yields the same connected-sum alternatives or the conformal Fubini–Study and product exceptions. Simple connectivity excludes \(\mathbb{RP}^4\) and every cocompact cylindrical summand. The equality exceptions have nonzero \(b_2\), since a compact simply connected product of surfaces has both factors diffeomorphic to \(S^2\). This identifies the standard smooth type of the limit and contradicts the topology of the globally marked sources. [Chen–Zhu, Theorems 1.4 and 1.6, pp. 814–816](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2014/0022/0005/CAG-2014-0022-0005-a002.pdf)

For a single positive class on a hypothetical nonstandard homotopy sphere set \(P=4e+3\ell^2\). Signature gives both total chiral energies equal to \(e\). Gauss–Bonnet in the volume-one Yamabe gauge gives the exact identity and bounds
\[
 P-8T=D_g+(36\ell^2-Y^2)/12\ge0,\qquad
 \frac92e\le P\le8e.
\]
For the lower bound use \(|W^\pm|^2\le6L^2\) for either fixed sign. For the upper bound use \(L^2\le\frac23(|W^+|^2+|W^-|^2)\). Thus any contrary nonstandard sequence with \(P\to8T\) has \(\limsup e\le16T/9<2T\) and both initial defects vanishing. The limit argument excludes this sequence uniformly over varying smooth types. Consequently there is a universal \(\eta_P>0\) such that for every fixed \(0<\varepsilon_P<\eta_P\), the inclusive bound \(\inf_{Y>0}P\le8T+\varepsilon_P\) recognizes the standard type. The empty family has infinite infimum. The upper comparison \(P\le8e\) gives the ordinary inclusive infimum criterion for \(I^+\le T+\varepsilon_P/8\). The proof yields existential constants. No positive Yamabe entry is supplied for a smooth type without one.

## Weaker curvature-tail premise

The Einstein limit in the preceding recognition argument uses the vanishing initial traceless Ricci defect. A distinct sufficient recognition premise retains \(\Delta_i\to0\) in the strict chiral band and replaces the traceless Ricci defect assumption by asymptotic \(L^2\) uniform integrability of \(U_{q_i}\), in the precise form
\[
 \lim_{K\to\infty}\limsup_i\|(U_{q_i}-K)_+\|_{2,q_i}=0.
\]
It suffices to assume the corresponding tail statement for \(|\operatorname{Ric}_{0,q_i}|\), by the Weyl and scalar bounds above. Initial smooth approximations preserve this condition, choosing errors in the norm and volume density separately at each index. The key point is that the E57 smallness threshold for the fixed-cutoff tail can be taken independent of \(K\) at the expense of a shorter fixed horizon.

Indeed, take \(K\ge1\) and \(t_*=c/(1+K)\) so \(\phi\le2K\). Before absorption the weighted Sobolev constants depend on the common initial inhomogeneous bound, scalar lower bound and a time horizon at most one. After absorption keep the coefficients separate, \(\|f\|_4^2\le A_1\|df\|_2^2+B_K\|f\|_2^2\), with \(A_1\) independent of \(K\) and \(B_K\le C(1+K)\). The cubic term in the truncated-curvature energy estimate and the potential term for \(w\) use a cutoff-independent gradient absorption threshold. The remaining Gronwall coefficient is at most \(C(1+K)\), with a uniform growth factor on this horizon. Ball noncollapse holds with a cutoff-independent constant for \(r\le(1+K)^{-1/2}\), because the dyadic cutoff bound uses \(A_1+B_Kr^2\le C\). At a high record \(t\max U\), the record curvature is at least a fixed multiple of \(1+K\). The record argument therefore uses just these admissible radii, giving a uniform positive lower bound on the squared tail energy if the record is too high. Taking the initial tail sufficiently small relative to those uniform bootstrap and record constants proves common existence and positive-time compactness. Vanishing \(w_i(0)\) still yields vanishing \(w_i(t)\). Only a sufficiently small fixed-cutoff tail is used in this version, and the assumed uniform integrability lets one choose that cutoff. The resulting limit is nonnegative isotropic.

For completeness, the nonnegative-isotropic homotopy-sphere limit has a strict positive-isotropic metric by the E57 restart argument. Restart its smooth Ricci flow and use the preceding minimum-trace inequality to preserve both \(F\ge0\). Scalar curvature is nonnegative and initially somewhere positive, since if it vanished identically, both Weyl blocks would vanish and Gauss–Bonnet would contradict \(\chi=2\). Scalar curvature is therefore positive at positive restart times. A zero of \(F\) at such a time forces \(F\equiv0\) throughout earlier positive times by the strong maximum principle and then \(a=b=0\) by the squares in the trace inequality. That block has rank one with positive remaining eigenvalue \(R/4\). For a local kernel section \(\xi\), differentiate \(A\xi=0\) and apply the block equation to \(\langle A\xi,\xi\rangle=0\). The resulting equality is \(0=2|B^\top\xi|^2+2\langle A\nabla\xi,\nabla\xi\rangle\), so the kernel and orthogonal eigenline are parallel. The same reasoning applies to the other block. On a simply connected manifold the parallel line supplies a nonzero parallel harmonic two-form, contradicting \(b_2=0\). Thus both chiral sums are strictly positive at positive restart times. Chen–Tang–Zhu's Main Theorem identifies the standard type under simple connectivity.
