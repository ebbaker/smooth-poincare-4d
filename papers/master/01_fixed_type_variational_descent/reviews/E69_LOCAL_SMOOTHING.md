## Initial metrics and source hypotheses

Take the volume-one modified metrics $q_i$ on closed oriented smooth homotopy four-spheres with $e_i\le 2T-\delta$, $T=4\pi^2$, fixed $\delta>0$, and $36\sigma_i-Y_i^2\to0$. Here $\sigma_i=\int L_{q_i}^2\,dV_{q_i}$ and $L$ is the larger of the two top eigenvalues of the Weyl blocks. Gauss–Bonnet in the physical Yamabe gauge and the sharp upper Yamabe bound give $0<\sqrt{48\delta}\le Y_i\le S$. The modified equation is $R_{q_i}-6L_{q_i}=c_i$, with $Y_i\le c_i+6\int L_{q_i}\le c_i+6\sqrt{\sigma_i}$ and $c_i\le0$. Consequently
\[
 6\sqrt{\sigma_i}-Y_i\to0,\qquad c_i\to0,\qquad
 \|L_{q_i}-\sqrt{\sigma_i}\|_2+\|R_{q_i}-Y_i\|_2\to0.
\]
Indeed, the squared norm of $L_{q_i}-\sqrt{\sigma_i}$ is $2\sqrt{\sigma_i}(\sqrt{\sigma_i}-\int L_{q_i})$. The statements hold along the full sequence, even if $Y_i$ varies. The ordinary Yamabe inequality and Hölder give a uniform inhomogeneous Sobolev bound since the scalar error can be absorbed using the positive lower bound for $Y_i$. The modified equation also bounds both negative isotropic pair sums pointwise by $|c_i|/6$.

Choose smooth positive conformal approximations $h_i$ with volume one separately at each index. The $C^2$ errors can make the distance distortion multiplicative $1+o(1)$, the curvature-square measures close in total variation by $o(1)$, and the scalar and isotropic pointwise errors $o(1)$. The upper volume ratios of $q_i$ give uniform upper ratios for $h_i$ at radii up to $r_0/2$ by metric comparison and the corresponding measure densities. The initial scalar lower bound $-1$ then holds at all late indices. Suppose at some fixed $0<r<\min(1,r_0/2)$ an infinite subsequence has $\|\mathrm{Rm}_{h_i}\|_{L^2(B_{h_i}(x,r))}\le\epsilon$ for every center. On $r^{-2}h_i$, the full tensor curvature $L^2$ norm is unchanged, the zero-order coefficient in the initial Sobolev inequality is multiplied by $r^2$, and the upper volume ratios through unit scale are uniform.

Lee–Lee, Theorem 1.1(a–c), applies to the rescaled metrics with initial upper volume ratios through radius one, inhomogeneous Sobolev for compactly supported unit-ball tests and unit-ball curvature $L^2$ at most $\epsilon\le\delta_1$. On each compact source take $\bar r$ larger than the rescaled diameter, so all the balls specifying the domain are the whole source and are relatively compact there. The constants depend on dimension, initial upper volume ratio and Sobolev bounds, and apply uniformly over $\bar r$. The flow is smooth on each closed source from its own initial time. In dimension four, (1) bounds curvature at fixed positive time, (2) gives $\operatorname{inj}\ge\sqrt{t/\Lambda_1}$, and (3) bounds the evolving radius-$1/2$ curvature $L^2$ norm by $\Lambda_1(\epsilon+\sqrt t)$. Restrict time to $0<t\le t_*=\min(\hat T,1,\epsilon^2)$. Then the local potential norms are at most $2\Lambda_1\epsilon$. The theorem also supplies the localized entropy lower bound in (4). The transport below uses the scalar-weighted Sobolev inequality from the initial global bound on the closed source. I checked the published statement and arXiv v2, revised 1 June 2026. ## Scalar-weighted Sobolev and transport down to zero

For $Q_t(f)=\int(|df|^2+Rf^2/4)dV_t$ and $\|f\|_{2,t}=1$, Jensen and the initial Sobolev inequality give
\[
 \operatorname{Ent}_0(f)\le 2\log\|f\|_{4,0}^2
 \le 2\log[S_0(Q_0(f)+5/4)].
\]
The bound $R\ge-1$ persists and does not involve the rescaled volume. The logarithm tangent inequality gives the initial entropy bound $\operatorname{Ent}_0(f)\le sQ_0(f)-2\log s+C_0+5s/4$. Perelman's monotonicity compares the time-$t$ entropy constant with that at initial parameter $s+4t$, so for $0<s<1$ the last two terms are bounded uniformly. Ye's Theorem 5.5 with potential $R/4$, dimension and exponent parameter both four, now gives $\|f\|_{4,t}^2\le A Q_t(f)+B\|f\|_{2,t}^2$. Its constants depend on the lower bound for the potential and the uniform logarithmic inequality, so are independent of $r,i,t$. The initial argument uses $L^2$ normalization and accommodates the rescaled volume $r^{-4}$. [Perelman, §3, (3.1)–(3.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0211159#page=8) · [Ye, arXiv:0707.2424, Theorem 5.5, (5.17)–(5.18)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0707.2424#page=22) · [E57r entropy review](E57_CRITICAL_SMOOTHING.md#initial-metrics-and-entropy-transport)

Write $U=|\mathrm{Rm}|_{\rm tens}$. For tests supported in an evolving radius-$1/2$ ball, $R_+\le c U$ and Hölder allow absorption of the scalar term using $2\Lambda_1\epsilon$. This gives a local ordinary Sobolev bound with constant $S_1$ independent of $r,i,t$, for $\epsilon$ below a single threshold. A cutoff on each ball of radius $0<s\le1/4$, equal to one on the half-ball and with derivative norm at most $2/s$, gives $m(s)\ge b\sqrt{m(s/2)}$ for $m(s)=s^{-4}\mathrm{Vol}\,B_s$. Iteration uses $m(s)\to\omega_4$ separately for each smooth slice and shows $\mathrm{Vol}\,B_s\ge b_1s^4$ uniformly. Since $\partial_t dV_t=-R\,dV_t$, the total rescaled volume is at most $e^{t_*}r^{-4}$.

At each positive time, a maximal $1/8$-separated net thus has at most $N(r)$ centers by its disjoint radius-$1/16$ balls. Take cutoffs with support within radius $1/4$, equal to one inside radius $1/8$, and divide each by the square root of their squared sum. The resulting Lipschitz functions satisfy $\sum_j\chi_j^2=1$ and $\sum_j|d\chi_j|^2\le C(r)$ almost everywhere. Expansion at each fixed time gives
\[
 \sum_j\|d(\chi_j f)\|_2^2
 =\|df\|_2^2+\int f^2\sum_j|d\chi_j|^2\,dV_t,
\]
because $\sum_j\chi_jd\chi_j=0$. Applying the local potential bound to each term $\chi_j f$ proves
\[
 \int U f^2\,dV_t
 \le 2\Lambda_1\epsilon S_1
      \left(\|df\|_2^2+(1+C(r))\|f\|_2^2\right).
\]
The net cardinality enters only the lower-order term. The cutoffs define a spatial quadratic form and need no time derivative or choice across time slices.

In the evolving orthonormal bundle the smaller two eigenvalues $a,b$ of each chiral block $W^\pm+(R/12)\operatorname{Id}$ give $F_\pm=a+b=R/6-\lambda_{\max}(W^\pm)$. The sum of the two eigenvalue reactions has a lower bound $2(a^2+b^2)+4d(a+b)$ with $d$ the remaining eigenvalue, by dropping the nonnegative off-diagonal-square term. The convex negative parts $w=(-F_\pm)_+$ then satisfy $(\partial_t-\Delta)w\le C U w$ weakly, including at tied eigenvalues, as in E62. Testing against $2w$ and adding the volume variation gives
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\|w\|_2^2+2\|dw\|_2^2\le
 2C\int Uw^2\,dV_t+\|w\|_2^2 .
\]
Choose $\epsilon>0$ below the source, scalar absorption and gradient absorption thresholds, independently of $r$. Then Gronwall gives $\|w(t)\|_2^2\le e^{C'(r)t}\|w(0)\|_2^2=o_i(1)$ at the fixed $r$. The negative parts have scale-invariant squared integrals in dimension four and start with vanishing norms. Smoothness for each source at $t=0$ permits integrating first from positive times and taking the individual time down to zero. The positive coefficient $C'(r)$ is uniform for this limit and for the subsequent limit in $i$. ## Recognition and the order of the ball selection

At $t_*/2$, Lee–Lee gives uniform bounded curvature and positive injectivity radius on the entire sources. Positive-time derivative bounds follow by applying Shi on an interior time interval. The lower volumes on fixed-radius balls and bounded total volume bound diameter by packing along a minimizing geodesic. Thus a subsequence converges smoothly to a closed metric under whole-manifold diffeomorphisms. Its two $w$ vanish by their integrated bounds. The limit is a smooth homotopy sphere with nonnegative isotropic curvature. The inherited short smooth restart argument makes both pair sums strictly positive. In that argument $R\ge0$ and $R\equiv0$ would force $W=0$ and violate Gauss–Bonnet. At positive times $R>0$ and vanishing of either pair sum at an interior point forces it to vanish at earlier positive times by the strong minimum principle. The nonnegative terms in its reaction then force $a=b=0$ on that time interval. Testing the block equation on its kernel gives a parallel rank-one eigenline, hence a nonzero parallel two-form by simple connectivity, contradicting $b_2=0$. Chen–Tang–Zhu's differentiable classification recognizes the resulting compact simply connected positive-isotropic-curvature four-manifold as $S^4$, identifying the late sources via the diffeomorphisms. [E62 direct recognition argument](../notes/E62_ISOTROPIC_FLOW.md#when-the-curvature-tail-is-supplied-directly) · [Chen–Tang–Zhu, Main Theorem and Corollary 1](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1343133700)

This contradiction on every contrary subsequence yields an all-late statement for each fixed $r$. Some ball has $L^2$ curvature norm above the same $\epsilon$. Take a decreasing sequence $r_j\to0$ and increasing source-index thresholds for those statements, then choose $r_j$ on each corresponding index interval. The contradiction uses a fixed $r$ at a time, so the growth of $C'(r)$ as $r\downarrow0$ is immaterial. Enlarge the selected balls by the relative distance error and transfer them to $q_i$. Each retains squared full tensor curvature at least $\epsilon^2-o(1)$, and has volume tending to zero by the assumed upper ratios. The modified spectral and scalar norm estimates give vanishing squared Weyl and scalar integrals there. In the full four-index decomposition the Ricci term is $2|Z|^2$. Thus any sufficiently small fixed $c>0$, for instance $\epsilon^2/8$, is a valid late lower bound for the Ricci charge. The inherited peak-scale estimate implies $a_i\sqrt{\max|\mathrm{Rm}_{q_i}|_{\rm tens}}\to\infty$ on these balls.

The added upper ratios concern only the modified $q_i$ distances and volumes. The argument leaves their derivation and the smooth marked geometry at the selected intermediate scale open.
