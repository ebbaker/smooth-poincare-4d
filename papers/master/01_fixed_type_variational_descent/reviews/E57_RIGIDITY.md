## Initial data and transport

The endpoint calculation is per class through its auxiliary curvature-density estimate. The physical identity $Y_i^2=384\pi^2-48e_i+12D_i$ and $Y_i^2<36\|L_i\|_2^2=48e_i-36B_i$ give $D_i+3B_i<8(e_i-T)$. The classifications give this strict inequality on each nonstandard type. Simple connectivity excludes any extra connected-sum factor in the classifications. At equality, $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and a compact simply connected product of surfaces have nonzero second homology. The modified Yamabe constant satisfies $Y_i-6\|L_i\|_2\le c_i\le0$. Its volume-one $C^{2,\alpha}$ representative satisfies $R_i=c_i+6L_i\ge c_i$. The integral of $L_i$ approaches its $L^2$ norm, so $R_i\to y_*=8\sqrt3\,\pi$ in $L^2$. Gauss–Bonnet gives $\|Z_i\|_2\to0$, and the chiral density computation gives $\||\mathrm{Rm}_i|_{\mathrm{tens}}^2-64\pi^2\|_1\to0$. Smooth positive approximations of the conformal factors in $C^2$, normalized back to volume one, preserve these estimates index by index and give $R_i\ge-1$. The bound $(|\mathrm{Rm}_i|_{\mathrm{tens}}-8\pi)_+^2\le \big||\mathrm{Rm}_i|_{\mathrm{tens}}^2-64\pi^2\big|$ gives the initial tail. [Chen–Zhu, Theorems 1.4, 1.6, 1.7, §2 and proof of Theorem 1.4](https://www.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2014/0022/0005/CAG-2014-0022-0005-a002.pdf)

Let $P_i=\mathrm{Ric}_i-\lambda(s)g_i(s)$ with $\lambda_0=y_*/4$, $\lambda'=2\lambda^2$, and a common time interval before its pole. The coordinate Ricci evolution has a $-2\mathrm{Ric}^2$ term. Passing to a frame satisfying $\partial_s e_a=\mathrm{Ric}^{\sharp}e_a$ cancels it. Using $\mathrm{Rm}(g)=\mathrm{Ric}$ for the curvature contraction yields $(\partial_s-\Delta)P_i=2\mathrm{Rm}(P_i)+2\lambda P_i$ in that frame. The minimum scalar lower bound persists along the flow. With $U_i=|\mathrm{Rm}_i|_{\mathrm{tens}}\le K+v_i$ and $\sup_s\|v_i(s)\|_2=o(1)$, integration gives

$$
\frac{d}{ds}\|P_i\|_2^2+2\|\nabla P_i\|_2^2
\le C\|P_i\|_2^2+C\|v_i\|_2\|P_i\|_4^2.
$$

Kato's inequality and the uniform inhomogeneous Sobolev inequality absorb the last gradient term. Gronwall and $\|P_i(0)\|_2=o(1)$ give $\sup_s\|P_i(s)\|_2=o(1)$. This step uses constants uniform through time zero. [Hamilton, 1982, Corollaries 7.3 and 7.5](https://math.cs.kitami-it.ac.jp/~kouno/paper/Hamilton1982.pdf)

The evolving measure satisfies $V_i'=-\int R_i$ and $V_i(s)\le e^s$. Since $R_i-4\lambda=\operatorname{tr}P_i$, its $L^2$ norm is at most $2\|P_i\|_2$. Thus $V_i'+4\lambda V_i=o(1)$ uniformly. The initial value $V_i(0)=1$ gives $V_i(s)\to(1-2\lambda_0 s)^2$. Expanding the square gives $\int R_i^2\to16\lambda_0^2=y_*^2$, and $Z_i=P_i-\tfrac14(\operatorname{tr}P_i)g_i$ tends to zero in $L^2$. Signature and Gauss–Bonnet therefore give $2E_+(g_i(s))=16\pi^2-\int R_i^2/24+\int |Z_i|^2/2\to2T$.

## Marking and the uniform conclusion

At fixed positive time the uniform Sobolev inequality applied to a cutoff from radius $r$ to $r/2$ gives $m_i(r)\ge c\sqrt{m_i(r/2)}$ for $m_i(r)=r^{-4}\operatorname{Vol} B_i(x,r)$ and $0<r\le1$. Iteration with the Euclidean small-ball limit gives $m_i(r)\ge c^2$. Packing disjoint fixed-radius balls along minimizing geodesics bounds diameter using $V_i\le e^s$. The two-sided sectional bounds and fixed small-ball volume then give injectivity at every point by setting the curvature and volume basepoints equal in Theorem 4.7(i). [Cheeger–Gromov–Taylor, printed p. 47](https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/536.pdf#page=33)

Uniform bounds on all curvature derivatives and the injectivity lower bound give smooth pointed metric compactness. The complete pointed limit is compact by the diameter bound. Indeed, embeddings of a domain containing a closed limit ball of radius $D+1$ would otherwise produce a point at source distance $>D$ from the basepoint, as any shortcut leaving the domain must first cross the image of that ball's boundary. Once the limit is compact, the embeddings on its entirety are surjective by openness and compactness in the connected sources. Thus the limit is smoothly diffeomorphic to every sufficiently late source on the subsequence. Hamilton's survey states the marked static theorem with bounds on curvature derivatives on every fixed ball and injectivity at the basepoint. Andrews–Hopper explicitly states completeness of the limit for the globally bounded geometry hypotheses used here. [Hamilton, *The formation of singularities in the Ricci flow*, Theorem 16.1, printed p. 59](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/sdg/1993/0002/0001/SDG-1993-0002-0001-a002.pdf) · [Andrews–Hopper, Definition 8.4 and Theorem 8.8](https://maths-people.anu.edu.au/~andrews/book.pdf)

The global smooth limit has $\mathrm{Ric}=\lambda(s)g$, $R=4\lambda(s)>0$, and $E_+=E_-=T$. Its conformal Yamabe constant is positive by the scalar lower bound and the Sobolev inequality on the closed limit. In Chang–Gursky–Yang's full tensor norm, $\int|W|^2=4(E_++E_-)=32\pi^2=16\pi^2\chi$. Theorem B applies at equality to the remaining types. The Fubini–Study alternative has $H_2\ne0$. The alternative covered by $S^3\times S^1$ has nontrivial fundamental group by the injection induced by a covering. Simple connectivity also excludes $\mathbb{RP}^4$. This forces the standard smooth type and contradicts the global source markings. [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A and Remarks 1–2, p. 107, and Theorem B, p. 108](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf)

If there were no uniform $\delta>0$ excluding nonstandard positive classes with $e<T+\delta$, choosing one such class for each $\delta=1/i$ would give the sequence just ruled out. Every nonstandard type consequently has $I^+\ge T+\delta$, including the empty positive family with $I^+=+\infty$. Taking $\epsilon=\delta/2$ gives recognition under either $e\le T+\epsilon$ or $I^+\le T+\epsilon$.
