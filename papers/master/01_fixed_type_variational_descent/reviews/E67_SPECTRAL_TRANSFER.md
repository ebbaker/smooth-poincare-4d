## Transfer without a minimizing hypothesis

Let \((M_i,g_i)\) be smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian four-manifolds, possibly varying, with positive Yamabe constants \(Y_i\) and \(\limsup_iY_i>0\). In the intended applications \(M_i\) are homotopy spheres with unit-volume physical Yamabe representatives, \(e_i=E_+(g_i)=E_-(g_i)\le 2T-\delta\), \(T=4\pi^2\), and \(Y_i\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\). Keep the Weyl-operator Hilbert–Schmidt convention, with source chiral measures \(\lambda_i^\pm=|W_{g_i}^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2dV_{g_i}\), and write
\[
 L_g=\max\{\lambda_{\max}(W_g^+),\lambda_{\max}(W_g^-)\},
 \qquad \sigma(g)=\int L_g^2\,dV_g,
 \qquad \Delta(g)=36\sigma(g)-Y([g])^2.
\]
Let \(X\) be a smooth closed oriented filled four-manifold with finite set of ordinary points \(P\). Assume oriented smooth embeddings of neighborhoods of a compact exhaustion of \(X\setminus P\) into the late sources. On each fixed compared compact assume the source pullbacks have comparison representatives \(m_i\) with local uniform and strong \(W^{1,4}\) convergence to a smooth metric \(h\) and a local \(L^2\) full-curvature bound in the comparison gauge. Assume the limiting conformal class fills smoothly, either because \(h\) already extends to \(X\) or because \(h\) is smoothly conformal to the restriction of a smooth filled representative \(\bar h\). The conformal multipliers can vary with the source index and need only be smooth positive on the corresponding compared neighborhoods.

On a fixed oriented coordinate chart, let \(F_\pm(m_i)\) be the real symmetric trace-free Weyl block matrices in the oriented orthonormalized coordinate frame multiplied by \((\det m_i)^{1/4}\), relative to common coordinate measure \(dx\). The lower-index curvature has a constant-coefficient linear principal term in \(D^2m_i\) and a term quadratic in \(Dm_i\) with smooth metric coefficients. The two terms converge distributionally and strongly in \(L^2\), respectively. Local uniform ellipticity and the curvature bound promote the first distributional conclusion to weak \(L^2\) convergence of the full lower-index curvature. The chiral projections and volume factors are uniformly converging algebraic maps, so \((F_+(m_i),F_-(m_i))\rightharpoonup(F_+(h),F_-(h))\) in local \(L^2\).

For a pair of real symmetric trace-free matrices \(F=(F_+,F_-)\) the function \(L(F)=\max_\pm\lambda_{\max}(F_\pm)\) is the supremum of its Rayleigh linear functionals and is nonnegative. Its square is therefore convex, and \(L(F)^2\le C|F|^2\) implies the usual weak lower semicontinuity of its integral in \(L^2\). In detail, \(L\) is Lipschitz in the matrix norm and \(L^2\) satisfies the corresponding difference bound \(C(|F|+|F'|)|F-F'|\), so the integral is norm-continuous and convex. Nonnegative compactly supported chart weights preserve those properties. Applying a finite partition of a fixed cutoff, bounded by one, gives
\[
 \int_{X\setminus P}\chi L_h^2\,dV_h\le \liminf_i \sigma(g_i).
\]
Indeed \(L(F(m_i))^2dx=L_{m_i}^2\,dV_{m_i}\), a conformally invariant measure, and the common marked embedding carries each weighted integral into the actual source once. Exhaust the punctured manifold after these fixed-compact tests. The finite fill set is null in the smooth representative, giving \(\sigma(\bar h)\le\liminf_i\sigma(g_i)\). The same argument works for positive compact weights and needs no equality in the total Weyl budget.

For \(f\in C_c^\infty(X\setminus P)\), conformal covariance transfers the Yamabe inequality to \(m_i\), with \(Y_i\|f\|_{4,m_i}^2\le Q_{m_i}(f)\). The corresponding source test on the marked image is smooth and compactly supported because it uses the reciprocal of the local positive conformal multiplier. Gradient and denominator terms pass by uniform convergence. The scalar density is a coordinate divergence linear in first derivatives with smooth coefficient dependence on the metric, plus a quadratic first-derivative term. Testing it against the fixed smooth \(f^2\) passes by strong \(W^{1,4}\) and uniform convergence. This gives \((\limsup_iY_i)\|f\|_{4,h}^2\le Q_h(f)\). Move fixed compact tests conformally to \(\bar h\) if necessary. A smooth cutoff removing the ball of radius \(r\) about one ordinary point has \(L^2\) gradient square \(O(r^2)\). Multiplying any bounded smooth test by the product of these cutoffs therefore approximates it in \(H^1\) and \(L^4\); smooth density gives the filled Yamabe inequality on all tests. Thus \(Y([\bar h])\ge\limsup_iY_i>0\). The argument uses a finite set of ordinary smooth fills; a singular or orbifold compactification would need an appropriate test-space and classification result. [Lee–Parker, §2, (2.7)](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/lee-parker.yamabe.pdf)

Combining the bounds, with positive Yamabe constants, yields
\[
 \Delta(\bar h)=36\sigma(\bar h)-Y([\bar h])^2
 \le 36\liminf_i\sigma(g_i)-(\limsup_iY_i)^2
 \le\liminf_i\Delta(g_i).
\]
The inequality goes in this direction even when the source limits of \(Y_i\) and \(\sigma(g_i)\) are not taken on a common subsequence. In particular \(\Delta(g_i)\to0\) gives \(36\sigma(\bar h)\le Y([\bar h])^2\). If \(X\) is a homotopy four-sphere, Chen–Zhu Theorem 1.4 gives the differentiable classification at strict inequality and Theorem 1.6 at equality. Simple connectivity excludes the nontrivial projective or cylindrical connected-sum factors. The equality exceptions have nonzero second homology: the Fubini–Study alternative is \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), and a product in the simply connected compact cover must be a product of two spheres. Thus \(X\) is diffeomorphic to the standard four-sphere. A nonstandard smooth filled homotopy sphere would instead force a strictly positive lower limit of the source spectral deficit. These conclusions concern the type of the filled base even with zero chiral energy. A standard filled base can still have a nonround positive-energy metric without minimizing hypotheses. If there are no fill points, the whole closed marking is surjective by openness, compactness and connectedness, so the source types are then identified as well. [Chen–Zhu, Comm. Anal. Geom. 22 (2014), Theorems 1.4 and 1.6, pp. 814–816](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2014/0022/0005/CAG-2014-0022-0005-a002.pdf)

## Fixed-type restart consequence

Assume now that all sources lie on one hypothetical nonstandard oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere \(M\), with \(e_i\to I^+(M)=I<2T\), a fixed strict energy margin, \(\Delta(g_i)\to0\), and the full hypotheses of the inherited whole-base comparison. That comparison uses minimizing energy and the actual whole-truncation markings to get strong local chiral convergence, to install the smooth limit near each marked face, and to cap on a slow diagonal with vanishing splices. There is one distinct exterior component per face by Mayer–Vietoris. If \(E_+(\bar h;X)>0\), its two-sided energy accounting would identify \(X\) with \(M\) and attain the infimum on the filled base. The spectral transfer instead identifies this \(X\) as standard. Consequently the positive-energy base is excluded.

The only filled-base case remaining on this sequence is \(E_+(\bar h;X)=E_-(\bar h;X)=0\) by signature. Both Weyl blocks then vanish identically. The smooth simply connected filled conformal structure develops onto the round sphere: the developing map is a local diffeomorphism, and compactness makes it a covering of the simply connected sphere. The fixed-type whole-base theorem gives a unique actual raw exterior \(V_i\) carrying all limiting energies \(I>0\) in both signs, after a subsequence and the stipulated slow exhaustion. Two positive exits would give a prohibited two-sided cut. Its cap is positive on \(M\) and has energy tending to \(I\). The raw spectral mass is likewise all in this exit, since
\[
 \sigma(g_i;M\setminus V_i)\le\tfrac23(\lambda_i^++\lambda_i^-)(M\setminus V_i)\longrightarrow0.
\]
This deduction uses the actual exterior in the whole-base theorem. Local weak convergence or knowledge of the filled type alone supplies no such exit. [Kuiper, *On conformally-flat spaces in the large*, Ann. Math. 50 (1949), 916–924](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969587) · [Inherited whole-base comparison](../../manuscript/variational_comparison.tex)

The actual round-case splices also preserve \(\sigma\) up to \(o(1)\). The conformal class off the splice is retained, the added exact cap has zero Weyl curvature, and the changed bands have vanishing incoming and outgoing two-sign Weyl energy on the slow diagonal. The latter uses strong local chiral comparison on the incoming fixed annuli and the separate small Euclidean-normal splice on the installed round metric. The same pointwise spectral bound controls both band integrals, giving \(\sigma(c_i)=\sigma(g_i)+o(1)\) for the cap \(c_i\). A signed bound for the total chiral surcharge by itself would leave the spectral change under general exterior alterations uncontrolled.

If the additional long-retained-buffer Yamabe estimate of E21 is supplied for these same caps, then \(\liminf_iY([c_i])\ge y\) along a subsequence with \(Y_i\to y>0\). The nonstandard cap type has \(36\sigma(c_i)-Y([c_i])^2>0\) by Chen–Zhu. Since \(36\sigma(c_i)\to y^2\), these bounds give \(Y([c_i])\to y\) and \(\Delta(c_i)\to0\). This is persistence of the spectral regime under that restart. The signed chiral gain still requires a strict comparison of the discarded mass and the actual surcharges. The inherited positivity bound from the scalar-flat wall alone gives a positive lower bound for the cap constants, which can be weaker than the needed \(y\). ## Smooth compactification of an exact bubble

Suppose a marked complete exact bubble has a smooth conformal compactification \((X,\bar h)\) by finitely many ordinary points and the same exhaustion convergence. The transfer applies to its noncompact metric on fixed compacts and hence to the filled conformal class. If the filled manifold is already identified as the original nonstandard smooth type, the spectral transfer directly excludes it. If it is only known to be a homotopy four-sphere, the fixed-type minimizing whole-base theorem identifies the type whenever the bubble has positive chiral energy. That alternative is excluded as above. For a scalar-flat physical bubble in the already established one-Euclidean-end case, the complete-limit identity \(\int|\operatorname{Ric}|^2=4E_+(\bar h;X)\) makes nonflatness imply positive chiral energy. A second check uses the round class conclusion directly. If the filled conformal class is round and has just one puncture, stereographic projection makes its scalar-flat metric \(u^2\delta_{\rm Euc}\) on all of \(\mathbb R^4\) for a positive harmonic function \(u\). Liouville's theorem makes \(u\) constant. [Complete physical limit and compactification argument](../../manuscript/complete_limits.tex)

With several punctures, nonflatness alone does not force positive Weyl energy. The scalar-flat metric \((1+a|x|^{-2})^2\delta_{\rm Euc}\), \(a>0\), on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\}\) is complete, nonflat and conformally flat with a round two-point compactification. In the strict physical curvature-budget setting its two-ended analogue is excluded independently by the marked Gauss–Bonnet and Yamabe comparison. The E67 argument adds no extraction of whole markings, no automatic filling of a quotient end, and no termination of the single-exit restart. [E64 marked end budget](E64_REPAIR_AND_RICCI.md)
