\section{Concentration at a physical energy radius}\label{sec:energy-radius}

A complete comparison obtained by magnifying the physical metric has
more structure than a collection of unrelated annular comparisons.
We first determine its ends, then test its entire interior and exterior
on the original smooth type.

\begin{theorem}[Complete physical limits]\label{thm:complete-limit}
Let \(M\) and \(g_i\) be as in Theorem~\ref{thm:local-min}, with \(M\)
nonstandard. Suppose \(a_i\downarrow0\) and a connected, complete,
boundaryless, smooth oriented Riemannian four-manifold \((X,h)\)
admits oriented smooth embeddings of neighborhoods of a compact
exhaustion into \(M\), with
\(\Phi_i^*(a_i^{-2}g_i)\to h\) locally uniformly and strongly in
\(W^{1,4}\). Then \(h\) is flat.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Pass to \(Y_i\to y>0\), and put \(e=I^+(M)\), \(S=8\sqrt6\,\pi\).
On each compact the full curvature integral of the rescaled
physical metric has the source bound. The weak curvature limit
has scalar curvature zero since the rescaled scalars are \(a_i^2Y_i\).
Theorem~\ref{thm:local-min} gives \(B_h=0\) and strong local
convergence of both chiral energy densities.
Transporting smooth compactly supported Yamabe tests gives
\(6\|df\|_2^2\ge y\|f\|_4^2\) on \(X\), by the scalar rescaling
and uniform convergence there. Hence \(X\) is noncompact. Weak
lower semicontinuity on fixed compacts followed by exhaustion gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:limit-ric-budget}
 \int_X|\Rm_h|^2<\infty,\qquad d:=\int_X|\Ric_h|^2
 \le 4e+y^2/12-32\pi^2 .
\end{equation}
The second estimate uses the physical trace-free Ricci tensors in
\eqref{eq:budgets}. Integrated tensor norms here use \(h\).

The homogeneous Sobolev inequality implies
\(\operatorname{Vol}_h B_s(x)\ge c(y)s^4\) at every radius.
Indeed a cutoff of slope \(2/s\) yields
\(yV(s/2)^{1/2}\le24s^{-2}V(s)\). Iteration of this inequality
for \(V(s)/s^4\) uses the Euclidean small-ball limit at \(x\).
The scalar-flat Bach equation has the form
\(\Delta\Ric=\Rm*\Ric\) \cite[\S2.1]{TianViaclovsky2004Moduli}.
Apply critical-metric interior regularity on a ball of radius
\(r/2\) centered at distance \(r\) from a fixed origin. Its
curvature integral is bounded by the tail beyond \(r/2\).
Thus \(r^2\sup_{\partial B_r}|\Rm_h|\to0\).
The finite Sobolev constant, this decay and
\(\int|\Ric_-|^2<\infty\) give finitely many asymptotically locally
Euclidean (ALE) ends of order zero
\cite[Theorems 1.2 and 1.1]{TianViaclovsky2008Volume}.
Scalar-flat obstruction regularity improves the end order to two
in a suitable chart \cite[Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2]{AcheViaclovsky2011Obstruction}.
All assertions here concern the exact limit equation.

We give the topology and energy argument for these ends. At large
radius \(R\) an end is modeled on a Euclidean annulus modulo a finite
group \(\Gamma\) acting freely on the unit sphere. Rescale this
annulus to unit size in its end coordinates. On any fixed short
logarithmic interval about \(R\), the limiting metric differs from
the quotient flat metric in \(C^2\) by \(O(R^{-2})\). At fixed \(R\)
the same marked source has an additional \(o_i(1;R)\) first-order
error. The energy estimate for a radial cutoff in log radius descends in rotated Euclidean frames
on the finite cover. The full curvature integral of the pulled-back, twice rescaled
source on the splice is bounded by its physical total in dimension four. It produces one closed metric on
\(M\) exactly product near this link with energy
\(e_i+O(R^{-2})+o_i(1;R)<9\pi^2\), for \(R\) large and then \(i\)
large. Extending the radial multiplier away from the splice preserves
both Weyl integrals there.

For clarity, a spherical space form embedded in an integral homology
four-sphere has, in the finite group case, group \(1,Q_8\) or the
binary icosahedral group \cite[Theorem 5.2(1)]{Hillman2024LocallyFlat}.
The link separates. Each side has \(H_2=0\) by Mayer--Vietoris
and thus signature zero. In the icosahedral case the homology
sphere bounds the even \(E_8\) plumbing up to orientation
\cite{KirbyScharlemann1979Poincare}. Each side is spin, and gluing
the plumbing uses the unique boundary spin structure. The resulting
closed smooth spin signature \(\pm8\) contradicts Rokhlin divisibility
\cite{Rokhlin1971Submanifolds}. For \(Q_8\) a free orthogonal
action is quaternionic up to orientation: the central involution is
\(-1\), and two generators yield an orthonormal quaternion basis.
The signature eta invariant on even forms has absolute value \(3/4\).
Indeed each cyclic subgroup of order four gives \(-1/2\) in the
quaternion orientation by the cotangent formula, while their common
order-two subgroup gives zero. Averaging gives
\(8\eta=3\cdot4(-1/2)\)
\cite[Proposition 2.12]{APS1975SpectralII}. On a product boundary
the signature formula gives opposite values \(\pm9\pi^2\) for
\(E_+-E_-\) on the two sides \cite[Theorem 4.14]{APS1975SpectralI}.
The positive value contradicts the global \(E_+<9\pi^2\).
Every end therefore has trivial group.

Take a whole truncated core with \(k\) spherical faces. Connectedness
and Mayer--Vietoris in degrees zero and one give one complementary
component per face in the source. Van Kampen and Mayer--Vietoris in
degree two give trivial fundamental group and \(H_2=0\) on the core.
Capping it by \(k\) ordinary disks gives a homotopy sphere.
Thus \(\chi(X)=2-k\), and the two limiting chiral energies agree,
say \(E_+(h)=E_-(h)=e_X\). For the latter equality apply signature
after interpolating to round cylindrical ends and capping; the Weyl
cost tends to zero by the differentiated end estimates.
In the scalar-flat Gauss--Bonnet formula each Euclidean end contributes
the boundary integral \(8\pi^2\), by rescaling its outer face to the
unit Euclidean sphere \cite[\S7]{TianViaclovsky2005ALE}. Hence
\[
 2e_X-\tfrac12d=8\pi^2(2-2k),\qquad
 d=4e_X+32\pi^2(k-1).
\]
Since \(d\le4e<32\pi^2\), we have \(k=1\). If \(h\) is nonflat,
then \(e_X>0\), since \(d=4e_X\) and \(R_h=0\).

We need strictness of the limiting Yamabe comparison. Invert the
order-two Euclidean end, writing \(\rho=|z|=1/|x|\). The conformal
metric \(|x|^{-4}h\) there is Euclidean plus an error whose derivatives
of order \(j\) are \(O(\rho^{2-j})\). It extends as a \(C^{1,1}\)
metric at the origin with bounded scalar curvature. On a sufficiently
small ball solve its conformal Laplacian Dirichlet problem with
boundary value one. To see positivity, after scaling the ball to
unit size the potential is \(O(b^2)\) at radius \(b\), the principal
coefficients are uniformly elliptic and \(C^1\)-close to Euclidean.
The Dirichlet inverse for the Laplacian and absorption give a solution
\(w\) with \(\|w-1\|_{W^{2,p}}\le C_p b^2\) there for a fixed
\(p>4\). It is smooth off the origin. On annuli of radius \(\rho\),
interior rescaled elliptic estimates and the Lipschitz oscillation
of \(w\) give \(D^jw=O_j(\rho^{1-j})\) for \(j\ge1\): the rescaled scalar potential and each of its fixed-order derivatives on a unit annulus are \(O_j(\rho^2)\) by the differentiated metric bounds.
Thus \(w^2|x|^{-4}h\) is scalar-flat near the puncture and has
the order-zero point bounds of the obstruction-flat removable
singularity theorem (after a constant coordinate normalization).
Bach-flatness is conformally invariant. The trivial group gives
a smooth extending chart \cite[Definition 1.6, Theorem 1.8 and
Remark 7.2]{AcheViaclovsky2011Obstruction}. Extend the positive
multiplier into the core to obtain a closed smooth conformal
compactification. Its chiral energy is \(e_X>0\). The compact-test
Yamabe constant of \(X\) equals the closed class constant by conformal
covariance and the zero \(H^1\) capacity of the smooth point.
The closed class cannot be conformally round, so sharp Yamabe
rigidity gives \(y\le Y_{\rm closed}<S\) \cite[Theorem 2]{Schoen1984Yamabe}.
Using \(d=4e_X\) in \eqref{eq:limit-ric-budget} yields
\begin{equation}\label{eq:physical-residual}
 e-e_X\ \ge\ (S^2-y^2)/48>0 .
\end{equation}

Finally take the middle sphere of the single end at radius \(R\).
Its entire inner raw side is the marked compact truncation, and
its entire outer raw side is the source complement. For each fixed
\(R\) their limiting chiral energies are \(E_+(h;K_R)\) and
\(e-E_+(h;K_R)\) by Theorem~\ref{thm:local-min}. As \(R\to\infty\)
these tend to \(e_X>0\) and \(e-e_X>0\).
On a slow diagonal the marked short charts have
\(a+d_{\rm chart}=O(R^{-2})+o_i(1;R)\) in the norms of
Proposition~\ref{prop:short-cut}; here \(d_{\rm chart}\) denotes
the first-derivative norm. Choose the source threshold at each fixed
\(R\) for both full-side integrals and the whole short chart.
The resulting cut contradicts Proposition~\ref{prop:short-cut}.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Energy and curvature scales]\label{cor:energy-radius}
Keep the hypotheses on the closed minimizing sequence. Let
\(\lambda_i=|W^+_{g_i}|_{\rm HS}^2dV_{g_i}\),
\(K_i=\max_M|\Rm_{g_i}|_{\rm tens}\), and let \(r_i\) be the
least physical radius of a ball with \(\lambda_i\)-mass \(e_i/2\),
attained at \(p_i\). If \(K_i\to\infty\), then for each fixed \(A>0\)
\begin{equation}\label{eq:peak-energy}
 \sup_{x\in M}\int_{B_{g_i}(x,A/\sqrt{K_i})}
              |\Rm_{g_i}|_{\rm tens}^2\,dV_{g_i}\longrightarrow0,
 \qquad r_i\sqrt{K_i}\longrightarrow\infty .
\end{equation}
On every subsequence with \(r_i\to0\) there is also a fixed finite
\(A\) and a further subsequence on which
\[
 r_i^2\sup_{B_{g_i}(p_i,A r_i)}|\Rm_{g_i}|_{\rm tens}\to\infty .
\]
If \(K_i\) is bounded on a subsequence, the infimum is attained by
a smooth closed positive Yamabe minimizer on \(M\).
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Distance spheres have zero physical four-volume and zero chiral
mass. The ball mass is continuous in center and radius, which
gives the stated attainment. Consider more generally \(a_i\to0\)
and centers \(x_i\) with \(a_i^2|\Rm_{g_i}|\le C_D\) on each
\(B_{g_i}(x_i,Da_i)\), where \(D\) is fixed before \(i\).
Put \(\widehat g_i=a_i^{-2}g_i\). The source Yamabe cutoff inequality
gives uniform small-ball noncollapse on these metrics:
for \(s\le1\), \(y_0 V(s/2)^{1/2}\le(24/s^2+a_i^2Y_i)V(s)\).
Iteration gives \(V(s)\ge cs^4\), as in the preceding proof.
Local sectional curvature and volume bounds give uniform injectivity
radii on smaller fixed windows \cite[\S4]{CheegerGromovTaylor1982}.
The local harmonic radius bound gives uniformly elliptic charts with bounded \(W^{1,p}\) coefficients, for any fixed \(p>8\) \cite[Theorem 0.3]{AndersonCheeger1992Harmonic}. In these coordinates the Ricci equation
\[
 g^{ab}\partial_{ab}g_{cd}=-2\Ric_{cd}+Q_{cd}(g^{-1},\partial g)
\]
first gives an interior \(W^{2,p/2}\) bound, since the coefficients are H\"older and the quadratic term lies in \(L^{p/2}\). This bounds the first derivatives in \(C^{0,\alpha}\) for some \(\alpha>0\); reinserting it gives bounds in every finite \(W^{2,q}_{\rm loc}\) and subsequential \(C^{1,\beta}\) convergence on smaller charts for \(\beta<\alpha\).
Here one uses a bound on each larger window before passing to
a diagonal in the windows. Local volume comparison and the
injectivity bound give finite chart covers there. The harmonic
compactness construction identifies smaller whole compact domains
by embeddings in a complete pointed limit, with image containing the source ball of any fixed smaller radius \cite[Theorem 3.1 and its proof]{BelegradekKapovitch2000Finiteness}.
Indeed a path leaving the image of the limit ball of radius \(D+1\)
from the base point must traverse length at least \((1-o(1))(D+1)\)
before first exit. Harmonic transition maps satisfy the scalar
harmonic equation and converge in \(C^{3,\beta'}\) on smaller
overlaps for \(\beta'<\beta\). Once the limiting charts are smooth
the compact-domain identifications can be taken smooth, by gluing
the charts using cutoff coordinate embeddings and tubular projection.
These identifications preserve the first-order convergence.

We justify regularity of the limit \(h\) before applying
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-limit}. In a single limiting harmonic
chart it is \(W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}\), with \(R_h=0\) and locally bounded
curvature by the weak limit of the bounded rescaled curvatures.
The interpolation proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:local-min} applies
first to a smooth approximation \(h_j\to h\) in \(W^{2,p}\),
\(p>4\), on the cutoff support. In comparison with the formal
interpolation using \(h\), the \(L^2\) curvature-density error is
at most \(C\|h_j-h\|_{W^{2,p}}\), uniformly in \(i,s\), by the
local \(W^{2,p}\) bounds and the all-lowered formula. Use first
large fixed \(j\) to retain the strict path margin. Taking \(i\to
\infty\) and then \(j\to\infty\) gives strong chiral convergence.
For a smooth variation \(H\) supported where \(\chi=1\), the energy
change after repair to \(h_j\) is exactly that of \(h_j+sH\)
relative to \(h_j\). For small fixed \(s\) the additional energy
along the path stays below the remaining margin uniformly in \(j\).
The repaired source energy differs from \(e_i\) by at most
\(o_i(1)+O(\|h_j-h\|_{W^{2,p}})\) after strong convergence.
Take \(i\to\infty\) and \(j\to\infty\). Continuity of the density
in \(W^{2,p}\) gives nonnegative energy change for \(h+sH\)
and hence the weak Bach equation.

The distributional Ricci equation for a scalar-flat \(W^{2,p}\)
Bach-flat metric reads \(\Delta_h\Ric_h=\Rm_h*\Ric_h\), with locally
bounded right-hand side. This identity also follows by smooth
approximation in \(W^{2,p}\) in the formula with its scalar
Hessian terms. Local regularity for the rough tensor Laplacian
gives \(\Ric_h\in W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}\)
\cite[Theorem 2.2(ii), Lemma 5.1]{AvalosCogoRoyoAbrego2026Bach}.
The harmonic-coordinate Ricci equation and the Ricci Laplacian
equation then bootstrap together to smoothness. Thus the whole
compact comparisons meet Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-limit} and
\(h\) is flat.

On a fixed Euclidean coordinate patch of this flat limit the
comparison connection forms tend to zero in \(L^4\). The Euler
transgression estimate following Theorem~\ref{thm:local-min}
therefore gives \(\int\chi\mathcal E_i\,dV_{\widehat g_i}\to0\)
for every smooth compact cutoff. The Weyl energies tend to zero
there by strong convergence, and \(a_i^2Y_i\to0\) with bounded
local rescaled volume. The same Euler identity gives vanishing
of the full curvature integral on smaller compacts. A finite
cover gives the result on each entire fixed source ball in the
pointed comparison.

For \(a_i=K_i^{-1/2}\) the rescaled curvature is bounded by one for
every center. Failure of the supremum estimate in
\eqref{eq:peak-energy} would select a subsequence and centers with
a fixed positive integral, contradicting the preceding conclusion.
The median ball has full curvature integral at least \(4(e_i/2)\);
this proves the scale separation. At \(a_i=r_i\to0\), bounded
rescaled curvature on every fixed multiple about \(p_i\) gives the
same contradiction already on the unit ball. This proves the
local assertion with its stated subsequences.

Finally, at bounded unscaled curvature the small-ball noncollapse
and unit total volume bound the physical diameter by packing
disjoint small balls along a minimizing segment. Harmonic compactness
now identifies a whole closed limit with the original smooth type.
The same local substitution and elliptic regularity apply with
constant limiting scalar \(y=\lim Y_i>0\). The closed embeddings
are diffeomorphisms. Passing the Yamabe inequality to all fixed tests
and using the constant test gives \(Y([h])=y\) at unit volume.
Strong chiral convergence on the compact manifold gives \(E_+(h)=e\).
\end{proof}

If \(I^+(M)=4\pi^2\) on a candidate nonstandard smooth type, this
last closed alternative is impossible by Proposition~\ref{prop:recognition}.
Every such minimizing sequence would have \(K_i\to\infty\) and
would satisfy \eqref{eq:peak-energy}. A point of maximum curvature
need not retain positive integrated curvature on its own scale.

There is a measured choice of the next annulus which does not
place any chiral mass at the maximum-curvature point. Define, for
\(0<\theta\le1/2\),
\[
 r_i(\theta)=\inf\{s:\max_{x\in M}\lambda_i(B_{g_i}(x,s))
                         \ge\theta e_i\},\qquad r_i=r_i(1/2).
\]
If \(K_i\to\infty\), \eqref{eq:peak-energy} gives
\(r_i(\theta)\sqrt{K_i}\to\infty\) for each fixed \(\theta>0\).
Exactly one of the following holds on a given sequence.
\begin{enumerate}
\item For some fixed \(0<\theta<1/2\) there is a subsequence with
\(r_i(\theta)/r_i\to0\). Choose any attaining center \(x_i\) for
\(r_i(\theta)\). The full inner ball has positive chiral mass
\(\theta e_i\), and the entire exterior of \(B_{g_i}(x_i,r_i)\)
has mass at least \(e_i/2\) by the defining maximum.
Between them fit increasingly many disjoint metric annuli of any
previously fixed logarithmic width. One has full curvature integral
tending to zero by \eqref{eq:budgets}. It supplies genuine opposite
mass sets to a short marked cut if the embedded middle sphere of
such a chart separates these sets on the source.
\item The positive chiral measure satisfies
\[
 \lim_{b\downarrow0}\limsup_i\sup_{x\in M}
       \frac{\lambda_i(B_{g_i}(x,b r_i))}{e_i}=0 .
\]
Indeed failure supplies a positive \(\theta<1/2\), \(b_j\downarrow0\)
and a subsequence with \(r_i(\theta)\le b_j r_i\). Conversely the
first case gives a lower bound \(\theta\) in the displayed limit.
Here a bounded number of balls of radii \(o(r_i)\) captures
vanishing chiral mass, even if the pointwise curvatures in them
diverge. The half-energy ball itself still carries \(e_i/2\).
\end{enumerate}
The annuli in the first case are distance sets; their topology and
the first derivatives in any proposed splice chart require separate
estimates. In the second case the estimate is for metric balls and
does not imply uniform absolute continuity on all sets of small
physical volume.
