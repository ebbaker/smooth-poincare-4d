\section{Physical Ricci scales and marked annuli}\label{sec:ricci-scales}

Write \(Z_g=\Ric_g-\tfrac14 R_g g\). For \(2<p<\infty\) put
\(\gamma=2-4/p\), and, for a physical ball, set
\[
 P_i(x,a)=a^\gamma\|Z_{g_i}\|_{L^p(B_{g_i}(x,a),g_i)}.
\]
This quantity is invariant under simultaneous constant rescaling of
metric and length. In general it changes under the conformal
transformation used to locate physical volume.

\begin{lemma}[Interior Ricci control]\label{lem:ricci-radius}
Let \(g_i\) be smooth unit-volume positive Yamabe metrics on oriented
smooth homotopy four-spheres, with \(E_+(g_i)\le8\pi^2-\delta\)
for fixed \(\delta>0\). If \(0<a_i\le1\) and \(P_i(x_i,a_i)\le L\),
then each point of \(B_{g_i}(x_i,a_i/2)\) has a harmonic chart of
radius at least \(c(p,\delta,L)a_i\). At this radius the scaled metric
is uniformly elliptic with bounded \(W^{2,p}\) components.
For \(0<b\le c a_i\), after reducing \(c\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ricci-mass}
 \int_{B_{g_i}(x_i,b)}|\Rm_{g_i}|^2\,dV_{g_i}
       \le C(p,\delta,L)(b/a_i)^{4-8/p}.
\end{equation}
The lemma does not require minimization.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
Use the harmonic radius \(h\) defined by eigenvalues between \(1/2\)
and \(2\) and, in length coordinates on a ball of radius \(s\), by
\[
 s^{1-2/p}\|Dg\|_{2p}+s^{2-4/p}\|D^2g\|_p\le1 .
\]
All chart derivative norms here are Euclidean. The supremum of
admissible \(s\) scales as length and is 1-Lipschitz by restricting
charts to smaller balls. These are the harmonic-radius compactness
coordinates of \cite[\S2]{AndersonCheegerGromovAuthorSurvey}.
Suppose the asserted interior floor fails. Minimize \(h/d\) on the
protected ball, where \(d(z)=a_i-d_{g_i}(x_i,z)>0\), and let \(b_i\)
be the harmonic radius at a minimizing point \(z_i\). Smoothness and
compactness give an interior minimum. Then \(b_i/d(z_i)\to0\).
On every fixed pointed ball for \(b_i^{-2}g_i\), harmonic radii are
at least \(1-o(1)\), while the central radius equals one. The
trace-free Ricci norm on that ball is at most
\(L(b_i/a_i)^\gamma\). The scalar curvature is the constant
\(b_i^2Y_i\to0\).

We give the compactness details pertinent to the critical energy.
Uniform charts on slightly larger windows give local upper and lower
volume bounds on balls of a fixed small radius by ellipticity.
Small balls thus have a uniform finite covering number at half that
radius. Iteration along minimizing segments gives finite nets on each
fixed pointed window, and a uniform upper volume there. In particular
the scalar part of Ricci tends to zero in \(L^p\) on these windows
as well. The harmonic atlas gives a complete pointed limit on a
diagonal, weakly \(W^{2,p}\) and strongly \(C^0\cap W^{1,2p}\)
on chart interiors. Indeed \(2p<4p/(4-p)\) when \(2<p<4\);
the other exponents follow by the usual finite-exponent embeddings.
Harmonic transition functions satisfy the scalar harmonic equation.
Interior estimates first give bounded \(W^{2,s}\) for finite \(s\)
on smaller overlaps (the transitions have bounded first derivatives
by ellipticity). Differentiating gives bounded \(W^{3,p}\), using
\(Dg\in L^{2p}\) and \(D^2\) of the transition in \(L^{2p}\).
Thus the charts glue with \(C^1\) convergence of transitions and
strong \(W^{2,2p}\) on smaller overlaps. These estimates give
whole compact markings: on a finite subatlas embed with the coordinate
blocks \((\eta,\eta x)\) for cutoffs whose positive sets cover the
compact, project the corresponding source atlas to the limiting
embedded manifold, and invert on a slightly smaller neighborhood.
The maps have local inverses on uniform neighborhoods by \(C^1\)
convergence. Injectivity on the smaller neighborhood follows from
the separated chart nets and the injective limiting embedding.
After smoothness of the limit is established in its harmonic atlas
this construction uses smooth cutoffs and a smooth tubular projection.
It gives oriented smooth embeddings, with the same strong first-order
metric convergence. The image of a larger fixed ball contains each
smaller source ball, by metric comparison up to first exit.

The harmonic-coordinate equation
\(g^{ab}\partial_{ab}g_{jk}=-2\Ric_{jk}+Q_{jk}(g,Dg)\)
passes to the limit, since \(Q\) is quadratic in \(Dg\).
The limiting metric is Ricci-flat and smooth by interior elliptic
regularity. In fact in these charts the convergence is strong in
\(W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}\): subtract the limiting equation, apply the
interior estimate for its continuous elliptic principal coefficients,
and absorb the uniformly small coefficient difference. The right
side converges strongly in \(L^p\). The transition equations then
give strong \(W^{3,p}_{\rm loc}\) for transitions (differentiate in
the harmonic coordinates); the compact markings have the same order
of convergence.

The global curvature bound \eqref{eq:budgets} and the transported
homogeneous Yamabe inequality in the proof of
Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-limit} give finite total curvature and
Euclidean lower volume growth on this Ricci-flat limit. The end
argument of that proof up through the exclusion of quotient links
uses only the exact critical equation on the limit, the marked
first-order end comparisons, the topology of the sources and their
energy bound. It therefore applies here before any minimization.
Every end is Euclidean at infinity. Such an end alone gives
asymptotic volume ratio at least one; Bishop--Gromov monotonicity
and its equality case on the complete Ricci-flat manifold give
Euclidean space. To see the contradiction with unit central radius,
take a Euclidean ball of radius four in this limit and pull the
metrics back by its compact markings. They converge strongly to
\(\delta_{\rm Euc}\) in \(W^{2,p}\) on radius three. Solve the
harmonic Dirichlet problem there with affine coordinate boundary
data. For the coordinate correction the contracted Christoffels
tend to zero in \(L^{2p}\); the small principal coefficient error
and the first-order term are absorbed by the flat Dirichlet
\(W^{2,2p}\) estimate, since \(2p>4\). The corrected coordinates
converge in \(C^1\) to the identity. Differentiating their equation
on an interior ball gives convergence in \(W^{3,p}\), using
\(Dg,D^2u\in L^{2p}\), \(D^2g\in L^p\), and the convergence just
proved. They give an admissible chart on a source ball of radius
strictly larger than one. This proves the floor.
Finally on the scaled central chart, \(D^2g\in L^p\) and
\(Dg\in L^{2p}\) bound \(\|\Rm\|_p\). Ellipticity bounds the
volume of each concentric subball of radius \(b/a_i\) by
\(C(b/a_i)^4\). H\"older gives \eqref{eq:ricci-mass}.
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Ricci scale of a minimizing sequence]\label{thm:ricci-scale}
Keep the hypotheses of Corollary~\ref{cor:energy-radius}. Suppose
\(a_i\to0\) and for one fixed finite \(p>2\) the bounds
\[
  a_i^\gamma\|Z_{g_i}\|_{L^p(B_{g_i}(x_i,D a_i),g_i)}
       \le C_D
\]
hold for each fixed \(D<\infty\) eventually on the same subsequence.
For every fixed \(A<\infty\),
\begin{equation}\label{eq:ricci-vanish}
 \int_{B_{g_i}(x_i,A a_i)}|\Rm_{g_i}|^2\,dV_{g_i}\longrightarrow0 .
\end{equation}
In particular, write \(N_i=\|Z_{g_i}\|_{L^p(M,g_i)}\). A bounded
subsequence of \(N_i\) gives a smooth closed positive Yamabe
minimizer on the original smooth type. On any subsequence with
\(N_i\to\infty\), \eqref{eq:ricci-vanish} holds uniformly in the
center at \(a_i=N_i^{-1/\gamma}\), and
\[
              r_i(\theta)N_i^{1/\gamma}\longrightarrow\infty
              \qquad(0<\theta\le1/2).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Apply Lemma~\ref{lem:ricci-radius} on successively larger protected
windows. The same harmonic atlas construction gives a complete
\(W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}\) limit, with uniform and strong \(W^{1,2p}\)
convergence. Ricci now has only a bounded scaled \(L^p\) norm.
We justify smoothness without an equation on the source metrics.

In each fixed chart the cutoff interpolation in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:energy-radius} applies to a \(W^{2,p}\) comparison.
Smooth approximation in that norm is strong in \(C^0\cap W^{1,4}\);
the all-lowered curvature formula and chiral projection are continuous
from this topology with strong \(W^{2,p}\) approximation into \(L^2\).
The source curvatures are bounded in \(L^2\), their lower jets converge
strongly, and their second jets converge weakly in \(L^p\). Thus the
same quadratic defect identity yields strong \(L^2\) convergence of both
Weyl matrices at the infimum. The compactly supported first variations
then pass to the limit as in that proof: the varied Weyl matrix has
weakly convergent derivative, paired with a strongly convergent matrix,
and the second variation along each fixed smooth test has a uniform
bound. The sign is protected by \eqref{eq:zero-wall}. The limit is
stationary with distributional scalar zero (constant scalar \(y>0\)
in the unscaled case).

For clarity, local stationarity gives \(B_h=0\) in \(W^{-2,p}\)
by strong \(W^{2,p}\) approximation of the smooth first-variation
identity. The contractions with \(h^{-1}\) and density can be
inverted on tests by Sobolev multiplication, since \(p>2\)
(in the second derivative product rule use \(Dh\in L^{2p}\), \(D\phi\in L^{2p/(2p-3)}\) and \(\phi\in L^{p/(p-2)}\) for \(\phi\in W^{2,p/(p-1)}_0\)) \cite[Lemma 2.1 and proof of Theorem 1.1]{AvalosCogoRoyoAbrego2026Bach}.
In dimension four and constant scalar, the weak tensor equation is
\(\Delta_h\Ric_h=\Rm_h*\Ric_h\)
\cite[Lemma 5.1]{AvalosCogoRoyoAbrego2026Bach}. Its right side
belongs to \(L^{p/2}_{\rm loc}\), and \(\Ric_h\in L^p\subset
L^{p/(p-1)}_{\rm loc}\). The interior tensor estimate
\cite[Theorem 2.2(ii)]{AvalosCogoRoyoAbrego2026Bach} gives
\(\Ric_h\in W^{2,p/2}_{\rm loc}\). For \(2<p<4\) this yields
\(\Ric_h\in L^t_{\rm loc}\) with \(t=2p/(4-p)>p\).
The harmonic metric equation has right side in this same \(L^t\),
since \(Dh\in L^{2t}\). For this gain in exponent, localize by a smooth cutoff, freeze the inverse metric on a small ball and invert the small principal perturbation of the flat Dirichlet Laplacian at \(t\). The cutoff right side belongs to \(L^t\); uniqueness at \(p\) identifies the solution. Thus interior estimates for the
continuous coefficients upgrade \(h\) to \(W^{2,t}_{\rm loc}\).
Iteration reaches \(p\ge4\) in finitely many steps
(\(1/t=2/p-1/2\)). At \(p=4\) use any finite \(t>4\);
above four the Ricci and harmonic equations bootstrap on nested
interiors to smoothness. Transition maps are then smooth in the
limiting harmonic atlas.

Theorem~\ref{thm:complete-limit} makes the complete limit flat.
The localized Euler transgression in the proof of
Corollary~\ref{cor:energy-radius}, strong Weyl convergence, and
vanishing rescaled scalar imply \eqref{eq:ricci-vanish} on each
fixed marked compact interior. The first-exit observation covers
the whole indicated source ball. Subsequence selection proves
the uniform assertion when \(N_i\to\infty\); a bounded multiple
of this length cannot contain \(\theta e_i\) of chiral mass.
If \(N_i\) is bounded, the radius floor holds everywhere at fixed
scale. Unit volume and the physical small-ball lower bound give a
bounded diameter. The compact marked limit is thus closed and
diffeomorphic to the source. The preceding local argument gives
strong chiral convergence and smoothness there. Passing all Yamabe
tests and the unit-volume constant test to the limit identifies its
positive Yamabe constant and proves attainment.
\end{proof}

In the separated-quantile case, let \(u_i=r_i(\theta)\ll r_i\)
and let \(x_i\) be its attaining center. For every choice
\(u_i\ll a_i\le\min(1,r_i)\), \(P_i(x_i,a_i)\to\infty\).
Indeed a bounded subsequence would contradict \eqref{eq:ricci-mass}
at \(b=u_i\). One can also construct the marked cut on the hypothetical bounded
subsequence. Use the central harmonic chart, normalize its metric at
the origin by an oriented linear map, and dilate coordinates by
\(s_i=\sqrt{u_i a_i}\). On the previously fixed annulus
\(e^{-2}<|x|<e^2\), the uniform error and the \(L^4\) norm of the
first derivative are \(O((s_i/a_i)^{1-2/p})\) by Morrey and
H\"older applied to \(Dg\in L^{2p}\). The metric here is a constant
physical rescaling. The chart contains a whole physical ball of
comparable radius and distances are comparable up to first exit.
Thus the middle sphere encloses the whole \(u_i\)-ball, and its
entire coordinate disk is inside the physical \(r_i\)-ball. The
opposite side retains the entire median exterior. The two chiral
masses are at least \(\theta e_i\) and \(e_i/2\), respectively.
The full curvature is bounded on the splice; Proposition~\ref{prop:short-cut}
would give two positive caps with total excess \(o(1)\), contradicting
fixed-type minimization. The marked disk description uses no global
smooth parametrization. The forced divergence concerns balls
containing the inner mass. A bound confined to a buffered annulus
requires a separate argument for its global marking.

\subsection{A recognition test near the positive Einstein class}

For a real chiral Weyl endomorphism let \(\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le
\lambda_3\) denote its ordered eigenvalues. For \(0\le s<1\) define
\[
 H_s^\pm(g)=\int_M
    (\lambda_2(W^\pm_g)-s\lambda_3(W^\pm_g))_+^2\,dV_g .
\]
This measures the failure of the pointwise eigenvalue condition in the
conformal K\"ahler rigidity theorem of Catino and Dameno
\cite[Theorem 1.4]{CatinoDameno2026ConformalKahler}.

\begin{theorem}[A uniform near-Einstein recognition criterion]
\label{thm:near-einstein-recognition}
Fix \(\delta>0\), \(2<p<\infty\), \(L<\infty\) and \(0\le s<1\).
There exists \(\epsilon(\delta,p,L,s)>0\) with the following property.
Let \(g\) be a smooth volume-one positive Yamabe minimizer in its own
class on a closed oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere \(M\). Suppose
\[
 E_+(g)\le8\pi^2-\delta,\qquad \|Z_g\|_{L^p(g)}\le L .
\]
Then \(M\) is diffeomorphic to \(S^4\) whenever
\[
 \int_M|Z_g|^2\,dV_g+\min\{H_s^+(g),H_s^-(g)\}<\epsilon(\delta,p,L,s).
\]
More generally, for every \(\eta>0\) a sufficiently small threshold
\(\epsilon(\delta,p,L,s,\eta)>0\) in the same defect bound forces
\(E_+(g)<\eta\) for the tested metric itself. The constants are
obtained by compactness. The tested metric may have
nonzero trace-free Ricci tensor. No minimization over conformal classes
is needed.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If the stronger assertion failed, there would be metrics, possibly on
different smooth types, for which the displayed sum tends to zero
while \(E_+(g_i)\ge\eta>0\). Pass to a subsequence using the same
chirality for the minimum, with \(Y_i\to y\ge\sqrt{48\delta}>0\).
Lemma~\ref{lem:ricci-radius} at radius one gives a uniform harmonic
radius and \(W^{2,p}\) bounds on the whole physical manifolds.
Unit volume and physical noncollapse bound their diameters.
The harmonic atlas compactness and compact markings in that lemma
therefore give a closed oriented limit on the same smooth type as all
sufficiently late members of the subsequence, weakly \(W^{2,p}\) and
strongly \(C^0\cap W^{1,2p}\) in harmonic chart interiors.

Fix \(2<r<p\). Interpolation gives \(Z_{g_i}\to0\) in \(L^r\).
In the harmonic metric equation the quadratic first derivatives
converge strongly in \(L^p\), and \(\Ric_{g_i}\to yh/4\) strongly in
\(L^r\) for the limiting metric \(h\). The weak equation makes \(h\) Einstein. In harmonic coordinates, the same interior exponent iteration as in Theorem~\ref{thm:ricci-scale} makes it smooth.
Subtract the Einstein limit equation on nested harmonic chart interiors.
The product of the uniformly vanishing difference of inverse metric
coefficients with the uniformly \(L^p\)-bounded Hessians tends to zero
in \(L^r\). The interior elliptic estimate gives strong \(W^{2,r}\)
convergence there. Thus the curvatures converge strongly in \(L^2\)
on the finite covering. The chiral projections converge uniformly in
oriented frames. Ordered eigenvalues are Lipschitz in a symmetric
matrix, so the chosen \(H_s^\pm(h)\) is zero, passing the integrals
with a partition of unity in these charts.

The pointwise eigenvalue inequality follows by continuity. If the
corresponding Weyl block is nonzero, Theorem 1.4 of Catino and Dameno,
with orientation reversed for the negative block, makes a cover of
degree at most two conformally K\"ahler. The limit is simply connected
and \(H^2(M;\mathbb R)=0\), whereas the square of a K\"ahler class on a
closed complex surface has positive integral. Hence that Weyl block
vanishes. Signature zero forces the other block to vanish as well.
The limit is round up to scale. Strong convergence of the chiral
curvatures then gives \(E_+(g_i)\to0\), a contradiction. Taking
\(\eta=4\pi^2\) and applying Proposition~\ref{prop:recognition} proves
the smooth conclusion on the tested physical class.
\end{proof}
