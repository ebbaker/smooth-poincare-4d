\begin{proposition}[A necessary disk-side saving]\label{prop:balanced-disk-saving}
Let \(I=I^+(M)<2T\) on a nonstandard smooth homotopy four-sphere.
On the minimizing subsequence in Theorem~\ref{thm:band-disk-residual},
the original charged disk and its marked bicollar can be preserved in
the interior of a larger parametrized standard disk \(B_i\).
For any fixed \(k\ge1\) its exterior \(V_i\) satisfies, in both signs,
\[
 E_s(g_i;V_i)=2^{-k}\bigl(e_i-E_s(g_i;Q_i)\bigr)+o(1),
\]
where \(Q_i\) is the outer disk enclosing the original bicollar.
The same division holds for the spectral measure \(L_i^2dV\)
and for the original full curvature measure.
If a joined path from \(g_i\) stays strictly below \(2T\) and ends at
\(h_i\) with an exactly matched round product germ in the final
boundary marking, up to a conformal factor, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:balanced-disk-saving}
 E_s(g_i;B_i)-E_s(h_i;B_i)>(1-2^{-k})e_i+2^{-k}E_s(g_i;Q_i)+I-2T-o(1).
\end{equation}
One universal finite \(k\) makes the limiting lower bound larger than
\(\varepsilon_*\) for every surviving type.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
At a fixed source enclose all but an arbitrarily small error of the
four exterior measures in a single auxiliary standard disk. Use
finitely many small disjoint coordinate balls and thin tubes along
a tree of embedded arcs in the connected exterior. Removing tree
leaves identifies its rounded neighborhood with a smooth ball.
In its ball chart an affine hyperplane bisects all four restricted
measures by the ham-sandwich theorem \cite{StoneTukey1942Sandwich}.
Choose a smooth inner approximation of one convex half, then attach
it by one thin tube to a slight enlargement of \(Q_i\). The arc and
all smooth faces are null for the measures. Thus its parametrized
boundary connected sum leaves half the former exterior to tolerance.
Iterate at fixed \(k\), taking the errors to zero along the sources.
The new boundary admits a bicollar of arbitrarily small raw measure.
Its marking need not satisfy the first-order splice estimates.

At the endpoint stretch into the actual disk side and cap the
retained round end. The cap is conformally flat, and compactly
supported tests on the stretched end inherit a positive Yamabe
bound from the strict joined energy ceiling, as in
Proposition~\ref{prop:exterior-replacement}. The disk parametrization
identifies the capped exterior with \(M\). Hence
\(E_s(h_i;V_i)\ge I\), while \(E_s(h_i;M)<2T\).
Subtract and use the original mass of the whole disk side to obtain
\eqref{eq:balanced-disk-saving}. In particular, at \(k=1\) and
\(I=4T/3\), the protected core has a fixed positive charge in the
sign selected by Theorem~\ref{thm:band-disk-residual}. Its contribution
forces a strictly positive disk-side saving even at this equality.
Choose
\(2^{-k}(T+\varepsilon_*)\le\varepsilon_*\) and use
\(I>T+\varepsilon_*\). A change confined to a final collar of
vanishing raw curvature can save only \(o(1)\) there. For this
\(k\) every such path to the matched seam reaches \(2T\) for all
large indices.
\end{proof}

Already at \(k=1\) the retained raw exterior lies below \(T\) in
both signs throughout the strict band. For an independently positive
cap one may instead use \(p=2(dE_++dE_-)+3L^2dV\), where \(dE_\pm\)
are the two chiral measures. Its raw exterior mass is
\(\tfrac12(P([g_i])-p(Q_i))+o(1)\).
Adding the entire signed exterior and filling cost and comparing
inclusively with \(8T+\eta\) tests this particular positive class.
The descent paper bounds this cost using the two chiral density
errors on the actual cutoff widths \cite{Baker2026FixedDescent}.
