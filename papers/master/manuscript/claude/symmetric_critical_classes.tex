\paragraph{Two calibrations.}
For a positive Einstein class on \(M\) which is not round, Gursky's
inequality for \(\delta W^+=0\), in its strict form on a homotopy
sphere, gives \(E_+>\tfrac{4\pi^2}{3}(2\chi+3\tau)\)
\cite{Gursky2000EinsteinConstants}%
\cite[Strict half-harmonic bound]{Baker2026MarkedNecks}. With
\(e=2T-Y^2/48\) this reads
\[
 \tfrac43T<e<2T,\qquad Y^2<\tfrac13\,Y_{\rm rd}^2 .
\]
An Einstein minimizer on a nonstandard type would therefore lie in the
part \(e>\tfrac43T\) of the band \eqref{eq:band}.

The second calibration is a symmetric test of the kind just mentioned,
for the rotations which fix a circle. Let \(SO(3)\) act on
\(S^4\subset\mathbb R^3\oplus\mathbb R^2\) by rotations of
\(\mathbb R^3\), with fixed circle \(C\).

\begin{proposition}[Symmetric critical classes on the standard sphere]
\label{prop:symmetric-critical}
A Bach-flat conformal class on \(S^4\) which is invariant under this
action is the round class.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
Average a representative over the group. The complement of \(C\) is the
product of the open orbit disc \(\mathbb D\) and \(S^2\), and the
isotropy circle of a point rotates the tangent plane of its orbit while
fixing that of \(\mathbb D\). The averaged metric is therefore
\(\tilde q+f^2g_{S^2}\) for a metric \(\tilde q\) and a positive
function \(f\) on \(\mathbb D\). The class is that of \(q+g_{S^2}\)
with \(q=f^{-2}\tilde q\). Let \(K\) be the curvature of \(q\).

For a symmetric two-tensor \(k\) with compact support in \(\mathbb D\),
\eqref{eq:product-energy} and the first variations of curvature and
area give
\[
 \frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\big(E_++E_-\big)(q+tk+g_{S^2})
 =\frac{4\pi}{3}\int_{\mathbb D}\big\langle
   \nabla^2K-\Delta K\,q+\tfrac12(1-K^2)\,q,\ k\big\rangle\,dA_q ,
\]
with the energies taken over a neighborhood of the support. The Bach
tensor is a nonzero multiple of the gradient of the Weyl integral. It is
invariant and trace-free, so it has no mixed components and its \(S^2\)
block is determined by its \(\mathbb D\) block. Hence the class is
Bach-flat exactly when
\begin{equation}\label{eq:hessian-K}
 \nabla^2K=\tfrac12(1-K^2)\,q .
\end{equation}
Then \(X=J\nabla K\), with \(J\) the complex structure of \(q\), has
\(\nabla X=\tfrac12(1-K^2)J\) and is a Killing field of \(q\).

Near \(C\) use coordinates \((\theta,x)\in S^1\times\mathbb R^3\) and
\(\rho=|x|\). Smoothness and invariance give
\[
 \tilde q=a\,d\theta^2+2b\,\rho\,d\theta\,d\rho
          +(c_1+c_2\rho^2)\,d\rho^2,\qquad f^2=c_1\rho^2 ,
\]
with \(a,b,c_1,c_2\) smooth functions of \((\theta,\rho^2)\) and
\(a,c_1>0\). Thus \(\tilde q\) and \(f\) are smooth on the closed disc
\(\overline{\mathbb D}\), with \(f=0\) and \(|df|_{\tilde q}=1\) on its
boundary. By the formula
\(K=f^2K_{\tilde q}+f\Delta_{\tilde q}f-|df|^2_{\tilde q}\) for the
conformal change, \(K\) is smooth on \(\overline{\mathbb D}\) and equals
\(-1\) on the boundary, and \(X=f^2J\nabla^{\tilde q}K\) vanishes there.
A Killing field of \(q\) is conformal for \(\tilde q\). In a
uniformizing coordinate \(z\) of \((\overline{\mathbb D},\tilde q)\),
\(X=F\,\partial_z+\bar F\,\partial_{\bar z}\) with \(F\) holomorphic in
the open unit disc, continuous on its closure and zero on the unit
circle. Hence \(F=0\), so \(K\) is constant and equal to \(-1\). By
\eqref{eq:product-energy} the class is flat on \(S^4\setminus C\), hence
on \(S^4\), and it is round by Kuiper's theorem \cite{Kuiper1949}.
\end{proof}

Equation \eqref{eq:hessian-K} has other solutions. The constant
\(K=1\) is the Einstein product on \(S^2\times S^2\). A complete
nonconstant solution on the plane is
\[
 q=ds^2+a(s)^2\,d\varphi^2,\qquad
 a=2\operatorname{sech}^2\!\tfrac s2\,\tanh\tfrac s2,\qquad
 K=-1+3\operatorname{sech}^2\!\tfrac s2 .
\]
With \(r=1+\cosh s\) and \(\tau=4\varphi\), the metric
\(r^2(q+g_{S^2})\) is the Euclidean Schwarzschild metric
\((1-2/r)\,d\tau^2+(1-2/r)^{-1}dr^2+r^2g_{S^2}\). It is Ricci-flat,
and \eqref{eq:product-energy} gives
\[
 E_\pm=\frac{4\pi^2}{3}\int_0^\infty(K+1)^2a\,ds=8\pi^2=2T ,
\]
the value of \eqref{eq:zero-wall} at vanishing Ricci curvature. This
metric is complete, scalar-flat and Bach-flat, as are the blow-up
limits in Theorem~\ref{thm:attainment}. It has cubic volume growth and
exactly the threshold energy, so both the Sobolev estimate and the
strict energy bound there exclude it.
