**Subsequent author-directed priority, 22 November:** the
[flow-first decision](FLOW_FIRST_PRIORITY_20261122.md) supersedes selection
of small-defect pinching as the first target. The assessment below is
retained as historical reasoning and a candidate test. Nonlinear evolution
and continuation analysis should now determine the intermediate hypotheses.

The best-aligned candidate is a sphere recognition theorem from intrinsic
pinching of a positive definite connection, with no known round reference
on the underlying manifold. It would restrict the smooth Poincaré problem
by assuming suitable initial geometric data and leave their existence on
arbitrary homotopy spheres for later.

The broad metric target C > 0 and C² − BBᵀ > 0 is already
Fine–Krasnov–Panov's Conjecture 4.10. The more general positive definite
connection target is their Conjecture 4.8 and Fine's Conjecture 1.7.
These are provenance for the direction, not results proved by this
project. A targeted search did not locate a general resolution.

The narrower candidate asks whether an ε₀ > 0 exists such that
\[
\|Q-I\|_\infty<\varepsilon_0
\]
on a smooth closed homotopy four-sphere, for a definite connection of the
same algebraic sign as the round model, forces standard smooth structure.
The curvature-plane positivity and the curvature-map orientation sign
are separate conditions. No path to a known perfect connection is assumed.
The viability of the small-defect-only statement is unresolved. Any added
noncollapse, derivative or metric curvature assumption must be named,
and the constants must state their dependence.

Hamilton's analogy supports seeking a preserved intrinsic condition.
It does not justify carrying Ric > 0 from dimension three into four:
Ricci curvature leaves Weyl curvature uncontrolled. Positive curvature
operator, full positive isotropic curvature, the Chang–Gursky–Yang
positive-Yamabe small-Weyl regime, and circle-invariant definite
connections already give sphere recognition in this topology.
Fine also already proves local convergence near a known perfect connection.

The next ready research task remains the existing six-claim audit
SP4-CME-E01. After its disposition, the following finite check would
inform SP4-CME-B01; this note does not replace that task's frozen packet.

1. Fix a gauge for the independent connection flow and derive the exact
   principal and lower-order terms in Q_t and in the evolution of
   \(\mathcal T_A=d_A(-K^{-1}Q^{-1/2}F_A)\).
2. Test one specified small-defect regime for an inequality of the form
   \[
   (\partial_t-\mathscr L_A)|Q-I|^2
   \leq -cK|Q-I|^2-c_1|\nabla_AQ|^2+\mathscr R.
   \]
   Here \(\mathscr L_A\) must be derived and shown uniformly parabolic;
   the constants and any bound on the remainder \(\mathscr R\) must follow
   from stated intrinsic hypotheses. The displayed inequality is a desired
   acceptance test, not a claimed equation.
3. If the remainder needs an uncontrolled compatibility derivative or
   geometric quantity, record that obstruction and specify the missing
   estimate. Do not infer failure of every pinched-connection approach.
4. Before calling a successful criterion new, compare its exact hypotheses
   with Fine's stability and the known metric sphere theorems. If it merely
   restates their assumptions, classify it as a flow or reconstruction
   result rather than a new special case of smooth Poincaré.

A preserved lower eigenvalue bound is only the first component. A sphere
conclusion by flow also needs regularity, noncollapse, compactness and
identification of a perfect limit. None is supplied by this assessment.
