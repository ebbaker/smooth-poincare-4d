# B42 — Extract a quantitative smoothing estimate from the parameter proof

22 November 2026, America/New_York. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI assistance. Selected lead settings preserved; actual identifiers unavailable.

B41 and E62 deliberately use only a continuity modulus for the positive-time smoothing step. A sharper, separately testable implication is already suggested by E42's proof: its transformed fixed-point solution is jointly C-infinity in the finite spatial-flow parameter and the C0 initial datum, as a map into the completed X space.

At a round stationary reference the zero initial datum gives the zero centered solution. The linear pullback of a zero section remains zero for every parameter, so all parameter derivatives of that solution vanish at zero datum. For any fixed finite parameter derivative order, joint differentiability with respect to the initial datum should give a local bound proportional to its C0 norm by the Banach-space mean-value formula. Continuity of that derivative gives boundedness on a sufficiently small neighborhood of the single zero datum; no compactness of an infinite-dimensional ball is needed.

The spatial inverse maps and evaluation operations in E42 are fixed independently of the datum. On a compact positive-time strip, finitely many spanning-field neighborhoods should therefore turn those parameter bounds into a C^k bound linear in the initial C0 norm, for each fixed finite k. This would yield a linear H^m smoothing bound at one fixed positive time and sharpen the whole-time critical budget to C delta, if all other premises are discharged. It does not produce bounds uniform as time tends to zero, or a single threshold independent of every derivative order.

Freeze E63 on this finite-order inference, including the parameter-domain margins, bounded derivative argument, evaluation and reconstruction, and any time derivatives actually claimed. Until assessed, retain the weaker modulus in E62 and all integration. The task is a derivative of the existing proof, not an assumed new regularization theorem.
