# B09 — Test energy and dissipation against spatial concentration

E07 only rules out a same-point algebraic speed-to-strain estimate. E05 gives a global integrated gradient bound under pinching, so the next useful check must respect that nonlocal structure. In four dimensions, a fixed-amplitude connection-curvature perturbation on a shrinking ball has defect energy proportional to the fourth power of its radius and squared speed proportional to the second. This suggests a stronger, but still static, actual-connection obstruction to using just those two scalar integrals to bound a supremum or the transport path cost.

Choose the standard round S4, K=1, a radial gauge near p, and one smooth compactly supported connection profile with curvature change −delta c_i Sigma_i at p, c=(1,−1,0). Scale it as a_epsilon(x)=delta epsilon a(x/epsilon) in components. The profile is fixed; choose a sufficiently small fixed nonzero delta so all connections remain uniformly definite, optionally within |q|≤1/8. Then let epsilon tend to zero. Prove, rather than assume, uniform metric comparability, P=O(epsilon^4), ||v||²=O(epsilon²), and a fixed center distortion >1. Since the connection equals the round one outside the ball, E04 then gives C_T≥log D(p)/sqrt2 independent of epsilon.

If valid, this excludes universal static bounds C_T≤f(P,||v||²) with f tending to zero at the origin, even on this fixed closed bundle and with uniform definiteness. It also excludes inferring vanishing sup|q| from those integrals. It does not exclude estimates with higher regularity, geometry plus appropriate Sobolev norms, scale-sensitive quantities, spacetime control, or properties specific to actual evolved solutions. No flow trajectory is to be constructed. Delegate this single scaling family in E10.
