# B37 — A finite-p critical norm for the principal-term problem

22 November 2026, America/New_York. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI assistance. Selected lead settings preserved; actual identifiers unavailable.

The near-round stability route addresses existence and decay from small values but does not by itself bound the temporal strain integral near the initial time. E25 rejects a specific L1-time L^(4,1) second-derivative forcing estimate. A different norm can keep the highest coefficient perturbation inside a closed parabolic estimate.

Choose a bounded flat calibration with value unknown U and a finite exponent, p=8, critical spatial order s=4/p=1/2. The intended input is the inhomogeneous B^(1/2)_(8,1) norm. Its positive order is useful for multiplication and composition; no zero-order L-infinity Besov algebra property is assumed. First test whether a full gradient in L^(4,1) controls this norm, with a low-frequency value term. This is an embedding of the full gradient, not an assertion that the geometric strain alone controls a full connection jet.

Freeze E53 on the unit flat T4: define smooth dyadic blocks, prove or disprove

    ||U||B^(1/2)_(8,1) <= C (||U||L8 + ||dU||L^(4,1)),

and derive the damped heat estimate with the sum of blockwise time suprema and the L1-time B^(5/2)_(8,1) norm. The latter should control integral ||D2 U||infinity. One allowed direct proof uses the frequency-localized inverse derivative and L2/L8 interpolation with theta=2/3, giving L^(4,1); all interpolation and low-frequency details must be supplied rather than named.

If this closes, freeze E54 on a specified vector system U_t=Delta U−U+A(U)D2U+Q(U)[DU,DU], A(0)=0 and smooth value maps near zero. Test a small-data contraction in those critical parabolic norms, with exact product and difference estimates. This would be a genuinely quasilinear flat model, adding a principal-coefficient mechanism to E23, but still would not be the geometric flow. Its transfer to the compact bundle, evolving reference, original gauge and intrinsic T requires separate decisions. E53 is useful independently while E51/E52 audit the round stability theorem.
