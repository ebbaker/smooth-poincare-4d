### Fine v2, the same equation and distinct existence statements

Joel Fine, *A gauge theoretic approach to the anti-self-dual
Einstein equations*, arXiv:1111.5005v2, posted 29 November 2011
at 11:54:34 UTC. The version record lists v2 as the latest shown;
the author bibliography lists the preprint without journal
details. Journal publication remains unverified.
[Version record](https://arxiv.org/abs/1111.5005v2) ·
[Author bibliography](https://joel.fine.web.ulb.be/papers.html).

1. **Transition from §4.1 to §4.2, printed p. 30.** Proposition
   4.2, proof and (4.1), followed by Theorem 4.3 and the opening
   recovery discussion, were checked in versioned HTML. The
   equation is \(-d_A^{*g_A}(B_AF_A)\) with
   \(B_A=4Q-\frac23\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)I\). The formal adjoint
   uses the current reconstructed positive Riemannian metric.
   Since \(F_A\) is self-dual and satisfies Bianchi,
   \(d_A^{*g_A}F_A=0\). With \(q=Q-I\) the coefficient
   becomes \(2I+4(q-|q|^2I/6)\), giving precisely the branch
   native vector field. This is the trace-three independent
   \(SO(3)\) connection problem, with the orientation and
   sign as in the inherited R15 record. The nearby displayed
   Yang–Mills equation is for a fixed metric and unweighted
   curvature. Theorem 4.3 states short-time existence from a
   supplied definite connection and uniqueness on the
   existence interval. For the smooth compact problem here,
   this statement stays within the definite class and does
   not assert a spectral ratio bound. The branch uses its
   separately checked corrected local theorem and recovery.
   The source statement alone does not replace those checks.
   [Fine v2 §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2#S4.SS1) ·
   [Fine v2 §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2#S4.SS2) ·
   [Branch local assessment](E28_E29_LOCAL_FLOW_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).
2. **§4.3, Theorem 4.11, printed p. 37.** The statement
   assumes a supplied perfect connection \(\widehat A\)
   on a compact four-manifold, integer \(k\ge3\), and a
   definite initial connection sufficiently close to
   \(\widehat A\) in fixed-background \(L^2_k\). It states
   global original evolution with convergence at
   \(o(e^{-\alpha t})\) for some \(\alpha>0\) in \(L^2_k\)
   after time-dependent gauge transformations from the
   larger bundle-action group. It concerns the same flow
   under stronger data hypotheses and its asymptotic limit.
   An initial ratio cap alone is absent from the hypotheses,
   and retention of the exact initial ratio supremum is
   absent from the conclusion. In particular the checked
   statement does not contradict short-time growth followed
   by near-round attraction. The E248–E250 result has only
   the arbitrary-\(r\) short-time scope specified above.
   [Fine v2 §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2#S4.SS3).

### Fine–Krasnov–Panov, a separate volume gradient

[Published volume and title page](https://nyjm.albany.edu/j/2014/20-18v.pdf).

   displayed upward gradient is
   \(\Lambda d_A^{*g_A}\Phi_A/(6\pi^2)\). In the inherited
   §§2.3–2.5 notation, \(S(A)=\frac{\Lambda^2}{12\pi^2}
   \int \nu_A\) is the connection's reconstructed-volume

   \sqrt{M_\nu})^2\nu\) for reference volume \(\nu\), and

   The real Riemannian definite-connection setting uses

   definite initial connection the theorem states short-time
   flow in this compact-manifold setting. No ratio cap is in

   branch counterexample transfers between them by rescaling

### Fine–He–Yao, scalar matrix equation under torus symmetry

Joel Fine, Weiyong He and Chengjian Yao, *Convergence of the
hypersymplectic flow on \(\mathbb T^4\) with \(\mathbb T^3\)-symmetry*,
arXiv:2404.15016v1, sole listed version, posted 23 April 2024
at 13:22:43 UTC. The arXiv metadata records J. Lond. Math. Soc.
(2) **111** (2025), no. 6. Fine's author list still says
“to appear.” The inspected body is the versioned preprint.
Journal DOI, article number, publication day and final body
were not verified. The HTML's separate 24 August 2026 display
date is not the arXiv submission date.
[Version and journal record](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15016) ·
[Author bibliography](https://joel.fine.web.ulb.be/papers.html).

5. **§1.4, (1.3)–(1.4), Definition 1.4 and Theorem 1.5.**
   The statement assumes a supplied smooth hypersymplectic
   triple on the compact real four-torus, invariant under
   translations in the last three coordinates, in symmetric
   normal form. Its coefficient \(\alpha_{ij}(x_0)\)
   in (1.4) is symmetric. The closed triple orients the
   reconstructed positive Riemannian metric and consists
   of its self-dual forms. The theorem states global
   hypersymplectic flow and convergence after a path of base
   diffeomorphisms starting at the identity to a cohomologous
   flat hyperkähler limit. The symmetric-normal-form premise
   is retained from the theorem statement. There is no
   connection sign or \(S^4\) datum here.
   [Fine–He–Yao v1 §1.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15016v1#S1.SS4).
6. **§2.3, Lemma 2.14 and Proposition 2.15/(2.5)–(2.8).**
   For the same symmetric-normal-form data and its smooth
   flow, \(Q=\alpha/V\), \(V=(\det\alpha)^{1/3}\), so
   \(\det Q=1\). The reduced equation is
   \(\partial_t Q_{ij}=\Delta_{g_{\underline\omega(t)}}Q_{ij}
   -\frac13\mathcal T Q_{ij}\), where
   \(\mathcal T=2|\mathbf T|^2\ge0\) is torsion of the
   associated \(G_2\) structure on \(\mathbb T^7\). The same
   scalar metric Laplacian acts on every fixed matrix entry.
   The checked proof includes the direct reduction from the
   symmetric coefficient equation. It is a differential
   equation for the matrix of closed forms in a fixed
   triple labelling, with the live triple metric.
   [Fine–He–Yao v1 §2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15016v1#S2.SS3).

**Comparison inference from (2.6).** For any two fixed
Euclidean unit vectors \(e,b\in\mathbb R^3\) and any fixed
\(r>1\), the scalar \(H=e^TQe-r\,b^TQb\) satisfies
\[
  (\partial_t-\Delta_{g_{\underline\omega(t)}})H
                   =-\tfrac13\mathcal T H.
\]
The ratio bound everywhere at the initial time is equivalent
to \(H\le0\) there for all such fixed pairs. On any compact
smooth time slab the evolving metric and coefficients are smooth
and the Laplacian is uniformly elliptic. The scalar maximum
principle on the closed torus preserves the sign for each pair.
Since this holds for all
fixed pairs, it bounds the largest Rayleigh quotient by \(r\)
times the smallest at each point and time. Thus the same
ratio sublevel is invariant along the supplied symmetric
triple flow on its existence interval. This deduction is
explicitly from the displayed equation, rather than a ratio
theorem attributed verbatim to the paper. Its scalar
Laplacian and reaction on fixed entries are the relevant
difference from the independent-connection quotient equation
in E248–E250. The closed-cap conclusions therefore concern
different evolutions, even though the ratio is unchanged by
scalar renormalization of either positive matrix.
[Primary equation (2.6)](https://arxiv.org/html/2404.15016v1#S2.SS3).
