## 1. Positive planes and reconstruction

Fix the orientation and a reference Euclidean metric \(g\) on a real
four-space. The wedge form, divided by \(dV_g\), is positive on
\(\Lambda_g^+\) and negative on \(\Lambda_g^-\). In the manuscript's bases
\(\Sigma_i^\pm\) their Gram matrices are \(\pm2I\). A wedge-positive plane
\(P\) projects isomorphically onto \(\Lambda_g^+\), since the kernel would
lie in the negative plane. Write the rows generating \(P\) as
\(\Sigma^++T\Sigma^-\). Positivity is exactly \(I-TT^T>0\).

Every conformal class has a unique representative with volume \(dV_g\),
of the form \(g_H=g(e^H\,\cdot,\cdot)\) with self-adjoint trace-free \(H\).
The positive coframe transform \(e^{H/2}\) acts infinitesimally on two-forms
by \(\mathscr D_H\). In a diagonal frame it multiplies \(e^{ab}\) by
\((h_a+h_b)/2\), whose value on the complementary pair is its negative.
Therefore \(\mathscr D_H\) is off-diagonal on the two chiral spaces.
Its upper-right block \(B=\beta_g(H)\) has diagonal entries
\((h_1+h_j)/2\), \(j=2,3,4\), in that frame. The identity
\(\sum_{j=2}^4(h_1+h_j)^2=\sum_{a=1}^4h_a^2\), equivariance under
oriented orthogonal frames and the equal dimensions \(9\) give
\(\lvert\beta_g(H)\rvert^2=\lvert H\rvert^2/4\) and invertibility.

The other off-diagonal block of \(\mathscr D_H\) is \(B^T\) by
self-adjointness. The first block row of its exponential is
\((\cosh D,(\sinh D/D)B)\), \(D=(BB^T)^{1/2}\), where the quotient is
extended at zero. Thus the graph matrix of \(\Lambda^+_{g_H}\) is
\[
 T=\frac{\tanh D}{D}B,\qquad
 B=\frac{\operatorname{arctanh}\sqrt{TT^T}}{\sqrt{TT^T}}T .
\]
The inverse follows by applying the two transformations to the signed
singular values. The inverse matrix function is analytic even at singular
or repeated eigenvalues, as its power series is
\(\sum_{m\ge 0}(TT^T)^m/(2m+1)\) on \(\|T\|_{\rm op}<1\).
Combining it with \(\beta_g^{-1}\) establishes existence, uniqueness
and smooth dependence for all positive planes.

For a smooth \(SO(3)\) connection \(A\) on a fixed oriented Euclidean
rank-three bundle \(E\), use \([e_i,e_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}e_k\) in its oriented
orthonormal frames, with internal metric
\(\delta_{ij}=-2\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C^2}(T_iT_j)\) in the
local \(\mathfrak{su}(2)\) model. In particular this convention applies
without requiring a global spin lift. For positive curvature plane
\(P=\operatorname{span}\{F_i\}\), the scalar four-form
\(\mu=\tfrac13\sum_iF_i\wedge F_i\) is positive and frame independent.
The normalized curvature Gram matrix \(Q\) satisfies
\(F_i\wedge F_j=Q_{ij}\mu\), \(Q>0\) and \(\operatorname{tr}Q=3\).
Fix \(K>0\). Scaling a representative \(g_*\) of the conformal class by
\(\big(\mu/(2K^2 dV_{g_*})\big)^{1/2}\) yields the unique
\(\widehat g_A\) with \(dV_{\widehat g_A}=\mu/(2K^2)\).
The plane and scale are invariant under changes of internal frame, so
local smooth constructions agree on overlaps. The adapted triple
\[
 S=Q^{1/2},\qquad \Sigma_A=-K^{-1}S^{-1}F,\qquad
 (\Sigma_A)_i\wedge(\Sigma_A)_j
     =K^{-2}\mu\,\delta_{ij}=2dV_{\widehat g_A}\,\delta_{ij}
\]
gives the isometry \(E\to\Lambda_{\widehat g_A}^+\) after division by
\(\sqrt2\). It has the handedness of the round adapted triple when the
round curvature-map sign is imposed. Wedge positivity and the sign of
the curvature map are separate properties. Fine v2 Definition 1.6 fixes
its target orientation via the Lie algebra, with the sign of a perfect
connection agreeing with scalar curvature. In the manuscript's
round-compatible ordered coframe \(\det(-KS)<0\); this coefficient sign
does not change the sign assigned to the positive-scalar curvature round
connection by Fine. For example under the skew-matrix identification
\(\Sigma_i\longmapsto(\eta_{i,ab})\), the round self-dual matrices satisfy
\([\eta_i,\eta_j]=-2\epsilon_{ijk}\eta_k\), and \(-\eta_i/2\) give
the bracket-oriented basis. On the standard sphere,
\(F_0=-K\Sigma_0^+\) gives \(Q_0=I\) and
\(\mu_0=2K^2dV_{g_0}\). Hence \(\widehat g_{A_0}=g_0\). The proof
requires a round reference only for this benchmark and for specifying
the sign by comparison. [Fine v2, Definitions 1.3–1.6 and §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2).

## 2. Exact derivative and checks

Take a smooth path \(A(t)\) on the fixed bundle through positive
curvature planes, in a fixed local frame of \(E\). Allow the reconstructed
chiral form bases to vary with \(A\). At the time of evaluation write
\(v=\partial_t A\), \(\dot F=d_Av=U\Sigma_A+V\Sigma^-\),
\(h=\partial_t\widehat g_A\), \(H=\widehat g_A^{-1}h\) and
\(H_0=H-\tfrac14(\operatorname{tr}H)I\). Let \(t=0\) denote that time.
In the splitting evaluated at \(t=0\), the graph of the curvature
plane at nearby \(s\) has matrix
\[
 (-KS+sU+O(s^2))^{-1}(sV+O(s^2)).
\]
Its derivative is \(-K^{-1}S^{-1}V\). The derivative of the graph from
the metric is \(\beta_{\widehat g_A}(H_0)\) by §1. The volume-changing
part of \(H\) leaves the plane fixed. This proves the trace-free
variation, including the order \(S^{-1}V\).

Write \(Z_{ij}=\dot F_i\wedge F_j/\mu\). In the normalized-scale metric
\(F=-KS\Sigma_A\) and the chiral wedge relations give
\[
 Z=-\frac{1}{K}US,\qquad
 \frac{\dot\mu}{\mu}=\frac23\operatorname{tr}Z
    =-\frac{2}{3K}\operatorname{tr}(SU).
\]
The equation \(F_i\wedge F_j=\mu Q_{ij}\) gives
\[
 \dot Q=Z+Z^T-\frac23\operatorname{tr}(Z)Q
     =-\frac1K(US+SU^T)+\frac2{3K}\operatorname{tr}(SU)Q.
\]
In particular \(\operatorname{tr}\dot Q=0\), and the trace subtraction
involves \(Q\) throughout the definite region. Differentiating the
reconstructed volume gives
\[
 \operatorname{tr}H=2\frac{\dot\mu}{\mu}
                   =-\frac4{3K}\operatorname{tr}(SU),\qquad
 H=-K^{-1}\beta_{\widehat g_A}^{-1}(S^{-1}V)
   -\frac1{3K}\operatorname{tr}(SU)I.
\]
Fine's \(L_A(d_Av)\) is exactly \((Z+Z^T)/2\) when using its own
orthonormal internal frame and curvature volume. The metric-independent
four-form ratios \(Z\) and \(Q\) keep the same matrices under the common
constant rescaling in §4. This gives the same normalized derivative in
the two conventions. [Fine v2, §3.1, Lemma 3.1, equations (3.1)–(3.3)](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2).

If internal component columns are changed by \(R(t,x)\in SO(3)\), put
\(Q'=RQR^{-1}\), \(\Omega=\dot R R^{-1}\). The fixed-frame tensor
\(\dot Q\) has new components \(\partial_tQ'-[\Omega,Q']\); the
curvature variation has components \(\partial_tF'-\Omega F'\).
These express covariance of the identities. Choosing a PDE gauge
or adding a base diffeomorphism to the flow is a separate step.

- **Curvature scale check.** At a point \(\dot F=\alpha F\) gives
  \(U=-\alpha KS\), \(V=0\), hence \(\dot Q=0\),
  \(\dot\mu/\mu=2\alpha\), \(h=\alpha\widehat g_A\).
- **Internal rotation check.** At a point \(\dot F=\Omega F\) for
  skew \(\Omega\) gives \(U=-K\Omega S\), \(V=0\) and
  \(\operatorname{tr}(SU)=0\). Thus \(\dot Q=[\Omega,Q]\) and
  \(h=0\), as expected for the corresponding active curvature
  rotation in a fixed frame.
- **Chiral shear check.** At round, the diagonal conformal-class
  variation \(H_0=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-1,-1)\) in
  `rec:form-bases` has graph velocity \(\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)\).
  The formula requires \(V=-K\operatorname{diag}(1,0,0)\).
  More generally a specified \(V\) with \(U=0\) can be realized
  in \(d_Av\) at one point by prescribing the first jet of \(v\)
  there, with \(v=0\) at that point. It contributes to \(h_0\) with
  \(\dot Q=0\) and pointwise zero volume derivative. This check
  concerns arbitrary connection variations.
- **Total volume check.** Bianchi gives
  \(\dot\mu=\tfrac23 d\langle v\wedge F\rangle_E\), with an
  invariant global three-form on the right. On a closed four-manifold
  the total reconstructed volume is therefore constant for any such
  path. On the round sphere the direct computation
  \(2K^2\operatorname{Vol}(g_0)=16\pi^2/3\) agrees with
  \(\int\mu=(4\pi^2/3)\langle p_1(E),[S^4]\rangle\),
  \(p_1[\Lambda^+]=4\). This fixes the total volume for the fixed
  round chiral bundle, while \(\dot\mu(x)\) can vary with \(x\).

## 3. Compatibility identity

Transport the chiral Levi–Civita connection to \(E\) by the adapted
isometry and denote it by \(\Gamma_A^+\). The tautological form
\(\Sigma_A\) is parallel for the induced connection, so
\(d_{\Gamma_A^+}\Sigma_A=0\). For \(b=A-\Gamma_A^+\), Bianchi gives
\[
 \mathcal T_A=d_A\Sigma_A=[b,\Sigma_A]
     =-K^{-1}(\nabla_A S^{-1})\wedge F
     =-[S^{-1}(\nabla_A S)]\wedge\Sigma_A .
\]
Here \(\nabla_A S\) denotes the induced covariant derivative on
\(\operatorname{End}E\). The order in its contraction with \(S^{-1}\)
is essential.

For the round-compatible triple in the `rec:compatibility` coframe,
write \(J_i\alpha=*(\alpha\wedge(\Sigma_A)_i)\). Its matrix is
\((\eta_{i,dc})_{c,d}\). The explicit \(\eta\) components give
\(J_iJ_j=-\delta_{ij}I+\epsilon_{ijk}J_k\) and \(J_i^*=-J_i\).
Thus \(\mathscr C b=*[b,\Sigma_A]\) has blocks
\(\mathscr C_{ij}=\epsilon_{ijk}J_k\). They give
\[
 (\mathscr C^2)_{i\ell}
    =J_\ell J_i-\delta_{i\ell}\textstyle\sum_k J_k^2
    =2\delta_{i\ell}I-\mathscr C_{i\ell}.
\]
The two skew factors in its blocks make \(\mathscr C\) self-adjoint.
Its eigenvalues are \(1\) and \(-2\), of multiplicities \(8\) and \(4\)
by its vanishing trace and dimension \(12\). Hence
\(\mathscr C^{-1}=(\mathscr C+I)/2\), \(b=\mathscr C^{-1}*\mathcal T_A\)
and \(|b|\le|\mathcal T_A|\le2|b|\). The formula controls \(b\) in terms
of a first covariant derivative of \(S\) with an inverse \(S\) factor.
Perfection \(Q=I\) yields \(b=0\). With \(b=0\), the curvature of the
chiral Levi–Civita connection has zero opposite-chirality block and
\(\widehat g_A\) is Einstein. Perfection in the chosen round
normalization also gives \(W^+=0\) and \(s=12K\) by the chiral curvature
decomposition. Fine's torsion criterion applies to the transported
metric connection. Away from perfection the unnormalized curvature
map and the adapted isometry generally differ, so the condition
\(d_AF=0\) alone in the curvature identification does not identify
the metric connection transported by the adapted isometry.
[Fine v2, §2.1, Proposition 2.3 and Lemmas 2.2 and 2.4, and §2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2).

## 4. Gradient and time normalization

For a smooth variation \(a\) on a closed base, \(Q\) has trace \(3\)
and \(\int\mu\) is constant. Thus
\(\mathcal P=\int(\operatorname{tr}Q^2-3)\mu\) has first variation
\[
 \delta\mathcal P(a)=2\int\operatorname{tr}(Q\dot Q)\mu
                      +\int\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)\dot\mu
    =\int\langle d_Aa\wedge B F\rangle_E,\qquad
 B=4Q-\tfrac23\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)I.
\]
The last equality uses the two variation identities for \(Z\) in §2
and symmetry of \(Q\). Since \(BF\) is self-dual, the wedge pairing is
exactly the two-form metric pairing integrated against
\(dV_{\widehat g_A}\). Integration by parts for the manuscript's
one-form inner product gives \(\nabla\mathcal P=d_A^{*\widehat g_A}(BF)\).
There is no additional \(2K^2\) in that pairing. Bianchi and
\(*_{\widehat g_A}F=F\) give \(d_A^{*\widehat g_A}F=0\).
With \(q=Q-I\), one has \(B=2I+4q-\tfrac23|q|^2 I\); hence
\[
 v=-4d_A^{*\widehat g_A}\big[(q-\tfrac16|q|^2I)F\big],
 \qquad
 \frac{d\mathcal P}{dt}=-\|v\|^2_{L^2(\widehat g_A,\delta)}.
\]
The gradient is defined by the inner product at the point \(A\) in
the space of connections, so its variation is unnecessary for this
directional derivative. At round \(q=0\) and the flow is stationary.
This is the gradient on the space of independent connections. The
metric energy pullback additionally differentiates \(g\mapsto A_g^+\)
and takes the adjoint for a metric-variation inner product.
[Fine v2, Definition 1.10, §4.1, Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.2, equation (4.1)](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2).

To compare the metric and time with Fine's printed convention, set
\(L_i=\operatorname{ad}e_i\) in the real rank-three representation.
Then \(L_i u=e_i\times u\), and
\(\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb R^3}(L_iL_j)=-2\delta_{ij}\). For the
curvature \(\mathcal F_A=\sum F_i L_i\) as a skew endomorphism,
\[
 p_1(A)=-\frac{1}{8\pi^2}
           \operatorname{Tr}_{\mathbb R^3}(\mathcal F_A\wedge\mathcal F_A)
        =\frac{1}{4\pi^2}\sum_iF_i\wedge F_i .
\]
Fine v2 Definition 1.3 uses the Killing form for the internal norm,
and Definition 1.4 and Proposition 1.12 set the precise positive
normalization through \(\mu_{\rm F}=(8\pi^2/3)p_1(A)\). The real
negative Killing norm is \(2\delta\) on the bracket basis above.
Consequently \(\mu_{\rm F}=2\mu\) and \(Q_{\rm F}=Q\) as endomorphisms.
The canonical volume stipulation in Fine then gives
\(g_A^{\rm F}=2K\,\widehat g_A\). If Fine's definitions are instead
expressed from the outset with the internal metric \(\delta\), the
corresponding canonical volume is \(\mu\), giving
\(g_A^{{\rm F},\delta}=\sqrt2 K\,\widehat g_A\). The latter is the
comparison printed at manuscript lines 1502–1506, with the
internal normalization conversion implicit there.
[Fine v2, Definitions 1.3–1.4 and Proposition 1.12](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2).

For a constant \(c>0\) and two-forms,
\(d_A^{*(c\widehat g_A)}=c^{-1}d_A^{*\widehat g_A}\).
Since \(B\) as an endomorphism and \(\mathcal F_A\) are the same,
the manuscript vector field is \(2K\) times the vector field in
Fine's printed normalization. Equivalently
\(A_{\rm manuscript}(t)=A_{\rm F}(2Kt)\) for corresponding solutions.
The \(\delta\)-normalized version has the factor \(\sqrt2 K\).
This also follows from the respective energy and one-form norms
scaling by \(2\) and \(4K\) in the printed comparison. Accordingly
constants or rates read directly in Fine's time require conversion.
The manuscript's internal \(t\), \(-4\) vector field, and its
normalization relative to comparisons carried out wholly within
that \(t\) stay as computed. [Fine v2, §1.6 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2).

## Premises and packet boundary

The plane construction and component computations are pointwise.
Global reconstruction assumes a globally defined definite connection
on the fixed bundle. Smoothness of the velocity identities uses a
smooth path on an interval on which \(Q>0\) and \(\mu>0\) hold at every
point. The scale parameter \(K\) is fixed in time. The identities
hold in fixed internal and base identifications, with the stated
covariant time derivative for a moving internal frame. No spatial
gauge fixing or additional conditions on \(b\) are required for
these calculations. Closedness enters total volume, integration by
parts and the stated energy law. The benchmark uses the standard
smooth \(S^4\) and its induced chiral connection.

These results supply exact formulas to use on the open definite
region. Existence of such initial data on an arbitrary smooth
homotopy four-sphere, preservation of the region, metric and
compatibility derivative estimates and nonlinear analytic closure
remain separate research dependencies. E01A ends with the audited
identities and the source-normalization correction for lead integration.
Fine v2 was consulted as full-text HTML at the cited passages.
The local reading PDF was identified by hash. No additional review
status is claimed for passages outside the assigned calculations.
