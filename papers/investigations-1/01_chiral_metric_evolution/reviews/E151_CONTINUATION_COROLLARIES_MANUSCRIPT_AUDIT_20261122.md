## Disposition

**Scientific integration passes, with local precision edits
below.** The sequential criterion has one common original
lifespan for smooth compatible data in one fixed reference
ball. It uses existence of one uniformly value-convergent
normalized sequence on the same bundle and gives an equivalence
with smooth definite extendibility there. The real criterion
uses the complete target sphere and has the assessed universal
constants throughout the chosen positive definite component.

assessed original continuation theorem. Neither corollary
supplies an a priori estimate or the compactness required to
produce a positive accumulation pair.

## Sequential value accumulation and original recovery

At lines 3430–3448 the original supplied path, native time,
fixed smooth principal bundle and signed definite category
are inherited from Theorem `thm:original-positive-endpoint`.
The connection values and curvature values of one sequence
of time slices approach one continuous definite pair after
smooth equivariant maps covering orientation-preserving
diffeomorphisms of the same base. Orientation and bundle
equivariance preserve the signed positive component. The
limit stays in that sign because it is definite and the
curvature-map sign is locally constant there. The fixed
affine identification for the connections is explicit in
the compatibility proof at 3471–3475. It may also be named
in the statement, together with \(\tau_n\in[0,\tau_*)\).
There is no monotonicity requirement on the supplied
sequence. The converse can take any increasing sequence
to \(\tau_*\) and the identity maps.

Lines 3451–3469 give precisely the radius in E148,
\[
 r_{\rm data}=\min\{\epsilon/2,\rho_{\rm gr}/(2L_0),R/(4L_0)\}.
\]
The first bound puts every smooth datum in this ball within
the same pair-value radius. The second controls the graph
symbol in its own reference pairing, and the third leaves
room in the completed \(X^\circ\) ball after reference norm
comparison. At each individual smooth trace the proof of
the preceding theorem supplies a smooth graph lift on some
short interval of its own. Restriction of the rough fixed
point and short-slab completed uniqueness identify that
lift, so the adjusted path is smooth through its own zero.
It is smooth on every later compact positive strip. This
gives the required smooth generator on every closed
subinterval of \([0,T_{\rm ref})\), with no common
derivative bound for the generators. Smoothness and compactness
of the principal bundle give the same original time
\(3T_{\rm ref}/4\) for every smooth compatible datum in
this one ball. The reference may be nonstationary. Its
physical velocity is retained.

At 3471–3487, naturality gives \(F_n=F_{A_n}\) for
\(A_n=h_n^*A(\tau_n)\). In a fixed smooth affine background
\(A_b\), \(d_{A_b}(A_n-A_b)\) passes to the limit by testing
with the fixed smooth adjoint, while \(F_n\) and the quadratic
bracket converge uniformly. The limit is thus distributionally
compatible. The endpoint graph is compact and lies in the
open positive component. A closed fiber-ball tube gives a
compact common range. The radii are chosen on that range
before picking the smooth compatible approximation by the
density argument in the rough-local proof. The density
argument works in an arbitrary smooth affine background and
requires no nearby smooth compatible reference as input.
Choose that one reference within \(\delta\), then fix its
positive \(T_{\rm ref}\), and only then take \(n\) so that
\(\|(A_n-\bar A,F_n-\bar F)\|<2\delta<r_{\rm data}\)
and \(\tau_*-\tau_n<T_{\rm ref}/2\). Name the tube's
radius \(r_{\rm tube}>0\) where it is chosen at 3475–3477
before using it in the bound for \(\delta\). There is
no simultaneous choice along a sequence of different
references with shrinking times. [E148][e148] ·
[Sequential assessment][a148].

At 3336–3370 and in the application at 3489–3505 the
fixed vertical adjoint, live horizontal adjoint and signs
agree with E140. In particular
\[
 \beta=d_{\mathcal A}(\mathcal A-\bar A),\quad
 \phi=d_{\mathcal A}^{*\bar g}(\mathcal A-\bar A),\quad
 z=S_F^*d_{\mathcal A}^{*g}\beta,\quad
 \xi=-\phi-\chi_{\mathcal A}(z).
\]
The infinitesimal action under the inverse-pullback left
action is \(R(\xi,\mathcal A)=-\mathcal L_\xi\mathcal A\).
Thus \(\mathcal A_s=V(\mathcal A)+\mathcal L_\xi\mathcal A\)
and \(\dot j=-\xi\circ j\) cancels the Lie term in the time
derivative of \(A^\sharp=j^*\mathcal A\). Returning to the
old identification uses the single fixed inverse map,
\[
 (h_n^{-1})^* A^\sharp(s)
   =(j(s)\circ h_n^{-1})^*\mathcal A(s),\qquad
 (h_n^{-1})^* h_n^*A(\tau_n)=A(\tau_n).
\]
The fixed map preserves the original vector field, and
the clock only undergoes a translation. Smooth original
uniqueness can be recentered at any common smooth slice
before \(\tau_*\), so the maximal overlap of equality
cannot end earlier. The time \(3T_{\rm ref}/4\) crosses
\(\tau_*\) strictly after translation. The same bundle,
old identification and definite sign persist under the
splice. No derivatives of a sequence of normalizations
are needed. [E148][e148] · [General continuation assessment][a140].

Lines 3511–3523 retain both boundaries. Supplied
normalized convergence is a hypothesis. The common time
does not ensure continuity of the zero-based original
solution map with respect to the pair \(C^0\) norm.
For compatible data converging in the fixed reference
ball, the adjusted pairs and their recovery generators
converge smoothly on each fixed interior compact strip.
Identity-start recovery at \(s_0>0\) then converges by

as E150 required. For precision, say “on a fixed strip

zero-based \(j\) as one for smooth data. This avoids
suggesting recovery at zero from general continuous data.

## Real transport comparison and continuation

At 3527–3559 the pointwise criterion is stated on any
supplied native definite slice in the chosen component.
The norm uses real round area \(4\pi\) on the complete
target sphere. The derivative is along the target
\(\Gamma\)-horizontal lift; subtracting
\(\mathcal R_{\nabla^g_YX}\) makes it tensorial in the old
base slot, while \(Y\) is the new covariant derivative
slot. Since \(P_f\) and \(f\) are parallel along this
lift, the projected derivative is
\(P_f(\nabla^\Gamma_YT)_Xf\). All four base components
of \(\mathcal R\) and all sixteen ordered components of
its derivative are squared and summed before fiber
integration. This is neither an alternating two-form
norm nor the Hermitian \((1,0)\) norm.
[E145][e145] · [E147][e147].

Lines 3570–3593 give the correct round second and fourth
moments. The mixed real pairing for symmetric trace-free
\(M,N\) has coefficient \(4\pi/3-8\pi/15=4\pi/5\)
on \(\operatorname{tr}(MN)\). The projected first-matrix
moment against \(f\) subtracts
\((4\pi/15)((\operatorname{tr}M)I+2M)\) from
\((4\pi/3)M\), giving \((4\pi/5)M\). Both recoveries
and the two \(L^2_f\) norms therefore match the assessed
pointwise formula. No source-to-target area conversion
is made. The formulas are regular at both poles.

Lines 3595–3625 recover the native estimates on the
full positive trace-three cone. The relative Sylvester
ratio \((s_j-s_i)/(s_i+s_j)\) is uniformly bounded by
one and zero when \(s_i=s_j\), with no differentiated
eigenframes or quantitative floor. Its symmetric part
has squared norm at most \(3|T_X|^2\) by
\(|Q-I|^2\le6\), which gives \(|P|\le2|T|\).
The compatibility operator is half the unit-bracket
operator with eigenvalues \(1,-2\), because native
bracket and unit self-dual forms each cost \(1/\sqrt2\).
Its inverse norm is two. The right-hand wedge-star
bound \(\sqrt{3/2}|P|\) yields \(|b|\le2\sqrt6|T|\).
The two base connections differ only in the internal
endomorphism slot. Taking
\(|[B_Y,T_X]|\le 2\|B_Y\|_{\rm op}|T_X|
=\sqrt2|b_Y||T_X|\) and summing both base slots gives
the stated \(C_0=4\sqrt3\).
[E145][e145] · [E147][e147].

For \(k=\sqrt{4\pi/5}\) and \(y=|T|^2\),
\(\Psi=k|\nabla^\Gamma T|+k^2y\).
Its lower comparison uses
\(|\nabla^AT|+y\le |\nabla^\Gamma T|+(1+C_0)y\)
and \(k\le1+C_0\). Its upper comparison has coefficients
\(k\) and \(k(C_0+k)\), with the second larger. This
gives exactly \(k^2/(1+C_0)\) and \(k(C_0+k)\).
At zero strain the equal derivative norms give
\(\Psi=k\mathcal G\). The proof takes the spatial supremum
of the pointwise sum and then the native time integral,
with the square on the remainder norm only. Nonnegative
monotone limits give the same constants on half-open or
infinite intervals. The finite-\(\Psi\)-budget implication
uses the pointwise lower bound and the assessed
finite-\(\mathcal J_*\) criterion. A finite maximal smooth
definite time therefore forces divergence of the real
budget as well. That last sentence may be stated explicitly
to mirror the assessed corollary. [Real assessment][a145].

**Precision at 3642–3646.** Attribute the base and fiber
naturality to the appropriate maps. With \(h\) covering
\(\varphi\), the pullback metric is \(g'=\varphi^*g\)
and \(d\varphi\) identifies its Levi–Civita connection
with the original one. The induced sphere map from the
fiber isometries takes the pulled-back chiral horizontal
lifts to the original ones and preserves round target
area. The current shorthand attributes preservation of
the live base connection to the fiber isometry alone.
With the explicit maps, both scalars compose with
\(\varphi\) and their base suprema are invariant. This
works slice by slice with no time differentiation of
the pulling-back family. [E145, naturality][e145].
