## Claim 5 — algebraic bound and fixed-bundle integral

Work in a local oriented orthonormal adjoint frame with
\([T_i,T_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}T_k\) and \(\delta_{ij}=-2\operatorname{tr}(T_iT_j)\) in the defining two-dimensional complex representation.
Here \(F_A=dA+\frac12[A\wedge A]\),
\(\mu_A=\frac13\sum_i F_i\wedge F_i\), and \(Q_{ij}=F_i\wedge F_j/\mu_A\).
An SO(3) gauge change conjugates \(Q\) and preserves \(\mu_A\). The sign
\(F_{0i}=-K\Sigma_i^+\) is fixed at the round benchmark and the curvature
plane is positive for the chosen base orientation. The sign of an individual
frame entry cannot change any wedge norm used here.

For \(Q>0\), \(\operatorname{tr}Q=3\), and eigenvalues \(\lambda_i>0\),
\[
 |Q-I|^2=\sum_i(\lambda_i-1)^2=\sum_i\lambda_i^2-3
 =6-2\sum_{i<j}\lambda_i\lambda_j<6.
\]
For \(m=\lambda_{\min}(Q)\le 1\), the last two eigenvalue deviations
have sum \(1-m\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives
\[
 |Q-I|^2\ge (1-m)^2+\tfrac12(1-m)^2,
 \qquad m\ge 1-\sqrt{2/3}\,|Q-I|.
\]
Equality in this lower bound occurs when the other two eigenvalues agree.
The matrix range includes families approaching \(\operatorname{diag}(3,0,0)\),
so the universal pointwise upper bound is a supremum. A bound
\(\sup|Q-I|\le d<\sqrt{3/2}\) gives the uniform lower bound
\(m\ge 1-\sqrt{2/3}\,d>0\).

For the integrated estimate, take any smooth affine path of connections
\(A_s\) on the same SO(3) bundle and write \(a_s=\partial_s A_s\). This path
may pass through indefinite connections. The invariant fiber pairing and
\(d_{A_s}F_{A_s}=0\) give
\[
 \partial_s\mu_{A_s}=\tfrac23\langle d_{A_s}a_s\wedge F_{A_s}\rangle
 =\tfrac23 d\langle a_s\wedge F_{A_s}\rangle.
\]
The scalar pairing inside the exterior derivative is global. Stokes' theorem
on the closed oriented base makes the integral independent of \(A_s\). For
\(A_0\) round, \(\Sigma_i^+\wedge\Sigma_j^+=2\delta_{ij}dV_{g_0}\), so
\(\mu_0=2K^2dV_{g_0}\). The radius is \(K^{-1/2}\), hence
\(\operatorname{Vol}(S^4,g_0)=8\pi^2/(3K^2)\) and
\(\int_{S^4}\mu_A=16\pi^2/3\). The positive volume form \(\mu_A\) then
integrates the strict pointwise inequality to
\(0\le\mathcal P(A)=\int|Q-I|^2\mu_A<32\pi^2\).

[Fine v2, Definitions 1.3–1.5 and 1.9–1.10](https://arxiv.org/html/1111.5005v2)
provides the definite-plane construction, topological volume and trace-square
energy. Definition 1.4 sets the reconstructed volume to Fine's \(\mu\), and
uses the Killing-form fiber convention with
\(\mu_{\rm Fine}=(8\pi^2/3)p_1(A)\). With the standard real three-dimensional
trace, \(\operatorname{tr}(\operatorname{ad}T_i\operatorname{ad}T_j)=-2\delta_{ij}\),
so \(p_1(A)=\left(1/(4\pi^2)\right)\sum_i F_i\wedge F_i\) in the component
norm frozen here. Therefore the manuscript's \(\mu_A=(4\pi^2/3)p_1(A)\).
The two \(Q\) matrices have the same entries in their respective orthonormal
frames because a common factor cancels in the wedge quotient. Returning to
Fine's volume prescription after expressing the curvature in the manuscript's
fiber norm gives \(\sqrt{2}K\,\widehat g_A\). Comparing with Fine's printed
Pontryagin coefficient and native Killing norm instead gives
\(2K\,\widehat g_A\), since \(\mu_{\rm Fine}=2\mu_A\) under the conventional
\(p_1\) identification. This source-normalization distinction leaves the
manuscript's internal algebra and integrated bound intact. In a single
chosen convention \(\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)-|Q-I|^2=3\), so subtracting the
trace-square and defect energies in that convention yields a topological term.

### Exact conformal curvature and reconstruction at the boundary

Let \(p\) be the origin of oriented round normal coordinates, let
\(f=((y^1)^2-(y^2)^2)/2\), and set \(g_\varepsilon=e^{2u}g_0\) with
\(u=\varepsilon f\) and \(z=\varepsilon/K\). These metrics depend smoothly on
\((\varepsilon,y)\) through \(z=1\) on a fixed small coordinate neighborhood.
At \(p\), \(u=du=0\) and
\(\nabla^2u=\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0,0)\). The four-dimensional
conformal Ricci change is
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}_{e^{2u}g_0}
 =\operatorname{Ric}_{g_0}-2(\nabla^2u-du\otimes du)
   -(\Delta u+2|du|^2)g_0.
\]
Thus \(g_\varepsilon(p)=g_0(p)\), \(s(p)=12K\), and
\(\operatorname{Ric}_0(p)=\operatorname{diag}(-2\varepsilon,2\varepsilon,0,0)\).
The Weyl tensor vanishes throughout the chart by conformal covariance.

At \(p\) the Schouten eigenvalues are \(K/2+\rho_a/2\), where \(\rho_a\)
are the displayed trace-free Ricci eigenvalues. Therefore the curvature
operator is diagonal on \(e^{ab}\) with eigenvalue
\(r_{ab}=K+(\rho_a+\rho_b)/2\). In the manuscript's paired self-dual and
anti-self-dual bases
\[
 (e^{12}+e^{34},e^{13}-e^{24},e^{14}+e^{23}),\quad
 (e^{12}-e^{34},e^{13}+e^{24},e^{14}-e^{23}),
\]
which each have squared norm 2, the \(++\) block entries are the half-sums
of the corresponding complementary \(r_{ab}\) and the mixed block entries
are their half-differences. This gives \(C=KI\) and
\(B=\varepsilon\operatorname{diag}(0,-1,-1)\). In the source metric's
oriented chiral frame, \(F=-(C\Sigma^++B\Sigma^-)\). Pairing the forms by
wedge product therefore gives \(F_i\wedge F_j=2M_{ij}dV_{g_\varepsilon}\)
and, at \(p\),
\[
 M=CC^T-BB^T=K^2\operatorname{diag}(1,1-z^2,1-z^2),\qquad
 \frac{\mu_A}{dV_{g_\varepsilon}}=\frac23\operatorname{tr}M
 =\frac{2K^2}{3}(3-2z^2),\qquad Q=\frac{3M}{\operatorname{tr}M}.
\]
This yields precisely the positive range \(|z|<1\) at \(p\). The graph of
\(\operatorname{span}\{F_i\}\) over \(\Lambda^+(g_\varepsilon)\) is
\(\operatorname{diag}(0,-z,-z)\) in these bases. More generally, if the
blocks act by left multiplication on columns of basis forms, the graph as
an operator on coefficient column vectors is \(B^T C^{-T}\). Here it agrees
with the manuscript expression \(C^{-1}B\) because both matrices are diagonal.

For the shape metric \(h=\operatorname{diag}(e^\alpha,e^{-\alpha},1,1)\) in
this coframe, an orthonormal coframe is
\((e^{\alpha/2}e^1,e^{-\alpha/2}e^2,e^3,e^4)\). The first self-dual form
is unchanged. The second has self-dual coefficient \(\cosh(\alpha/2)\) and
anti-self-dual coefficient \(\sinh(\alpha/2)\) in the displayed bases. The
third has the same coefficients in its corresponding pair. The graph is
therefore \(\operatorname{diag}(0,\tanh(\alpha/2),\tanh(\alpha/2))\), and
its equality with the curvature graph gives
\(e^\alpha=(1-z)/(1+z)\). The shape has determinant one. A scalar factor
\(c>0\) on a real four-dimensional metric multiplies its volume by \(c^2\).
Using the prescribed reconstructed volume gives
\[
 \widehat g_A(p)=\sqrt{1-\tfrac23z^2}\,
 \operatorname{diag}\left(\frac{1-z}{1+z},\frac{1+z}{1-z},1,1\right).
\]
The second and third eigenvalues of \(Q-I\) are \(-z^2/(3-2z^2)\) and the
first is \(2z^2/(3-2z^2)\). Thus
\[
 |Q-I|^2=\frac{6z^4}{(3-2z^2)^2},\qquad
 |Q-I|^2\frac{\mu_A}{dV_{g_\varepsilon}}=\frac{4K^2 z^4}{3-2z^2}.
\]
As \(z\uparrow1\), \(Q\to\operatorname{diag}(3,0,0)\), the density tends to
\(4K^2\), and the scalar metric factor tends to \(1/\sqrt3\). The first and
second metric eigenvalues tend to zero and infinity, respectively. The
volume ratio tends to \(1/3\). This exhibits anisotropy at bounded positive
volume density, with a regular source jet and a fixed reference frame.

Definiteness holds on some neighborhood of \(p\) for each parameter in the
open definite range by continuity. The check supplies no neighborhood
uniform in \(z\) and no global definiteness of a cutoff extension near the
boundary. A smooth cutoff gives metrics on the standard \(S^4\) and the same
jet, while it permits earlier loss of definiteness elsewhere. The parameter
is a label for a family of chiral Levi–Civita connections. Neither the
independent-connection equation
\(A_t=-4d_A^{*_{\widehat g_A}}[(q-|q|^2I/6)F_A]\), \(q=Q-I\), nor the
metric pullback gradient was imposed. Energy finiteness is automatic in the
verified fixed-bundle class. This example addresses pointwise algebraic
coercivity and leaves quantitative small-energy thresholds under additional
global or flow hypotheses open.

## Claim 6 — signature, developing map and sphere characterization

Let \(X\) be a smooth closed manifold homotopy equivalent to \(S^4\). The
homotopy equivalence gives connectedness, simple connectivity,
\(H^2(X;\mathbb Z)=0\) and \(\chi(X)=2\). Simple connectivity implies
orientability, and the real degree-two intersection form has dimension zero,
so \(\tau(X)=0\). Choose an orientation. The round pullback under a smooth
diffeomorphism to \(S^4\) is locally conformally flat (LCF). LCF gives
\(W=0\), so it implies ASD in the fixed convention \(W^+=0\).

For a closed oriented differentiable four-manifold, the degree-four signature
theorem is \(\tau(X)=\langle p_1(TX),[X]\rangle/3\). This is Hirzebruch's
own statement in [*The signature theorem: Reminiscences and recreation*,
§4, p. 11, equation (16)](https://hirzebruch.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/84/1/signature%20theorem.pdf).
With the real tangent-bundle curvature matrix \(\Omega\), its Chern–Weil
representative is \(-\operatorname{tr}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)/(8\pi^2)\).
In an orthonormal tangent frame this is
\(\sum_{a<b}\Omega_{ab}\wedge\Omega_{ab}/(4\pi^2)\). In orthonormal Hodge
bases the coefficient of \(dV_g\) in this sum of wedge squares is the norm
squared of the self-dual curvature rows minus that of the anti-self-dual
rows. The off-diagonal Ricci blocks cancel as transpose blocks of equal
norm. The scalar parts \((s/12)I\) cancel because they have equal norm and
both Weyl blocks are trace free. This gives
\[
 12\pi^2\tau(X)=\int_X\bigl(|W^+|_{\rm op}^2-|W^-|_{\rm op}^2\bigr)dV_g,
\]
where \(|\cdot|_{\rm op}\) here denotes the Hilbert–Schmidt endomorphism
norm, not the largest singular value. A four-index contraction counts each
unordered pair twice on each side of the curvature matrix, giving four
times this norm squared. This matches the coefficient \(48\pi^2\) in
[Gover–Gursky (2024), p. 294 following Definition 1.1](https://www.degruyterbrill.com/document/doi/10.1515/crelle-2024-0028/pdf?licenseType=open-access)
and the explicit norm conversion of
[Chang–Gursky–Yang (2003), p. 130, equation (4.13)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf).

For a smooth Riemannian ASD metric on \(X\), \(\tau(X)=0\) forces the integral
of \(|W^-|^2\) to vanish. Positive definiteness of the metric ensures
nonnegativity, and continuity implies pointwise vanishing. Consequently
\(W=0\). The dimension-\(\ge4\) equivalence between Weyl-flatness and LCF
is stated in [Schoen–Yau (1988), §1, pp. 49–50](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen-yau.conformal.pdf).

For the global step, compose local conformal coordinates with inverse
stereographic projection. Liouville's theorem in dimension at least three
extends the transition on each connected overlap component to a unique
Möbius transformation. On triple overlaps the extensions satisfy the
cocycle law by uniqueness on connected open pieces. Continuing a coordinate
germ across a chain of charts by these transformations develops it along a
path. Common refinements of chart chains preserve the germ. A subdivision
of a homotopy square into chart-contained pieces shows invariance under path
homotopy relative to endpoints using the same cocycle law. This gives a
smooth conformal local diffeomorphism on \(\widetilde X\), with the
holonomy representation accounting for changes under deck transformations.
The basic Liouville/development and holonomy statements appear in
[Schoen–Yau (1988), opening p. 47 and §1, pp. 49–50](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen-yau.conformal.pdf).

Since \(X\) is simply connected, \(\widetilde X=X\) and holonomy is trivial.
The image of \(\mathrm{dev}:X\to S^4\) is open by local invertibility and
closed by compactness of \(X\). The target is connected, so the map is onto.
A proper local diffeomorphism is a covering map. Indeed, each fiber is
compact and discrete, hence finite. Use pairwise disjoint inverse-chart
neighborhoods around its points and shrink the target neighborhood using
properness to exclude additional inverse images. This produces evenly
covered neighborhoods. The connected domain and simply connected target
force a one-sheeted covering, hence a smooth diffeomorphism. The smooth
positive conformal factor is global since on each chart its square is
\(\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}_g(\mathrm{dev}^*g_{\rm round})\).

The [Kuiper 1949 paper](https://doi.org/10.2307/1969587), Ann. of Math.
(2) 50, 916–924, remains the historical attribution. The DOI and JSTOR
lookup did not provide readable original article text. The original theorem
number and page were therefore not asserted. The inspected
[Schoen–Yau paper](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen-yau.conformal.pdf),
Invent. Math. 92 (1988), 47–71, contains the precise basic development
passages used above. Its broader Kleinian conclusion, §4, Theorem 4.5,
pp. 65–66, assumes completeness and \(R(g)\ge0\) and concludes injective
development with a discrete holonomy group, giving a quotient description
when the manifold is compact. The sphere argument uses only the basic local
conformal development and its own compact-covering argument. It applies
to a given ASD metric regardless of its scalar-curvature sign, and needs
neither Einstein geometry nor a spin structure.

## Local and global dependencies

- The matrix bound and graph reconstruction are pointwise. Fixed-bundle
  constancy of \(\int\mu_A\) uses a global closed oriented base and evaluation
  at the round connection on the same bundle. It does not presuppose a
  definite path or compactness of a family of reconstructed metrics.
- The boundary family is smooth as source metrics. Its reconstructed
  positivity is verified at the displayed point for the open parameter
  range. It supplies no flow lifespan, preservation, concentration or
  compactness estimate.
- The endpoint theorem assumes a smooth, everywhere positive Riemannian
  ASD metric on the original closed manifold. A limiting flow argument
  must separately produce a limit with those properties, on that smooth
  manifold (or one already proved diffeomorphic to it). Local equations,
  a singular or collapsed limit, and unverified changes of underlying
  manifold do not establish this premise.
- The universal assertion that every smooth homotopy four-sphere carries
  an ASD Riemannian metric is equivalent to smooth Poincaré in dimension
  four. The checked proof supplies the implication after metric existence.
  The audit identifies no additional mathematical prerequisite for B01,
  whose proposed nonlinear calculation still needs its own flow estimates.
