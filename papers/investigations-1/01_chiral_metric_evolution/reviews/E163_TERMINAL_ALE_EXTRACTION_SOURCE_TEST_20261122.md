## Disposition and precise input

**The exact bounded-Ricci smooth terminal extraction remains
unverified in the checked source corpus.** The precise Einstein
version and its correction can be verified. The directly cited
bounded-Ricci original is identified, but its Remark 3.1 was
inaccessible. This does not contradict the cited bounded-Ricci
extraction or the separate topology test.

The sequence is on one fixed smooth closed oriented homotopy
four-sphere with smooth Riemannian metrics, uniformly bounded
absolute Ricci curvature and \(W^+\) in the fixed orientation,
positive total volume lower bound and diameter upper bound.
Bishop–Gromov gives local volume lower bounds on all scales up
to the diameter, and an upper total volume bound. The fixed
Euler characteristic and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet give a uniform full
curvature \(L^2\) bound. These are available without a pointwise
full-curvature bound, covariant Ricci-derivative bound, energy
smallness, or the Einstein equation. In rescalings
\(g'_i=\lambda_i^2g_i\), \(\lambda_i\to\infty\), the curvature
integral is scale invariant in dimension four. The Ricci and
\(W^+\) norms decay by \(\lambda_i^{-2}\). With oriented
positive metric convergence in strong \(C^1_{\rm loc}\) and weak
\(W^{2,p}_{\rm loc}\), this passes Ricci and \(W^+\) to zero
on the regular limit in distributions, by the local curvature
formula. Smoothness of a manifold limit and topology of
embeddings are additional matters. [E160][e160] · [R29 §2][r29].

The source input needed by B83 has specific quantifiers. If
the sequence fails to admit the required positive \(C^1\)
metric limit modulo diffeomorphisms of the fixed compact base,
it should supply a subsequence, points and diverging rescalings
with a smooth complete nonflat Ricci-flat ALE four-manifold
limit, possibly selected at the end of a finite tree.
It should give smooth embeddings on compact subsets of that
manifold into the original \(M\), with enough metric convergence
to pass the chiral bound. In particular one sufficiently large
smooth compact core together with its entire end section and
an end collar needs an embedding into the original smooth
manifold. A global embedding of the entire noncompact end
with metric control out to infinity is unnecessary. A regular
part of an intermediate orbifold may contain an embedded end
section while missing its singular compact core.

## Bando [Ba90a] — verified published Einstein extraction

Inspected the published journal PDF of *Bubbling out of
Einstein manifolds* on 22 November 2026, printed pp. 205–206
and 209–212, with the Einstein–Kähler scope at pp. 212–215.
The J-STAGE metadata give June 1990, *Tohoku Mathematical
Journal*, second series, 42(2), 205–216,
DOI 10.2748/tmj/1178227654. The shorter journal PDF URL
worked after the language-suffixed PDF URL timed out.
The MPI/89-10 preprint was opened as a different version of
the same work to cross-check garbled symbols. Locators
and theorem claims below refer to the published journal
pages. No local copies were retained.
[Published primary PDF][bando] · [J-STAGE metadata][bandometa] ·
[MPI preprint][bandopre].

Theorem A, p. 205, is the preliminary compactness statement
cited in the paper. It takes smooth \(n\)-manifolds,
\(n\ge4\), **Einstein** metrics with uniformly bounded
Einstein constants, diameter upper bound, volume lower
bound, and a uniform full-curvature \(L^{n/2}\) integral.
The root limit is a compact Einstein orbifold with finitely
many isolated singular points. It gives \(C^\infty\)
pullback convergence on the regular part and names a
selected smooth complete nonflat Ricci-flat ALE manifold
blow-up with into-diffeomorphisms into the approximants.
The ALE order stated is \(n-1\) in general, \(n\) if
\(n=4\) or the blow-up is Kähler. Qualifying that statement
by its Einstein assumption is necessary. It does not make
every rescaling smooth.

In §2, p. 209, the author retains the hypotheses of
Theorem A, uses the resulting uniform Sobolev constant,
and invokes the small full-curvature energy estimate in
Proposition 1. On p. 210, an annular full-curvature
\(L^{n/2}\) energy level selects \(r_{0,j}\to0\).
Proposition 2 gives a pointed limit of
\((X_j,r_{0,j}^{-2}g_j,x_{a,j})\) that is complete,
noncompact, nonflat and Ricci-flat ALE, of the same
orders as above, **possibly with finitely many isolated
orbifold singularities**. The claimed convergence is
\(C^\infty\) away from those singularities in addition
to the pointed Hausdorff limit. The ALE definition at
pp. 205–206 is a one-ended smooth orbifold definition
with a free spherical group at infinity and metric
decay \(\tau>0\) in the lifted end chart. It states
zeroth and first derivative decay, together with a
first derivative Hölder decay condition. The source says
the one-end restriction is satisfied in its Ricci-flat case.

Propositions 3–4, pp. 210–211, control the neck. In particular
Proposition 3 uses a differential inequality for \(|R|\)
from the Einstein condition and annular small full-curvature
energy. The iteration at further singular points stays
within the Einstein prelimit sequence. Theorem 1, p. 212,
gives finite assembly by gluing the ends to the corresponding
orbifold points. A terminal smooth manifold piece in this
construction has the regular-part comparison, including
compact cores and fixed smooth end cross-sections at
sufficiently large finite radius. The statement about
cyclic quotients after the Einstein–Kähler hypothesis,
p. 206 and Theorem 3 at p. 214, is stronger and uses that
extra Kähler assumption.

The B83 bounds supply the volume and energy part, but
supply no Einstein identity for the prelimit metrics.
Pointwise Ricci or \(W^+\) control alone does not give
the Einstein curvature differential inequality or
\(C^\infty\) prelimit convergence. Bando's extraction
and neck proof cannot be imported with merely the
available zeroth-order Ricci bound.
[Bando Theorem A and §2][bando].

## Bando [Ba90b] — separately published correction

Inspected both pages of *Correction and addition: Bubbling
out of Einstein manifolds*, *Tohoku Mathematical Journal*,
second series, 42(4), 587–588 (December 1990), DOI
10.2748/tmj/1178227575, on 22 November 2026. It is counted
as a separate work. It replaces the Euclidean conclusion
of the original Theorem 2 by flatness. The correction also
gives the missing nonflatness argument for Proposition 2:
if the candidate were a flat cone, neck estimates and the
selected annular energy force a positive curvature integral
on a fixed annulus in that putatively flat limit. It then
accounts for disjoint and overlapping scale annuli in showing
finite termination under the original energy bound. It
explicitly refers to Anderson–Cheeger for a different treatment
of bubbling. The repair neither makes intermediate nodes
smooth nor removes the original Einstein input.
[Published correction][corr] · [J-STAGE metadata][corrmeta].

## R29 harmonic-radius passage and its scope

Reopened the inherited author PDF, Definition 2.1,
Theorem 2.2 proof and (2.5)–(2.9), and Remark 2.4(ii),
printed pp. 5–8. In the proof Anderson selects the global
minimum \(\rho_i\) of a scale-invariant \(W^{2,p}\) harmonic
radius on the closed manifold and rescales by \(\rho_i^{-2}\).
All rescaled harmonic radii are then bounded below by one,
and the basepoint radius equals one. The compactness
step in the proof produces a complete pointed manifold
limit with weak \(W^{2,p}\) convergence on compact
regions, and the harmonic-coordinate elliptic argument
uses \(\operatorname{Ric}\to0\) to obtain a smooth
Ricci-flat limit and strong local \(W^{2,p}\) convergence.
This is source evidence for the normalization and the
local analytic ingredients of a possible direct approach.

Theorem 2.2 assumes a positive injectivity lower bound,
which the proof explicitly uses at (2.8) to obtain
minimizing lines and conclude a Euclidean limit. Theorem
2.3 offers a related argument with a supplied positive
lower bound on the \(1\)-cross, also absent in B83.
Remark 2.4(ii) distinguishes stronger convergence from
the availability of Ricci derivative estimates or an
elliptic system such as Einstein. The checked proof does
not state the desired no-injectivity, nonflat ALE alternative.
For a separate derivation one would still check the
pointed extraction/embeddings under the normalized
harmonic radius alone, the nonflatness inference from
the basepoint radius, and the exact ALE theorem consuming
limit volume growth and \(L^2\) curvature. This packet
does not carry out those steps. [R29 §2][r29].
