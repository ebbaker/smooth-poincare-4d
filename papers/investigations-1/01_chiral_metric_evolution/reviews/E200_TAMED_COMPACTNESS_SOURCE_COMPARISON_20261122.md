## Disposition

The inspected passages give two distinct inputs and precedents. Fine's Theorem 3 excludes hyperkähler spheres under **supplied pointed \(C^2\)** metric convergence and metric twistor taming. His Corollary 4 gives **smooth compactness** in the normalized ASD Einstein class. Anderson's Theorem 2.2 gives the **\(C^{1,\alpha}\) and weak Sobolev compactness** conclusion on a fixed closed manifold under a **supplied injectivity floor**. The nearby no-injectivity statement gives orbifold convergence. Neither checked text states the assessed combination for varying closed bases with \(\operatorname{Ric}\) and \(W^+\) bounds, volume lower bound, diameter and Euler upper bounds, and supplied strict metric twistor taming. This comparison is restricted to the specified sources and passages. [Fine v2](https://arxiv.org/html/1602.03829v2) · [Anderson author PDF](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/cargese.pdf) · [Assessed theorem](E196_E197_TAMED_METRIC_COMPACTNESS_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).

## Fine v2

- **The metric hypothesis.** For a smooth oriented Riemannian four-manifold the positive sphere bundle parametrizes orthogonal almost complex structures compatible with the orientation. The metric Reznikov form is built from the metric's Levi–Civita connection on this bundle. In the ordinary Hodge curvature blocks write \(A=W^++(s/12)I\) and \(B:\Lambda^+\to\Lambda^-\) for the traceless-Ricci block. Definition 2 calls the space tamed under \(|\langle A\theta,\theta\rangle|>|B\theta|\,|\theta|\) for every \(0\ne\theta\in\Lambda^+\). Theorem 1 gives the \(J_+\) or \(J_-\) tamer according to the sign of \(\det A\). This is the same metric condition as in the branch. A separate definite chiral connection or its own symplectic sphere bundle is different data. Neither text transfers its positivity to the metric condition from bounded strain alone. [Fine v2, §1.1, (1)–(2), Thm. 1 and Def. 2, printed pp. 1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03829v2#page=2) · [E186 assessment](E186_TWISTOR_COMPARISON_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).
- **Theorem 3, printed p. 3.** The sequence may have varying \(M_i\). Its twistor spaces are tamed, and its pointed \(C^2\) metric convergence to a locally hyperkähler manifold is supplied. The universal cover of that limit contains no holomorphic two-sphere for any of its hyperkähler structures. The theorem gives a restriction on such smooth pointed limits, with no Ricci bound, Euler bound, total-volume bound, diameter bound, or injectivity floor as stated hypotheses. It does not supply extraction or precompactness. The proof uses actual local comparisons and a sphere \(S\subset\widetilde X\) with a regular horizontal lift. The embedded sphere used by the branch lies within this proof's scope. There is no need here to extend the normal-bundle argument to arbitrary multiply covered or singular curves. [Fine v2, Thm. 3 and §2.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03829v2#page=3).
- **Corollary 4, printed p. 3.** Its \(M_i\) are compact oriented four-manifolds, with ASD Einstein metrics of nonzero scalar curvature normalized by \(|s|=1\), plus a uniform diameter ceiling and positive volume floor. The statement fixes no prior common smooth type, injectivity floor, or Euler bound. The Einstein normalization gives \(|\operatorname{Ric}|_{\rm HS}=1/2\) in dimension four, \(W^+=0\), \(A=(s/12)I\), and \(B=0\), so metric taming is automatic there. Fine obtains smooth \(C^\infty\) subsequential convergence to a nonzero-scalar ASD Einstein metric. His explanation uses the cited Einstein orbifold convergence and ALE-rescaling results, then Theorem 3 and the cited \((-2)\)-curve premise for nonflat gravitational instantons. The explanation in §1.2 is not a bounded-Ricci extraction proof. In the connected compact subclass, global smooth convergence modulo diffeomorphism would imply finitely many oriented types at fixed \(D,V\) by the usual distinct-type contradiction and a choice of pullback orientation sign. That is a consequence of the convergence convention, not a separate statement in Corollary 4. [Fine v2, §1.2, printed pp. 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03829v2#page=3).
- **Remark 5, printed pp. 4–5.** The remark explicitly identifies the loss from bounded-Ricci \(C^{1,\alpha}\) metric convergence to \(C^{0,\alpha}\) convergence of twistor almost complex structures. It locates the required \(C^1\) control in Proposition 7. Lemma 6 proves \(C^1\) twistor comparison from the pointed \(C^2\) metric input and separately records equality of closed-form cohomology classes. In Proposition 7, (3)–(4) control the value of the curve equation's perturbation by the \(C^0\) difference, whereas differentiation of the parameter-dependent term \((J'-J)(\exp\xi)\) in (5) uses the first derivative of \(J'-J\). The proof uses this \(C^1\) estimate for implicit-function persistence. The final contradiction uses regularity of the lift, homotopy and equality of closed-form classes to force area zero, while strict taming gives positive area for the perturbed nonconstant sphere. The assessed branch supplies a different persistence argument at \(C^0\), so it applies at the \(C^1\) metric level that Fine's proof leaves open. [Fine v2, Remark 5, Lemma 6, Prop. 7 and §§2.3–2.4](https://arxiv.org/html/1602.03829v2) · [E191/E194 assessment](E191_E194_C1_TWISTOR_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).

The two global compactness statements have different assumptions and conclusions. Fine imposes normalized ASD Einstein geometry and gets smooth ASD Einstein convergence. The assessed class permits general smooth metrics with the stated component bounds and strict metric taming, adds an Euler ceiling, and obtains strong \(C^{1,1/4}\) and weak \(W^{2,8}\) convergence. Corollary 4 itself does not impose that Euler ceiling. Its automatic taming on the Einstein subclass provides no automatic taming for the broader metric class. [Fine v2, Cor. 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1602.03829v2#page=3) · [Assessed theorem](E196_E197_TAMED_METRIC_COMPACTNESS_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).

## Anderson author survey

- **Definition 1.1, pp. 2–3, and Definition 2.1, p. 4.** Definition 1.1 allows indexed \(M_i\) in its *definition* of convergence. It calls for diffeomorphisms \(F_i:M\to M_i\) and coordinate convergence of their pullbacks in fixed charts. Definition 2.1 is the scale-invariant harmonic-radius criterion. A geodesic ball has harmonic coordinates with \(c_0^{-1}I\le g\le c_0I\) and \(r^{kp-n}\int|\partial^k g|^p\,dV\le c_0-1\) with fixed \(c_0>1\) and \(kp>n\). This last displayed condition uses the order-\(k\) derivative and metric volume. The branch instead fixes \(p=8,m=2,\eta=1\), with the sum of weighted first- and second-derivative norms in coordinate Lebesgue measure. Both radii scale as a distance. [Anderson, Defs. 1.1 and 2.1, (1.3), (2.1)–(2.2)](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/cargese.pdf#page=4).
- **Theorem 2.2, p. 5, proof sketch pp. 5–6.** With one closed \(n\)-manifold \(M\), the stated metric class has \(|\operatorname{Ric}|\le k\), \(\operatorname{inj}\ge i_0>0\), and \(\operatorname{diam}\le D\). It has subsequential \(C^{1,\alpha}\) and weak \(L^{2,p}\) convergence modulo diffeomorphisms, for \(0<\alpha<1\) and finite Sobolev exponents \(1<p<\infty\), to a metric on \(M\). Here the survey's \(L^{2,p}\) is \(W^{2,p}\). There are no additional Weyl, Euler, taming or independent volume premises. Definition 1.1's allowance for indexed manifolds does not change the fixed-\(M\) quantifier in Theorem 2.2. The proof sketches a minimum harmonic-radius rescaling (2.5)–(2.6), uses (2.8) to get arbitrarily long minimizing segments in every direction and invokes splitting to make the limit Euclidean. Analytically it invokes the harmonic Ricci equation (2.9), strong \(L^{2,p}\) convergence and continuity of its radius in the strong topology. In (2.7) the survey prints a one-power bound in the original radius. Exact constant metric rescaling yields \(kr_h^2\), so the printed bound is also a weaker bound for small radius. The branch uses the squared scaling. [Anderson, Thm. 2.2 and (2.3)–(2.9)](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/cargese.pdf#page=5).
- **Remark 2.4, p. 7.** The aim of point (i) is a qualitative, historically non-effective radius floor under the theorem's Ricci/injectivity/diameter assumptions. It says that a full-curvature bound allows an effective floor. Point (ii) discusses replacing the pointwise Ricci bound by \(L^q\), \(q>n/2\), with weak \(L^{2,q}\) convergence, and stronger convergence with extra Ricci derivative control or elliptic equations such as Einstein. Point (iii) gives an interior variant for open manifolds. None of these remarks removes the other assumptions of Theorem 2.2 on a four-dimensional sequence. The text around Theorem 2.3 and (2.10) on p. 6 keeps a positive lower bound on the length of a minimizing geodesic through every point, the 1-cross, together with Ricci, volume and diameter bounds. Its volume-only replacement (2.11) at the top of p. 7 is explicitly stated for dimension three. The Bishop–Gromov ratio and lower-volume formula (2.12)–(2.13) on p. 7 are the relevant comparison input in dimension four as well. [Anderson, pp. 6–7](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/cargese.pdf#page=7).
- **Example 2.5, pp. 7–8, including (2.16) on p. 8.** The survey gives Ricci-flat Eguchi–Hanson metrics on \(TS^2\) with a shrinking totally geodesic round zero-section and homothety \(g_\lambda=\lambda^2\psi_\lambda^*g_1\). The label (2.16) is that equation. The metrics converge smoothly away from the tip to the flat cone over \(\mathbb{RP}^3\), with fixed-scale ball volumes bounded below and injectivity tending to zero. The precise injectivity coefficient is unnecessary: with the zero-section's stated radius \(\lambda\), opposite tangent directions along a great circle meet at the antipode at length \(\pi\lambda\), giving the upper bound \(\pi\lambda\) in the ambient space by total geodesy. The survey prints \(2\pi\lambda\) as an equality, which need not be imported. The displayed family is noncompact, and the next paragraph separately asserts compact modifications satisfying (2.11). The example in the hyperkähler orientation has \(A=B=0\), so it does not meet strict metric taming. It illustrates why lower volumes cannot alone do the work of the injectivity floor in general four-dimensional bounded-Ricci compactness. [Anderson, Example 2.5 and (2.15)–(2.16)](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/cargese.pdf#page=8).
- **Nearby Theorem 2.6, p. 8, proof discussion pp. 8–9.** This is the survey's compact, fixed-\(M\), four-dimensional statement under \(|\operatorname{Ric}|\le k\), \(\operatorname{vol}\ge v_0\), and \(\operatorname{diam}\le D\), without injectivity. It gives Gromov–Hausdorff convergence to an orbifold with finitely many singular points, a \(C^{1,\alpha}\) or \(L^{2,p}\) regular-set metric extending continuously in a local uniformization, and embeddings of the regular set into \(M\) with strong \(C^{1,\alpha}\) convergence there. Its proof discussion mentions the Euler-characteristic control of total \(L^2\) curvature, a nontrivial Ricci-flat ALE rescaling at singularities, energy per bubble, and a topologically essential embedding assertion. These are proof remarks. They do not assert absence of the orbifold singularities or provide the unscaled inter-base smooth identifications of E196. The energy discussion prints \((8\pi^2)^{-1}\int|R|^2\) with its own curvature notation. The branch explicitly derives \(32\pi^2\chi=\int(|\operatorname{Rm}|_{\rm full}^2-4|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+s^2)\,dV\); the survey's prefactor is not transferred to that component norm. The fixed Euler characteristic in the source discussion is replaced in E196 by a supplied upper bound for varying \(M_i\). [Anderson, Thm. 2.6 and (2.17), pp. 8–9](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~anderson/cargese.pdf#page=8) · [E196/E197 assessment](E196_E197_TAMED_METRIC_COMPACTNESS_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).

## Additional arguments in the assessed theorem

1. **Extract the smooth comparison limit from the actual hypotheses.** The upper Euler bound and Ricci/volume control give a uniform full-tensor \(L^2\) curvature bound by Gauss–Bonnet. The diameter and positive total-volume bound give lower volumes at every center by Bishop–Gromov. At vanishing approximate minimum radius, the individual-manifold harmonic charts and actual smooth compact embeddings yield a strong \(W^{2,8}\) and pointed \(C^1\) comparison to a smooth complete Ricci-flat limit. Rescaling also kills the positive Weyl block, producing the \(W^+=0\) orientation. The full energy and Euclidean growth pass to the limit. The flat alternative is ruled out using quartic growth and the genuine radius-two harmonic chart test. This is the branch's explicit minimum-radius construction, without using Anderson's injectivity-based flatness step or importing a smooth orbifold exclusion from Theorem 2.6. Bando–Kasue–Nakajima is applied only to the resulting supplied nonflat candidate to obtain its smooth ALE end. [E165/E167 assessment](E165_E167_PRE_ALE_EXTRACTION_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md) · [E164 assessment](E164_RICCI_FLAT_ALE_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).
2. **Exclude that limit at the available regularity.** Strict metric taming survives the constant rescaling and the positive compact comparisons. First jets of the metric give \(C^0\) twistor structure comparison, and the separately assessed compact-map persistence step works with that topology and the fixed right inverse at the smooth embedded sphere lift. This addresses the precise gap of Fine's Remark 5 rather than applying his \(C^2\) theorem to the weaker convergence. The assessed ALE sphere input gives the necessary embedded sphere in the positively oriented hyperkähler universal cover. The Kronheimer metric classification is imported as attributed in the checked Wright and Lyu–Xu preprints, with the original construction and Torelli bodies uninspected. The resulting contradiction yields a harmonic-radius floor for the stated metric-tamed class. [E191/E194 assessment](E191_E194_C1_TWISTOR_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md) · [E193 assessment](E193_ALE_SPHERE_SOURCE_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).
3. **Identify the varying unscaled smooth bases.** The fixed Euclidean cutoff-coordinate target, uniform tubular projection estimates and late reference selection give actual smooth orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms. The inverse-coordinate regularity yields the strong \(C^{1,1/4}\) and weak \(W^{2,8}\) convergence in a fixed smooth atlas, retaining a positive limit. Finiteness of oriented diffeomorphism types for the stated fixed bounds follows from those identifications by a sequence of pairwise distinct types. The branch makes that global identification a proved result. [E196/E197 assessment](E196_E197_TAMED_METRIC_COMPACTNESS_ASSESSMENT_20261122.md).

No part of these comparisons supplies metric taming from the native flow's curvature or extends compactness to a class lacking its stated metric taming. The comparison also supplies no full-curvature supremum bound, smooth convergence of the unscaled general metrics, or novelty determination.
