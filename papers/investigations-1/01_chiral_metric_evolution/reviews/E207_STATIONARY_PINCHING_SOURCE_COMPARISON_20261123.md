## Fine's exact problem and normalization

**Fine2011 / R15.** Joel Fine, *A gauge theoretic approach to the anti-self-dual Einstein equations*, arXiv:1111.5005v2, revised 29 November 2011 at 11:54:34 UTC. - **Category and sign.** Definitions 1.3–1.6, printed pp. 4–6, consider definite metric \(SO(3)\) connections over real four-manifolds. In the matching orientation the definite curvature plane is \(\Lambda^+_{g_A}\), where \(g_A\) is the reconstructed positive Riemannian metric. The curvature-map orientation sign in Definition 1.6 is separate from wedge positivity of the plane. Fine calls the perfect positive-scalar round connection positive in the Lie-algebra orientation on \(\Lambda^+\). The assessed conversion gives the manuscript's round-compatible coefficient \(F=-KS\Sigma\), \(S=Q^{1/2}\), in its adapted coframe. Its negative determinant in that coframe and Fine's positive sign use different target orientations. [Fine v2, pp. 4–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5005v2#page=4) · [E01A sign calibration][e01a]
- **Energy and question.** Definitions 1.9–1.10 and Proposition 1.12, pp. 8–10, use \(\mu_A=\frac13\langle F_A\wedge F_A\rangle=(8\pi^2/3)p_1(A)\) in the native negative-Killing pairing, \(Q_{ij}=F_i\wedge F_j/\mu_A\), and \(E(A)=\int\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)\mu_A\). On a fixed bundle over a closed base, \(\operatorname{tr}Q=3\) gives \(E(A)=8\pi^2\langle p_1(E),[M]\rangle+\mathcal E(A)\) with \(\mathcal E(A)=\int|Q-I|^2\mu_A\). Thus minima are exactly perfect connections and independent-connection criticality is the same for \(E\) and \(\mathcal E\). The final paragraph of §1.6, p. 11, asks whether there are intermediate critical points in the definite-connection class, and records the author's knowledge in that 2011 version. It refers separately to Proposition 5.9 for triples. The passage specifies no spectral pinching hypothesis. [Fine v2, §§1.6 and 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5005v2#page=8)
- **The same original gradient.** Section 4.1, pp. 29–30, Proposition 4.2 and (4.1) give \(\partial_t A=-d_A^{*g_A}(B_AF_A)\), where \(B_A=4Q-\frac23\operatorname{tr}(Q^2)I\). The tangent pairing is the live \(L^2(g_A)\) pairing of adjoint-valued one-forms, as specified in §1.6, p. 10. For \(q=Q-I\), \(B_A=2I+4(q-|q|^2I/6)\). Self-duality and \(d_AF_A=0\) give \(d_A^{*g_A}F_A=0\), so (4.1) equals the branch's native \(V=-4d_A^{*g_A}[(q-|q|^2I/6)F_A]\). The older pairing \(\delta\), with \(\mu_{\rm old}=2K^2dV_{\widehat g}\) for fixed \(K>0\), is half the native pairing. Thus \(\mu_A=2\mu_{\rm old}\), \(g_A=2K\widehat g\), \(\mathcal E=2\mathcal P_{\rm old}\) and \(t_{\rm native}=2K t_{\rm old}\). The normalized wedge quotient \(Q\) and its ratio are unchanged. The equality of critical sets follows from the energy identity under every smooth variation in this open definite class. [Fine v2, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5005v2#page=29) · [Foundation assessment][foundation]
- **Stated local and stability results.** Theorem 4.3, p. 30, states short-time existence from a definite connection and uniqueness for the duration of the solution. The compact smooth setting is the relevant one here. Section 4.2, Lemma 4.4 and (4.2)–(4.4), pp. 31–32, use the automorphism action on the bundle, including base motion, and Theorem 4.10, pp. 35–36, states parabolicity of the adjusted equation near its smooth reference. Theorem 4.11, p. 37, assumes a *supplied perfect* connection \(\widehat A\) on a compact four-manifold and a definite \(A_0\) sufficiently close in \(L^2_k\) for an integer \(k\ge3\), measured in a fixed background. The conclusion is global original existence with \(\|g(t)^*A(t)-\widehat A\|_{L^2_k}=o(e^{-\alpha t})\) for some \(\alpha>0\) and time-dependent bundle transformations. Equation (4.9), Proposition 4.12 and Theorem 4.13, pp. 38–40, give the separate gauge-fixed stability setup. These are statement and selected-passage checks. The existing operator-order and implementation qualifications in the canonical record still apply. [Fine v2, §§4.2–4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5005v2#page=30) · [Canonical source notes][special]
- **Fine's distinct triple energy.** Section 5, pp. 40–43, instead works with a definite triple of global individually closed symplectic two-forms on a compact four-manifold. It normalizes integrated wedge pairings by (5.1) and varies by exact triples \(\omega+da\) in fixed cohomology classes. Definitions 5.2–5.5 and Proposition 5.7 use the corresponding trace-three matrix and ordinary exterior derivatives, with equation (5.2) on triple potentials. Proposition 5.9, p. 43, gives only hyperkähler critical triples. Its harmonic-form argument applies to the ordinary self-dual closed forms in that triple problem. [Fine v2, §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1111.5005v2#page=40)

E205 starts with a supplied maximal smooth native original flow on one fixed oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere and \(SO(3)\) bundle, with positive-plane and round-compatible sign. One sequence approaching the maximal endpoint carries simultaneous bounds on the whole area-\(4\pi\) sphere density \(\sup_M\Psi\) and the native diameter, and the limsup of the supremum of the unshifted ratio is at most \(9/4\). Its frozen proof assumes \(H_{\rm gap}\), the smooth stationary perfection statement. From the strong positive trace and the smooth critical adjusted limit, it transfers the ratio through actual bundle automorphisms on closed positive-time subintervals. It then applies the stationary theorem at a single smooth positive slice, obtains zero limiting energy, and uses the assessed round-basin tail to prove convergence of the original pair to one fixed round pullback. Fine's Theorem 4.11 concerns a supplied nearby perfect reference and convergence after time-dependent transformations. The late-slice hypotheses, the passage to a critical limit and the fixed original limit are branch arguments. The proof uses the ratio bound only at the selected original times and claims no pinching estimate along the original flow.
