## Multiplication, time cutoff and trace

Let \(Z\) be E42's gradient/flux norm and \(Y\) its scalar-source norm with the indicated scaled weights, both applied to the appropriate bundle norms. On \(Q_r^+\), Hölder gives \(\|GH\|_{p/2}\le\|G\|_p\|H\|_p\) and \(r^{2-12/p}=(r^{1-6/p})^2\). On \(Q_r\) use Cauchy–Schwarz and \(r^{-4}=(r^{-2})^2\). Thus \(Y(GH)\le Z(G)Z(H)\), also with bounded bilinear coefficients via their operator norm. Finite chart volume gives \(Y(k)\lesssim T\|k\|_\infty\), \(Z(G)\lesssim\sqrt T\|G\|_\infty\) and \(Y(G)\lesssim\sqrt T Z(G)\). For the last estimate the upper-cylinder Hölder volume factor is \(O(r^{6/p})\), and the tent Cauchy–Schwarz factor after normalization is \(O(r)\). Coefficient multipliers are bounded by their sup norms. The coefficients are smooth on the fixed value tube and have smooth cutoffs to all values. Subtracting \((P(U)-P_0)DU\) at two sections gives

\[
 Z(\Delta q)\le L_P\left(R Z(D(U-V))+\|U-V\|_\infty Z(DV)\right).
\]

The quadratic term has two differences in gradient slots and one difference in its coefficient, bounded by \(M_2[Z(DU)+Z(DV)]Z(D(U-V))+M'_2\|U-V\|_\infty Z(DV)^2\). The zeroth and first degree terms have differences of sizes \(O(T)\) and \(O(\sqrt T)\) times \(X(U-V)\) on the ball. For the transformed principal coefficient use \(\|P_s-P_0\|\lesssim |U|+|s|\). The results are exactly the size and difference bounds required by E42. Smooth sections give smooth products, so continuity of the nonlinear maps preserves the source completions. No density statement about the full finite-norm spaces is used. [E42, (3)–(5) and (9)][e42].

Write \(A\ge1\) for the linear estimate constant used in E42. If \(T\le R^2\), \(R\le1\), \(|s|\le R\), and \(R\le(64AK)^{-1}\), the nonlinear term of the fixed-point map is at most \(4AKR^2\le R/16\), and its Lipschitz constant is at most \(4AKR\le1/16\). With datum norm at most \(R/(8A)\), the map has image in the radius-\(3R/16\) ball. The theorem starts at \(\epsilon=R/(16A)\), leaving room for varying data and uniform compatible approximation. Taking \(R\le\rho/4\) keeps the values in the region where the cutoff agrees with the genuine operator. Convex interpolation to the fixed coefficient retains the reference-pairing symbol margin. Lipschitz dependence follows by absorption, with constant at most \(A/(1-1/16)\). [E42, (6)–(7)][e42].

The smooth-evaluation premise places the linear maps in the completion. A direct justification using the exact E41 Cauchy kernel is as follows. For smooth data \(u_0\), the identity \(H(t)u_0=u_0+\int_0^t H(s)Lu_0\,ds\) follows by uniqueness for the linear Cauchy problem. Its gradient correction is bounded by \(C\sqrt t\|Lu_0\|_\infty\) by E41's first spatial kernel bound. For smooth \(f,G\), apply that same bound to \(k=f+\operatorname{div}_D G\) in the integral to obtain \(\|DS(f,G)(t)\|_\infty\le C\sqrt t\|k\|_\infty\). The homogeneous and forced evaluations therefore extend continuously in the spatial \(C^1\) norm at zero, and are smooth at positive times. On a closed short cylinder they can be approximated in uniform spatial \(C^1\) by smooth sections, which implies convergence in \(X_T\) by the bounded-gradient estimate. Applying the two E41 bounds to smooth approximants gives the maps into \(X_T^\circ\), and sup convergence preserves the trace. For a bare finite-norm source the E41 statement has distributional zero trace. In the contraction only completed sources occur. [E41, (1), (5) and trace section][e41].

## Energy identification of weak solutions

A finite radius-\(\sqrt T\) cover bounds the global \(L^2\) norm of the spatial gradient in the completed class. If a distributional solution lies in the completed radius-\(R\) ball, subtract its linear mild solution for its own completed sources and trace. The difference \(z\) has zero uniform trace, \(z\in L^2H^1\), and \(z_t=Lz\in L^2H^{-1}\). Time regularization justifies \(\frac12\partial_t\|z\|_2^2=\operatorname{Re}\langle Lz,z\rangle\). The fixed-coefficient Gårding bound, obtained from the rank-one inequality by freezing and a squared partition of unity, gives \(\partial_t\|z\|_2^2\le 2c_2\|z\|_2^2\). Integrating from a positive slice and using the trace proves equality of the two solutions. This applies also to transformed equations because the transformed extra terms are on the source side, and to restrictions because the completed norms restrict monotonically. Uniqueness is in the stated small ball. [E42, (8)][e42].

## Global lifted parameter maps and derivative convergence

For \(\varphi_{t,s}\) generated by the finite collection of spanning smooth fields, \(d(\varphi_{t,s}(y),y)\le C|s|t\) and its Jacobian and inverse are uniformly bounded on the compact chart-time-parameter ranges. Thus the image of a radius-\(r\) ball for \(0\le t\le r^2\) lies in a fixed enlargement of the ball. Cover that enlargement by a uniformly bounded number of radius-\(r\) balls. This keeps the exact anchored time intervals, including at the largest allowable radius. The lift via the fixed connection has derivative \(D(\mathcal T_s U)=B_s(DU\circ\varphi_{t,s})+C_s(U\circ\varphi_{t,s})\) with smooth bounded coefficients. Consequently

\[
 \|\mathcal T_s U\|_\infty\le C\|U\|_\infty,\qquad
 Z(D\mathcal T_s U)\le C Z(DU)+C\sqrt T\|U\|_\infty.
\]

The same covering and bounded smooth tensor multipliers give bounded pullbacks on \(Y^\circ\) and \(Z^\circ\). On smooth sections the pullbacks are strongly continuous in each norm by uniform convergence of the needed derivatives and the bounded-value estimates. Density and the uniform operator bounds extend strong continuity to the named completions. The maps need only be continuous in parameter on rough sections at this step. [E42, positive-time smoothness][e42].

On smooth sections, conjugating the equation pullback acts on its top coefficient by the inverse base Jacobian in two derivative slots and by smooth fiber conjugation. The time derivative adds a drift linear in \(DU\) and a zeroth order term. Derivatives of the lift or base map, and the return to fixed divergence form, leave the lower terms polynomial of degree at most two in \(DU\). The transformed array at \(s=0\) is the original array and has the required \(O(|U|+|s|)\) difference from \(P_0\). A smooth-section approximation in \(X_T^\circ\) suffices for the residual identity. The time derivative converges in distributions by uniform convergence, the linear and divergence terms by gradient/flux \(L^2\) convergence, and the quadratic forcing by \(Y\) convergence and hence \(L^1\) convergence. A smooth spacetime diffeomorphism and smooth fiber transport pull back distributions continuously by transforming the test section and density. Therefore the residual identity holds in distributions and the trace is the same since \(\varphi_{0,s}=\mathrm{id}\) and transport is the identity there.

The strict margin and strong parameter continuity put \(\mathcal T_s U\) in the uniqueness ball for small \(s\). Its placement persists locally as the datum varies by Lipschitz dependence and uniform pullback bounds. It equals the transformed fixed point \(U_s\) in that neighborhood. The multipliers \(P_s,h_{j,s}\) have uniform Taylor remainders in the sup norm on compact value ranges, and each derivative of a source still has at most two gradient factors. Hence the transformed mild map is smooth into the completed spaces. The inverse of \(I-D_U\Phi_s\) is given by a Neumann series with \(\|D_U\Phi_s\|\le1/16\). This gives joint smoothness of the implicit fixed point in the parameter and the continuous datum. The datum enters via the same bounded linear map \(H\), with no spatial derivative of the initial section in the parameter calculation.

For each \(t_*>0,x_*\), four fields spanning at \(x_*\) give an invertible derivative of the selected parameters in \(s\mapsto\varphi_{t_*,s}(x_*)\), namely \(s\mapsto t_*\sum s_\alpha V_\alpha(x_*)\). Invert locally with \(t\) included as a variable. Evaluating \(U_s(t,x_*)\) and undoing smooth transport then supplies every spatial derivative at nearby points, jointly continuous in time and datum, because evaluation on \(X_T^\circ\) is bounded into the sup norm. A finite cover gives uniform derivative convergence on compact strips in \(0<t<T\), shrinking parameter neighborhoods around the limit datum if needed. Differentiating the distributional evolution spatially then gives a continuous time derivative for each spatial derivative by integrating its continuous right-hand side. Iteration with the smooth coefficient formulas gives convergence of all mixed derivatives on the same strips. This verifies the smoothing step without starting from regularity of the rough fixed point. [Koch–Lamm v1, (2.1)–(2.3) and §3][kl]. [E42][e42].

## Smooth compatible comparisons at fixed reference

E38 supplies \(A^k_0\to A_0\) and \(F_{A^k_0}\to F_0\) in uniform norms. The common lifetime of their fixed points follows from the value margin in the contraction, irrespective of their first-jet sizes. To get an individual smooth connection comparison at this same reference, write the E36 term \(W\) in coordinates as \(W_0(x,a,F)+W_A(x,a,F)Da+W_F(x,a,F)DF\). On the graph,

\[
 F_A=\bar F+\operatorname{Alt}(Da)+J(x,a),\qquad
 DF_A=\operatorname{Alt}(D^2a)+J_1(x,a,Da),
\]

where \(\operatorname{Alt}\) in each line denotes the fixed exterior derivative symbol in the appropriate slots. No second derivatives occur in \(J_1\). The contribution of \(W\) to the adjusted connection principal array is therefore its \(W_F\) coefficient applied to that fixed second-derivative map. The fixed-metric vertical term contributes its own fixed top coefficient. The constraint correction is zero identically on the graph. Thus the ordinary connection principal coefficient is smooth in \(x,a,F_A\), even though the equation has general first-jet lower terms. Compactness and the E29 reference symbol give a common strict symbol and definiteness margin for value closeness in the fixed pairing. This justifies the use of a single smaller value tube before choosing \(R\) and \(T\). [E36, (1)–(2) and order inventory][e36]. [E29, reference symbol][e29].

For each smooth approximant its initial first-jet image is compact inside the inverse image of this open value tube. Choose a smooth cutoff equal to one near that jet image and compactly supported in this parabolic region, extending the leading array by convex interpolation to the fixed Laplacian. E29's finite-chart spectral Galerkin argument then applies. Its constants and smooth lifespan may depend on the jet image and Sobolev norms of this approximant. Sobolev continuity of the smooth solution in \(H^6\) on the closed short interval gives continuous spatial \(C^2\) for \(a\) and continuous spatial \(C^1\) for the lift \((a,F_A-\bar F)\) in dimension four. E36 gives the equation for the lift. The lift belongs to the completion and on \([0,\ell]\) its norm is at most its value sup plus \(C\sqrt\ell\) times its spatial derivative sup. As \(\ell\downarrow0\) its norm tends to the initial value sup, strictly inside the uniqueness ball. Comparing in that ball therefore gives agreement on some \(\ell_k>0\). The uniform contraction lifespan is independent of this comparison interval. [Taylor, (7.3.1)–(7.3.2), Propositions 7.3.A–B][tay]. [E29][e29].

Start the E36 constraint identity at any positive time within this interval for the \(k\)th solution. On each compact positive-time strip its smooth coefficients and positive definite metric give a bound on \(\|g^{-1}g_t\|_\infty\). The constraint energy satisfies \(E_C'\le 3\|g^{-1}g_t\|_\infty E_C\) on such strips. It propagates the zero constraint forward to all \(0<t<T\) for this approximant, without a uniform-in-\(k\) energy constant. Sup convergence then suffices to close the curvature identity at each slice. The derivative term \(d_{\bar A}a^k\) passes by spatial integration by parts, the bracket term by uniform convergence, and the pairs are continuous up to the time endpoints. The smooth limit is consequently compatible pointwise at positive times. No singular-time constraint energy estimate is needed. [E36, (5)–(6)][e36]. [E38, closure of compatibility][e38].
