## Conventions and identification

Work on a smooth closed oriented real four-manifold with positive
Riemannian metric. For the round calculation use \(g_0\) on the standard
\(S^4\), of sectional curvature \(K>0\), with
\(R_{abcd}=K(g_{ac}g_{bd}-g_{ad}g_{bc})\) and \(\operatorname{Ric}=3Kg_0\).
Use \(E\) in the self-dual chirality and the sign \(F_0=-K\Sigma^+\).
Write \(\Sigma_i^+=\tfrac12\eta_{i,ab}e^a\wedge e^b\) with
\(\eta_{i,jk}=\epsilon_{ijk}\), \(\eta_{i,j4}=\delta_{ij}\) and
\(\eta_{i,4j}=-\delta_{ij}\). Then
\(\Sigma_i^+\wedge\Sigma_j^+=2\delta_{ij}dV\). Use
\([T_i,T_j]=\epsilon_{ijk}T_k\) with
\(\delta_{ij}=-2\operatorname{tr}(T_iT_j)\).
The definition \(\mu=\tfrac13\sum_i F_i\wedge F_i\),
\(F_i\wedge F_j=Q_{ij}\mu\) gives \(\operatorname{tr}Q=3\). The choice
\(dV_{\widehat g}=\mu/(2K^2)\) gives \(\widehat g_{A_0}=g_0\).
Write \(L=\nabla^*\nabla=-\nabla^a\nabla_a\). All differentiations below
use smooth paths and sections. The fixed bundle identification comes
from the positive symmetric change of frames
\(\dot e^a=\tfrac12 h^a{}_b e^b\), so no global coframe or spin frame
is needed. A frame identification with a skew variation may add an
infinitesimal internal gauge term.

## Contracted Bianchi and Ricci variations

The chiral projection of the linearized torsion equation is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot\omega_{ab}&=\tfrac12(\nabla_bh_{ac}-\nabla_a h_{bc})e^c,\\
(\mathcal H_g h)_{ic}
 &=-\tfrac12\eta_{i,ab}\dot\omega_{ab,c}
   =\tfrac12\eta_{i,ab}\nabla_a h_{cb}.
\end{aligned}
\]
The torsion equation fixes the displayed sign: differentiate
\(de^a+\omega_{ab}\wedge e^b=0\) in a normal coframe at the point and
use \(\dot e^a=\tfrac12 h_{ab}e^b\). The chiral contraction is the
same \(-\eta_{i,ab}/2\) projection for which \(F_0=-K\Sigma^+\).

Set \(r=\operatorname{Ric}-sg/4\). Contracted Bianchi gives
\(\nabla^a r_{ab}=\nabla_b s/4\) and
\(\nabla^a R_{abcd}=\nabla_c\operatorname{Ric}_{bd}-
\nabla_d\operatorname{Ric}_{bc}\).
In the divergence of the Weyl decomposition, the trace-free Ricci
part contributes half of
\(\nabla_c r_{bd}-\nabla_d r_{bc}\) and a scalar contribution
\(\tfrac18(g_{bd}\nabla_c s-g_{bc}\nabla_d s)\).
The pure scalar curvature part contributes a further \(1/12\) of
the same scalar expression. Subtracting these from \(\nabla^a R\)
leaves
\[
\nabla^a W_{abcd}
=\tfrac12(\nabla_c r_{bd}-\nabla_d r_{bc})
  +\tfrac1{24}(g_{bd}\nabla_c s-g_{bc}\nabla_d s),
\]
where \(1/24=1/4-1/8-1/12\).
With the usual two-form action,
\((W^+\Sigma^+)_{i,ab}=\tfrac12\eta_{i,cd}W_{abcd}\).
The joint covariant derivative preserves \(\eta\). Contracting
with \(-\eta_{i,cd}/2\) gives \(d_{A_g^+}^{*g}(W^+\Sigma^+)
=-\mathcal H_g(r)-\mathcal H_g(sg)/12\).
Here \((\mathcal H_g(sg))_{ib}=\eta_{i,cb}\nabla_c s/2\).
This is a local identity for an arbitrary smooth oriented Riemannian
four-metric, including metrics whose chiral connection may not be
definite. Its adjoint uses \(g\).

At round \(W^+=r=0\) and \(ds=0\), so the variation is
\[
d_0^*(\mathcal D h\,\Sigma^+)
=-\mathcal H(r')-\tfrac1{12}\mathcal H(s'g_0),\qquad
\mathcal D h=(W^+)'_{g_0}h .
\]
For TT \(h\), varying Christoffel symbols gives
\(2\operatorname{Ric}'_{ij}=Lh_{ij}+
\nabla^a\nabla_i h_{ja}+\nabla^a\nabla_j h_{ia}\).
The constant-curvature commutator on covariant two-tensors is
\[
[\nabla_d,\nabla_a]h_{cb}
=-K(\delta_{ca}h_{db}-\delta_{cd}h_{ab}
     +\delta_{ba}h_{cd}-\delta_{bd}h_{ca}).
\]
In particular \([\nabla^b,\nabla_a]h_{cb}
=4Kh_{ca}-K g_{ca}\operatorname{tr}h\).
Thus \(s'=0\), \(\operatorname{Ric}'=\tfrac12(L+8K)h\) and
\(r'=\tfrac12(L+2K)h\). Differentiating
\(\operatorname{div}r=ds/4\) and \(\operatorname{tr}_g r=0\)
at \(r=0\) gives the TT conditions on \(r'\). This also proves
that \(L\) preserves TT.

## Flow normalization, intertwining and defect heat equation

Fine's \(\mathcal E=\int|Q|^2\mu\) and the defect energy
\(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{conn}}=\int|Q-I|^2\mu\) differ by
\(3\int\mu\), topological on a fixed bundle over a closed manifold
by Chern–Weil theory. Equivalently the derivative of \(\mu\) in
direction \(a\) is \(\tfrac23 d\sum_i(a_i\wedge F_i)\) by Bianchi.
The adjoint is taken with the reconstructed metric in the connection
flow. Since \(F\) is self-dual for this metric and \(d_AF=0\),
\(d_A^*F=0\). Thus the \(2I\) term in
\(4Q-\tfrac23|Q|^2 I=2I+4(q-\tfrac16|q|^2I)\)
drops out of the vector field, where \(q=Q-I\).
Fine's normalization is \(dV_F=\mu\) on the same internal pairing.
Writing \(g_F=c\,\widehat g\), \(c=\sqrt{2}K\); on two-forms
\(d_A^{*g_F}=c^{-1}d_A^{*\widehat g}\). More generally, if the
positive Lie-algebra pairing in Fine is \(\kappa\delta\), then
\(\mu_F=\kappa\mu\), \(Q_F=Q\) and \(c=\sqrt{2\kappa}K\).
The same trajectory has \(t_F=ct\), where \(t\) is manuscript
connection time. The check uses the chiral sign tied to the round
model. Fine's curvature-map orientation is defined by the Lie
algebra brackets, and at a perfect connection his sign agrees with
scalar curvature. [Fine, arXiv:1111.5005v2, Definitions 1.4,
1.6 and 1.9–1.10, Proposition 1.12, Lemma 3.1,
and Proposition 4.2 eq. (4.1)][fine].

Now let \(q=\delta Q(a)\) denote the linearized defect.
The flow linearizes to \(a_t=-4d_0^*(qF_0)\), since the variation of
the adjoint acts on a zero factor at \(Q=I\).
To compute \(q\) on the image of \(\mathcal H\), let
\(M=C^2-BB^T\), \(C=W^++sI/12\), \(B\) the trace-free Ricci
block. Its wedge-product formula gives \(Q=3M/\operatorname{tr}M\).
At \(C=KI\), \(B=0\) one has
\(M'=2K\mathcal D h+Ks'I/6\) and
\(\operatorname{tr}M'=Ks'/2\). The scalar terms cancel, leaving
\(q=(2/K)\mathcal D h\). Substituting in the connection equation
gives
\[
a_t=8d_0^*(\mathcal D h\,\Sigma^+)
=\mathcal H[-8r'-\tfrac23s'g_0],\qquad
a_t=\mathcal H[-4(L+2K)h]\quad(h\ {\rm TT}).
\]
The identity applies in the specified coframe gauge without a
projection or a gauge correction. For smooth TT initial data,
solve \(h_t=-4(L+2K)h\) on the closed round sphere. The resulting
\(a=\mathcal Hh\) solves the linearized connection equation and
keeps the TT image invariant. Uniqueness of smooth solutions of
the linearized connection equation also follows from its
nonnegative Hessian, computed below: the squared \(L^2\) norm of the
difference has nonpositive derivative and starts at zero.

The normalized Ricci vector field in four dimensions is
\(\mathcal F(g)=-2\operatorname{Ric}+\bar s g/2\) with
\(\bar s=\int s\,dV/\operatorname{Vol}(g)\). At round
\(\bar s'=0\) on TT because \(s'=0\) and the volume terms cancel
in the normalized average. It follows that
\(\mathcal F'(h)=-(L+2K)h\) and \(\tau=4t\).
From the graph and trace formulas of
`rec:linear-reconstruction-formula`, the curvature-plane tilt
contributes \(r'/K\) to the metric derivative and the volume scale
contributes \(s'g_0/(12K)\). Indeed \(T'=B'/K\) and
\((\operatorname{tr}M/(3K^2))'=s'/(6K)\); the determinant
scale in the metric is the square root of the latter ratio.
Thus the reconstruction derivative on TT is
\(\widehat h=h+r'/K=(L+4K)h/(2K)\), commuting with \(L\).
It obeys \(\widehat h_t=-4(L+2K)\widehat h\).
For a conformal source variation \(h=2fg_0\), \(\mathcal D h=0\)
gives \(q=0\) and \(a_t=0\). In contrast,
\(\mathcal F'(2fg_0)=4\nabla^2 f+2(\Delta f)g_0+
12K(f-\bar f)g_0\), which can be nonzero. The manifold-wide
comparison proved here concerns the TT directions at the round
benchmark.

For general smooth \(a\) at round, let \(U_{ik}\) be the
\(\Sigma_k^+\) coefficient of \((d_0a)_i\). Then
\(U_{ik}=\tfrac12\eta_{k,ab}\nabla_a a_{ib}\) and
\(q=-(2/K)\operatorname{sym}_0 U\). As \(F_0=-K\Sigma^+\),
\((a_t)_{ib}=-4K\eta_{j,ab}\nabla_a q_{ij}\).
Using \(\eta_{k,ab}\eta_{j,cb}
=\delta_{kj}\delta_{ac}+\epsilon_{kjl}\eta_{l,ac}\), the
antisymmetric part of the second derivatives contributes
\[
\begin{aligned}
\tfrac12\epsilon_{kjl}\eta_{l,ac}[\nabla_a,\nabla_c]q_{ij}
&=-2K\epsilon_{kjl}(\epsilon_{ilm}q_{mj}
+\epsilon_{jlm}q_{im})\\
&=-2K(q_{ki}-\delta_{ik}\operatorname{tr}q+2q_{ik})
=-6Kq_{ik}.
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(\eta_{l,ac}\eta_{t,ac}=4\delta_{lt}\) and the curvature
commutator acts once on each internal index. Thus
\(q_t=-4(L_E+6K)q\), with \(L_E\) the induced nonnegative rough
Laplacian on \(\operatorname{Sym}^2_0 E\).
Tracing \(U(a_t)\) uses \(\operatorname{tr}q=0\) and symmetry of
\(\nabla_a\nabla_c q_{ij}\) in \(i,j\) against \(\epsilon_{ijl}\).
It gives \(\operatorname{tr}U(a_t)=0\) and zero linearized
local volume velocity. These contractions hold on the round
background for arbitrary smooth infinitesimal connection data.

## Hessian and the two configuration-space pairings

With ordinary connection \(L^2(g_0,\delta)\) and symmetric tensor
\(L^2(g_0)\), expand \(4\langle\mathcal Hh,\mathcal Hk\rangle\)
using
\(\sum_i\eta_{i,ab}\eta_{i,de}
=\delta_{ad}\delta_{be}-\delta_{ae}\delta_{bd}+\epsilon_{abde}\).
The first product integrates to \(\langle Lh,k\rangle\).
The second product before its minus sign is
\[
\int \nabla_a h_{cb}\nabla_b k_{ca}\,dV
=-\int k_{ca}[\nabla^b,\nabla_a]h_{cb}\,dV
=-4K\langle h,k\rangle
\]
for TT \(h\), and the \(\epsilon_{abde}\) term integrates to
\(-\tfrac12\int\epsilon_{abde} k_{ce}[\nabla_d,\nabla_a]h_{cb}\,dV=0\).
In the latter contraction two of the commutator terms pair an
antisymmetric index pair against a symmetric pair of \(h\) and the
other two repeat an epsilon index. This proves
\(\mathcal H^*\mathcal Hh=(L+4K)h/4\) paired with **any** smooth
symmetric tensor \(k\). It gives TT injectivity of \(\mathcal H\).

Write \(\mathscr B a=\delta Q(a)\) with target pairing
\(\int\langle q_1,q_2\rangle\mu_0=2K^2\int\langle q_1,q_2\rangle
dV_{g_0}\). From the formula for \(U\) and integration by parts,
\[
\langle\mathscr B a,q\rangle_{\mu_0}
=-2K\int\eta_{j,ab}\nabla_a a_{ib}\,q_{ij}\,dV
=2\langle a,d_0^*(qF_0)\rangle_{g_0,\delta}.
\]
Since \(\mathcal P_{\mathrm{conn}}'(A_0)=0\), its Hessian is
\(2\mathscr B^*\mathscr B\), which equals
\(4d_0^*((\mathscr B\,\cdot)F_0)\). In particular the associated
quadratic form is nonnegative. The flow intertwining gives
\(\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conn}}\mathcal Hh
=4\mathcal H(L+2K)h\) on TT.
The vanished first derivative removes the second derivative of
\(g\mapsto A_g^+\) from the Hessian chain rule. Composing gives
\[
\mathcal L_{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{met}}}h
=\mathcal H^*\mathcal L_{\mathrm{conn}}\mathcal Hh
=(L+4K)(L+2K)h .
\]
Independently the bilinear second variation of the pullback is
\(2(2/K)^2(2K^2)\langle\mathcal Dh,\mathcal Dk\rangle
=16\langle\mathcal Dh,\mathcal Dk\rangle\).
For \(\mathcal E_+=\int |W^+|_{\mathrm{op}}^2dV_g\) it is
\(2\langle\mathcal Dh,\mathcal Dk\rangle\).
Consequently \(\mathcal D^*\mathcal D=(L+2K)(L+4K)/16\)
on TT and
\(\mathcal L_{\mathcal E_+}
=\mathcal L_{\mathcal P_{\mathrm{met}}/8}
=(L+2K)(L+4K)/8\) there. The negative metric-gradient
linearizations are the negatives of these operators. The metric
pullback has a nonzero fourth-order TT principal symbol. The
independent connection flow has a second-order TT generator
even after reconstruction. Their generators have different
principal orders and no constant time rescaling equates them.

As a separate published comparison, in four dimensions
\(\widetilde{\mathcal F}_{-1/3}=\mathcal F_{-1/3}
=\int(|\operatorname{Ric}|^2-s^2/3)dV\) in Gursky–Viaclovsky.
Their eq. (1.1) and the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet formula immediately
following it use the four-index Weyl norm:
\[
\mathcal W_\mathrm{tensor}=32\pi^2\chi+2\mathcal F_{-1/3}.
\]
The tensor norm is four times the operator squared norm and the
integral of the difference of the chiral squared operator norms
is topological by the signature theorem. Hence
\(\mathcal E_+=\mathcal F_{-1/3}/4+\text{constant}\).
Under the Einstein/TT hypotheses of their Theorem 3.10,
\(\Delta_L=-L-8K\) on the round sphere by eq. (3.28).
Using \(n=4\), \(s=12K\), and their functional parameter
\(-1/3\) in eq. (3.41) yields
\(\tfrac12(\Delta_L+6K)(\Delta_L+4K)\), and multiplication by
\(1/4\) gives the same metric Hessian for \(\mathcal E_+\).
[Gursky–Viaclovsky, arXiv:1105.4648v2, §1.1,
§3.3 eqs. (3.28)–(3.29), Theorem 3.10 eq. (3.41), and §7.1][gv].

## Checks run and limits of the result

- The two inherited Python blocks were extracted verbatim to
  temporary scripts under `/private/tmp` and executed with Python 3.
  Both exited successfully. In [note 02][note2] the relevant exact
  check tests the projected \(-6Kq\) contraction at \(K=1\) on
  two diagonal and three off-diagonal basis matrices. Linearity in
  \(q,K\) extends that algebraic check to the displayed contraction.
  It does not check the covariant differential equation or its
  volume variation. The script also tested sample rational
  noncommuting curvature jets, sample degeneration parameters and
  one local adjoint source, outside these assigned claims.
- The relevant exact checks in [note 01][note1] test \(q=2W'/K\)
  at one rational matrix input, a Weyl-operator normalization
  at one local curvature jet, and the published Hessian arithmetic
  at four specified \(\lambda\) values (plus the unit-curvature
  rate \(15\) at \(\lambda=8\)). The BPST polynomial check is
  outside these claims. Neither script checks the full Bianchi
  derivation, time conversion, image preservation or spectral
  theorem. The formulas above provide the mathematical audit.
- The full chiral Bianchi contraction is local in a smooth source
  metric. Pointwise linearization and round commutators use the
  background and its chirality. The \(L^2\) arguments, conservation
  of the total curvature volume, and the normalized scalar average
  use compactness and absence of boundary. The connection and
  reconstruction formulas apply to the fixed round bundle for
  smooth infinitesimal variations; nearby smooth definite metrics
  provide the domain of the pullback near \(g_0\).
- Infinitesimal internal gauge and base-diffeomorphism directions
  kill \(\delta Q\) at \(Q=I\). Infinitesimal conformal and
  diffeomorphism directions kill \(\mathcal D\) at \(W^+=0\).
  The latter conformal kernel is a Hessian statement for the
  pullback. A nonlinear gauge fixing or metric-gradient PDE
  theorem requires additional work. In particular, replacing
  \(d_{A_g^+}^{*g}\) by \(d_{A_g^+}^{*\widehat g_{A_g^+}}\)
  away from round changes the full Bianchi calculation.
- Fine's local nonlinear existence starts with definite data.
  The stability theorem assumes definite initial data, a fixed perfect
  reference on a compact four-manifold, and sufficiently small
  \(L_k^2\) distance for \(k\geq3\) in a fixed background metric.
  It gives all-time existence and exponential convergence to
  that reference modulo bundle automorphisms. Neither the TT
  calculation nor that theorem supplies definite initial data,
  estimates away from the perfect neighborhood, or a global
  convergence theorem on arbitrary smooth homotopy four-spheres.
  [Fine, Theorems 4.3, 4.10 and 4.11][fine].

The requested identities agree with the manuscript in the stated
normalization. The dependence on the source adjoint and the round
linearization is the boundary for subsequent nonlinear use. Any
stronger comparison, gauge treatment or preservation question belongs
