## Universal-cover applicability

Take \(X\) smooth, connected, oriented, complete, Riemannian and four-dimensional, with \(\operatorname{Ric}(g)=0\) and \(W^+(g)=0\) for the stated orientation. Assume it is nonflat and ALE in Wright's §2 sense. Outside a compact set it has a smooth chart on \((\mathbb R^4\setminus B)/\Gamma_\infty(X)\) with the finite group acting freely on \(S^3\). The lifted metric approaches the Euclidean metric with order \(\tau>0\) and corresponding decay for all derivatives. Pull back both metric and orientation by the universal covering map \(p:\widetilde X\to X\). The covering is a local isometry, so Ricci flatness and \(W^+=0\) persist. The lift is complete by geodesic lifting and nonflat by surjectivity and the local curvature identity. [Wright v2, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2442v2#page=2).

- If \(X\) is simply connected, take \(\widetilde X=X\). In the chosen chirality, the induced connection on \(\Lambda^+\) is flat because its curvature blocks \(W^++s/12\) and trace-free Ricci both vanish. On the simply connected cover there is a global parallel frame of \(\Lambda^+\). Its quaternionically normalized forms yield a hyperkähler triple. Equivalently, Wright §2 gives restricted holonomy in the compatible \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\), which is the full holonomy group in the simply connected case. [Wright v2, §2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2442v2#page=3).
- For nonsimply-connected \(X\), Wright's Theorem A applies to the nonflat Ricci-flat ASD ALE class and gives a finite isometric quotient of a Gibbons–Hawking space, with cyclic deck group. More directly for the general cover reduction, §3 states finiteness and one-endedness using splitting, and ALE of \(\widetilde X\) using Bando–Kasue–Nakajima. Proposition 1's proof identifies the restriction of the finite cover over the ALE exterior with the connected exterior quotient by \(\Gamma_\infty(\widetilde X)<\Gamma_\infty(X)\). The inverse image of the compact core is compact. Each exterior component would project onto the base exterior and yield an end, so one-endedness gives connectedness. In those lifted exterior coordinates the metric has the lifted ALE bounds. The finite cover and parallel-frame argument produce a complete smooth hyperkähler ALE universal cover. This check uses Wright's statement of the splitting and ALE inputs and his finite-cover proof. [Wright v2, Thm. A and §3, Prop. 1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2442v2#page=4).
- Wright §2 Remark 2 imports order-four ALE coordinates for Ricci-flat ALE metrics from Bando–Kasue–Nakajima, meeting Lyu–Xu's decay convention. Wright §2–3 place the end group of the hyperkähler cover in \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\). The trivial subgroup is cyclic. The cited cyclic classification in §3 together with Table 1 identifies its metric case as \(k=1\), and §7.1 Thm. 2 uses \(k\) monopoles for \(\mathbb Z_k\); the §8 proof of Cor. B identifies the one-monopole metric as flat \(\mathbb R^4\). The cyclic classification is a statement about the simply connected hyperkähler spaces, so this reasoning applies regardless of the base deck degree. Nonflatness of the lift forces \(\Gamma_\infty(\widetilde X)\ne 1\). [Wright v2, §2 Remark 2 and Table 1, §3, §§7.1 and 8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2442v2#page=3).

The metric-model input is the statement that this simply connected hyperkähler ALE metric with its end group is represented by some smooth admissible Kronheimer \(X_\zeta\), with the hyperkähler structures identified up to rotation. Wright §3 explicitly identifies the cover as one of Kronheimer's constructed gravitational instantons. Lyu–Xu §1.1 states that construction and classification give a complete family of hyperkähler ALE metrics with each fixed nontrivial end group. The classification input is stronger than the diffeomorphism to a standard minimal resolution mentioned separately in Wright §2. Under the metric identification, the sphere of parallel orthogonal complex structures is identified, and positive constant metric rescaling, if used, preserves it. The natural orientation of each hyperkähler metric makes its Kähler forms self-dual and its \(W^+\) vanish. If a metric identification reversed orientation, both Weyl blocks would vanish and Ricci flatness would give flatness. Thus the identification has the chirality required here. [Wright v2, §§2–3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2442v2#page=3) · [Lyu–Xu v3, §1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18761v3#page=2).

## Smooth embedded curve in an actual hyperkähler rotation

Fix \(1\ne\Gamma<\mathrm{SU}(2)\) finite, with its irreducible ADE root system \(\Phi\) of rank \(r\ge1\). Lyu–Xu take \(Z\) as the dual of the center of the Lie algebra of \(\mathrm U(\Gamma)\), naturally identified with the real Cartan space \(\mathfrak h\). The admissible triples \(\zeta=(\zeta^1,\zeta^2,\zeta^3)\in(Z\otimes\mathbb R^3)^\circ\) avoid simultaneous annihilation of all three components by any root. Their \(X_\zeta\) is smooth with the hyperkähler quotient metric, and \(\operatorname{rank}\zeta=\dim\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb R}\{\zeta^1,\zeta^2,\zeta^3\}\) lies in \(\{1,2,3\}\). By (1.1), the complex fibers of \(Z(X_\zeta)\to\mathbb P^1\) are \(X_\zeta\) with the parallel complex structures \(I_\zeta^u\) indexed by \(u\in\mathbb P^1\). [Lyu–Xu v3, §§1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18761v3#page=2).

**Exact theorem check.** Theorem 1.2 and (1.2)–(1.7) give the following counts over twistor fibers.

| Parameter rank | Number of fibers containing a rational curve | Sum of the numbers of rational curves in those fibers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | \(2\) | \(2r\) |
| 2 | Between \(4\) and \(\#\Phi\) | Between \(2r\) and \(\#\Phi\) |
| 3 | Between \(3\) and \(\#\Phi\) | Between \(2r-1\) and \(\#\Phi\) |

Theorem 1.1 instead characterizes when a parallel complex structure is biholomorphic to the standard complex minimal resolution of \(\mathbb C^2/\Gamma\). This occurs exactly in rank one, where two members of the parallel family give that complex surface. Theorem 1.2 therefore has the all-ranks scope required here. [Lyu–Xu v3, §1.2, Thms. 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18761v3#page=3).

**Minimum proof for existence and embeddedness.** The count proof's combinatorial bounds are unnecessary for the one-sphere premise. The checked ingredients give the following direct argument.

1. The homogeneous lift of the holomorphic period in §2.3, (2.3), is
   \[
   \widetilde p_\zeta(z_1,z_2)
      = z_1^2[\omega_\zeta^2+i\omega_\zeta^3]
          + 2z_1z_2[\omega_\zeta^1]
          - z_2^2[\omega_\zeta^2-i\omega_\zeta^3]
       \ \in\mathfrak h_{\mathbb C}.
   \]
   Pick any root \(\alpha\in\Phi\). Then \(\alpha\circ\widetilde p_\zeta\) is a homogeneous quadratic in two complex variables. A nonzero homogeneous quadratic has a zero in \(\mathbb P^1\), including a possible zero at infinity. If it were identically zero the root condition would hold at each point as well. The root-kernel condition at a projective point is independent of the choice of nonzero homogeneous lift. [Lyu–Xu v3, §2.3, (2.3)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18761v3#page=10).
2. At such a point \(u\) the root subsystem \(\Phi_\lambda\) is nonempty for \(\lambda=\widetilde p_\zeta(\widetilde u)\). Corollary 2.14(1)–(3) identifies the corresponding complex twistor fiber as the minimal resolution of the corresponding deformation fiber, gives the root-kernel criterion for a rational curve, and counts its curves by \(\operatorname{rank}\Phi_\lambda\). Proposition 2.12 and Lemma 2.13 give the simultaneous resolution and the period pullback. Lemma 2.6 identifies the isolated Kleinian singularities with the irreducible components of the nonempty root subsystem. [Lyu–Xu v3, Lemma 2.6 and Prop. 2.12–Cor. 2.14](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18761v3#page=8).
3. Lemma 2.10 places the rational curves in the exceptional locus since any holomorphic map from \(\mathbb P^1\) to the affine deformation fiber is constant. Corollary 2.11 counts the actual exceptional components. The local description in §2.1 following Def. 2.1 gives each irreducible exceptional component as a smooth \(\mathbb P^1\) in a smooth resolved surface. Choose one single component and carry it over by the biholomorphism from Corollary 2.14(1). Its inclusion gives a smooth holomorphic embedding into \((X_\zeta,I_\zeta^u)\). Other components can meet it while its own embedding remains smooth. This uses the component itself and establishes the required embedding without any assumption on whether an arbitrary rational map is immersed or injective. [Lyu–Xu v3, §§2.1–2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18761v3#page=5).

For the Gibbons–Hawking case there is a corroborating description in Wright §7.1. His proof of Proposition 7 describes the inverse image of a monopole line as a chain of spheres and two ends, holomorphic for the corresponding parallel structure. The proposition's stated implication starts with an existing \((-2)\)-curve; the existence check here works uniformly for all ADE types through Lyu–Xu. [Wright v2, §7.1, Prop. 7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1102.2442v2#page=16).

In particular the embedded component is a \((-2)\)-sphere. The hyperkähler complex surface has a nowhere zero holomorphic two-form, as used in §2.3 before (2.3). Its determinant bundle is therefore trivial. The tangent-normal exact sequence along the sphere gives \(\deg N=-\deg T\mathbb P^1=-2\), so its holomorphic normal line is \(\mathcal O(-2)\) and its self-intersection is \(-2\). This is an adjunction consequence for the actual embedded sphere. [Lyu–Xu v3, §2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.18761v3#page=10).
