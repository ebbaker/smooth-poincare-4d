## Disposition

Pass. E148's criterion is equivalent to smooth extendibility within
the specified definite category. It requires only one sequence of
smooth incoming slices with a uniformly positive continuous pair
limit after smooth equivariant orientation-preserving automorphisms
of the same fixed bundle. Here “positive” refers to the definite
curvature planes with the stipulated round-compatible curvature-map
sign. The proof uses the native negative Killing form and clock.
It supplies neither the converging sequence nor a derivative bound
on the normalizations. No temporal strain budget is used.

## Common original lifespan

The compact positive range fixes the native reference-pairing
margin for the independent pair symbol, with one admissible
\(\lambda\) for the bounded cross block and uniformly positive
closed curvature block. The graph-connection margin is separate.
Its positive real quadratic form at reference equality controls
unit amplitudes on the compact unit cosphere by the assessed
exact symbol sequence. The off-reference graph symbol depends
on the actual current/reference pair values. Substitution of
curvature introduces the fixed exterior symbol, not a bound
on large symmetric connection jets. Thus the background-to-native
supremum comparison \(L_0\) and graph radius \(\rho_{\rm gr}\)
are available before selecting a smooth reference.

With one such reference fixed, E148 (1) gives
\[
 r_{\rm data}\le\epsilon/2,\qquad
 L_0r_{\rm data}\le\rho_{\rm gr}/2,\qquad
 L_0r_{\rm data}\le R/4.
\]
The first allows the fixed-reference pair construction for
one common \(T_{\rm ref}\); the others place each smooth
datum strictly in the graph region and leave room in the
native completed ball. The reference may be nonstationary,
since E138 retains its value forcing and chooses time
after the radii. The resulting continuous trace and
interior smoothness alone are not used to infer smoothness
at zero. For each particular smooth datum, its compact
first-jet image is inside the open strongly parabolic
graph region. The assessed smooth local construction,
with a cutoff near this image, gives an individual smooth
connection on a possibly much shorter interval. Its
actual curvature lifts it to the pair system. The
gradient seminorm is at most
\(C_{\rm ref}\sqrt{\ell}\sup|DU_{\rm sm}|\) there. The
supremum approaches its initial value, at most \(R/4\).
Thus the lift belongs to the smaller-slab \(X_\ell^\circ\)
ball for some individual \(\ell>0\). The fixed point
also restricts to this ball: restriction decreases the
norm and preserves the smooth completion. Shortened-slab
uniqueness identifies the two solutions, giving the
needed smoothness at zero without a uniform lower bound
for the individual smooth comparison time.

## Compatibility, reference selection and original overlap

With \(g_n\) the base map of \(h_n\), the underlying
adjoint bundle map is a native internal isometry.
Pullback of curvature is exact on its two base slots
and its internal slot. Orientation preservation of
\(g_n\) retains positivity of the wedge pairing and
the orientation of the chiral three-plane. With a
fixed affine origin \(A_b\), write
\(a_n=h_n^*A(t_n)-A_b\). For a smooth test two-form
in the fixed background, \(d_{A_b}a_n\) converges
distributionally by using the fixed formal adjoint.
The actual curvature identity has uniformly
convergent curvature and bracket terms on the other
side. This establishes
\[
 F_\infty=F_{A_b}+d_{A_b}a_\infty
                 +\tfrac12[a_\infty\wedge a_\infty]
\]
as continuous curvature in distributions. The sign
is locally constant on nondegenerate values, so the
assumed definite limit has the same sign.
[E38][e38] · [E148, lines 183–199][e148].

Use a closed fiber-ball tube of radius \(r_{\rm tube}\)
inside the open signed positive region. It is compact
because the base and endpoint graph are compact and
the fibers are finite-dimensional. On this tube fix
\(L_0,\epsilon,R,\rho_{\rm gr},r_{\rm data}\) first.
Then choose \(\delta<\min(r_{\rm tube},r_{\rm data})/4\).
E38's smooth graph density starts from an arbitrary
smooth background and the verified distributional
curvature identity. It requires no nearby compatible
smooth reference as input. It produces one such
reference within \(\delta\), with values in the
tube. Fix that reference's \(T_{\rm ref}\) and
\(\theta=3T_{\rm ref}/4\). Only then use convergence
to take one \(n\) within \(\delta\) of the limit
and with \(t_*-t_n<T_{\rm ref}/2\).
The normalized datum is within \(2\delta<r_{\rm data}\)
of the same reference. These quantifiers avoid taking
a shrinking reference time along the sequence.
[E38][e38] · [E138][e138] · [E148, lines 201–227][e148].

For any orientation-preserving fixed bundle map \(H\)
in this category, \(F_{H^*B}=H^*F_B\), the native metric
and volume pull back, and internal endomorphisms act
by conjugation. Testing the covariant exterior derivative
against pulled-back forms in the live metric gives
equivariance of the live adjoint as well. Consequently
\(V(H^*B)=H^*V(B)\) on this general fixed bundle. This
pointwise argument does not need a round reference.
With the one selected \(h_n\) fixed in time, the
adjusted recovery followed by its inverse pullback is
\[
 A_{\rm ext}(\tau)
   =(h_n^{-1})^*j(\tau-t_n)^*\mathcal A(\tau-t_n)
   =(j(\tau-t_n)\circ h_n^{-1})^*\mathcal A(\tau-t_n).
\]
At \(t_n\) the composition on the old datum is
\(h_n\circ h_n^{-1}\). Thus the initial condition
is exactly \(A(t_n)\) with only a native time
translation. The inverse map may have arbitrarily
large derivatives but is smooth and fixed. It need
not lie in the identity component of the bundle
automorphism group. Both solutions now solve the
original equation in the original identification.
Short agreement follows from smooth uniqueness.
Any first endpoint of agreement strictly before
\(t_*\) would yield a common smooth slice and
then more agreement by recentered uniqueness.
The resulting smooth splice crosses \(t_*\) since
\(t_n+\theta>t_*\).

## Positive-strip normalization and logical scope

For compatible data converging in the reference value
ball, smooth convergence of the adjusted pairs on an
interior strip implies smooth convergence of the
time-dependent recovery generators there, by their
local derivative formulas and the positive value tube.
Identity-anchored ODE flows on a fixed compact strip
then converge smoothly. For instance local ODE
differences are controlled by the bounded derivatives
of the limiting vector field and Gronwall. Differentiating
the ODE for the spatial Jacobian and then higher
derivatives gives successively the smooth dependence
and the equation yields time derivatives. The maps
are equivariant and preserve orientation. For smooth
data with a recovery \(j\) started at zero, the strip
map \(k(s)=j(s)\circ j(s_0)^{-1}\) has the same
Eulerian vector field and starts at the identity at
\(s_0\). Therefore
\(j(s)^*\mathcal A(s)=j(s_0)^*k(s)^*\mathcal A(s)\).
The pullback factor \(j(s_0)^*\) has no time variation
on this strip but can depend on the datum without a
convergence estimate. The strip statement establishes
neither \(C^0\)-datum continuity of the zero-based
original maps nor recovery from continuous data at
zero. For integration, identify the converging
data in the optional strip sentence explicitly as
compatible if calling the pulled-back paths original
solutions. The continuation implication needs only
the common lifespan.
[E138][e138] · [E148, lines 150–181][e148].

At a finite maximal smooth definite lifespan, such a
sequence would contradict maximality. Conversely a
smooth definite extension across the endpoint gives
both uniform limits in the original fixed background by
taking identity automorphisms and any time sequence
increasing to the endpoint. This is an equivalence
within the same signed definite category, including
definiteness at the endpoint. It does not rule out
normalized limits that lose definiteness. No extraction
of a normalization sequence, compactness from merely
bounded values, varying-bundle result, time-budget
estimate, or general existence theorem is proved.
