## Class, volume and full curvature energy

1. The class consists of smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian four-manifolds admitting the stated strict taming on their positive *metric* twistor spaces. The smooth manifolds may vary. The absolute Ricci tensor bound is in its ordinary \((0,2)\)-tensor Hilbert–Schmidt norm. The \(W^+\) bound is in the Hilbert–Schmidt norm as an endomorphism of ordinary self-dual two-forms, as clarified immediately after (1). Under the standard curvature-operator identification its full component tensor norm is twice the endomorphism Hilbert–Schmidt norm, by the two antisymmetric index pairs. The Euler bound is upper only.
2. The Ricci bound gives \(\operatorname{Ric}\ge-Kg=-3\kappa g\) with \(\kappa=K/3\). For \(D'>D\), every intrinsic ball of radius \(D'\) is the whole manifold. For the model ball volume \(\mathcal V_{-\kappa}\), the Bishop–Gromov ratio is nonincreasing and tends to one as the radius tends to zero. At all centers it follows that
   \[
   \operatorname{Vol}(M_i,g_i)\le\mathcal V_{-\kappa}(D'),
   \qquad
   \operatorname{Vol}_{g_i}B(y,\ell)\ge
      v\,\mathcal V_{-\kappa}(\ell)/\mathcal V_{-\kappa}(D')
      \ge c\ell^4
      \quad(0<\ell\le D').
   \]
   Here \(c=v\omega_4/\mathcal V_{-\kappa}(D')>0\), since the nonpositive model's volume is at least Euclidean. These are (2), including \(K=0\).
3. In the full \((0,4)\) component tensor convention the four-dimensional Euler density integrates as
   \[
     32\pi^2\chi(M_i)
       =\int_{M_i} \bigl(|\operatorname{Rm}|^2-4|\operatorname{Ric}|^2+s^2\bigr)\,dV .
   \]
   For an independent convention check, with \(E=\operatorname{Ric}-sg/4\) this integrand is \(|W|_{\rm full}^2-2|E|^2+s^2/6\), which gives the ordinary \(8\pi^2\) formula upon division by four and use of the Weyl endomorphism norm. Rearrangement gives exactly (3) with \(C_E=32\pi^2\chi_0+4K^2\mathcal V_{-\kappa}(D')\). One may enlarge to \(\max(1,C_E)\) on a nonempty class. If \(C_E<0\), nonnegative curvature energy forces the class to be empty. On replacing \(g\) by \(b^2g\), the full curvature norm scales by \(b^{-2}\) and measure by \(b^4\), so the energy is unchanged.
