## Degree and local weights

Write the cyclic brackets as \((a,a,-a)\), \(a>0\), in a dual orthonormal frame \(e_i\). The third direction acts by right rotations of angular speed \(a\). The base curvature is \(-a^2\) from the submersion formula and its area is \(A=22\pi/(70a^2)\) by orbifold Gauss–Bonnet. In Milnor's central triangle extension the relations \(g_i^{\alpha_i}=g_1g_2g_3=c\) give an abelianization of order \(11\). The central element \(c\) generates because killing it forces each \(g_i\) to have order dividing both \(11\) and \(\alpha_i\). The commutator thus meets the rotation center in \(\langle c^{11}\rangle\) and projects onto the base triangle group. The generic fiber has length \(22\pi/a\), giving \(\operatorname{Vol}(Y,\gamma_a)=242\pi^2/(35a^3)\). [Milnor, *On the 3-dimensional Brieskorn manifolds*, Theorem 3.1 and Corollaries 3.2, 6.5](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnbries.pdf)

Take \(\theta=(a/11)e^3\), with orientation \(\theta\wedge d\theta>0\) and positive base area form \(dA=e^{12}\) on horizontal lifts. Then \(d\theta=(a^2/11)dA\) and the first Chern number is \(d=-\int d\theta/(2\pi)=-1/70\). For the local action \((z,w)\mapsto(\zeta z,\zeta^\beta w)\), the degree is \(b+\sum_i\beta_i/\alpha_i\), where \(b\) is the degree of the desingularized line and \(0<\beta_i<\alpha_i\). Equivalently the abelianized Seifert presentation has relations \(\alpha_i q_i=\beta_i h\) and \(\sum_i q_i=-bh\), using the local lifts given by \(w=(z/|z|)^{\beta_i}\) and the generic fiber \(h\). Its determinant in absolute value is \(70|d|\), also giving \(d=-1/70\) from the trivial \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\) and the curvature sign. Reducing
\[
 70b+35\beta_2+14\beta_5+10\beta_7=-1
\]
modulo \(2,5,7\) gives \((b;\beta_2,\beta_5,\beta_7)=(-1;1,1,2)\). The complementary weights \((1,4,5)\) give degree \(+1/70\) with \(b=-2\) in the same convention. [Biquard–Herzlich–Rumin (BHR), §5, pp. 602–603](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2007_4_40_4_589_0.pdf) · [Mrowka–Ozsváth–Yu, §2, Example 2.3, Definitions 2.6–2.7 and the degree formula on p. 694](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/1997/0005/0004/CAG-1997-0005-0004-a003.pdf)

## Adiabatic and finite eta

For a direct sign calculation, take the disk orbibundle of the line with degree \(d\) and excise small neighborhoods of the three isolated quotient points. The complex orientation of the disk bundle induces the contact orientation on its outer boundary. Each excision link in its complex outward orientation is the round lens space \(L(\alpha_i,\beta_i)\). Its eta is \(-4s(\beta_i,\alpha_i)\) by APS II. The disk retracts to the base, and its rank-one rational intersection form has the sign of the zero-section self-intersection \(d\) by the Thom class formula. The excisions preserve the rational intersection form, since their links are rational homology spheres. Thus the smooth punctured disk has signature \(-1\). [APS I, Theorem (4.14)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf) · [APS II, Proposition (2.12)](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps002.pdf)

Put the exact metric \(g_v=(e^1)^2+(e^2)^2+v^2(e^3)^2\) on the outgoing boundary for \(v>0\), with an exact product normal germ. Choose the filling metric flat in each excision chart before excision. Conformal rescaling there makes the incoming normal germs product and keeps the Pontryagin density zero. Compare the Levi-Civita connection with the split pullback connection on the horizontal tangent and vertical line bundles. The split connection has \(p_1=0\) because it pulls back from a two-dimensional base. Along the outgoing boundary use the radial and angular frame on the vertical line, rescaled together by \(a/(11v)\) to be unit in the boundary product metric. In this frame the split vertical connection rotates by \(\theta\). By the product germ the outward unit normal is parallel, and the boundary Levi-Civita connection converges in these frames to the horizontal base connection with trivial radial and angular blocks as \(v\to0\). Indeed, the horizontal frame can be chosen basic and its coupling to the unit angular field is proportional to \(v,d\theta\). Transgressing \(p_1=-\operatorname{tr}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)/(8\pi^2)\) therefore removes the vertical primitive \(\theta\wedge d\theta/(4\pi^2)\), whose negative integral is \(d\). This comparison is on the full orbifold disk with smooth orbifold connections until excision. The flat and conformally flat chart regions contribute zero to the filling's Pontryagin integral. Ordinary APS on the smooth excised filling, with the three incoming links oriented oppositely, gives
\[
 \eta_{mathrm{ad}}=1+d/3-4\sum_i s(\beta_i,\alpha_i)
                   =1-1/210-4(0+1/5+1/14)=-19/210 .
\]

BHR (75)–(77) also give the minus sign upon regularizing the periodic terms directly. Pairing the \(n\) and \(-n\) terms of (76), the fractional part in (77) contributes \(\{n\beta/\alpha\}-\{-n\beta/\alpha\}=2((n\beta/\alpha))\) for \(n>0\), using the sawtooth with value zero at integers. The Hurwitz values \(\zeta(0,r/\alpha)=1/2-r/\alpha\) give \(-2s(\beta,\alpha)\) after summing residue classes at zero. Equation (75) doubles this contribution. The printed plus sign in (27) and Theorem 5.2 also fails the check in §10.1 using the effective Hopf action on \(L(3,2)\). Its reduced local weights are \(1,1\) at the two order-three points, so the two Dedekind sums add to \(1/9\). The other finite terms sum to \(2/3\). The round value \(2/9\) from (93) requires subtraction of \(4/9\). Using the opposite sign for the Dedekind term alone gives \(1237/420\) on the Mazur model, so even with that isolated ambiguity the conservative floor uses \(65/84\). Ouyang's original full text was unavailable for comparison. [BHR, §5, equations (27), (75)–(77) and (93)](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2007_4_40_4_589_0.pdf)

In Nicolaescu's restatement of Ouyang, the local actions and degree in (1.1)–(1.2) use \(\beta_i\) as above and the signature eta formula includes \(-4S(\beta,\alpha)\). Baraglia–Hekmati's restatement on an orientable base uses \(+4\sum_i s(b_i,a_i)\) and defines the defining line bundle by a divisor with coefficients \(-b_i\) at its cone points. Thus \(b_i=-\beta_i\bmod\alpha_i\), and both statements give the sign found by excision. [Nicolaescu, *Finite energy Seiberg–Witten moduli spaces on 4-manifolds bounding Seifert fibrations*, pp. 1034–1037, 1073](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2000/0008/0005/CAG-2000-0008-0005-a003.pdf) · [Baraglia–Hekmati, arXiv:2604.19195v2, §3 and proof of Proposition 5.1](https://pure.mpg.de/rest/items/item_3708304_1/component/file_3708305/content)

For the finite correction, in the coframe \((e^1,e^2,ve^3)\) the skew connection entries are \(\omega_{12}=-a(1+v^2/2)e^3\), \(\omega_{23}=av e^1/2\) and \(\omega_{31}=av e^2/2\). With \(de^1=-ae^{23}\), \(de^2=-ae^{31}\) and \(de^3=ae^{12}\), the Chern–Simons trace is \(-a^3(2+2v^2+v^4)e^{123}\). The product-ended interpolation cylinder has zero signature and the global coframe extends across it. APS transgression reproduces the positive finite correction \(a^3\operatorname{Vol}(Y,\gamma_a)(2v^2+v^4)/(24\pi^2)\), also the finite term of BHR Theorem 5.2 with \(t=11v/a\), \(V=A\) and \(\chi=-11/70\). Thus in the contact orientation
\[
 \eta(v)=\frac{121(2v^2+v^4)-38}{420},\qquad
 \eta(1)=\frac{65}{84},\qquad
 \eta(v_*)=0\quad\text{at}\quad v_*^2=\frac{\sqrt{159}}{11}-1 .
\]
Reversing the orientation negates \(\eta\).

## Consequence for separating product hypersurfaces

Let a single smooth connected separating \(Y\) in a smooth oriented closed homotopy four-sphere \(M\) have an exact Riemannian product neighborhood, with retained strip \([0,L]\times (Y,\gamma)\). Each side of the strip has signature zero. Interior representatives of any absolute rational two-cycles in one side have the same intersection as their images in \(M\), where \(H_2(M;\mathbb Q)=0\). This proves the vanishing even when a side has nonzero \(H_2\) or \(Y\) has nontrivial homology. Contractible fillings and homology-ball fillings are particular cases. A global positive smooth conformal rescaling can recover the same bound for a conformally product neighborhood. Extend the logarithm of the local conformal multiplier by a cutoff about the strip and use conformal invariance of each chiral Weyl energy in four dimensions.

With \(E_\pm(P)=\int_P |W^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2\,dV\) for the curvature operators on real two-forms, APS gives \(E_+(P)-E_-(P)=12\pi^2\eta(\partial P,\gamma)\) on either side. The boundary orientations are opposite, so one side supplies \(12\pi^2|\eta|\) to \(E_+\) and the other supplies the same to \(E_-\). A product with Ricci eigenvalues \(r_i\) on the cross-section has Weyl eigenvalues \(R_\gamma/6-r_i/2\) in each chiral block. Hence
\[
 E_\pm(M)\ge 12\pi^2 |\eta(Y,\gamma)| + \frac{L}{4}\int_Y|\operatorname{Ric}^0_\gamma|^2\,dV_\gamma .
\]
The eta term also applies to a single cut at \(L=0\) with a product germ from both sides. In the E56 punctured model round caps contribute zero to the Weyl integral.

For \(g_v\) the bracket constants are \((a/v,a/v,-av)\), giving Ricci eigenvalues \((-a^2(1+v^2/2),-a^2(1+v^2/2),a^2v^2/2)\). Their trace-free squared norm is \((2/3)a^4(1+v^2)^2\) and the volume is \(v\operatorname{Vol}(Y,\gamma_a)\). Thus the exact strip term is \((121\pi^2/105)aL\,v(1+v^2)^2\). At \(v_*\), the APS term vanishes for this different face metric. An entry-class conclusion there would still require both filling energies and the ordinary sign on the actual assembled metric. [Milnor, *Curvatures of left invariant metrics on Lie groups*, §4, Theorem 4.3](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82428733.pdf)
