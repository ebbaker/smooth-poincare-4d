## Trace form and length

The criterion passes for two fixed compact Riemannian halves with exact product germs, round puncture ends in the actual cap markings, and seam identifications that pull the two face metrics back to the same \(\gamma\) on \(Y\). The orientation-preserving marking \(f\) between the original boundaries requires the second trace to be pulled across as \(\psi_1\circ f^{-1}\) in its physical coordinates. One original half is reversed in the double. For contractible fillings with connected boundary, van Kampen and Mayer–Vietoris give a smooth simply connected homology four-sphere after capping, since the common boundary is an integral homology three-sphere. Inserting length reparametrizes the collars and preserves the smooth type.

For either potential \(V=R\) or \(R-6\Lambda\), the puncture term in the half energy is \(6\langle\psi,\sqrt{1-\Delta_{S^3}}\psi\rangle\). On each round end, a harmonic mode of degree \(m\) gives an energy \(6\int_0^\infty(b'^2+(m+1)^2b^2)\), with minimum \(6(m+1)b(0)^2\). The round product compactifies in the given marking using a factor equal to one near the junction and \(e^{-s}\) far down the end. The resulting round cap is Weyl-flat, and the ordinary and modified quadratic forms both transfer by conformal covariance. The zero-cap-trace remainder has strictly positive energy on every nonzero test. Positivity of the closed quadratic form on all nonzero \(H^1\) functions gives positive lowest eigenvalue by compactness, and then a positive Sobolev-normalized infimum by the bounded potential and the gradient term. The analogous argument on a fixed half gives coercivity whenever the puncture-corrected form is strictly positive on its zero-seam-trace subspace. [Master round-boundary form and conformal identification](../../../../master/manuscript/marked_cut.tex#L117)

For the product metric \(dt^2+\gamma\), the opposite pair to the plane \(0i\) has sectional curvature \(R_\gamma/2-r_i\), where \(r_i\) are Ricci eigenvalues of \(\gamma\). Projection onto a chiral block halves this entry and subtracting \(R_\gamma/12\) leaves \(R_\gamma/6-r_i/2\). Thus \(V_\gamma=3r_{\min}\) in the modified case and each chiral energy per unit length is \(\rho_\gamma=\tfrac14\int|\operatorname{Ric}^0_\gamma|^2\). The ordinary potential is \(R_\gamma\). The cross-section operator \(A=-6\Delta_\gamma+V_\gamma\) is self-adjoint with compact resolvent even for the bounded eigenvalue-defined potential. A mode of eigenvalue \(\ell_j\) has zero-trace coercivity exactly when \(\ell_j+6\pi^2/L^2>0\), so \(\ell_1+6\pi^2/L^2>0\) is necessary and sufficient on that subspace. If this holds, the solution of \(6b''=\ell_j b\) gives the two prescribed trace cost as \(6[b b']_0^L\). At \(k^2=|\ell_j|/6\), its matrix has diagonal/off-diagonal entries \(6k(\coth kL,-\operatorname{csch}kL)\) for \(\ell_j>0\), \(6(1,-1)/L\) at zero, and \(6k(\cot kL,-\csc kL)\) for \(\ell_j<0\). Large positive modes have growth \(O(\sqrt{\ell_j})\), compatible with the \(H^{1/2}\) traces.

On each half the affine minimum with prescribed seam trace exists by zero-trace coercivity and a bounded trace lift, and is unique. The weak exterior normal pairing gives its cost \(d_i\), the contribution after the round-end Robin condition. The sum of these costs and the cylinder matrix form is the exact joined trace test. Subtracting each minimizing extension leaves the three zero-trace forms, with mixed terms zero by the weak equations. This establishes both directions of the criterion, including failure at zero in a zero-trace subproblem.

For duplicate halves the reflected free-end condition gives \(b'(L/2)=0\) and the trace cost eigenvalues \(6k\tanh(kL/2),0,-6k\tan(kL/2)\) for the positive, zero, and negative cases respectively. The first zero-junction Dirichlet–Neumann eigenvalue is again \(\ell_1+6\pi^2/L^2\). If \(q_h(u)\ge c\|u\|_{H^1}^2\) and \(\|\operatorname{Tr}u\|_{2}\le C_{\rm tr}\|u\|_{H^1}\), the bound \(6k_0\tan(k_0L/2)C_{\rm tr}^2<c\), \(k_0=\sqrt{-\ell_1/6}\), proves the stated sufficient positive window for \(\ell_1<0\). It is within the strictly positive Dirichlet–Neumann range. A compatible isometric twist preserves the two free half tests by relabeling the product. The identity double admits unrestricted even extension. Extension through the twist of a particular reflected test requires the midpoint trace to be invariant, and the two-trace test handles the general twist. Thus the sufficient length is conditional on the whole-half estimate, which includes the retained interpolation and round-end terms.

## Topology, degree and eta sign

Isoshima's Figure 37 with \(n=1\) depicts the identity double of \(W^-(0,3)\), and Theorem 3.3 gives its standard smooth type. Akbulut–Kirby Proposition 1 takes \(W^-(0,3)\) to \(W^+(2,-2)\) and then twice in reverse direction in part (1) to \(W^+(0,0)\). Theorem 2(1) identifies the boundary as \(\Sigma(2,5,7)\). Proposition 1 and its orientation remark justify keeping this identification up to orientational sign. The separate cork example has identity and twisted doubles both diffeomorphic to \(S^4\), as stated in Auckly–Kim–Melvin–Ruberman §1 and Figure 2. The eta conclusion below is for the specified homogeneous Mazur metric. The assertion for the separate cork uses a prescribed invariant metric and the conditional trace comparison. [Isoshima, arXiv v2, Theorem 3.3 and Figure 37](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17356v2) · [Akbulut–Kirby, Proposition 1, its Remark 2 and Theorem 2(1)](https://math.berkeley.edu/~kirby/papers/Akbulut%20and%20Kirby%20-%20Mazur%20manifolds%20-%20MR0544597.pdf) · [Auckly–Kim–Melvin–Ruberman, §1, Figure 2](https://msp.org/agt/2017/17-3/agt-v17-n3-p11-s.pdf)

Use Milnor's commutator quotient of the inverse image of the orientation-preserving \((2,5,7)\) triangle group. The presentation has \(g_1^2=g_2^5=g_3^7=g_1g_2g_3=c\), where the \(g_i\) are lifts of the rotations by \(2\pi/\alpha_i\) and \(c\) is a full turn in that same sign. The abelianization determinant is \(70(1-1/2-1/5-1/7)=11\). The class of \(c\) generates it because a trivial class of \(c\) would force all \(g_i\) to vanish, using coprimality of each \(\alpha_i\) and 11. Thus the commutator still projects onto the base triangle group, and its intersection with the central fiber subgroup is \(\langle c^{11}\rangle\). In the cyclic frame \([e_2,e_3]=ae_1\), \([e_3,e_1]=ae_2\), \([e_1,e_2]=-ae_3\), the adjoint action of \(\exp(t e_3)\) rotates \(e_1,e_2\) positively at speed \(a\). The base orientation is \(e^1\wedge e^2\), also the sign of \(de^3\). The regular fiber therefore has length \(22\pi/a\). From \(K(e_1,e_2)=-7a^2/4\) and the vertical bracket \(a\), the base curvature is \(-a^2\), so its area is \(22\pi/(70a^2)\) and \(\chi=-11/70\). The volume of \(Y\) is \(242\pi^2/(35a^3)\). [Milnor, Brieskorn manifolds, Lemma 3.1 and proof, §3, Corollary 6.5](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnbries.pdf) · [Milnor, left-invariant curvature, §4, Theorem 4.3](https://core.ac.uk/download/pdf/82428733.pdf)

Normalize \(\theta=(a/11)e^3\) so the Reeb flow has period \(2\pi\) on regular fibers. Then \(d\theta=(a^2/11)dA\) with \(dA\) the oriented physical base area form, and \(d=-\int d\theta/(2\pi)=-1/70\) in BHR's convention. With \(g_i\) representing \(m_i[c]\), one has \(m=(6,9,8)\) modulo 11. The commutator generator over each cone is \(g_i c^{-m_i}\). In an equivariant local disk section, left translation by \(g_i\) rotates the base by \(+2\pi/\alpha_i\) and advances the regular fiber angle by \(2\pi/(11\alpha_i)\). The compensating translation by \(c^{-m_i}\) gives the local quotient action
$$
 (z,u)\longmapsto (e^{2\pi i/\alpha_i}z,e^{2\pi i\beta_i/\alpha_i}u),
 \qquad \beta_i=(1-\alpha_i m_i)/11\bmod\alpha_i.
$$
Thus the BHR weights are \((1,1,2)\) with base weight \(+1\) at each cone. The sign also follows from the positive complex-link action with weights \((35,14,10)\). At a cone of order \(\alpha_i\) the coordinate \(z_i\) that vanishes is a complex transverse disk coordinate. The exceptional fiber time \(2\pi/\alpha_i\) acts on it by the corresponding weight modulo \(\alpha_i\), namely \((1,4,3)\). In the displayed quotient chart, that same time gives the transverse exponent \(-\beta_i^{-1}\), by using the inverse quotient action to return to the original angle. It recovers \((1,1,2)\). Milnor's automorphic-form realization is homogeneous in the complex fiber and the right rotation maps to this positive action. [Milnor, §6, proof of Corollary 6.5](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnbries.pdf) · [Biquard–Herzlich–Rumin, §5, p. 603, local weights and degree](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2007_4_40_4_589_0.pdf)

### Adiabatic sign and finite correction

The plus sign on the Dedekind sum in BHR Theorem 5.2, read literally with the local-action convention of their §5, conflicts with their positively oriented round lens-space formula (93). Their §10 gives local data \((\rho,\beta)=(1,1),(-1,2)\) for \(L(3,2)\). The two local sums are \(1/18\) each and the round-metric polynomial term is \(2/3\), predicting \(10/9\) if the local sums are added. Formula (93) gives \(2/9\). The discrepancy can be resolved for this seam by applying the signature theorem to a disk orbibundle, using (93) only on round links. [Biquard–Herzlich–Rumin, §5, Theorem 5.2, and §10.1, (93)](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2007_4_40_4_589_0.pdf)

Take the disk bundle of the complex orbifold line with circle bundle \(Y\). Near a point over a cone it has isolated quotient chart \(\mathbb C^2/\langle(e^{2\pi i/\alpha_i},e^{2\pi i\beta_i/\alpha_i})\rangle\), with complex orientation. Use the metric \(dr^2+f_\varepsilon(r)^2\theta^2+\gamma_{\mathrm{base}}\), where \(f_\varepsilon=r\) at the zero section and \(f_\varepsilon=\varepsilon\) near the outer boundary, with \(f_\varepsilon>0\) away from zero. It is smooth in the orbifold charts. For the vertical plane use the connection with rotation form \(f_\varepsilon'\theta\) in the polar frame; near zero its subtraction of the polar angle differential is smooth in a Cartesian frame. Use the pullback base Levi–Civita connection on the horizontal plane. The Pontryagin form of the split connection is \((d(f_\varepsilon'\theta))^2/(4\pi^2)\), since the pulled-back horizontal curvature has zero wedge square. With \(dr\wedge\theta\wedge dA\) positive, integration gives
$$
 \int p_1^{\rm split}
 =\frac{f_\varepsilon'(\mathrm{out})^2-f_\varepsilon'(0)^2}{4\pi^2}
       \int_Y\theta\wedge d\theta=d .
$$
The integral uses orbifold measure. At the outer boundary the Levi–Civita connection differs from this split connection by \(O(\varepsilon)\) in fixed boundary forms, with the same bound on the tangential exterior derivative. This follows from \(d(f_\varepsilon\theta)=\varepsilon\,d\theta\) there by the coframe equations. The inner contribution to the transgression tends to zero when shrinking balls in smooth orbifold charts. It follows that the Levi–Civita \(p_1\) integral tends to \(d\) as the boundary fiber collapses.

For APS, remove small quotient balls around each singular point. At fixed \(\varepsilon\), flatten the metric near the singular centers before removing balls of radius \(\delta\). In normal coordinates the correction to Euclidean coefficients is \(O(\delta^2)\), with first derivative \(O(\delta)\); cutting it off at scale \(\delta\) keeps the second derivative bounded and changes the \(p_1\) integral by \(O(\delta^4)\). The flat quotient cone can be made product at each inner boundary by a radial round warping with zero Pontryagin density. Each boundary then has round lens metric in the orientation opposite to the complex link. The underlying disk orbibundle has rational signature \(\operatorname{sign}(d)=-1\): the square of the real relative Thom class integrates to the Euler number \(d\), and the base has one degree-two class. Removing the quotient balls preserves this intersection form because their lens links are rational homology spheres. Apply APS, first letting \(\delta\to0\) and then \(\varepsilon\to0\). The result with the sign of each inner boundary is
$$
 \eta_{\mathrm{ad}}
 =1+d/3+\sum_i\eta_{\mathrm{sig}}(L(\alpha_i,\beta_i)_{\rm round})
 =1+d/3-4\sum_i s(\beta_i,\alpha_i)
 =-\frac{19}{210}.
$$
Here \(s(1,2)=0,\ s(1,5)=1/5,\ s(2,7)=1/14\). [APS I, Theorem 4.14](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf) · [Biquard–Herzlich–Rumin, (93), the round lens formula](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2007_4_40_4_589_0.pdf)

The finite correction can be checked separately in the global coframe. With \(e^3\) scaled to \(ve^3\) and \(e^1,e^2\) fixed, the skew connection entries are \(\omega_{12}=-a(1+v^2/2)e^3,\ \omega_{23}=(av/2)e^1,\ \omega_{31}=(av/2)e^2\). Inserting the cyclic structure equations into \(\operatorname{tr}(\omega\,d\omega+2\omega^3/3)\) gives \(-a^3(2+2v^2+v^4)e^{123}\). On the interpolating product-ended cylinder the APS signature is zero, and \(-1/(24\pi^2)\) times the difference of these integrals is the eta change. From \(v\downarrow0\) to \(v=1\) this gives \(121/140\). It agrees with the metric-dependent polynomial of Theorem 5.2, whose ratio \(\pi t^2/V\) is 385 after rescaling to the Levi base metric \((a^2/11)\gamma_{\mathrm{base}}\). The exact value in the positive contact orientation is therefore \(\eta_{\mathrm{sig}}=65/84\). [Biquard–Herzlich–Rumin, (10) and the polynomial term of Theorem 5.2](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2007_4_40_4_589_0.pdf)

## All-length boundary cost

Use APS I Theorem 4.14 for a compact product-boundary filling, with \(B=(-1)^p(*d-d*)\) on even forms \(\Omega^{2p}(Y)\) in dimension three and the outward-normal-first boundary orientation. The nonzero-eigenvalue eta at zero is the correction to the topological signature. APS cancels the kernel contributions in the signature theorem. The formula with a kernel contribution in a general boundary operator index is a different statement. BHR's §2 gives the matching \(L\)-density \((|W^+|_{\rm HS}^2-|W^-|_{\rm HS}^2)dV/(12\pi^2)\) and zero extrinsic term for a product germ. Capping all marked round holes first contributes zero Weyl energy, preserves contractibility of the filled half, and leaves the \(Y\) boundary exactly product. Consequently each original half has chiral energy sum at least \(12\pi^2|\eta_{\mathrm{sig}}|\), and their original signed differences coincide under the compatible marking. Reversal of one half makes the differences cancel in the closed double, whose two chiral energies are equal. [Atiyah–Patodi–Singer I, (1.7), Theorem 4.14, pp. 65–66](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf) · [Biquard–Herzlich–Rumin, §2, pp. 596–597](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_2007_4_40_4_589_0.pdf)

The physical Ricci eigenvalues are \(a^2(-3/2,-3/2,1/2)\), from the bracket curvature formula. This gives \(\rho_{\gamma_a}=(2/3)a^4\operatorname{Vol}(Y)=484\pi^2 a/105\). Therefore the common closed chirality has the all-length lower bound
$$
 E_+(L)=E_-(L)\ \ge\ \frac{65\pi^2}{7}+\frac{484\pi^2}{105}aL .
$$
The first term alone exceeds \(8\pi^2\), including a zero inserted length with the same retained product germs. Orientation reversal only reverses the sign of eta. The invariant bound holds for the exact homogeneous seam with contractible halves and conformally flat marked round caps, and survives a global smooth conformal change of that construction. The general short-seam trace criterion continues to apply for other product face metrics, with positivity and the retained half energies depending on those actual metrics. No qualifying retained half for that general case was supplied.
