\section{The spin boundary test at the eta-zero face}
\label{sec:eta-zero-filling}

For a smooth oriented spin four-manifold \(W\) with marked
product boundary \((Y,\gamma)\), first restore the marked round
puncture caps and include the cylinder up to its midpoint.
Let \(h\) be this compact metric. The earlier round-end
minimization and even extension identify the ordinary sign
of the reflected identity double with positivity of
\[
 Q_h(u)=\int_W(6|du|_h^2+R_hu^2)\,dV_h,
 \qquad u\in H^1(W)\setminus\{0\}.
\]
In particular the minimization has free trace at the seam.

\begin{proposition}[A necessary spin boundary condition]
\label{prop:free-trace-spin}
If \(Q_h>0\) on every nonzero test, the boundary spin Dirac
operator of \(\gamma\) is invertible and the chiral spin
APS index on \((W,h)\) is zero. With outward-normal-first
boundary orientation this gives
\[
 4\eta_{\rm spin}(Y,\gamma)+\eta_{\rm sig}(Y,\gamma)
  =-\sigma(W).
\]
The two eta values are unreduced. On a contractible filling
with signature eta zero the spin eta must therefore vanish.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Take the positive first Neumann eigenfunction of
\(-6\Delta_h+R_h\). Its positive eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\)
gives \(R_{u^2h}=\lambda_1u^{-2}>0\), and \(\partial_\nu u=0\)
preserves the vanishing second fundamental form. The induced
boundary metric is \(u_Y^2\gamma\).
For a harmonic chiral spinor with nonpositive APS boundary
trace, the boundary term in the integrated Lichnerowicz identity
is nonpositive because the boundary is minimal. The bulk
term \(\int(|\nabla\psi|^2+R|\psi|^2/4)\) is positive for a
nonzero spinor. Thus both chiral kernels vanish with either
choice at the zero spectral space. Normal Clifford multiplication
interchanges the spectral signs and yields the adjoint boundary
condition. Both cokernels vanish as well. The index change on
adding the zero boundary eigenspace equals its dimension,
so that dimension is zero. The argument uses the intrinsic
adapted boundary operator even at the nonproduct conformal
metric.
The boundary identity and adjoint domains are given in
\cite[(13), (16)--(17), Theorem~6]{HijaziMontielZhang2001Boundary}
and \cite[Examples 4.21 and 5.12, Corollary 6.2]{BaerBallmann2016Guide}.

In dimension three the conformal law
\(D_{e^{2f}\gamma}(e^{-f}\phi)=e^{-2f}D_\gamma\phi\)
keeps the boundary Dirac kernel zero throughout this conformal
path. The adapted negative spectral projections then vary
continuously, giving a continuous family of Fredholm problems.
For instance the sign operators vary continuously on fixed
trace Sobolev spaces by elliptic functional calculus with the
common spectral gap, and the trace right inverse locally
identifies the moving domains. The product metric and the
PSC metric therefore have the same APS index. For the original
product metric the two APS formulas give
\[
 \operatorname{ind}_{\mathrm{APS}}D^+
 =-\frac{\sigma(W)+\eta_{\rm sig}}8
    -\frac{\eta_{\rm spin}+\dim\ker D_\gamma}{2},
\]
since \(\widehat A_4=-p_1/24\) and \(L_4=p_1/3\).
\cite[Theorems (4.2) and (4.14)]{APS1975SpectralI}
\end{proof}

We evaluate the condition for the actual marked Mazur face of
Section~\ref{sec:short-marked-seam}. Write
\(\gamma(v)=(e^1)^2+(e^2)^2+v^2(e^3)^2\) in its fixed Milnor
coframe. The global framing gives the unique spin structure of
the integral homology sphere. Let \(X_i\) be its dual fields,
\(C_i\) the corresponding constant Clifford matrices in the
rescaled orthonormal frame, and \(\omega=C_1C_2C_3=\pm1\).
The signs in the next norm estimate hold in either oriented
Clifford convention. In the global framing
\[
 D_v=H+v^{-1}V-\frac{av}{4}\omega,\qquad
 H=C_1X_1+C_2X_2,\qquad V=C_3X_3+\frac a2\omega .
\]
Indeed the spin connection for cyclic brackets
\((a/v,a/v,-av)\) contributes
\((a/(2v)-av/4)\omega\). Their horizontal commutators give
\(HV+VH=0\). The period \(22\pi/a\) of \(X_3\) means that its
frequencies in this spin frame are in \(i(a/11)\mathbb Z\).
Consequently \(\|V\psi\|_2\ge(a/22)\|\psi\|_2\).
The anticommutator identity, integrating in Haar volume, now
gives
\[
 \operatorname{dist}(0,\operatorname{spec}D_v)
  \ge\frac{a}{22v}-\frac{av}{4}>0
 \qquad(0<v^2<2/11).
\]
In particular, there is no zero crossing between a sufficiently
collapsed fiber and \(v_*^2=\sqrt{159}/11-1\).

To determine the index on this interval, normalize the base
area to \(\pi\) by a common metric factor \(35a^2/11\).
The fiber radius is \(r=\sqrt{385}\,v\) and its connection
satisfies \(d\theta=-2\ell\,dA_{\rm base}\) with
\(\ell=-1/70\). This is the fixed constant-curvature base
and connection normalization for the small-fiber spin
computation. In its notation the cone orders are \((2,5,7)\),
the local actions have weights \((1,1,2)\), the canonical
degree is \(11/70\), and the flat spin parameter is
\(\rho=\{-11/2\}=1/2\).
The spin isotropy data are \((0,4,2)\), from multiplying
the local action weights by \(-6\) modulo the cone orders.
Substitution in the flat adiabatic formula yields the three
Dedekind--Rademacher terms \(0,1/5,-1/7\) and the three
fractional terms \(1/4,9/10,3/14\). Thus in the contact
orientation, and with the spin APS convention of
Proposition~\ref{prop:free-trace-spin},
\[
 \lim_{v\downarrow0}\eta_{\rm spin}(\gamma(v))
 =-\ell/4+\ell/6+3/2-2(1/5-1/7)-(1/4+9/10+3/14)
 =19/840 .
\]
The Levi--Civita and the adiabatic values have the same limit
by the small-fiber metric correction.
For the operator comparison and sum conventions, see
\cite[\S1.2, (1.34)--(1.36), Remark~1.13]{Nicolaescu2000Seifert}
and \cite[\S1]{Nicolaescu2001Lattice}.

The small-fiber signature eta is \(-19/210\).
The combined APS term \(4\eta_{\rm spin}+\eta_{\rm sig}\)
is constant on the gap interval. This follows by applying
the combined formula along a path of product-germ metrics
on one fixed capped filling, since the invertible boundary
operators give a constant spin APS index. Its limiting value
is zero. Hence the earlier exact signature calculation gives
\[
 \eta_{\rm spin}(\gamma(v))=
     \frac{38-121(2v^2+v^4)}{1680}
 \qquad(0<v^2<2/11).
\]
Both eta values and the spin APS index therefore vanish at the
eta-zero face. The ordinary transverse potential is still
\(-a^2(2+v_*^2/2)<0\), so the retained-half trace estimate
remains necessary.

\begin{proposition}[Equivalent strict-budget filling test]
\label{prop:totally-geodesic-half-filling}
Fix a marked compact half \(W\), a smooth boundary metric
\(\gamma\), and a strict budget \(C>0\). A positive Yamabe
reflected double with exact product germ \(\gamma\) and both
closed chiral energies below \(C\) exists if and only if
\(W\) admits a smooth positive-scalar-curvature metric \(g\)
with totally geodesic boundary conformal to \(\gamma\) in the
given marking and
\[
 E_+(g;W)+E_-(g;W)<C.
\]
The double may use marked conformally flat round puncture caps
and an arbitrarily short positive inserted product cylinder.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
For necessity use the Neumann conformal eigenfunction on the
capped half, including any half-cylinder. Reflection swaps
chiralities, so the closed energy of either sign is precisely
the half sum. Conformal change preserves it.

For sufficiency write \(g|_Y=b^2\gamma\), extend \(b>0\)
with zero normal derivative, and use \(h=b^{-2}g\).
Its Fermi collar is \(dt^2+h_t\) with \(h_0=\gamma\) and
\(\partial_t h_0=0\). In the smooth double defined by this
collar the even extension of \(h_t\) is \(C^2\), and the
corresponding \(C^2\) even conformal change via \(b^2\)
has positive scalar curvature. The even \(h\) therefore
has a coercive ordinary quadratic form.
Let \(\chi\) be smooth, zero near \(0\), one on \([1,\infty)\)
and valued in \([0,1]\). On each half replace \(h_t\) by
\[
 h_t^\delta=\gamma+\chi(t/\delta)(h_t-\gamma).
\]
The double is now smooth and exact product at its seam.
Taylor expansion at zero gives \(O(\delta)\) convergence in
\(C^1\) and uniformly bounded \(C^2\) norms. Uniform scalar
density bounds and the \(O(\delta)\) volume of the changed
region bound the scalar form difference by
\(O(\delta^{1/2})\|u\|_{H^1}^2\) by H\"older and Sobolev.
The gradient term is \(o(1)\|u\|_{H^1}^2\) by \(C^0\)
closeness. Thus the ordinary
sign stays positive. Uniform curvature on the modified
collar gives a chiral energy change \(O(\delta)\).

Disjoint small interior balls can be flattened with the same
estimates in normal coordinates. In a fixed smooth ball
marking, normal first jets can be arranged by a chart
diffeomorphism supported in its interior neighborhood.
A radial reparametrization in that chart can carry a larger
prescribed puncture and its margin into the flat ball by a
homothety there. A smooth conformal factor matching the inverse
Euclidean radius on the surrounding annulus then makes the
unit round product and keeps the cap conformally flat in the
actual marking. These changes preserve the strict tests for
small enough initial flattening balls. After the seam is
flattened, the positive free-trace form persists for sufficiently
short insertions by Proposition~\ref{prop:short-seam-trace}
and its reflected test. The added Weyl energy is precisely
the length times the measured chiral density integral.
\end{proof}

For \(v=v_*\), Proposition~\ref{prop:totally-geodesic-half-filling}
reduces the remaining construction to a positive scalar
metric with totally geodesic boundary in the indicated
conformal class and half budget below \(8\pi^2\). This
criterion supplies the collar once such a metric is given.
The spin and signature boundary tests give no obstruction
at this specific face. The equality \(E_+=E_-\) holds on
this half by signature APS on the approximating product
metrics and convergence of the energies, so the strict
half budget is equivalently \(E_+(g;W)<4\pi^2\).

Any such positive-scalar filling of the Mazur face would
have a strictly unstable minimal boundary in the reflected
double. If \(\mu\) is the lowest boundary conformal-Laplacian
eigenvalue, then \(\mu<0\) by
Example~\ref{ex:mazur-spectral-seam}. At zero second fundamental
form, \(2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)=R_g-R_{g|_Y}\).
Testing the Jacobi form on a unit \(\mu\) eigenfunction
\(\phi\) gives the upper bound
\[
 \lambda_1(-\Delta_Y-\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu))
 \le \tfrac12(\mu-\min_Y R_g)-3\int_Y|d\phi|^2<0.
\]
Thus stable minimal constructions cannot meet this filling test.

\begin{corollary}[Inclusive recognition for marked isometric gluings]
\label{cor:inclusive-isometric-filling}
Let \(W\) be smooth, compact, oriented and contractible, with
marked boundary \(Y\). Suppose \(h\) is a smooth positive
scalar curvature metric on \(W\) with totally geodesic boundary
conformal to a prescribed \(\gamma\) in that marking. Set
\[
 B_h=E_+(h;W)+E_-(h;W),\qquad G_h=\int_W\Lambda_h^2\,dV_h,
\]
using the larger of the two largest signed Weyl eigenvalues
for \(\Lambda\). For every orientation-preserving
\(\gamma\)-isometry \(f:Y\to Y\), the fixed closed smooth
homotopy sphere \(M_f=W\cup_f(-W)\) has positive Yamabe
classes for which \(E_\pm\to B_h\) and
\(P=4E_++3\int_{M_f}\Lambda^2dV\to 4B_h+6G_h\).
In particular, with \(T=4\pi^2\) and the universal
constants of the inclusive recognition theorem, either
\[
 4B_h+6G_h\le8T+\eta\qquad\text{or}\qquad
 B_h\le T+\varepsilon_*
\]
implies \(M_f\cong_{\rm diff} S^4\).
\cite[section on the uniform isotropic spectral gap]{Baker2026FixedDescent}
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Use the conformal correction and seam flattening of
Proposition~\ref{prop:totally-geodesic-half-filling}.
For small widths the reflected form is coercive. Even
extension gives positive free-trace sign on its single
product half. The signed Weyl eigenvalues
have conformal weight \(-2\), so \(\Lambda^2dV\) and
each chiral density are conformally invariant. The curvature
is bounded uniformly on the changed collars of vanishing
volume. Both half chiral energies and the half \(\Lambda^2\)
integral therefore converge to those of \(h\).

Glue the two product halves using the given isometry
\(f\). Restriction and addition of the two positive
free-trace forms proves positive ordinary sign on the
actual glued class. One may also choose an inserted collar
whose length tends to zero and meets the reflected
free-trace bound for each width. Its two halves are
relabeled by \(f\) as in the short-seam test, and the
sum of the two ordinary forms remains positive.
Collared reparametrization identifies every such joined
manifold with the same \(M_f\), using \(f\) on the
marked faces and fixing the interiors away from the seam.
Contractibility of the halves and the homology sphere
boundary give the homotopy sphere condition.

Reversal of the second half's orientation exchanges the
chiralities and preserves \(\Lambda\). Each inserted
strip contribution tends to zero with its length.
If marked round punctures are used, perform the interior
ball flattenings at shrinking scales and cap in their
markings as above. Both changes also have vanishing cost.
Thus each closed chirality tends to \(B_h\) and the
closed \(\Lambda^2\) integral tends to \(2G_h\).
The infima of the positive classes on this fixed smooth
type obey the stated upper bounds, so equality in either
threshold is sufficient.
\end{proof}
