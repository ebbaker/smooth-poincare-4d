## Hyperbolic pants and the marked transition bands

The exact \(K=-1\) base times the unit sphere and the exact hyperbolic disk caps are locally conformally flat. In upper half-plane coordinates \((x,z)\), the Euclidean pullback by \((x,z,p)\mapsto (x,zp)\) is \(dx^2+dz^2+z^2g_{S^2}=z^2(g_{\mathbb H^2}+g_{S^2})\). The lift of each orientation-preserving hyperbolic isometry acting on the first factor extends to a Möbius map. For example, real translations and positive dilations lift to the corresponding Euclidean maps, and \(w\mapsto-1/w\) lifts to \((x,u)\mapsto(-x,u)/(x^2+|u|^2)\). These generate the hyperbolic isometry group in the chosen orientation. The extension is injective because the chart identifies the whole product with the complement of a line in \(\mathbb R^4\).

The double of a compact hyperbolic pair of pants across its geodesic boundaries is a smooth closed hyperbolic surface. The fold gives a retraction onto either copy of the pants. Its injective pants subgroup acts faithfully by deck transformations of \(\mathbb H^2\). Restricting the resulting product conformal atlas to the retained product gives nonidentity conformal holonomy for any loop whose pants projection is nontrivial. The spherical projection gives no extra factor since the sphere is simply connected.

In each \(\Phi_i\) tube use the fixed fiber point \(p_0=(0,0,1)\) in the framed \((u,v,w)\) coordinates. The outer longitude at this point projects exactly to \(c_i\) in the pants. The rows \((0,-1)\) and \((-1,0)\) of the relator exponent matrix already prove nontriviality in the original free group. Thus the outer holonomy of each longitude is nonidentity. The matching inner longitude lies in the exact cap collar and is null-homotopic in the locally conformally flat cap \(D^2\times S^2\). Its conformal holonomy is the identity. These identifications also hold if either end is multiplied by a positive smooth conformal factor.

If a smooth band had \(E_++E_-=0\), its Weyl tensor would vanish everywhere by nonnegativity and continuity. Along with the two exact end neighborhoods it would be a locally conformally flat thickening of \(S^1\times S^2\). The marked end longitudes are freely homotopic in this thickening, so its developing holonomies must be conjugate. The two calculated values contradict that requirement separately in each band. This proves \(E_++E_->0\) on each individual band under the frozen exact end data. A uniform strictly positive numerical lower bound and positivity in a specified chirality on a band still require estimates. The bound \(e<8\pi^2\) and the closed Yamabe sign remain to be tested for the changed exterior.

## Twin, framing and relative signs

Let \(Y=\Sigma_2(S^3,P(-2,3,7))\cong-\Sigma(2,3,7)\), with branch deck involution \(\iota_K\), and let \(J_m=\tau^m\rho P(-2,3,7)\). Equation (2) of Kuhrman identifies \(X_m=\Sigma_2(S^4,J_m)\) as a mapping torus of \(Y^\circ=Y\setminus\operatorname{int}B\) filled by \(S^2\times D^2\). The twin is the core \(S^2\times\{0\}\) of this upstairs cap. The disk factor gives its ordered normal frame. For \(m=18\) the branch knot \(J_{18}\) is smoothly unknotted and \(X_{18}\) is standard. For \(m=0\) the source gives the homotopy sphere \(X_0\). The unknotting input is checked here through the two cited accounts of Teragaito. [Kuhrman v1, §§2.2–2.3, Theorems 1 and 4 and Corollary 2.9](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03798v1#S2) · [Hughes–Kim–Miller v2, proof of Corollary 1.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2402.11706v2#S1) · [Miyazawa v1, proof of Theorem 4.32](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02041v1#S4.SS2)

For even \(m\) put \(f_m=\tilde\tau^m\tilde\rho\) and use the convention \((y,1)\sim(f_m y,0)\). Kuhrman's isotopy is
\[
\phi_t^m(s,w)=(s+2\pi b(|w|)t,e^{i\pi m b(|w|)t}w)
\]
in the lifted longitude and complex normal chart, with \(b=1\) near the fixed knot and \(b=0\) outside the tube. The identity \(\phi_{t+1}^m=\phi_t^m f_m\) verifies the map \(L_m([y,t]_m)=(\phi_t^m(y),e^{2\pi it})\) across the mapping-torus cut. Its inverse uses \((\phi_t^m)^{-1}\). For the small ball at the chosen point in the \(b=1\) region, each translation of the round coordinate ball is independent of \(m\) as a set. It follows that both \(L_m\) identify the retained exterior with \(E=(Y\times S^1)\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu(\gamma)\), where \(\gamma(t)=((2\pi t,0),e^{2\pi it})\).

Write \(\kappa_m(u,e^{2\pi it})=[\beta(u),t]_m\) for the native cap-to-exterior map and \(a_m=L_m\kappa_m\) for the same map in common exterior coordinates. If \(R_\alpha(x_0,z)=(x_0,e^{i\alpha}z)\) and \(T(u,e^{i\theta})=(R_\theta u,e^{i\theta})\), the rotation along the boundary is \(\pi mt=(m/2)\theta\). Thus \(a_{18}=a_0 T^9\), and the target cap map measured against the standard marking is \(a_0=a_{18}T^{-9}\), with inverse \(T^9 a_{18}^{-1}\). The exterior diffeomorphism \(L_0^{-1}L_{18}\) has native boundary map \(\kappa_0 T^9\kappa_{18}^{-1}\), with inverse \(\kappa_{18}T^{-9}\kappa_0^{-1}\). The cap has boundary orientation \(\operatorname{or}(S^2)\wedge d\theta\) when \(S^2\) is oriented as \(\partial B\); the exterior outward normal points into \(B\) and gives the opposite boundary orientation. The rotations preserve the sphere and angle orientations. Away from the rotation poles \(T^{-9}\) pulls back the old azimuth form to \(d\psi-9\,d\theta\). The odd exponent represents the Gluck mapping class, and the particular exponent fixes the literal gluing.

By transversality, removing the circle \(\gamma\) in dimension four preserves the ambient fundamental group. Retraction of its open complement onto \(E\) gives \(\pi_1(E)=\pi_1(Y)\times\mathbb Z\). The group \(\pi_1(Y)\) has the nonabelian hyperbolic triangle group \(\Delta(2,3,7)\) as a quotient. The smooth unknotted two-sphere exterior \(D^3\times S^1\) has cyclic group. This proves that the standard-side twin itself is knotted. [Kuhrman v1, §4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03798v1#S4.SS1)

## Closed double page and the seam bound

In either native mapping torus, join the \(\theta=0,\pi\) pages by the real diameter in \(S^2\times D^2\). At each endpoint the intrinsic collar preserves \(\theta\), and in the exterior common coordinates \(T^{m/2}\) merely rotates the boundary sphere at that angle. The rotations extend over the ball. For a regular-level description, use \(\operatorname{Im}z\) in the cap and \(v\sin\theta\) in the exterior, where \(v>0\) equals the continued radial coordinate in the common collar and extends smoothly away from it. The union is smooth and two-sided because zero is a regular value of this glued function. The two induced page orientations are opposite, giving \(L\cong Y\#(-Y)\).

Milnor's hyperbolic Brieskorn case applies since \(1/2+1/3+1/7=41/42<1\). The finite circle-bundle cover over a closed hyperbolic surface has an injective central fiber \(\mathbb Z\), by the homotopy sequence and orientability. With a lift of any infinite-order element in the surface group this gives \(\mathbb Z^2\) in \(\pi_1(Y)\). The free product \(\pi_1(L)=\pi_1(Y)*\pi_1(-Y)\) contains it. A closed oriented integral homology sphere with positive scalar curvature is \(S^3\) or a connected sum of oriented Poincaré spheres. Its group is a free product of finite groups and thus virtually free, so it cannot contain \(\mathbb Z^2\). In particular \(Y\) and \(L\) have no PSC representative. [Milnor, *On the 3-dimensional Brieskorn manifolds*, Theorem 2.7 and Corollary 6.6](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnbries.pdf) · [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee v1, Corollary 2.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1#page=5)

On either closed oriented homotopy sphere, \(Y(X_m,[g])>0\) and \(e<8\pi^2\) imply \(4\int\sigma_2(A)=16\pi^2-2e>0\) for \(A=(\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/6)/2\). The sign agrees with Chang–Gursky–Yang's convention \(2A\). Theorem A and Corollary B give a conformal representative with \(R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)>0\) for every unit normal. The Gauss formula on \(L\) gives \(R_h+|B^\circ|_h^2=R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+2H^2/3\) in that representative. For \(\hat g=b^2g\) one has \(\hat h=v^4h\), \(v=(b|_L)^{1/2}\), and \(|\hat B^\circ|_{\hat h}^2=v^{-4}|B^\circ|_h^2\). Thus \(P_{h,B}=-8\Delta_h+R_h+|B^\circ|_h^2\) transforms by \(P_{\hat h,\hat B}u=v^{-5}P_{h,B}(vu)\), preserving the positive first-eigenvalue sign. [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A and Corollary B](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583#page=4) · [Seam computation](SEAM_REPLAY.md)

In a cap conformal to the exact hyperbolic disk times the unit sphere, with the stated polar angles, the prescribed cap part of \(L\) is umbilic because its diameter is geodesic in the product representative. If both full exterior pages are also umbilic in the same smooth metric, then \(L\) is umbilic and the seam test gives positive Yamabe sign on \(L\), a contradiction. This excludes that joint ansatz at any model radius, with no reflection hypothesis. It applies on \(X_{18}\) and applies conditionally on \(X_0\) if its own cap and native pages satisfy the same geometric hypotheses. Using the induced three-dimensional Yamabe minimizer of \(L\) as a positive-form test and applying Hölder gives
\[
Y(L,[h])+
\left(\int_{L\cap E}|B^\circ|_h^3\,dA_h\right)^{2/3}>0.
\]
Since \(Y(L,[h])\le 0\) for every \(h\), exterior bending is required under the exact cap model. This is a condition on the metric of the full page double, with no uniform positive numerical bound established.

The separate round-class obstruction uses only the knotted core. The exact model makes that core totally geodesic, hence umbilic under a conformal change. A connected totally umbilic surface in the round sphere is a round two-sphere if closed and embedded. Indeed its Euclidean second fundamental form in \(\mathbb R^5\) is also pure trace, with a nonzero normal vector coefficient \(\eta\) due to the radial contribution. Codazzi makes \(\eta\) parallel in the normal bundle. The tangent planes together with \(\eta\) then span a fixed three-plane of directions, and \(x+\eta/|\eta|^2\) is constant. The closed embedded surface fills the resulting Euclidean sphere in an affine three-plane, which is round and unknotted in \(S^4\). This contradicts the twin's group calculation.

## Deck distinction

In common exterior coordinates the deck action is \(\iota_K\times\mathrm{id}\) because the isotopy commutes with \((s,w)\mapsto(s,-w)\). On the cap the action is \((u,z)\mapsto(R_\pi u,z)\) and commutes with \(T^{-9}\) on the boundary. Its fixed set on the cap is the two polar disks. The deck involution is orientation preserving, distinct from any reflection across the double page. It preserves each page setwise.

For the target, Miyazawa's Theorem 4.32 with roll index \(1\) gives \(|\deg(J_0)|=3\); the same theorem gives \(|\deg(J_{18})|=1\). Kuhrman's Theorem 3 and §4.2 use the invariant-PSC obstruction. In this homology-sphere case its decisive input is that invariant PSC forces degree of absolute value \(1\): the real Seiberg–Witten count in Miyazawa's Proposition 3.13 reduces by PSC to the single transverse reducible as in Proposition 3.14, using \(b_1=b_2^+=0\). The value \(3\) rules out invariant PSC for the target \(\delta_0\). The degree-\(1\) standard side is outside this obstruction and in fact has an invariant PSC metric by smooth unknotting of \(J_{18}\) and the standard rotation model. [Kuhrman v1, Theorem 3 and §4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.03798v1#S4.SS2) · [Miyazawa v1, Propositions 3.13–3.14](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02041v1#S3.SS3) · [Miyazawa v1, Theorems 4.32 and 4.46](https://arxiv.org/html/2312.02041v1#S4)

If \(\delta_0\) preserves a conformal class, averaging a representative with its pullback yields an invariant metric in the class. Its positive normalized first eigenfunction for \(-6\Delta+R\) is invariant by simplicity. A positive first eigenvalue would then give an invariant PSC representative, so every preserved target class has nonpositive Yamabe sign. A target assembly that breaks deck symmetry still needs a closed sign and whole-domain energy estimate.
