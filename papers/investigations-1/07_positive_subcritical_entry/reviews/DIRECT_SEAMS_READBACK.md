## Seam geometry and the small-cuff obstruction

In the fixed product marking the two enlarged tubes have latitude displacements at most \(5/64\) about \(\pi/3\) and \(2\pi/3\). Both miss an open neighborhood of the equator. The end coordinates \(z=vp\in\mathbb R^3\), with \(v=\arcsin(\operatorname{sech}r)\), extend the seam as \(z_3=0\) through each of the three added circles. The core charts and the product equator give a consistent coorientation. Sphere reflection is an isometry on the retained neighborhood, which gives zero second fundamental form there, including at \(z=0\). The seam is closed and has no boundary at those circles. The conclusion applies if the completion retains this ambient conformal germ, or more generally retains the induced class and umbilicity. Their Corollary B gives \(-\operatorname{Ric}+Rg/2>0\) in a conformal representative. The seam stays umbilic there and the Gauss equation gives \(R_{\hat h}=R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+2H^2/3>0\), for trace mean curvature \(H\). In dimension three this implies positivity of the closed form for \(-8\Delta_h+R_h\) by conformal covariance, with \(\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla\). ## Reflection group and the compact quotient

For the normals \(n_i=(\sinh b,\cosh b\,e_i)\) at angles \(2\pi i/3\), the Lorentz inner product satisfies \(-\langle n_i,n_j\rangle=\tfrac32\cosh^2 b-1=D\) for \(i\ne j\). The distance between mirrors is \(d=\operatorname{arcosh}D\). In Klein coordinates the inward cutoff for their perpendicular is \(x\cdot(e_i+e_j)\le 1/(2a)\), where \(a=\tanh b\in(1/2,1)\). The squared radius of its intersection with either mirror is \(\tfrac43(a^2+1/(4a^2)-1/2)<1\), equivalent to \((4a^2-1)(a^2-1)<0\). The three closed outer mirror caps are disjoint, as are the three closed outer cutoff caps. On a given mirror the endpoint intersections are inside the disk and give a finite side between neighboring perpendiculars. This yields the right-angled hexagon. Its double along the mirror sides has each cuff of length \(2d\). Reflection in the incident mirrors acts on the adjoining Fermi strip as \(t\mapsto-t\) and \(t\mapsto2d-t\), giving the periodic coordinate of length \(2d\) on the quotient funnel.

The first-letter cap property gives the free product of three reflections and disjoint chamber interiors. Successive caps for a reduced word are strictly nested and successive walls are distance \(d\) apart. An infinite descent through walls is impossible at a fixed finite distance from \(o\), which verifies coverage. For word length \(k\ge1\) the orbit distance is at least \((k-1)d\). The count \(3\cdot2^{k-1}\) gives \(\delta(\Gamma)\le\log2/d\) for the even subgroup. The upper orbit distance \(2bk\), with that same count for each even \(k\), gives \(\delta(\Gamma)>0\). Cyclic reduction proves the even subgroup torsion-free. Its compact geodesic core lifts to the convex region bounded by the inward cuff half-planes and has compact quotient. Its endpoints at infinity form the limit set, since a ray in the lifted core stays a bounded distance from an orbit. The ends have complementary ideal intervals. This establishes convex cocompactness, and the distinct cuff endpoint pairs give non-elementarity. These properties and orbital distances persist when the group fixes the two normal coordinates of the totally geodesic plane in \(\mathbb H^4\). At \(\cosh b=2\), \(L=2\operatorname{arcosh}5=4.584863339\ldots\) and the bound is \(\delta\le0.302363289\ldots\). More generally \(L>4\log 2\) gives \(\delta<1/2\). Outside the ideal circle in \(S^3\), the future null section \((X,\omega)\), \(X\in\mathbb H^2\), \(\omega\in S^1\), pulls back the conformal metric to \(q+d\theta^2\). On a lifted funnel the rescaled section \((\cosh t,\sinh t,\cos v,\sin v\,\omega)\) extends at \(v=0\) across the full ideal interval with coordinate \(t\). Its metric \(dt^2+dv^2+\sin^2v\,d\theta^2\) is smooth in \(v\omega\) and is the seam end metric up to a positive constant. The period \(2d\) adds the required circle with the marked disk coordinates. Thus the conformal identification with \((S^3\setminus\Lambda)/\Gamma\) holds on the compactified seam, including its three core circles. ## Regular hexagon contraction

At \(\cosh b=\sqrt2\) the perpendicular length is \(d=\operatorname{arcosh}2\). The two feet \((n_j+2n_i)/\sqrt3\) and \((n_k+2n_i)/\sqrt3\) have inner product \(-2\), so the sides along the mirrors also have length \(d\). This is the fixed regular hexagon. The elementary word-length bound \(\log2/d\) exceeds \(1/2\) here. The improved estimate follows from the actual first two letters. In axes aligned with the first mirror normal, an orbit point before that first reflection lies in a distinct cap, and its axial Klein coordinate \(x\) is at most \(\cos75^\circ<13/50\). Reflection gives
\[
 (X,Y)=\frac{(2\sqrt2-3x,y)}{3-2\sqrt2 x}.
\]
The denominator exceeds \(4-\sqrt3>9/4\) for points in the open disk, and \(|y|<1\). The axial fraction decreases with \(x\) at \(q=\sqrt2\) and increases with \(q\) when \(\sqrt2\) is replaced by \(q\in[24/17,\sqrt2]\). The respective derivative numerators are \(-9+4q^2\) and \(6(1-x^2)\). Evaluating the resulting lower bound at \((x,q)=(13/50,24/17)\) yields \(X>193/214>9/10\), with \(|Y|<4/9\).

For \(T(w)=\cosh d(o,wo)\), the two allowed left extensions have ratios \(A\pm B\), with \(A=3+\sqrt2 X>213/50\), \(B=\sqrt6|Y|<10/9\), and both ratios below \(6\). Their inverse square roots sum to less than \((1417/450)^{-1/2}+(2417/450)^{-1/2}<249/250\). The first inequality uses the fact that the sum decreases with \(A\) and increases with \(B\ge0\). The second is certified by \(1417\cdot141^2=28171377>450\cdot250^2\) and \(2417\cdot54^2=7047972>450\cdot125^2\). Replacing exponent \(1/2\) by \(499/1000\) increases each weight by at most \(6^{1/1000}\). The binomial lower bound \((250/249)^{1000}\ge1+1000/249+499500/249^2>6\) proves strict uniform contraction. Every length-\((k+1)\) word has a unique length-\(k\) suffix. Iterating from \(k=2\) gives convergence of \(\sum_w T(w)^{-499/1000}\), hence of the Poincaré series since \(e^{-s d(o,wo)}\le T(w)^{-s}\). This proves the exact bound \(0<\delta(\Gamma)\le499/1000<1/2\). [Regular-hexagon calculation](../notes/REGULAR_HEXAGON_SEAM.md)

The scalar curvature in equation (2.1) on p. 13 is \((n-1)(n-2-2\delta)\operatorname{tr}_{g_N} A\), where p. 14 identifies \(A\) as the covariance of \(d_x\log(\tfrac12|x-\xi|^2)\) for the normalized boundary measure. In boundary dimension \(n=3\) the coefficient is \(2(1-2\delta)\), with \(\delta\) the critical exponent used in the orbital series. In the round metric the covector identifies with \(-[\xi-(x\cdot\xi)x]/(1-x\cdot\xi)\). This is injective in \(\xi\ne x\) by stereographic projection. The positive density has the same support as the equivariant Patterson–Sullivan density. The support cannot be a singleton because the cuff translations have disjoint endpoint pairs. It follows that \(\operatorname{tr}_{g_N} A>0\) at every point. The smooth canonical metric descends and has positive scalar curvature on the entire compact quotient. Its positive closed form gives \(\lambda_1(-8\Delta_h+R_h)>0\) in the induced class of both the long-cuff and the regular-hexagon examples.
