## Relative tubular construction and matching

Write \(g\) for the smooth metric on the closed presurgery manifold \(N\). Its scalar curvature is nonnegative and positive on an open part of at least one compactified funnel. The quadratic form of \(L_g=-6\Delta_g+R_g\), with \(\Delta_g=\operatorname{div}\nabla\), is nonnegative. A zero first eigenvalue would force its eigenfunction to be constant on connected \(N\) and the potential term would then force that constant to vanish. The lowest eigenvalue \(\lambda>0\) is attained by a smooth \(v>0\). The metric \(g_+=v^2g\) has \(R_{g_+}=\lambda v^{-2}>0\) and is Weyl-flat. [Presurgery metric](../notes/FUNNEL_EXTERIOR.md)

On each marked attaching circle let \(B(\tau)\) be the projection of the three derivatives of \(\Phi_i(\tau,z)\) in \(z\) at \(z=0\) onto the \(g_+\)-normal space. It is an isomorphism since \(\Phi_i\) is a tubular embedding. The orthonormal frame \(B(B^*B)^{-1/2}\) retains the full normal framing. In particular it varies smoothly around the same longitude \(\tau\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), with the angular coordinate \(\theta=2\pi\tau\). Form the normal exponential chart \(\Psi_i\) in this frame. On a small common domain \(F_i=\Phi_i^{-1}\Psi_i\) fixes the zero section and has transverse derivative in the normal coordinates \((B^*B)^{-1/2}\), which is positive definite. Conjugating the chart germ by fiber dilations, \(D_\epsilon^{-1}F_iD_\epsilon\), gives at \(\epsilon=0\) the smooth limiting chart \((\tau,z)\mapsto(\tau,(B^*B)^{-1/2}z)\). A convex path of positive definite matrices joins its transverse derivative to the identity. On a uniformly smaller tube this gives an isotopy of embeddings. Isotopy extension, obtained by cutting off its velocity field inside the enlarged \(\Phi_i\) chart, gives an ambient diffeomorphism whose composition with \(\Phi_i\) agrees with \(\Psi_i\) on that smaller tube and equals \(\Phi_i\) near the outer band. The two diffeomorphisms have disjoint supports. Thus a normal exponential cut can be identified in the given frame, with the original longitude, sphere mark and outer collar.

On p. 429 Gromov–Lawson bend the normal exponential metric inside an arbitrarily small tubular neighborhood. The curve starts on the horizontal axis, so the induced metric outside its bend is the input metric. Normal rank three is sufficient because the leading positive coefficient \((q-1)(q-2)\) in the bending formula is \(2\) at \(q=3\). At the vertical end the normal radius is constant, giving a cylinder over the induced metric on the small normal sphere bundle. Lemma 2 deforms this slice through positive-scalar-curvature metrics to the round product in the chosen normal frame. The deformation of the base metric and the subsequent deformation to the product horizontal planes can both be done in the fixed longitude. The marked round base metric is a constant multiple of \(d\theta^2\). Lemma 3 lets one traverse such a smooth path sufficiently slowly, with the path constant near its ends for literal product collars. A further slow path through products \(L_s^2d\theta^2+r_s^2g_{S^2}\), with both radii positive, has the same property, since every slice has scalar curvature \(2/r_s^2\). This gives the prescribed product collar \(d\ell^2+L^2d\theta^2+g_{S^2}\) on each exterior side. [Gromov–Lawson, pp. 429–431](https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/827.pdf#page=8)

For disk arclength \(t\), take a smooth cutoff \(0\le\chi\le1\) as in the assembly and \(a(t)=\int_0^t\chi(u)\cosh u\,du\). Through \(t=1+1/16\) this is \(\sinh t\), so both the cap and the prescribed inner smooth germ are exact. The monotonicity of \(\chi\) gives \(a''=\chi'\cosh t+\chi\sinh t\le\chi(t)\sinh t\le a(t)\). Hence \(R=2(1-a''/a)\ge0\), with the scalar-flat germ extending to the smooth cap center and a strictly positive product cylinder wherever \(a=L>0\) becomes constant. At the join the interval factors match by opposite collar normals and the cross-section metrics agree with the literal \((\theta,p)\) marking. The annulus between these collars is diffeomorphic to the given material band. Using a smooth increasing interval parametrization with \(t=1+s\) near its inner end and the original tubular radius near its outer end preserves both germs and accommodates the required physical lengths. The framed tubular identification above makes this parametrization relative to both boundary maps, whose outward-normal orientations are already opposite under the closing. The result is a smooth metric \(\bar g\) on the same oriented \(M\) with \(R_{\bar g}\ge0\) and a positive region, so its conformal Laplacian has positive first eigenvalue by the same compactness argument.

In this identification \(\bar g=g_+\) on the full retained exterior and an open germ inside the outer ends of the bands. Choose a smooth positive multiplier \(F\) equal to \(v^{-1}\) on that region and equal to 1 on the two caps and their adapters through their cylindrical joins. The two prescriptions are separated by open parts of the bands. The metric \(F^2\bar g\) restores \(g\) on the entire retained exterior and leaves the inner cap germ exact. The conformal Laplacian transforms by \(L_{F^2\bar g}u=F^{-3}L_{\bar g}(Fu)\), so its quadratic-form sign stays positive. Weyl energy on every fixed subregion is conformally invariant in each chirality. The exterior and the two caps are Weyl-flat, and all other energy is contained in the two bands.

## Annular Gauss–Bonnet and the two chiral estimates

In the cap-side product annulus the normalized base is \(dt^2+a(t)^2d\theta^2\), with \(K=-a''/a\) and \(dA=a\,dt\,d\theta\). For a positive-facing cap of radius \(\rho>0\), the annulus has its inward normal at the inner boundary pointing toward increasing \(t\). Thus \(k_{\mathrm{in}}=-a'(\rho)/a(\rho)=-\coth\rho\), and its boundary length is \(2\pi\sinh\rho\). At the cylindrical cut \(k_{\mathrm{in}}=0\). Annular Gauss–Bonnet gives \(\int_A K\,dA=2\pi\cosh\rho\), also obtained by integrating \(Ka=-a''\) with \(a'(\rho)=\cosh\rho\) and zero final slope.

For the product with the unit sphere the two chiral Weyl blocks each have eigenvalues \((K+1)(1/3,-1/6,-1/6)\) in the real two-form operator convention. Integration over the fiber of area \(4\pi\) gives
\[
 E_\pm(A\times S^2)=\frac{2\pi}{3}\int_A (K+1)^2\,dA
 >\frac{8\pi}{3}\int_A K\,dA=\frac{16\pi^2}{3}\cosh\rho.
\]
The strict inequality follows from the remainder \((K-1)^2\), which is positive on the hyperbolic collar. For a smooth connection metric normalized to unit round fiber, let \(z=|*_qF_A|^2\), with \(q\) the annular base metric. The chiral diagonal estimate and the fiber moments give a lower bound in either sign of \(2\pi/3\) times the base integral of
\[
 4K+\left(K-1-\tfrac23 z\right)^2+\tfrac13 z+\tfrac4{45}z^2.
\]
The estimate is local and uses the unit round-fiber disintegration, so the annular topology affects only Gauss–Bonnet. With the exact cap germ and a cut of zero geodesic curvature in this normalized gauge, the same boundary integral gives the same strict bound. The strictness comes from the positive remainder on the exact inner germ, where \(K=-1\) and \(z=0\). Orientation reversal exchanges the chiral blocks, so the estimate holds for the given orientation regardless of the horizontal and vertical orientation convention. [Independent curvature replay](MARKED_BUDGET_READBACK.md#chiral-diagonal-and-signed-cap-estimate)

The two actual adapters are disjoint and each uses \(\rho=1\). The inequality \(\cosh(1)>1+1/2\) gives more than \(8\pi^2\) from either alone. Together they give \(E_\pm(M)>32\pi^2\cosh(1)/3\) in the same positive class, even before adding the nonnegative remainder of the bands. With two arbitrary positive radii the estimate on their sum remains greater than \(32\pi^2/3\). The connection extension requires the normalized ansatz throughout each counted annulus and its own normalized boundary curvature. These hypotheses leave the Weyl cost and Yamabe sign of a direct matched transition without such a cut undecided.
