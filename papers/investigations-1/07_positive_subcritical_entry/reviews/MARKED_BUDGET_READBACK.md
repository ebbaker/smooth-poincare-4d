## Chiral diagonal and signed cap estimate

Normalize the cap to unit round fibers by dividing by the positive conformal factor and squared base-dependent fiber radius. The real Hodge star on two-forms and each density \(|W^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2dV\) are conformally invariant in dimension four. Write \(q\) for the resulting disk metric, \(K\) for its Gaussian curvature, and \(b=*_q F_A\) in the cross-product identification of \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\). Set \(C=b\times p\), \(h=b\cdot p\), and \(c=|C|^2\). Choose horizontal orthonormal lifts \(e_1,e_2\) and vertical orthonormal \(e_3,e_4\) with \(e_4=p\times e_3\) and use orientation \(e_{1234}\). Use \(\mathcal R_{ij,kl}=\langle R(e_i,e_j)e_l,e_k\rangle\) with \(R(X,Y)=[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}\).

The bracket of horizontal lifts has vertical part \(-C\) in the \(1,2\) pair. The horizontal lift preserves the round fiber metric, so Koszul makes each fiber totally geodesic. The vertical part of \(\nabla_{e_1}e_2\) is \(-C/2\), and for a vertical field \(U\), \(\nabla_Ue_1=\langle C,U\rangle e_2/2\) and \(\nabla_Ue_2=-\langle C,U\rangle e_1/2\). The \(-\nabla_{e_2}\nabla_{e_1}e_2\) and bracket terms contribute \(-c/4\) and \(-c/2\) to the horizontal sectional curvature. The mixed sectional curvature is \(|\nabla_U e_i|^2\) for unit vertical \(U\). It follows that
\[
 \mathcal R_{12,12}=K-3c/4,\quad \mathcal R_{34,34}=1,\quad
 \mathcal R_{i\alpha,i\alpha}=\langle C,e_\alpha\rangle^2/4,\quad
 R_g=2(K+1)-c/2.
\]
On the unit fiber, \(\nabla^S_U C=h\,p\times U\), so \(d_S C^\flat=2h\,d\omega\). The normal-plane curvature of the totally geodesic fiber is \(d_S C^\flat\,J/2\), where \(Je_1=e_2\). This gives \(\mathcal R_{12,34}=-h\). Also \(\langle R(e_1,U)V,e_2\rangle=-\langle\nabla^S_U C,V\rangle/2\), from differentiating the horizontal coefficient of \(\nabla_{e_1}V\) and using the fiber connection in \(\nabla_{e_1}\nabla_U V\). In particular \(\mathcal R_{13,24}=-h/2\) and \(\mathcal R_{14,23}=h/2\), with \(\mathcal R_{12,34}-\mathcal R_{13,24}+\mathcal R_{14,23}=0\) as required by Bianchi.

Compressing to the chiral blocks subtracts \(R_g/12\) from each curvature diagonal to obtain the Weyl diagonal. For the first basis vector \((e^{12}\pm e^{34})/\sqrt2\) this gives
\[
 d_\pm=(K+1-c)/3\mp h .
\]
The two other chiral diagonals are \(-d_\pm/2\), and any off-diagonal entries add a nonnegative contribution to \(\|W^\pm\|_{\rm HS}^2\). The sign assignment here agrees with the [frame derivation](../notes/CONNECTION_CURVATURE.md). The [budget derivation](../notes/CONNECTION_BUDGET.md) uses the opposite assignment in its stipulated diagonal. Align those labels when carrying the pointwise formula forward. Both labels give the same fiber-averaged bound.

Write \(z=|b|^2\). Normalized averages of \((b\cdot p)/|b|\) have second and fourth moments \(1/3\) and \(1/5\), with vanishing odd moments. Hence the average of \(c\) is \(2z/3\), the average of \(c^2\) is \(8z^2/15\), and the average of \(9h^2\) is \(3z\). At \(b=0\) these expressions hold directly. Orthogonality of the connection splitting gives the volume density \(dA_q\,d\omega\), as is also seen from the triangular change from a product frame to horizontal lifts and vertical vectors. Thus
\[
\begin{aligned}
 E_\pm(\mathrm{cap})&\ge\frac{2\pi}{3}\int_D P(K,z)\,dA_q,\\
 P(K,z)&=(K+1)^2-\frac43(K+1)z+\frac8{15}z^2+3z\\
 &=4K+(K-1-2z/3)^2+z/3+4z^2/45 .
\end{aligned}
\]
Here \(2\pi/3=4\pi(3/2)/9\). Equality of the polynomial bound \(P\ge4K\) requires \(K=1,z=0\). On a flat-connection region the product eigenvalues are \((K+1)(1/3,-1/6,-1/6)\), so the fiber integral is exactly \((2\pi/3)(K+1)^2\).

For the full normalized disk let \(k_q=\langle\nabla_T T,\nu_{\rm in}\rangle\), positive on the Euclidean circle with inward normal. For an inward coordinate \(u\) and metric \(du^2+\sinh^2(\rho+u)d\theta^2\), one has \(k_q=-\coth\rho\) and \(L_q=2\pi\sinh\rho\). The first trial's common coordinate has \(u=-s\) at its first cap. Gauss–Bonnet and the polynomial bound give
\[
 C_D:=\int_D K\,dA_q=2\pi(1+\cosh\rho),\qquad
 E_\pm(\mathrm{cap})\ge \frac{8\pi}{3}C_D
 =\frac{16\pi^2}{3}(1+\cosh\rho)>\frac{32\pi^2}{3}.
\]
For an exact hyperbolic product match the remainder integral is positive, since \(K=-1\) at the boundary and persists close enough to that value on a positive-area neighborhood. The threshold using only the signed integral is \(\int_{\partial D}k_q\,d\ell_q\le-\pi\), which implies \(E_\pm\ge8\pi^2\) and defeats strict entry. Even with arbitrary changes within the ansatz, Gauss–Bonnet still counts the whole disk. If the ansatz stops before the seam, the resulting bound instead uses the signed curvature of its actual disk boundary. The opposite-facing second cap has \(k_q=+\coth\rho\), constant \(K=-1\) and flat connection, giving zero Weyl energy in both signs.

For the area refinement, minimizing the quadratic in \(z\ge0\) gives \(z_{\min}=\tfrac54(K-\tfrac54)_+\) and \(\varphi(K)=(K+1)^2-\tfrac56(K-\tfrac54)_+^2\). The derivative is continuous and the second derivatives on the two sides are \(2\) and \(1/3\), so Jensen gives \(E_\pm\ge(2\pi/3)A\varphi(C_D/A)\) for normalized area \(A\). With \(z=0\) the stronger product expression is \(E_\pm\ge(2\pi/3)(A+C_D)^2/A\). Either algebraic bound optimizes to \((8\pi/3)C_D\) when \(A=C_D\). The infimum among smooth metrics realizing an exact match remains open in this calculation. If a positive-area opposite-curvature product band \(A_0\) lies inside the disk, it contributes zero Weyl energy and normalized curvature integral \(-A_0\). Jensen on its complement then gives \((2\pi/3)(A-A_0)\varphi((C_D+A_0)/(A-A_0))\). In the displayed first trial the inner product collar \(3/4\le t'\le1\) has \(A_0=2\pi(\cosh(5/4)-\cosh 1)\). This improves the area-independent lower bound on that cap to \(16\pi^2(1+\cosh(5/4))/3\), the radius bound for the disk ending at \(t'=3/4\).

## Retained area and closed sign scope

The rounding misses \(F_*\times S_*\) since \(F_*\) stays at least \(1/12\) from the planar boundary. The tube and interpolation miss it because \(5/64<1/12\) in latitude. Therefore both trial exteriors have the exact product of the Euclidean planar metric and the unit sphere metric on this region. The two excluded base disks are disjoint and lie inside the outer disk. The two removed latitude bands each have width \(1/6\) and their union has area at most \(2\pi/3\), by \(d\omega=\sin\phi\,d\phi\,d\theta\). More explicitly,
\[
 |F_*|=\pi\left((39/10)^2-2/9\right)>72\pi/5,
 \qquad |S_*|=4\pi\bigl(1-\sqrt3\sin(1/12)\bigr)>10\pi/3.
\]
The product chiral density is \(1/6\) in each sign, so \(E_\pm(F_*\times S_*)=|F_*||S_*|/6>8\pi^2\). This bound survives any replacement that preserves the metric on this region, as well as any conformal change there. For the original inverse-facing trial the cap and retained exterior region add, yielding \(E_\pm(M)>\frac{16\pi^2}{3}(1+\cosh 1)+8\pi^2\). For the opposite-facing trial both caps have zero energy and the retained exterior gives \(E_\pm(M)>8\pi^2\). The other smooth pieces have nonnegative chiral densities, and the seams have zero four-volume. These local bounds concern each sign independently, with equality of the *closed* chiral integrals following from signature zero.

For either assembly, \(4\int_M\sigma_2=16\pi^2-(E_++E_-)<0\), where \(A=(\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/6)/2\) is the Schouten tensor in \(\sigma_2\). The pinched representative is therefore excluded throughout either trial's conformal class. This energy obstruction uses no hypothesis on \(Y(M,[g])\). The product-circle seams have \(R_h=2\) and bending eigenvalues \((\pm\coth\rho,0,0)\), giving first modified seam eigenvalue \(2+2\coth^2\rho/3\). That sign concerns the seam. The existence of some PSC metric on the marked type follows separately from Gromov–Lawson's Theorem A, applied to the point and framed-circle surgeries of codimensions four and three. These facts leave the closed conformal-Laplacian sign of the prescribed metrics open. The cap result requires the normalized connection ansatz and signed boundary data on the counted disk.
