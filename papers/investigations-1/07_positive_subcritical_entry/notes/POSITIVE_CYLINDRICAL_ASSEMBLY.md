## The signed cost of the adapter

The annulus from the radius-\(\rho\) cap boundary to the terminal product cylinder has unit round fibers, angular period \(2\pi\), and Gaussian curvature \(K=-a''/a\) on its base. The initial slope is \(\cosh\rho\) and the final slope is zero. Hence \(\int K\,dA=2\pi\cosh\rho\). The same signed product formula as on the cap gives in either chirality
\[
 E_\pm(\mathrm{adapter})=\frac{2\pi}{3}\int (K+1)^2 dA
 \ \ge\ \frac{8\pi}{3}\int K\,dA
 =\frac{16\pi^2}{3}\cosh\rho .
\]
For the actual radius one, \(\cosh1>3/2\), so either adapter costs more than \(8\pi^2\) per sign on its own. The two disjoint adapters cost at least \((32\pi^2/3)\cosh1\) per sign in the very same positive closed class. All exterior, cap, Gromov–Lawson transition and conformal-factor regions are accounted for by conformal invariance, the exact zero costs off the bands and the nonnegative remainder within them. Even varying the two positive cap radii, their separate lower bounds sum to more than \(32\pi^2/3\). The connection calculation gives the same lower bound on any annular adapter that stays in the normalized round-fiber connection class all the way from the positive-facing cap to a round cylindrical cut with zero base boundary curvature: replace the annulus product integrand by its proved fiberwise estimate and apply annular Gauss–Bonnet. This excludes that way of obtaining a positive closing for strict entry. A direct matched transition, with no such intermediate cylindrical cut, retains the two-band energy equation and still needs its own sign and cost.
