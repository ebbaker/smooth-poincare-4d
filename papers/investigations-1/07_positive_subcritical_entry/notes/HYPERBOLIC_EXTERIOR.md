# A Weyl-flat pants exterior forces curvature in the two transition bands

B02 revision after the two complete signed bounds in the [marked assembly](MARKED_CONNECTION_ASSEMBLY.md). This is a zero-cost test on specified bands of that same marked exterior. It imposes no curvature assumption on the other faces.

Replace the flat metric on the pants by a curvature-minus-one metric with geodesic boundary (for example double a compact regular right-angled hyperbolic hexagon across alternate sides and identify the resulting pants with the ordered planar pants). On the part of the face outside the two transition bands retain its product with the unit round sphere. This cancels both Weyl densities on the formerly expensive product region. Keep the two positive-facing hyperbolic disk caps and their markings. The exterior on other faces may be smoothly extended and counted separately. The two boundary annuli to compare are the exact outer product at the *exterior* end of each transition and the exact cap collar at its inner end.

The conformal chart for the product of the upper half-plane with the unit sphere is
\[
 (x,z,p)\longmapsto (x,zp)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^3,qquad z>0 .
\]
Its Euclidean metric pulls back to \(z^2(g_{\mathbb H^2}+g_{S^2})\). The hyperbolic pants holonomy is faithful. For instance, doubling across its geodesic boundary gives a closed hyperbolic surface, the folding retraction injects the pants group into its group, and the closed hyperbolic universal cover develops isometrically into the plane. Any hyperbolic deck isometry acting on the first factor extends in this chart to a Möbius transformation. It is the identity only if its action on the first factor is the identity. Equivalently this follows from uniqueness of a conformal chart transition extended to the conformal sphere in dimension four (Liouville's theorem). [Frances, §4, Fact 4.1 and (8)](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43352)

At the outer end take a longitude \(\theta\mapsto\Phi_i(\theta/2\pi,rp_0)\) for constant unit \(p_0\) and fixed framed radius just outside the transition. Its planar projection is the relator path translated by a small constant vector, hence freely homotopic to that relator in the pants. Its spherical projection contributes no monodromy. Both relators are nonempty freely reduced words in the free group before the handles are attached. Thus each outer longitude has nonidentity conformal holonomy. On the exact cap collar the same marked longitude bounds the disk times a point in a simply connected, locally conformally flat cap, so its conformal holonomy is the identity.

If one smooth transition had zero combined Weyl energy, both Weyl blocks would vanish everywhere on it. The smooth metric with its two exact end germs would give a locally conformally flat structure on a thickened \(S^1\times S^2\). Transporting its developing charts along its two homotopic longitudes must give conjugate holonomies. Identity cannot be conjugate to the outer holonomy. This excludes a completely Weyl-flat transition at *each* tube. It proves strict positivity of the combined cost of each such band, without a quantitative lower bound. A finite, nonzero pair of band costs could still fit the closed budget. The [funnel construction](FUNNEL_EXTERIOR.md) subsequently fixed the rest of the retained exterior with zero Weyl energy. The band magnitudes and closed sign remain to be tested together for any proposed direct transition. The conclusion also does not follow from the closed topology alone. It uses the actual longitude, faithful hyperbolic pants marking and exact disk germ.
