## The mirror group and its cuffs

Use the future unit hyperboloid \(\mathbb H^2\subset\mathbb R^{2,1}\), with Lorentz product \(\langle X,Z\rangle=-X_0Z_0+X_{\mathrm{sp}}\cdot Z_{\mathrm{sp}}\). Put \(o=(1,0,0)\), let \(e_i=(\cos(2\pi i/3),\sin(2\pi i/3))\) for \(i=0,1,2\), and set
\[
 n_i=(\sinh b,\cosh b\,e_i),\qquad a=\tanh b,\qquad \tfrac12<a<1.
\]
The mirror \(P_i\) is \(\langle X,n_i\rangle=0\). Write \(H_i=\{\langle X,n_i\rangle\ge0\}\) for the half-plane away from \(o\), and \(s_iX=X-2\langle X,n_i\rangle n_i\) for the reflection. In the Klein disk the three half-planes are \(x\cdot e_i\ge a\). Their closures on the closed disk are disjoint, since \(|e_i+e_j|=1<2a\). In the Poincaré disk the mirror circles have Euclidean centers \(e_i/a\) and radii \(\sqrt{a^{-2}-1}\). For \(\cosh b=2\) these are \((2/\sqrt3)e_i\) and \(1/\sqrt3\), respectively. The pairwise distances between the centers are 2. In the Poincaré \(4\)-ball the same centers and radii give three orthogonal hyperspheres, with their centers in the first spatial two-plane. The Euclidean balls they cut off have disjoint closures there as well.

The reflection group \(G=\langle s_0,s_1,s_2\rangle\) is the free product of three groups of order two. Indeed each \(s_i\) sends the interior of the complement of \(H_i\) into \(\operatorname{int}H_i\). A nonempty reduced word sends \(o\) inside its first half-plane and cannot be the identity. The chamber \(P=\mathbb H^2\setminus\bigcup_i\operatorname{int}H_i\) tiles by reflection. To see coverage, any point not in a reflected copy would lie in successively nested reduced-word half-planes. The distance from \(o\) to their walls tends to infinity by the wall separation estimate below. Chamber interiors are disjoint by the same reflection property. The orientation-preserving subgroup \(\Gamma=\langle s_0s_1,s_0s_2\rangle\) consists of the even words. It is torsion-free, since any nonidentity even word has a nonempty even cyclically reduced conjugate. The chamber \(P\cup s_0P\) for \(\Gamma\) is bounded by the four disjoint sides of \(H_1,H_2,s_0H_1,s_0H_2\), with the side pairings \(s_0s_j\) for \(j=1,2\). Reflections extend to \(\mathbb H^4\) by fixing the two normal coordinates.

For \(i\ne j\), put \(D=-\langle n_i,n_j\rangle=\tfrac32\cosh^2 b-1>1\). The feet of the common perpendicular are \((n_j+D n_i)/\sqrt{D^2-1}\) and \((n_i+D n_j)/\sqrt{D^2-1}\), with Lorentz product \(-D\). Thus each mirror separation is \(d=\operatorname{arcosh}D\). In the Klein disk the common perpendicular line has equation \(x\cdot(e_i+e_j)=1/(2a)\), since its Lorentz normal is orthogonal to \(n_i,n_j\). The half-plane past this line lies in the gap opposite the third mirror. The three cutoff half-planes have disjoint closures in the disk, since their spatial unit normals again have pairwise sums of length 1 and \(1/a>1\). Each cutoff meets precisely the two adjoining mirror lines. The squared Klein radius of each vertex is \(\frac43(a^2+1/(4a^2)-1/2)<1\). Cutting \(P\) by the three common perpendiculars therefore leaves a compact right-angled hexagon. Doubling it along the three mirror sides gives the compact geodesic pants \(F_L\). Each of the remaining sides doubles to a cuff of length \(L=2d\). In the gap beyond a cuff side, Fermi coordinates on its common perpendicular have metric \(dr^2+\cosh^2r\,dt^2\) on \(r\ge0,\ 0\le t\le d\). The two adjacent mirrors are the lines \(t=0,d\). Reflection across them doubles this strip and identifies \(t\) modulo \(2d\). Thus \(\mathbb H^2/\Gamma\) is exactly \(F_L\) with all three funnels attached. At \(\cosh b=2\) one has \(D=5\). As \(a\) ranges from \(1/2\) to 1, \(L\) ranges over all positive lengths.

## The orbital exponent

For a reduced word \(w=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_k}\), write \(w_m=s_{i_1}\cdots s_{i_m}\) and \(w_0=1\). The half-planes \(w_{m-1}H_{i_m}\) are strictly nested, enclose \(wo\), and omit \(o\). The distance between successive walls equals \(d\), by applying \(w_{m-1}^{-1}\) and reflecting in \(P_{i_m}\). Therefore the geodesic from \(o\) to \(wo\) crosses the walls in order and \(d(o,wo)\ge(k-1)d\). There are \(3\cdot2^{k-1}\) reduced words of length \(k\). The Poincaré series of \(G\), and hence of \(\Gamma\), converges for \(s>\log2/d\). The orbit of \(o\) lies in the totally geodesic \(\mathbb H^2\subset\mathbb H^4\), so the same bound holds for the exponent of the extended Möbius action on \(S^3=\partial_\infty\mathbb H^4\). In particular
\[
 0<\delta(\Gamma)\le \frac{\log2}{d}=\frac{2\log2}{L}<\frac12
 \qquad (L>4\log2).
\]
For strict positivity of \(\delta\), each generator moves \(o\) by \(2b\), giving \(d(o,wo)\le2bk\), and the even words have the stated exponential count. The concrete upper bound is \(\log2/\operatorname{arcosh}5\approx0.3024\).

## Identification of the closed conformal class

Use the orientation-preserving cuff-labeled identification of \(F_L\) with the marked pants \(F\) to put this metric on \(F\). In the given product coordinates the equator \(S^1\subset S^2\) at latitude \(\phi=\pi/2\) misses both enlarged tubes: \(5/64<\pi/6\). It extends across the compactified ends by the equatorial disks, meeting their three core circles. Reflection in the equator preserves the metric near this hypersurface. In the smooth disk charts at the cores it is an ordinary reflection in a normal coordinate. The equator is closed and two-sided, with second fundamental form zero in the inherited representative \(h\). All changes on the two bands or caps are off this seam. The lift of the compact pants to \(\mathbb H^2\) is convex, since it is the intersection of the inward closed half-planes of the lifted cuffs. Each lifted funnel is the outward half-plane of its cuff, with the cuff stabilizer acting by translations of length \(L\). The lift of the compact pants has a compact quotient and contains the convex hull of the limit set \(\Lambda\). Conversely its ideal boundary is contained in \(\Lambda\), since a ray in the lift stays a bounded distance from the orbit of a fixed point there. Thus \(\Gamma\) is convex cocompact in \(\mathbb H^2\) and also in \(\mathbb H^4\), where the convex hull of \(\Lambda\) lies in the totally geodesic plane. On the ideal circle \(S^1_\infty\) the complementary intervals of \(\Lambda\) are precisely the intervals of the lifted funnels, with three orbits. The three original cuff axes have disjoint ideal endpoint pairs by the disjoint closed cutoff half-planes. Each pair is fixed by the corresponding product of reflections, so \(\Gamma\) is non-elementary.

Identify \(S^3\) with future null rays in \(\mathbb R^{4,1}=\mathbb R^{2,1}\oplus\mathbb R^2\). The rays outside the ideal circle \(S^1_\infty=\partial_\infty\mathbb H^2\) have a unique representative \((X,\omega)\), where \(X\in\mathbb H^2\) and \(\omega\in S^1\subset\mathbb R^2\). Pulling back the Lorentz form by this section represents the round conformal class by \(q+d\vartheta^2\). The action on the second factor is trivial. Hence the noncompact equatorial product is the quotient of \(S^3\setminus S^1_\infty\) by \(\Gamma\).

For a lifted funnel, write \(X=(\cosh r\cosh t,\cosh r\sinh t,\sinh r)\) in an axial Lorentz frame. The coordinate \(t\) is cuff arclength, periodic with period \(L\) after taking the quotient. Multiply \((X,\omega)\) by \(\operatorname{sech}r\) to obtain the section
\[
 (\cosh t,\sinh t,\cos v,\sin v\,\omega),\qquad v=\arcsin(\operatorname{sech}r).
\]
It extends across \(v=0\) in the normal disk coordinate \(v\omega\) and represents the conformal class by \(dt^2+dv^2+\sin^2v\,d\vartheta^2\). At \(v=0\) it adds the whole ideal interval of the funnel, modulo \(t\mapsto t+L\). The compactified funnel metric restricted to the equator is a positive constant multiple of this representative for \(r\ge1\). This proves that the closed class \([h]\), including its core circles, is the standard quotient conformal class of \(\Omega/\Gamma\), where \(\Omega=S^3\setminus\Lambda\). In particular the scalar sign calculation must be made for this smooth compact quotient, with no boundary conditions imposed at the added circles.

## Strict sign on the quotient

The published primary account by Hiroyasu Izeki and Shin Nayatani, *Canonical metric on the domain of discontinuity of a Kleinian group*, Séminaire de Théorie Spectrale et Géométrie 16 (1997–1998), pp. 9–32, was checked on 26 November 2026 at Proposition 1.1 and §2, pp. 11–14, especially (2.1). For the torsion-free convex-cocompact Möbius group with \(\delta>0\), the Patterson–Sullivan density gives a smooth \(\Gamma\)-invariant metric \(g_N\) on \(\Omega\), conformal to the round metric. Equation (2.1) with boundary dimension \(n=3\) gives
\[
 R_{g_N}=2(1-2\delta)\operatorname{tr}_{g_N}A,
\]
where \(A\) is the covariance of the covectors \(d_x\log(\tfrac12|x-\xi|^2)\) under the normalized positive measure at \(x\). At fixed \(x\), the covectors as functions of \(\xi\ne x\) are injective by stereographic projection. The measure has support at more than one point: the positive Radon–Nikodym factors and equivariance in Proposition 1.1 make its support \(\Gamma\)-invariant, whereas the distinct cuff translations have no common fixed ideal point. Thus \(\operatorname{tr}_{g_N}A>0\) everywhere. The strict exponent bound gives \(R_{g_N}>0\) on the entire quotient. On the closed seam the compact smooth positive representative gives \(\lambda_1(-8\Delta_{g_N}+R_{g_N})>0\), with \(\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla\). Conformal covariance gives the same sign for \(-8\Delta_h+R_h\). Since \(B^\circ=0\) along the retained equator, this is exactly the required seam sign.
