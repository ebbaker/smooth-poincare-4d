In the stated smooth oriented two-point class, the filled outlet is the cut exterior \(X\) with its actual port tori joined by \(T^2\times I\). Both ends of this product are attached through the annuli on the drilled slab. If the outlet has free fundamental group, at least one of these port tori has a properly embedded compressing disk in \(X\). The proof holds on reducible \(X\) and for every integral moving slope. The port correspondence retains the framing twist before imposing the rank condition.

## The block and the actual complementary boundaries

Write \(E=Y_1\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu(Q\cup K)\) and \(T_K=\partial\nu K\). Choose the tubes and collars so that the supplied two-sided sphere \(S\) meets \(\nu K\) in two transverse meridian disks and misses \(\nu Q\). Set \(A=S\cap E\) and let \(f=\mu_K\) be the positively oriented meridian for an orientation of \(K\) in \(Y_1\). The boundary circles of the oriented annulus \(A\) have opposite signs measured against \(f\) on \(T_K\). Indeed, orienting the two removed disks by \(S\) gives their meridians the two intersection signs, and the boundary orientation on \(A\) reverses both. Consequently one can parameterize the \(S^1\) factor of \(A=S^1\times I\) so that it follows \(f\) at both ends. Smooth orientation-preserving reparameterizations on the two circles can be accommodated through the annulus.

Use a collar \(T_K\times[0,1]\) in \(E\) and a bicollar of the remaining part of \(A\), attached to the inner edge of the torus collar. Their union, with corners rounded, is \(B=\nu(T_K\cup A)\) relative to \(T_K\). In meridional product coordinates on the collar this construction is the product by \(S^1_f\) of a base annulus with one band attached to two intervals of its inner boundary. The two annuli of attachment are fiber-preserving by the chosen parameterization of \(A\). The band is oriented because \(B\) and its circle fibers are oriented. An oriented band on a single boundary circle splits that circle into two, while the outside circle stays fixed. The base is therefore a pair of pants \(P\), and \(B\cong S^1_f\times P\). This description comes from a neighborhood of the given \(A\) and \(T_K\).

Let \(S_J,S_V\) denote the two faces of the cut along the original \(S\), and orient both returns by \(K\). Opposite intersection signs mean that the return \(J\) has both endpoints on \(S_J\) and \(V\) has both endpoints on \(S_V\). Cutting the inlet along \(S\) and drilling \(Q,J,V\) gives a connected manifold \(X_0\) with the \(Q\) torus and the two port tori. Connectedness follows by perturbing paths off the three one-dimensional cores in the connected slab. On each face the two-punctured sphere is an annulus \(A_i\), and the lateral annulus \(L_i\) of the corresponding return's tube joins its two boundary circles. The port \(T_i=A_i\cup L_i\), for \(i=J,V\), is the corresponding rounded exposed boundary of \(E\setminus\operatorname{int}B\). To see the identification of the inclusion, first trim the two sides of the face bicollar and then trim the extra tube collar. The pieces left on the exposed boundary are exactly the displaced \(A_i\) and displaced \(L_i\), attached along their original meridians. These trims rescale collars of \(X_0\). The \(Q\) torus is outside \(B\), so filling it gives the complement identified with \(X\).

Completing the returns with boundary-parallel arcs in separate capping balls gives the same marked exterior up to collar length. In each ball, the exterior of the trivial arc is \(S^1\times D^2\) with its attaching annulus and the complementary annulus both running once along the \(S^1\) factor. For the product arc in \(D^2\times I\), this is
\((D^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2_\epsilon)\times I\), with the two annuli corresponding to complementary boundary arcs of the radial rectangle. It adds an annular collar to \(A_i\) after rounding. The \(L_i\) inclusion is carried along, and \(f\) becomes the positively oriented meridian \(\mu_i\) of the corresponding capped knot. This checks the actual inclusions into \(X\) also in the capped-link coordinates.

## Filling and orientation of the two port maps

Orient \(P\) so that its original boundary direction projects in the direction of \(K\). Choose a product section on \(B\), and let \(b_K,b_J,b_V\) be the lifts of its three oriented boundary circles. Then \(b_K+b_J+b_V=0\) in \(H_1(B;\mathbb Z)\). The section can be chosen with \(b_K=\lambda_K\), the preferred longitude in the exterior of \(K\) alone. Indeed \(K\) is nullhomologous in \(S^1\times S^2\) by the two opposite signs, so a preferred longitude exists. Changing the section by a map \(P\to S^1\) changes the winding numbers on its boundary circles by any three integers that sum to zero.

Write the actual integral moving slope as \(b_K+p f\), taking its sign to make the coefficient of \(b_K\) positive. Its distance from \(f\) is one. Glue the filling solid torus \(S^1_\phi\times D^2\) by the boundary chart
\((\phi,t)\mapsto(\phi+pt,t)\), with \(t\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\) along \(b_K\). Orient the base disk opposite to the \(P\) boundary direction under the identification, so the attachment reverses boundary orientation. This chart implements the prescribed meridian and extends the oriented circle fibration. The filled block \(B_p\) has base an annulus and is an oriented product \(T^2\times I\). In detail, one can change the section on \(P\) by a circle-valued function with degree \(p\) on its \(K\) boundary and compensating degree at the other boundaries. In that section the filling is fiberwise product, so the resulting product is explicit. The boundary maps to \(X\) are the original collar identifications \(g_i:\partial_i B_p\to T_i\subset X\) described above. Each reverses the induced orientation on the paired boundary components. If \(u:\partial_J B_p\to\partial_V B_p\) is transport through this product, the port map is \(h=g_V u g_J^{-1}\), with inverse \(g_Ju^{-1}g_V^{-1}\). Base circles on the two ends have opposite boundary directions and the meridian fiber has the same direction. From \(b_K=-pf\) in the filled block, the induced map is

\[
h_*(f_J)=f_V,\qquad h_*(b_J)=-b_V+p f_V.
\]

This has determinant \(-1\) in the two boundary bases. For the capped-link preferred longitudes \(\lambda_J,\lambda_V\), each oriented base circle of an exposed port runs backward on its original lateral interval, since the inner boundary of the starting base annulus has the orientation opposite to \(b_K\). Thus \(b_i=-\lambda_i+e_i\mu_i\) for integers \(e_i\). In the exterior of \(K\) alone the class of \(\lambda_K=b_K=-(b_J+b_V)\) vanishes. Ignore \(Q\) in the capped slab, and write \(c=\operatorname{lk}(J,V)\) with the return orientations. Then \(\lambda_J=c\mu_V\) and \(\lambda_V=c\mu_J\) in the homology of the two-component capped-link exterior. In the \(K\)-only inlet exterior both meridians map to \(f\), which has infinite order because the algebraic intersection of \(K\) with the generator of \(H_2(Y_1;\mathbb Z)\) vanishes. The boundary relation therefore gives \(e_J+e_V=2c\). The actual port correspondence in the preferred capped bases is

\[
h_*(\mu_J)=\mu_V,\qquad h_*(\lambda_J)=-\lambda_V+(2c-p)\mu_V.
\]

In the rank-raising case \(p=0\). Both attachment circles have zero class in \(H_1(Y_1;\mathbb Z)\), so the pair sequence and excision give \(H_1(E;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^3\), with an injected meridian for each circle and quotient \(H_1(Y_1;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\). Filling both tori adds the two slope relations. If the filled \(H_1\) has rank three, both relations already vanish in \(H_1(E;\mathbb Z)\). Project the moving relation to the exterior of \(K\) alone by restoring the \(Q\) tube. There it is \(p f=0\), so \(p=0\). This rank argument is independent of the product construction.

There is also a direct cut in the filled outlet. In the moving solid torus both circles of \(\partial A\) are fibers in the boundary chart above. Join their base points by a proper embedded chord \(d\subset D^2\). The annulus \(S^1_\phi\times d\) and \(A\) form a smooth torus after rounding. The two oriented boundary circles of each annulus run with opposite signs on the common fiber, so the annuli can be oriented oppositely at both seams. Cutting the filling disk along \(d\) gives two disks, each with one original boundary interval and one copy of \(d\). Each corresponding solid torus piece extends the collar of its actual lateral annulus \(L_i\). The cut exterior is \(X\) up to these collar extensions. This construction realizes the same two port inclusions and the same twist and also shows that the torus is nonseparating.

## Compression forced by a free outlet

Suppose both inclusion maps \(i_i:\pi_1(T_i)\to G=\pi_1(X)\) are injective. Choose paths for the basepoints from one point in \(X\), and an oriented traversal of the attached product for the stable letter \(t\). Van Kampen for the cylinder attached at both ends gives, with a choice of direction for \(t\),

\[
\pi_1(Y_{\rm out})=
\langle G,t\mid t^{-1}i_J(g)t=i_V(h_*g)\quad(g\in\pi_1(T_J))\rangle.
\]

The two images are subgroups of \(G\), and \(h_*\) and the inclusions define the required isomorphism between them. The HNN inclusion theorem injects \(G\), hence the two actual peripheral copies of \(\mathbb Z^2\), into this group. Every centralizer of a nontrivial element of a free group is cyclic. For example, that element acts by translation on its axis in the free Cayley tree, and its centralizer acts by translations on that axis with trivial kernel because the action on vertices is free. A free outlet therefore contradicts simultaneous peripheral injectivity. This use of HNN is the base-group injection of Higman, B. H. Neumann and H. Neumann, *Embedding Theorems for Groups*, Theorem I and the presentation immediately following its proof. Both passages were checked in the published text. [J. London Math. Soc. 24 (1949), 247–254, pp. 248–249](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1112/jlms/s1-24.4.247)

For a port with nontrivial kernel, the Loop Theorem gives a properly embedded disk with essential boundary in that same port. One can delete the other boundary component when applying the theorem, so the disk interior lies in the interior of \(X\), and take the disk smooth by rounding the piecewise linear embedding. This needs no irreducibility hypothesis. The checked formulation and proof are Hatcher, *Notes on Basic 3-Manifold Topology*, Theorem 3.1, including the removal-of-boundary argument in the proof of Corollary 3.2. The original Stallings publication is bibliographically identified. [Hatcher, §3.1, pp. 45–48](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/3M/3M.pdf#page=46) · [Stallings, *On the Loop Theorem*, Ann. of Math. 72 (1960), 12–19](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970146)

The disk existence uses the actual two-return sphere, the distance-one moving filling, and freeness of the actual outlet group. For a slope at distance greater than one the same construction yields a filled circle-fibered block with a multiple fiber, whereas the product argument uses distance one. The longitude identification of a compressing disk and the ensuing bridge and relative trace questions belong to the separately issued SUR-E02w.
