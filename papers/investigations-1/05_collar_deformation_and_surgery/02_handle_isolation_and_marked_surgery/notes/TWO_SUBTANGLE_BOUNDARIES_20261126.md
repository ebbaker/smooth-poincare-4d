Doubling gives a three-component zero-framed link with designated unknot `A`, simultaneous surgery \(Y_3=\#_3(S^1\times S^2)\), and both native cuts after \(A\cup B\) and \(A\cup C\) irreducible. ## Surface and bands

Use coordinates \(s,t\) modulo one on an oriented torus, with orientation \(ds\wedge dt\). Remove small round disks with these centers.

| Spot | \(s\) | \(t\) |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| \(\alpha_+\) | \(1/3\) | \(1/4\) |
| \(\alpha_-\) | \(1/3\) | \(0\) |
| \(\beta_+\) | \(2/3\) | \(3/4\) |
| \(\beta_-\) | \(2/3\) | \(0\) |

Mark the arcs \(m_x\) from \(\alpha_+\) to \(\alpha_-\) and \(m_y\) from \(\beta_+\) to \(\beta_-\), each at constant \(s\) with decreasing \(t\) in the displayed interval. Mark the full circle \(m_z=\{s=0\}\). Use two reference arcs.

- The arc \(\delta_0\) runs from \(\alpha_+\) to \(\beta_+\) with horizontal end segments at \(t=1/4,3/4\) and a middle segment at \(s=1/2\) with decreasing \(t\) through zero. In particular its middle segment meets \(t=1/8\) before \(t=7/8\). It misses all three meridians.
- The arc \(\rho\) runs from \(\alpha_-\) to \(\beta_-\) at \(t=0\) with decreasing \(s\) through zero. It misses \(\delta_0,m_x,m_y\) and crosses \(m_z\) once.

Glue each plus spot to its minus spot by an orientation-reversing circle map matching its meridian endpoints and the endpoints of \(\delta_0,\rho\). There are exactly two designated points on each circle, so such gluing maps exist. Round the joins. The result is a genus-three surface \(\Sigma\) with simple circles \(m_x,m_y,m_z\). The spot seams \(\alpha,\beta\) meet their respective meridians once and miss the other two. The circle \(t=7/8\) meets just \(m_z\) once. These dual intersections prove that the three meridian classes are independent. Consequently their complement after cutting is connected, since any boundary of a cut component at a seam between components would give a nontrivial linear relation. Euler characteristic then gives a sphere with six holes. Define the genus-three handlebody \(H\) with this boundary and these meridian disks. To specify the marking, fill the six holes by the two faces of each disk and take a ball, then identify paired faces. In the free group of \(H\), write \(x,y,z\) for the crossings specified by the clockwise plus-spot orientations of \(\alpha,\beta\) and increasing \(s\) at \(m_z\). Write capital letters for inverses. A path avoiding the meridians gives the empty word with basing in the cut ball.

The curves \(\alpha,\beta\) bound the two disks \(D_\alpha,D_\beta\) on the other side, in the compression body \(C\) obtained by attaching two 3-dimensional 2-handles to the positive-boundary collar. Its negative boundary is a torus. The union \(V=H\cup_\Sigma C\) is a solid torus. Indeed the two displayed geometrically dual pairs cancel the \(x,y\) handles, leaving the \(z\) handle of the ball. This also gives a particular solid-torus identification.

For the first slide, change \(\delta_0\) to \(\delta\) by twisting twice in a narrow strip about \(t=1/8\) and once in one about \(t=7/8\). In a strip use \((s,t)\mapsto(s+f(t),t)\) modulo one, with \(f\) zero near the upper edge and equal to the indicated integer near the lower edge. The resulting arc is embedded because the two strips define diffeomorphisms and the endpoints of the reference arc are fixed. Both supports miss \(\rho\). Orient \(m_x,m_y\) from plus to minus in the cut torus, so their tangent is \(-\partial_t\) from the bottoms of the plus spots through the strip at \(t=1/8\). The clockwise tangent on either plus spot at its marked intersection is \(-\partial_s\), which defines the positive crossing for its generator. Every increasing-\(s\) lap of the twisted arc crosses the two meridians with the opposite tangent sign. From \(s=1/2\) the order of crossings on a lap at \(t=1/8\) is \(s=2/3,0,1/3\) modulo one, giving \(YzX\). At \(t=7/8\) only the \(s=0\) crossing remains, giving \(z\). The band core therefore reads
\[
w=(YzX)^2z .
\]

Slide \(D_\alpha\) over \(D_\beta\) along \(\delta\), giving \(D_1\). Let \(P\) be the band pair of pants on the two plus spots and \(r_1\) its outer boundary. Both spot cuffs inherit their clockwise boundary orientations from \(P\), so the two spot words are \(x,y\). Orient \(r_1\) opposite to its induced boundary orientation in \(P\). Choose the connector from the \(\alpha\) seam to \(r_1\) on the side of the band foot for which this directed boundary circuit follows \(\alpha_+\) first, traverses the band, follows \(\beta_+\), and returns along the band. This choice is seen by retracting \(P\) onto the two spot circles and the band core. Thus \(r_1=xw y w^{-1}\) in this basing. The two disks now are \(D_1,D_\beta\).

Slide \(D_\beta\) over \(D_1\) using the band from \(r_1\) in the same sector of \(P\), through the \(\alpha\) seam and along \(\rho\) to \(\beta_-\). This is an embedded band in the complement of the two current disk curves. Its based connector reads \(Z\), since \(\rho\) crosses \(s=0\) in the decreasing direction. The new pair of pants meets \(r_1\) on the \(P\)-side, so its induced cuff orientation reads \(r_1^{-1}\). Its other cuff is \(\beta_-\), whose clockwise orientation is carried to the opposite of the clockwise \(\beta_+\) orientation by the spot gluing. This cuff therefore reads \(Y\). The new outer boundary with its induced orientation reads the inverse of \(r_1^{-1} Z Y z\), up to cyclic conjugacy. The resulting unoriented boundary of \(D_2\) has representative \(r_2=r_1 Z y z\). The system \(D_1,D_2\) still cuts \(C\) to the product of its negative torus with an interval. At each slide, cutting along the fixed disk turns the slide into pushing over a disk spot on the boundary of the cut compression body. Reversing the order and the bands gives the two inverse slides.

## Whole-boundary certificates

The two cyclically reduced words are
\[
r_1=xYzXYzXzyZxZyxZy,\qquad
r_2=xYzXYzXzyZxZyxZyZyz.
\]
For successive letters \(u,v\) the Whitehead graph has an edge from \(u\) to \(v^{-1}\). The successive pairs \(xY,XY,Xz,Zy,zy,zX\) occur in the interior of both words and exhibit the spanning cycle
\[
x-y-X-Z-Y-z-x .
\]
Each graph is thus connected with no cut vertex. By Stallings's separable-set form of the Whitehead cut-vertex lemma, neither curve is conjugate into a proper free factor of \(\pi_1(H)\). Here a compression disk disjoint from either curve would give a free splitting with the curve in a proper factor, by cutting \(H\) along the disk. This includes a nonseparating disk and a separating essential disk. Hence each curve by itself is disk-busting on \(\partial H\). [Suraj Krishna M. S., *Vertex links and the Grushko decomposition*, Definition 6.1 and Proposition 6.4, pp. 571–572](https://afst.centre-mersenne.org/item/10.5802/afst.1744.pdf#page=19)

The boundary surgery along each curve is a connected genus-two surface, since it is one element of a complete nonseparating disk pair on \(C\). Apply the handle addition lemma to \(Q=H\), the compressible surface \(S=\partial H\), and each disk-busting curve separately. It proves that each \(H[r_i]\) is irreducible and that its whole boundary is incompressible. In particular the proof includes any annuli from the tangle strands. [Culler–Gordon–Luecke–Shalen, *Dehn Surgery on Knots*, Lemma 2.1.1, p. 267](https://marc-culler.info/static/home/papers/CyclicSurgery.pdf#page=32)

## Tangle and native closing

Fix the annulus \(A_a=S^1_s\times[1/2-\epsilon,1/2+\epsilon]\) in the cut torus for sufficiently small \(\epsilon\). Its neighborhood misses the disk seams, the two bands and all the twist strips. Use its product in the compression-body collar to carry it to \(\partial V\). On \(\partial V\) the horizontal core at \(t=1/2\) meets the boundary of the surviving \(m_z\) meridian disk exactly once. Thus \(A_a\) is a longitudinal annulus. Identify \(V\) with the exterior of a straight proper arc \(a\) in \(B^3\), sending \(A_a\) with its \(s\) and transverse interval coordinates to the lateral annulus \(\partial D^2_a\times I_{s,a}\) of the arc neighborhood. Such an identification is obtained by extending the annulus map across the complementary annulus of the torus with the meridian matched. A boundary twist fixing \(A_a\) adjusts any integral meridian mismatch. The resulting meridian-preserving map extends across the solid torus.

For \(b,c\) take the transverse arcs of the 3-dimensional 2-handles on \(D_1,D_2\), extended by four disjoint fibers of the cut compression-body product to \(\partial V\). Take the product to agree with the unchanged collar on \(A_a\), so the four fibers reach the complement of \(A_a\), which lies in the outer punctured sphere. Equivalently, thicken each disk as \(D^2\times I_s\), use its \(\{0\}\times I_s\), and absorb the outer collar of the compression body into the strand neighborhoods and the boundary collar of the exterior. This gives proper strands in the original ball. Their full drilled exterior is the marked \(H\), with collars restored as just described. Drilling only \(a,b\) restores the \(D_2\) product, giving \(H[r_2]\), and drilling only \(a,c\) gives \(H[r_1]\).

Take \(I_t\) as the doubling interval. For each strand with neighborhood \(D^2\times I_s\), the exact reverse 2-handle is \(D^2\times\(I_s\times I_t\)\) attached by \(\partial D^2\times\(I_s\times I_t\)\) in the boundary of the drilled product. In the forward direction the attaching knot is the doubled arc \(\{0\}\times\partial\(I_s\times I_t\)\) in \(\partial(B^3\times I_t)=S^3\), with its doubled product framing. These framings are zero, since the original proper disks obtained by multiplying the strands by \(I_t\) are pairwise disjoint with their product normal markings. The original \(A\) is the unknot, and its surgery alone is the double of the solid torus \(V\). The two two-strand surgery levels are the doubles across the whole connected boundaries just certified. Irreducibility follows by the incompressible-boundary gluing lemma applied to two copies of each irreducible piece. [Doubling convention and product handle maps](TERMINAL_PAIR_SOURCE_AUDIT_20261126.md#doubled-tangles)

The upper body is \(H\times I_t\). The outgoing boundary identification is the pointwise identification of the two copies of \(H\) and the rounded collar \(\partial H\times I_t\) with the corresponding faces of this product. Its inverse identifies those same faces back into the drilled product boundary. The doubled \(m_x,m_y,m_z\) meridian disks give actual belt spheres. For \(b,c\), the reverse attaching regions have core circles \(r_1,r_2\) at the middle of the collar and the \(I_s\times I_t\) normal coordinates from the individual strand products. The original reverse tube \(A^*\) is \(A_a\times I_t\), with the interval marking of the straight strand. In particular the belt system belongs to the same product closing used to compute the outlet.

Neither original reverse attaching circle \(r_1,r_2\) can meet a belt of a complete system once and miss the remaining belts. Such a word is primitive in the free group of the upper body, contrary to the Whitehead certificates.

## The returned rank-two closing

Undo the second disk slide on the upper collar using its inverse band, thickened with the \(I_t\) coordinate. This restores \((r_1,\beta)\) with its framed tubes. The band and tube changes are supported away from \(A_a\times I_t\). Attach the restored \(\beta\) handle first in reverse. Its core meets the doubled \(m_y\) disk once and misses the other two belts. This is a geometric cancellation. Write \(M_2=H[\beta]\). The actual upper body after the cancellation is \(M_2\times I_t\), and \(M_2\) is a genus-two handlebody with the surviving meridian disks \(m_x,m_z\). Cutting along those two disks in \(H\) leaves the \(y\) solid torus, where \(\beta\) cancels the remaining handle. Thus these are a complete system for \(M_2\).

The returned rank-two level is the double of \(M_2\). Its map to \(\partial(M_2\times I_t)\) identifies the unchanged parts of the two \(H\)-faces and their lateral collar pointwise. The two \(D^2_\beta\times I_s\) additions to the \(H\)-faces and the lateral faces along the two ends of \(I_s\) are identified by their product coordinates. The inverse forgets these product names on the same faces, with the same corner rounding. This is the boundary map of the restored \(\beta\) filling itself. The surviving \(r_1\) and \(A^*\) tubes lie on this returned level with the transported coordinates.

On \(\partial M_2\), one of the two \(\beta\) disk spots caps the \(\beta_+\) cuff of \(P\). The result is an embedded annulus between \(r_1\) and \(\alpha\). The inverse first slide across this spot is now an isotopy along the annulus, supported in it and a thin collar of its two ends. Extend the surface isotopy through a collar of \(\partial M_2\) in \(M_2\) and take the product with \(I_t\). This carries the full \(r_1\) tube to the \(\alpha\) tube with the normal parameter on the surface carried by the isotopy and \(I_t\) fixed. The reverse isotopy runs along the annulus from \(\alpha\) back to \(r_1\) and recovers the same tube. The cap, annulus and product isotopy miss the marked \(A_a\) tube. After this identification the \(\alpha\) core hits the doubled \(m_x\) disk once and misses \(m_z\). Its reverse cancellation gives \(M_2[\alpha]\times I_t=V\times I_t\) with the original solid-torus marking.

The reserved reverse \(A^*\) tube on this actual \(Y_1=\partial(V\times I_t)\) is \(A_a\times I_t\). Its core is the horizontal circle at \(t=1/2\) in the middle \(I_t\) slice. Its two normal coordinates are the transverse interval of \(A_a=\partial D^2_a\times I_{s,a}\) and \(I_t\). It meets the doubled \(m_z\) disk once. The last reverse cancellation restores the original straight-strand neighborhood \(D^2_a\times I_{s,a}\times I_t\) and yields \(B^3\times I_t\).

In the forward direction, dualize these last two reverse handles by exchanging the role of the product disk factors \(D^2\) and \(I_s\times I_t\). From \(S^3=\partial(B^3\times I_t)\) the marked \(A\) attachment gives \(\partial(V\times I_t)\). The next forward trace is the inverse of the \(\alpha\) attachment with the inverse annulus transport at its upper end, giving the returned \(Y_2=\partial(M_2\times I_t)\) with the preceding boundary map and its inverse. In reverse this compares the two actual remaining tubes and closes against the doubled \(m_x,m_z\) disks. The first inverse slide preserves the three middle and three upper handles. The three cancellations give \(n_2=n_3:3\to2\to1\to0\) in the forward count, equivalently \(n_2=n_1:3\to2\to1\to0\) in the reversed count, with zero births. This control establishes the marked return with two irreducible native cuts and prescribed inverse bands.
