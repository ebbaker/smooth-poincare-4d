# Lifted links and intersections with a nonseparating sphere

SUR-B04/E02m, [fixed task and inputs](../../research-progress/handoffs/WINDING_AND_CURVATURE_FOLLOWUP_20261126.md). Work in the smooth oriented marked inlet \(Y_1=S^1\times S^2\) after a split zero-framed unknot has been attached to a four-ball. The [one-turn construction](GENERATOR_BAND_PAIR.md) gives the elementary link-splitting details and the two end maps. All links below are parametrized before framing changes.

Put the Figure 5 square knot \(Q\) and its unknotted partner \(U\) in a ball \(B\) of the inlet. They are a nonsplit pair in the capped ball. Otherwise zero surgery gives \((S^1\times S^2)\# S^3_0(Q)=Y_2\), forcing \(S^3_0(Q)=Y_1\) by prime uniqueness and first Betti number; Property R contradicts the nontriviality of \(Q\). The displayed Figure 5 slide of \(Q\) over \(U\) gives the split zero-framed unlink. Choose the marked zero-framed parallel \(Q^+\). Fix an integer \(n\ge1\). Slide \(U\) over \(Q\) by joining \(U\) to \(Q^+\) along a band that runs \(n\) times around the positive generator. Write \(K_n\) for the moving component. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, §2 and Figure 5, pp. 2310–2311](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=6)

Here is a choice with controlled geometry. In the cyclic cover \(S^2\times\mathbb R\), place \(B_j\) about height \(j\) with mutually disjoint lifts. Connect the mouths in \(B_0\) and \(B_n\) by fixed short access strips and a band whose central arc increases strictly in height. Its section in a small disk of \(S^2\) outside the projection of the balls moves monotonically along an embedded interval in that disk. Distinct integer translates of the central arc are then disjoint. Arrange the two access arcs in the complement to enter opposite ends of that interval, miss its interior, and be disjoint in the quotient. The lifted band and its translates have disjoint thickenings of positive width for each fixed \(n\) by compactness downstairs. The entrances can be taken as product collars of arcs cut from \(U,Q^+\) (a short finger to the ball boundary with parallel return). Capping the two sides of any middle horizontal sphere along the band thus closes the original knot arcs. A varying position in the small disk along a monotone arc straightens by planar isotopy at each height. This specifies an untied, untwisted connecting band in the quotient and keeps it disjoint from every \(B_j\) except its chosen ends. Its framing is the framed-slide framing. No independent surgery coefficient is assigned.

Lift the resulting moving knot using those mouths and call it \(K\); its projection to \(K_n\) has degree one. Indeed the loop goes out and back along the band and the two end knots are null-homotopic. Compactify the two ends of the cover to obtain \(S^3\). Let
\[
 {\cal S}(K,Q)=\{j\in\mathbb Z:K\cup Q_j
                  \text{ is nonsplit in this }S^3\}.
\]
Then \({\cal S}(K,Q)=\{0,n\}\). For any other \(j\), the sphere \(\partial B_j\) separates the knots. To test \(j=0\), cut in the middle of the band. The two-component sublink is the original nonsplit \(U_0,Q_0\) with a local copy of the knot \(Q^+\) tied into \(U_0\). Tying a local knot into one component of a nonsplit link preserves nonsplitting. For a direct proof use the ball isolating the tied arc, and suppose a sphere splits the new sublink. Minimize its circles of intersection with the boundary of that ball. An innermost circle on the splitting sphere cannot be essential in the twice-punctured boundary sphere: either cap of such a circle there contains one of the knot punctures, and its union with the innermost disk is a sphere pierced once by a closed knot. Inessential circles can be removed by surgery using a puncture-free disk innermost on the punctured boundary, retaining a separating sphere since separation is additive mod two (intersect an arc between the two components). A disjoint splitting sphere is outside the tied-arc ball, as both components have points outside it. Untying the arc there would preserve separation of the original pair. For \(j=n\) forgetting \(Q_0\) leaves the parallel pair \(Q_n^+,Q_n\) with only the local unknot \(U_0\) tied into its first component. Two zero-framed parallels of a nontrivial knot are nonsplit. A splitting sphere must meet their spanning annulus in an essential circle, since the ends of the annulus lie on opposite sides. Such a circle is isotopic to \(Q_n\) on the annulus but lies on a sphere, so it is an unknot. The same middle sphere exhibits \(K\) alone as the connected sum \(U_0\#Q_n^+\), a square knot.

The set above changes only by translation and a possible reflection of its integer index under changes of lift and diffeomorphisms of the marked inlet. Any such lifted diffeomorphism is proper, permutes the two ends, and extends to their compactification as a homeomorphism, enough to preserve splitting. For these smooth compact links a locally flat splitting sphere can also be taken smooth (equivalently move a smooth splitting sphere off the two end points before transporting it on the open cover). A link lying in an embedded ball uses mutually disjoint ball lifts. For a given moving lift, all stationary lifts except possibly the one in its ball are separated from it by that ball boundary. Hence every pair here is genuinely nonlocal. The integer \(n\) is determined by the distance between the two indices in \({\cal S}\), so these are pairwise inequivalent marked links. Their two free boundary types and ordinary linking matrices are the same.

There is a downstairs measurement of this distance. For smooth embedded nonseparating spheres \(S\subset Y_1\) missing \(Q\), transverse to \(K_n\),
\[
                 \min_S |S\cap K_n|=2n .
\]
The complement of such an \(S\), cut open at the two faces, is connected. Its cyclic stacking is the given cover: a loop joining the two faces and closing once pairs to one with \(S\). The lifted spheres bound consecutive open regions, and each \(Q_j\) lies in a region \(j\) steps from that containing \(Q_0\), up to reversal. The moving lift has to enter the open regions containing \(Q_0\) and \(Q_n\). Otherwise a boundary sphere of the missed region separates the two knots, after a small push if necessary. To visit both regions and return, the closed lift must cross each of the \(n\) distinct intervening lifted spheres at least twice. Projection counts their crossings without multiplicity on the moving lift. Conversely a horizontal sphere outside the end balls cuts the two parallel strands in each of the \(n\) monotone turns, giving \(2n\) transverse points and missing \(Q\). One may shrink the stationary tube and choose the sphere off the entire tube. This is a relative geometric intersection count on the two marked components.

The inverse specified framed slide takes \(K_n,Q\) to \(U,Q\) for every \(n\). The Figure 5 slide then produces the split unlink. In the one-sided three-middle closure both original penultimate cuts fail: the prefix consists of a four-ball and two 2-handles and is simply connected. Its boundary has first homology \(\mathbb Z^2\) (single zero surgery of a null-homologous component in \(Y_1\)). Domination would identify that boundary with \(Y_2\) by the rank-two compression argument. The rank-increasing knot theorem would then make that first component a local unknot in \(Y_1\). The lift of either component is a nontrivial square knot. These arguments use neither simple connectivity of the inlet nor an identification of either individual prefix across the slides. [Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06561#page=9) · [Compression and extension](../manuscript/recognition_from_marked_handle_cuts.tex)

Without the two slides neither actual outgoing slope bounds a disk in the common link exterior: such a compression forces the original split unlink at these free ends by the [peripheral argument](TWO_ATTACHMENT_GROUP.md). The minimum generating rank of that exterior group is therefore greater than three; a two-tunnel certificate is excluded as well. The construction does not determine the minimum tunnel number beyond this bound. After the slides the two separated dual cores cancel against two actual upper handles using the extending \(H_3\) marking. There are zero temporary stabilizations. The terminal pair decreases both counts by two, leaving the original initial single attachment with its transported upper body. Its single cancellation ends at count zero. On an interior occurrence of the block compose each later tube with the actual outgoing diffeomorphism \(P\), use its inverse on its image, and change the closing to \(\gamma P^{-1}\) (inverse \(P\gamma^{-1}\)), extending first across all transported later handles before using their final outlet. The relative slide equivalence is available there even when there is no terminal cancellation at that level. A different link with the same integer separation need not admit either framed band.
