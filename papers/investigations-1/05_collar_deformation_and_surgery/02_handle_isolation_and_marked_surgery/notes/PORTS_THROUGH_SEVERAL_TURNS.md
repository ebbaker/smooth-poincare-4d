# Recovering the stationary band through several turns

26 November 2026. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI Codex assistance. Selected lead GPT-6.2 Astra/ultra, independent serving telemetry unavailable. SUR-B04 / E02q at [issue 2](../../research-progress/handoffs/EXTERIOR_RECOVERY_AND_NATIVE_CAPS_20261126.md). Scientific baseline 8471728a1549614f155a8751c709904cbcf994d6. This is a smooth oriented relative computation in the product-port representatives of the [marked winding family](LIFTED_LINKS_AND_SPHERES.md), using the [one-period exterior calculation](CUT_TANGLE_RECOVERY.md) and its [independent reading](../../reviews/CUT_TANGLE_READER.md).

Take a small transverse disk at the opposite mouth, immediately before the rails meet the local part of the moving knot. Its two intersections are joined by a chord c in the port chart. Removing that chord from the once-completed local knot gives the retained arc V. The other arc R of the actual moving knot follows two rails and the almost complete stationary longitude between them. For n>1 these are arcs cut off at the mouth, not the list of returns on a single copy of the nonseparating sphere. The horizontal sphere meets the whole moving knot in 2n points.

Here is an explicit subfamily and the geometric input needed at the cut. Use the monotone lane coordinate of the cited construction; on the straight long part take planar coordinates (x,y) on a small angular disk outside the end balls. At lifted height t use transverse rail positions \((x(t),-b),(x(t),b)\) with x strictly increasing along that long part. At a horizontal cut the pairs are ordered by x, with equal x within each pair. Use product entrances and arrange a short collar of each seam with the same smooth chart on its two sides; access arcs come from disjoint boxes in the complement of the central lanes. This is a permissible representative at each winding n. One can equally use small disjoint transverse disks in each lane chart, with the edge positions varying and positive separation from every other lane. Use continuous marked pairings across every seam. Neither a cyclic linking number nor 2n unlabelled points specifies this geometry.

Within each transverse disk join the observed positions by their straight interval. Sections at different lifted heights have different t. At the same projected height the sections from different turns have distinct x, so the corresponding intervals are disjoint. On each quotient seam the intervals from the adjoining slab agree by the same marking and product germs. The two end rectangles, obtained by the same rule in the disjoint access boxes, attach smoothly after rounding. Thus the projected surface is an embedded rectangle running from the missing short longitude segment to c, disjoint from V and the stationary tube in its interior. This is a direct embeddedness test on the cut diagram. In particular no choice of which deck winding to undo is needed to select a sliding band.

Join this rectangle to the radial annulus at the mouth of the stationary zero longitude. The resulting annulus has outer boundary \(R\cup c\) and inner boundary the stationary filling slope. It is embedded: away from their common boundary interval the access box points out of the stationary collar, and the long portions are in the complement of the end balls. Choose a radial rectangle over any short interval of the visible longitude away from its gap, from R to a nearby marked zero-framed parallel. Slit the truncated annulus along this rectangle and slide with opposite orientation on the parallel. The remaining disk takes the new R onto a short push-off of c, relative to its endpoints in the opposite port. Its interior is disjoint from V, including after the paired seam identifications. The resulting knot is exactly the local completion of V by c. In the displayed coordinates this is the Figure 5 unknot with its original stationary partner. The two zero framings and vanishing mutual linking give zero framing after the subtraction slide. Figure 5 supplies the second slide. The first band for every n is a short radial rectangle near the stationary tube; the disk proving its effect can traverse all n turns. The net reduction, both trace maps and the transport of arbitrary later framed tubes follow the [one-period formulas](CUT_TANGLE_RECOVERY.md#relative-maps-and-handle-count). For the terminal three-middle assembly this removes the marked pair and then the initial unknot, with zero stabilizations. The two-component interior trace alone has unchanged middle count.

There is also a conclusion on exteriors with a different opposite tangle. Suppose the same geometric annulus and slit disk are found for a marked pair with free ends \(Y_r\to Y_{r+2}\), and that completing V by c gives a local unknot in the inlet. The stationary partner need not have been identified with a particular knot. After the recovered slide the algebraic free-end calculation gives framing zero on the unknot and stationary partner and mutual linking zero. Put the unknot first. Its outgoing boundary is \(Y_{r+1}\). The remaining *actual* knot gives a nonmeridional surgery to \(Y_{r+2}\); the single-knot theorem identifies it there as a zero-framed local unknot. Isotope it to a little split ball in the common exterior. Make this tube isotopy from its initial to final knot generic against the first filling core. The core-circle sweep has only finitely many transverse passages (shrink the moving tube in the course of the argument). Detouring each passage in the original inlet corresponds to a framed slide over the first component, by its specified filling slope. This gives a finite relative reduction with no temporary handles; its length can depend on the knot. This is the [marked single-unknot argument](TWO_ATTACHMENT_GROUP.md#a-local-unknot-after-one-chosen-framed-slide). On an immediate upper closing by \(H_{r+2}\) it cancels the two terminal middle handles. The relative map with later handles instead carries their marked tubes and compares the actual final closing. A knotted opposite completion requires another test.
