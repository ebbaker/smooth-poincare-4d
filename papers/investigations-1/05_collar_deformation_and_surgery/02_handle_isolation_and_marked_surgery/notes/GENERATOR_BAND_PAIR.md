## Test the link in the compactified cover

Compactify $S^2\times\mathbb R$ to $S^3$ using $(x,t)\mapsto e^t x$ in $\mathbb R^3\setminus\{0\}$ and adjoining $0$ and $\infty$. The sphere $S^2\times\{1/2\}$ bounds a ball on each side and intersects $K_0$ twice, on the two band edges. Cap each cut arc along the segment of the strip in this sphere. Within the respective half-balls the band rectangles isotope each capped arc configuration back to $U_0$ and $Q_1^+$, with the isotopies disjoint from the corresponding stationary $Q_0$ and $Q_1$. In particular $K_0$ as a knot in $S^3$ is $U_0\# Q_1^+$ and has the square-knot type. For the pair with $Q_0$, restoring the opposite tangle ties a local square knot into the $U_0$ component of the Figure 5 pair. For the pair with $Q_1$, the opposite tangle ties the local unknot $U_0$ into $Q_1^+$. All claims about splitting below concern just the two named components in the compactification.

**Local knot tying preserves nonsplitting.** Suppose a ball $D$ meets one component in a single proper arc and misses the other. Replacing the arc by a boundary-parallel arc gives the link before the local knot was tied. If the link after tying had a splitting sphere $T$, put it transverse to $\partial D$. Each intersection circle in the twice-punctured $\partial D$ is inessential there. An essential circle would link the modified component with linking number $\pm1$, while it bounds a disk in $T$ disjoint from that component. If an intersection remains, take an innermost disk in $\partial D$ missing both punctures. Such a disk exists by taking a minimal disk among the puncture-free disks of the intersection circles. Compress $T$ across it and push the two resulting disks slightly off $\partial D$. The two resulting spheres lie in the link complement and at least one separates the same two components. Indeed, the mod-two intersection of a path between points on the two components is additive across the surgery and was one for $T$. Keep a splitting sphere from the two results. It has fewer intersection circles. After finitely many steps a splitting sphere is disjoint from $\partial D$. It lies outside $D$, since both components have points outside $D$. It would still split the link after the boundary-parallel replacement. This proves the claim.

**A nontrivial knot and its framed parallel are nonsplit.** They bound an embedded annulus. A splitting sphere transverse to that annulus must intersect it in an essential circle, since finitely many inessential circles cannot separate its two boundary components. Such a circle has the knot type of the boundary of the annulus, but bounds an embedded disk in the splitting sphere. This would make the knot trivial.

The first observation and Figure 5 give nonsplitting of $(K_0,Q_0)$. Both observations give nonsplitting of $(K_0,Q_1)$, using the parallel $Q_1^+$. If the original pair lay in a common ball in $Y_1$, all lifts of that ball would be disjoint compact balls. The one containing $K_0$ would contain exactly one lift of $Q$, so its boundary would split at least one of these two pairs after compactification. This rules out a common ball. Separately, an embedded spanning disk for either component in $Y_1$ would lift to a disk in the cover and give one in $S^3$. The lifts of $Q$ are square knots and $K_0$ is a square knot, so both individual disk tests fail. In particular neither specified filling longitude compresses in the common exterior, since an exterior disk and the longitude annulus would give an embedded spanning disk in $Y_1$. ## The original penultimate boundaries

Let $C_\beta$ be the marked trace of $(K,Q)$ on $Y_1$. Its outgoing level is $Y_3$ by the two explicit framed slides below. For $E=Y_1\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu(K\sqcup Q)$, excision and the two null-homologous cores give a split exact sequence of abelian groups

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\langle\mu_K,\mu_Q\rangle
\longrightarrow H_1(E;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\longrightarrow0.
$$

Write $\alpha_K,\alpha_Q$ for the actual framing slopes. Both classes vanish in $H_1(E;\mathbb Z)$ because their quotient is $H_1(Y_3;\mathbb Z)$ of the same free rank three. The longitude of either knot has class given by linking with the other times the other meridian. Thus both specified framings are zero in the Seifert convention and the linking number is zero. For $J\in\{K,Q\}$ let $Z_J$ be its surgery on $Y_1$, ordered first within the pair. Filling along $\alpha_J$ and the meridian of the other component gives $H_1(Z_J;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2$. [Excision and sign conventions](TWO_ATTACHMENT_GROUP.md#the-trace-group-and-both-systems-of-slopes)

The actual prefix with boundary $Z_J$ is $W_J=D^4\cup h_A^2\cup h_J^2$, with $h_J^2$ attached to the outgoing $Y_1$ of the first handle. It is simply connected and has $H_2(W_J;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^2$ and $H_i(W_J;\mathbb Z)=0$ for $i\ge3$, by its handles. Poincaré–Lefschetz duality and the relative sequence give

$$
0\longrightarrow H_2(Z_J;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow \mathbb Z^2
\xrightarrow{\;I_{W_J}\;}\mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow \mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow0.
$$

The last map is a surjection of free groups of equal rank, so the middle intersection map vanishes and the boundary inclusion is an integral $H_2$ isomorphism. If $Z_J$ were PSC-dominated, Proposition 2.2 of Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee would express it as a sum of spherical space forms and $S^1\times S^2$ factors. Its first homology gives exactly two product factors and forces every spherical factor to be an integral homology sphere. The residual sum of spherical factors admits PSC and is $S^3$ or a connected sum of signed Poincaré spheres by their Corollary 2.3. Attach two auxiliary 3-handles to $W_J$ along the product spheres. Their classes form a basis of $H_2(Z_J;\mathbb Z)$ and hence of $H_2(W_J;\mathbb Z)$. This kills $H_2$ integrally, adds no $H_3$, and preserves simple connectivity. The smooth result is contractible, with boundary the residual homology sphere. Their Proposition 4.2 forces that residual boundary to be $S^3$. This would give $Z_J\cong Y_2$. Meier–Schirmer–Zupan Theorem 5.1 for a nonmeridional surgery from $Y_1$ to $Y_2$ requires $J$ to be an unknot in a ball, contradicted by the lift check for each $J$. [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, arXiv:2407.05574v1, Proposition 2.2, Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1) · [Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1, pp. 9–10 of the preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06561#page=9)
