The operation below changes two specified arcs of the marking, without changing a tube, filling or gluing.

Use the four drilled arcs and stationary filling of [the issue](../../research-progress/handoffs/FOUR_POINT_MARKED_RETURNS_20261126.md), with face chords joining 12 and 34. Cap both faces outside the slab. Join the two through strands there along their original short chords, 34 on the lower face and 12 on the upper. This caps them to the original moving constituent C=V4. Complete the returns along the other original chords in the caps to J_- and J_+. Link with the oriented C in the capped S³. This gives an integral homomorphism on the first homology of the unfilled drilled slab. Its value on the stationary filling slope is lk(Q,C)=0, since the zero surgeries of the actual local pair give Y2. It therefore descends to a homomorphism h on H1(X) after filling Q. Through meridians have value +1 or -1 according to orientation. Both return meridians have value zero. C and its caps in this calculation are fixed throughout.

On the common four-holed face take a circle d separating holes 1,3 from 2,4 and meeting each original short chord once. It bounds a face disk before the holes are drilled. Choose d, for instance, as the boundary of a neighborhood of holes 1 and 3 and an arc joining them which avoids the short chords away from its ends. Use the same Dehn twist on both copies of the face. Replace the two specified chords by their images under the kth power of the twist about d. The image chords are disjoint, still join the prescribed points, and match across the actual seam. The twist fixes each of the four boundary circles pointwise. The two returns, attaching framings, the face identification and both ends are exactly those of the original free-outlet specimen.

On either face d encloses one through hole and one return hole. Thus |h(d)|=1 there. For each original return chord b let w(b) be the chord closed by a strip on its own return wall. Twisting b changes its relative homology class by plus or minus k[d], since it crosses d once. The strip is unchanged. Any other strip adds return meridians, on which h vanishes. The old disk gives h(w(b))=0. Hence every wall closure of the new chord has |h|=|k| on each side. At k≠0 neither prescribed return bounds a bridge disk, even after the stationary filling, with any allowed wall winding. Neither boundary loop can appear in a simultaneous bridge system with those individual prescribed disk boundaries. This excludes disks disjoint from the other drilled strands. The test concerns exactly these prescribed face arcs. If the input permits choosing the face arcs up to a pure mapping class, the original disjoint disks still reduce this same link. This obstruction says nothing against another pattern of tunnel system or against smooth handle reduction.

For general fixed disjoint chords with the same mixed pairing, each allowable wall winding changes the candidate class by one moving meridian. In the full link exterior after stationary filling the moving meridian has infinite order, and is primitive, by the maximal rank jump. Its image is thus also infinite in the stationary-filled slab. Test integral homology first, leaving at most one winding per chord. For each survivor, test its boundary word in the fundamental group of the slab. Dehn's lemma makes triviality equivalent to a properly embedded disk for that simple boundary. For distinct boundary curves on opposite faces, eliminate circles of intersection by innermost disk replacement to obtain a disjoint system if both tests pass. The quantifier over a freely chosen face arc is different from the fixed-word test.
