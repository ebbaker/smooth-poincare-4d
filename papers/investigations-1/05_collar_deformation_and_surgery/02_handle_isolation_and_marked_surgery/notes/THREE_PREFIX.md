## A rank-one second prefix suffices for a strict decrease

Let a smooth oriented homotopy four-sphere $M$ have reduced counts $(1,1,3,2,1)$. Fix a marked presentation with lower body $P=S^1\times D^3$, top body $Q=\natural_2(S^1\times D^3)$ read from the top, and three disjoint framed tubes $a,b,c$ in $\partial P$. Choose genuine framed slides to normalize their algebraic windings to $(1,0,0)$ and carry the upper gluing $\gamma:Y_{abc}\to\partial Q$ through the slides. Let $W_{ab}$ include $P$ and attachments $a,b$, in either order, and put $Y_{ab}=\partial W_{ab}$. Suppose $Y_{ab}$ receives a nonzero-degree map from a closed oriented PSC three-manifold. Then $Y_{ab}\cong S^1\times S^2$ and the **same $M$ has a mixed presentation with two middle handles** which retains the framed pair $a,b$. The proof applies with $b,c$ interchanged and uses the second prefix without a condition on $Y_a$.

Replace the dotted lower unknot by $U_0$ for the *boundary calculation*. The full linking matrix is
\[
\begin{pmatrix}
0&1&0&0\\
1&d&p&q\\
0&p&s&t\\
0&q&t&u
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
It has rank two since the full outgoing boundary has first homology $\mathbb Z^2$. The leading $2\times2$ block has determinant $-1$, and the Schur complement is exactly the bottom-right block, so $s=t=u=0$. Congruence over $\mathbb Z$ splits off the leading block from each prefix containing $a$. Thus $H_1(Y_{ab};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$ and $H_1(Y_{bc};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^3$.

For rank one use **the actual** $W_{ab}$. The winding-one relation kills its cyclic fundamental group. Its handle chain complex gives $H_2(W_{ab};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$. The intersection form vanishes since the cycles can be intersected inside the interior and their images have zero intersection in $M$, where $H_2(M;\mathbb Z)=0$. The pair sequence and duality now give an integral isomorphism
\[
H_2(Y_{ab};\mathbb Z)\xrightarrow{\ \,\cong\ \,}H_2(W_{ab};\mathbb Z).
\]
The previous term is $H^1(W_{ab};\mathbb Z)=0$ and the next map is the zero intersection map. Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, Proposition 2.2, gives the connected-sum decomposition of $Y_{ab}$ under PSC domination. Exactly one $S^1\times S^2$ summand survives rationally. The spherical summands have trivial integral first homology because the homology of the connected sum is a direct sum and $H_1(Y_{ab};\mathbb Z)$ is torsion free. Corollary 2.3 applied to the residual integral homology sphere $\Sigma$ makes it a sum of signed Poincaré spheres. Attach an auxiliary 3-handle to $W_{ab}$ along the marked pullback of a factor sphere in $S^1\times S^2$. Its relative boundary map is a unit by the isomorphism above, and higher-index attachment preserves simple connectivity. The resulting smooth filling of $\Sigma$ is contractible by Whitehead's theorem. Proposition 4.2 forces the sum to be empty, so $Y_{ab}\cong S^1\times S^2$. Conversely the product PSC metric and the inverse boundary diffeomorphism supply a degree-one witness. The auxiliary 3-handle has only been used in the boundary identification. [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, arXiv:2407.05574v1, Proposition 2.2, Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1)

The actual forward tube of $c$ sits in $Y_{ab}$ by survival in the exterior of the first two tubes. Its surgery gives $Y_{abc}\cong\#_2(S^1\times S^2)$. Choose a marking $Y_{ab}\to S^1\times S^2$ and view the latter as zero surgery on an unknot $U\subset S^3$. Make the marked $c$ disjoint from the surgery core of $U$ and shrink the two tubes so it pulls back to the exterior of $U$ in $S^3$, with its integral normal framing. The resulting two-component framed link $U_0\cup c$ surgered in full gives $\#_2(S^1\times S^2)$. Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson Proposition 3.2 applies. Its proof gives the needed isotopy of $c$ **in $S^1\times S^2$**, into a ball as an unknot. This is the intermediate step of their proof before converting that isotopy to slides in $S^3$. In the ball the framing is the disk framing, since local surgery must add a free summand to first homology. Carry the framing in the isotopy and identify the outgoing face of the local zero surgery with $D^2_+\times S^1$ in the new $S^2\times S^1$ summand. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, Proposition 3.2 and its proof, pp. 2313–2314](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=9)

For the inverse marking, let $o_c:D^2\times S^1\to Y_{abc}$ be the outgoing face of the actual $c$ handle, let $\tau(z,v)=(v,z)$ swap the factors of its standard domain, and set $c^*=\gamma o_c\tau$. Its inverse on its image is $\tau^{-1}o_c^{-1}\gamma^{-1}$. The local unfolding of the previous paragraph gives a diffeomorphism $\lambda:Y_{abc}\to\partial Q_0$ to the boundary of a standard rank-two body $Q_0$, with the new summand distinguished. It carries $o_c$ to the framed outgoing face in that summand, by the complement identification and the handle model. The change of boundary marking $\lambda\gamma^{-1}$ extends to $H:Q\to Q_0$ by the Laudenbach–Poénaru boundary extension. In $Q_0$, $Hc^*$ meets the belt sphere $S^2\times\{z_0\}$ of the distinguished 1-handle once transversely, and misses the belt sphere of the other 1-handle. The comparison between the two upper traces is $H$ on $Q$ and identity on the dual handle coordinates. Its inverse uses $H^{-1}$ and identity on those coordinates. [Laudenbach–Poénaru, proof of Theorem A, pp. 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=6)

Take the distinguished 1-handle last in the upper body viewed from the top and cancel it with $c^*$ by Milnor's Theorem 5.4. Fix incoming collar coordinates for the product flow on the cancelling cobordism. Composing the upper trace comparison with the product identification gives $\Xi:Q\cup c^*\to Q_1$, where $Q_1=S^1\times D^3$ and the inverse uses reverse product flow followed by the inverse trace comparison. If $\delta:Y_{ab}\to\partial(Q\cup c^*)$ is the actual boundary map from reversing the surgery on $c$, the returned assembly is
\[
W_{ab}\ \cup_{\,\partial\Xi\,\delta}\ Q_1,
\qquad (\partial\Xi\,\delta)^{-1}=\delta^{-1}(\partial\Xi)^{-1}.
\]
This is a diffeomorphism of the closed assemblies by identity on $W_{ab}$ and $\Xi$ on the upper side, with the indicated inverse. Turning the cancellation forward removes a 2/3 pair and gives $(1,1,2,1,1)$. The two tests from the remaining pair are still $Y_a$ with $a$ attached first and $Y_b$ in the alternate order. The latter is the level from that reordering of the same marked pair. If either cut is PSC-dominated the existing mixed recognition theorem gives $S^4$; without either witness, the upper cancellation alone gives the strict $3\to2$ decrease. At no stage is a filling of $Y_a$ or $Y_{ab}$ substituted for the actual lower trace. [Milnor, Theorem 5.4, p. 48](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/hcobord.pdf#page=53) · [Mixed recognition including both orders](../../../04_hypersurface_immersions/02_two_handle_recognition/manuscript/recognition_from_marked_morse_heights.tex)

## The zero-winding second prefix

For $Y_{bc}$ the ordinary prefix is $U_0\cup b\cup c$ with zero $3\times3$ linking matrix. Its comparison filling, obtained by three ordinary 2-handles on a ball, is simply connected with $H_2=\mathbb Z^3$ and zero intersection pairing. As above the boundary inclusion induces an integral isomorphism on $H_2$. A PSC-domination hypothesis gives three $S^1\times S^2$ factors and a residual sum of signed Poincaré spheres. Their three disjoint factor spheres form a basis, so compressing with three auxiliary 3-handles gives a smooth contractible filling of the residual sum. Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee Proposition 4.2 now gives $Y_{bc}\cong\#_3(S^1\times S^2)$. This cut type itself supplies the converse PSC witness. [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, Proposition 2.2, Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1)

The remaining forward handle $a$ takes the identified rank-three boundary to rank two. Its dual $a^*=\gamma o_a\tau$ gives the reverse surgery from $\#_2$ to $\#_3$. Write $X=Y_{bc}\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu a$ and let $m,\ell$ be the meridian and parallel specifying the actual surgery, respectively. Then $H_1(Y_{abc})=H_1(X)/\langle\ell\rangle$ and $H_1(Y_{bc})=H_1(X)/\langle m\rangle$ by eliminating the solid-torus core generator in Mayer–Vietoris. The rank difference one makes $m$ torsion and $\ell$ of infinite order. The torsion of $H_1(X)$ injects into $H_1(X)/\langle\ell\rangle$ because $\ell$ has infinite order. Since the quotient is free, $m=0$ in $H_1(X)$. The dual core $a^*$ is represented up to sign by $m$ after filling along $\ell$, so it is null homologous in $\partial Q$. This prevents $a^*$ from meeting any upper belt sphere exactly once. Also $H_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z^3$ and $[a]$, represented by $\ell$ after filling along $m$, is primitive.

The auxiliary filling comparison leaves open the smooth type of the actual marked $P\cup b\cup c$. For example, a sufficient additional input for a lower cancellation would be a relative diffeomorphism to $P\natural(S^2\times D^2)\natural(S^2\times D^2)$ carrying the actual $a$-tube to one that meets the belt sphere of the displayed $P$ once in a region disjoint from the two split attachments. One could then return that diagram with the transported upper map, reorder, and cancel. The boundary identification and the primitive homology class do not supply this marked relative diffeomorphism. The unstabilized zero-winding test stops at that distinction.

## An explicit diagram with both spectator tubes and both closures

Use the product meridian $a=S^1\times\{p\}$ in $\partial P=S^1\times S^2$ from the [marked control](MARKED_CONTROL_AND_CLUSTER.md). Fix its product framed tube and orient its winding to be $+1$. Let $G:W_a\to D^4$ be the rounded radial comparison of that control, and $L=\partial G$. Choose two small disjoint balls $D_b,D_c$ in a common coordinate ball in the untouched patch of $Y_a\cong S^3$, obtained via $L$ from the complement of the $a$-tube. Each contains the round disk of radius $r$ in its own coordinates, with framed tube
\[
e_j(z,(v_1,v_2))=((r+\epsilon v_1)\operatorname{Re} z,
(r+\epsilon v_1)\operatorname{Im} z,\epsilon v_2),\qquad j=b,c,
\]
where $z\in S^1$, $v\in D^2$ and $0<2\epsilon<r$. The ball is large enough to contain the displayed tube. The radial push-off gives the disk longitude. The initial lower tubes are the pullbacks by $L$ on the untouched patch, so both have winding zero. Work with the split diagram first and then slide $c$ over $b$ once in that patch, using the band along a simple arc between the balls and a framed parallel $b^+$ just outside $e_b$. The endpoints meet the disk parallels with no twist. The moved framed tube $e_{c'}$ is the framed band sum $c\#_{\mathrm{band}} b^+$. These choices leave $a,b$ and the row $(1,0,0)$ fixed. The product parallel and the band fix the framing of $c'$.

In the split diagram, doing $b,c$ surgery replaces $D_b,D_c$ by punctured copies of $S^1\times S^2$. Mark each copy by $S^1\times(D^2_+\cup_{S^1}D^2_-)$, with $D^2_+$ the outgoing face disk. Use the exterior disk and product boundary coordinates of the [split two-handle control](MARKED_CONTROL_AND_CLUSTER.md), punctured in its exterior away from the two faces' common torus and the distinguished factor sphere. Choose $Q_0$ as two $S^1\times D^3$ bodies joined across boundary disks, using the same twice-punctured $S^3$ as the connecting part of its boundary. The marking $\gamma_0$ sends the two surgery local models to the two summands of $\partial Q_0$ and is the chosen identification on the connecting part. Its inverse uses the two local markings backwards and the inverse on the connecting part. Each product sphere meets the corresponding belt circle once. This is a closed marked example with both upper 3-handles specified.

After $a,b$ both $e_c$ and $e_{c'}$ lie in $Y_{ab}$ in the actual survivor region. Here $b^+$ bounds the disk $D^2_+\times\{z_0\}$ together with a short exterior annulus, and its parallel push-offs bound parallel disks. Compress the band detour across that disk to obtain a framed ambient isotopy $J_t$ of $Y_{ab}$ carrying $e_c$ to $e_{c'}$ with $J_0=\mathrm{id}$. One can extend the moving-tube velocity with a cutoff and integrate it to obtain $J_t$, choosing the endpoint parametrization of $e_{c'}$ by this extension. A new-to-old diffeomorphism $F:W_{abc'}\to W_{abc}$ is identity through $W_{ab}$ and on the last handle coordinates, with $(y,t)\mapsto(J_{\eta(t)}^{-1}y,t)$ on the collar before the last handle. Here $\eta$ is zero near the incoming end and one near the outgoing end. The inverse uses $J_{\eta(t)}$ and the same identities. The slid closing is $\gamma'=\gamma_0\partial F$ and its inverse is $(\partial F)^{-1}\gamma_0^{-1}$. The closed comparison with the split diagram is $F\cup\mathrm{id}_{Q_0}$, whose inverse is $F^{-1}\cup\mathrm{id}_{Q_0}$.

In particular the dual tube of $c'$ is $\gamma'o_{c'}\tau=\gamma_0o_c\tau$ because $F$ is identity in the last handle coordinates. The dual tube of $b$ is $\gamma'o_b\tau$, with $o_b$ the face surviving in the $c'$ outgoing exterior. On that face $\partial F$ is the survivor map $J_1^{-1}$ into the split $c$ exterior, hence the inverse tube marking is $\tau^{-1}o_b^{-1}(\gamma')^{-1}$ on its image. Under the inverse upper cancellation the surviving $a,b$ attachments are the given meridian and $b$, with closing map $\partial\Xi\delta$ as above. The $a,b$ prefix of this example is $S^1\times S^2$ and its PSC witness is the degree-one inverse product marking. The alternative two-zero prefix $b,c'$ is $\#_3(S^1\times S^2)$: after $b$ the same band compression uses the $b$ face in the original untouched lower patch together with the original $S^1\times S^2$ summand. This particular diagram has the extra relative standardization of the two-zero lower piece, supplied by undoing the slide. The upper homology obstruction for $a^*$ still applies.

Alternatively cancel the lower $1/2$ pair $P,a$. The two surviving framed embeddings in $S^3$ are precisely $e_b,e_{c'}$ by the chosen pullback and $L$. Put $V'=D^4\cup(e_b,e_{c'})$ and let $\Psi:V'\to W_{abc'}$ be $G^{-1}$ on the ball and identity on both handle models, using the common collar. Its inverse is $G$ on the first piece and identity on the models. Thus the actual one-sided closing is $\gamma'\partial\Psi$ with inverse $(\partial\Psi)^{-1}(\gamma')^{-1}$. The two framed components are carried by the inverse of the same $c$-over-$b$ slide to the split zero-framed unlink, since the band and both framings were defined inside the unchanged patch. That slide has the same trace comparison $F$ in the ball coordinates. The returned one-sided lower trace is $\natural_2(S^2\times D^2)$ and the actual closing to $Q_0$ gives $S^4$ by Laudenbach–Poénaru Theorem A. This identifies the closed type and checks the strict counts $3\to2$ from either end on the control diagram. [Framed slide convention, Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, §2](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf) · [Laudenbach–Poénaru, Theorem A](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf)

## A rank-one witness with a failed first cut

Apply the same split last 2/3 stabilization to Isoshima's Figure 37 at $n=1$. Here $a$ is the long component and $b$ is its small zero-framed meridian in a ball disjoint from the dotted circle. Choose the stabilizing $c$ tube and its disk in another ball in the surviving exterior of both original tubes. Keep the full lower trace of the published double and its upper $S^1\times D^3$ marking on the inherited part. The last zero surgery adds the product summand, with its ending $S^1\times D^3$ body joined across a boundary disk as in the split construction. In this three-middle presentation $Y_a=\Sigma(2,5,7)$ up to orientation. It has nontrivial fundamental group and bounds the smooth contractible Mazur piece, so PSC domination would contradict the winding-one recognition argument in the [previous result](POSITIVE_SCALAR_INTERMEDIATE_CUTS.md). The marked $Y_{ab}$ is the original full outgoing level of the double, so it is $S^1\times S^2$ with its product PSC witness. Reversing the local stabilization gives the actual mixed Mazur diagram, and its alternate $b$-first cut is $\#_2(S^1\times S^2)$ by split zero surgery on $b$ alone. Thus the rank-one criterion can remove the last pair with a genuinely failed $a$-first test. These are topological PSC witnesses on the specified cut types. A measurement on a supplied physical four-metric would require its own marked comparison. [Isoshima, arXiv:2403.17356v2, Figure 37 and Theorem 3.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17356#page=35) · [Akbulut–Kirby, §1 and Theorem 2](https://math.berkeley.edu/~kirby/papers/Akbulut%20and%20Kirby%20-%20Mazur%20manifolds%20-%20MR0544597.pdf)

After the first readback the [free-group and knot argument](../../reviews/LAST_HANDLE_READER.md) identifies the null-homologous dual attaching knot at the last pure prefix as an unknot in a ball. This standardizes the actual upper piece. The [successive-prefix argument](../../reviews/ZERO_WINDING_TOWER_READER.md) determines the lower piece when the preceding zero-winding prefix is also certified. The resulting recognition uses interior sphere surgery.
