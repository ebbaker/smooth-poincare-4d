## From the zero-framed longitude to two caps

Let \(Q^*\) be the core of the solid torus of the stationary zero filling.
In the framed \(Q\) collar, the zero parallel \(L\) at the prescribed
azimuth bounds a disk in the filled slab. The disk is the radial annulus
from \(L\) to \(\lambda_Q\), followed by the filling meridian disk. Choose
the narrow carrier \(V=\nu L\) so that the part of this disk in \(V\) is
a radial annulus from the core to the appropriate longitude on
\(\partial V\). The portion outside \(\operatorname{int} V\), denoted \(D_V\),
is therefore a proper disk with one transverse intersection with \(Q^*\).
The longitude on \(\partial V\) is the native zero longitude. Indeed a
parallel of \(L\) taken in the framed annulus is a zero parallel, as can
also be checked in the unfilled \(S^3\) capping of the two slab faces.
Take the product neighborhood of \(D_V\) so that its two parallel copies
are again transverse to \(Q^*\) and each meet it once.

Use the standard Whitehead link \(J_{\mathrm{st}}\cup C\). The carrier
\(V_{\mathrm{st}}=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu C\) is an unknotted
solid torus with a zero-framed core. Use the twice-punctured disk bounding
the Whitehead component in Thurston's example. In the standard diagram
the component with no self-crossing can be taken flat, with its spanning
disk just inside that curve. The two passages of the other component
enter and leave on opposite sides of this disk. Shrinking \(\nu C\) at
these passages gives two disks of intersection with the spanning disk,
each a meridian of \(\nu C\). Their boundary slopes on \(V_{\mathrm{st}}\)
are its zero longitude, since its meridian and longitude are interchanged
with those of \(\nu C\) in the standard genus-one splitting of \(S^3\).
The two induced orientations are opposite, since the Whitehead linking
number is zero. Removing the interiors of these small meridian disks
leaves the actual planar three-boundary surface in \(V_{\mathrm{st}}\),
with outer boundary \(J_{\mathrm{st}}\).
[Thurston, *A Norm for the Homology of 3-Manifolds*, §2, Example 1 and
Figure 2, pp. 108–109](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/thurston/norm/norm.pdf)

Carry this surface into \(V\) by the untwisted embedding of the frozen
diagram. Its two inner cuffs are parallel native zero longitudes of \(V\).
On the boundary torus carry their ordered pair to the two parallel
boundaries of \(D_V\) by a collar isotopy. The two caps lie on the other
side of that boundary torus, so they meet the Whitehead surface only
at their boundaries. Their orientations are set by the cuffs. Round
the seams to obtain a smooth embedded disk \(\Delta\) with
\(\partial\Delta=J\). It has geometric intersection two with \(Q^*\),
one positive and one negative. The only core intersections are in the
two copies of \(D_V\). A twist of the pattern chart could change the
longitude on each cuff to a different slope. The native chart is used
here to make the two fillings actual disks.

The two azimuths of the double insertion use disjoint radial annuli and
distinct meridian disks of the stationary filling, with disjoint buffers.
By the support assumptions of the issue the access fingers and their
product rectangles miss the other disk supports, the other moving arcs
and the lower GST mouth. Thus one disk construction is available at
each azimuth, supported away from the other attaching data. These
support choices are relative to the actual carrier tori and their
framing charts.

## Geometric minimum for the marked completion

A disk for the marked \(J\) chord with zero core intersections would give
an embedded spanning disk for the capped \(J\) disjoint from \(Q\). Take
a sufficiently small tube of \(Q^*\) missing the disk. The rest of the
filling torus is a product collar on the old stationary tube boundary.
Absorb this collar, fixing the moving arcs and the capped face chords.
Cap the slab faces by balls to read the disk in the ordinary unfilled
\(S^3\) diagram. For the same test on the opened pattern, restore the
mouth by a disk in its small product ball. A bridge disc has interior
away from the marked moving tube. Thus its spanning disk in the capped
diagram would split \(J\) from \(Q\).

Here the capped joint pair \(J\cup Q\) is nonsplit. The companion torus
\(\partial V\) is incompressible in the complement. On its inside,
\(V\setminus \operatorname{int}\nu J\) is the Whitehead-link exterior,
with its torus incompressible by the Whitehead link calculation. For
example, the ideal octahedron gives the complete hyperbolic exterior
with incompressible cusp tori. On the outside a compression would also
compress the boundary of the knot exterior of \(L\) in \(S^3\). Its
boundary would be the zero longitude by first homology and would give
a spanning disk for \(L\), which is a square-knot parallel. These
two side tests imply global incompressibility by van Kampen and the
injection for the amalgamated product, or by an innermost disk.
[Thurston, *The Geometry and Topology of Three-Manifolds*, electronic
edition 1.1, §3.3 and the opening of Chapter 4](https://library.slmath.org/nonmsri/gt3m/PDF/Thurston-gt3m.pdf)

If a splitting sphere existed, put it transverse to \(\partial V\).
An innermost circle on the sphere bounds a disk disjoint from the
torus and is inessential on the torus by incompressibility. Choose
an inessential circle innermost on the torus and do sphere surgery
along its disk. At least one of the two resulting spheres still
separates \(J\) from \(Q\), by mod-two intersection with a joining
path. This decreases the intersection count. A splitting sphere
inside \(V\) would put \(J\) in a ball there, splitting the standard
Whitehead link on re-embedding \(V\). A sphere outside \(V\) would
split \(L\) from \(Q\). The latter is impossible because the radial
annulus from \(Q\) to \(L\) meets a splitting sphere in an essential
circle. An arc joining the annulus boundaries has odd intersection
with the sphere, which forces such a circle, and it would place the
knot type of \(Q\) on a sphere. This proves the claimed local
nonsplitting.

Exactly one core intersection is also impossible for the marked
completion. After removing the smaller tube of \(Q^*\), a
once-punctured spanning disk is an annulus. The two boundary classes
in the unfilled capped square-knot exterior would be conjugate up to
orientation. They are the capped \(J\) and \(\lambda_Q\), since the
meridian of the core tube is the native filling slope on the old
stationary tube. But \(J\) has winding zero in \(V\), hence trivial
class in \(\pi_1(V)\) and in the ambient square-knot exterior.
The longitude \(\lambda_Q\) is nontrivial there. If it were trivial,
Dehn's lemma would give its embedded proper disk, and an annulus
from the longitude to the knot would make \(Q\) unknotted. Combining
these two lower bounds with \(\Delta\) gives minimum geometric
intersection two for the fixed local Whitehead completion at each
azimuth. The signs of the two exhibited punctures sum to zero.
[Papakyriakopoulos, Dehn's lemma, p. 169](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf#page=2)

An algebraic check for this fixed completion uses the square-knot
fibration. Its exterior group is \(F_4\rtimes\langle m_Q\rangle\),
with \(F_4\) the group of the once-punctured genus-two fiber. The
longitude is represented, up to conjugacy and inversion, by the
fiber boundary word
\([a_1,b_1][a_2,b_2]\). In the central abelian quotient
\(\Gamma_2(F_4)/\Gamma_3(F_4)\cong\bigwedge^2\mathbb Z^4\) its class
is \(a_1\wedge b_1+a_2\wedge b_2\), which is nonzero. Conjugation by
the fiber group is trivial on this quotient and the monodromy
preserves the boundary class. Cutting a punctured disk along
paths to all its small meridians expresses its outer word as a
product of conjugates of \(\lambda_Q\) with the puncture signs.
All those factors lie in \(\Gamma_2(F_4)\) and have the same
unsigned class there. Since the outer \(J\) word is trivial, the
sum of the puncture signs vanishes. This applies whenever the
disk is carried to the same marked \(J\) completion in the capped
square-knot exterior. Changing a four-port face chord can change
the outer word, so the calculation has that completion as a
hypothesis.
[GST, fibered square knot, pp. 2312 and 2323](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=8)
