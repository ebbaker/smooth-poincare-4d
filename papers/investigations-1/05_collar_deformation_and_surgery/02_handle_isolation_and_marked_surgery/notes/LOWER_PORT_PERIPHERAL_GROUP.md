## Capping the drilled slab

Work smoothly and orient the ambient three-manifolds by the slab
orientation. Write \(W=S^2\times[-1/2,1/2]\), with faces \(S_-\) and \(S_+\),
stationary tube \(\nu Q\) in the interior and proper returns \(R_-,R_+\) on
their respective faces. Use disjoint product tubes for the returns,
meeting each face in the two marked normal disks. On removing the
relative interiors of all three tubes and rounding corners, call the
connected exterior \(E\). Its boundary is \(T_Q\sqcup T_-\sqcup T_+\).
Each \(T_\pm\) is the union of the twice-punctured face \(F_\pm\) and the
side annulus of the return tube, joined at their two boundary circles.
The latter has product coordinates \(S^1\times[0,1]\) with meridian
\(S^1\times\{t\}\). Let \(c_\pm\) be the marked face chords.

Cap the two spherical faces of \(W\) by separate standard balls in
the slab's sphere coordinates, extending each return by a cap arc
parallel to its chord. The cap can be chosen as
\((D^2\times[0,1],\{0\}\times[0,1])\) relative to its marked
boundary-parallel disk. Extend the return tube by
\(D^2_\epsilon\times[0,1]\) in this chart. The cap exterior is then
\(S^1\times[\epsilon,1]\times[0,1]\). Its attaching annulus is the product
of \(S^1\) and the three consecutive outer sides of the rectangle in the
last two coordinates. The other annulus is the product of \(S^1\) and
the remaining side. After rounding, this pair is an annular collar
attached along \(F_\pm\). Absorbing the two collars into the boundary
identifies \(E\) with the capped exterior, fixed near \(T_Q\). Under this
identification the face annulus is replaced by the complementary
annulus of the cap tube, the waist circle still gives the meridian, and
the truncated face chord is carried to the corresponding interval on
the cap tube. These properties follow by taking the collar in the
rectangle factor and keeping the \(S^1\) coordinate and seam circles.
This specifies the transfer of a longitude drawn using the chord as
well as the transfer of the meridians.

The capped ambient manifold is \(S^3\). In the product mouth chart each
return has a rectangle joining its two rails to the marked chord and
to the short segment deleted at that mouth. The transverse segments
between the two rails are embedded and disjoint at different product
levels. At the stationary mouth the product box points out of the
parallel's radial collar. The two access boxes are disjoint and their
interiors miss \(Q\) and the opposite return. Attach the boundary-parallel
disk in each cap ball to the corresponding rectangle along the face
chord. Pushing the completed detours across these disks restores the two
deleted segments by an ambient isotopy fixing the stationary tube.
Consequently the capped exterior is the exterior of the actual
\((Q,J,V_4)\) in \(S^3\), with \(J\) the zero parallel of \(Q\). This argument
tracks the positions relative to \(Q\) and to each other throughout.
On the upper torus it transports the capped meridian and the zero
longitude of the published \(V_4\) component. The stationary filling
slope is the given \(\lambda_Q\), the oriented zero longitude. The lower
strip slope is identified below.

## The lower longitude and its bridge disk

In the zero-framed chart around \(Q\) join the marked longitude on
\(T_Q\) to \(J\) by the constant-azimuth radial annulus. The observed
lower return follows the outer edge except at the short mouth
segment. Join the mouth rectangle along that segment and round the
seams. This gives an embedded annulus from \(\lambda_Q\) to
\(R_-\cup c_-\) whose interior misses both returns and the cut faces.
Drilling the lower return clips its edge to a section of the tube side
annulus and truncates the chord at the two end disks. This gives a
proper annulus from \(\lambda_Q\) to the lower strip-push-off \(\gamma_-\)
on \(T_-\). The product collar of the annulus at \(R_-\) supplies the radial
strip used for clipping. After capping the faces, the same strip slope
is the parallel-annulus push-off on \(T_J\). Its self-linking with \(J\) in
\(S^3\) is \(\operatorname{lk}(J,Q)=0\), since the annulus carries the
push-off toward \(Q\) in the complement of \(J\). Thus \(\gamma_-\) transfers
to the zero longitude \(\lambda_J\). Orient \(J\) along the parallel so that
the two annulus boundary loops, taken in their longitudinal directions,
are freely homotopic. In \(\pi_1(E)\) their based representatives are
conjugate. [Radial collar and clipping coordinates](CUT_TANGLE_RECOVERY.md#peripheral-annulus-and-a-local-band)

For homology use positively oriented meridians and integral
coefficients. The linking pairing with the three link components
identifies \(H_1(E)\) with
\(\mathbb Z\langle[\mu_Q],[\mu_J],[\mu_4]\rangle\). In particular a zero
longitude has homology coordinates given by the linking numbers with
the other components. GST constructs \(L_{4,1}=(Q,V_4)\) with the
two-component framed surgery giving \(\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\), so its
linking number is zero by Proposition 2.2. Parallelism in the
complement of \(V_4\) gives
\(\operatorname{lk}(J,V_4)=\operatorname{lk}(Q,V_4)=0\). All three
longitudes therefore vanish in first homology. Filling \(T_Q\) along
\(\lambda_Q\) takes the homology quotient by \([\lambda_Q]\), so the
three meridians are still a basis. On the lower torus a class
\(p\mu_J+q\lambda_J\) maps to \(p[\mu_J]\) both before and after filling.
A disk boundary must vanish under this map, and a slope is primitive
up to sign. The sole possible slope is therefore \(\lambda_J\).
A bridge loop uses the tube side once, so has geometric intersection
one with \(\mu_J\). The actual \(\lambda_J\) has this property and the
specified chord representative.
[GST, Proposition 2.2, p. 2310 and §7, Figure 9 and the paragraph following Question Two](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf)

Write \(E_f=E(\lambda_Q)\) and denote the core of the attached solid
torus by \(Q^*\). The proper annulus capped by a filling meridian disk
is an embedded disk in \(E_f\) with boundary \(\gamma_-\). It meets \(Q^*\)
transversely once. Restoring the radial strip makes it a bridge disk
for \(R_-\cup c_-\), with interior disjoint from \(R_+\) and from the
cut faces. In particular \(\lambda_J=1\) in \(\pi_1(E_f)\).

Conversely, if a bridge disk for this chord avoided \(Q^*\), attach the
boundary-parallel chord disk in the lower capping ball to obtain a
spanning disk for \(J\) in the capped filled manifold, still avoiding
\(Q^*\). After choosing a smaller tube about the core disjoint from the
disk, the region between the original filling boundary and that tube
is a torus collar. Absorb it back into the unfilled exterior by a
collar diffeomorphism supported near \(T_Q\) and fixed on \(J\). Meridional
refilling of \(Q\) now gives a spanning disk for \(J\) in the original
\(S^3\). The square knot admits no such disk. For example, a trefoil's
Wirtinger generators satisfy \(aba=bab\) and map onto \(\mathfrak S_3\)
by sending the two meridians to \((12)\) and \((23)\). The mirror has the
same quotient. In a connected sum, cutting along the decomposing
meridional annulus gives the amalgam of the two meridians by van
Kampen, so these quotients combine with the chosen meridians sent to
the same transposition. The square knot has noncyclic group and thus
is nontrivial. Every transverse bridge disk has at least one geometric
intersection with \(Q^*\), so the displayed disk realizes the minimum.
The same obstruction to an unfilled spanning disk implies
\(\lambda_J\ne1\) in \(\pi_1(E)\) by Dehn's lemma and the longitude strip
in \(\nu J\).

## Peripheral criterion for another marked cut

In a compact connected oriented smooth cut manifold, drill an interior
stationary tube and the product tubes of disjoint properly embedded
returns with paired endpoints on their faces. Give the designated
return a marked chord. The chord is embedded, and outside its
end disks its interior lies in the punctured face. Suppose the
designated return gives a boundary torus \(T_-\), formed from the
punctured face and the tube side annulus as above, and the stationary
torus \(T_Q\) is distinct from it. Specify a primitive stationary
filling slope \(\alpha_Q\). Specify the bridge curve \(\gamma\) on
\(T_-\) as the truncated chord joined to a section of the tube side,
with the section and chord joined at their common endpoints and
corners rounded. This requirement fixes the strip framing being
tested. Let \(i_-\) and \(i_Q\) be the two peripheral homomorphisms to
\(\pi_1(E)\), using paths to one basepoint. The criterion for a bridge
disk with that strip and the specified chord, disjoint in its
interior from the other returns and cut faces after stationary
filling, is
\[
 i_-(\gamma)\in
 \langle\!\langle\,i_Q(\alpha_Q)\,\rangle\!\rangle_{\pi_1(E)} .
\]
The criterion is independent of the base paths. Van Kampen gives the
displayed normal-closure quotient as the group of the filled exterior.
Truncating a bridge disk in product position at the specified strip
gives a proper disk there. Tubes about other returns can be taken
sufficiently small, as their cores miss a compact bridge disk and
their distinct face collars miss its boundary. Radial collar
identifications compare the exterior after shrinking a tube.
Conversely, a null word gives a singular disk bounded by the
simple boundary curve. Use the boundary collar to make the outer
annulus embedded and push the remaining map into the interior.
Dehn's lemma gives an embedded disk on that same curve. Applying the
PL statement and smoothing relative to the collar gives a smooth
proper disk. Attach the radial strip in the return tube to recover
the prescribed return and chord. If the disk boundary was read as a
slope, a boundary isotopy first puts it on the actual curve \(\gamma\).
The rest of the disk lies in the drilled exterior, giving the
required disjointness. [Papakyriakopoulos, p. 169, orientable
triangulated form of Dehn's lemma](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf)

A finite triangulation of the marked exterior with combinatorial
representatives of \(\alpha_Q\) and \(\gamma\) gives a terminating test
for this loop. Use a maximal tree in the one-skeleton and the triangle
relators to present \(\pi_1(E)\) and read the two peripheral words.
Adding the stationary word as a relator gives a presentation of the
fundamental group of a compact 3-manifold. Apply the uniform word
problem to the bridge word in this presentation. In the proof by
residual finiteness one enumerates normal-relator proofs of triviality
and, in parallel, homomorphisms to finite groups detecting
nontriviality. Exactly the appropriate search eventually succeeds.
This is an existence decision for the specified bridge disk. The
sharp intersection count for this fixture uses the parallel annulus
and the square-knot obstruction. The triangulated procedure is a
termination statement for a marked finite input.
[Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton, *Decision problems for 3-manifolds and
their fundamental groups*, Geom. Topol. Monogr. 19 (2015),
§4.1, Theorems 4.1–4.2 and Lemma 4.3, pp. 217–218](https://msp.org/gtm/2015/19-1/gtm-v19-n1-p08-s.pdf#page=17)
