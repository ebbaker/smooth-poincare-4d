Let $Q$ be a smooth stationary knot in the interior of a ball in the oriented $Y_1=S^1\times S^2$. Assume the knot obtained by completing that ball to $S^3$ is nontrivial. Let $K$ be a smooth oriented circle disjoint from $Q$. Work in the unfilled exterior of $Q$, with $K$ retained as a curve. The conclusions use the isotopy class of $K$ in that exterior and are independent of the integral tube framings.

**Proposition.** Every smooth embedded nonseparating sphere $S$ in $Y_1$ disjoint from $Q$ yields the same cyclic stable-letter length of the free homotopy class of $K$ in $Y_1\setminus Q$. Suppose a displayed sphere sees $K$ transversely in four points, and the resulting based word for $K$, up to conjugacy, is
\[
 w=a t b t^{-1}c t d t^{-1},\qquad a,b,c,d\in H\setminus\{1\},
\]
where $H$ is the cut vertex group and $t$ is the once-crossing generator for that sphere. Then the minimum transverse intersection count with $K$ over **all** nonseparating spheres disjoint from $Q$ is exactly four. The same minimum holds allowing $K$ to vary by isotopy in the exterior of $Q$.

## The vertex subgroup

Use a closed tube $\nu Q$ small enough to miss the finitely many spheres and curves being compared. The compact exterior $E=Y_1\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu Q$ is $E_Q\#Y_1$, where $E_Q$ is the exterior of the ball-completed knot in $S^3$. Indeed, the boundary of the local ball identifies the two punctured summands. Use a basepoint on the unchanged torus and put $G=\pi_1 E$ and $H_Q=\pi_1 E_Q\le G$. Van Kampen gives $G=H_Q*\mathbb Z$. The exterior $E_Q$ is irreducible since a sphere in its interior bounds a ball on the side missing $\nu Q$ in $S^3$. For the group, Papakyriakopoulos's Theorem 2 says geometric splitting and nontrivial free-product decomposition are equivalent for a link in $S^3$. A single knot is geometrically nonsplit, so $H_Q$ is freely indecomposable. Theorem 3(i) gives that $H_Q$ is noncyclic because the knot is nontrivial. [Papakyriakopoulos, *On Dehn's Lemma and the Asphericity of Knots*, PNAS 43 (1957), p. 170, Theorems 2 and 3](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf#page=3)

For any $S$ as in the proposition, cut $E$ at $S$ and cap the two sphere boundaries by balls, producing $E_S$. The cut exterior is connected: the complement of $S$ in $Y_1$ is connected, and paths through the removed tube can be rerouted in its boundary. Adding the two balls leaves the fundamental group of the cut unchanged. Rejoining the two sphere faces gives a trivial-edge HNN extension. Thus, with the basepoint kept on the torus, the inclusion of the cut gives $H_S=\pi_1 E_S\le G$ and $G=H_S*\langle s\rangle$, with $s$ crossing the product collar of $S$ once. Filling $E_S$ by the original meridian gives the closed manifold obtained by cutting $Y_1$ at $S$ and capping. Adding back the handle expresses $Y_1$ as that closed manifold summed with $Y_1$. Primeness of $Y_1$ forces the meridian-filled $E_S$ to be $S^3$. Consequently $E_S$ is a knot exterior in $S^3$, hence irreducible by the same ball argument, and $H_S$ is freely indecomposable by Theorem 2. The primeness argument takes place in oriented PL dimension three after triangulating the smooth cut. [Milnor, *A Unique Decomposition Theorem for 3-Manifolds*, Amer. J. Math. 84 (1962), Theorem 1 and Lemmas 1–2, journal reproduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/856462048/Milnor-UniqueDecompositionTheorem-1962)

The Grushko uniqueness step has a direct Kurosh proof here. In $G=H_S*\langle s\rangle$ the freely indecomposable noncyclic subgroup $H_Q$ lies in a conjugate of $H_S$: the Kurosh subgroup decomposition has only one nontrivial factor for such a subgroup, and that factor cannot come from the cyclic part. This also shows that $H_S$ is noncyclic. Applying the subgroup theorem in $G=H_Q*\mathbb Z$ gives $H_S$ inside a conjugate of $H_Q$. The common based meridian lies in both subgroups and is nonidentity, since it generates $H_1(E_Q;\mathbb Z)$. A free factor is malnormal by reduced normal form: a conjugator outside the factor leaves an outside syllable in the conjugate of any nontrivial element of the factor. Since $H_Q$ and $H_S$ contain the common meridian, malnormality makes each conjugate in the two inclusions equal to the corresponding original factor. Therefore $H_S=H_Q$ with these basings. With arbitrary basings the conclusion is equality up to conjugation. [Baer and Levi, *Freie Produkte und ihre Untergruppen*, Compos. Math. 3 (1936), p. 392, subgroup theorem, and pp. 397–398, refinement and uniqueness](https://www.numdam.org/article/CM_1936__3__391_0.pdf#page=3)

## Changing the generator of the cyclic complement

**Relative basis lemma.** If $G=H*\langle t\rangle$ and $H$ together with $s$ generates $G$, then $s=x t^\epsilon y$ for $x,y\in H$ and $\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}$.

Strip the initial and terminal $H$ syllables of the reduced $t$-normal form of $s$ to obtain $s=x r y$. The remaining word $r$ begins and ends in $\langle t\rangle$, and $H$ together with $r$ still generates $G$. The quotient killing $H$ is infinite cyclic, so the image of $r$ generates it and $r$ has infinite order.

An infinite-order reduced word starting and ending in the same free factor has every nonzero power starting and ending in that same factor, with at least as many syllables. For positive powers prove this by induction on the syllable count. The one-syllable statement follows from infinite order. If the final syllable times the initial syllable is nonidentity, concatenation merges exactly one pair at each join. Otherwise delete the inverse end syllables to write the word as $u v u^{-1}$. The middle word $v$ has infinite order and starts and ends in the other factor, so the induction applies to $v^n$ and restoring $u,u^{-1}$ adds two syllables. For negative powers use the inverse word.

Suppose $r$ contains an $H$ syllable. Each $r^n$ for $n\ne 0$ then has an interior $H$ syllable. Any expression in $H$ and powers of $r$ can be written $h_0r^{n_1}h_1\cdots r^{n_j}h_j$ with all powers nonzero and all intermediate $h_i\ne 1$. Expanding the powers yields a reduced word in the original free factors, since the powers start and end in $\langle t\rangle$. For $j>0$ the expanded word contains an interior $H$ syllable. For $j=0$ the expression is in $H$. Neither case can represent $t$, contradicting generation. Hence $r=t^k$ and its image in $G/\langle\!\langle H\rangle\!\rangle$ gives $k=\pm1$.

For $G=H*\langle t\rangle$, write $L_t(g)$ for the sum of absolute exponents of the $\langle t\rangle$ syllables of the reduced word. This is the least number of letters $t^{\pm1}$ in any expression with coefficients in $H$, since reduction never increases that number. The basis lemma allows one $s^{\pm1}$ for each $t^{\pm1}$ and one $t^{\pm1}$ for each $s^{\pm1}$, with coefficients in $H$. Thus $L_t(g)=L_s(g)$ for the fixed based subgroup. Passing to
\[
 \ell([g])=\min_{v\in G}L_t(vgv^{-1})
\]
makes the length independent of basing as well. This counts each traversal, so a syllable $t^k$ contributes $|k|$.

## The geometric minimum

For a given transverse sphere $S$, use a small collar that meets $K$ in crossing intervals, one per intersection. Base both sphere faces by paths in the connected cut exterior. Each collar interval contributes one $s$ or $s^{-1}$, with its endpoints joined to the chosen face basepoints by paths on the simply connected spheres. The complementary arc paths contribute elements of $H_S$. The resulting expression represents a conjugate of the oriented class of $K$. When $K$ misses $S$, the whole curve gives a conjugate in $H_S$. Thus $\#(K\pitchfork S)\ge \ell([K])$ for every eligible $S$.

For the displayed word, $w^n$ is a reduced concatenation of $n$ copies for $n\ge 1$, so $L_t(w^n)=4n$. For any conjugate $u w u^{-1}=g$, subadditivity gives $4n=L_t(u^{-1}g^nu)\le 2L_t(u)+nL_t(g)$. Letting $n\to\infty$ yields $L_t(g)\ge4$. The word $w$ attains four, and the displayed sphere attains the same geometric count. Isotopy missing $Q$ preserves the conjugacy class throughout.

For the proposed single-longitude diagram, the sharp conclusion applies once its four vertex elements are computed in this unfilled group and seen to be nonidentity. In particular a zero longitude of a nontrivial $S^3$ knot is nonidentity in its exterior group: if it were nullhomotopic, Dehn's lemma applied to its simple boundary representative would give an embedded longitude disk, which extends by an annulus in $\nu Q$ to a spanning disk for $Q$. The length estimate concerns the exterior of the fixed stationary knot before surgery. A slide or a filling that kills a segment changes the word to which the estimate applies. [Papakyriakopoulos, Dehn's lemma, p. 169](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf#page=2)
