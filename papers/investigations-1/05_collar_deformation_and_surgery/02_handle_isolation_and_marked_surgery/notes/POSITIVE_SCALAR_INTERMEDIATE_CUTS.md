Sources are the [pinned height and energy manuscripts](../../references/README.md) at c8fb08dc and the [primary passage record](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_COLLAR_SURGERY_20261126.md). The proof uses the **actual** end bodies, knots and boundary maps. Its conclusions about smooth type require no assumption on four-dimensional Weyl energy.

## The two cuts

After end reductions suppose there is one 1-handle, two 2-handles and one 3-handle. Use genuine framed slides to normalize the incidence row to $(1,0)$ and carry the upper gluing through each slide. Let $Y_a$ be the boundary after the winding-one component is attached first, $Y_b$ the boundary after the winding-zero component is attached first. These can be cuts of different auxiliary presentations on the same smooth $M$. Write $Y_2=\#_2(S^1\times S^2)$.

**Recognition test.** Suppose some closed oriented three-manifold admitting a positive scalar curvature metric maps with nonzero degree to either $Y_a$ or $Y_b$. Then $M$ is diffeomorphic to $S^4$. More precisely the domination condition for $Y_a$ is equivalent to $Y_a\cong S^3$, and the condition for $Y_b$ is equivalent to $Y_b\cong Y_2$. A disconnected source suffices when one component has nonzero degree. The target may itself be the source of the map.

By Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, Proposition 2.2, a dominated target is a connected sum of spherical space forms and $S^1\times S^2$ factors (including $S^3$). Conversely the two claimed targets admit positive scalar curvature. We next identify which summands survive on the two actual cuts. [Primary passages](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_COLLAR_SURGERY_20261126.md)

*Winding one first.* The lower piece $A$ has a 0-handle and a single 1/2 pair. The attaching circle kills the generator of its infinite cyclic fundamental group. The cellular differential in degree two is a unit. Thus $A$ is simply connected and acyclic, hence contractible, and $Y_a=\partial A$ is an integral homology sphere. By Corollary 2.3 of the same source, the only possible nontrivial summands are signed Poincaré spheres. Its Proposition 4.2 excludes every nonempty sum of those spheres as the boundary of a **smooth contractible** four-manifold, including sign-balanced sums. Hence $Y_a\cong S^3$.

*Winding zero first.* In the ordinary boundary surgery description replace the dotted unknot $U$ by $U_0$. With components ordered $U,K_a,K_b$, the symmetric linking matrix has rows $(0,1,0),(1,d,b),(0,b,c)$. Its determinant is $-c$, zero since full surgery gives the boundary of the upper 1-handlebody. Thus $U_0,K_b$ has zero two-by-two matrix, and its surgery is the actual $Y_b$ with $H_1(Y_b)=\mathbb Z^2$. The smooth **comparison filling** $V$ built by attaching those two ordinary 2-handles to a ball is simply connected and $H_2(V)=\mathbb Z^2$. Its intersection pairing is zero. In the pair sequence
\[
 H_3(V,Y_b)=H^1(V)=0 \longrightarrow H_2(Y_b)
 \longrightarrow H_2(V)\longrightarrow H_2(V,Y_b)
\]
the last map is the zero intersection map under duality. The inclusion is therefore an integral isomorphism.

In the PSC decomposition of $Y_b$ there are exactly two $S^1\times S^2$ factors. Torsion-free $H_1$ forces each spherical summand to have trivial abelianization. The spherical-space-form classification in the proof of Corollary 2.3 leaves only the oriented Poincaré spheres and $S^3$. Write the residual sum as $\Sigma$. Pull back the two disjoint cross-section spheres of the $S^1\times S^2$ factors to the actual boundary of $V$. They form a basis of its $H_2$ by the preceding isomorphism. Attach two 3-handles along them with their product normal intervals. The new relative 3-generators map isomorphically onto $H_2(V)$, so the resulting four-manifold has trivial reduced homology. It remains simply connected, hence contractible. Its boundary is $\Sigma$, since each boundary compression removes one $S^1\times S^2$ summand. Proposition 4.2 now forces $\Sigma=S^3$. This proves $Y_b\cong Y_2$. The two auxiliary 3-handles and conversion of the dot served only to identify a three-manifold. The [original mixed theorem](../../../04_hypersurface_immersions/02_two_handle_recognition/manuscript/recognition_from_marked_morse_heights.tex) applies to $M$ with its original attaching and upper maps in either order.

The winding-one proof works for the first attachment above a single lower 1-handle with any finite number of remaining marked attachments. If its boundary is $S^3$, the dual surgery knot in $S^3$ has surgery to $S^1\times S^2$. Homology forces framing zero and Gabai's Property R gives the unknot. The original knot is the surgery core, hence the product core up to the boundary coordinates of $S^1\times D^3$ (these re-markings extend over that body). It can meet the belt sphere once. Carry the residual framed tubes through the product cancellation and compose the inverse outgoing change with the upper attachment. This reduces $n_2$ by exactly one on the original smooth type. It applies in particular to a third middle tube. At a general zero-matrix $r$-component ordinary surgery, the same argument with $r$ auxiliary 3-handles identifies a PSC-dominated boundary with $\#_r(S^1\times S^2)$. Identifying that boundary alone leaves the general link trace and its recognition open.

## Three ways to test the geometry on the actual cut

1. **A whole band.** On a compact smooth marked product $B=Y\times[a,b]$ about either cut, let $d$ be the distance between the two boundary components measured within $B$ and let $\rho=\min_B R_g>0$. If
   \[
                     \rho\,d^2\ \geq\ 3\pi^2,
   \]
   then the mixed presentation is recognized. Indeed, if $Y$ admits no PSC metric, applying Räde's Corollary 2.25 in dimension four to $(\rho/12)g$ gives $\sqrt{\rho/12}\,d<\pi/2$. The strict estimate includes equality on the recognizing side. It concerns the full native metric distance. Coordinate length and width on a different cut require a geometric comparison. On a unit-volume positive Yamabe source $R_g=Y([g])$, so the test is directly $Y([g])d^2\ge3\pi^2$ there. [Räde, version and passage](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_COLLAR_SURGERY_20261126.md)

2. **One adapted slice.** If $g=f^2dt^2+h_t$ in the physical marking, $f>0$, put $K=\partial_t h/(2f)$, $H=\mathrm{tr}_h K$, and $\Delta=\mathrm{div}\nabla$ on the slice. The Gauss and mean-curvature variation equations with normal $f^{-1}\partial_t$ give
   \[
   R_h=R_g+H^2+|K|^2+2f^{-1}\partial_tH+2f^{-1}\Delta f .
   \]
   For the positive test $v=f^{1/4}$ this yields
   \[
   v^{-1}(-8\Delta_h+R_h)v=
   R_g+H^2+|K|^2+2f^{-1}\partial_t H+\tfrac32|\nabla_h\log f|^2 .
   \]
   If the right side is nonnegative everywhere and positive somewhere, pairing with the positive first eigenfunction shows the three-dimensional conformal Laplacian has positive lowest eigenvalue. Its first eigenfunction produces a PSC metric on $Y$. This test is invariant under reversing the coorientation with its time coordinate. Positive scalar curvature of the joined four-metric alone leaves the normal derivative of $H$ undetermined.

3. **A stable separator.** In such a product band it suffices to find a smooth closed two-sided stable minimal three-dimensional component $S$ whose projection to $Y$ has nonzero degree, with $R_g>0$ along $S$. Stability here is for all normal variations. The second variation and Gauss give for all smooth $u$
   \[
   \int_S(2|\nabla u|^2+R_Su^2)\ \ge\ \int_S(R_g+|K|^2)u^2 .
   \]
   Hence $-8\Delta_S+R_S$ has positive first eigenvalue and $S$ admits a PSC metric. An oriented collection representing the slice class has total projection degree one. A null-homologous small stable component, or a volume-constrained test on mean-zero functions, does not furnish this argument. Obtaining a protected separator or the width on a particular unreduced presentation is the remaining selection step.

All three criteria return to the smooth mixed theorem directly. No neck is capped and no four-dimensional positivity is transferred between metrics in these implications. On a hypothetical unresolved mixed presentation both normalized intermediate boundaries must fail to admit PSC, every compact product comparison with $\rho>0$ has $\rho d^2<3\pi^2$, and the same statements apply after actual dualization and remarking. A standard sphere can also have nonspherical intermediate cuts in a particular diagram. The tests certify presentations on which their hypotheses hold.

## Finite end data and a check on a published closed diagram

The mean curvatures of both ends can strengthen the width test. Let $a,b$ be the infima of the two boundary mean curvatures, each taken as the divergence of the **outward** normal. If the normalized intermediate $Y$ fails its recognition test, Räde's comparison gives
\[
 \arctan\frac{2a}{\sqrt{3\rho}}+
 \arctan\frac{2b}{\sqrt{3\rho}}+\sqrt{\rho/3}\ d\ \le 0.
\]
In the metric $(\rho/12)g$, put $A=\arctan(2a/\sqrt{3\rho})$, $B=\arctan(2b/\sqrt{3\rho})$. The product over this non-PSC $Y$ has Property A. Corollary 2.9 implies $a+b<0$. The finite model interval $[A/2,-B/2]$ therefore has ordered endpoints in $(-\pi/4,\pi/4)$, and Theorem 2.7 gives the displayed bound. A strictly positive left side recognizes $M$, for example if $a+b\ge0$ and $\rho>0$. For a scalar-constant flat-torus warped strip the finite-end inequality is an equality, so its equality case calls for separate evidence. With the end curvatures omitted, the strict width theorem still includes $\rho d^2=3\pi^2$ on the recognizing side. [Mean-curvature conventions and model check](../../reviews/END_CURVATURE_READER.md)

The third-tube winding-zero case also has precise scope. In a homotopy sphere with a single lower 1-handle, one minimum and one maximum and $n_2=m$, the upper rank is $m-1$. With normalized row $(1,0,\ldots,0)$ the full ordinary boundary matrix has rank two. The leading unit-determinant block $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has inverse with bottom-right entry zero. Its Schur complement is exactly the rest of the framed winding-zero linking block, which must therefore vanish. For any winding-zero component taken first, the preceding rank-two cut argument applies. If it passes, the proof of Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson Proposition 3.2 puts that component as a zero-framed unknot in a ball in the lower $S^1\times S^2$. The isotopy carries the other tubes. Additional upper data are required to obtain a net 2/3 cancellation in this larger family. [Independent reverse-order derivation](../../reviews/REVERSE_ORDER_READER.md)

For the winding-one test the attaching knot can also be probed with another integer framing. The one-handle/one-attachment trace is smooth contractible for every such framing. A PSC-dominated boundary in any probe framing gives $S^3$ by the proof above. Alternatively an integral homology sphere filling certified to be a Heegaard Floer $L$-space gives the product-core knot by Hayden–Mark–Piccirillo, Proposition 3.2. Use that knot isotopy on the **original** framed embedding with its own normal parametrization, then cancel the original pair with its actual spectator tubes. No auxiliary framed surgery replaces the original closed assembly. [Primary passage](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_COLLAR_SURGERY_20261126.md)

In Isoshima's Figure 37 for $DW^-(0,n+2)$, the second circle is a small zero-framed meridian of the long attaching circle, in a three-ball disjoint from the dotted circle. With the meridian attached first its disk may meet the future long strand. The meridian *alone* is a split zero-framed unknot relative to the dotted unknot. Its surgery therefore replaces that ball by punctured $S^1\times S^2$ and the actual intermediate level is $Y_2$ for all the displayed integers $n$. Follow the long framed tube in the complement of the meridian tube into this surgery level and keep the given upper $3/4$-handle closure. This verifies the second-order recognition on a supplied closed family. At $n=1$ the long-strand-first boundary is $\Sigma(2,5,7)$ up to orientation, bounds the Mazur piece, and cannot admit PSC by the first-order argument. The same smooth standard sphere thus supplies a failed cut and a successful cut in different orders. The positive metric on $Y_2$ certifies the topology; a width or scalar claim about a particular four-metric would need that metric on the selected cut. ## A three-middle-handle class that closes after reduction

In a reduced $(1,1,3,2,1)$ presentation normalize the lower row to $(1,0,0)$ by actual framed slides. Choose an order $a,b,c$ with the winding-one attachment first. Suppose the prefix just after $a$ and the prefix just after $a,b$ each receive a nonzero-degree map from a closed oriented PSC three-manifold. Then the **closed smooth source is standard**.

The first prefix is spherical by the winding-one test. Cancel the pair, pulling back both remaining framed tubes and composing the upper gluing with the resulting boundary identification. This gives a two-component link in $S^3$ whose full framed surgery is $\#_2(S^1\times S^2)$ by the dual upper body. Its entire two-by-two linking matrix vanishes because its integral cokernel is free of rank two. The second prefix is therefore zero surgery on the first residual knot in $S^3$. Apply the zero-matrix argument above with $r=1$ to its simply connected one-2-handle trace. The PSC-dominated boundary is $S^1\times S^2$. Property R gives an unknotted component of the actual remaining two-component link. Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson Proposition 3.2 supplies slides to the zero-framed unlink. Transport the upper boundary map after every slide. Laudenbach–Poénaru's Theorem A at rank two then gives $S^4$ for the original closed assembly. The same reasoning applies in the reversed presentation with one upper 3-handle. Each PSC test can be supplied by the degree, slice or band criterion on that *particular* prefix; the two metrics need not be compared in an energy argument. [One-sided theorem and gluing](../../../04_hypersurface_immersions/02_two_handle_recognition/manuscript/recognition_from_marked_morse_heights.tex) · [Independent three-handle check](../../reviews/THREE_HANDLE_READER.md)

The first test alone always returns the strict decrease $3\to2$. Its target is specifically a **one-sided** two-component presentation. It can also close by the established tunnel-number or generalized-square-knot tests. Having a spherical first prefix does not select any of those further conditions on the resulting link.
