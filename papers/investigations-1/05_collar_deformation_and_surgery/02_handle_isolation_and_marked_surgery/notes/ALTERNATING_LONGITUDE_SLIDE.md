## Minimum of the original moving knot on spheres missing \(Q\)

The unfilled glued exterior has group \(G*\langle t\rangle\), where \(t\)
crosses the seam from a lower port to an upper port. With these based
identifications the free homotopy class of \(K^\lambda\) is represented
by
\[
 a\,t\,\ell\,t^{-1}\,a^{-1}\,t\,b\,t^{-1}.
\]
The intervening vertex words are all nontrivial, so this expression is
cyclically reduced. The filled image reduces to \(t\bar b t^{-1}\),
conjugate to a vertex element. The algebraic sphere count of
\(K^\lambda\) is zero.

For a competing transverse nonseparating sphere disjoint from \(Q\),
take the stationary drilling tube sufficiently small to miss it. Its
complement in the ambient knot exterior is connected: paths in the
connected cut of \(Y_1\) can be perturbed off \(Q\) and pushed outside
the tube. Cutting now gives a splitting \(H*\langle u\rangle\) with
one stable letter \(u\) and \(H\) the group of the connected cut.
All groups are finitely generated by compactness. The original knot
factor \(G\) is freely indecomposable and noncyclic. In fact
\(\langle\mu_Q,\lambda_Q\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\) since the longitude
has infinite order and the meridian abelianizes to a generator. In
a free splitting with trivial edge groups, such an abelian subgroup
fixes a vertex: if it contained an element translating on an axis,
its commuting subgroup would inject into the integer translations
of that axis. Otherwise a nontrivial element fixes a unique vertex
and every commuting element fixes that vertex. The meridian normally
generates \(G\) by meridional filling to \(S^3\), so it cannot lie
in a proper factor of any nontrivial free splitting. Grushko uniqueness
on \(G*\langle t\rangle\) and \(H*\langle u\rangle\) identifies \(H\)
as a conjugate of the actual subgroup \(G\) and gives \(H\) free rank
zero. This uses uniqueness of the embedded indecomposable factors
up to conjugacy.
[Diao–Feighn, *The Grushko decomposition of a finite graph of free groups*, Theorem 1.1, p. 1836](https://msp.org/gt/2005/9-4/gt-v9-n4-p02-p.pdf#page=2)

Conjugate the new splitting to make \(H=G\). Then \(u=g t^{\pm1}h\)
for \(g,h\in G\). To see this, strip the initial and terminal \(G\)
syllables of \(u\), leaving a word \(w\) that begins and ends in
\(\langle t\rangle\), with exponent \(\pm1\) in the quotient by \(G\).
Every nonzero power of such a word begins and ends in the same factor,
and if \(w\) has a \(G\) syllable then every power still does. For the
general first-and-last-factor assertion, induct on syllable length.
If the end and start syllables have nonidentity product, concatenation
merges exactly those syllables at each joint. If they are inverses,
strip the conjugating syllable and apply induction to the shorter
word whose two ends are now in the other factor. Nonzero exponent
ensures no tested power is trivial. The assertion for negative
powers follows by inversion. If \(w\) has a \(G\) syllable, an alternating
product of nonzero \(w\)-powers and nonidentity intervening \(G\)
elements is reduced at every such joint and retains a \(G\) syllable.
It cannot equal \(t\), which contradicts generation by \(G,w\).
The only remaining possibility is \(w=t^{\pm1}\).

Upon replacing \(t\) by the new basis, all four vertex letters become
conjugates in \(G\) of the original letters. At each vertex the
adjacent stable-letter signs are opposite, so the two factors contributed
by the basis change are inverse. Thus the cyclic stable-letter
length for the displayed conjugacy class remains four. Each traversal
of the competing sphere contributes a single occurrence of \(u\) or
\(u^{-1}\) before reduction, giving a four-point lower bound. The marked
sphere supplies equality. In particular neither unfilled four-holed
face has a proper compression: an essential nonperipheral boundary
would split the four holes \(2+2\), and compressing would give two
spheres with two moving points each. Their oriented sum is the old
nonzero sphere class, so at least one would be nonseparating and
would contradict the lower bound. The sphere minimum is relative to
the actual stationary component in the inlet.
