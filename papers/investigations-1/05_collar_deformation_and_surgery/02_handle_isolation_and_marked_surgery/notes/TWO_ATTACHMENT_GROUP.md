Let $Y_j=\#_j(S^1\times S^2)$, $Y_0=S^3$, and let $H_j=\natural_j(S^1\times D^3)$, $H_0=D^4$. Let $C$ be the trace of two integrally framed attachments along $L=K_1\sqcup K_2$ in $Y_r$ with outgoing level $Y_{r+2}$. The labels are attached to parametrized tubes. Write $E=Y_r\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu L$ for the compact common exterior. On its two tori let $\mu_i$ be the incoming meridians and $\alpha_i$ the outgoing meridians given by the original framing. In the outgoing filling the cores are $K_i^*$. In the inlet and outlet all individual core words are taken up to conjugacy and orientation.

**Result.** Both incoming knot words are trivial in $F_r$ and both actual filling slopes vanish in $H_1(E;\mathbb Z)$. The incoming link has zero framings and linking number zero. A sphere of the common exterior separating its two tori, or the triviality of either $\alpha_i$ in $\pi_1(E)$, gives the zero-framed unlink in the original inlet. A framed slide making one component a local unknot also suffices, even when its spanning disk meets the other component. A complete tunnel system with at most $r+2$ arcs gives a terminal reduction, possibly after $r+1$ temporary cancelling 2/3 pairs. Each of these certificates, when the outlet closes by the actual $H_{r+2}$, removes two middle handles and two upper handles in the net count.

## The trace group and both systems of slopes

Write $u_i=[K_i]\in F_r$ and $v_i=[K_i^*]\in F_{r+2}$ for based representatives in fundamental groups. Van Kampen in both directions gives

$$
G=\pi_1(C)=F_r/N
 \cong F_{r+2}/\langle\!\langle v_1,v_2\rangle\!\rangle,
\qquad N=\langle\!\langle u_1,u_2\rangle\!\rangle.
$$

The reverse presentation yields $\operatorname{rank}G_{\rm ab}\ge r$. The forward presentation therefore has both abelianized relators zero and $G_{\rm ab}=\mathbb Z^r$. The two reverse relators in $\mathbb Z^{r+2}$ are linearly independent and form a basis of a primitive rank-two sublattice since their quotient is torsion-free. The reverse presentation complex has injective cellular differential $\mathbb Z^2\to\mathbb Z^{r+2}$ in degree two. Attaching cells of dimensions at least three to build a classifying space gives $H_2(G;\mathbb Z)=0$. Hopf's formula and $N\subset[F_r,F_r]$ imply $N=[F_r,N]$. By induction $N$ is contained in every lower central term of $F_r$, so residual nilpotence gives $N=1$. In particular the **specified incoming inclusion** $\pi_1(Y_r)\to G$ is an isomorphism. This proof works with $r=0$. The rank jump of two is used in both bounds, and the argument uses second homology of the group. [Brown–Ellis, opening formula](https://groupoids.org.uk/pdffiles/brown-ellis.pdf) · [The residual-nilpotence argument in the single-attachment proof](../../reviews/LAST_HANDLE_READER.md)

Excision in $(Y_r,E)$ gives $H_2(Y_r,E)=\mathbb Z^2$ and $H_1(Y_r,E)=0$. The map into $H_2(Y_r,E)$ from $H_2(Y_r)$ records intersection with $K_1,K_2$ and is zero. Consequently

$$
0\longrightarrow\mathbb Z\langle\mu_1,\mu_2\rangle
 \longrightarrow H_1(E;\mathbb Z)
 \longrightarrow \mathbb Z^r\longrightarrow0.
$$

Integral filling gives $H_1(Y_{r+2};\mathbb Z)=H_1(E;\mathbb Z)/\langle[\alpha_1],[\alpha_2]\rangle$. Both sides have rank $r+2$, so each $[\alpha_i]$ is zero in the free abelian group $H_1(E;\mathbb Z)$. For an oriented null-homologous $K_i$ use its Seifert longitude $\lambda_i$. An oriented spanning surface in $Y_r$, truncated at both tubes, shows $[\lambda_i]=\operatorname{lk}(K_i,K_j)[\mu_j]$ for $j\ne i$, with positively oriented meridians. The linking number is independent of the chosen surface since $[K_j]=0$ in $H_1(Y_r)$. With oriented slopes $\alpha_i=\lambda_i+n_i\mu_i$, the independent meridians give $n_i=0$ and $\operatorname{lk}(K_i,K_j)=0$. The original meridian $\mu_i$ intersects $\alpha_i$ once on its torus, hence maps to the outgoing core up to sign and conjugacy. Thus the marked kernel of $H_1(Y_{r+2})\to H_1(C)$ is precisely the span of those meridians, equivalently the span of the actual dual core classes. Each single component surgery, ordered first, has $H_1=\mathbb Z^{r+1}$ by the same quotient. That intermediate group calculation leaves its prime decomposition undetermined.

The reverse rank argument identifies a primitive **abelian** pair and a free joint quotient. At the group level these facts alone permit, for example, $v_1=x^2yx^{-1}y^{-1}$ and $v_2=y$ in $F(x,y)*F_r$. Killing both gives $F_r$ and their abelianizations are $[x],[y]$. Killing $v_1$ alone leaves a nonabelian quotient by sending $x(t)=t+1$, $y(t)=2t$ and the other generators to the identity. For $r=0$ this already rules out $v_1$ as an element of a free basis since that individual primitive quotient would be cyclic. In any rank the $F(x,y)$ quotient has a nontrivial element conjugate to its square and hence is nonfree, and is a subgroup of the corresponding free-product quotient after killing $v_1$ alone. This is an illustration of the inference gap for a fixed pair of words. It asserts no realization by a link and gives no obstruction to changing the pair by slides.

## Exterior spheres, disks and rank

Suppose a smooth sphere $S\subset\operatorname{int}E$ separates the two boundary tori. Cut at $S$ and cap both new sphere boundaries with 3-balls. Since fillings commute with this operation, the two incoming closed pieces are $Y_a,Y_b$ and the outgoing pieces are $Y_{a'},Y_{b'}$ by Kneser–Milnor, with $a+b=r$ and $a'+b'=r+2$. Each piece contains one surgery. The increase of first Betti number under a single knot surgery is at most one: the knot exterior has first Betti number at most one greater than the meridional filling and both fillings are quotients of its first homology. Thus $a'=a+1$ and $b'=b+1$. Each capped piece contains a local zero-framed unknot by the one-knot theorem. Shrink the capping balls and move them off the spanning disks by ambient isotopies fixed on the framed tubes. Pull back the disks and restore the separated pieces along the original sphere. The disks and their little ball neighborhoods are disjoint. A nonseparating sphere in $E$ instead removes an $S^1\times S^2$ factor from **both** fillings, leaving a two-component block with the same rank difference and prescribed torus slopes. A separating sphere with both tori on one side can also remove a common piece, with no split of the two attachments. [Milnor, unique decomposition, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800) · [Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1, preprint pp. 9–10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06561v1#page=9)

If one $\alpha_i$ is trivial in $\pi_1(E)$, Dehn's lemma gives a properly embedded disk for the specified slope in $E$. Adjoining the annulus to $K_i$ in its incoming tube gives a smooth disk missing the other tube. Its small ball has boundary a separating sphere as above. Equivalently the exterior disk caps off in the outgoing solid torus to a sphere meeting $K_i^*$ once and missing $K_j^*$. For the converse of this equivalent formulation, a sphere with precisely those geometric intersections can be arranged to meet the dual tubes in just a meridian disk by shrinking the tubes and using the product collar of their boundary. One outgoing geometric dual therefore suffices. In both formulations the test is in the common exterior with the **specified** peripheral data. [Papakyriakopoulos, Dehn's lemma, PNAS 43 (1957), p. 169](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf)

There is a group-rank test that only uses this exterior. Suppose $\pi_1(E)$ has a generating set of size at most $r+2$. The filling epimorphism $\pi_1(E)\to F_{r+2}$ factors as $F_{r+2}\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(E)\twoheadrightarrow F_{r+2}$. The composite is an isomorphism by Hopficity of free groups. It can also be checked by the same Hopf-formula argument above since the abelian map of the composite is injective and the quotient is free. Hence the filling epimorphism is itself an isomorphism and both $\alpha_i$ are trivial. Conversely the zero-framed split unlink in $Y_r$ has exterior group $F_r*\mathbb Z*\mathbb Z$. The tests by a separating sphere, one trivial exterior slope, minimum exterior generator rank, and an already split unlink are equivalent on these endpoints. The tunnel-number-$1$ proof of Alan Reid in $S^3$ uses the same exterior-group epimorphisms. A complete tunnel system with $r+1$ arcs supplies the $r+2$ generators because the resulting genus-$r+2$ handlebody surjects onto the exterior on $\pi_1$. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, Proposition 3.1, pp. 2312–2313](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=8)

The first geometric obstruction for a fixed diagram is that each $\alpha_i$ may remain nontrivial in $\pi_1(E)$ even though its homology class vanishes. A detected nontrivial image in a quotient of the exterior group rules out the specified outward sphere and disk for that component. Failure for both rules out the original split link. A nonseparating exterior sphere only removes a shared summand. There is no argument here that after removing all such spheres a sphere separating the tori must remain. The group calculation and the homology of a one-attachment prefix do not select a slide or a tunnel system.

## A local unknot after one chosen framed slide

Assume that one component $U$ is already an unknot in a ball of $Y_r$, or becomes one after a supplied **framed handle slide** of a component over a framed parallel of the other. Here $U$ need only bound an embedded disk in $Y_r$. The disk need not miss the remaining component $V$ and the initial pair need not lie in a common ball. The slid trace, if used, has the same end manifolds by the trace diffeomorphism. Apply the slope calculation to that framed pair. Surgery on $U$ alone makes $Y_{r+1}$, even if $V$ runs through its ball. The carried $V$ in that boundary has surgery to $Y_{r+2}$. The single-knot theorem identifies it as a local zero-framed unknot there. Choose a target small disk ball in the surviving exterior, disjoint from a small disk ball of $U$ in the original coordinates. The local unknot in $Y_{r+1}$ can be isotoped to the chosen target by shrinking its ball and moving it along a path.

Choose this isotopy generically with respect to the core $U^*$ of the first filling. Away from the finite transverse passages the moving framed tube is transported in the common exterior with collars, shrinking the tubes locally. At a passage, detour the moving arc around $U^*$ on a tiny meridian disk. The difference between the incoming and outgoing detours is a band sum with the meridian of $U^*$, up to orientation. Its original slope is $\alpha_U$, the specified framed parallel of $U$. Thus each passage, undone in the original $Y_r$, is precisely an addition or subtraction slide of $V$ over $U$ with the band framing. The terminating core bounds the target disk disjoint from $U$ and its framing is zero by recalculating on the slid pair. The procedure gives a finite slide sequence to a split zero-framed unlink in $Y_r$. This is the core-crossing argument of Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson Proposition 3.2 with the all-rank single-knot result in the once-filled manifold. The initial slide, if needed, must use the given framed parallel. A band sum of the two original cores with an unspecified framing does not supply that slide. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, §2, Lemma 2.1 and Proposition 3.2](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf) · [Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06561v1#page=9)

Theorem 5.1 of Meier–Schirmer–Zupan already states the all-rank one-knot result, including rational slopes. They identify it as implicit in Gordon's Theorem 6.9 and give a proof using prior knot-surgery results. This explicit source supplements the earlier bounded literature check. The trace-group and Ni proof in [the last-handle readback](../../reviews/LAST_HANDLE_READER.md) remains an alternative.

## Marked comparison and net count

For an ordinary isotopy or framed-slide comparison use the handle diffeomorphism $F:C\to\widetilde C$ from the given trace to the new trace, fixed at the lower collar. Let $F_+=\partial_+F$. If the old outgoing identification is $\gamma:\partial_+ C\to\partial P$, then the replacement of this block uses

$$
\widetilde\gamma=\gamma F_+^{-1},\qquad
\widetilde\gamma^{-1}=F_+\gamma^{-1}.
$$

Equivalently a later framed tube $s:S^1\times D^2\to\partial_+ C$ is represented by $F_+s$ with inverse $s^{-1}F_+^{-1}$ on its image. This includes entire parametrizations and not only cores. On input, operations in a collar of the lower boundary can be interpolated back to the identity at the lower face. Movable later markings are carried by the actual comparison. A prescription to fix some common exterior region pointwise requires that the isotopies and bands be supported away from that region. In the outgoing filled model a tube of the reversed handle is $o_i\tau$ where $o_i:D^2\times S^1\to\partial_+\widetilde C$ is its actual outgoing face and $\tau$ interchanges the factors. In the top piece the full tube is $\widetilde\gamma o_i\tau$ with inverse $\tau^{-1}o_i^{-1}\widetilde\gamma^{-1}$. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, §2 and Figures 3–4](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf)

When $P$ is the actual upper $Q\cong H_{r+2}$, put the new filled unlink summands into product coordinates. For each of the two new sphere factors the associated core crosses it once, and misses the other belt spheres. The difference between this upper marking and $\widetilde\gamma$ extends over $Q$ by Laudenbach–Poénaru. From the reversed end, both 1/2 pairs now cancel. This gives an identification $\Xi$ of the actual upper piece including the original block with $H_r$. If $\delta$ is the old gluing of the lower prefix into that piece, its replacement is $\partial\Xi\,\delta$, with inverse $\delta^{-1}(\partial\Xi)^{-1}$. The original closed manifold is diffeomorphic to the returned one by the identity on the prefix and $\Xi$ on the upper piece. The reverse cancellations remove forward 2/3 pairs. Without the actual upper body, the conclusion is the marked relative trace comparison and the count is conditional on the terminal position. [Laudenbach–Poénaru, proof of Theorem A, pp. 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=6) · [Milnor, first cancellation theorem, Theorem 5.4](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/hcobord.pdf#page=53)

**One more tunnel.** For a complete tunnel system with one handlebody on one side of the exterior splitting, filling its compression-body side gives a splitting of $Y_{r+2}$. Its genus is at least $b_1(Y_{r+2})=r+2$, so the tunnel number is at least $r+1$. If the tunnel number is $r+1$, Meier–Schirmer–Zupan Corollary 1.3(1) also gives the unlink directly. If the tunnel number is $r+2$, Corollary 1.3(2), with $c=2$ and $k=r$, supplies $s=r+1$ distant zero-framed unknots whose union with $L$ slides to the $(2+s)$-component zero-framed unlink. Place the $s$ additions as actual cancelling forward 2/3 pairs, disjoint from the original framed link and with the new sphere attachments in their own outgoing summands. Before the slides the enlarged upper body read in reverse is $H_{r+2+s}$, with the original upper marking on its old portion and product coordinates on the added factors. Carry all of that gluing by $F_+$ as above. Apply the same extension and geometric cancellations to the $2+s$ new product summands. The middle count is $n_2+s-(2+s)=n_2-2$ on the original closed smooth type, and the upper count drops by two as well. The allowance of $s$ temporary pairs is essential to this argument. For a nonterminal trace with no such upper body the stable slide sequence alone gives no asserted unaugmented relative trace identification.
