In the issued GST Figure 5 fixture, the mouth circle is the only lower

any face chord or wall section. The result also holds on the full lower

## Separated factors and native fillings

Let \(B\) be the mouth ball. Its intersection with the lower face is
\(D_{12}\), which contains the two return endpoints. The rest of
\(\partial B\) is the hemispherical disk \(\Delta\), disjoint from the
drills and stationary tube, with \(s=\partial\Delta\). Write \(F\) for
the lower four-holed face and \(F=P_R\cup_s P_T\) for its decomposition
into the return and through pairs of pants. The return wall annulus is
\(A\) and \(V=P_R\cup A\) is a once-holed torus. The full lower port is
\(U=V\cup_s P_T\). Take \(X\) to be the stationary-filled drilled slab.

The disk \(\Delta\) cuts \(X\) into the return-side \(P\) and the outside
exterior \(Z\). The boundary of \(P\) is the marked torus \(V\cup\Delta\).
To identify it, put back the short-arc ball of the native link. Its drilled
exterior is an annulus times an interval, with the annular part of the
ball boundary as one end. In a short-arc chart this exterior is
\(S^1\) times a rectangle. The two end collars and the outer radial
edge together give one interval in the rectangle boundary, and the inner
radial edge gives the complementary interval. Thus attaching the
short-arc exterior just extends a collar of \(P_R\cup\Delta\) and carries
the wall meridian to the native meridian \(\mu_J\). After rounding,
\(P\) is the compact exterior of \(J\) in \(S^3_0(Q)\). The native short
chord with its zero-longitude section gives the preferred longitude
\(\lambda_J\) on \(\partial P\). This marking is the one from the actual
completed link. A change in the initial wall section adds an integral
multiple of \(\mu_J\).

The exterior of \(Q\cup J\) in \(S^3\) has first homology free on the two
meridians. Each given filling quotients by its slope class. The joint
outlet has integral first homology of rank two, so both classes vanish
in the link exterior. In the preferred bases this forces both framings
and the mutual linking number to be zero. Therefore
\[
 H_1(P;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\langle[\mu_Q],[\mu_J]\rangle,\qquad
 \ker\bigl(H_1(\partial P;\mathbb Z)\longrightarrow H_1(P;\mathbb Z)\bigr)
       =\mathbb Z\langle[\lambda_J]\rangle.
\]
The two native fillings are
\[
 P(\mu_J)=S^3_0(Q),\qquad
 P(\lambda_J)=S^3_{0,0}(Q\cup J)=Y_2.
\]
The first restores the native \(J\)-tube and kills its meridian. The
second kills its zero longitude. Their slopes have distance one.
[GST, Proposition 2.2, pp. 2310–2311](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=6)

The through meridians in \(Z\) have infinite order. In fact, removing
the mouth ball replaces the patch \(D_{12}\) of the slab boundary
by \(\Delta\). Gluing a ball back along a single boundary disk
extends a collar, so the complementary undrilled slab still has the
homology of \(S^2\times I\). Excision in the pair with the three
proper arcs drilled gives one relative meridian-disk generator for each
of the two through arcs and the upper return. A sphere just inside the
upper face meets the through arcs once each and meets the upper return
twice with opposite signs. Its signed relative class therefore gives a
single relation identifying the two consistently oriented through
meridians, up to orientation. The undrilled slab has \(H_1=0\), so
\(H_1(Z;\mathbb Z)\) is free on the through meridian \([\mu_t]\)
and the upper return meridian for arbitrary complementary arcs of these
three types.

## The peripheral injection

Suppose \(\pi_1(\partial P)\to\pi_1(P)\) had a kernel. The Loop Theorem
gives a proper compression disk \(D\) with essential boundary on the
torus. Because a simple essential torus curve is primitive and the
homological kernel is generated by \([\lambda_J]\), the disk has
slope \(\lambda_J\). The sphere obtained by compressing the torus
is the interior boundary of a regular neighborhood of the torus and
\(D\). Capping this sphere gives a solid torus \(T\) with meridian
\(\lambda_J\). One can see this by reversing the compression on the
sphere collar via one 1-handle and then capping the sphere by a ball.
The complementary piece is connected, since it meets the neighborhood
along just this sphere. Capping its sphere gives a closed oriented
manifold \(C\), and \(P=T\mathbin{\#}C\), with the whole boundary in
the \(T\) factor. This argument allows closed summands in \(P\).
[Stallings, Loop Theorem](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970146) ·
[Hatcher, Theorem 3.1, p. 45](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/3M/3M.pdf#page=46)

The two native slopes act in the same \(T\) factor. Filling \(T\) by its
meridian gives \(Y_1\), by doubling the meridional disk factor of the
solid torus. A distance-one slope gives \(S^3\), by the genus-one
Heegaard gluing with the meridians intersecting once. Thus the assumed
compression gives
\[
  S^3_0(Q)\cong C,\qquad Y_2\cong Y_1\mathbin{\#} C .
\]
Uniqueness of prime decomposition for closed connected oriented
3-manifolds gives \(C\cong Y_1\), since \(S^1\times S^2\) is prime and
both displayed product summands of \(Y_2\) must be accounted for.
Gabai's Property R states that a knot in \(S^3\) with zero surgery
giving \(S^1\times S^2\) is the unknot. This contradicts the stationary
knot hypothesis. Hence the map
\[
 \mathbb Z^2\longrightarrow \pi_1(P),\qquad
             (p,q)\longmapsto \mu_J^p\lambda_J^q
\]
is injective, and every nonzero peripheral element has infinite order.
Both fillings kept exactly the same \(C\) in the hypothetical
compression. If closed prime factors are removed from the actual \(P\),
the boundary-bearing factor still contains the injected torus group
by the sphere free-product decomposition. In particular the
obstruction persists when a test discards all closed factors.
[Milnor, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800) ·
[Hatcher, Theorem 1.5, p. 5](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/3M/3M.pdf#page=6) ·
[Gabai, Corollary 8.3 and the following remark](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214441488) ·
[GST, Theorem 1.1, p. 2305](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=1)

For the issued diagram \(J=U_{\rm GST}\) is the unknot and \(Q\) is
the square knot. The slide of \(Q\) over \(J\) in published Figure 5
gives the joint \(Y_2\) filling. The same paper describes \(S^3_0(Q)\)
as a bundle with closed genus-two fiber over \(S^1\); the fibration
sequence injects the fiber group into the fundamental group, which
therefore cannot be cyclic. This gives a second verification that
the two native fillings contradict a return-side compression in
this specimen.

## The face and full port disk slopes

Based at a point of \(s\), the separating disk gives
\(\pi_1(X)=\pi_1(P)*\pi_1(Z)\) by van Kampen. Capping \(s\) on both
face pairs of pants gives annuli. Their core classes map to the infinite
cyclic subgroups generated by \(\mu_J\) and \(\mu_t\) in separate
factors. On the full port, capping \(V\) gives \(\partial P\) and
capping \(P_T\) gives the same through annulus. The free-product normal
form gives the two injections
\[
 \pi_1(F)/\langle\!\langle s\rangle\!\rangle
     \ \cong\ \mathbb Z*\mathbb Z
     \ \hookrightarrow\ \pi_1(X),\qquad
 \pi_1(U)/\langle\!\langle s\rangle\!\rangle
     \ \cong\ \mathbb Z^2*\mathbb Z
     \ \hookrightarrow\ \pi_1(X).
\]
In particular these identify the entire based kernels as the respective
normal closures of \(s\).
[Preceding free-product face computation](FOUR_PORT_UNIQUE_FACE_SLOPE.md)

On \(F\), an essential nonperipheral circle disjoint from \(s\) is
isotopic to \(s\). A circle that intersects \(s\) can be put in minimal
position. It then alternates between the two pairs of pants. Every
same-side arc from \(s\) to \(s\) separates the other two cuffs. An arc
cutting off a cuff-free disk would give an innermost bigon. Closing
each arc along \(s\) thus gives the corresponding annular generator or
its inverse. The choice of path along \(s\) disappears in the capped
group. The circle reads as a nonempty reduced word alternating between
the two factors, so it cannot bound a disk in \(X\). The cuff
meridians also survive.

The same argument applies to \(U\), with one added surface fact.
An essential proper arc from \(s\) to \(s\) in \(V\) cannot separate
\(V\), since such a separating arc would cut off a disk on the genus-zero
side. Joining it across the capping disk gives a nonseparating circle
in the capped torus. For this last assertion, the new circle cuts the
capping disk into two pieces which attach separately to the cut
once-holed torus and cannot disconnect it. Each \(V\)-side arc in a
minimal representative therefore gives a nonzero peripheral class,
while each \(P_T\)-side arc gives the through generator up to sign.
The resulting word survives. A circle disjoint from \(s\) on the
\(V\) side either is parallel to \(s\) or stays essential after
capping. On the \(P_T\) side it is parallel to a cuff. This proves
that \(s\) is the unique essential simple disk slope on \(U\) as well,
up to surface isotopy relative to the cuffs. In particular every
lower-port compression boundary separates \(U\).

## Arbitrary chords, endpoint motion and the solid-torus test

For the canonical short chord, a wall section with winding \(m\)
relative to the zero-longitude section gives, up to orientation and
base path, the explicit word \(\mu_J^m\lambda_J\) in the return
factor. It has infinite order in \(P\) and \(X\). In particular the
word obstruction is in the drilled return factor before either
\(J\)-filling, with the torus and winding still available.

For any chosen face chord between the return cuffs and any chosen
section of \(A\), the clipped bridge boundary has signed intersection
of absolute value one with the marked wall meridian. This remains so
when the endpoints slide on either cuff or when extra turns are put in
their collars. Every simple essential loop on \(U\) that bounds a
proper disk has slope \(s\), whose intersection is zero. Hence this
test excludes all lower bridges, including chords leaving \(P_R\).
Each chord-and-section word has infinite order. Its nontrivial image
lies in the injected \(\mathbb Z^2*\mathbb Z\), where a single-factor word
has infinite order and powers of a cyclically reduced word of length
at least two grow in reduced length.
A compression disk with boundary \(s\) also cannot cut off a solid
torus on the marked return side. If another such disk did, its cap of
\(V\) would have the same capped torus group
\(\pi_1(V)/\langle\!\langle s\rangle\!\rangle\) in \(X\). The solid
torus meridian would be killed, contrary to the injection. Clipping
and restoring a wall section uses the original parametrized tube.
[Marked bridge criterion](FOUR_PORT_BOUNDARY_PATTERN.md#the-marked-disk-test)

The result covers the lower side of supplied one-mouth embeddings.
The disk \(\Delta\) itself is present before the stationary filling.
The obstruction concerns replacing the lower return by a bridge on
the specified drilled slab. It leaves the upper side, the trace after
cutting on \(\Delta\), and selection of another splitting sphere to
their own geometric tests.
