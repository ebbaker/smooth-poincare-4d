## The diagram, duals and closing

All manifolds, framings and moves here are smooth and oriented. Put $Y=S^3_0(L_{3,1})$. The left panel of GST Figure 9 at $(n,k)=(3,1)$ is the reverse trace from $Y$ to $S^3$ with its incoming 1-handlebody $H=\natural_2(S^1\times D^3)$ already drawn. The two dotted circles give the two actual 1-handles. Let $B_x,B_y$ be their oriented belt spheres $\{0\}\times\partial D^3$. In the dotted-circle diagram they are obtained by capping the spanning disks across the zero-surgery solid tori. Cutting along both belts gives a three-sphere with four balls removed. The based oriented passages through the two handles generate $\pi_1(Y)=F(x,y)$, and intersection with each belt gives the corresponding signed exponent. The identification of the two words with named components can be checked without identifying a small meridian by its projected size. Scharlemann moves $V_3$ onto a fiber of $S^3_0(Q)$ by a framed isotopy in the filled manifold, as in Figures 16–21. Back in $S^3$ such passages through the filling amount to slides of $V_3$ over $Q$, with $Q$ fixed. By GST Lemma 2.1 these forward slides carry the reverse $V_3^*$ individually and let the reverse $Q^*$ slide over it. Scharlemann's Lemma 7.1 gives the group of $Y$ with the single $V_3^*$ 2-handle as the braid group $\langle x,y\mid xyx=yxy\rangle$. For $r$ this group has the presentation $\langle p,q\mid p^2=q^3\rangle$ via $p=xyx$, $q=xy$, $x=q^{-1}p$, $y=p^{-1}q^2$. For $s$ the group is $\langle x,y\mid x^4=y^3\rangle$. In $\langle p,q\mid p^m=q^n\rangle$ for $m,n\ge 2$, the subgroup generated by the common power is central and its quotient $C_m*C_n$ has trivial center by free-product normal form. Thus the orders of the abelianizations of the central quotients are respectively $6$ and $12$. This identifies $r$ with $V_3^*$ and $s$ with $Q^*$ in the literal GST marking. Scharlemann's later calculation of the $Q$ relator takes place after attaching the $V_3^*$ handle and serves as a check on the presentation. ## The band and its algebraic obstruction

In the left panel of Figure 9 use the central open corridor between the upper and lower dotted-handle regions, on the right of the thin minus-one component. Join its right-hand vertical strand to the facing strand of the zero-framed component by a short strip across that corridor. Equivalently, straighten those two strand intervals in a ball in the cut exterior and use the connecting rectangle there. Use a narrow pushoff $s^+$ of the stationary zero-framed reverse component toward the strip, extending by the displayed framing elsewhere. The strip attaches to $r$ and $s^+$, meets neither belt, and has interior disjoint from the two core tubes. Fix its orientation attachment to give the addition of $s$, using a local half turn if required and zero full twists. The target is $B_y$. Reversing the attachment convention can also be tested without knowing any word for the connecting path. This rectangle is in the reverse diagram; the dashed band of Figure 1 is a forward band sum in $S^3$ with a different purpose. [GST, Figures 1 and 9, and §2 handle-slide convention](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf)

This follows by traversing the two long edges of the band in opposite directions. The two signed exponent rows are $(-1,1)$ and $(4,-3)$. The test stops before a cancellation, with two middle handles, no births and no change in the upper marking.

## Cyclic and Whitehead tests on the original two components

Reading a transverse curve against both disjoint belts gives a word with one letter per crossing. Free and cyclic reduction delete pairs of letters, so the cyclic occurrence count gives a lower bound for each belt separately. The starting words are cyclically reduced and have counts $(3,3)$ and $(4,3)$. In particular $r$ passes an algebraic $\pm1$ test against either belt, while its cyclic word requires at least three crossings against each in the given marking.

For a cyclic word, draw an undirected Whitehead edge from $u$ to $v^{-1}$ for each cyclic adjacency $uv$. The graph of $r$ contains the spanning cycle $y,x,Y_-,X,y$, and the graph of $s$ contains the spanning cycle $x,X,Y_-,y,x$. Both graphs are connected with no cut vertex, so both elements are nonprimitive by Whitehead's cut-vertex lemma. A single geometric crossing against a belt of any complete rank-two system would read as $t^{\pm1}h$ after cyclic rotation, with $h$ a word in the other generator. This element is primitive by inversion and the elementary change $t\mapsto th$. Therefore neither unchanged component permits a one-intersection cancellation even after a change of complete belt system. The table for the **slid** component uses the two original belts, while its possible primitivity after a basis change requires a separate argument.
