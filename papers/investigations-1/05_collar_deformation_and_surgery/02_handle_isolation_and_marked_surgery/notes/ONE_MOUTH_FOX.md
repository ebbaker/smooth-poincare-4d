Give a Wirtinger arc of $Q$ colour $c\in\mathbb Z/3$ and send its meridian to
the reflection $r_c\colon x\mapsto 2c-x$. The crossing equation
$c_{\rm out}=2c_{\rm over}-c_{\rm in}$ enforces the Wirtinger relation
because $r_b r_a r_b=r_{2b-a}$. On a standard three-crossing diagram
of either trefoil, the three arc colours can be $0,a,2a$, with $a$ arbitrary.
Fixing the common meridian to colour zero leaves the two choices of $a$
independent in the connected sum. This gives nine homomorphisms with that
meridian image, including the constant colouring. Every such map descends to
zero surgery on $Q$ because the preferred longitude commutes with the meridian and
has exponent sum zero. Its image is therefore even and centralizes a
transposition in $S_3$. The centralizer's even subgroup is trivial.

For a spanning disk of $U$ punctured twice by $Q$, use paths on the
punctured disk from a common boundary basepoint to the punctures. Reading its
boundary gives the ordered product of the two corresponding *based*
meridians with their intersection signs, up to common conjugation and
orientation. Even when the signs are opposite, both factors map to
reflections because each reflection is its own inverse. If their transported
colours are $p,q$, the product acts by $x\mapsto x+2(p-q)$ in one order
and by the inverse translation in the other. Thus a difference of transported
colours detects $U$ in the zero-filled $Q$ complement. This test needs the
puncture paths and their relation to the Wirtinger paths. The colours of
unbased arcs at the projected punctures alone do not specify the product.
The three-colour criterion gives neither an identity nor a nonidentity
conclusion for this particular $U$ until that diagram reading is completed.
