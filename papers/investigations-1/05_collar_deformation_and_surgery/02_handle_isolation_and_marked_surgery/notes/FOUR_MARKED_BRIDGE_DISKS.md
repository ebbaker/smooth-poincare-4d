The returns run from \(1_-\) to \(2_-\) and from \(4_+\) to \(3_+\), and \(T_4\) runs from \(2_+\) to \(4_-\). In cyclic order \(3,1,2,4\) on \(K\), the intersection signs are \(+,+,-,-\) for positive crossing from the upper face of one period to the lower face of the next.

Use the stationary zero-framing chart \(q(s,r,\theta)\), where the exterior boundary of the prescribed tube is \(r=r_0\). The two values \(\theta_-\) and \(\theta_+\) are distinct around the entire chart. In the stationary exterior set

\[
A_i^0=\{q(s,r,\theta_i):s\in S^1,\ r_0\le r\le r_i\},
\qquad
\lambda_i=\{q(s,r_0,\theta_i):s\in S^1\}.

The two fingers of \(R_i\) in its access box bound a ruled rectangle \(C_i\) with the gap interval and its prescribed face chord. Indeed, in each transverse product disk at parameter \(v\), take the segment from one rail position \(z_{i,0}(v)\) to the other \(z_{i,1}(v)\). Distinct positions and disjoint cross-sections give an embedded rectangle. At the ends the rectangle is the gap interval and the face chord. The boxes of \(T_3,T_4\) and the exterior strands in the diagram ball are disjoint from these stationary sheets and return rectangles. Narrow the neighborhoods where needed for rounding. ## Framed subtraction bands

In each return choose a closed subinterval \(I_i\subset\operatorname{int} J_i\) and a radius \(r'_i\) between \(r_0\) and \(r_i\). Truncate the annulus at \(r'_i\), push its inner boundary to the actual zero-framed parallel there and take the band

\[
q(I_i\times[r'_i,r_i]\times\{\theta_i\}).
\]

The two bands have disjoint supports away from the seam. Slit each annulus on its band. The remainder is an embedded disk whose boundary consists of the old face chord and the shortened return. Its induced orientations make the operation the subtraction of the stationary parallel seen along that return. In an orientation on \(Q\) fixed in advance, if the return runs along the parallel with sign \(\delta_i\), the signed band sum uses \(-\delta_i Q^\parallel\). This recovers the bands from the arcs and the filling data. In the filled level, parallel values of \(\theta\) give parallel meridian caps and the product strip carries the moving normal disks across the band. This is the framed slide, with the inverse supplied by reversal of the same filled-level isotopy. [GST, §2, pp. 2308–2309, and Proposition 3.2, pp. 2313–2314](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=9)

The original \(K\) and \(Q\) are null-homologous in \(Y_1\), by the seam signs and the location of \(Q\) in the diagram ball. The integer linking numbers and self-linkings are therefore well defined. For the mutual linking, complete the two through strands by both face chords and complete each return by its own chord. The oriented chords cancel in the glued level, so their completed cycles add to \(K\) in \(H_1(Y_1\setminus Q;\mathbb Z)\). The completed through cycle is the actual \(V_4\) relative to \(Q\), by pushing the chords through their two access rectangles. The completed returns are homologous to signed zero longitudes by \(A_-\) and \(A_+\). They link \(Q\) trivially, and \(\operatorname{lk}(V_4,Q)=0\) by the published framing and surgery calculation. Consequently \(\operatorname{lk}(K,Q)=0\). For a slide of a moving knot \(J\) over \(Q\) with sign \(\varepsilon\), the parallel band sum gives the coefficient \(f_J+f_Q+2\varepsilon\operatorname{lk}(J,Q)\). The mutual linking stays zero because the added parallel has linking \(f_Q=0\) with \(Q\). The prescribed coefficients are zero and each slide and subsequent disk isotopy preserves them. The product slide and disk isotopies carry the full parametrized tubes, with the normal disks in the port germs carried along the actual arcs.
