## The peripheral loop and its framing

Let \(W=S^2\times[-1/2,1/2]\) be the slit inlet, \(S_-,S_+\) its faces,
and \(R,V\) the proper moving returns with both ends on \(S_-\) and \(S_+\),
respectively. The stationary tube \(N(Q)\) lies in the interior of \(W\).
Fill along the actual marked longitude \(\lambda_Q\) to obtain \(W_Q\).
Write \(Q^*=\{0\}\times S^1\) for the core of its filling torus
\(D^2\times S^1\). Form the doubly drilled manifold
\[
 X=W_Q\setminus\operatorname{int}_{W_Q}\bigl(N(R)\cup N(V)\bigr).
\]
The drills remove the interiors of the end disks from the original faces
as well. Use the product tubes at the end disks and round the boundary
corners. The lateral annulus of \(N(R)=[0,1]\times D^2\) is \(A_R\).
The lower face chord \(c_-\) meets the end disks in radial segments in
the product charts. Its retained arc \(c^0\) ends at \(x_0,x_1\in A_R\).
Take a smooth section \(b\) of \(A_R\to[0,1]\) from \(x_0\) to \(x_1\),
with compatible end germs. Smoothing \(b\cup c^0\) in the two corner charts
gives a simple loop \(\ell_b\subset\partial X\). Use the rounded image of
\(x_0\) as basepoint. The section specifies the
long edge of the strip. In the restoring tube this strip is the union of
the radial intervals from \(R\) to \(b\).

The exact condition for the \(b\)-marked bridge disk is
\[
                         [\ell_b]=1\quad\text{in }\pi_1(X,x_0).
\]
Indeed, a null-homotopy can be collared inwards along this boundary loop
with the remaining interior pushed off the boundary. Dehn's lemma gives
an embedded disk with the specified boundary. Collaring and smoothing
give a smooth proper disk in \(X\), up to rounding the prescribed loop's
corners. Restore the radial strip inside \(N(R)\) and the two end segments
of \(c_-\). Smooth its seam using the disk's product collar along \(b\).
The resulting embedded disk \(D\) has boundary \(R\cup c_-\), lies in
\(\operatorname{int}W_Q\) away from its chord, and misses \(V\). Its inward direction along
\(R\) is the specified strip direction. Conversely such a bridge disk,
collared along \(R\cup c_-\), clips to a disk for the corresponding loop
on the doubly drilled boundary. For the clipping, one may narrow the
drills in their product charts and identify the resulting collars by
the radial coordinates. This also identifies section classes relative
to the end marks. [Papakyriakopoulos, *On Dehn's lemma and the asphericity of knots*, PNAS 43 (1957), p. 169, semilinear theorem for oriented 3-manifolds](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf#page=2)

For the test allowing a choice of strip, use the same chord and allow all
sections with the specified end marks. Relative section classes are indexed
by an integer. With basepoint \(x_0\), orient the meridian \(\mu_R\) so that
an increase of one in the relative winding gives
\[
                 [\ell_{b_m}]=[\mu_R]^m[\ell_{b_0}].
\]
This follows by comparing the sections in the lateral annulus before
following \(c^0\) back to \(x_0\). Thus some section succeeds precisely
when \([\ell_{b_0}]\) belongs to the cyclic subgroup generated by
\([\mu_R]\). If one section gives a trivial loop, its
proper disk in \(X\) pairs with a meridian of \(A_R\), pushed into \(X\),
with algebraic intersection \(\pm1\). Thus the meridian has infinite order
already in \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\), and the successful section class is unique.
A separately prescribed long edge must pass the test for its own class.
The closed integral framing of the moving knot supplies the attaching
data for the slides. The section in this test specifies the relative
direction of the disk along the proper return between the two endpoint
marks.

## Converting core punctures to framed slides

Make \(D\) transverse to \(Q^*\) in its interior, keeping the boundary and
the opposite return fixed. Compactness gives finitely many intersections.
For a sufficiently small core tube, each cut-off disk is a graph over a
normal disk to \(Q^*\) at a distinct core parameter. This follows from
transversality, and compactness keeps the rest of \(D\) out of that tube.
Straighten these graphs in disjoint core charts. The region between the
small tube and the given filling boundary is a product torus collar. A
radial coordinate change supported there and in a small exterior collar
puts the meridian disks at the original filling boundary, preserving the
parallel parameters and fixing both moving returns and both faces. Thus
in the original stationary exterior the punctured bridge disk is a
planar surface with outer boundary \(R\cup c_-\) and one inner boundary
of slope \(\lambda_Q\) per puncture. Push those inner circles slightly
into the attaching exterior as disjoint framed parallels of \(Q\), using
the stationary tube coordinates.

On the punctured disk take disjoint embedded arcs from distinct interior
points of \(R\) to the inner boundary circles, one per circle. They exist
by successively cutting the planar surface along an arc from the outer
boundary to a new inner boundary. After each cut the surface stays
connected, and the remaining open points of \(R\) still belong to its
outer boundary. Thicken the arcs to narrow disjoint bands. Cut away the
bands, viewed as slits between separate boundary circles. The remainder
is a disk with boundary \(c_-\) and the return obtained by the band sums.
The induced boundary orientations choose the slide signs. Each move is
an ordinary slide over a preferred framed parallel of \(Q\). The chosen
bands have support away from the opposite return and the faces.

For the frame comparison, after the anchor is filled the disk
\(D^2\times\{\theta_i\}\) at the puncture caps its framed parallel by an
exterior collar. Parallel values of \(\theta_i\) give parallel caps in the
marked filling chart. The product neighborhood of the band joins these
caps to parallel tracks of the moving attaching interval, giving the
framed slide and its inverse in the filled level. The final disk then
pushes the modified return to a short inward parallel of \(c_-\), with
its normal tube carried by the isotopy and with the marked face fixed.
These constructions apply also when there are zero punctures, in which
case the remaining operation is the disk isotopy. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, *Geometry & Topology* 14 (2010), §2, framed band sum, and Proposition 3.2, pp. 2313–2314, conversion of passages through an actual filling core into slides](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=9)
