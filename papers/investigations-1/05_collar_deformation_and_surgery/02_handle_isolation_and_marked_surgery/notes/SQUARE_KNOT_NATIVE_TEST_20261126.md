# Native co-core intersections for the GST links \(L_{n,1}\)

26 November 2026 · Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI Codex assistance. Actual model/effort were unavailable to the derivation writer. Bounded SUR-B04 execution, revision 2, on the alternate rank-two input in the [surgery register](../research-progress/REGISTER.md). Repository base 1c81a3048c8c21a4c1d1fb23db72f37168a15ca5. Evidence is a smooth, oriented three- and four-dimensional surgery calculation for each specified marked link. All homology is integral.

For every integer \(n\ge3\), neither native surgery dual of the original GST link \(L_{n,1}=Q\cup V_n\) meets an embedded sphere in the outlet exactly once. Among nonseparating spheres with algebraic intersection zero against the given dual, the geometric minimum is at least \(2n(n-1)\) against the dual to \(Q\) and exactly \(4\) against the dual to \(V_n\). At \(n=3\) the first lower bound is \(12\). These bounds apply to the original framed co-cores and to the pullbacks of upper belts under the actual closing.

## Fix the input and closing

Fix any integer \(n\ge3\) and set \(k=1\) in the published GST family. Use Figure 1, equivalently the two unbracketed zero-framed curves of Figure 19 after blowing down the two bracketed circles. Section 7 and Figures 11–13 identify \(Q=T_{3,2}\#T_{-3,2}\) and \(V_n=T_{n,n+1}\#T_{-n,n+1}\). The joint outlet is \(Z\cong Y_2=\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\) by the Figure 9 construction. Both framings are the preferred zero longitudes and the linking number is zero, in agreement with GST Proposition 2.2. The brackets are part of a surgery description of the incoming \(S^3\). The complete test uses the two black two-handles and the immediate two-handlebody closing inherited from the *left* side of Figure 9 at this \(n\) and \(k=1\), read upside down. In that panel the two dotted circles are the two 1-handles of the upper body read in reverse. [GST, published Figures 1, 9, 11–13, 19, Proposition 2.2 and §7](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=25)

Write \(U\cong\natural_2(S^1\times D^3)\) for this upper body, and \(\gamma:Z\to\partial U\) for the orientation-reversing identification of outward boundaries given by the above trace. The result also holds for any given such \(\gamma\). In each forward two-handle let \(o_i:D^2\times S^1\to Z\) be its original outgoing face, and let \(d_i=o_i(\{0\}\times S^1)\) be its co-core boundary, for \(i=Q,V\). If \(\tau(z,v)=(v,z)\), the reverse framed tube on \(\partial U\) is \(\gamma o_i\tau\), with inverse \(\tau^{-1}o_i^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\) on its image. Undoing that single surgery fills the common exterior \(E_i=Z\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu d_i\) on the original meridian \(\mu_i\). Filling by the original zero longitude \(\lambda_i\) gives \(Z\) and core \(d_i\), so the native reverse slope has distance one from the meridian of \(d_i\). In the reverse filling the core is the original component \(i\) viewed after the other component has been surgered.

| --- | --- | --- |
| \(d_Q\) | \(M_V=S^3_0(T_{n,n+1}\#T_{-n,n+1})\) | \(n(n-1)\) |
| \(d_V\) | \(M_Q=S^3_0(T_{3,2}\#T_{-3,2})\) | \(2\) |

The exterior of a generalized square knot is the fibered connected sum of the two torus knot exteriors. Its fiber is the boundary connected sum of the two fibers of genus \((p-1)(q-1)/2\). Filling the boundary longitude caps each fiber by a disk. Both closed bundles above are irreducible. Their universal covers are \(\mathbb R^3\): first take the cyclic cover of the bundle and then the universal cover of the fiber. An embedded sphere lifts to a sphere bounding a smooth ball there. Distinct translates of its boundary are disjoint, and strict nesting of the bounded balls would contradict equal finite volumes in a deck-invariant metric. Thus the ball descends. [Meier–Zupan, arXiv:1904.08527v1, §§2.2 and 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1904.08527#page=17)

## No native cancellation and the two lower bounds

Suppose an embedded sphere \(S\subset Z\) met \(d_i\) just once. A small collar of \(S\) with the tube of the complementary arc of \(d_i\) attached to its two sides forms a punctured \(S^1\times S^2\); \(d_i\) is its product core. After capping that puncture, the drilled product core has solid-torus exterior whose compressing slope is the meridian of \(d_i\), as seen from the punctured \(S\). The native reverse slope has distance one from that slope, so the local surgery replaces this punctured summand by a ball. The total result is \(Y_1\), by uniqueness of prime decomposition, contradicting the irreducibility of \(M_Q\) or \(M_V\). Also, an essential sphere disjoint from \(d_i\) would survive reverse filling if nonseparating. If separating, it cuts \(Z\) into two punctured \(Y_1\)'s, so the unoperated summand gives a \(Y_1\) factor after filling. Both cases contradict irreducibility. In particular, every essential sphere meets each native dual at least twice. If the algebraic count for one dual is odd, its geometric count is at least three. [Milnor, *A unique decomposition theorem for 3-manifolds*, Theorem 1 and Lemma 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800)

For a nonseparating \(S\) with \(S\cdot d_i=0\), let \(k=\#(S\cap d_i)\), with \(S\) transverse to \(d_i\). Pair adjacent opposite signs on the oriented circle by adding an oriented tube to \(S\) along the intervening arc of \(d_i\). The new surface is homologous to the old one since the difference is the oriented boundary of the tube neighborhood. Its genus rises by one and the two intersections disappear. Iterate on the remaining signs, taking each tube small enough to miss the preceding surface away from its ends. This gives a connected closed surface \(T\) of genus \(k/2\), homologous to \(S\) in \(Z\) and disjoint from \(d_i\). Shrinking its product neighborhood, or moving through the torus collar, places \(T\) in \(E_i\).

For either filling \(N\) of \(E_i\), with core \(b_N\), excision and the long exact sequence identify
\[
 H_2(E_i)\ \hookrightarrow\ H_2(N),\qquad
 \operatorname{im} H_2(E_i)=\ker\big(H_2(N)\xrightarrow{\ \cdot b_N\ }\mathbb Z\big).
\]
Indeed \(H_3(N)\to H_3(N,E_i)\cong\mathbb Z\) is surjective by the orientation class, and the map to \(H_2(N,E_i)\cong\mathbb Z\) counts intersections with the core. In \(M_Q\) or \(M_V\), the new core has homology zero because the original components have zero linking. Also \(H_2(M_Q)\cong H_2(M_V)\cong\mathbb Z\) by single-knot zero surgery and Poincaré duality, and the connected closed fiber is a primitive generator. The class of \(S\) is primitive in \(H_2(Z)\) because a loop through a nonseparating sphere once gives pairing \(1\). Consequently \(T\) represents a fiber generator, up to sign, in the reverse filling. The fiber minimizes the Thurston norm in this homology class, with value \(2g-2\). Since \(T\) is connected and the closed fiber has \(g\ge2\), \(k/2\ge g\). The two fiber genera therefore give \(k\ge 2n(n-1)\) for \(d_Q\) and \(k\ge 4\) for \(d_V\). [Thurston, *A norm for the homology of 3-manifolds*, Theorem 3 and the proof, pp. 113–114](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/thurston/norm/norm.pdf)

The two duals pass the algebraic primitivity test. They represent the two meridians in \(H_1(Z)\cong\mathbb Z^2\), hence give a basis. Pullback by \(\gamma^{-1}\) puts each of the two Figure 9 belt spheres in \(Z\). For these or any other upper belt system, each nonseparating sphere has at least one odd pairing with the dual basis, so its total intersection with the original cores is at least five. An upper belt system algebraically dual to the original cores is obtainable using upper 1-handle slides, exchanges and orientation choices, which realize elementary unimodular changes of basis. For such a system the belt paired by \((\pm1,0)\) meets the two original cores at least \(3+4=7\) times, and the belt paired by \((0,\pm1)\) meets them at least \(2n(n-1)+3\) times. These two belts also exhibit nonseparating spheres in both algebraically orthogonal classes tested above. The argument uses the actual native reverse slope for every upper basis.

## Four intersections are attained for \(d_V\)

In \(M_Q\) the framed partner is isotopic to a curve \(c\) in a closed genus-two fiber \(F\), with the fiber longitude. GST Corollary 5.4 applies for each \(n\) since the fixed \(Q\) has genus two and the actual joint zero filling gives \(Y_2\). It places the framed partner on the fiber by slides of \(V_n\) over \(Q\). Corollary 6.4 gives the quotient-curve description. Scharlemann Theorem 4.1 computes the downstairs slope \(n/(2n+1)\), giving \(3/7\) when \(n=3\). His Figure 26 illustrates \(n=4\), while the proof uses Figure 22 with the counts \(2n\) and \(4n+2\) for general \(n\). The compression argument uses the fiber and monodromy conditions of Corollary 5.4. The monodromy carries \(c\) to a disjoint, nonparallel curve up to isotopy in the **closed** fiber. The isotopy may pass through the cap disk. GST Proposition 5.1 concerns the stronger condition of disjointness by an isotopy in the punctured fiber. Both curves are nonseparating, since two essential separating curves in a closed genus-two surface that are disjoint are parallel. The forward slides amount in the \(Q\)-first level \(M_Q\) to a framed isotopy of \(V_n\), using the meridian disks of the \(Q\) filling torus. This isotopy extends across the subsequent \(V_n\) filling with its full face coordinates, so the core of that filling maps to the new core \(d'_V\). Equivalently, GST Lemma 2.1 exchanges anchor and moved knot in reverse, so the dual \(d'_Q\) is the moved one. Write \(P:Z\to Z'\) for the outgoing comparison. It carries \(d_V\) to \(d'_V\), and the closing in this model is \(\gamma P^{-1}\) with inverse \(P\gamma^{-1}\). [GST, Lemma 2.1, Proposition 3.2, Corollaries 5.4 and 6.4](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf) · [Scharlemann, arXiv:1208.1299v3, §4, Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1208.1299v3#page=21)

Cut the mapping torus at the level of \(c\) and take a middle fiber in the resulting product. The two copies of \(c\) at the ends run in the middle fiber as \(c\) and its monodromy image, using the appropriate direction of the product coordinate. Realize them disjoint, as permitted above. Each runs by an annulus in its own half-product to a surface longitude of \(c\) on \(\partial\nu c\), approached from opposite sides of the surgery level. Remove thin annuli around both middle curves. Their complement in \(F\) is a connected four-holed sphere. Two disjoint nonparallel nonseparating curves in genus two have connected complement, since a disconnected complement would contain an annulus between them. Extend its four boundary circles by paired parallel copies of the two half-product annuli, choosing four distinct longitudes on \(\partial\nu c\). Each pair bounds a small annulus there reached from its own half-product. Cap the four circles by four distinct meridian disks in the \(c\)-surgery solid torus. This is the compressed sphere in the weak reduction of GST Proposition 5.2, with the disk caps of both compressions recorded. Each cap meets \(d'_V\) transversely once, with opposite signs in each pair. [GST, Example 4.5, Proposition 5.2 and Corollary 5.4, pp. 2318 and 2321–2323](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=18)

To verify that the sphere is nonseparating, replace each pair of meridian disks by the annulus between their boundaries in the exterior collar. This preserves the class in \(Z'\), since the two disks and connecting annulus enclose a product \(D^2\times I\) in the filling torus and its collar. In \(M_Q\) the resulting genus-two surface represents the middle fiber, by the product annuli used to build it. Injectivity of \(H_2(E_V)\to H_2(Z')\) then gives a nonzero class for the sphere. Pull it back by \(P^{-1}\) and use the original \(\gamma\) for its image in the actual upper boundary. The pullback meets \(d_V\) in exactly four points and is algebraically orthogonal to it. This establishes sharpness for this axis for every \(n\ge3\). The minimum on the \(d_Q\)-orthogonal axis is only bounded below here.

## What can change after a slide or a trace comparison

The one-intersection obstruction rules out either first native reverse \(1/2\) cancellation, for the original pair and every choice of upper belts, with zero births or cancellations in the calculation. The fiber repositioning keeps the reverse anchor \(d_V\) and changes the other reverse tube. An arbitrary new framed slide that changes a core requires recomputing its individual reverse filling. The tested bound also distinguishes the native marks from the product cores of any replacement trace.

The Figure 9 closure \(D^4\cup h_Q^2\cup h_V^2\cup_\gamma U\) is closed and simply connected, with Euler characteristic \(1+2-2+1=2\), hence a homotopy four-sphere by duality, Hurewicz and Whitehead. Since \(Q\) is a generalized square knot, Meier–Zupan Theorem 1.1 identifies that closed smooth manifold with \(S^4\). The previously recorded relative recognition can use this theorem and Laudenbach–Poénaru's embedded upper-body comparison to replace the lower trace by the split two-handle trace while transporting the outlet. If \(B\) denotes its outlet diffeomorphism, the closing and its inverse are \(\gamma B^{-1}\) and \(B\gamma^{-1}\), and the original reverse tubes still read \((\gamma B^{-1})(B o_i)\tau\). The split 2/3 cancellations use its new product co-cores. An explicit *diagrammatic* comparison is GST §10, Proposition 10.1 and Figures 18–20, which uses one canceling Hopf pair for the successive parameter changes from \(n\) to \(0\). The unstabilized slide-only question for \(L_{n,1}\) remains beyond this calculation, which uses no physical collar metric.
