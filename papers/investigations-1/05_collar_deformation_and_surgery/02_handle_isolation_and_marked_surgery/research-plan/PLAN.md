## The first test beyond a known slide

In a mixed diagram the algebraic intersection numbers of the two attaching circles with the lower belt sphere form a primitive row because $H_1(M)=0$. Framed slides implement the Euclidean algorithm and put it in the form $(1,0)$. This gives the class a common starting point. The first circle may still cross three or more times, and the second may cross in both directions. [Incidence calculation and marked slide convention](../../../04_hypersurface_immersions/02_two_handle_recognition/manuscript/recognition_from_marked_morse_heights.tex)

Fix such a diagram in which both circles meet the belt sphere. Suppose the first circle has three crossings, with signs $+,+,-$ up to simultaneous reversal. Cut the common three-dimensional level along the belt sphere and examine the arcs of the attaching link. Can two opposite crossings be removed across an embedded disk bounded by a subarc of the circle and an arc on the belt sphere? The interiors of the disk and its boundary arcs must be clear of the other strands, and the interior of the disk must miss the belt sphere. Transport the full framed tubes and upper gluing through the move. A disk with these properties gives a concrete move to replay. Its existence in a general three-crossing diagram is a research question.

If every candidate disk meets the second tube, test a marked slide involving that tube, reverse the order, or include both attachments in the region to be isolated. Record the obstructing arc or the topology of the new boundary. For a next class, allow an arbitrary odd number of crossings of the chosen circle with algebraic intersection of absolute value one. An induction on crossings would need a removable pair for **each** member of the stated class, with a move that keeps it in that class. This subsidiary count can lead to an eventual handle cancellation. Every claimed decrease in the global $n_2$ is measured after restoring all residual attachments.

The first worked diagram tests the method. The first substantive success would be a geometric or smooth reduction previously missing on a specified family. The [first test with three middle handles](../../research-plan/PLAN.md#scope-of-a-successful-return) asks whether the move works relative to a third framed tube and how to treat the one-sided link. The universal result needs a proof that every unresolved type supplies a member of a covered family, with the side identifications and energy estimates used on that very member.
