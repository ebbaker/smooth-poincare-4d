# Recovering the source metric on subballs of the specified core

CDS-B03 / E02p at [issue 2](../research-progress/handoffs/EXTERIOR_RECOVERY_AND_NATIVE_CAPS_20261126.md), checkout 8471728a1549614f155a8751c709904cbcf994d6. Smooth oriented Riemannian four-manifolds, both real chiral Hilbert–Schmidt energies, and the ordered full curvature tensor norm. Fix one entered E61 index with marked disk D, complete exterior V, and smooth compact subdisk Q inside D with charge \(q^\sigma\). The [balanced construction](../../../master/01_fixed_type_variational_descent/manuscript/balanced_exterior.tex) uses a small original physical-curvature disk for this Q. Denote its complete tensor curvature measure by \(\nu(Q)\). The result below uses this physical quantity.

## Retention up to a selected fraction at the endpoint

Fix \(0<\theta<1/4\) and any error \(\epsilon>0\). There is a joined smooth path on the original marked source to a round matched face such that the endpoint metric on a finite disjoint union \(P\subset\operatorname{int} Q\) is exactly its original metric, with
\[
 E_\sigma(g;P)\ge (1-\theta)^4 q^\sigma-\epsilon,\qquad
 H\le e+C\theta^{-4}\nu(Q)+\epsilon .
\]
Here C is an absolute constant for the fixed cutoff construction. There is no common coordinate or curvature-radius assumption across source indices. The core can temporarily lose all of its charge during the path. The inequality asserts exact endpoint geometry on the selected parts of the specified Q, with control in both original measures. On the whole of Q it asserts an endpoint mass lower bound. When the bound for H is strictly below \(2T\), both complete caps are positive. This gives a strictly subcritical example for every fixed fractional loss if E61's original tolerance \(\zeta>\nu(Q)\) is chosen small compared with \((2T-I)\theta^4\) before selecting the disks. The resulting positive charge floor can depend on that tolerance. For a previously selected Q the finite inequality uses its own measured \(\nu(Q)\).

Use oriented normal coordinates in balls of radius r about points p of int Q. In the rescaled unit chart write \(g_r=r^{-2}(\exp_p(r\cdot))^*g\). At each fixed smooth point Taylor expansion in normal coordinates gives
\[
 \|g_r-\delta\|_{C^2(B_1)}
       \le C r^2(|\mathrm{Rm}_g(p)|+a)
\]
for any \(a>0\) and all sufficiently small r, with a dimensional C independent of the point and source. Indeed the quadratic Taylor coefficient is a fixed linear combination of curvature components at p, and the remainder is \(o_p(r^2)\) through two scaled derivatives. Frames are orthonormal. Require also \(r^2(|\mathrm{Rm}(p)|+a)\ll1\), uniform ellipticity and volume ratios between one half and two. For \(0\le t\le1\) make the local family
\[
 k_t=\delta+t\chi_\theta(g_r-\delta)
\]
on the unit ball. The radial cutoff equals one through radius \(1-\theta\), vanishes on a neighborhood of the outer face and has derivatives of orders 1 and 2 bounded by \(C/\theta\) and \(C/\theta^2\). The ordinary curvature formula and ellipticity give \(|\mathrm{Rm}(k_t)|\le C\theta^{-2}r^2(|\mathrm{Rm}(p)|+a)\), uniformly in t, including the quadratic gradient term. Thus the two integrated energies are at most \(C\theta^{-4}r^4(|\mathrm{Rm}(p)|+a)^2\). Scaling back to the full normal chart changes neither integral. In the inner ball the resulting endpoint is exactly g.

At fixed index the allowed balls form a fine Vitali covering of int Q. Shrink radii further so the oscillations of \(|\mathrm{Rm}|\) and of both chiral volume densities expressed in normal coordinates are at most arbitrary fixed tolerances from their central values. For each ball this gives
\[
 \lambda^\sigma(B_{(1-\theta)r})\ge(1-\theta)^4\lambda^\sigma(B_r)-a'\operatorname{Vol}_g B_r,
 \quad
 r^4(|\mathrm{Rm}(p)|+a)^2\le C\int_{B_r}(|\mathrm{Rm}|^2+a^2)dV_g .
\]
For the second inequality require the curvature norm oscillation below a. Vitali selection gives countably many disjoint chart balls covering up to volume zero and therefore up to zero for all three smooth measures. A finite subfamily misses arbitrarily little of both chiral masses. Shrink these finitely many radii slightly inside the disjoint open balls to obtain disjoint enlarged chart buffers; the smooth faces are null, so the additional loss can be made arbitrarily small. Reapply the same local oscillation estimates to the shrunk radii. The smooth boundary of Q has zero mass. Summing the two estimates proves the mass claim on the inner subballs and bounds the cost of every simultaneous local interpolation by \(C\theta^{-4}(\nu(Q)+a^2\operatorname{Vol}Q)\). The tolerances and finite truncation can be chosen at this very index. The same Vitali selection on physical subballs is used in the [balanced exterior proof](../../../master/01_fixed_type_variational_descent/manuscript/balanced_exterior.tex).

## Placing all subballs in the original disk

First use the [fixed-source transport](TRANSPORT_ON_THE_CHARGED_DISK.md) with no angular shear, to arbitrary two-sign error \(\eta>0\). It gives a smooth metric on the original source with exact positive native subwidths at the original face, cylindrical of radius \(\ell\). The complete exterior now has energy \(e+\Delta\) in each sign, \(|\Delta|\le\eta\). Its round, flat-Weyl filling is the same positive flattened original type. The retained D is conformally Euclidean in one smooth oriented chart (the radial image in the small flat ball), and the marked face is unchanged in this chart's boundary parametrization. Keep both of these outer collar germs and V fixed from here on.

A compactly supported isotopy of int D identifies the chosen finite set of parametrized source normal balls with disjoint affine round balls in that Euclidean chart, including small open buffers for the gluing. Here the map on each ball is the prescribed oriented normal coordinate followed by a positive similarity. To see that this can be arranged in the identity component, use embeddings of slightly enlarged disjoint balls. First shrink each parametrized ball about its center by precomposing its parametrization with a small dilation. In chart coordinates its image on the fixed parameter domain is then \(c+sLx+O(s^2)\) in \(C^1\) with invertible positive L. The straight interpolation to its affine jet remains embedded for small s, since its scaled derivative differs from L by less than its least singular value. Connect L inside \(GL^+(4)\) to a positive similarity, keeping all small balls disjoint. These isotopies of the parametrizations extend ambiently by cutting off their velocity fields on disjoint neighborhoods inside D. Thus no assertion about the full diffeomorphism group of a four-ball is required. Pull back the conformally Euclidean metric along the isotopy. A further conformal multiplier, equal to one near the face and chosen on the disjoint chart balls with a small buffer, makes this base metric exactly the Euclidean metric in each original physical normal coordinate. This corrects both the affine scale and the existing pointwise conformal factor. Throughout these two steps every integral on D is zero, the exterior and seam stay fixed, and the remainder of D may take any smooth conformally Euclidean metric so obtained.

Insert all the local \(k_t\) in these physical charts, scaled back by \(r^2\); their outer germs agree with that Euclidean base. Off the subballs the disk stays conformally flat; every annular transition inside every ball was included in its price. The full exterior has energy \(e+\Delta\) at all insertion times, while D has exactly the sum of the insertion energies. Each summand is equal for both signs: extend its Euclidean outer germ by a conformally flat exterior on the standard four-sphere, then apply closed signature. This extension is for the local integral equality and presupposes no scalar sign. The same-type cap is unchanged at \(e+\Delta\ge I\). The capped D at time t uses the original radial compactification and has the sum of these new energies. The radial attaching maps and inverses are those of the [original transport](TRANSPORT_ON_THE_CHARGED_DISK.md#complete-sides-and-both-caps); all subsequent maps are the identity near the face. The full joined peak satisfies the claimed inequality on choosing the several additive errors with sum below \(\epsilon\). Strict headroom implies a positive class along the whole path and for both caps by the round-cylinder argument.

The family can recover as high a fixed fraction of the original charge as specified before selection, with greater potential cost as \(\theta\) decreases. Refining through infinitely many subballs would require marked smooth control which these estimates do not give. This construction never improves the preexisting exterior cap energy. In particular it gives neither a cap below I nor all-time preservation of the original metric on the prescribed Q.
