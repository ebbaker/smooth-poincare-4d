For this *fixed* component and framing, the following are equivalent.

1. $\mu=1$ in $\pi_1(X)$.
2. $\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z$.
3. Framed surgery on this component gives $S^3$.
4. After a boundary re-marking extending over the lower body, the component meets the belt sphere once. The full framed spectator tubes may move with this operation.

Filling the meridian quotients the group by the normal closure of $\mu$ and gives $\mathbb Z$. Hence 1 implies 2. If the group is infinite cyclic, its surjection onto the same $\mathbb Z$ is an isomorphism and 1 follows. The framed filling group is also a quotient of this cyclic group. Its abelianization vanishes, since the ordinary two-component surgery determinant for the winding-one component is $-1$ regardless of framing. Thus 2 gives simple connectivity of the filled closed three-manifold and the three-dimensional Poincaré theorem gives 3. Under 3 the dual knot in $S^3$ has surgery to $S^1\times S^2$. A change from those coordinates to the original boundary marking extends over $S^1\times D^3$ by Laudenbach–Poénaru. This gives 4 with the original integer framing carried through the map. Finally, puncturing the dual belt sphere at its single transverse intersection with the core gives a meridional disk in $X$ after shrinking the tube by isotopy. This proves 1.
