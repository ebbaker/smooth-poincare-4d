## From two middle handles to three

After the end trees are reduced to one minimum and one maximum, the handle counts satisfy $n_1+n_3=n_2$ by Euler characteristic. For $n_2=2$ there are two cases up to reversal. The mixed case $(n_1,n_3)=(1,1)$ starts with the common belt sphere. The one-sided case $(0,2)$ starts with a two-component framed link in $S^3$. It needs a recognizing trace or a marked reduction of that link. The surgery branch tests both families and records which diagrams they cover. [Handle counts and recognition tests](../../04_hypersurface_immersions/02_two_handle_recognition/manuscript/recognition_from_marked_morse_heights.tex)

The first test above that count is $n_2=3$. In the $(1,2)$ presentation, look for the earlier two-attachment move in the presence of a third framed tube. Even when that tube crosses the region of the move, a successful reduction must return its actual attaching map and the upper gluing. One net cancellation then gives a two-middle-handle diagram to which the previous classification can be applied, provided the returned diagram lies in a covered family. The other case up to reversal, $(0,3)$, requires a corresponding test for a three-component framed surgery link. It gives the first one-sided family left out by the mixed argument. Proving the move relative to *arbitrarily many* residual tubes would make induction possible on its domain. A separate selection result must show which presentations contain such a move.

There is also a shorter route to a class of smooth types. On a hypothetical nonstandard type with a Euclidean hypersurface immersion of induced energy $E_+<8\pi^2$, the [immersed height theorem](../../../master/manuscript/immersion_entry.tex) already supplies a set of directions of relative measure greater than three quarters with exactly two middle handles. For this input it suffices to find **one** such height whose actual marked diagram the surgery branch can recognize. Proving recognition for all reduced two-middle-handle diagrams would remove the direction-selection issue for that class. Producing an immersion below this energy on every remaining type is a separate open entry problem. A direct handle reduction on an arbitrary Morse presentation can proceed without this metric hypothesis.

## What would make the argument terminate?

Choose a presentation of a hypothetical nonstandard $M$ with the least possible number $n_2$ of index-two handles. Such a minimum exists among finite Morse presentations. The end-tree reductions and the cases $n_2\le1$ are covered by the [marked height argument](../../04_hypersurface_immersions/02_two_handle_recognition/manuscript/recognition_from_marked_morse_heights.tex). No special immersion is needed for the Morse handle count in these base cases.

A sufficient **open selection theorem** would give, for every remaining least-count presentation, at least one of the following outcomes.

1. A marked equivalence to a recognized presentation, or a new presentation on the same $M$ with strictly fewer index-two handles after all slides and temporary stabilizations. A strictly smaller count contradicts minimality.
2. A direct recognizing metric on $M$, for example $Y([g])>0$ and $E_+(g)\le T$, where $T=4\pi^2$.
3. Positive subcritical entry on this $M$ and a marked repair on at least one finite member of a minimizing sequence which gives a positive class on $M$ below $I^+(M)$. For a spherical cut, a sufficient test is to violate the [one-cut inequality](../../../master/manuscript/marked_cut.tex) on the two complete charged sides.

For a class-by-class proof, a new complexity measure can replace $n_2$ only after proving well-foundedness, strict decrease on the full returned presentation and adequate base cases. Repeated slides at constant complexity can be substeps of one finite reduction. Repeated metric improvements converging to the same infimum do not prove termination. Smooth closed critical limits, concentration with no controlled cut, round limits with charge escaping through one end, and types outside the entry band remain available alternatives until covered. The first application alone supplies none of these universal selection statements.

## Selecting the penultimate cut

On a one-sided presentation, or on a normalized one-lower presentation whose penultimate prefix includes the winding-one attachment, test that boundary first. A PSC-dominated prefix now removes the last middle handle and an upper 3-handle. Compare this mixed successor with the one-sided successor from a winding-one first cut. When the winding-one component is placed last instead, test the consecutive zero-winding levels. A full chain recognizes by a single inverse circle surgery. A gap of two successive attachments over a free boundary is the first case beyond the single-knot argument. The [assessment](../reviews/PENULTIMATE_CUTS_AND_CURVED_FILLINGS_20261126.md) identifies the remaining selection and metric premises.

## Beyond the single-cut test

On two consecutive attachments retain both peripheral meridians and both actual filling slopes. A relative split pair is sufficient inside a longer chain, while the terminal tunnel criterion can use temporary 2/3 pairs. On the physical disk evaluate both finite costs at an admissible width and the positive capped exterior form. For a path with common support, test the actual isolated collar energies over the entire deformation. ## After the nonlocal family and the fixed-source transport

Begin the next topological test with the actual exterior of a further pair and its filling slopes. Recover a simplifying band or disk from those data, then carry it across the residual tubes and the actual closing. The cyclic intersection invariant recognizes complexity in the family already reduced but does not select such a band on another diagram.

On a positive subcritical type, test the *entire* capped exterior at a finite index. A supported transport can already make the specified face round at arbitrarily small extra joined energy by moving the disk mass into the exterior. For a proposed improvement that preserves charge \(q_i^\sigma\) in a marked core, require room for a peak of at least \(I+\max_\sigma q_i^\sigma\), strictly below \(2T\). Evaluate the cap boundary response and both finite connector costs at the same width, with any mass away from the strip included. Positive subcritical entry on a further type and smooth compactness on the original markings each require evidence on their own supplying source.
