## The sphere quantifiers

Cut the inlet along any eligible sphere $S$. Nonseparation makes the cut piece connected and gives a loop crossing $S$ once, so the intersection homomorphism $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ is an isomorphism. Stacking copies of the cut piece gives the specified cyclic cover. Each stationary lift is contained in the interior of one copy, with the copies of $Q_0$ and $Q_n$ separated by $n$ interfaces. If the moving lift missed one of these two open copies, an interface on its side of the missed copy would split it from that stationary lift in the compactification. Thus the closed moving lift visits both copies and crosses each intervening interface at least twice. Its projection to the moving component is one-to-one, so the lower bound counts points downstairs.

A horizontal sphere at height $1/2$ modulo the deck period meets the band in exactly $n$ sections and the moving knot in $2n$ points. The fixed stationary tube can be placed entirely in $B$ from the outset. More generally, a sphere missing only the core can be made to miss a specified stationary tube disjoint from the moving knot without changing its intersection count. Take a smaller concentric tube disjoint from the sphere and expand it to the specified tube by a radial diffeomorphism supported in a slightly larger tube missing the moving knot.

The exact $2n$ is a **minimum**. The independent-read dispatch can be read as asking for equality on each eligible sphere. A small finger isotopy of an attaining sphere across a moving strand, supported in a ball disjoint from the stationary tube, introduces two transverse points and preserves nonseparation. All eligible intersection counts are even since the moving component is null-homologous.

## Prefixes, exterior rank and closing

The joint filling is $Y_3$ by the two displayed slides. Both cores are null-homologous and excision gives $H_1(E)=\mathbb Z^3$, with the meridians giving two independent generators. Both filling slopes vanish in this group because the joint filling still has free rank three. Expressing each slope as its Seifert longitude plus a meridional multiple gives zero framings and zero linking. A single filling followed by meridional filling on the other torus gives $H_1(Z_J)=\mathbb Z^2$ for $J=Q,K_n$.

The actual prefix $W_J$ has a ball and two index-two handles. It is simply connected and $H_2(W_J)=\mathbb Z^2$. Poincaré–Lefschetz duality and the pair sequence give the exact sequence
$$
0\longrightarrow H_2(Z_J)\longrightarrow H_2(W_J)
 \longrightarrow H_2(W_J,Z_J)\longrightarrow H_1(Z_J)\longrightarrow0 .
$$
The last map is a surjection of free groups of rank two, so $H_2(Z_J)\to H_2(W_J)$ is an isomorphism. PSC domination would give exactly two product factors and a residual sum of spherical integral homology spheres. Add auxiliary 3-handles along the two product spheres. Their relative boundary map kills the displayed integral basis of $H_2(W_J)$, giving a simply connected acyclic smooth filling of the residual sum. The obstruction to such a contractible filling makes the residual sum $S^3$. Thus $Z_J\cong Y_2$, and rank-increasing single-knot surgery in $Y_1$ would make $J$ a local unknot. A disk would lift to a disk in the compactified cover for a square knot. This contradiction applies to both original orders.
