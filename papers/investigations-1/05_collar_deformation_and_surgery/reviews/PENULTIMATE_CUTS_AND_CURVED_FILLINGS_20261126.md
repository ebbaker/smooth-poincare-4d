## A reducing cut before the last handle

Let $Y_r=\#_r(S^1\times S^2)$, with $Y_0=S^3$. In a reduced presentation of a closed oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere, let the upper handlebody have rank $q\ge1$. If the sublevel through all but the last middle handle is simply connected and its actual boundary receives a nonzero-degree map from a closed oriented PSC three-manifold, **one middle handle and one upper 3-handle cancel on the original smooth manifold**. In a least-middle-count presentation this is already a contradiction. No property of the surviving link is needed to count the decrease.

There are two steps. The sublevel has zero intersection pairing in the homotopy sphere. Compressing the product summands of its PSC-dominated boundary in an auxiliary filling, followed by the Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee obstruction, identifies the actual boundary as $Y_{q-1}$. A single integral surgery $Y_r\to Y_{r+1}$ is a split zero-framed unknot. To prove the latter, the two presentations of the trace group show that it has zero second integral homology and that the entering free generators map isomorphically on first homology. Hopf's formula and residual nilpotence force the incoming knot word to be trivial. Common product summands can be removed while preserving both slopes. Ni's theorem now yields a disk for the knot, which survives restoration of those summands. In the outgoing level the surgery core crosses a factor sphere once. The boundary change extends over the actual upper handlebody. For the original closing map $\delta$ to the upper trace and cancellation map $\Xi$, the new gluing is $\partial\Xi\,\delta$ and its inverse is $\delta^{-1}(\partial\Xi)^{-1}$. ## The actual mixed metric

On Isoshima's $n=1$ double use the displayed small meridian first. Both actual pieces at this level are $(S^1\times D^3)\natural(S^2\times D^2)$. A PSC metric with product collar on each can be joined along the actual boundary map by a slow PSC concordance. Inserting a constant product of length $L$ gives a global smooth PSC metric whose central $Y_2$ is the marked meridian-first level. Its projection has degree one, its second variation for normal variations is nonnegative, its intrinsic width is $L$, and both outward means are zero. With $\rho=\min R_h$, the choice $L=\pi\sqrt{3/\rho}$ meets the admitting width threshold. In the opposite order the Mazur boundary has no PSC domination in either orientation. The metric was constructed on the meridian-first trace. No width from that band is transferred to the reordered level.

Each chirality of the product costs $L\int|\operatorname{Ric}^0_h|^2/4$, half on each complete side, in addition to the fixed pieces and transition. The coefficient is positive. Thus a large width is compatible with positive scalar curvature and with energy above $8\pi^2$. The example verifies a genuine geometric witness on an already standard smooth type. ## The physical disk and its exterior

A strictly convex flat filling gives a finite-source test which also covers nonumbilic first jets. In a volume-one positive Yamabe metric $g$ use the actual boundary metric $h$, shape endomorphism $K$ in the normal pointing from the disk into the exterior, and $H=\operatorname{tr}K$. Put $G=R_h\operatorname{Id}/2-\operatorname{Ric}_h^\sharp$. When $G>0$ the only candidate positive Euclidean shape is $S=\sqrt{\det G}\,G^{-1}$. Codazzi gives the global convex filling. If $S^\circ=K^\circ$, a boundary value and normal derivative of the conformal multiplier match the complete first jets. Keep the whole exterior fixed and smooth on the cap side at a positive width inside the two available inward chart radii. At fixed index the sole connector costs $O_i(w)$ in **each** sign, while the rest of the replacement ball is conformally flat.

Signature alone forces $m_D^+=m_D^-$ under this exact fit. With $H\le\operatorname{tr}S$ the endpoint quadratic form is coercive. Its boundary term is $2\int (\operatorname{tr}S-H)f^2$, in addition to the exterior Yamabe form and the flat cap's Dirichlet energy. The positive same-type metrics obtained this way give $m_D^\pm\le e-I^+(M)$. The angular discrepancy extends over the parametrized disk. For any strict reverse inequality some **positive** width already contradicts the infimum. The argument prices a replacement of the entire original disk and establishes positivity of its endpoint independently. A path to that endpoint with a prescribed joined peak, or a modification keeping an interior core unchanged, needs its own evidence. The evaluation excludes all the simultaneous boundary conditions on each sufficiently late selected charged disk; it gives no measured convex filling on one of those disks. For an independent compact interior preparation, positivity already limits its attainable saving to $d_i=e_i-I$. If its path decreases monotonically by $S$ and its complete support is disjoint from all parts of a prescribed seam path of peak $H_C$, the optimal combined peak for these two paths is $\max(e_i,H_C-S)$. The exterior cap, including all its changed strips and connectors, has the same energy as with the seam path alone. On the original raw bicollar a matched, subcritical comparison of this kind must have $H\ge I+(1-\eta)m_i^{s_*}-d_i$ in the selected sign. The available charge floor need not reach the wall margin.

On an enlarged disk retain the exact mass $b_i^\sigma=(1-2^{-k})e_i+2^{-k}q_i^\sigma-r_i^\sigma$. A matched path with $H_i<2T$ needs $S_D^\sigma\ge b_i^\sigma+I-H_i$ and complete exterior increase at least $b_i^\sigma-d_i$, for each sign. An independently positive disjoint preparation plus a seam of raw disk-side mass $c_i^\sigma$ supplies at most $d_i+c_i^\sigma$. For fixed sufficiently large $k$ and $c_i^\sigma\to0$ this excludes that path. A simultaneous disjoint comparison with a strict joined ceiling must then have an isolated crossing and a full peak in $[2T-d_i,2T)$. A subcritical endpoint by itself does not bound this peak.
