## Both pattern disks

Use the stationary tube coordinates \(q(s,r,\theta)\) determined by
its zero framing. Take the two companion tori in disjoint small
outward sectors centered at \((r_1,\theta_i)\). Their inward radial
sheets run from the respective preferred longitude to \(\partial\nu Q\)
at azimuth \(\theta_i\). A push-off along one sheet is a zero parallel,
since the native framing of \(Q\) is zero. In the stationary filling
\(D^2_f\times S^1_\theta\), the sheet closes with
\(D^2_f\times\{\theta_i\}\). Parallel copies use two close but distinct
values of \(\theta\) in the interval for that sector. They have
disjoint buffers and meet the core \(\{0\}\times S^1_\theta\) once
each. Take the angular intervals for the two carrier tori disjoint.

In the standard Whitehead link, use the disk bounded by the
component without self-crossings in the displayed planar diagram.
The companion passes through it twice with opposite signs. Removing
the meridian disks about these intersections gives an embedded pair
of pants in the complementary solid torus. Its two inner cuffs are
meridians of the deleted component. The meridian–longitude
interchange makes them both native zero longitudes in the satellite
torus, with opposite orientations. Ordered parallel copies of the
radial caps can be matched to those cuffs in a collar, preserving
the outward access sector. Gluing and rounding produces an embedded
disk at each azimuth with one puncture of each sign. The two sets of
cap pieces are disjoint. [Hedden–Kirk, arXiv:1009.5361v2, §§3.1–3.2
and Figure 4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1009.5361#page=18) ·
[Thurston, *A Norm for the Homology of 3-Manifolds*, Example 1 and
Figure 2](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~ctm/home/text/others/thurston/norm/norm.pdf)

Choose the mouths at regular boundary points of the disks, away
from the core and from the capped cuffs. Near either mouth the disk
has a boundary half-disk chart. An access path enters the torus on
its outward side, and its entrance germ continues the mouth chart.
The cap sheets stay within the two stationary charts. The path
from the rail gap leaves on the side away from the
sweep. The complement of the swept rectangle is connected around
its free rail edges. Thus the access paths can be kept off the sweep
away from the marked rail gap, off the shallow-cap collar except
at its own entrance, and away from the other strands, with disjoint
interiors by general position of paths in dimension three. The
paths to the carrier tori may cross the old hemisphere, whose own
survival in the unfilled drill is unnecessary. Thicken the paths
using the two product fingers and disjoint buffers. This realizes
the relative product geometry in the issue.

rectangle extends the disk to the prescribed short chord. In the

swept rectangle joins the actual rail gap chord to the \(12\)

## Sharpness for each marked completion

Capping the appropriate slab faces and using the short product
chords recovers the capped square-knot diagram with that completion
\(J\). Any rival prescribed-chord disk can be capped at its short
mouth in the same chart. Forgetting the other drilled arcs only
enlarges the class of candidates. After this restoration the chord
can be isotoped with endpoint collars on the capped sphere, away
from \(Q\) and the filling core; it gives the same capped knot.
For a zero-puncture disk, shrink
the core tube to miss it and absorb the remainder of its
stationary filling as a collar. Such a disk would give an embedded
spanning disk of \(J\) in the unfilled capped exterior of \(Q\).

The Whitehead pattern exterior has an incompressible outer
torus. After restoring \(J\), a compression would be a meridian
disk of the carrier solid torus disjoint from \(J\), contrary to
its geometric index two. This positive geometric index also
precludes splitting the pattern from the deleted companion unknot:
shrink the companion tube away from a putative splitting sphere,
and its complementary solid torus would contain the pattern in
a ball, giving a disjoint meridional disk. Thus the pattern
exterior is irreducible. The parallel link \(Q\cup L\) is nonsplit.
A splitting sphere must meet the parallel annulus in an essential
circle by mod-two intersection of a joining arc, putting the
nontrivial knot type \(Q\) on a sphere. Hence its exterior is
irreducible. The companion torus injects into it, since it
already injects after forgetting \(Q\), by the Loop Theorem and
the fact that a longitude disk for nontrivial \(L\) would unknot
it. Gluing the irreducible exteriors along their incompressible
torus preserves irreducibility by removing innermost intersection
circles. The capped link \(J\cup Q\) is therefore nonsplit,
excluding the zero-puncture disk. [Andrist–Garity–Repovš–Wright,
arXiv:1711.04267v1, Figure 3(a) and §5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1711.04267)

For one puncture, deleting its small core-meridian disk makes the
rest an annulus in the capped unfilled exterior. It would conjugate
\(J\) to \(\lambda_Q^{\pm1}\). The left class is trivial because
the winding in the carrier solid torus is zero. The right class
is nontrivial by Dehn's lemma and the consequence of joining a
proper longitude disk to \(Q\). There is also a direct check of
all algebraic counts for the fixed boundary. The capped filled
manifold \(M=S^3_0(Q)\) is a bundle over \(S^1\) with a closed
genus-two fiber, so \(\pi_2(M)=0\). Two spanning disks for that
boundary give a sphere map on gluing their boundaries with opposite
orientations. Its homology class, and therefore its algebraic
intersection with the oriented filling core, is zero. Every
rival disk for that boundary has the same algebraic count as the
exhibited disk, namely zero. Together with nonsplitting this proves
sharpness even for disks leaving the support of the charts. The
comparison retains the marked capped completion throughout. These
are geometric puncture bounds for the indicated bridge moves.
[GST, §6, p. 2323](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=19) ·
[Papakyriakopoulos, Dehn's lemma](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf#page=2)

## Support and the three sphere minima

Index the moving lift by its two through rails in chamber \(0\).
Across the bottom copy of the seam it enters the upper \(34\)
return of chamber \(-1\); across the top copy it enters the
lower \(12\) return of chamber \(1\). The two internal seams each
meet this moving lift twice. Cutting there and capping off the
ends with the native chords reads the three joint sublinks
\((Q_{-1},J_{\rm upper})\), \((Q_0,J_{\rm rail})\) and
\((Q_1,U)\). The first two are nonsplit by the companion-torus
argument. The third is nonsplit since the restored unknot \(U\)
has a nontrivial class in \(S^3_0(Q)\), whereas a ball disjoint
from \(Q\) containing \(U\) would survive that surgery.

Keeping one chosen stationary component and forgetting the
other copies reads the omitted arcs as local knots tied into
its capped moving component. A splitting sphere for the
extended link may be made disjoint from each tying sphere.
An innermost disk on the splitting sphere cannot cut off a
single arc endpoint on the tying sphere, since capping it
would give odd mod-two intersection of a closed knot with
a sphere. A puncture-free disk can instead be used for sphere
surgery, retaining a sphere separating the components by
mod-two intersection with a joining path. Repeating eliminates
the intersections. Thus adding the other local arcs preserves
nonsplitting. Every unoccupied stationary copy lies in a
ball disjoint from the moving lift, and the support is exactly
\(\{-1,0,1\}\).

For any transverse nonseparating sphere disjoint from the
stationary \(Q\), its slit complement and deck translates
stack in the same cyclic cover. The three stationary indices
occupy consecutive chambers for that sphere as well, since
the deck generator takes every chamber to the next. The
nonzero pair supports force the closed moving lift to enter
each indicated stationary chamber. If it missed that chamber,
a boundary sphere would separate it from the chosen stationary
copy. In the two-end compactification each intervening
sphere must accordingly be crossed at least twice. The
closed moving lift projects bijectively to the moving knot
because its winding is zero. The displayed four intersections
are therefore sharp. An unfilled essential disk on either
four-holed face separates the punctures \(2+2\), so surgery on
the sphere would give two two-point spheres whose oriented
sum is the original sphere class. At least one would be
nonseparating, contradicting the bound. A bridge after
stationary filling and an unfilled face disk consequently give
different tests on this example.

After shortening the upper pattern, its shallow cap can be
