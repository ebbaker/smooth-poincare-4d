## Native slopes and the finite quotient

In the base fixture the lower hemisphere separates the drilled slab.
The short-arc ball exterior has annular product structure relative
to the annulus left on the ball boundary. Reattaching it identifies the
lower factor with \(P=S^3_0(Q)\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu U\) and
preserves the meridian \(\mu\) and the zero longitude \(\lambda\) of
the capped Figure 5 unknot \(U\). Restoring its tube gives
\(P(\mu)=M=S^3_0(Q)\). Filling by \(\lambda\) gives \(P(\lambda)=Y_2\)
by the published slide of \(Q\) over \(U\). The slope classes before
either filling have \([\mu]\) primitive and \([\lambda]=0\) in
\(H_1(P;\mathbb Z)\). This follows from the free rank-two homology
of the joint exterior and joint outlet, which forces both original
slope relations to vanish. [GST, Figure 5 and Proposition 2.2,
pp. 2310–2311](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=6)

The two native fillings also independently exclude a compressing
torus disk. Its primitive slope would be \(\lambda\). Compressing
a boundary collar separates a punctured solid torus, leaving a
closed summand \(C\) after capping the sphere. The two filled
manifolds would then be \(C\) and \(C\#Y_1\), respectively, since
the native slopes have distance one. This would make \(\pi_1(M)\)
a free factor of \(F_2\). The independently checked genus-two
surface bundle description of \(M\) embeds the fiber group in
\(\pi_1(M)\). That fiber group cannot embed in a free group, since
it has second integral group homology \(\mathbb Z\) while free
groups have zero second homology. The argument includes a reducible
putative return factor because both fillings retain \(C\).
[GST, §6, p. 2323](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=19)

## The projection for the two insertions

For each of the zero-, one- and two-insertion slabs, let
\(X_i\) be its stationary-filled drilled exterior. Restore all
moving arc tubes to get the same \(W_Q\), the stationary-filled
slab, then cap its two sphere boundaries. The latter step
preserves the fundamental group and gives \(M\). The composite
\(\pi_1(X_i)\to\pi_1(M)\to H\rtimes\mathbb Z/3\) is onto. For the
first arrow, a loop in \(W_Q\) can be moved off the proper arcs
and then pushed to the drilled complement. Each marked wall
section restores to an arc parallel to the same \(U^\circ\).
Radial projection in the restored tube removes its winding.
Every chord between the same two endpoints is homotopic rel
endpoints on the restored lower \(S^2\), and tracks of variable
endpoints stay in their filled end disks. The resulting loop
therefore closes \(U^\circ\) by the original short chord, giving
the class of \(U\) in \(M\) up to conjugacy and reversal. This
uses the unchanged lower return and stationary tube in each
insertion, even when its access path passes through the old
lower disk. It proves the lower bridge obstruction for every
chord and winding in both nonlocal variants.
