## Embedded bands and signed words

At the plus spots, clockwise orientation has tangent toward decreasing \(s\) at the bottom of a spot. The \(\alpha_+\) foot of \(\delta_0\) is at the east point and the \(\beta_+\) foot at the west point. Thus a sufficiently narrow twist strip at \(t=1/8\) gives two laps of \(YzX\), while \(t=7/8\) is above both meridian arcs and gives one lap of \(z\). The gluings match precisely the two marked endpoints on each spot and preserve the required reversal of the surface-boundary orientation. The meridian classes are independent by the three dual test curves, so cutting along all of them gives one six-holed sphere. The associated paired-disk gluing defines the asserted marked handlebody.

Use the side of the \(\alpha_+\) band foot just below its east point to join the seam to the first outer curve \(r_1\). It avoids \(m_x\), whose endpoint is at the bottom of the spot. Starting with the clockwise \(\alpha_+\) circuit at that sector gives \(r_1=xw y w^{-1}\), where \(w=(YzX)^2z\). The strip on the same side can connect \(r_1\) through the matched \(\alpha\) seam to \(\rho\), avoiding meridians before entering \(\rho\). The latter travels in decreasing \(s\) through \(m_z\), giving \(Z\). The second band leaves \(r_1\) on the first pair-of-pants side and approaches \(\beta\) from its minus side. The boundary directions with the second pair of pants on the left are \(r_1^{-1}\) at the former end and \(Y\) at the latter. Its outer boundary therefore reads \(r_1^{-1} ZYz\) up to conjugacy and inversion. Inverting and rotating gives \(r_2=r_1Zyz\). The seam \(\alpha\) is available to cross because the first slide replaced the disk with boundary \(\alpha\); the band has interior disjoint from both current disk curves.

I checked that both expanded words are cyclically reduced and contain the six adjacencies specified in the construction. With edge convention \(u-v^{-1}\), they give the spanning cycle \(x-y-X-Z-Y-z-x\). The separable-set cut-vertex lemma excludes a proper free factor carrying either word. Cutting \(H\) along a compressing disk disjoint from the relevant curve would yield such a factor, also for a separating disk. The curves are essential individually, so any loop essential in \(\partial H\setminus r_i\) and bounding a compression disk is also essential in \(\partial H\). The compression bodies supply connected genus-two surfaces after either single boundary compression. Hence \(H[r_i]\) meets every hypothesis of CGLS Lemma 2.1.1, including the nonspherical compressed-boundary condition. Its boundary is the entire compressed genus-two surface and is incompressible, and \(H[r_i]\) is irreducible. [Stallings–Stong criterion, proof in Heusener–Weidmann Corollary 3.5](https://d-nb.info/1364519550/34#page=6) · [CGLS Lemma 2.1.1, p. 267](https://marc-culler.info/static/home/papers/CyclicSurgery.pdf#page=32)

## Proper arcs and the carried third tube

The initial attaching curves \(\alpha,\beta\) have geometric intersection one against only their corresponding meridians. Attaching both three-dimensional two-handles cancels those two one-handles and leaves the solid torus \(V\). Disk slides in the upper compression body preserve its cut-open product with its negative torus. For the slid pair, collapse that product collar to recover \(V\) as \(H\) with the two marked \(D^2\times I_s\) handles attached. Each transverse co-core \(\{0\}\times I_s\) is then a proper arc, with its ends on the two free disk faces of the handle. Equivalently extend the ends along separate product fibers to \(\partial V\) before collapsing the collar. For either handle, drilling a smaller co-core neighborhood leaves \((D^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D^2_\epsilon)\times I_s\), just a collar on the annular attaching face. Drilling both therefore recovers \(H\). Drilling \(b\) alone restores the second handle and gives \(H[r_2]\); drilling \(c\) alone gives \(H[r_1]\).

Take a sufficiently small horizontal annulus about \(t=1/2\) in the original four-spotted torus. It misses the spots, the full support of \(\delta,\rho\), and all the thin pants and bands used by the two slides. It is consequently carried through the specified compressions and their inverse disk slides by a product collar over that annulus. In the original compression along \(\alpha,\beta\), its core meets the remaining meridian \(m_z\) once, so it gives a longitudinal annulus in \(V\). Choose the identification of \(V\) with the straight-arc exterior \((D^2\setminus\operatorname{int}D_\epsilon^2)\times I_s\) to send this longitudinal annulus to \(\partial D_\epsilon^2\times I_s\). Such an identification is available by the solid-torus boundary maps that preserve the meridian and by a boundary isotopy. The co-core endpoints are away from this unaffected annulus and therefore lie on the outer punctured sphere. Restoring \(D^2_\epsilon\times I_s\) makes the ambient ball \(D^2\times I_s\) with \(a=\{0\}\times I_s\). This supplies the claimed third arc and a product tube for it disjoint from both inverse band supports.

Multiplying the three arc neighborhoods by \(I_t\) gives attaching faces \(\partial D^2\times I_s\times I_t\) for the reverse two-handles. The forward faces are \(D^2\times\partial(I_s\times I_t)\). The product push-off of each boundary knot bounds a disjoint parallel disk in \(B^3\times I_t\), which gives framing zero by its linking number. The drilled product boundaries for \(A\cup B\) and \(A\cup C\) are respectively the full doubles of \(H[r_2]\) and \(H[r_1]\). Gluing irreducible pieces along their whole incompressible boundaries gives irreducible doubles. The drilled product for all three arcs is \(H\times I_t\), with the indicated identity face coordinates for the closing. The two inverse disk slides, thickened in the product, return the two reverse attaching circles to \(\alpha,\beta\) while preserving the chosen third attaching tube. This checks the supplied control and its prescribed moves.
