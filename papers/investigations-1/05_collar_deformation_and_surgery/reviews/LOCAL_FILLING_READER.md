## Local cap and its two-handle complement

Write $Y_s=\#_s(S^1\times S^2)$, $Q_s=\natural_s(S^1\times D^3)$, with $Y_0=S^3$ and $Q_0=D^4$. Take a two-attachment block whose incoming face is $Y_r$ and whose two disjoint framed solid tori lie in the interior of a specified smooth ball $B\subset Y_r$. Let $L$ be their framed link in the sphere obtained by capping $B$ with another three-ball. Suppose the outgoing face is $Y_{r+2}$ and a constituent of this local $L$ is a generalized square knot $T_{p,q}\#T_{-p,q}$ with $p,q\ge2$ coprime. The split as a connected sum along $\partial B$ identifies the outgoing face with $Y_r\# S^3_L$, so uniqueness of oriented prime decomposition gives $S^3_L\cong Y_2$. If local surgery to $Y_2$ is given directly, this prime-decomposition step is unnecessary. The integral two-by-two surgery matrix has cokernel $\mathbb{Z}^2$, hence both coefficients and the linking number vanish. The two endpoint levels suffice. [Milnor, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800) · [Meier–Zupan, p. 70](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10464744#page=2)

Let $V_L$ be the local filling, $B\times[0,1]$ with the two handles attached on top and corners rounded. Its full boundary is $S^3_L$. Glue $Q_2$ along any orientation-reversing boundary identification. The closure has a single minimum, no 1-handles, two 2-handles, two 3-handles and a single maximum. It is simply connected since $V_L$ is simply connected and $\partial Q_2\hookrightarrow Q_2$ induces an isomorphism on $\pi_1$. It has Euler characteristic two and is a homotopy four-sphere by orientability, duality and the simply connected homology criterion. The *published* Meier–Zupan Theorem 1.1 applies to this auxiliary closure. Its conclusion is standard $S^4$, for which Laudenbach–Poénaru's Lemma 1 gives a diffeomorphism of pairs with the standard $S^4\supset Q_2$. Restriction to the complement gives $f_0:V_L\to V_U\cong\natural_2(S^2\times D^2)$, where $U$ is the split zero-framed unlink in $B$. Choose $f_0$ orientation preserving, using a reflection of the standard model if necessary. The proof invokes Theorem 1.1 on the closed cap and the embedded-handlebody assertion on the actual image of the cap. [Meier–Zupan, Theorem 1.1, p. 70, and §7](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1668186788) · [Laudenbach–Poénaru, §2, opening model and Lemma 1, p. 338](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=3)

The codimension-three argument underlying the embedded-handlebody assertion can be checked using the 0-handle ball and the 1-handle cores. Match the parametrized 0-handle balls by the disk theorem and choose the core germs in common endpoint collars about the matched feet. The complement of the common 0-handle ball in standard $S^4$ is a standard $D^4$, by the same disk theorem applied to the embedded 0-handle ball. The resulting finite collections of neat arcs are homotopic relative to the common endpoint germs in this $D^4$. For a generic homotopy with fixed germs, equal-time coincidences of two parameters have a domain of dimension $1+1+1=3$ against diagonal codimension four, and failure of immersion has a domain of dimension $1+1=2$ against codimension four. This gives an isotopy of the arcs, extended ambiently relative to the 0-handle ball. Shrinking the handles to thin tubular neighborhoods and using collar/tubular-neighborhood uniqueness identifies the thickened *images*. Twists in parametrized normal frames do not obstruct this step because orthogonal normal changes preserve the normal disk fibers as sets. The proof uses the parametrized 0-handle ball and needs no input on an arbitrary embedded $S^3\subset S^4$. [Palais, Theorem B and Corollary 1, pp. 275–276](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1960-0117741-0) · [Laudenbach–Poénaru, Lemma 1](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=3)

## Parametrized interface and inverse

The interface $P$ is the single three-disk obtained by rounding $B\times\{0\}\cup\partial B\times[0,1]$. Use the product coordinates to parametrize it on both sides, denoted $p_L:D^3\to\partial V_L$ and $p_U:D^3\to\partial V_U$. The two parametrizations $\partial f_0\circ p_L$ and $p_U$ have the same orientation. Apply Palais's Theorem B and Corollary 1 on the connected three-manifold $\partial V_U$, using a slightly enlarged disk in an attachment-free collar of $\partial P$. The boundary isotopy $a_t$ starts at identity and satisfies $a_1\circ\partial f_0\circ p_L=p_U$ on the original parametrized disk and its edge germ. Extend $a_t$ into the collar by $(x,s)\mapsto(a_{t\rho(s)}(x),s)$, with $\rho=1$ near $s=0$ and zero towards the interior. Straighten the chosen collars so $f:V_L\to V_U$ agrees with the product coordinates along the full interface. This allows replacement in the original corners. The boundary isotopy gives the required extension directly. [Palais, Theorem B and Corollary 1](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1960-0117741-0)

Let $W_L,W_U$ be the full cobordisms over $Y_r$ containing the local blocks. Use $f$ on the local block and identity on $(Y_r\setminus\operatorname{int}B)\times[0,1]$ to obtain $F:W_L\to W_U$. Its inverse uses $f^{-1}$ and the same identity. The incoming map is the identity and the outgoing map is $\delta=\partial_+F:Y_{\mathrm{out}}^L\to Y_{\mathrm{out}}^U$. For each subsequent parametrized framed attachment $e$ at that level use $\delta\circ e$ on the entire tube, with inverse on its image $e^{-1}\circ\delta^{-1}$. The extension is identity in the new handle coordinates and induces the next boundary map, so this applies inductively to every later attachment. A closing map $\gamma:Y_{\mathrm{out}}^L\to\partial Q$ to an already assembled upper piece at this level becomes $\gamma_U=\gamma\circ\delta^{-1}$, with inverse $\delta\circ\gamma^{-1}$. The diffeomorphism on the assembled pieces is $F$ and identity on $Q$, with inverse $F^{-1}$ and identity on $Q$. A later tube that crosses the local seam is transported by this same formula. A constraint prescribing that tube's location on the changed face would require a further relative theorem. ## Terminal block and handle count

Suppose the pair is the final two index-two handles and the upper assembly consists of the 3-handles and one 4-handle. In reverse this upper body $Q$ has rank $r+2$, since its boundary is $Y_{r+2}$. After replacement, the two outgoing dual cores of $U$ are the product circles in the two new $S^1\times S^2$ summands. For each, choose the product sphere meeting that core once transversely and the other core nowhere. Choose a boundary identification $\lambda:Y_{\mathrm{out}}^U\to\partial Q_{r+2}$ carrying these spheres to the last two standard 1-handle belt spheres and the two cores to their product circles. Here $\lambda$ is oriented as a closing map. Laudenbach–Poénaru extends $\lambda\circ\gamma_U^{-1}$ to a diffeomorphism $H:Q\to Q_{r+2}$. With $o_i:D^2\times S^1\to Y_{\mathrm{out}}^U$ the actual outgoing solid torus in the split model and $\tau:S^1\times D^2\to D^2\times S^1$ the factor switch, the reverse attaching tubes become $\lambda\circ o_i\circ\tau$. In the original upper-body coordinates for this replacement they were $\gamma_U\circ o_i\circ\tau$. The comparison uses $H$ and identity in the dual handles, and its inverse uses $H^{-1}$ and the same identities. Each transverse intersection is the geometric hypothesis of adjacent-index cancellation. The pairs are supported in the two split summands, so canceling the first leaves the second transverse intersection intact. In the original direction the count changes by $(\Delta n_2,\Delta n_3)=(-2,-2)$, with all other indices unchanged. For $r=0$ the remaining upper body is a ball. [Laudenbach–Poénaru, proof of Theorem A, pp. 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=6) · [Milnor, Lectures on the h-Cobordism Theorem, Theorem 5.4](https://www.jstor.org/stable/jj.28248897)

For the closing map all the way back to the incoming cut, write $A_L=W_L\cup_\gamma Q$, $A_U=W_U\cup_{\gamma_U}Q$, and $\widehat F:A_L\to A_U$ for the first assembled comparison. Let $J$ compare $A_U$ with the remapped reverse handlebody using $H$ as above, and $K$ be the cancellation diffeomorphism from that model to $Q_r$ including its remaining boundary collar. If $\kappa:Y_r\to\partial A_L$ is the original gluing to the lower source, the reduced upper gluing is $\partial(K\circ J\circ\widehat F)\circ\kappa$. Its inverse is $\kappa^{-1}\circ\partial(\widehat F^{-1}\circ J^{-1}\circ K^{-1})$. This uses the original upper assembly and leaves the lower source in the same coordinates.

The conclusion for a nonterminal block is a same-type smooth replacement, with two index-two handles and all later parametrized tubes carried to the new face. To obtain strict descent there one must additionally locate actual compatible upper cancellations or prove a marked reordering that puts the block at the end. The outgoing map is allowed to change inside the local surgery face. Prescribing an isotopy of the original attaching link or classifying $2R$-links by slides would be a separate problem. The ball condition is essential to this inference because null-homotopy of an attaching word in $Y_r$ alone does not put a two-link in a common ball. The generalized-square-knot hypothesis, or another one-sided theorem recognizing the particular local closure, is needed at the cap step.
