## Cyclic-cover calculation

Orient \(K\) along \(3_-\to1_+\), \(1_-\to2_-\), \(2_+\to4_-\), and \(4_+\to3_+\). The signed seam crossings \(+,+,-,-\) give winding zero, as does the stationary placement of \(Q\). The inlet character is trivial on both boundary tori of the joint exterior and surjective on that exterior. It therefore extends surjectively across the prescribed two fillings. The lifted inlet is \(S^2\times\mathbb R\). In its pair sequence with the lifted exterior, the relative \(H_2\) consists of one meridian disk per lifted tube, with zero incoming intersection map because all lifted cores are closed and null-homologous. Together with \(H_1(S^2\times\mathbb R)=0\) this gives \(\Lambda^2\) on the two lifted meridians for \(\Lambda=\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]\).

A compact bounding two-chain for a lifted framed push-off \(L_j^f\) gives the relation with \(\mu_i\)-coefficient \(\sum_n\operatorname{lk}(L_j^f,t^nL_i)t^n\). The same intersection calculation makes linking independent of the bounding chain. The sum is finite and equals the corresponding linking calculation after compactifying the two ends. The original meridian maps to the filling core up to sign for an integral slope, so Mayer–Vietoris eliminates the added core generators and leaves precisely the slope relations. A free basis of the outlet group can be chosen with character values \((1,0,0)\) by Nielsen operations. Its infinite cyclic cover has first homology \(\Lambda^2\), as seen from the boundary row \((t-1,0,0)\) for the cover of the rose. This calculates the abelianization of the same kernel with the same deck action. The relation matrix thus has rank zero over \(\mathbb Q(t)\) and is zero integrally.

Choose \(Q_0\) in \(W_0\) and \(K_0\) with its through arcs in \(W_0\). In the charted completions write \(J_-,J_+\) for the capped returns and \(C\) for the capped through cycle. The seam chord occurrences cancel with opposite orientations in
\[
 K_0=(J_-)_{1}+C_0+(J_+)_{-1}.
\]
For example, the upper \(12\) chord of \(C_0\) points \(1\to2\) and the lower \(12\) chord of \((J_-)_{1}\) points \(2\to1\). The lower \(34\) chord of \(C_0\) points \(4\to3\) and the upper \(34\) chord of \((J_+)_{-1}\) points \(3\to4\). For \(K_0,K_1\) their only shared decomposition seam has separate \(12\) and \(34\) chords. The common slabs \(W_0,W_1\) give the pairings \((C,J_+)\) and \((J_-,C)\). For \(K_0,K_2\) only \(W_1\) is common and gives \((J_-,J_+)\). All larger positive shifts give zero, and the negative shifts follow by symmetry. These arguments apply to arbitrary common disjoint chords because the decompositions use their collars and can be pushed off the faces with the cross-set curves disjoint.

With rows indexed by meridians and columns by push-offs, the result is
\[
 M(t)=
 \begin{pmatrix}
 f_Q & a_+t^{-1}+a_0+a_-t\\
 a_-t^{-1}+a_0+a_+t &
 F_K+(b_-+b_+)(t+t^{-1})+c(t^2+t^{-2})
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Here \(a_\pm=\operatorname{lk}(J_\pm,Q)\), \(a_0=\operatorname{lk}(C,Q)\), \(b_\pm=\operatorname{lk}(J_\pm,C)\), \(c=\operatorname{lk}(J_-,J_+)\), and the two constants are the chosen-lift self-framings. A push-off can be replaced by its core in all linking terms except the unshifted self-pairing. Pushing the bounding chain down gives the inlet framing \(f_K=F_K+2(b_-+b_+)+2c\). Replacing lifts by \(t^{u_i}L_i\) multiplies entry \(ij\) by \(t^{u_j-u_i}\), and exchanging row and column inverts \(t\). Consequently
\[
 a_-=a_0=a_+=c=f_Q=F_K=f_K=0,\qquad b_-+b_+=0.
\]
The degree-three cover independently gives the three \(a\)'s. Its six null-homologous core lifts give exterior rank seven. The index-three subgroup of \(F_3\) also has rank seven, so every lifted slope relation vanishes. A lift of a stationary Seifert surface in one chamber sees the two returns and the through cycle on three distinct moving lifts, hence reads the three coefficients separately.

## The complete abelian test and common chord normalization

Let \(E\) be the drilled slab and \(X\) its stationary filling. The pair sequence for \(E\subset W\) has five relative meridian disk generators. The product sphere supplies the relation equating the two oriented through meridians because the through arcs run with opposite height directions. The stationary and both returning arcs have zero signed intersections. Thus \(H_1(E;\mathbb Z)\) has basis \((\mu_Q,\mu_-,\mu_+,\mu_t)\). Completing the arcs in two cap balls sends this basis to the four corresponding link meridians, giving an integral isomorphism on \(H_1\). The stationary filling imposes the row with entries \(f_Q,a_-,a_+,a_0\), all zero. In particular, the cohomology class \(h\) obtained by linking with the capped \(C\) is the \(\mu_t\)-coordinate on \(H_1(X)\), independent of the choice of common chords used to complete \(C\).

Join each return chord to a wall section, orient the loop along its return, and count section winding positively by adding the corresponding meridian. Measured from the section giving that completion's capped preferred longitude, the loop classes are
\[
 [\gamma_{-,m}]=m\mu_-+b_-\mu_t,\qquad
 [\gamma_{+,m}]=m\mu_++b_+\mu_t.
\]
The absent coordinates vanish by the matrix test. An arbitrary wall section differs from the zero section by an integral own-meridian term. Thus at fixed marks there is a candidate on a side exactly when its \(b\)-coefficient is zero, and the candidate winding is unique. Applied with the original \(C\) on the bridged specimen, the fixed-chord \(13\) twist moves each \(h\)-value off zero by absolute value \(|k|\) and changing a section cannot restore it. The marked bridge therefore fails even in homology at any nonzero twist of that specimen.

For freely chosen common chords, start from any disjoint pair with the prescribed endpoints and germs. Choose a curve \(d\) cutting off holes \(1,3\) and crossing each chord once. This can be drawn as the boundary of a small neighborhood of these two holes joined by an arc avoiding the two chord interiors. Cutting \(F\) along the two chords leaves a connected annulus in which to draw the joining arc, and the neighborhood takes a short initial segment of each chord. Oriented using the common seam chart with positive normal in the increasing height direction, \(d\) represents \(\mu_-+\mu_t\) on the lower face and \(\mu_++\mu_t\) on the upper face. The upward-oriented through strand has end \(3\) below and end \(1\) above. The other enclosed end is \(1\) below for the lower return and \(3\) above for the upper return, each also with upward tangent. These signs use the common chart orientation on both copies of the face.

The lower return chord is oriented \(2\to1\), entering the \(13\) side, and the upper one \(3\to4\), leaving that side. Hence the same chart twist \(T_d^k\), extended by the identity over the walls, adds \(\epsilon k(\mu_-+\mu_t)\) and \(-\epsilon k(\mu_++\mu_t)\), respectively, to the loops with their old sections. Here \(\epsilon\in\{1,-1\}\) encodes the convention for a positive twist. The intrinsic \(h\)-coordinate gives \(b_-(k)=b_-+\epsilon k\), \(b_+(k)=b_+-\epsilon k\), even though the capped through cycle used for linking also changes. Taking \(k=-\epsilon b_-\) kills both through coordinates together. With the old zero sections, the winding adjustments are \(-\epsilon k\) below and \(+\epsilon k\) above. Equivalently, use the new capped zero section on each side. This kills both complete classes, including the own-meridian coordinates, and uniqueness follows from meridian independence. The twist fixes the end germs and uses a single seam map for the two copies.

## Surface and disk tests

On either twice-punctured torus \(U\), write the class of a curve as \(p[\mu]+q[\lambda]+u\delta\), with \(\mu\) the return-wall meridian, \(\lambda\) the fixed zero-section longitude, and \(\delta\) a through boundary class oriented to map to \(\mu_t\). A nonseparating curve stays nonseparating upon disk capping the two holes, so \((p,q)\) is primitive in the capped torus. The inclusion into \(H_1(X)\) then forces \(p=0\), \(q=\pm1\) and \(u=-qb_\pm\) for a compression boundary. An essential separating curve with one hole on its planar side has nonzero meridian image. With both holes on that side the boundary class vanishes. Also, disjoint nonisotopic nonseparating curves on one \(U\) would separate its cut surface into two regions each containing one through hole. Their coherently oriented classes differ by \(\pm\delta\), excluding two such compression boundaries.

The three-crossing construction produces a single curve after gluing the two cut meridian circles. Its two same-side arcs each cut off one through hole from the opposite meridian copy. Neither can form a bigon, and the remaining arc spans the copies. The algebraic intersection has absolute value one. A twist along an \(F\)-curve enclosing one return and one through hole changes the \(\delta\) coefficient by a unit, and a twist along \(\mu\) can then set the \(\mu\) coefficient. Both fixes preserve geometric intersection three. The wrong-chord construction uses the separator \(s\) about the through holes whose joining arc crosses the fixed chord once. The two intersections of \(s\) with the fixed longitude are minimal. One complementary segment separates the two holes on the pants side, and the other crosses \(\mu\) once on the torus side. The twist about \(s\) preserves the entire longitude class since \(s\) separates \(U\), preserves intersection one with \(\mu\), and has geometric intersection four with the original longitude. Other fixed-chord windings have different classes. The twist can be viewed as pushing one through mark around the other after replacing the holes by marks. These are surface constructions allowing arbitrary interiors of the slab. [Farb–Margalit, Propositions 1.7 and 3.2 and §4.2](https://euclid.nmu.edu/~joshthom/teaching/ma589/farbmarg.pdf)

The sufficient criterion uses the specific separator \(s_0\subset F\) enclosing the through holes and missing the marked chord. A nonseparating compression boundary disjoint from \(s_0\) lies in its once-holed torus side. There it realizes the absolute algebraic intersection one with \(\mu\) geometrically. In the pair of pants cut along \(\mu\), opposite crossing signs would give essential returns on both meridian copies. The first such return separates the other copy from the remaining boundary, leaving that other return in an annulus, where it is boundary-parallel. This contradicts minimal position. The single crossing now gives a chord joining the two return boundaries in the face pair of pants. That chord is unique up to endpoint motion on its two circles. The angular turns are absorbed into the wall section when the germs are restored. The boundary isotopy extends in a collar of the disk and gives the marked bridge at its unique possible winding. An arbitrary separator from a compression argument need not have this marked position. Filling the through tubes kills the meridians in the group and allows disks meeting their cores, so capping alone does not yield the criterion.

For fixed chords the surviving test is whether each simple closed chord-and-section curve is trivial in \(\pi_1(X)\). A boundary collar and Dehn's lemma give a proper disk for each null word, and any two such disks on opposite faces can be made disjoint by innermost-circle replacements. Restoring the radial strips gives the bridge disks. [Papakyriakopoulos, Dehn's lemma, p. 169](https://www.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf#page=2)

## Relation to the fixed-chord obstruction in the fundamental group

The later geometric question in the issue concerns the specimen known to admit the [original two bridges](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/FOUR_MARKED_BRIDGE_DISKS.md). A neighborhood of either original bridge cuts off a drilled ball, a solid torus, along a disk with boundary the separator \(s\) of the chord pair on its face. The disk gives a free product of the return meridian group and the complementary group. A different essential face separator intersecting \(s\) minimally gives alternating arcs in the two face pairs of pants. Each such arc, based along \(s\) and closed using its trivial boundary word, encloses one hole. The arcs on the solid torus side give a generator or its inverse, and those on the other side give through meridians with nonzero \(\mu_t\)-coordinate. The resulting alternating word is reduced and nontrivial. Any bridge for the altered chord would instead supply a disk on its own separator.

In particular, if \(s_1=T_d(s)\), it still separates the same labeled pairs and has zero class on both twice-punctured tori. Twisting both original chords about \(s_1\) fixes their complete homology classes with their old sections. But \(T_{s_1}^k(s)\) intersects \(s\) essentially for \(k\ne0\), since \(i(s_1,s)>0\) by the twist intersection formula and the distinct \(13\) and \(12\) partitions. The reduced-word test then excludes both altered prescribed bridges. This gives the additional geometric obstruction on that fixture despite the full abelian vanishing. The original freely selectable chords still give the original disks. [Farb–Margalit, Proposition 3.2](https://euclid.nmu.edu/~joshthom/teaching/ma589/farbmarg.pdf)
