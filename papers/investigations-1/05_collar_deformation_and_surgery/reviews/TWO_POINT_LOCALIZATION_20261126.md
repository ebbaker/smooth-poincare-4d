## Which side supplies a disk?

Call the stationary knot \(Q\) and the moving one \(K\). The two intersections have opposite signs. Cut at the supplied sphere, drill \(Q\) and the two returns, then fill \(Q\) along its prescribed zero longitude. The resulting connected manifold \(X\) has two boundary tori. Each consists of the twice-punctured sphere on that face and the lateral wall of the corresponding return tube. Capping the faces with trivial arcs merely extends their collars, so the preferred slopes can be read on the complete link \(Q,J,V\) in \(S^3\). A bridge disk takes one return to its matched face chord and misses the other return.

In the actual \(K\)-filling the two meridians are longitudinal circles because the slope is integral. An annulus between them completes the punctured sphere to a nonseparating torus. Cutting the outlet along this torus recovers \(X\) with its actual collars. If both port groups injected into \(\pi_1 X\), the HNN extension identifying them would embed \(\mathbb Z^2\) in the outlet group \(F_3\). The Loop Theorem therefore gives a compressing disk in \(X\). The rank jump supplies more information than freeness alone. Write \(a=\operatorname{lk}(Q,J)\), \(b=\operatorname{lk}(Q,V)\), \(c=\operatorname{lk}(J,V)\). Oriented chord completion gives \(a+b=0\), using the zero total linking forced by the rank jump. For a compressing slope \(p\mu_J+q\lambda_J\), its vanishing in integral homology gives \((qa,p,qc)=k(0,a,b)\). A meridian compression would give \(ka=\pm1\) and \(kb=0\), impossible. Thus \(a=b=c=p=0\) and \(q=\pm1\). The other port has the interchanged calculation. One may hold the face chord fixed and choose the winding of the incident strip to realize the longitude. Restoring the strip produces an embedded bridge disk in the stationary-filled slab. Puncturing this disk at the filling core gives the framed slides in the original exterior. After the disk push the pair misses the sphere and fits inside a slab ball. The local pair has joint surgery \(Y_2\). ## What remains for smooth Poincaré

In this terminal class, a sphere missing both components puts them directly in a ball. A sphere missing one and meeting the other twice gives the strict reduction above. All intersection counts are even. Thus **a least-three presentation of the specified form requires at least four intersections with the other core for every transverse nonseparating sphere missing one tube**. A selection lemma for arbitrary least-count presentations still needs an actual cut or a different relative move.
