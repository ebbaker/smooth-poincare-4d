## Input and conclusion

Let $a,b,c$ be disjoint smoothly and properly embedded arcs in the marked ball $B$, with $a$ boundary-parallel as an arc in $B$. A disk witnessing this need only be disjoint from $a$ in its interior. Suppose the full exterior $H=E_{abc}$ is a genus-three handlebody. The balls and arcs, strand neighborhoods and product coordinates are fixed. In $S^3=\partial(B\times I_t)$ take the doubled strands with the framings obtained from their product disks. The upper marking identifies the simultaneous outlet with $\partial(H\times I_t)$ by the supplied doubling map. All statements below concern that product-framed trace with the original caps.

There is an unstabilized sequence that slides the two reverse attachments for $b,c$, changes to a complete upper belt system, cancels the two pairs, then cancels the surviving $a$ pair. The counts in the forward direction are $(n_1,n_2,n_3)=(0,3,3)\to(0,2,2)\to(0,1,1)\to(0,0,0)$. The two native partial surgeries are allowed to be irreducible. Their irreducibility is irrelevant to this existence argument. ## The two three-dimensional inputs

Put $V=E_a$, a solid torus by boundary-parallelism. Round the product corners and take a slightly shrunken copy $H_0$ of $H$ in the interior of $V$. The closure $C$ of $V\setminus H_0$ is the compression body with positive boundary $\Sigma=\partial H_0$ and negative boundary $\partial V$. In fact it is the positive collar with two three-dimensional $2$-handles restoring the $b,c$ tubes. The original meridian disks of those tubes, extended across the positive collar, are a complete disk pair in $C$. Cutting along both gives $\partial V\times I$. Thus $V=H_0\cup_\Sigma C$ is the Heegaard splitting of the actual $V$, with the full torus assigned to the negative boundary of $C$. Shrinking $H$ serves only to place its boundary in the interior. The given collar identifies its positive attaching annuli with those in the product trace.

Waldhausen proves that an unstabilized Heegaard splitting of a handlebody is parallel to its boundary. His §1 defines *minimal* as admitting no destabilization, and §4(2)–(3) works with the prescribed partition of the boundary and this same stabilization by the genus-one splitting of $S^3$. The genus-three splitting of $V$ is therefore obtained from the genus-one boundary-parallel splitting by two stabilizations. In the standard model choose the stabilizations in disjoint balls. Their meridian disks on each side meet geometrically once in matching pairs and miss the other pair. The two disks on the $C$ side cut it to $\partial V\times I$. On the handlebody side, the two disks together with the remaining solid-torus meridian cut the handlebody to a ball. Transport these finitely many disks back into the given $C$ and $H_0$ using the equivalence of splittings. Since Waldhausen works piecewise linearly, round the pulled-back disks and their boundary curves in the smooth halves, with the boundary crossings first made transverse in small boundary charts. Local rounding preserves the disjointness and the two single crossings. No boundary reparametrization of the actual torus is used in subsequent moves. [Waldhausen, *Heegaard-Zerlegungen der 3-Sphäre*, Topology 7 (1968), §1, p. 195 and §4(2)–(3), p. 202](https://pub.uni-bielefeld.de/download/1782178/2313707/Waldhausen_020.pdf) · [Schleimer, *Waldhausen’s Theorem*, Theorem 6.3, p. 314](https://sschleimer.warwick.ac.uk/Maths/2007waldhausen.pdf)

Bonahon's Proposition B.1 relates any two elements of his disk-system class by finitely many slides and isotopies. The class consists of systems with boundaries in the exterior (positive) boundary such that cutting yields boundary-irreducible pieces, with the system minimal under deleting components. Both $C$ systems qualify. The full cuts give a torus product, and omitting either nonseparating disk leaves a positive boundary compressed by the remaining disk of the complete cut. The proof specifies slides supported at the positive boundary using a disk and a band. Their isotopies can be taken away from the negative boundary, since the disks are compact and meet only the positive boundary. The same proposition applies to complete disk systems of $H_0$ that cut it to a ball. Thus if a particular product belt system is part of the intake it can be changed to the geometric dual one by disk slides and isotopies on the handlebody side. The two sides have distinct roles. The change in $C$ is a slide of reverse $2$-handles. The change on the $H_0$ side changes the $0/1$-handle decomposition of the same upper body, with the attaching tubes carried in its fixed product boundary. [Bonahon, *Cobordism of automorphisms of surfaces*, Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. 16 (1983), Appendix B, Definition of $\mathcal D$, Figure 3 and Proposition B.1, pp. 267–269](https://www.numdam.org/article/ASENS_1983_4_16_2_237_0.pdf#page=31)

## Doubling, grading and the original glues

A disk of the complete pair on the $C$ side has three-dimensional thickening $D^2\times I_s$, attached along a surface-framed annulus $\partial D^2\times I_s$. Taking its product with $I_t$ supplies the actual reverse $2$-handle and its marked attaching tube $\partial D^2\times I_s\times I_t$. The boundary arc for a disk slide gives the usual framed ribbon at the central level of $\Sigma\times I_t$. The framed parallel is displaced in its attaching annulus, and the boundary of the boundary-connected sum of the disks carries the surface product framing. Thus a disk slide changes exactly the reverse handle decomposition in the way used by a framed $2$-handle slide. In the forward direction the roles of the two surgery-dual components are exchanged. [GST, *Fibered knots and potential counterexamples to the Property 2R and Slice-Ribbon conjectures*, §2, Lemma 2.1 and Figure 3, pp. 2308–09](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=4)

For each complete pair, its two disk neighborhoods and the cut-open torus product give a relative decomposition of the fixed $C$. Identify their thickenings with their images in the actual $C\times I_t$ using these product collars. Composing one identification with the inverse of the preceding identification gives a comparison of successive descriptions. Its restriction to the positive boundary of $C$ uses the same $\Sigma$ coordinates used to glue to $H_0$, and at the negative boundary uses the original $\partial V$ coordinates. After rounding and restoring the collars, these are descriptions of the same $V\times I_t$ relative to the product boundary receiving the last reverse attachment. In particular the original $a$ tube on $\partial V\times I_t$ is untouched in these coordinates. The upper cap and its identification with the doubled $H$ are also retained. If a comparison is written in separate outlet charts by a boundary map $P$, use the transported tube $P t_a$ with inverse $t_a^{-1}P^{-1}$ on its image, where $t_a$ is the original attaching map. The unchanged remaining handle coordinates extend this comparison to the next outlet. For any old cap map $j$, use $P_{\rm out}j$ and inverse $j^{-1}P_{\rm out}^{-1}$ there. These maps are the original gluings expressed in successor charts.

If a different rank-three $4$-dimensional $1$-handlebody $Q$ is specified with its own closing $\gamma$ from the outlet, let $\lambda$ denote the product closing. The orientation-preserving boundary comparison $\lambda\gamma^{-1}:\partial Q\to\partial(H\times I_t)$ extends to a diffeomorphism $Q\to H\times I_t$. This is the extension proved in the proof of Laudenbach–Poénaru's Theorem A. Pulling back the product handles and cancellation data by this diffeomorphism realizes them in the supplied $Q$ with its original gluing. The inverse comparison on the boundary is $\gamma\lambda^{-1}$. For an input in the natural product coordinates this extra re-marking is unnecessary. [Laudenbach–Poénaru, *A note on 4-dimensional handlebodies*, Bull. Soc. Math. France 100 (1972), proof of Theorem A, pp. 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=6)

For each matching pair, the disk on the $H_0$ side doubles to an actual belt sphere of the product $1$-handle decomposition, since a 3-dimensional disk co-core becomes the product 4-dimensional co-core after multiplying by $I_t$. The matching reverse circle sits on the same middle seam and meets that sphere transversely once. It misses both other belt spheres. The two geometric pair cancellations can be made in the two stabilization neighborhoods, whose disjointness keeps the second cancellation marked after the first. The remaining reverse body before attachment of $a$ is the original solid-torus product $V\times I_t$ with its boundary marking carried through the collar identifications.

To see the last cancellation on the original $a$ tube, trim a boundary-parallelism disk for $a$ at $\nu a$. In $V$ this gives a properly embedded meridian disk with boundary crossing the strand meridian $m_a$ once in the lateral annulus. Its double is a belt sphere of $V\times I_t$, and the actual remaining attaching circle is $m_a$ at the middle of that same product annulus. Thus the original tube crosses that belt once. This disk is used after the other tubes have been restored, so it imposes no disjointness restriction from $b$ or $c$ in the source ball. The geometric adjacent-index theorem applies at all three stages. It replaces each pair on the same relative smooth trace and extends with the given boundary identifications over the unchanged caps. [Milnor, *Lectures on the h-Cobordism Theorem*, Theorem 5.4, pp. 48–49](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/hcobord.pdf#page=53)

## Scope of the designated lower zero

The first forward attachment alone is a zero-framed unknot, and its outgoing level is $Y_1$. This describes the isolated first stage. If the same unknot were split from one of $B,C$ in the original diagram, the corresponding two-component surgery would split as $Y_1$ connected-summed with the surgery on that other component. It would contain a nonseparating sphere. Global splitness of $A$ from $B\cup C$ therefore forces both native cuts reducible. The two-irreducible-cut target can use a designated lower zero only in the stage-specific sense. The theorem gives no cancellation sequence for an untreated diagram without the doubled-free-tangle presentation and its product framings. Even for an input in this class, the theorem produces no prescribed short sequence, first band, first target belt, or diagram-level certificate until the disks are actually located.
