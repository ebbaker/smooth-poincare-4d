**For an integral pair from \(Y_1=S^1\times S^2\) to the immediate \(Y_3=\#_3(S^1\times S^2)\), a reducible cut after either single attachment forces joint localization by framed slides.** With one split lower zero and an immediate rank-three upper body, this removes a middle handle on the same smooth type. Consequently a least-three presentation of this shape must have two irreducible native second cuts, both of first Betti number two. In particular neither of those two residual cuts admits a nonzero-degree map from a closed oriented three-manifold of positive scalar curvature. The three-manifold prime classification giving this last exclusion is recorded in the [surgery manuscript, Recognition at intermediate levels](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/manuscript/recognition_from_marked_handle_cuts.tex).

## The separate reducing system

On the chosen stationary-first order \(Q,K\) each surgery raises \(b_1\) by one. The true native single cut \(Z\) has torsion-free first homology of rank two. A nonseparating sphere in \(Y_1\) misses \(Q\) precisely when \(Q\) lies in a ball. In that case \(Z=Y_1\# M\), \(M=S^3_0(Q)\).

For the converse suppose that \(Z\) is reducible. Apply Scharlemann's Theorem 6.1 to the two fillings at \(Q\). The inlet contains a nonseparating sphere. A compressible boundary of an irreducible exterior with just one torus gives a solid torus, impossible for the rank-two filling. The closed manifolds exclude both solid-torus alternatives. A genuine cable at its cabling slope contributes a lens summand with first homology of order at least two. The correction added in proof requires a rational-homology-sphere outlet. Thus the knot exterior itself must be reducible. An essential nonseparating sphere there misses \(Q\). An essential separating sphere splits off a nontrivial capped side away from the tube, necessarily \(Y_1\) by prime decomposition in the inlet. Its punctured copy supplies the desired sphere. This proves the native-cut criterion in both directions. [Independent proof from the marked trace](FILLED_SPHERE_LOCALIZATION_READER.md) · [Published theorem and correction](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~mgscharl/papers/reducible.pdf)

For nontrivial local \(Q\), Gabai gives irreducibility of \(M\). Apply the same reducible-surgery theorem to \(Z\to Y_3\) at \(K\). The capped side of an essential separating exterior sphere would be a common nontrivial summand of \(Y_1\#M\) and \(Y_3\), necessarily \(Y_1\). In all cases there is a nonseparating sphere in \(Z\) missing the moving tube. It is isotopic in \(Z\) to the **original marked seam**. Indeed, any two disjoint nonseparating spheres cobound a product since the capped cut is irreducible; innermost-disk surgery connects any two by disjoint pairs. Carry the framed tube by this isotopy and make its core track transverse to the fixed stationary filling core. The finite transverse events are signed \(K\)-over-\(Q\) slides in the actual integral chart. After expanding the outside torus collar, the resulting unfilled pair misses the original seam. The cap-joining arc in the cut slab puts both tubes in a ball. On any originally nonlocal pair in this class every such common nonseparating sphere meets the stationary core. Otherwise the untouched sphere survives the meridional refill and localizes the original pair in a slab ball. Its algebraic intersection with the core is zero because it is isotopic in the filled level to the original seam. Thus a transverse common sphere must have at least two geometric core intersections there. For a local unknot \(Q\), the \(Y_2\to Y_3\) knot-surgery theorem already gives a disk-framed unknot in the filled inlet. Its ball can be moved to a small ball in the original exterior disjoint from \(Q\). [Filled sphere and transport](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/REDUCIBLE_FILLINGS_SPHERE.md) · [Independent group and geometric reading](TRACE_GROUP_CHAMBER_READER.md)

The native local surgery is \(Y_2\) by prime uniqueness. With the terminal split lower handle \(A\), move the local ball into a chart of the original \(S^3\) off \(A\). Put the local pair first, \(A\) last. Its split dual meets its re-marked upper belt once, and the change of boundary marking extends over the rank-three upper body. The actual two-component trace now has a rank-two closing, with induced map \((\partial C)b\) and inverse \(b^{-1}\partial(C^{-1})\) for the local outlet attachment \(b\) and upper comparison \(C\). This gives \((0,3,3)\to(0,2,2)\) without births even when this local closure is unrecognized. For general later attachments there is a relative replacement. At each successive cut carry \(t_j\) by \(P_jt_j\), inverse \(t_j^{-1}P_j^{-1}\) on its image, and extend in the corresponding handle coordinates. The full closing and its inverse become \(\gamma P^{-1}\) and \(P\gamma^{-1}\). With the outgoing tube \(o\) at the appropriate level, the transported old reverse face is \((\gamma P^{-1})(Po)\tau=\gamma o\tau\). Without an identified cancellation after later attachments the relative net count is zero. [Closing and inverse](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/ALTERNATING_RIBBON_TRACE.md)

## The capped alternatives on four ports

There is an independent algebraic check. In the last single-handle trace the group has presentations
\[
 (G*\langle t\rangle)/\langle\!\langle K\rangle\!\rangle
   =F_3/\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle,\qquad G=\pi_1M ,
\]
where \(d\) is the actual reversing core. Rank forces \([d]\) primitive in abelianization, hence the dual presentation gives an aspherical two-complex. For nontrivial stationary \(Q\), \(G\) is a closed aspherical three-manifold group, locally indicable and of cohomological dimension three. A cyclically reduced relator using both free factors would embed \(G\) by the Freiheitssatz. The same dimension obstruction rules out \(K=1\). Thus \(K\) is conjugate to a nonidentity element of \([G,G]\); for the unknot \(K=1\). In the height cover, exactly one stationary chamber retraction survives for a nontrivial \(Q\). The alternating pairing is realized with its native seams and integral outlet. The two lower returns are opposite sides of an opened ribbon on the Figure 5 unknot \(U\) jointly placed with the stationary square knot \(Q\). Upper \(41\) is a third parallel of \(U\). Their classes survive even after the moving walls are restored. For upper \(23\), join \(n\ge1\) coherent zero parallels of \(Q\) by a tree of fusion bands. In the unfilled knot group this arm completes to \(\lambda_Q^n\); in the stationary filling the \(n\) meridian disks and the same bands form one embedded bridge. The access strip and all other arms miss its interior. Exactly \(n\) signed-positive core intersections occur after choosing the boundary orientation. Any competing bridge, after closing its chord and absorbing its wall section, differs by a sphere map into the aspherical square-knot zero surgery. Thus \(n\) is the **minimum over all four returns, all chords, windings and competing transverse bridge disks**. It is not a minimum over general slide sequences. ## Scope at the next cut

The unresolved terminal rank-one inputs are now the two **irreducible** native second cuts, or lack of recognition of a localized rank-two successor. For nontrivial fibered \(Q\) one may apply the surface-bundle surgery theorem on the capped local \(S^3_0(Q)\) with outlet \(Y_2\) to place the partner on the closed fiber with its fiber slope. The proof of GST Theorem 3.3 goes further. Compress the fiber on the partner, keep the component meeting the stationary dual once, and slide the other reverse dual off it. In the forward diagram this exposes a component of strictly smaller genus. These slides preserve the middle count. Iteration requires information about the new component, in particular whether it is fibered or already recognizable. The generalized-square-knot closure has the existing Meier–Zupan recognizing theorem, and the actual Figure 5 example here has a specific slide to the unlink. General one-sided two-component traces and arbitrary handle selection on a smooth homotopy four-sphere remain open.
