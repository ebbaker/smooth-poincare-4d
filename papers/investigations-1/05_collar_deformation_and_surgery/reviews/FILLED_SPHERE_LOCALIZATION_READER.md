## The surgery obstruction in both directions

Here is the closed-manifold consequence of Scharlemann's Theorem 6.1 used twice. Let $P$ contain a nonseparating sphere and suppose a nonmeridional filling of a knot exterior $X$ in $P$ yields a reducible closed $P'$ with torsion-free integral first homology and positive $b_1$. If either filling has first Betti number at least two, $X$ is reducible. Assume irreducibility. If its sole torus boundary compressed, a compressing disk and a collar of the torus would cut off a spherical boundary. Irreducibility caps the remaining side by a ball, yielding a solid torus. Every solid-torus filling has cyclic first homology, so the Betti-number hypothesis excludes compression. The sphere in $P$ cannot bound a rational homology ball, since it does not separate. Theorem 6.1(a) and (b) require solid-torus boundaries on the fillings, and (d) requires an irreducible $P'$. The added-in-proof case (e) concerns $H_2(P';\mathbb Q)=0$, excluded by Poincaré duality. Thus (c) would give a proper cable and the cabling slope. [Scharlemann, *Producing Reducible 3-Manifolds by Surgery on a Knot*, Topology 29 (1990), Theorem 6.1, p. 497, and added-in-proof correction, p. 499](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~mgscharl/papers/reducible.pdf#page=17)

To check the integral obstruction in (c), use a companion solid torus $V$ in $P$ and a cable whose surface slope on $\partial V$ is $p\mu_V+q\lambda_V$, with $|q|\ge2$. In the cable exterior inside $V$, the small meridian $m$ and a lift $l$ of the outer longitude freely generate first homology, and $[\mu_V]=q m$ by a meridian disk of $V$. The cabling annulus identifies the filling slope with $q(pm+l)$ in that group. The filling therefore gives a manifold with $H_1=\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/|q|$. The annulus capped by the filling meridian disk compresses the outer torus along an essential curve. Cutting along that disk gives a connected piece with spherical boundary. After capping it, the original filled companion is the connected sum of this closed piece and a solid torus. Hence the $\mathbb Z/|q|$ lies in a closed connected summand that survives gluing back the exterior of $V$. This contradicts torsion-freeness of $H_1(P')$.

For the native-cut criterion, the first homology rank of the exterior of both original components is $3-r$, where $r$ is the rank of the winding row $H_2(Y_1;\mathbb Q)\to\mathbb Q^2$. This follows from the pair exact sequence and excision to two meridian disks. Filling is surjective on first homology, so the joint rank three forces $r=0$ and both components are nullhomologous in $Y_1$. For either component $Q$, its single exterior has $H_1\cong\mathbb Z^2$, with a primitive meridian and a homologically zero Seifert longitude. Its integral slope $\lambda+n\mu$ gives $H_1(Z)\cong\mathbb Z\oplus\mathbb Z/n$, with $\mathbb Z/0=\mathbb Z$. The second surgery raises $b_1$ by at most one, because drilling one knot adds at most one and filling is a quotient. Thus $b_1(Z)=2$, $n=0$, and $H_1(Z)$ is torsion-free. Apply the obstruction in the preceding two paragraphs to $P=Y_1$ and $P'=Z$ if $Z$ is reducible. The native $Z$, rather than the meridional $Y_1$, supplies the rank-two test against a solid torus.

Take an essential sphere in the resulting reducible exterior of $Q$. If it is nonseparating in $Y_1$, its connected cut capped by two balls is $S^3$ by prime decomposition, and the original cut is a twice-punctured $S^3$, hence $S^2\times I$. In product coordinates a vertical arc disjoint from $Q$ exists because the projection of a smooth compact curve has empty interior in $S^2$. Removing a small neighborhood of that arc gives a ball containing $Q$. If the essential sphere separates, cap the side disjoint from $Q$. That side is either $S^3$ or $Y_1$ by prime decomposition of $Y_1$. In the first case its uncapped side is a ball in the exterior, contradicting essentiality. In the second case the other side is a ball containing $Q$. Conversely, surgery on a knot in a ball retains the original $Y_1$ summand, so its native cut is reducible. In particular both untouched native cuts are irreducible when neither component is local. [Milnor, *A Unique Decomposition Theorem for 3-Manifolds*, Theorem 1 and Lemmas 1–2, journal reproduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/856462048/Milnor-UniqueDecompositionTheorem-1962)

## Localization with a nontrivial stationary knot

For a local nontrivial $Q$, its native cut is $Z=M\#Y_1$, where $M=S^3_0(Q)$ is irreducible and $H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z$. Apply the same obstruction with $P=Z$, $P'=Y_3$, and moving knot $K$. Its exterior has an essential sphere. A nonseparating such sphere gives the required one. If the sphere separates $Z$, cap the side disjoint from $K$, obtaining $W$. This summand survives in $Y_3$, so $W\cong Y_j$ by prime uniqueness. It is also a summand of $M\#Y_1$, so $j\le1$. For $j=0$ its uncapped piece is a ball disjoint from $K$, contradicting essentiality. For $j=1$ choose a product sphere in the punctured $W$, which is nonseparating in $Z$ and misses the moving tube. [Gabai, *Foliations and the Topology of 3-Manifolds III*, Corollary 8.3 and Remarks 8.3′(1), p. 525, journal reproduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/1026013102/2-Gabai) · [Milnor, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800)

All nonseparating spheres in $Z$ are ambiently isotopic as unparametrized spheres. First take disjoint spheres $S,T$. Cutting and capping at $S$ gives $M$ by prime uniqueness. In this capped $M$, $T$ bounds a ball. With zero or two cap balls inside, the restored handle stays on one side of $T$ and $T$ separates $Z$. Thus exactly one cap lies in its ball. The region between $T$ and that sphere face is a twice-punctured $S^3$, equivalently $S^2\times I$, giving the isotopy. For intersecting spheres fix $S$ and choose an innermost disk of $S$ among intersection circles. Compress $T$ along that disk and push the two replacements off $T$ to the side from which the disk meets it. The push can be arranged with fewer intersection circles with $S$ since the two copies of the compression disk sit off $S$ and the identified circle disappears. The two oriented replacement classes add to $[T]$, using the boundary of the compression thickening. At least one class is nonzero and hence nonseparating. This replacement is disjoint from $T$, so it is isotopic to $T$ by the previous argument. Iterate to disjointness from $S$ and apply the disjoint case again. Extend the resulting isotopy to $Z$ by the smooth isotopy extension theorem. [Milnor, *Lectures on the h-Cobordism Theorem*, Theorem 5.8](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/hcobord.pdf#page=68)

Use an ambient isotopy carrying the sphere found in the exterior of $K$ to the original $S_0$. Carry the entire moving framing in this isotopy. The endpoint misses $S_0$ and, by a small perturbation supported away from $S_0$, misses the stationary filling core $Q^*$. The solid torus of $Q^*$ and a short external collar miss $S_0$. Radial expansion in this neighborhood pushes the compact moving curve out of the filling torus while preserving its gap from $S_0$. Explicitly, choose a strictly increasing radial coordinate change fixed near the core and the outer edge of the collar and taking all radii attained by the moving curve inside the filling torus beyond its original boundary. A sufficiently small framed tube follows the curve. The endpoint now pulls back to the original exterior of $Q$, and a vertical arc avoiding both curves in the cut $S^2\times I$ provides the common ball. To enclose both full tubes with their original parametrizations, first find a product ball enclosing the two cores. Shrink the full tubes in their own disk coordinates into that ball by an ambient isotopy fixed near the slab ends, then pull the ball back by that ambient isotopy.

For a generic framed isotopy of the moving curve relative to its endpoints, its track crosses $Q^*$ transversely at finitely many distinct times. Away from short crossing windows the track is compact and misses the core. Push its portions in the filling torus into the outer collar. At a crossing, detours to opposite sides of a core meridian disk differ by one band sum with its boundary. Its target slope when read on the original stationary tube is exactly the stationary attaching framing. The product in a meridian disk and a short interval along $Q^*$ carries the two normal coordinates of the moving arc, giving the framed band sum with the sign of the passage. Equivalently, the detour after filling is a disk isotopy carrying those normal coordinates. The resulting finite sequence consists of isotopies and forward or inverse slides of $K$ over $Q$. Each isotopy is used on a collar of the once-filled level with identity towards the inlet. Reverse the time parameter for the inverse isotopy and undo the slides in reverse order. The smooth two-handle trace and actual outlet map are carried by this comparison. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, §2, Lemma 2.1, and proof of Proposition 3.2, pp. 2313–2314](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=9)

For local unknotted $Q$, the once-filled level is $Y_2$. The rank-increasing single-knot surgery theorem places the moving knot as a disk-framed unknot in a ball there. Shrink and move that ball off the stationary core and $S_0$, then use the same endpoint and core passage argument. This case requires the knot theorem rather than the assertion about isotopy of all nonseparating spheres. [Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, *Classification of Trisections and the Generalized Property R Conjecture*, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06561#page=9)

If nontrivial $Q$ is fibered in the capped $S^3$, the fiber argument applies to the localized capped $M=S^3_0(Q)$. The local joint outlet is $Y_2$, and the moving knot has winding zero over the base of that fibration by the zero local linking matrix. The ball alternative preserves $M$ as a summand of $Y_2$, which is impossible. The cable alternative adds the torsion computed above. The surface-bundle surgery theorem then places the moving knot in a fiber with the fiber slope, by an isotopy realized as slides over $Q$ in the capped $S^3$. The closed fiber meets the stationary core once, so the moving curve can be displaced from that point within the fiber before reading the curve in the punctured fiber of the capped knot exterior. This gives fiber placement for the localized pair. The original $Z=M\#Y_1$ has the extra nonseparating sphere and is outside that surface-bundle application. [Scharlemann–Thompson, Corollary 4.2](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~mgscharl/papers/SurgerinSurfacexI.pdf) · [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, proof of Theorem 3.3, p. 2314](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=10)

## Terminal cancellation on the actual closing

Let a split zero-framed $A$ on $D^4$ supply the incoming $Y_1$, and let the immediate joint outlet be glued by the actual $\gamma$ to an upper body $U\cong\natural_3(S^1\times D^3)$. The ball carrying the localized pair can be shrunk and moved by ambient isotopy of $Y_1$ into a small ball in the unchanged part of $S^3$ before $A$-surgery. This can be done by shrinking the embedded ball in its own parametrization and then moving it along an arc, extending both isotopies to the ambient manifold. Extend the resulting boundary isotopy into a collar of the $A$-trace by making its time parameter zero at the bottom. This produces a simultaneous $S^3$ diagram with $A$ split from the actual pair, so its product dual is visibly in the untouched $Y_1$ summand of the outlet. Write $N$ for the local surgery outlet. Prime uniqueness applied to $Y_1\#N\cong Y_3$ gives $N\cong Y_2$. This only identifies the boundary of the local two-handle trace. [Milnor, prime uniqueness](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800) · [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, Proposition 2.2](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=6)

Write $\delta$ for the composed outlet map from the original to the split diagram. The carried closing is $\gamma'=\gamma\delta^{-1}$ with inverse $\delta\gamma^{-1}$. Choose a model closing $\lambda$ to a standard rank-three upper body that sends the product sphere for $A$ to the belt sphere of its selected reverse 1-handle. The boundary change $\lambda(\gamma')^{-1}$ extends over $U$ to the standard upper body, with inverse boundary map $\gamma'\lambda^{-1}$. The product dual of $A$ meets that belt sphere once and misses the other chosen spheres. The two local reverse tubes lie in the other summand. Order the selected 1-handle last and the reverse $A$-handle first, and cancel the adjacent pair by the transverse one-intersection theorem. The product flow gives the comparison on the cancelling strip and the reverse flow gives its inverse. Forward counts are $(n_2,n_3)=(3,3)\to(2,2)$, with no births. Any subsequent recognition of the localized closure needs a property of the actual two-component link. [Laudenbach–Poénaru, Theorem A and the boundary extension in its proof, pp. 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=6) · [Milnor, Theorem 5.4](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/hcobord.pdf#page=53)

The [unfilled four-point lower bound](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/RELATIVE_SPHERE_LENGTH.md) supplies a check on the scope. It constrains the isotopy class of the moving knot in the fixed unfilled exterior. The present isotopy is in $Z$ and the return to the original diagram uses the corresponding stationary slides. Thus the argument gives existence of a finite framed return and permits a change in the unfilled isotopy class. The bridge results with prescribed ports, disk placement or puncture counts concern more restrictive moves. For a nonterminal occurrence the construction gives a relative trace map, with later tubes carried by the induced boundary map at their levels. The cancellation above uses the stated immediate actual upper body.
