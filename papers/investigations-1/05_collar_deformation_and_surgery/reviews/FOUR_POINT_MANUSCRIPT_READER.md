## The face and its only possible bridge chord

The boundary of the stationary-filled, drilled slab is connected. Before gluing along the seam, its two four-holed spheres joined by four lateral annuli give a genus-three surface. On one face, a clipped bridge has boundary given by a face chord and a single section of the return annulus. The once-punctured torus formed from the return-side pants and that annulus has a compression curve meeting the annulus core once. Compressing its inward collar by a thickening of the bridge yields a frontier disk with boundary the separator \(s\) of the chord. Reversing that compression adds one 1-handle to a disk collar, so the separated piece \(T\) is a solid torus. The complementary piece \(Z\) is connected because \(X\) is connected and their entire interface is a disk.

Capping each pair of pants along \(s\) gives an annulus on its own side of the interface. The return annulus generator is nonzero in \(\pi_1T\) and the through annulus generator is nonzero in \(\pi_1Z\), since their cuff meridians have infinite order in \(\pi_1X\). Both return meridians and the through meridians have infinite order by the integral rank calculation on the actual free outlet. The basepoint on the disk gives the free product \(\pi_1X=\pi_1T*\pi_1Z\). An essential nonperipheral circle disjoint from \(s\) is isotopic to \(s\). In minimal position any other such circle has successive waves alternating between the two pants. Each wave cuts off a single cuff. If it cut off zero cuffs, an innermost bigon would reduce the intersections. Closing that wave by paths on the capping disk gives a generator or its inverse in the corresponding annulus. All syllables are nonzero and alternate, including the last and first, so the circle is nontrivial. This proof works with arbitrary \(Z\). The return-side pair of pants has one joining arc class up to independent cuff turns. Extending the endpoint motions through the return annulus realizes those turns by changing its section winding.

For the general compression result, the arc order along the moving core gives the two adjacent ear intervals. In the moving filling, the remaining sector meets exactly the two through annuli. Giving each cut-surface inclusion its own paths lying in the corresponding \(U_\pm\) makes the attaching cyclic groups and stable letter agree on that inclusion. For an injective vertex group, a vertex word mapping into either of the full ambient edge images already lies in the corresponding surface edge image. Britton's reduced word test therefore reflects each pinch. If both surface groups injected in the cut outlet, their actual gluing would embed a genus-two surface group in \(F_3\). The Loop Theorem applies to the failed \(U_\pm\) injection, and its disk boundary cannot be peripheral because the through meridians survive. This produces a disk on \(U_\pm\). The wave argument needs an actual bridge on the four-holed face as its premise. [Higman–Neumann–Neumann, Theorem I](https://doi.org/10.1112/jlms/s1-24.4.247) · [Britton, Lemma 4](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970200) · [Stallings, pp. 12–19](https://doi.org/10.2307/1970146).

## Terminating decision with the supplied marking

1. Preserve the original boundary triangulation up to subdivision while finding its irreducible summand. On the current piece enumerate fundamental normal spheres and doubles of fundamental normal projective planes. The latter candidates are required in Matveev's Theorem 4.1.12(2). A nonseparating sphere is essential. For a separating sphere, the side containing the original connected nonspherical boundary cannot be a ball bounded by the sphere. Cap the opposite side and recognize \(S^3\) to decide whether that opposite side is a ball. Cut and cap at any essential sphere and retain the original boundary-bearing piece. This removes a nontrivial closed connected summand, including \(S^2\times S^1\) for a nonseparating sphere, so Kneser finiteness makes repetition terminate. The retained boundary inclusion induces the same disk-bounding test on each simple boundary curve by free-product injection and Dehn's lemma. [Matveev, 2nd ed., Theorems 3.3.28 and 4.1.12](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-540-45899-9) · [Jaco–Rubinstein, Theorem 5.11](https://www.maths.tcd.ie/EMIS/journals/NYJM/jdg/p/2003/65-1-3.pdf) · [Papakyriakopoulos, pp. 169–172](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.43.1.169).
2. Subdivide to include the selected four cuffs and the complementary boundary triangulation's edges as a graph. Its clean regions are the interior of the selected face and open disks. A loop essential in the face stays essential in the full boundary, since a boundary disk crossing out of the face would have to contain an entire cuff, which survives in \(\pi_1X\). A peripheral face loop likewise cannot bound a proper disk. Matveev's Theorem 4.1.13 therefore returns a candidate separator or decides there is none. If a bridge exists, every returned disk must have its unique separator, so a candidate of the wrong partition is a valid negative answer. This argument does not require a disk of the right partition, in isolation, to cut off a solid torus. [Matveev, 2nd ed., definitions in §3.3.2 and Theorem 4.1.13](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-540-45899-9).
3. For the right partition choose the joining chord in the return-side pants and a reference wall section. Solve the integral equation \([\gamma_0]+n[\mu]=0\) in \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\). The meridian has infinite order, so Smith normal form gives zero or one candidate. Its simple boundary passes exactly when its word is trivial in the actual slab group, by Dehn's lemma. Enumerating finite PL disks with this exact boundary in all rational PL subdivisions terminates after a positive test. A finite simplicial embedding check verifies each candidate, and smoothing can be relative to the marked boundary. [Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton, Theorem 4.1](https://msp.org/gtm/2015/19-1/gtm-v19-n1-p08-s.pdf) · [Papakyriakopoulos, pp. 169–172](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.43.1.169).
4. If both sides pass, transport one separator by the actual seam. Isotopy of these unoriented essential simple circles can be decided by conjugacy up to inversion in the free face group, or by reducing normal curves on the marked surface. Disjoint arcs joining the complementary pairs share the separator obtained by taking the frontier of their corresponding cuff-and-arc neighborhoods. Conversely, equal slopes allow representatives in opposite pants, with the four end germs fitted independently and the corresponding wall turns absorbed. Disk interiors can then be separated by innermost-circle replacement because the disk boundaries are disjoint. This is an existence test for representatives with the allowed endpoint turns. ## Twists, the unfilled test and the terminal count

Orient the return chords \(1\to2\) and \(4\to3\) in the common face orientation. A twist about the basic \(13\) circle adds opposite multiples of its class to the two absolute chord differences. Its lower image is \(\mu_-+\mu_t\) and its upper image \(\mu_t+\mu_+\). Thus the two through coefficients change by opposite units per turn, with only an own-return coefficient also changing on each side. The distance-one equivariant coefficient already gives \(b_-+b_+=0\). One common twist sets both \(b_\pm\) to zero, and the two wall windings cancel their own-meridian terms. The revised manuscript uses this direct calculation with the reversed orientations on both closing chords, which likewise yield opposite increments.

For the explicit example choose \(d\) crossing the original separator \(s\) geometrically twice. One can draw the boundaries of neighborhoods of the \(12\) and \(13\) joins to meet twice. The partitions preclude zero intersections and separating circles intersect an even number of times. For \(s_1=T_d(s)\), the twist formula gives \(i(s_1,s)=4\). It gives \(i(T_{s_1}^k(s),s)=16|k|\) for the second twist. The twist about \(s_1\) fixes each cuff and keeps both endpoints of each return on the same side of \(s_1\). The algebraic chord intersection is zero, so the absolute difference of old and new chords vanishes already in face homology. The unchanged wall sections give zero in the full integral slab homology. A new disk at any winding would force the new separator to bound a disk by the frontier construction, contradicting uniqueness and the positive geometric intersection. Its cut in \(S^1\times S^2\) is \(S^2\times I\), by cutting and capping followed by prime uniqueness. Its two intersection signs are opposite since the moving knot is null-homologous.
