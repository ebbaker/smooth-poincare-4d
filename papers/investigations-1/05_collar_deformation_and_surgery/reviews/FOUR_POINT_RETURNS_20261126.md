**A bridge, if it exists, determines its face chord.** For a sphere cutting an integral two-attachment block into one return on each face and two through arcs, a rank-three free outlet forces a compressing disk on at least one face with its return wall adjoined. The possible bridge chord can be recovered and tested on the four-holed face itself. The distinction matters on a single explicit exterior. Its original chords admit two disjoint disks and a strict terminal reduction. A different matched choice gives two null-homologous prescribed boundaries, neither of which bounds a disk. ## Which compression supplies a usable arc?

Let \(X\) be the slab with all four moving arcs drilled and the stationary knot filled. Each four-holed face together with its return wall is a twice-punctured torus \(U\). In the moving solid torus, two annuli complete the punctured cut sphere to a closed surface of genus two. On cutting this surface open, the two return sectors extend collars, but the middle solid torus attaches along **both** through walls. Their meridians have infinite order. The surface and ambient gluings have the same cyclic edge groups. HNN normal form shows that if both copies of \(U\) injected, the actual regluing would embed a closed genus-two group in the free outlet group. The Loop Theorem gives a nonperipheral compression on at least one original \(U\). Its boundary need not run over the prescribed return. [Gluing](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/FOUR_PORT_SUBSURFACE_COMPRESSION.md) · [Independent group and cut reading](FOUR_POINT_COMPRESSION_READER.md)

A bridge cuts off a ball containing its return as a trivial arc. Drilling the arc gives a solid torus cut off by a disk. The boundary \(s\) of that disk separates the return holes from the through holes on the four-holed face. A different essential nonperipheral circle meets \(s\) and has alternating arcs in the two pairs of pants. Each such arc cuts off one hole and contributes a nontrivial syllable in one of two different free factors. Thus \(s\) is the unique disk-bounding face slope whenever a bridge exists. Its return-side pair of pants determines the chord up to endpoint turns, which are absorbed by the wall section. Conversely a face disk cutting off the appropriate solid torus gives the bridge because its return meridian is primitive. [Unique face slope and converse](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/FOUR_PORT_UNIQUE_FACE_SLOPE.md)

For a triangulated input retain the marked boundary-bearing prime summand. A boundary pattern on the four cuffs and complementary surface restricts the disk search to the selected face. Matveev's patterned-boundary algorithm supplies any essential disk there or certifies its absence. Test the returned partition and the candidate chord, using the unique allowable wall winding and triviality of its simple boundary word in \(\pi_1 X\). If any return bridge exists on that face, uniqueness forces the procedure to test it. A simultaneous matched pair requires the two successful separators to agree under the actual seam, so the two chords can be represented in opposite pairs of pants. This is a decision procedure on a presented exterior. Universal existence for this pairing remains a different question.
