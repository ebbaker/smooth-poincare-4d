## Why the four-point lower bound is absolute before filling

Here use \(\Gamma=\pi_1(Y_1\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu Q)=H*\langle t\rangle\) **before** filling, where \(H\) is the local knot group and \(t\) the marked seam generator. In the oriented alternating diagram the four stable letters have alternating signs, with four intervening returns in \(H\). Three vertex words are nontrivial because they survive stationary filling. The fourth is conjugate to \(\lambda_Q^n\) and is nontrivial by peripheral injectivity. Thus the conjugacy class of \(K\) has cyclic edge length four in the one-loop splitting with trivial edge group. The following checks that changing the sphere preserves that length.

For any nonseparating sphere \(\Sigma\) disjoint from the stationary tube, capping the two faces of the cut in \(Y_1\) gives a closed manifold \(P\) with \(\pi_1(P)*\mathbb Z\cong\pi_1(Y_1)=\mathbb Z\), so \(P\cong S^3\). This yields \(\Gamma=H_\Sigma*\langle t_\Sigma\rangle\) with \(H_\Sigma\) the capped knot group. The original \(H\) and the candidate \(H_\Sigma\) each contain a representative of the same peripheral meridian and are normally generated by that representative, since meridional filling yields \(S^3\) in both capped chambers. In the Bass–Serre tree for the candidate sphere, restrict the action to \(H\). The trivial edge stabilizers express \(H\) as a free product of its vertex stabilizers and a free group. Choose the vertex representative containing the meridian. Quotienting by its normal closure leaves all other free factors, so normal generation shows these factors are trivial. Thus \(H\) fixes a vertex. Restricting the original tree to \(H_\Sigma\) gives the reciprocal inclusion up to conjugacy. Combining the two inclusions and using malnormality of a nontrivial free factor gives \(H_\Sigma=H\) after conjugation. [Serre, *Trees*, I.5.4–5.5](https://link.springer.com/book/10.1007/978-3-642-61856-7)

Here is a direct check of the possible change of stable letter once the vertex groups agree. Strip the initial and final \(H\) factors of \(t_\Sigma\) to obtain \(v\), whose reduced word starts and ends with bare \(t^{\pm1}\). Then \(H,v\) still generate \(\Gamma\) and \(v\) maps to \(\pm1\) in the cyclic quotient by the normal closure of \(H\). In the original one-loop tree \(v\) is hyperbolic. From the \(H\)-vertex the geodesic towards \(v^mH\) starts along the same edge as the geodesic towards \(vH\) for \(m>0\), or towards \(v^{-1}H\) for \(m<0\), by projection to its axis. Applying the same observation to the inverse shows that \(v^m\) starts and ends with bare \(t\)-letters. Its edge length is \(2d(H,\operatorname{Axis}(v))+|m|\ell(v)\), at least the edge length of \(v\). In a word alternating nonzero powers of \(v\) and nonidentity intervening \(H\) factors there is no pinch between successive blocks. Thus every element of \(\langle H,v\rangle\setminus H\) has edge length at least that of \(v\). Apply this to \(t\); it gives \(v=t^{\pm1}\) and hence \(t_\Sigma=a t^{\pm1}b\) for \(a,b\in H\).

Replacing the stable letter by this expression does not alter the cyclic edge length. At two inverse letters the intervening vertex element is conjugated by an \(H\)-factor, so a nonidentity element stays nonidentity. At two letters of equal sign there is no pinch. Reading a loop through the candidate transverse sphere gives an edge path with one edge per actual crossing, whose cyclic reduction can only decrease length. Hence every candidate sphere has at least four crossings. The displayed sphere realizes four. Finally, a nonperipheral unfilled face disk would cut the original four-holed sphere into two punctured spheres with two ports on each. After compressing, the associated oriented spheres sum to the original nonzero class; one must be nonseparating and would have only two crossings. This contradiction rules out unfilled nonperipheral face disks on both faces.
