The check concerns smooth, oriented surgery on the specified link and integral homology.

Write \(Z=\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\). For the original filling cores \(d_Q,d_V\), the geometric sphere minimum among embedded nonseparating spheres with algebraic intersection zero is at least \(12\) for \(d_Q\) and exactly \(4\) for \(d_V\). Neither core meets any embedded sphere once, so neither original reverse tube can give a first native cancellation with any upper belt. These results are independent of the chosen identification \(\gamma:Z\to\partial U\) with the actual upper body, since each belt pulls back to a sphere in \(Z\).

## The lower bounds

Figure 1 at \(n=3\) gives the square knot \(Q=T_{3,2}\#T_{-3,2}\) and \(V_3=T_{3,4}\#T_{-3,4}\). In GST §7 the two original \(0\)-framed components come from dualizing the two attachments of Figure 9. The joint surgery gives \(Z\) by that construction. The native reverse filling on the drilled \(d_i\)-torus uses \(\mu_i\), the original meridian, whose distance from the meridian of the dual \(d_i\) is one. The latter meridian is the original \(\lambda_i\). The reverse fillings are the single-knot zero surgeries \(M_V\) and \(M_Q\), respectively, whose fibers have genus \(6\) and \(2\) by boundary connected sum of fiber surfaces and capping on the original zero longitude. The reverse-filling core is the corresponding original knot in the other knot's zero surgery. Its homology vanishes by zero linking. [GST, Figs. 1, 9–13 and §7, pp. 2306 and 2328–2332](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=24) · [Meier–Zupan, published §§2.2 and 4.1](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10464744)

Let \(S\) be an oriented embedded nonseparating sphere in \(Z\) transverse to \(d_i\), with \(S\cdot d_i=0\) and \(k=|S\cap d_i|\). Successively join adjacent oppositely signed points on the oriented \(d_i\) by tubing along its intervening arc, in a small neighborhood disjoint from the rest of the current surface. The surface stays connected, the surgery preserves its homology class by the boundary of the tube neighborhood, and every tube removes two intersections and contributes one genus. The result is a connected genus-\(k/2\) surface in the common exterior \(E_i\), after shrinking the core neighborhood if needed.

For either closed filling \(N\) with core \(b\), the exact sequence of the pair \((N,E_i)\) gives an injection \(H_2(E_i)\to H_2(N)\) with image \(\ker(\cdot b)\). Excision identifies \(H_3(N,E_i)\) and \(H_2(N,E_i)\) with the oriented solid torus relative to its boundary. The orientation class maps to a generator in degree three, and the map in degree two counts intersections with \(b\). The class of \(S\) is primitive since a loop can cross a nonseparating sphere once. In \(Z\) the two marked original cores represent the meridian basis, so the exterior class is a generator of \(H_2(E_i)\). In the reverse filling it gives a generator of \(H_2(N)\cong\mathbb Z\) because the reverse-filling core is null-homologous. That is the fiber class up to sign.

There is a direct genus check in addition to the note's Thurston-norm argument. For an oriented embedded surface \(T\) representing a fiber generator, the bundle class vanishes on loops in \(T\), since the loops can be pushed immediately off \(T\). Hence \(T\) lifts to the cyclic cover \(F\times\mathbb R\). Its lifted homology class has degree \(\pm1\) under projection to \(F\), since the generator of the cover maps to the primitive fiber class downstairs. A nonzero-degree map of closed oriented surfaces injects rational first cohomology by the intersection pairing. It follows that \(g(T)\ge g(F)\) and \(k\ge 2g(F)\).

The one-intersection check uses the native slope. A sphere meeting \(d_i\) once, thickened and joined along the rest of that circle, gives a punctured \(S^1\times S^2\) with \(d_i\) as its product core. In the capped summand the complementary solid-torus disk slope is the meridian of \(d_i\). The distance-one reverse filling replaces this punctured summand by a ball, leaving \(S^1\times S^2\) by uniqueness of prime decomposition. Both native reverse fillings are positive-genus closed surface bundles and irreducible. An essential sphere disjoint from \(d_i\) would also force a nonseparating sphere or an untouched \(S^1\times S^2\) summand in the reverse filling. These checks yield the additional lower bounds of three for odd pairing and two for essential spheres with no homology restriction. [Milnor, *A unique decomposition theorem for 3-manifolds*, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800)

Since a primitive class has at least one odd coordinate in the dual basis, a nonseparating sphere meets the two original cores at least five times in total. On the algebraic axes with pairings \((\pm1,0)\) and \((0,\pm1)\) against \((d_Q,d_V)\), the corresponding total lower bounds are \(7\) and \(15\). The four-point attainment is a bound on intersection with \(d_V\) alone. It does not determine the total intersections of that sphere with the pair.

## A sphere with four original \(d_V\) intersections

In \(M_Q\) an ambient isotopy carries the original framed \(V_3\) to \(c\) on a closed genus-two fiber with its fiber slope. This follows from GST Corollary 5.4 with the actual joint surgery as input. Its forward slides over \(Q\) are realized in \(M_Q\) by moving the tube across disks of the \(Q\)-filling torus. Extend the isotopy across the \(V_3\) filling in its transported solid-torus coordinates to obtain \(P:Z\to Z'\). The new \(d'_V=P(d_V)\) is the labeled core of that filling. The transported \(Q\) core \(d'_Q=P(d_Q)\) lies in the exterior of that filling and represents the primitive generator of \(H_1(M_Q)\). The closing in the fiber presentation is \(\gamma P^{-1}\), with inverse \(P\gamma^{-1}\). Scharlemann identifies the downstairs curve as slope \(3/7\) in his convention. The intersection calculation only needs the fiber framing and the monodromy condition. [GST, Lemma 2.1, Proposition 3.2, Corollaries 5.4 and 6.4](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf) · [Scharlemann, arXiv:1208.1299v3, §§3–4 and Theorem 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1208.1299v3#S4)

Take a middle fiber \(\widehat F\) after cutting the closed bundle at the \(c\)-fiber. The opposite halves give tracks from \(c,h(c)\) on \(\widehat F\) to longitudes on the opposite sides of \(\partial\nu c\), up to choosing the inverse monodromy convention. GST Corollary 5.4 lets the two curves on \(\widehat F\) be disjoint and nonisotopic. Both are nonseparating because they have the same separating type and two essential separating curves in genus two cannot be disjoint and nonparallel. Their complement after deleting thin annuli is connected, since a disconnected complement would exhibit an annulus between the two curves. It has genus zero and four boundary components.

The two tracks live in opposite product halves. Each extends to a compression disk by adjoining one meridian disk of the \(V_3\)-filling torus. The longitude is meridional because GST determines the actual filling slope to be the fiber framing. The tracks may be thickened so that the two compression disks each have two parallel copies with four distinct meridian disks in the filling torus. Compressing \(\widehat F\) on both sides using these disks gives a connected smooth sphere after rounding. Its four transverse intersections with \(d'_V\) are the four meridian-disk centers, with opposite signs in each pair. GST's Surgery Principle and Proposition 5.2 supply this weak reduction. The four-point count follows by retaining the labeled filling torus in the compression model. [GST, Lemma 4.1, Example 4.5 and Proposition 5.2, pp. 2315–2322](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=11)

The compression preserves the homology class of \(\widehat F\) in \(Z'\), since each difference bounds a thickened compression disk. The pairing of \(\widehat F\) with \(d'_Q\) can be computed before the \(V_3\) filling in \(M_Q\), where the transported \(d'_Q\) is still the generator and \(\widehat F\) is a fiber. That pairing is \(\pm1\). The resulting sphere is therefore nonseparating and primitive and pulls back through \(P^{-1}\) with exactly four intersections against the original \(d_V\). The isotopy of monodromy curves can run through the capped part of the closed fiber. Requiring the same isotopy in the once-punctured knot fiber would introduce a false premise for this nontrivial partner, by GST Proposition 5.1. [GST, Proposition 5.1 and Corollary 5.4, pp. 2320–2323](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=16)

## Scope of the closing

Figure 9 left gives the actual upper body and the reverse two-handle cobordism. Its subsequent dualized diagrams encode the two marked forward handles. For each original outgoing face \(o_i:D^2\times S^1\to Z\), the reverse tube is \(\gamma o_i\tau\), where \(\tau(v,z)=(z,v)\), with inverse \(\tau^{-1}o_i^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\) on its image. Under a trace comparison with outgoing map \(P\), the same tube is \((\gamma P^{-1})(P o_i)\tau\). This identity carries the framing and applies even when the dual knot represented by an active diagram changes. [GST, §7, Figs. 9–13](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=25)

The actual capped trace is simply connected by its handle indices and has Euler characteristic two. Integral duality, Hurewicz and Whitehead give a homotopy four-sphere. Meier–Zupan's published Theorem 1.1 applies to this exact handle structure, since there are no one-handles, two two-handles, and \(Q\) is a generalized square knot. Their Corollary 1.3 also places the family in Gompf's prior standardness result. Comparison of the two embedded upper one-handlebodies in standard \(S^4\), as in Laudenbach–Poénaru's Lemma 1 proof, gives a diffeomorphism between the lower trace and the split trace. The split trace's new product co-cores permit two cancellations. The formulas above transport the old framed cores as annotations on that comparison. The smooth replacement leaves the native slide-only question open. The separate GST §10 diagrammatic procedure introduces one temporary canceling Hopf pair, uses the same pair for the \(3\to2\to1\to0\) parameter changes, and removes it at the slide-trivial \(L_{0,1}\) endpoint. The initial lower bound of twelve has no sharpness certificate in this review. [Meier–Zupan, JDG 122 (2022), Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 1.3](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10464744) · [Laudenbach–Poénaru, §2, Lemma 1 and proof](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=3) · [GST, §10, Proposition 10.1 and Figs. 18–20](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=36)
