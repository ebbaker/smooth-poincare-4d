## The trace and its two presentations

Write \(M=S^3_0(Q)\), \(G=\pi_1 M\) and
\(Z=(S^1\times S^2)\#M\). The trace has only one 2-handle seen from either
side. Van Kampen on the actual inlet and actual immediate outlet yields
\[
 \Pi=(G*\langle t\rangle)/\langle\!\langle K\rangle\!\rangle
      = F_3/\langle\!\langle d\rangle\!\rangle ,
\]
where \(d\) is the surgery dual. Since the source and outlet groups
before imposing the relations have abelian ranks two and three,
the common quotient has rank two. The first presentation then has
zero abelianized relation and gives free abelian homology of rank
two. Hence the exponent vector of \(d\) is primitive in
\(\mathbb Z^3\). Divisibility of the exponent vector would follow
from a proper power. The dual is therefore nonidentity and a
nonpower, without assuming it primitive in the free group.

I checked the relevant one-relator module statement in Antolín,
Dicks and Linnell, Setting 6.3 and Corollary 6.5(i) (preprint
numbering). On the Cayley tree of a free group a singleton
nonidentity relator is an admissible staggered set. The
abelianization of its normal closure is the permutation module
on the quotient modulo the image of the root subgroup. For the
dual nonpower the image of its root subgroup is trivial. Thus the
lifted relation boundaries form an integral basis of the first
homology of the universal cover's one-skeleton. The relative
homology sequence for the lifted 2-cells gives zero \(H_2\)
of the universal cover. It is simply connected and
two-dimensional, hence contractible. The dual presentation
complex therefore gives \(\operatorname{cd}_{\mathbb Z}\Pi\le 2\)
and \(H_2(\Pi;\mathbb Z)=0\), since the cellular differential
from the sole 2-cell is the nonzero exponent vector.
[Antolín–Dicks–Linnell, §§2 and 6](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/202225/1/1002.1934v1)
· [Louder–Wilton, §5.2](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/10131842/1/Louder_Negative%20immersions%20for%20one-relator%20groups_AAM.pdf)

For \(Q\) nontrivial, Gabai's Corollary 8.3 and Remarks 8.3′(1)
give irreducibility of \(M\). By the sphere theorem it has
\(\pi_2=0\). Its universal cover is noncompact because
\(H_1(M)=\mathbb Z\), so the remaining positive dimensional
homology vanishes and the cover is contractible. The closed
oriented \(K(G,1)\) then gives cohomological dimension three.
The local-indicability check covers infinite-index subgroups
as well as finite-index subgroups. For a nontrivial finitely
generated subgroup of infinite index take the Scott core in
the associated noncompact cover. The cover is irreducible by
Meeks–Simon–Yau. Each spherical boundary of the connected
core bounds a ball outside it, since the core lies on one side
and its injected fundamental group is nontrivial. The outside
balls are mutually disjoint and may be adjoined. The remaining
boundary is nonempty by noncompactness and has nonpositive
Euler characteristic. Doubling gives \(\chi(C)\le0\) for the
resulting core, hence \(b_1(C)\ge1\). The finite-index case
uses the restriction of \(G\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb Z\), whose
image still has finite index. [Gabai, Corollary 8.3 and Remarks
8.3′, pp. 525–526](https://www.scribd.com/document/1026013102/2-Gabai)
· [Scott, compact core theorem, p. 246](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1112/jlms/s2-7.2.246)
· [Meeks–Simon–Yau, Theorem 3, p. 647](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math748/meeks-simon-yau.pdf)

Theorem 2.1 in Howie–Short applies to both locally indicable
free factors and a cyclically reduced relator of free-product
length at least two, even when the relator is a power. It would
embed \(G\) in \(\Pi\), which is impossible by the restriction
of projective resolutions. The identity relator would also
leave \(G\) embedded. A representative in \(\langle t\rangle\)
would be identity by the abelianization test. The remaining
class is represented by a nonidentity element of \([G,G]\).
[Howie–Short, Theorem 2.1, p. 1089](https://pure.hw.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/99104038/11391675_10.4171_ggd_725_print.pdf)

For \(Q\) the unknot the filled inlet group is \(F_2\).
A nonidentity \(K\in[F_2,F_2]\) would give
\(H_2(\Pi;\mathbb Z)=N/[F_2,N]\) by the Hopf formula, where
\(N=\langle\!\langle K\rangle\!\rangle\). The one-relator module
described above, with its possibly nontrivial root subgroup,
has infinite cyclic coinvariants because the quotient acts
transitively on the basis. This contradicts the dual
calculation of zero \(H_2\). Thus \(K=1\) in \(F_2\).
The independent geometric check is Meier–Schirmer–Zupan,
Theorem 5.1 for the rank increase \(2\to3\), which puts the
moving knot in a ball in \(Z\) with disk framing.
[Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1, author version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06561#page=9)

For the algebra the required condition is \(F_3\) at the
immediate outlet of that one 2-handle. The homology alone is
insufficient. For example a split zero unknot in a ball in
\(Z\) has trivial attaching class and gives
\(M\#Y_2\), with the homology of \(Y_3\) for every \(Q\).
This violates the nonidentity conclusion as soon as \(Q\)
is nontrivial and exhibits why the reverse free presentation
is needed. The one-relator complex models \(\Pi\) and its group
homology. The actual trace can have independent topology.

## Words on the actual seam

I recomputed the words with left-to-right paths and \(t\) the
chosen track from the lower face to the upper face in the
restored slab. At each seam the final face whisker of one arc
cancels the initial whisker of the next. The resulting based
moving words are \(a_0b_1c_0d_{-1}\) for the mixed pairing and
\(a_0b_1c_0d_1\) for the alternating pairing, which begins on
the upper \(41\) arc in the agreed convention. The lifted group
is the free product of the stationary chamber groups. A conjugate
of \(g\) in one chamber \(G_h\) retracts to a conjugate of \(g\)
in \(G_h\) and to identity in every other chamber. A lift change
translates all the indices together.

| Pairing | Check by deletion and cyclic reduction |
| --- | --- |
| Mixed | With \(b,d\ne1\), both extreme letters survive and cannot cancel across the cyclic seam. With exactly one extreme letter, the central product must vanish for ellipticity. With neither extreme letter, the result is the central \(ac\). The exact identity test is \(b=d=1\) and \(ac=1\). |
| Alternating | All four surviving letters leave a cyclically reduced square. After deleting any identity letter the two orders reduce cyclically to the product of the two chamber projections \(ac\) and \(bd\), up to cyclic rotation. For three surviving letters, the two sharing a chamber must be inverse. With two surviving letters they must be in the same chamber for ellipticity. The identity tests are \(a=c=1,\ bd=1\) or \(b=d=1,\ ac=1\). |

The projection-only mixed criterion is complete for ellipticity.
In the alternating pairing the word with \(c=a^{-1},d=b^{-1}\)
and \(a,b\ne1\) gives a length-four commutator even though all
chamber projections vanish. Consequently the nontrivial
stationary case permits at most one surviving individual mixed
return and at most three surviving alternating returns. The
latter bound is attained by the supplied ribbon placement,
where its two lower sides have inverse based classes, the
upper \(23\) cap is trivial, and the upper \(41\) class is the
Figure 5 partner. The unknot case requires identity and leaves
no mixed return class, though two same-face alternating classes
can be inverse at the level of the word test.
[Alternating ribbon placement and local trace](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/ALTERNATING_RIBBON_TRACE.md)
· [GST, Figure 5, Lemma 6.2(3) and Corollary 6.4](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf)

The through completion is the two through arcs joined by the
upper \(12\) and lower \(34\) chords. Its common-based word is
\(ac\). The group letter of either through arc alone depends
on the inter-face path \(u\). Replacing \(u\) by \(hu\) changes
the pair to \(a h^{-1},h c\), so their product is unchanged.
The same change conjugates the upper return letters by a common
\(h\) and leaves the lower ones unchanged. An end chord with its
endpoint tracks is different data. On a restored spherical face
any two paths with the same ends are homotopic rel endpoints.
Wall-section winding disappears when the moving tube is restored.
Thus independently changing the end chords of the through
completion, or freely choosing a return chord, leaves the capped
test intact for fixed basing. Common basing is required to test
products of individual classes.

The forgetful restoration map only gives a necessary bridge
condition. An actual bridge supplies a disk after clipping in
the drilled exterior. Its image supplies a null-homotopy in
the restored slab. The inverse step has to account for all
moving tube meridians as well as the boundary pattern. For
prescribed paths this distinction can already be tested by
the twisted-chord example on one fixed exterior.
[Twisted prescribed chords](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/FOUR_POINT_TWISTED_CHORDS.md)

## A geometric reduction from Scharlemann's theorem

**Claim.** Suppose \(Q\) is local in the original \(Y_1\), misses
its marked nonseparating sphere \(S\), and has its zero coefficient.
Suppose the joint integral outlet is the actual \(Y_3\).
A finite sequence of framed slides of \(K\) over \(Q\), and
ambient isotopies, makes the pair lie in a ball of \(Y_1\)
while retaining the original collar below the two-handle trace.
There is no restriction on the number of points on \(S\).

First suppose \(Q\) is nontrivial. Set \(X=Z-\operatorname{int}\nu K\).
If \(X\) is irreducible and has compressible torus boundary,
compress the boundary and cap the resulting sphere with a
ball. This says \(X\) is a solid torus, excluded because a
solid-torus filling cannot have the rank-two first homology
of \(Z\). If \(X\) is irreducible and boundary-incompressible,
apply the opening theorem of Scharlemann, pp. 481–482. The
original \(Z\) has a nonseparating sphere, which cannot
bound a rational homology ball, and the other filling \(Y_3\)
is reducible. The solid-torus cases are excluded by closedness.
Only a genuine cable with the cabling slope remains. In this
case the cabling annulus capped by the two filling meridian
disks cuts off a punctured lens space. Concretely its capped
side is obtained by attaching a 2-handle to the companion
solid torus along the cable slope on that torus and capping
the sphere. That slope represents \(w\) times the core in
first homology, where the genuine cable has winding
\(|w|\ge2\). This gives a summand with nonzero torsion in
\(H_1(Y_3;\mathbb Z)\), impossible. Thus \(X\) is reducible.
The cabling alternative here uses the exterior hypotheses
and the nonseparating sphere in \(Z\).
[Scharlemann, *Producing reducible 3-manifolds by surgery on a
knot*, main theorem and §6, pp. 481–482, 497–498](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~mgscharl/papers/reducible.pdf)

Take a sphere reducing \(X\). If it bounds a ball in \(Z\),
the ball has to contain \(K\), since otherwise it would
already bound a ball in \(X\). Surgery on such a local \(K\)
would leave the irreducible nontrivial summand \(M\) present
in \(Y_3\), contradicting prime uniqueness. If the sphere
separates \(Z\) without bounding a ball, the two capped sides
are \(M\) and \(S^1\times S^2\), again by prime uniqueness.
The knot must be on the \(M\) side or that summand would persist.
Choose a nonseparating sphere on the other side, missing its
puncture and \(K\). A reducing sphere already nonseparating
in \(Z\) works directly. In all cases an actual nonseparating
sphere \(T\) in \(Z\) misses the moving tube.

The spheres \(T,S\) are isotopic in \(Z=M\#(S^1\times S^2)\).
Trace Proposition 4 supplies a finite chain of nonseparating
spheres with consecutive spheres disjoint. For a disjoint
pair, cut along the first sphere and cap the two boundaries.
The result is \(M\) by prime uniqueness, so the second sphere
bounds a ball. This ball contains exactly one of the two caps,
since zero or two caps inside would leave the second sphere
separating after the boundaries are rejoined. Deleting that
one cap leaves a product sphere collar between the two spheres,
by smooth Schoenflies. Thus the consecutive pairs are isotopic.
This also verifies the uniqueness used in Gompf's proof of
Theorem 5.3. Ambiently isotope \(T\) to \(S\), carrying the full
moving tube into \(Z\setminus S\).
[Trace, Proposition 4, pp. 176–177](https://msp.org/pjm/1982/99-1/pjm-v99-n1-p13-s.pdf)
· [Gompf, proof of Theorem 5.3, p. 2887](https://msp.org/agt/2017/17-5/agt-v17-n5-p11-s.pdf)

For \(Q\) the unknot, Theorem 5.1 already gives the disk-framed
local unknot in \(Z=Y_2\). Isotope it into a small ball missing
\(S\) and the stationary filling core. For either case, generic
perturbation of the moving core track makes its intersections
with the fixed stationary filling core \(Q^*\) transverse and
finite. The endpoints can be chosen disjoint from \(Q^*\),
and the terminal one also avoids \(S\). Away from a crossing
read the moving tube in the common exterior using the radial
collar of the removed core. At a crossing, the two detours
differ by band sum with the oriented meridian of \(Q^*\);
under reversing the stationary surgery this meridian is the
original framed longitude of \(Q\). A buffer around each
crossing and its band carries the moving normal coordinates.
This is the GST core-crossing argument with the same slope
and local product charts. The stationary framed tube remains
the anchor. Each event is an actual signed \(K\)-over-\(Q\)
slide in the original inlet. The final moving tube is in
the common exterior disjoint from \(S\), since the stationary
solid torus and its collar are disjoint from \(S\).
[GST, Proposition 3.2 proof, pp. 2313–2314](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-p.pdf#page=9)

Now \(Q\) and the resulting \(K'\) miss \(S\) in the original
\(Y_1\). Cut along \(S\), and choose a product arc between the
faces avoiding their cores. The two smooth core projections
to \(S^2\) have empty interior, so the arc can be taken vertical.
Small buffers around the tubes and a regular neighborhood of
the arc can be chosen disjoint. Deleting a product neighborhood
of the arc leaves a three-ball containing both tubes after
rounding, and a slight margin at the spherical ends puts the
tubes in its interior. The local pair has joint outlet \(Y_2\)
by the actual block comparison, because removing the untouched
\(S^1\times S^2\) product summand from the rank-three outlet
leaves \(Y_2\). The zero local matrix follows from the rank of
the local surgery homology.

The ambient track is read on \(Z\) in a terminal collar of the
\(Q\)-trace. The map is \((z,u)\mapsto (J_{\eta(u)}z,u)\),
where \(J_s\) carries the full old moving chart to the terminal
one and the bump \(\eta\) is zero and one near the collar ends.
Its inverse uses \(J_{\eta(u)}^{-1}\) in the same collar.
Both maps are identity through the moving handle coordinates
and below the collar, including the original inlet. With
composite outgoing map \(d\), a later chart \(e\) becomes
\(d e\) with inverse \(e^{-1}d^{-1}\) on its image. Extend through
each later handle to obtain the comparison on the final level.
The actual upper closing becomes \(\gamma d^{-1}\) and has
inverse \(d\gamma^{-1}\). Thus unspecified residuals give
a relative replacement of count change zero.

In the terminal order beginning with one split zero \(A\) on
\(D^4\), the new local ball can be moved to an available ball
in the common exterior of \(A\) by an ambient tube isotopy.
This lets the two local attachments be placed below \(A\),
with the comparison given by commuting disjoint attachments.
The dual of \(A\) now meets the product sphere in its split
outlet once. Re-mark the immediate actual upper rank-three
body by an extending boundary map and cancel this pair;
the two-sided closing and the inverse after cancellation are
those obtained by composing the comparison of the upper
piece and its inverse at the surviving cut. This is the
split-\(A\) argument of the assessed two-point theorem, now
applied to the pair localized by the filled sphere. It gives
\(n_2=n_3:3\to2\) without stabilization. Recognition of the
remaining local pair depends on that pair. On the original
exterior the construction gives neither an original unfilled
sphere nor an embedded bridge for a prescribed four-port
return. It gives a finite filled isotopy followed by framed
slides, with no uniform bound on the number of slides in
this argument.
