## Shape, orientation and the global cap

Write $S>0$ for the flat shape of the height face, with $D_Xn=dF(SX)$ in the height hyperplane. For disk conormal $a e_z+b n$, differentiation and tangential projection give $K=bS$. Derivatives of $a,b$ occur in the normal two-plane and drop out. Regularity means $1-b^2=a^2>0$. In an orthonormal frame diagonalizing the intrinsic Ricci tensor, $G_\kappa=R_h\operatorname{Id}/2-\operatorname{Ric}_h^\sharp-\kappa\operatorname{Id}$ has the three complementary sectional curvatures minus $\kappa$. Since the three-dimensional curvature operator is determined by Ricci, the Gauss equation on its positive branch is exactly
\[
 G_\kappa=(\det A)A^{-1},\qquad A=S_\kappa=\sqrt{\det G_\kappa}\,G_\kappa^{-1}.
\]
If $A=bS+c\operatorname{Id}$, from the $ij$ plane one obtains $(b s_i+c)(b s_j+c)=s_i s_j-\kappa$. Subtracting the equations with $ij$ and $i\ell$ yields $(s_j-s_\ell)((1-b^2)s_i-bc)=0$. At three distinct eigenvalues the equation is impossible. At $(u,u,v)$ with $v\ne u$, it gives $bc=(1-b^2)u$. The repeated eigenvalue of $A$ is then $u/b>0$, so $b>0$, and $\kappa=u^2-(u/b)^2<0$. Thus at every nonumbilic point the one constant curvature must satisfy
\[
 k^2=-\kappa=u^2(1-b^2)/b^2,\qquad
 b=u/\sqrt{u^2+k^2},\quad c=k^2/\sqrt{u^2+k^2}.
\]
This excludes a reversed conormal on a nonumbilic height and all nonnegative space-form curvatures there. Umbilic points have scalar shape and require $u^2>\kappa$ for the positive branch.

On the nonumbilic open set with repeated eigenspace $E$, the $E$ projection of Euclidean Codazzi gives $X(u)Y=Y(u)X$ for $X,Y\in E$, hence $du|_E=0$. The mixed Codazzi terms of $bS+c\operatorname{Id}$ reduce to $(u\,db+dc)(X)Y=0$ for $X$ in the simple eigendirection and $Y\in E$. On the interior of the umbilic set the scalar shape is locally constant by Euclidean Codazzi. The smooth intrinsic formula for $S_{-k^2}$ carries the identity to the boundary of these sets.

With the last scalar line timelike, the displayed connection on $T\Sigma\oplus\mathbb R^2$ preserves the Lorentz metric. The tangent curvature is $R^h$ minus the $S_{-k^2}$ shape product plus the $k^2$ constant-curvature term. Its off-diagonal blocks vanish by Codazzi. This yields the stated flat trivialization on simply connected $\Sigma$. With $F=k^{-1}(0,0,1)$ and $N=(0,1,0)$ expressed in parallel Lorentz coordinates, $dF(X)$ identifies $X$ with a tangent vector on the hyperboloid, and $dN=dF\circ S_{-k^2}$. In the Klein projection $q=F'/F_0$ on the future sheet, $\langle N,F\rangle_L=\langle N,dF\rangle_L=0$ give $N'\cdot q=N_0$ and $N'\cdot dq=0$. The derivative $N'\cdot D_Xdq(X)=-h(S_{-k^2}X,X)/F_0$ has the strict negative sign for the normal $N'/|N'|$. Its Euclidean Gauss map is a covering of $S^3$ and hence a diffeomorphism. For a height in any unit direction, its two critical points correspond to the normals in that direction and its opposite. The former is the unique maximum. The resulting support planes give injectivity and identify the entire boundary of the convex hull with the face. The hull remains compactly inside the Klein ball. Smooth radial interpolation from an interior point gives a standard ball. The chosen angular marking extends over the original and comparison balls by the components theorem, using a boundary isotopy to an orthogonal representative in a collar. The collar-wise identification uses the original exterior identity and the paired signed normal coordinates. ## Cap form, widths and the finite charge test

For $p=\mu^2 g_{\mathrm H}$ with $\mu=1$ and $\partial_{\nu_{\mathrm H}}\log\mu=-c$ on the boundary, the cap shape is $S_{-k^2}-c\operatorname{Id}$, so the original and cap metrics have matching first jets in paired Gaussian coordinates. In the Poincaré model write $p=U_c^2g_{\mathrm E}$, $U_c=\mu\Omega$. The dimension-four scalar transformation and a direct divergence computation give the cap form
\[
 Q_p(f)=6\int_C |d(U_cf)|^2\,dV_{\mathrm E}
        -6\int_\Sigma U_c\,\partial_{\nu_{\mathrm E}}U_c\, f^2\,dA_{\mathrm E}.
\]
The negative boundary sign follows from subtracting $6\operatorname{div}(U_c f^2\nabla U_c)$ from the expanded Dirichlet integrand. Since $\nu_{\mathrm E}=\Omega\nu_{\mathrm H}$ and $dA_{\mathrm E}=\Omega^{-3}dA_h$, its boundary coefficient relative to $dA_h$ is $-6\partial_{\nu_{\mathrm H}}\log U_c=6(c-\ell)$, where $\ell=\partial_{\nu_{\mathrm H}}\log\Omega$. Equivalently $\operatorname{tr}S_{-k^2}=\Omega^{-1}H_{\mathrm E}+3\ell$ and the coefficient is $2(\Omega^{-1}H_{\mathrm E}-H)$. With $R_g>0$ on the compact exterior, its form controls the full exterior $H^1$ norm and the $L^2$ trace. For $c\ge\ell$, the cap Dirichlet term and the common trace control the full cap $H^1$ norm by the inequality with boundary term. More generally the required test is coercivity of the summed form in a fixed equivalent $H^1$ norm.

The piecewise matched metric is $C^1$ across the original face, with bounded one-sided second derivatives and no scalar measure supported on the seam. Fix one physical source and work inside the minimum of its supplied inward width and both smooth Gaussian radii. The metric discrepancy across the inward strip is $O_i(t^2)$, with derivative $O_i(|t|)$ and bounded second derivative, uniformly in the angular variables on that face. Differentiating the cutoff at scale $w$ therefore gives a uniform fixed-index second-derivative bound. The cap equals the original smooth metric on an inward collar segment abutting the seam and equals the conformally flat comparison deeper into the disk. The joined metric is smooth, the complete exterior is unchanged, and the only disk Weyl cost is $C_w^\sigma\le L_i w$. The principal coefficient error is $O_i(w^2)$. The bounded scalar densities on a strip of volume $O_i(w)$ give a form error $O_i(w^{1/2})\|f\|_{H^1}^2$ by Hölder and $H^1\hookrightarrow L^4$. A positive width small enough to absorb both errors in the fixed coercivity gap gives $Y([p_w])>0$ by the closed Sobolev inequality.

With $e=E_+(g;M)=E_-(g;M)$ and $m_D^\sigma=E_\sigma(g;D)$, the energy in either sign is $e-m_D^\sigma+C_w^\sigma$. Closed signature first gives $C_w^+-C_w^-=m_D^+-m_D^-$. With positivity and $d_i=e-I^+(M)$, the endpoint gives $m_D^\sigma\le d_i$. For a strict reverse inequality, choose $w$ additionally below $(m_D^\sigma-d_i)/L_i$ for its nonzero cost bound. This is a finite positive-width contradiction on the same closed type, retaining the whole exterior. No index-uniform width or affine path follows from these endpoint hypotheses. ## Ellipsoid profile and whole positive path

For $z_0=\tau C>0$ on the ellipsoid, the ambient normal of the four-dimensional hypersurface is proportional to $p_0+d e_z$, with $p_0=(x/A^2,y/B^2)$ and $d=z_0/C^2$. The disk-to-exterior conormal in its tangent space points toward decreasing $z$ and is proportional to $-|p_0|e_z+d n$. Its $b$ is positive. The repeated curvature in the orbit directions is $u=1/(A^2|p_0|)$, which gives constant $k=1/(A^2d)=C/(\tau A^2)$. On the meridian with speed $L=(A_f^2\cos^2\theta+B_f^2\sin^2\theta)^{1/2}$ one gets
\[
 u=B_f/(A_fL),\qquad v=A_fB_f/L^3,\qquad v/u=A_f^2/L^2.
\]
Thus all interior meridian points are nonumbilic if $A_f\ne B_f$, with smooth coincidence at the poles.

The hyperboloid profile in the source induces the axial metric $d\rho^2+\cosh^2(k\rho)\,dZ^2+k^{-2}\sinh^2(k\rho)\gamma_{S^2}$. In its meridian, $\rho'=r'/\sqrt{1+k^2r^2}$ and $\cosh(k\rho)Z'=\sqrt{1-\rho'^2}>0$ off the poles, from $r'^2+y'^2=1$ and $y'=ru$. Its outward normal has positive $\partial_\rho$ component where $r>0$, so the repeated curvature is $\sqrt{1-\rho'^2}\,k\coth(k\rho)=\sqrt{u^2+k^2}$. Gauss gives the remaining $(uv+k^2)/\sqrt{u^2+k^2}$. At the two poles $dZ/d\theta=\sin\theta$ times a smooth positive function of $\cos^2\theta$. This verifies the nonsingular disk marking by $(x,y)\mapsto(x,\mathcal Z(y/B_f))$ in the smooth axis coordinates. At $B=A$, put $R=A_f$ and $t=\sqrt{1+k^2R^2}$. In centered Poincaré coordinates $\Omega=1+k F_0$. The outward radial derivative yields $\ell=k^2R/(1+t)$, whereas $c=k^2R/t$. Their difference $k^2R/[t(1+t)]$ is positive. The explicit embeddings vary smoothly on the compact face, so it persists for small $|B/A-1|$ at fixed $A,C,\tau$.

For the charge, at $y=0$ and $x\ne0$ with $0<z<C$, the shape of the ambient ellipsoid is its diagonal defining Hessian restricted to the tangent space and divided by $N_0$. The two orbit curvatures coincide at $p=A^{-2}/N_0$, the $y$ curvature is $q=B^{-2}/N_0$, and the meridional curvature $r_1$ is a strict weighted average of $A^{-2}/N_0$ and $C^{-2}/N_0$. With principal curvatures $(p,p,q,r_1)$ the Weyl curvature operator is diagonal in principal 2-planes. Subtracting the Ricci terms and adding $R/6$ gives $W_{1212}=(p-q)(p-r_1)/3=W_{3434}$. Its eigenvalue on each $\frac{1}{\sqrt2}(e^1\wedge e^2\pm e^3\wedge e^4)$ is this same nonzero number when both $B$ and $C$ differ from $A$. Each complete side contains an open nonzero-curvature neighborhood of such a point. It follows that both chiral charges are positive on each side.
