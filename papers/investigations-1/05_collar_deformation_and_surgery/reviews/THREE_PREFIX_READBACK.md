## Rank-one second prefix

In the normalized row \((1,0,0)\), the full ordinary surgery matrix has rank two over \(\mathbb Q\), since the actual outgoing level bounds the upper rank-two 1-handlebody and has free first homology of rank two. The leading block of the dotted-unknot replacement and winding-one component is \(B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)\). Its integral inverse has lower-right entry zero, and each column coupling it to the other components has first entry zero. Integer block elimination gives \(B\) plus the unchanged lower-right block, which must therefore vanish. Restricting the same elimination to \(a,b\) gives integral first homology \(\mathbb Z\) on \(Y_{ab}\). Restriction to the dotted-unknot replacement and \(b,c\) gives the zero matrix and first homology \(\mathbb Z^3\) on \(Y_{bc}\).

The actual \(W_{ab}\) is simply connected since the winding-one relation kills the lower cyclic generator. The differential from its two 2-handles to its one 1-handle is \((1,0)\), so \(H_2(W_{ab};\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\). Interior representatives of its classes have intersection product zero in \(M\), giving zero pairing on \(W_{ab}\). Under duality the pair sequence makes \(H_2(Y_{ab})\to H_2(W_{ab})\) an integral isomorphism, since the preceding group is \(H^1(W_{ab})=0\) and the following map is the zero intersection map. CMM Proposition 2.2 gives one \(S^1\times S^2\) factor and a sum of spherical factors with trivial first homology. Each spherical factor admits its quotient round PSC metric, so Corollary 2.3 applies factorwise to identify the nontrivial factors as signed Poincaré spheres. The product sphere generates \(H_2(W_{ab})\). Attaching an auxiliary 3-handle along its pullback has a unit relative boundary map, preserves simple connectivity, and leaves a smooth contractible filling of the residual homology sphere. CMM Proposition 4.2 then removes all the signed factors. This proves \(Y_{ab}\cong S^1\times S^2\). The product metric and inverse marking give the converse degree-one test. The argument applies to the second prefix with the two tubes in either order. [CMM v1, Proposition 2.2 and Corollary 2.3, pp. 4–5, and Proposition 4.2, p. 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1)

The surviving \(c\) tube is in \(Y_{ab}\) and its actual surgery gives \(\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\). An identification of \(Y_{ab}\) with zero surgery on an unknot puts \(c\), after a small isotopy, in the complement of the surgery core. Shrinking the surgery solid torus to a neighborhood of that core identifies this complement with the original unknot exterior, including the transported framing. Proposition 3.2 applies to the resulting two-component link in \(S^3\). The proof explicitly isotopes the second knot into a ball as an unknot **inside** \(S^1\times S^2\) before translating that isotopy to slides. Its framing in the ball is zero, since the surgery increases the first-homology rank. A framed version follows by transporting the normal trivialization throughout the isotopy and choosing the disk-framed tubular parametrization in its homotopy class. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, Proposition 3.2 and proof, pp. 2313–2314](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf#page=9)

For the marked upper cancellation, let \(i_c:S^1\times D^2\to Y_{ab}\) and \(o_c:D^2\times S^1\to Y_{abc}\) be the actual two faces. The dual incoming embedding in \(\partial Q\) is \(c^*(v,z)=\gamma o_c(z,v)\). The local surgery identifies the new rank-one summand with the double of the two meridional disks times the longitude corresponding to the outgoing core. This gives a model \(Q_0\) in which that core has one transverse intersection with the new 1-handle's belt sphere and misses the other belt sphere. Choose the outgoing marking \(\lambda:Y_{abc}\to\partial Q_0\) with the same orientation behavior as \(\gamma\). Laudenbach–Poénaru extends \(\lambda\gamma^{-1}\) over the upper body. In the model, place the distinguished 1-handle last before the dual handle \(c^*\). Milnor's product identification then cancels that pair. The fixed incoming product coordinates give the comparison \(\Xi:Q\cup c^*\to Q_1\), with inverse from reverse flow and the inverse boundary-body extension. [Laudenbach–Poénaru, proof of Theorem A, pp. 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf#page=6) · [Milnor, Theorem 5.4, p. 48](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/surgery/hcobord.pdf#page=53)

The reverse-surgery boundary map \(\delta:Y_{ab}\to\partial(Q\cup c^*)\) is \(\gamma\) on the common survivor exterior after the initial complement identification. On the replaced face \(i_c(z,v)\) it is the dual outgoing face at \((v,z)\). The two descriptions agree on the torus by the original handle corner coordinates. Its inverse uses the opposite survivor identification and the inverse swap. The closed comparison is identity on \(W_{ab}\) and \(\Xi\) on its upper complement, so the returned gluing and inverse are \(\partial\Xi\,\delta\) and \(\delta^{-1}(\partial\Xi)^{-1}\). This preserves the original lower attachments \(a,b\) and removes one forward 2-handle and one 3-handle. The result is the mixed count \((1,1,2,1,1)\) on the same smooth type. Recognition via the existing mixed theorem still needs one of its stated conditions on that remaining pair. In particular a specified cancellation retaining this whole lower prefix has the converse boundary requirement \(\partial W_{ab}\cong\partial Q_1\). ## Zero-winding second prefix

When \(Y_{bc}\) is PSC-dominated, its rank-three free first homology gives three \(S^1\times S^2\) factors and spherical factors of trivial first homology. The ordinary zero-matrix comparison trace of the three-component link is simply connected and its intersection form is zero on \(\mathbb Z^3\). The pair sequence identifies the boundary's second homology with that of the trace over the integers. Its three disjoint product spheres can therefore be compressed by auxiliary 3-handles. Their relative differential is unimodular, so the compressed trace is simply connected and acyclic, and hence contractible. CMM leaves only \(\#_3(S^1\times S^2)\) for the original boundary. A PSC metric on this connected sum supplies the converse. [CMM v1, Proposition 2.2, Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1)

For the actual remaining forward tube \(a\) let \(G=H_1(Y_{bc}\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu a;\mathbb Z)\). If \(m\) is its meridian and \(\ell\) is the specified reverse filling slope, the two fillings yield \(G/\langle m\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^3\) and \(G/\langle\ell\rangle\cong\mathbb Z^2\). Each filling drops rank by zero or one. Thus \(G\) has rank three, \(m\) is torsion, and \(\ell\) has infinite order. Quotient by \(\langle\ell\rangle\) injects the torsion, so \(G\) is free and \(m=0\). The inverse surgery core is represented by \(\pm m\), since the basis \((m,\ell)\) of the torus has intersection \(\pm1\). It is null homologous in the full upper boundary, ruling out a single transverse intersection with any upper belt sphere. The forward core represents \(\pm\ell\) and is primitive because the quotient by \(\ell\) is torsion-free. These calculations leave open a relative identification of the actual lower trace with its actual remaining tube. The prescribed relative identification would suffice for lower cancellation. The auxiliary 3-handles cancel classes only in the comparison filling, and do not give that identification.

## Both framed spectators and the Mazur stabilization

In the displayed control, the radial lower comparison is identity on the cancelling 2-handle coordinates, and pulls the two specified spectator tubes back from the untouched patch of \(S^3\). They are zero-framed there by the radial push-offs in their spanning disks. After surgery on \(b\), its zero-framing push-off can be joined by a short exterior annulus to a meridional disk in the outgoing face. Shrinking the band detour across this disk and its parallel disks yields the framed isotopy \(J_t\) from \(c\) to \(c'\). The interpolated collar map \(J_{\eta(t)}^{-1}\) therefore sends the slid attaching map back to the split attaching map and glues to the identity on the last handle coordinates. Its inverse uses \(J_{\eta(t)}\). On the outgoing face of \(c'\) the then-transported upper map agrees exactly with the original marking of the \(c\) face. On the surviving \(b\) face it uses \(J_1^{-1}\), so it is essential to keep the moved marking there. The split \(\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\) model has each dual attaching circle intersecting the belt sphere of its own upper 1-handle once. Under upper cancellation the two surviving forward tubes are \(a,b\). Under lower cancellation the two surviving forward tubes are the literal \(e_b,e_{c'}\) in the ball coordinates. The latter slide back to the split zero-framed unlink, with the actual closure transported by \(\gamma'\partial\Psi\) and inverse \((\partial\Psi)^{-1}(\gamma')^{-1}\). Laudenbach–Poénaru applies to this gluing and identifies the standard closed sphere. Each route gives one net reduction. The two-zero prefix in this example has the additional relative equivalence from undoing the explicit slide in the prior untouched patch.
