For every $u\in F_2=\langle x,y\rangle$ and $n\geq2$, the subgroup
\[
H_u=\langle r, u s_n u^{-1}\rangle
\]
contains no primitive element of $F_2$. This covers the neighborhood of the two original circles and any one embedded joining tree meeting each circle once. Every closed curve in that fixed neighborhood represents a conjugacy class from $H_u$ for the corresponding $u$.

## Exact basings and the regular neighborhood

Choose the common basepoint away from the two circles, and one contact point on each circle. Orient the cores to agree with the specified words. Start with paths from the basepoint to the contact points. Changing either path by prepending an ambient based loop conjugates the associated core word, so paths realizing the exact words exist, and prepending a representative of $u$ to the second path realizes any required relative conjugator. Represent the two resulting path classes by smooth paths, with distinct small radial germs at the basepoint and with germs normal to the cores at the contact points. Perturb their interiors relative to those germs. General position in dimension three gives embedded arcs with disjoint interiors, avoiding both core circles in their interiors. For paths of dimension one the potential double-point conditions for two moving parameters, or for one moving parameter and a parameter on a fixed core, have codimension three. The perturbation can therefore preserve the prescribed path classes and the endpoint germs. The union of the arcs is an embedded joining tree whose only intersections with the cores are the contact points. Corners may be smoothed within small disjoint coordinate neighborhoods.

Let $N$ be a smooth regular neighborhood of this tree and the two circles. It retracts to that graph and is a genus-two handlebody, formed by thickening the tree and then adding one 1-handle for each core circle. A basis for its fundamental group is obtained by following each rooted tree path to a core, going around the core, and returning along the tree. Thus its image in the marked $F_2$ is $\langle r,s_n\rangle$, the subgroup for the identity conjugator. More generally, if a single embedded tree meets each original core in exactly one point, first transport its root into the given marking by a path. The two resulting generators have the form $a r^{\pm1}a^{-1}$ and $b s_n^{\pm1}b^{-1}$. Conjugating both by $a^{-1}$ gives the subgroup $H_{a^{-1}b}$, since inversions of the generators preserve their subgroup. This also accounts for a change of basepoint or marked contact point on a core.

## Folded graph for the two prescribed basings

For $u=1$, start with the two oriented cycles at a root $O$, then identify the initial $y$ of $r$ with that of $s_n^{-1}$ and the initial $x$ of $s_n$ with that of $r^{-1}$. The result has paths $O\to A$ labelled $y$, $O\to B$ labelled $x$, $A\to B$ labelled $xyXY$, and $B\to A$ labelled $x^nY^{n-1}$. In the resulting theta graph, $O$ is of degree two. The outgoing labels at $A$ are $Y,x,y$ and at $B$ are $X,y,x$. All interior path labels are reduced. Hence the edge-labelled map to the two-petal rose is locally injective. The two cycles based at $O$ read $r,s_n$ and generate the graph group. Any cyclically reduced path using an edge of the $xyXY$ arc must follow that whole arc in one direction, because the internal vertices have degree two. Avoiding that arc leaves the $s_n$ cycle.

## Folded graph for arbitrary relative basing

In the Cayley tree for $x,y$, let $L_r,L_h$ be the translation axes of $r$ and $h=u s_n u^{-1}$, acting on the left. The edge labels on each axis repeat a cyclic rotation of its defining word, or of the inverse word in the other direction. The graph immersions below preserve reduced paths, including the cyclic junction of a cyclically reduced closed path, by local injectivity on outgoing edges. [Stallings, *Topology of finite graphs*, Invent. Math. 71 (1983), §§5.1(a), 5.3–5.4, pp. 556–557](https://www2.math.ou.edu/~nbrady/teaching/s11-5863/stallings.pdf#page=6)

**Disjoint axes.** Take nearest vertices $p\in L_r$ and $q\in L_h$. Make a circle for one period of each axis, with marked points at the period starts $p,q$, and join them by the bridge with label $p^{-1}q$. Its first edge in either direction is off the axis there, so the outgoing labels at each end of the bridge are distinct. This labelled barbell is immersed. The loops based at $p$ give $p^{-1}rp$ and $(p^{-1}q)(q^{-1}hq)(q^{-1}p)=p^{-1}hp$. These generate the full graph group image $p^{-1}H_u p$. Any cyclically reduced closed path that crosses an edge of the $r$ cycle must traverse the whole cycle in one direction. A cyclically reduced path avoiding the $r$ cycle stays in the other cycle, as the remaining bridge lies in a tree attached there.

**Intersecting axes.** The common segment has length at most two. Indeed, in either direction on the $h$ axis the period uses only $\{x,Y\}$ or only $\{X,y\}$. In the cyclic $r$ word, the $x,Y$ occurrences are isolated and the $X,y$ runs have length at most two. Reversal and inversion preserve the bound over those two alphabets. Since the axes are subtrees, the intersection is either one such segment or a vertex. In the vertex case the four outgoing labels are distinct, giving an immersed wedge of the two period loops, with image $p^{-1}H_u p$ based at the common vertex $p$.

For a nonempty common edge segment $[p,q]$, orient both period loops so that their first edge at $p$ runs toward $q$, inverting their generators as needed. The common segment is a proper initial segment of each period, since the periods have lengths six and $2n+1\geq5$. Identify these two initial segments. The result is a theta graph with a common $p$-to-$q$ arc and two complementary return arcs. At $q$ the outgoing tails of the two axes differ by maximality of the common segment, and both differ from the reverse direction on that segment. At $p$ the two outgoing directions on the inverted return paths carry the labels of the backward directions on the respective axes at $p$. This follows by translating each period endpoint back to $p$ by the corresponding group element. The backward directions differ by maximality, and both differ from the common forward label. Thus the quotient graph is already immersed, including the periodic joins. Its period loops still generate the graph group, with image $p^{-1}H_u p$.

In the intersecting cases, the $r$ arc outside the common segment has at least four consecutive letters of one cyclic period of $r$. Each cyclic four-letter block of $yxyXYX$ contains one generator with both signs. This property is preserved by cyclic rotation and inversion. Any reduced cyclic path using the arc must traverse the whole arc in one direction. A path avoiding it is carried by the remaining $s_n$ cycle.

Every element of $H_u$, up to ambient conjugation, is therefore read on a cyclically reduced path whose cyclic word either contains both signs of one generator or is conjugate to a power of $s_n$. The Christoffel classification gives a unique primitive conjugacy class at each primitive abelianization vector in $F_2$, whose cyclic word uses a single sign of each generator. At $(n+1,-n)$ the primitive representative is $x(xY)^n$, which is primitive by taking the free basis $x,xY$ and multiplying the first entry by the $n$th power of the second. For $n\geq2$, its cyclic word has isolated $Y$'s, while $s_n$ contains adjacent $Y$'s. These cyclic words are therefore in different conjugacy classes. Powers with exponent of absolute value at least two have nonprimitive abelianization, and the zero power is trivial. This proves the subgroup obstruction. At $n=1$, $s_1=x^2Y=x(xY)$ is itself primitive, so this parameter boundary is necessary. [Kassel–Reutenauer, arXiv:math/0507219v1, §3 and Corollary 3.3(b), pp. 14–17](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0507219#page=17)

## Consequence for moves in a fixed neighborhood

Any finite sequence of framed slides for which the evolving cores, parallel copies and bands remain in $N$ has its resulting core circles in $N$. The same conclusion holds for interchanging or reorienting the initial cores, or for changing which component is slid, provided the resulting curves remain in $N$. Every resulting core is therefore nonprimitive in the ambient free group. A single transverse meeting with a belt sphere in a complete two-belt system gives a single occurrence of the associated letter in a free basis, so that core would be primitive. Changes of upper basis preserve primitivity and cannot create such a crossing in this class. This conclusion applies separately to every regular neighborhood formed using one joining tree. A sequence allowed to leave its chosen neighborhood or to use the union of several neighborhoods requires a further argument, as do sequences using additional handle pairs. No marked diagram, framing or closing identification is inferred from the subgroup calculation.
