In the Bass–Serre tree for a free product of two finite cyclic groups, use edges indexed by group elements and vertices by cosets of the two factors. The turning syllable of an oriented two-edge passage is the first edge index inverted times the second edge index. It lies in the factor of the middle vertex and is invariant under left translation. Orientation reversal inverts syllables and reverses their order. A cyclically reduced alternating word of syllable length $L\ge2$ acts with translation length $L$, and the axis reads cyclic repeats of those syllables. Stabilizers of edges are trivial. When two positive conjugated axes have a common oriented edge at the same phase of a chosen period, their translates coincide. Indeed, translate the reference edge internally by a power of the period so that its indices align, and use the trivial stabilizer to identify the two translating elements. If two positive conjugates orient the same axis in the same direction, their translations agree on every edge and the elements coincide.

For two length-$L$ translations with disjoint axes separated by distance $D$, their product has length $2L+2D$. The concatenation along one period, the bridge, the other period and the returning bridge has no backtracking, including the periodic joins. Both periods occur as subsegments on the product axis. If the axes touch in one vertex or their directions agree on the overlap, the length is $2L$, and the axis contains one full period of each by concatenating the two periods starting from a common vertex and its translate. For opposed translation directions on an overlap of length $D$ with $0<D<L$, the length is $2L-2D$. If the first translation runs from $P$ toward $Q$ along the common segment and the second from $Q$ toward $P$, trim the $P$-to-$Q$ overlap from the start of the first period and the translated overlap from the end of the second. ## Extension to $L_{n,1}$, $n\ge4$

Use the same marked words, with $s_n=x^{n+1}Y^n$ and $S_n=(n+1,-n)$. In $C_2*C_3$ the positive period has $L=4n+2$ and its $C_3$ signs have one run of $n+1$ plus signs and one run of $n$ minus signs. An agreeing block at nonzero phase has no sign change and has at most $n$ turns. Thus positively aligned distinct axes overlap in at most $2n+2$ edges. An opposed signed product for the row $R$ has length at least $2L-2(2n+2)=4n$ unless the axes coincide and the factors cancel. Positively opposed axes cannot coincide because of the sign counts. This excludes the primitive quotient image of length four. The four other primitive lengths are $2L-4m$, for $m=1,-1,3,-3$. For $m<0$ disjoint axes leave the full period; for $m>0$ opposing translations must overlap in $2m$ edges. The residual arc always has at least 12 edges and hence at least five consecutive $C_3$ turns. A five-turn window has at most one change of sign, since two changes require traversing an interior run of length at least $n$ with the two opposite signs on its flanks. So a triple remains. The primitive candidates have $a-b=2$ and $b=2n-m>2$, so their words have only blocks $xY$ and $x^2Y$, with no triple in the quotient.

With $r$ initially moving, the quotient is $C_{n+1}*C_n$. The same turn matches bound the two types of overlap by two and three because $x\ne X$ and $y\ne Y$. The primitive targets have lengths $2n$ for $S_n$ and $2|mn-2|$ for $2R+mS_n$. To see that these are the actual cyclic lengths, the primitive block ratio $a/b$ is at most two, as follows from $a-b=|m|$, $b=|mn-2|$ in the latter case. Thus $x^j$ or $X^j$ for $j=1,2$ and the required signed single $y$ all survive as alternating syllables. Each target length at least 12 leaves a full six-edge period arc, including at length 12 where the axes meet in a vertex or translate the same way on their overlap. Three interior turns from one factor have both signs and exclude every candidate. At $n\ge9$ all lengths exceed 12. At $4\le n\le8$, the only targets below 12 are $S_n$ for $n=4,5$, and $2R+S_n$ for $n=4,5,6,7$. In the first case an opposed overlap is at most two, and in the second at most three. Therefore any surviving residual period of the positive $r$ contains at least two consecutive interior turns. For all these targets the only allowed turns occurring in the positive $r$ period are $x$ and $Y$, three positions apart. Hence a forbidden turn always survives. The quotient proof extends to every $n\ge3$, with the six-edge overlap phase check above needed for the specified $n=3$, $s$-first row.
