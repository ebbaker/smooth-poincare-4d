## The actual intermediate boundary and its marking

Let $W$ be the actual lower $0/1/2$ piece with one handle of each index. The chosen 2-handle attaches first with signed winding $\pm1$ around the lower 1-handle. The handle complex has $\partial_2=(\pm1)$, and the attaching relation kills the generator of $\pi_1(W)$. The simply connected acyclic handle complex is contractible by Whitehead's theorem. Thus $Y=\partial W$, with its boundary orientation, is a closed connected integral homology sphere by Lefschetz duality. If the larger diagram has additional lower 1-handles, this argument requires an isolated piece $W$ with the stated handles and its own actual boundary $Y$.

Suppose a smooth closed oriented 3-manifold $Z$ has positive scalar curvature and a continuous map of nonzero degree to $Y$. If $Z$ is disconnected, use a component with nonzero degree. Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee's Proposition 2.2 supplies the mapping step and implies that $Y$ admits a positive-scalar-curvature metric. Corollary 2.3 now gives $S^3$ or a connected sum of oriented Poincaré homology spheres and their reversals. Proposition 4.2 rules out every nonempty such sum because the filling $W$ is smooth and contractible. Its smooth conclusion concerns $Y\cong S^3$. The authors use the same subsequent handle argument in the proof of Theorem 1.1. [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, arXiv:2407.05574v1, 8 July 2024, pp. 4–5 and 10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1)

Reverse the chosen 2-handle in its actual trace. The attaching core of the reversed handle is the outgoing belt circle of the forward handle. Its surgery in $Y\cong S^3$ gives the incoming $S^1\times S^2$. The surgery coefficient is zero in the Seifert longitude convention because the resulting first homology is infinite cyclic. Gabai's Property R identifies this reverse knot as the unknot. The original attaching circle is the filling core of this zero surgery. Scharlemann's reverse formulation gives its isotopy in the actual $S^1\times S^2$ to the product core, which meets the belt sphere of the lower 1-handle transversely once. ## The compact band test

Let $X=Y\times[-1,1]$ be a smooth compact marked band with a smooth metric $g$ up to both boundaries, width $d=\operatorname{dist}_{(X,g)}(\partial_-X,\partial_+X)$, and $r=\min_X R_g>0$. If $Y$ has no positive-scalar-curvature metric, no closed separating hypersurface in $X$ can carry one. Take the union $\Sigma$ of those interior components that bound the chamber containing the lower end, oriented as its outgoing boundary. The fundamental chains give $[\Sigma]=[Y\times\{-1\}]$ in $H_3(X;\mathbb Z)$ when increasing $t$ induces the orientation of $Y$. The sum of the projection degrees is one, so some oriented component gives the required nonzero-degree map. Räde's Lemma 6.3 gives the same conclusion for any connected oriented compact band with a map to $Y\times[-1,1]$ carrying the two ends to their corresponding ends and having nonzero relative degree.

Räde's Theorem 2.8 applies with the no-positive-separator hypothesis and $n=4$. Corollary 2.25 states the product specialization directly. The scaled metric $(r/12)g$ has scalar curvature at least $12$ and must have width $\sqrt{r/12}\,d<\pi/2$. Hence $rd^2\ge 3\pi^2$ forces the positive face conclusion, including equality. Compactness and smooth boundary are part of the hypotheses. Width uses intrinsic lengths, and the lower bound on scalar curvature holds on the whole band. A certified lower bound for each quantity can be used. [Räde, arXiv:2104.10120v4, 23 May 2022, Definition 1.1, Theorem 2.8, Corollary 2.25 and Lemma 6.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.10120v4)

## Direct calculation on a level

In a smooth orthogonal collar write $g=f^2dt^2+h(t)$ with $f>0$, $\nu=f^{-1}\partial_t$, $K=\tfrac{1}{2f}\partial_t h$, $H=\operatorname{tr}_h K$ and $\Delta_h=\operatorname{div}_h\nabla_h$. The curvature convention gives positive scalar curvature on a round sphere. The traced Gauss equation and the normal variation of mean curvature are

$$
R_h=R_g-2\operatorname{Ric}_g(\nu,\nu)+H^2-|K|_h^2,
\qquad
\partial_t H=-\Delta_h f-f\bigl(\operatorname{Ric}_g(\nu,\nu)+|K|_h^2\bigr).
$$

Eliminating $\operatorname{Ric}_g(\nu,\nu)$ gives $R_h=R_g+H^2+|K|_h^2+2\partial_t H/f+2\Delta_h f/f$. In dimension three take $u=f^{1/4}$ and $L_h=-8\Delta_h+R_h$. Since $\Delta_hu/u=\tfrac14\Delta_h\log f+\tfrac1{16}|\nabla_h\log f|^2$ and $\Delta_hf/f=\Delta_h\log f+|\nabla_h\log f|^2$, one gets

$$
\frac{L_hu}{u}=R_g+H^2+|K|_h^2+\frac{2}{f}\partial_t H+\frac32|\nabla_h\log f|^2.
$$

The expression divided by $f$ is $R_{u^4h}=R_{fh}$. Strict positivity on the entire actual level is therefore sufficient. If the expression is nonnegative everywhere and positive somewhere, pairing $L_hu$ with the positive first eigenfunction gives $\lambda_1(L_h)>0$ on the connected closed face. That eigenfunction supplies a positive-scalar-curvature metric in the same face conformal class.

The topological implication accepts any genuine positive-scalar-curvature metric on a closed source with the required degree to the actual $Y$. Existence of a freely chosen product-band metric passing the width test is equivalent to positive scalar curvature on $Y$, since a positive-scalar-curvature product can be lengthened to meet the bound. The physical test needs curvature and width bounds for the supplied band. An application to the energy comparison additionally needs an ambient extension that matches the exterior collar jets in the given marking, with Weyl costs measured on both complete sides. Existence of a favorable physical metric and selection of a qualifying cut remain open in the general case.
