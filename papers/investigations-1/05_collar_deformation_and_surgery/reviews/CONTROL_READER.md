## Occupied bigon correction

The spectator circle in the local example must be specified in the **transverse plane $x=x_*$**. The phrase “that vertical plane” after the bigon plane $y=0$ is ambiguous. The claimed single intersection is checked by

$$
 (x,y,z)=(x_*,R\cos\theta,c+R\sin\theta),\qquad x_*=-a/2,\qquad c-R<0<c+R<f(x_*).
$$

The circle meets $y=0$ at heights $c\pm R$. Exactly the upper point is on $\operatorname{int}\Delta$. Its two crossings of $z=0$ are transverse because $|c|<R$. ## Peripheral implication

For the chosen winding-one $K\subset S^1\times S^2$, fill each spectator torus along its attaching meridian to recover $X$, the exterior of $K$ alone. This preserves the chosen meridian $\mu$ and full surgery longitude on $\partial X$ up to the usual conjugacy and orientation choices. Writing $G=\pi_1(X)$, van Kampen gives $G/\langle\!\langle\mu\rangle\!\rangle=\mathbb Z$. Thus $\mu=1$ implies $G=\mathbb Z$, and a surjection $\mathbb Z\to\mathbb Z$ is an isomorphism, giving the converse. The framed filling of cyclic $G$ is cyclic. The ordinary surgery matrix $\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&d\end{smallmatrix}\right)$ has determinant $-1$ for every integer framing $d$, so the filled abelianization is zero. The filling is simply connected and gives $S^3$ by the three-dimensional Poincaré theorem.
