## The filled block and the actual ports

Let \(A\) be the annulus obtained by drilling the sphere at its two intersections with the moving knot \(K\). The opposite signs orient the two circles of \(\partial A\) oppositely in the common meridian direction \(f\). On a torus collar of \(K\), use \(f\) as the circle fiber. A bicollar of \(A\) attaches along two annuli on the inner collar torus. On the base this attaches an oriented band along two subintervals of the same inner boundary, splitting it in two. The resulting block is \(S^1_f\times P\), with \(P\) a pair of pants. Each exposed torus comprises a displaced copy of the punctured face and the lateral annulus of the return with both ends on that face. These are the actual two ports of the drilled slab, up to rescaling collars. The trivial arc exterior in each cap is \(S^1\times D^2\), with the gluing annulus \(S^1\) times an interval in the boundary of the disk. The cap therefore only extends the face collar, preserving the return meridian and the inclusion of the lateral annulus. After stationary filling the drilled slab is the connected manifold \(X\).

Choose section boundary classes \(b_K,b_J,b_V\) in the base boundary orientations, with \(b_K=\lambda_K\) the preferred longitude of the null-homologous moving knot in its one-component exterior. Section changes realize any three boundary degrees summing to zero, so this choice is possible. In an integral moving filling the slope is \(b_K+s f\), normalized by orienting it along \(K\). The map from the filling boundary can be taken as \((\phi,t)\mapsto(\phi+st,t)\), with the disk boundary orientation reversed by the attaching map. This extends the circle fibration over a disk on the base. The resulting block over an annulus is a product. Equivalently, in the filling disk a chord between the two base points of \(\partial A\) gives a product annulus that closes \(A\) to a torus. Each half of the filling disk extends the lateral collar of the corresponding return. Cutting the torus thus recovers \(X\) with the same two inclusions. If \(g_i\) identifies an exposed block boundary with its actual port in \(X\) and \(u\) transports across the product, the map and inverse are \(g_Vu g_J^{-1}\) and \(g_Ju^{-1}g_V^{-1}\).

The identity \(b_K+b_J+b_V=0\) gives \(f_J\mapsto f_V\) and \(b_J\mapsto-b_V+s f_V\). In the capped preferred bases \(b_i=-\lambda_i+e_i\mu_i\), since the inner torus boundary of the original base collar traverses the return opposite to the oriented knot. Putting \(c=\operatorname{lk}(J,V)\), the two preferred longitudes each have image \(cf\) in the one-component exterior of \(K\). There \(b_K=0\) in homology and \(f\) has infinite order, so \(e_J+e_V=2c\). Consequently the preferred-basis port map is
\[
 \mu_J\longmapsto\mu_V,\qquad
 \lambda_J\longmapsto-\lambda_V+(2c-s)\mu_V.
\]
Its determinant is \(-1\) in the boundary bases. The inverse exchanges the labels in the same formula. The full hypotheses below force \(s=c=0\).

If both port inclusions into \(X\) are injective, van Kampen gives the HNN presentation with base \(\pi_1(X)\) and the isomorphism between the two peripheral subgroups induced by this product. The base injects, so the outlet group would contain \(\mathbb Z^2\), impossible in a free group. On a port with nontrivial kernel, the Loop Theorem gives a proper compression there. One may delete the other boundary component when applying the theorem to keep its conclusion on the specified port. The argument uses no irreducibility assumption. [Higman–Neumann–Neumann, Theorem I and the presentation on pp. 248–249](https://londmathsoc.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/pdf/10.1112/jlms/s1-24.4.247) · [Hatcher, Theorem 3.1 and Corollary 3.2](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/3M/3M.pdf#page=46)

## The primitive slope and the marked disk

The two cores are null-homologous in \(Y_1\). Excision identifies the map from \(H_2(Y_1;\mathbb Z)\) to the relative meridian-disk group with signed intersections with the cores, so it vanishes. The joint unfilled exterior has \(H_1\cong\mathbb Z^3\) with independent meridians; filling adds the two actual slope relations. The outlet rank three forces both relations to vanish. In the one-component exteriors this gives the zero framings, and in the joint exterior the remaining coefficient gives \(\operatorname{lk}_{Y_1}(Q,K)=0\). A Seifert surface for \(Q\) in the slab computes both the stationary preferred framing and \(a+b=0\), where \(a=\operatorname{lk}(Q,J)\) and \(b=\operatorname{lk}(Q,V)\) in the capped slab.

In the capped three-link exterior the stationary longitude has homology \(a\mu_J+b\mu_V\), and the \(J\)-longitude has homology \(a\mu_Q+c\mu_V\). Filling \(Q\) therefore sends a primitive candidate \(p\mu_J+q\lambda_J\) to zero in \(H_1(X;\mathbb Z)\) exactly when \((qa,p,qc)=k(0,a,b)\) for an integer \(k\). If \(q=0\), then \(p=\pm1\) and \(ka=p\), \(kb=0\) contradict \(b=-a\). If \(q\ne0\), the first coordinate forces \(a=b=0\), and the others give \(p=c=0\). Primitivity leaves \(q=\pm1\). The same test applies to \(V\). In the resulting free integral meridian group each meridian remains nonzero, so any compressed port has peripheral kernel precisely the subgroup generated by its longitude.

On either port, join the truncated marked face chord to a section of the lateral annulus. Orient the resulting loop along the return. Integer winding of the section relative to the fixed end germs realizes all meridian translates of a longitude. There is exactly one section class with the preferred slope. Isotope the disk boundary to that representative and extend through a collar. Restoring the radial strip and the small chord end segments makes the bridge disk, with its interior missing the faces and the other return. Conversely, a bridge disk for the chord clips in product tube coordinates, after shrinking the tube if necessary, to a proper meridian-dual disk on that same port. Equivalently, the chosen longitude word is trivial in \(\pi_1(X)\) by Dehn's lemma. The uniqueness is of the slope and relative winding. The original closed attaching framing is carried by the moves, while the winding specifies the local disk direction. Through the intervening torus collar their boundaries are actual stationary filling slopes. Slit the resulting planar surface along disjoint bands from the returning edge to the holes. Each slit merges two boundary components and corresponds to a signed slide over a stationary framed parallel. In the filled chart parallel meridian caps give the framed push and its inverse. The remaining disk pushes the changed return to a short inward parallel of its chord. The original seam identification carries this chord and its two short end germs into the opposite side, giving the opposite capped return relative to \(Q\). The resulting compact cores lie in the slab interior. Their smooth projections miss a disk in \(S^2\); the complementary closed disk times a closed interior height interval gives a common ball after rounding and shrinking the framed tubes. The ball complement in the capped slab is also a ball, exhibited by the two cap-side regions joined by the removed product corridor. The two-component capped surgery is \(Y_2\) by prime uniqueness applied to the actual outlet \(Y_1\# S^3_L\). Its integral surgery matrix is zero by the outlet rank, so the marked framings agree with the two preferred local longitudes. ## The exact dichotomy

If both ports compress, the two disks can be made disjoint by replacing subdisks along innermost intersection circles and pushing off. Their boundaries already lie on distinct tori. Restoring both strips and using the two bridge disks simultaneously makes the returns short parallels of the matched chords in the stationary-filled slab. After gluing the seam the moving knot bounds a small disk across the chord. The level before moving surgery is \(Y_1\#S^3_0(Q)\). Its first Betti number is two, so the required increase forces zero surgery on the seam unknot. Thus \(Y_3=Y_2\#S^3_0(Q)\), and prime uniqueness followed by Property R makes the capped \(Q\) an unknot. [Milnor, Theorem 1](https://doi.org/10.2307/2372800) · [Gabai, Corollary 8.3 and Remark 8.5](https://www.scribd.com/document/1026013102/2-Gabai)

For the converse, take \(Q\) unknotted and suppose \(J\) is the port furnished by the free outlet. Cap the stationary-filled slab to get \(M_Q=S^3_0(Q)\cong Y_1\). The bridge disk at \(J\) extended along its trivial cap chord gives a spanning disk for the completed \(J\) disjoint from \(V\). A small regular neighborhood yields a ball \(B_J\) containing \(J\) in its interior and disjoint from \(V\). The preceding localization identifies the returned local pair with the capped \((Q,V)\), with its actual zero coefficients. Thus the actual nonmeridional surgery on \(V\) in \(M_Q\) gives \(Y_2\). Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1, supplies a spanning disk for \(V\) in \(M_Q\). Choose a smaller ball inside \(B_J\) off this disk. A ball isotopy extended to a neighborhood of \(B_J\) disjoint from \(V\) takes \(B_J\) inside the smaller ball. Pull the disk back to make it disjoint from \(B_J\), and clip off the \(V\) tube to obtain a longitude compression in the capped exterior. The cap-collar identification carries it to \(X\). This proves the converse for the original ports. Together with forced existence it gives the dichotomy. [Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1507.06561#page=9)

## Slope stress tests

For a general primitive moving filling class \(s f+d b_K\) with \(d\ne0\), the two block boundary groups still inject. With \(k=b_K\) and \(j=b_J\) denoting the boundary loops, the filled group is
\[
 \langle f,j\mid[f,j]=1\rangle *_{\langle f\rangle}
 \langle f,k\mid[f,k]=1,\ f^s k^d=1\rangle .
\]
The second factor is infinite cyclic with \(f\) of infinite order because \(d\ne0\) and the filling class is primitive. For the other boundary rebase the pair of pants with \(b_V,b_K\). Attach the two ports by amalgamating along the first and applying the HNN inclusion at the second. A free outlet thus still forces a compression for a nonmeridional moving slope. The product calculation and its transported port map use \(|d|=1\). Under the full rank-three condition, projection to the one-component exterior gives \(s=0\), and primitivity itself gives \(|d|=1\).

At the meridional filling the general compression conclusion fails: take \(Q\) a split unknot and choose the two capped returns to be split nontrivial knots in their separate end neighborhoods. The stationary-filled cut has both peripheral torus groups injected, by the knot exteriors and the free-product decomposition. Restoring the moving tube by meridional filling gives the free outlet \(Y_2\) after zero surgery on \(Q\). A distinct test uses two integral zero slopes, with the capped \((Q,J)\) a Hopf link and \(V\) a split trivial return. Push the trivial return across the seam to localize the pair to the zero-framed Hopf link. The joint outlet is \(Y_1\), and the exposed \(J\) port compresses along \(\mu_J\) because that is the zero-filling slope on the other boundary of the Hopf exterior. Thus freeness alone gives the disk existence in the integral class, and the rank jump gives the longitude conclusion. These tests concern precisely the marked two-return class; the proof reaches arbitrary returned local pairs only through localization, with smooth recognition requiring its own hypothesis.
