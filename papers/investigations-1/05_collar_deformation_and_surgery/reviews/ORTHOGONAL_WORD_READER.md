The two conjugacy classes are those of the original, unframed GST reverse attaching circles in the smooth oriented outlet \(Y=\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\), for \(n\ge3\) and \(k=1\).

For every embedded nonseparating sphere \(S\subset Y\) algebraically orthogonal to the class of \(s=Q^*\), the minimum number of intersections with \(S\) over embedded knots freely homotopic to \(s\) is \(2n(n-1)\). For \(r=V_n^*\) the corresponding minimum is four for every sphere orthogonal to \(r\). The calculation for \(s\) leaves open whether the native knot attains the free-homotopy minimum. The separate native calculation constructs an orthogonal four-point sphere for \(r\). ## The splitting is fixed by the orthogonality class

Cut \(Y\) along \(S\) to obtain the connected cut-open manifold \(W\). The edge sphere is simply connected, so regluing gives \(F_2=\pi_1(W)*\langle t'\rangle\) by van Kampen, where \(t'\) makes one passage through the collar. The free factor \(\pi_1(W)\) is free by Nielsen–Schreier and has abelianization of rank one by the displayed splitting. Therefore \(\pi_1(W)=\langle u'\rangle\) with \(u'\) primitive. The primitive intersection homomorphism \(H_1(Y)\to\mathbb Z\) takes \(t'\) to one up to sign and has kernel generated by \([u']\). For \(S\) orthogonal to \(s\), this kernel is generated by \((n+1,-n)\), the primitive vector \([s]\).

Suppose \((u,t)\) is a free basis with \([u]=[s]\) and orient \(u'\) to have that vector. Write the matrix of the automorphism \(\alpha:(u,t)\mapsto(u',t')\) in the \(([u],[t])\) basis. Its columns are \((1,0)\) and \((a,\epsilon)\), with \(\epsilon=\pm1\). The automorphism \(\beta:u\mapsto u,\ t\mapsto u^a t^\epsilon\) has the same matrix. Nielsen's rank-two kernel theorem makes \(\alpha\beta^{-1}\) inner. Thus \(u'=gug^{-1}\) and \(t'=g u^a t^\epsilon g^{-1}\) for the same \(g\). Once \(u'\) has been based at \(u\), \(g\) centralizes \(u\) and belongs to \(\langle u\rangle\), so any complement differs from \(t\) by multiplication on both sides by powers of \(u\) and possibly inversion. The abstract Bass–Serre tree has vertices \(h\langle u\rangle\) and undirected edges with endpoints \(h\langle u\rangle,ht\langle u\rangle\) for \(h\in F_2\). Replacing \(t\) by \(u^a t u^b\) reindexes the edges, while \(t^{-1}\) reverses their directions. Conjugating the entire splitting also preserves its translation lengths. This proves the claimed independence from \(S\) and its base path. [Nielsen, *Die Isomorphismen der allgemeinen, unendlichen Gruppe mit zwei Erzeugenden*, Math. Ann. 78 (1917), 385–397](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01457113) · [Explicit modern theorem statement, Bogopolski–Martino–Maslakova–Ventura, Theorem 3.1](https://upcommons.upc.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/0fe19044-55d2-4190-85a1-7a3aac87cfda/content)

## Reduced words and their realizations

For \(s\), set \(t=xy^{-1}\) and \(u=xt^n\). These give a free basis by the inverse expressions \(x=ut^{-n}\) and \(y=t^{-1}ut^{-n}\). Direct substitution yields

\[
s=(ut^{-n})^{n+1}(t^nu^{-1}t)^n
 =(ut^{-n})^{n-1}ut\,u^{-1}(t^{n+1}u^{-1})^{n-2}t .
\]

The last expression is cyclically reduced. The negative \(t\)-count is \(n(n-1)\), and the positive count is \(2+(n+1)(n-2)=n(n-1)\). For the other native word \(r=yxyx^{-1}y^{-1}x^{-1}\), use the basis \(u=xy^{-1},\,t=x\). The inverse expression \(y=u^{-1}t\) gives

\[
r=u^{-1}t^2u^{-1}t^{-1}ut^{-1}.
\]

This is cyclically reduced with two \(t\)-letters of each sign. Here \([u]=-[r]\), so choose the sign of the sphere vertex generator to match \([u]\) in the preceding argument.

An oriented representative transverse to \(S\) reads one stable letter for each passage through the collar. Free and cyclic reduction can only decrease the number of stable letters, so the cyclically reduced count is a lower bound. Conversely, choose one short crossing arc in the collar for each letter of a cyclically reduced word, with distinct crossing points on \(S\), and realize the prescribed paths between their ends inside \(W\). Changing the chosen crossing points can be offset by paths on the boundary spheres, which are simply connected. The paths in \(W\) can be made embedded and mutually disjoint relative to their distinct endpoints by general position in dimension three. Smoothing then gives one embedded closed knot with exactly the word's crossings and the required free homotopy class. This operation does not supply an isotopy of the native \(s\) or a transport of its framing.
