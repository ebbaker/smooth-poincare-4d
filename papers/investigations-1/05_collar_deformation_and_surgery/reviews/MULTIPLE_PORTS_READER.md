## The horizontal cut and the opposite mouth

Write \(Y_j=\#_j(S^1\times S^2)\). In the generator family in \(Y_1\), use the cyclic cover \(S^2\times\mathbb R\) with the end mouths near heights \(0,n\). A horizontal sphere at a height between end balls cuts each central rail in \(n\) points downstairs. In the cover those points occur at the \(n\) successive lifted cutting heights. At each lifted height the chord joins the two rail points of that turn. The **opposite mouth disk** just before the encounter with the local knot gives a separate two-point port and its chord \(c\). Splitting at the endpoints of this chord gives the arcs \(R\) and \(V\) in the full moving knot. Here \(R\) contains both long rails and the stationary longitude outside its gap, and \(V\) completes the opposite end to a knot via \(c\). This identifies the intended disk boundary even when successive horizontal cuts give many pieces of \(R\).

In the central chart the ruled surface projects by
\[
(t,u)\longmapsto \bigl(x(t),(2u-1)b,[t]\bigr),\qquad 0\le u\le1.
\]
Equality of images implies that the lifted heights differ by an integer and that the \(x\)-coordinates agree. Strict monotonicity then gives the same lifted height, and \(b>0\) gives the same \(u\). The parameter derivatives in the height and transverse directions are independent. The horizontal cut therefore meets the strip in \(n\) disjoint intervals and all identifications recover a single embedded strip. At a seam the two sides of the *same* lifted interval have matching germs, as can be read in the coordinates \((x-x(t),y,t)\). Different lifted intervals at the same projected seam have different \(x\). For each fixed \(n\), compactness gives positive separation of the finitely many overlapping translates away from the intended joints, so the construction has room for sufficiently thin framed tubes. The end boxes have interiors disjoint from the central lanes and from each other, except for their intended attachment edges. Their end segments lie on the mouth gap of the stationary parallel and at \(c\). Matching product germs there and rounding give an embedded rectangle \(C\) with the two rails as its side edges. This argument uses the full marked chord germs across seams. Pairwise disjoint rail cores alone leave the interiors of spanning intervals unconstrained. The smoothed union is an embedded annulus with boundaries \(\lambda_{r_0}\) and \(R\cup c\). Its interior misses \(V\). If the moving tube is also drilled, the product neighborhood along \(R\) clips that edge to the tube boundary and truncates the two corners at the port.

Choose an interval \(I\) in the visible longitude away from \(G\) and \(r_0<r'<r_1\). The rectangle \(q(I,[r',r_1],\theta_0)\) is a band to the marked framed parallel \(\lambda_{r'}\). The band orientation subtracts the longitude. Truncating the annulus at \(r'\) and slitting along this joining rectangle leaves a disk with boundary the slid return together with \(c\). The seam check above ensures the disk remains embedded and misses the preserved arc \(V\) in its interior. Pushing the slid return across the disk, with its small normal neighborhood carried along, gives the completion \(V\cup c\). The marked access box identifies this completion with the actual unknot in the original Figure 5 ball for the displayed fixture. The choice of \(I\) and \(r'\) uses the visible longitude and the stationary framing. The long ruled strip and slit disk certify the effect of that local choice and use only the given port geometry. ## The conditional free-end extension

Assume the two-component trace \(W\) has actual ends \(Y_r\to Y_{r+2}\), the annulus and slit disk have the indicated marked slope and disjointness, and the completion \(V\cup c\) is an unknot in a ball of the inlet. For any framed pair in this trace, the forward and reverse handle presentations give \(\operatorname{rank}H_1(W)\le r\) and \(\operatorname{rank}H_1(W)\ge r\), respectively. The two incoming core classes in \(\mathbb Z^r\) therefore vanish. In the common exterior the two meridians are independent and \(H_1(E)=\mathbb Z^{r+2}\), by the pair sequence and zero intersections with the null-homologous cores. The outgoing filling kills the two actual slopes and leaves the same rank. Each slope thus vanishes in \(H_1(E)\). In the meridian subgroup the coefficients of \(\alpha_i=\lambda_i+f_i\mu_i\) are \(f_i\) on the component's own meridian and the linking number on the other meridian. This gives zero framings and mutual linking after the local slide as well. [Free-end slope calculation](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/TWO_ATTACHMENT_GROUP.md#the-trace-group-and-both-systems-of-slopes)

Put the returned local unknot \(U\) first. Its zero surgery is \(Y_{r+1}\) even if its original disk meets the remaining component. In that once-filled level the actual remaining integral surgery gives \(Y_{r+2}\), so its slope is nonmeridional. The rank-increasing case of Meier–Schirmer–Zupan, Theorem 5.1, makes the remaining knot a local unknot with zero slope in \(Y_{r+1}\). Move its disk ball into the common exterior, choosing a small target ball disjoint from an isolating ball for \(U\) in the original inlet. A smooth knot isotopy to that target can be perturbed relative to its ends so that the track in \(Y_{r+1}\times[0,1]\) meets the first filling core times \([0,1]\) transversely in a finite set. The detours before and after each passage differ by band sum with the filling-core meridian, hence by a slide over the actual framed parallel of \(U\) when read in the inlet. This yields a finite framed sequence to the split pair. Its length may depend on the partner. The initial radial slide and the detours use zero temporary handles. ## Relative maps and counts

At each slide, its anchor handle can be put first since the incoming tubes are disjoint. Extend the framed isotopy of the moving tube to an ambient isotopy of the once-filled level. Interpolate that isotopy over the next product collar and use the transported parametrization on the next handle. The resulting map is fixed on the incoming collar, with inverse given by the inverse collar diffeomorphisms and inverse tube identifications. For \(F=F_m\cdots F_1\) the inverse is \(F_1^{-1}\cdots F_m^{-1}\). Write \(P=\partial_+F\). A later parametrized tube \(T\) goes to \(PT\), with inverse \(T^{-1}P^{-1}\) on its image. Extend over later handles to the final outlet map \(\widetilde P\). The closing \(\gamma\) becomes \(\gamma\widetilde P^{-1}\), with inverse \(\widetilde P\gamma^{-1}\). For an immediate closing an original outgoing face \(o_i\) has the same dual marking, since \((\gamma P^{-1})(Po_i)\tau=\gamma o_i\tau\) with the usual exchange \(\tau\) of the two factors. With later handles this equality uses the actual final outlet faces and \(\widetilde P\). The relative trace comparison alone preserves the two middle handles. [One-period trace and closing maps](../02_handle_isolation_and_marked_surgery/notes/CUT_TANGLE_RECOVERY.md#relative-maps-and-handle-count)

With an immediate upper closure by the actual \(H_{r+2}=\natural_{r+2}(S^1\times D^3)\), the new split zero-framed components have two disjoint outgoing spheres. Each crosses its own surgery core transversely once and misses the other. Match the two spheres to distinct reverse belt spheres in a product marking of the upper body. The two boundary identifications have the same orientation type, so their discrepancy extends across the upper body. The two geometric reverse 1/2 cancellations remove two forward 2/3 pairs. In the one-sided three-middle fixture the extra surviving component is the original split unknot \(A\), with its own geometric cancellation. Thus the two counts are \((3,3)\to(2,2)\to(1,1)\to(0,0)\), with two slides and zero stabilizations in this fixture. For an interior block only the relative equivalence and transported markings follow until the upper intersections are supplied.
