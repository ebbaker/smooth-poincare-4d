## Exact chiral energy and the complete exterior

In an oriented orthonormal section Ricci eigenframe the curvature operator of $dt^2+h$ is diagonal in complementary pairs, with values $0,R_h/2-r_i$ in each pair. Its same-chirality blocks therefore have diagonal entries $(R_h/2-r_i)/2$. Subtracting the scalar term $R_h/12$ gives $-(r_i-R_h/3)/2$ in either chirality, for the Hilbert–Schmidt norm on real unit chiral two-forms. On a shoulder the Ricci eigenvalues are $2K_\perp,K_\perp+K_{\rm tan},K_\perp+K_{\rm tan}$. The squared norm of the trace-free Ricci tensor is $2(K_\perp-K_{\rm tan})^2/3$. Integrating the native volume differential $u^3\,dt\,d\omega$ on four shoulders and the product volume on two cylinders independently gives

$$
J=\frac{32\pi}{3}\int_T^{t_*}u^{-1}(q'-1)^2\operatorname{sech}^4(q)\,dt+\frac{16\pi}{3a}.
$$

One cylinder contributes $8\pi/(3a)$ because it has volume $4\pi a^3$ and squared trace-free Ricci norm $2/(3a^4)$. Each chiral band energy is $LJ/4$ and a half is $LJ/8$. Using $a\le \operatorname{sech}T$ and the exact $L_*$, one has $L_*/a\ge \pi\sqrt3\cosh B$, so the cylinder term alone is at least $4\pi^2\sqrt3\cosh B/3>8\pi^2$. The strict inequality follows already from $\cosh B>\cosh 2>11/3>2\sqrt3$. The lower bound also holds for all $L\ge L_*$.

Let $C_j^\sigma=U_j^\sigma+V_j^\sigma$ be the full fixed contribution of filling and transition on side $j$. Cut at the center and glue two equally oriented copies of either complete side by the section's orientation-reversing isometry, conjugated by the actual boundary marking on that side. The reflected normal coordinate and the product metric give a smooth closed oriented double with twice that side's energy in each unchanged chirality. In the $H_2$ model double, $H_2$ of both copies vanishes and the Mayer–Vietoris map from boundary $H_1$ into the sum of the two body $H_1$ groups is injective, so the double has $b_2=0$. In the $A_2$ double the map from boundary $H_2$ to the sum of the two copy groups is the graph of an isomorphism up to sign. Its cokernel has dimension two and either copy's $H_2$ injects in this cokernel. Since $H_1$ of either body vanishes, the boundary $H_1$ adds dimension two to $H_2$ of the double. The two disjoint interior square-zero spheres in one copy form an isotropic plane in the nonsingular rank-four form. The signatures of both doubles are zero. The closed signature identity in this normalization gives $C_j^+=C_j^-$. This identity is for each exterior sum and allows unequal individual filling and transition energies.
