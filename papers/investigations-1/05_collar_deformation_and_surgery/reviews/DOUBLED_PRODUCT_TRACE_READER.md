Remove regular neighborhoods of \(a,b,c\) from the ball to get the genus-three handlebody \(H\), and remove only \(a\) to get the solid torus \(V\). Shave a boundary collar off \(H\) to get \(H_0\subset\operatorname{int}V\). The complementary compression body \(C\) is a collar of \(\partial V\) with two three-dimensional \(1\)-handles, obtained from the \(b,c\) tubes joined to the collar at their ends. The corresponding co-core disks extend across the shaved collar to \(\Sigma=\partial H_0\). They cut \(C\) to \(\partial V\times I\). This identifies \(\Sigma\) as a Heegaard surface of the actual \(V\) with all of \(\partial V\) on the \(C\) side.

Waldhausen's §4(2)–(3) respects the boundary partition and sends an unstabilized splitting of a handlebody to the boundary-parallel splitting. Here the genus-three splitting is therefore equivalent to the genus-one splitting with two stabilizations in disjoint balls. Pull back the corresponding \(C\)-side dual disks \(D_1,D_2\) and the \(H_0\)-side complete meridian system \(E_1,E_2,E_3\). The \(C\) disks cut to a torus product. Each \(\partial D_i\) crosses just \(\partial E_i\) once. The pullback is used only to find disks. Their PL corners can be rounded in the original smooth halves, smoothing the finitely many boundary crossings transversely in surface charts and preserving disjointness and the cut manifolds. All doubling and handle cancellations are then smooth. The two stabilizations describe the existing three-dimensional splitting. The four-dimensional moves create no handles. Bonahon's \(\mathcal D\) consists of systems with boundary on the positive side, with boundary-irreducible cut manifolds, minimal under deletion. Both disk pairs in \(C\) satisfy this.
