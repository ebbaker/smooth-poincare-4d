# Collars and smooth four-sphere recognition

25 November 2026 (America/New_York). Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI Codex assistance. Exact serving model and reasoning effort unavailable. This is the agreed research design. Its implications are conditional and its proposed tests await activation.

## The common endpoint

Fix an arbitrary smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\). All metrics here are Riemannian. With \(W^\pm\) acting on the real bundles \(\Lambda^\pm\), write \(E_\pm(g)=\int |W^\pm_g|_{\rm HS}^2\,dV_g\) and \(Y([g])\) for the Yamabe constant. Producing on **this smooth \(M\)** a class with \(Y>0\) and \(E_+<4\pi^2\) gives smooth recognition by Chang–Gursky–Yang. Producing a smooth global metric with \(W^+=0\) also suffices. The signature formula forces \(W^-=0\), and the conformal developing map on the closed simply connected manifold gives the sphere. The local collar problems below can contribute to either construction. [Sections and connections §1.1](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L144) · [Dirac and twistor spinors §§2.1–2.2](../../02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L155) · [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A and norm convention in Remark 2](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf#page=3)

One route begins with volume-one positive Yamabe minimizers \(g_i\) on \(M\), in a fixed band \(4\pi^2\le E_+(g_i)\le8\pi^2-\delta\), with \(\delta>0\) and \(\mathfrak B_i=\int_M|B_{g_i}|\,dV_{g_i}\to0\). Write \(E_+(g_i)\to e\) after passage to a subsequence. The band gives \(Y_i\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\) and a total \(L^2\) curvature bound. A round marked ball in the conformal class can have vanishing volume in Yamabe gauge. On closed \(M\), \(E_+=E_-\) and the full four-index Weyl integral is \(8E_+\). On a collar the two chiral energies must be accounted for individually. [Sections and connections §§8.2, 11.1 and 13.2](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L3010) · [Band estimates](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L3868) · [Shrinking marks](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L4974)

## What a proof would still have to construct

1. **Enter the regime on every smooth type.** Start with an arbitrary \(M\). Obtain a recognized class directly, or justify a deformation or variational selection with positive Yamabe sign, a strict energy margin and the particular compactness or Bach control it uses. A marked local radial lift has a center-dependent step size. A minimizing sequence alone supplies no decay estimate for \(\mathfrak B_i\). [Local radial lift and step size, §4.4](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L1468) · [Radial strain, §10](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L3644) · [Continuation problem, §15](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L6375)
2. **Distinguish compactness from concentration.** A nondegenerate limit pulled back to the original smooth \(M\) permits tests there. A shrinking mark can instead approximate a positive closed Bach-flat class. In a concentration regime identify components, both scales at each interface and the topology of their links. Track whether every required core embeds. Existing smooth-base and smooth-ALE comparisons give an energy inequality. [Sections and connections §§11.2–12.4](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L3954) · [Accounting on a smooth base](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L4639) · [Compact gauges and marks, §13](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L4730)
3. **Account for an entire neck or cut at one controlled place.** For accounting, require \(\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_i\int_{Lr_i<\rho<R_i/L}|W|_{\rm tens}^2\,dV=0\) when \(r_i/R_i\to0\). Exhaust the full complement of all truncations, including any intermediate regions. In the manuscript's smooth comparison class equality \(e=e_0+\sum e_j\) then excludes a nonflat smooth ALE bubble. Such a bubble contributes \(e_X>4\pi^2\), leaving a strictly positive residual \(e-e_X<4\pi^2-\delta\). The other smooth bubbles are flat and the smooth positive-Yamabe Bach-flat base would be conformally round. Equality would force \(e=e_X\). Positive closed critical values still require an argument. Quotient necks, orbifold components and uncontrolled tails require their own comparison. [§14.3 metric estimate](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L5466) · [§14.4 intrinsic estimate](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L5768) · [§15, exact order of limits and conditional exclusion](../../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L6503)
4. **Use a strict improvement on the same smooth type.** A second test starts with one supplied nonflat smooth ALE bubble, smooth convergence on its full embedded core, the differentiated end decay of §11 and a usable spherical cut. If the cap audit proves positivity and gives \(E_+(\widehat M_{\rm ext})\le e-e_X+\varepsilon<4\pi^2\), the capped exterior is standard. Identify the capped core smoothly with \(\widehat X\). Recover \(M\cong\widehat X\#S^4\cong\widehat X\) using the actual boundary identification. Then \(\widehat X\) carries the positive-Yamabe critical class of energy \(e_X\le e-\kappa_\delta\) on the original smooth type. If \(e=I^+(M)=\inf_{[g]:Y([g])>0}E_+(g)\) **and** such an approximately Bach-critical minimizing sequence is supplied, this gives a contradiction. This conditional use of one cut need not wait for an identity over every neck. For repeated descent, prove preserved hypotheses and cumulative error bounds, and show finite termination or a nondegenerate limiting endpoint. 5. **Close the remaining global cases.** Show that the possibilities under selection are exhausted, including closed critical values and loss of the mark. If a positive closed minimizer survives on the original smooth type, one specific test is to exhibit a variation through positive-Yamabe classes with strictly lower energy. If every tested variation has nonnegative second variation, examine rigidity or change the variational selection. A possible alternative is to match actual spinors, retain their normalization and obtain an exact positive-chiral twistor field in a smooth nondegenerate global limit on \(M\). The chiral curvature vanishes off the zero set and hence everywhere by continuity. A local \(W^+\) bound can instead contribute to energy accounting with a justified bound for the other chirality wherever full energy is required. A chiral-evolution restart would require compatible global data, a definite wedge matrix where reconstruction is used, and quantitative continuation into a recognition regime. For each route, specify how the local data yield the global metric on the original smooth type. ### What matched spinors would contribute to the energy identity

The contradiction in SEC §15 uses the identity in **one chirality**. Let \(U_{i,L}\) be the full region left over after the matched, disjoint component truncations of §12.4. Suppose extraction and matching produce smooth positive half-spinors \(\psi_a\) there with \(\sum_a|\psi_a|^2\ge c_{i,L}^2>0\), and put \(T_a=P_{g_i}\psi_a\). The Dirac identity gives
\[
 E_+(U_{i,L})\ \le\
 2c_{i,L}^{-2}\sum_a\int_{U_{i,L}}|\Pi(d^\nabla T_a)^+|^2\,dV_{g_i}.
\]
If the right-hand side tends to zero in the neck limit (first the sequence limsup, then exhaustion), the component comparison yields \(e=e_0+\sum_j e_j\) in \(E_+\). The same argument can use spinors on some strips and direct \(E_+\) estimates on the rest if the bounds cover the residual region and their sum vanishes. This gives the spinorial branches a precise sufficient output. Full tensor no-neck is a stronger geometric target. On an individual strip a bound for \(W^-\) would require its own argument. ## When the geometry forces a revision

Return the smallest failed implication to its owner with the failing term, scale, mode or example. Record whether the failure is of a model, a coordinate choice, a length-uniform estimate or a proposed application. The lead records a B decision with alternatives, states the changed hypothesis, keeps the old result and issued request under their original IDs, and commissions a new bounded E check if needed. Update affected sibling handoffs by reference. Only dependent claims wait. For example, oscillating curvature can invalidate a \(C^2\) cap while leaving first-order spin comparison viable. Incompatible boundary traces can call for different end placement or a finite-dimensional correction even on a round strip. A successful bound on one strip leaves the separate problem of summing errors over the neck.
