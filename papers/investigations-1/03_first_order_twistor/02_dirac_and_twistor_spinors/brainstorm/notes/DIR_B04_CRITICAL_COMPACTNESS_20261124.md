The usual compactness claim for arbitrary volume-one minimizing sequences fails. Instead isolate the topology on coefficients of the first-order equation that permits a limit. Require uniform convergence of positive metrics, strong convergence of their first derivatives in (L^4), and a smooth positive limit on a fixed compact spin manifold. In dimension four an (L^4) coefficient multiplying an (H^1) spinor lies in (L^2). This suggests that convergence of first derivatives at this exponent suffices. Smoothness of the limit is a named premise; approximation by smooth metrics alone does not supply it.

Apply this single coefficient criterion to the two available errors. For Rayleigh energy, allow spin diffeomorphisms and keep the actual weighted mass one. For the critical error one can also choose conformal representatives and renormalize spinors, since the cost is invariant. Test the resulting absorption estimate, strong preservation of mass and distributional passage through the zeros. The outcome is conditional: E03 establishes an obstruction for the one-ball sequence. It supplies no universal choice of good representatives.
