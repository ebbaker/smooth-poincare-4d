Its second variation is degenerate on the *whole* first eigenspace. Use the exact quartic polynomial obtained by squaring the minimizing inequality. This tests a condition already satisfied by the limit.

Take the positive conformal test $1+t\phi+t^2 w$ and the choice $w=\phi^2-\int\phi^2$, suggested by the expansion of spherical conformal factors. The proposed bound is $\int\phi^4\le(15/7)(\int\phi^2)^2$, the round four-sphere's coordinate fourth-moment ratio. If it holds, average over linear combinations of an orthonormal first-eigenbasis and use the lower bound on the mean square of the sum of its squares. The round case suggests an upper bound of five on multiplicity. At five, seek equality for all directions and use variations in $w$ to identify a Laplace eigenvalue of the quadratic products.
