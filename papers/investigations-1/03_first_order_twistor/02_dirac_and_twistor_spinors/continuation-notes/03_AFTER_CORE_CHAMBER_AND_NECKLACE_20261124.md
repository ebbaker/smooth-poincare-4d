**Where things stand.** The flat-neck gluing of a core to a round chamber does not change the conformal class, so it cannot produce pinched minimizers. On $S^1\times S^3$ the actual Yamabe minimizers satisfy $3\lambda_5/s\to1$, so relative pinching alone does not recognize a sphere on a general closed four-manifold. On a homotopy sphere, recognition from a Yamabe metric follows from $\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2<s^2/12-16\pi^2$. A reduced model, tested only on the necklace, suggests that the spectral gap $A$, the Yamabe deficit $Y(S^4)-Y$ and the mass of a scalar-flat blow-up measure the same defect.

**Next task: SP4-FOT-DIR-B24.** Decide what the spectral route should now aim at. Three candidates, with what each would settle.

1. *The two-sided comparison between $\epsilon$ and $1-Y/Y(S^4)$ for unit-volume Yamabe minimizers.* If true it turns the construction problem into the statement $\sigma(M)=\sigma(S^4)$ for homotopy four-spheres. First test for "pinching implies $Y\to Y(S^4)$": whether the Gram matrix of the five modes converges in measure, which would give the Jacobian bound that the weak estimate lacks; the necklace has convergence in $L^1$ and not in $L^2$. First test for the converse: whether near-sharpness of the Sobolev inequality at a minimizer forces five soft directions; a convexity of the Yamabe quotient in the variable $r=u^2$ and the descent of small bubbles under the Yamabe flow are two tools not yet tried.
2. *Local minimizers.* The exact theorem holds for them. A gluing of several small positive-mass cores in balanced position, with inertia matrix below one third of its trace, is predicted to be a stable constant scalar curvature metric with small $A/s$. Establishing it would show that pinching at local minimizers is available wherever positive scalar curvature is, and carries no recognition. This closes a route and constructs nothing recognizing.
3. *Recognition through $E_+<4\pi^2$.* The only recognition criterion in play that sees a small region is the Chang–Gursky–Yang one. The sibling investigation's Weyl minimization and the Bach flow investigation aim at the same threshold. A cross-branch statement for the shared SH-B03 would record that the spectral, spinorial and Weyl routes meet at this one hypothesis and what each contributes.
