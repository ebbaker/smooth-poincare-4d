## Statement

Let $(M^4,g)$ be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented Riemannian
four-manifold with a fixed smooth metric. Let $h$ be the unit round
metric on the oriented $S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$, with volume form $\omega$
and $V=\int_{S^4}\omega=8\pi^2/3$. Suppose $f_j:M\to S^4$ are smooth
maps of degree $+1$, converge strongly in ambient $W^{1,2}$ to a smooth
map $F:M\to S^4$, and satisfy $\sup_j\int_M|df_j|^4\,dV_g<\infty$.
Here $|df|$ is the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, without an energy prefactor.
Along any subsequence on which both indicated measures converge weakly-*,
write

\[
\kappa=\mathop{\rm w^*\!-\!lim}_j f_j^*\omega,\qquad
\mu=\mathop{\rm w^*\!-\!lim}_j |df_j|^4\,dV_g,\qquad
\eta=\kappa-F^*\omega,\qquad \nu=\mu-|dF|^4\,dV_g .
\]

Such a subsequence exists. There are finitely many distinct $x_\ell\in M$
and nonzero integers $k_\ell$, with the empty set allowed, such that

\[
\eta=V\sum_\ell k_\ell\,\delta_{x_\ell},\qquad
\nu\ \ge\ 16|\eta|=16V\sum_\ell |k_\ell|\,\delta_{x_\ell},\qquad
\sum_\ell k_\ell=1-\deg F .
\tag{1}
\]

The proof uses smoothness of $F$, smoothness and constancy in $j$ of
the domain metric, and the stated convergence. It applies to the
homotopy-sphere domains in [B14][b14].

## Energy and Jacobian density splittings

In an oriented orthonormal frame $(e_i)_{i=1}^4$ write
$f^*\omega=J_f\,dV_g$, with
$J_f=\det(f,df(e_1),df(e_2),df(e_3),df(e_4))$ in the oriented ambient
$\mathbb R^5$. The product of singular values satisfies
$|J_f|\le |df|^4/16$ by the arithmetic–geometric mean inequality.
Thus the signed and energy measures have uniformly bounded total
variation and admit simultaneous weak-* limits on compact $M$.

Put $G_j=df_j-dF$, interpreted in the common ambient bundle. Strong
$L^2$ and the uniform $L^4$ bound give strong $L^p$ for $1\le p<4$,
by interpolation and finite volume. With $dF$ bounded, the expansion of
$|dF+G_j|^4$ gives

\[
\big\||df_j|^4-|dF|^4-|G_j|^4\big\|_{L^1}\longrightarrow0,
\]

because the remaining terms are bounded by a constant times
$|G_j|+|G_j|^2+|G_j|^3$. Thus $|G_j|^4\,dV_g\rightharpoonup^*\nu$
and $\nu\ge0$. The analogous determinant expansion gives

\[
J_{f_j}-J_F
 =\det(f_j,G_j(e_1),G_j(e_2),G_j(e_3),G_j(e_4))+r_j,
 \qquad \|r_j\|_{L^1}\longrightarrow0.
\tag{2}
\]

The mixed derivative terms have one to three $G_j$ columns and
bounded other columns. The term with all four $dF$ columns differs
from $J_F$ by a term linear in $f_j-F$, which tends to zero in $L^1$.
For the term displayed in (2), project the four columns onto
$f_j^\perp$ and apply the same singular-value inequality to this
projected linear map. Its norm is at most $|G_j|$, so the determinant
is bounded in absolute value by $|G_j|^4/16$. Testing with real
continuous functions $\phi$ gives
$|\int\phi\,d\eta|\le \frac1{16}\int|\phi|\,d\nu$.
The total variation characterization yields $16|\eta|\le\nu$.

## Good slices and convergence on balls

Fix any center $x\in M$ and restrict to $0<r<\operatorname{inj}_g(x)$,
so $B_r(x)$ has smooth boundary $S_r$. Pass to a common subsequence
with $\sum_j\|f_j-F\|_{L^2(M)}^2<\infty$, preserving both weak-* limits.
The coarea formula for distance from $x$ and $\lvert\nabla d_g(x,\cdot)\rvert=1$
off $x$ in this range give

\[
\sum_j\int_{S_r}|f_j-F|^2\,dA_g<\infty
\quad\text{for a.e. }r,\qquad
\int_0^{\operatorname{inj}_g(x)}\!\int_{S_r}|d_\tau f_j|^4\,dA_g\,dr
\le \int_M |df_j|^4\,dV_g.
\]

Fatou's lemma gives $\liminf_j\int_{S_r}|d_\tau f_j|^4\,dA_g<\infty$
for almost every $r$. For each such radius there is a further
subsequence with bounded tangential $W^{1,4}$ on this fixed
three-sphere. The $L^4$ norm of the map values is bounded by the area
and the unit sphere constraint. Morrey embedding and compactness in
$C^0(S_r)$ then give a further subsequence converging uniformly to
$F|_{S_r}$, since the $L^2(S_r)$ error tends to zero. The slice
subsequence may depend on $x$ and $r$. The limiting measures do not
change under any of these extractions.

Also exclude the at most countably many $r$ with $\mu(S_r)>0$, using
disjointness of the spheres at a fixed center. For the resulting good
radii, $\int_{B_r(x)} f_j^*\omega\to\kappa(B_r(x))$. Indeed, approximate
the indicator of the ball by continuous cutoffs that agree with it
outside a shrinking boundary neighborhood. The absolute discrepancy
for $f_j^*\omega$ is bounded by the $|df_j|^4\,dV_g/16$ mass of that
neighborhood. The weak limit of the energy measures bounds the
$\limsup$ of this mass for a closed neighborhood, and the limit as
the neighborhood shrinks is zero. The same cutoff error for $\kappa$
vanishes by $|\kappa|\le\mu/16$, obtained by taking weak-* limits
in the pointwise Jacobian bound.

## The geodesic homotopy and integer mass

Fix a good radius and use its slice subsequence. Write $B=B_r(x)$,
$S=\partial B$, and let $\delta_j=\sup_S d_h(f_j,F)\to0$. Interpolate
on $[0,1]\times S$ by the shortest geodesic from $f_j(y)$ to $F(y)$,
with a fixed smooth time reparametrization that is constant near each
endpoint. Denote the resulting map by $H_j$. The geodesic interpolation
is smooth with uniformly bounded derivatives in the endpoint variables
on a fixed compact neighborhood of the diagonal, away from antipodal
pairs. Its time speed is at most $C\delta_j$ and its tangential
derivative obeys $|d_\tau H_j|\le C(|d_\tau f_j|+|d_\tau F|)$.
The pullback of $\omega$ uses the time derivative and three
tangential derivatives. Therefore

\[
\int_{[0,1]\times S}|H_j^*\omega|
\le C\delta_j\int_S (|d_\tau f_j|+|d_\tau F|)^3\,dA_g
\longrightarrow0 .
\tag{3}
\]

Hölder's inequality bounds the last integral by the fourth-power
slice bound and the fixed area of $S$.

Glue $B$, the collar oriented by time followed by the boundary
orientation, and $-B$ to a closed oriented smooth double with collar.
Use $f_j$, $H_j$, and $F$ on the three pieces in that order. To make
the map smooth, first precompose both ball maps with a smooth
self-map of the closed ball which is the identity off a radial
boundary collar, fixes the boundary, and collapses a thinner collar
outwards onto that boundary. Interpolating the radial function to
the identity gives a homotopy relative to the boundary. It preserves
the integral of the pullback of $\omega$ by Stokes, since $\omega$
is closed and the pullback on the boundary cylinder of the homotopy
has rank at most three. The maps are now constant in the normal
direction near the seams. The smooth degree formula yields

\[
\int_B f_j^*\omega-\int_B F^*\omega
  +\int_{[0,1]\times S}H_j^*\omega\ \in\ V\mathbb Z.
\]

Taking the limit using (3) and the convergence on the ball gives
$\eta(B_r(x))\in V\mathbb Z$ for every good radius. For any
$0<r<\operatorname{inj}_g(x)$, choose good radii increasing to $r$.
Their open balls exhaust $B_r(x)$. Continuity from below of the
finite signed measure and closedness of $V\mathbb Z$ extend the
integer conclusion to every such open ball.

## Excluding a diffuse signed part

Let $A=\{x:|\eta|(\{x\})\ge V\}$. This set is finite. The integer
mass of small balls gives $\eta(\{x\})\in V\mathbb Z$ by decreasing
the radius to zero. If $x\notin A$, then $|\eta|(B_r(x))<V$ for all
sufficiently small $r$, so $\eta(B_r(x))=0$ for all such $r$.

This last vanishing is uniform for small enough radii when $x$ ranges
over a fixed compact subset of $M\setminus A$. For each point of the
compact set, take a ball of total variation less than $V$ and cover
the set by finitely many of the concentric half-radius balls. Any
ball centered in this compact set with radius below the minimum
of those half radii lies in one of the chosen larger balls. Take
the radius also below the injectivity radius of the compact manifold.
Thus, for $\phi\in C_c(M\setminus A)$ and all sufficiently small
$r>0$, Fubini gives

\[
0=\frac{1}{b_4r^4}\int_M\phi(x)\,\eta(B_r(x))\,dV_g(x)
 =\int_M\Big(\frac{1}{b_4r^4}\int_{B_r(y)}\phi(x)\,dV_g(x)\Big)d\eta(y),
\]

where $b_4$ is the Euclidean unit four-ball volume. The inner
average tends uniformly to $\phi(y)$ by uniform continuity and the
small-ball volume asymptotic $\operatorname{Vol}_g(B_r(y))/(b_4r^4)\to1$
in normal coordinates on compact $M$. Hence $\int\phi\,d\eta=0$.
The signed measure is supported on $A$, proving the first part of
(1). The energy bound was proved in (2), and testing $\kappa$ with
the constant function $1$ yields the last identity in (1).

## Strict local criterion and its scope

Along an extracted subsequence it suffices that
$\mu(\{x\})<16V$ at every $x\in M$. The smooth background energy
assigns zero mass to points, so the same criterion can be stated
using $\nu$. A sequence-level sufficient condition is

\[
\text{for every }x\in M,\qquad
\lim_{r\downarrow0}\limsup_{j\to\infty}
\int_{B_r(x)}|df_j|^4\,dV_g<16V.
\tag{4}
\]

For every extracted $\mu$, its atom at $x$ is bounded above by
the $\liminf$ of the energy of the subsequence on each open
$B_r(x)$, by the positive-measure portmanteau inequality. Consequently
(4) excludes all nonzero signed atoms and gives $\deg F=1$.
On the supplied Yamabe and spectral hypotheses in [E14][e14],
the strong $W^{1,2}$ limit and its smoothness are obtained before
the degree argument. A bounded fourth energy and (4) then supply
the degree needed for E14's conditional homothety conclusion.
Constructing sequences satisfying these bounds on arbitrary smooth
homotopy four-spheres remains a separate premise.

- Equality in the local cap can lose degree. On round $S^4$ take
  the orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphisms given by
  $x\mapsto jx$ in stereographic coordinates. They have critical
  energy $16V$ and tend smoothly to the projection pole off $x=0$.
  Bounded fourth energy and Hölder's inequality make the squared
  derivative integral over a shrinking neighborhood of $0$ uniformly
  small. Thus the limit is constant and convergence is strong
  $W^{1,2}$. All signed volume $V$ and energy $16V$ concentrate at $0$.
  This verifies sharpness for the general fixed-metric statement.
- Energy can concentrate without signed Jacobian concentration. Take
  a smooth degree-one $F$ constant on a coordinate ball and a nonzero
  compactly supported smooth real function $\psi$ on the Euclidean unit
  ball. In normal coordinates centered inside the constant region,
  replace $F=p$ by a unit-speed great-circle arc evaluated at
  $a\psi(x/\epsilon_j)$, starting at $p$. The perturbation has rank
  at most one, and the signed Jacobian is unchanged. Its energy
  concentrates with mass $a^4\int_{\mathbb R^4}|d\psi|^4$ at the
  center, while the squared derivative integral is $O(\epsilon_j^2)$.
  This realizes an arbitrary positive energy atom with $\eta=0$.
- Signed multiplicities can cancel. Insert oppositely oriented,
  compactly supported degree-one bubbles in disjoint shrinking
  balls converging to the same point in the constant region of
  $F$. Straight rescaling of the local degree insertion in [E15][e15]
  gives bounded fourth energy and strong $W^{1,2}$ convergence.
  The two signed measures cancel in the limit, although each
  shrinking ball carries absolute Jacobian mass at least $V$.
- The strict cap itself also permits concentration of absolute
  Jacobians. In the same constant region, replace $F=p$ by
  $\exp_p(a\,u(x/\epsilon_j))$, identifying $T_pS^4$ with $\mathbb R^4$
  and taking $u(y)=\chi(y)y$ for a smooth compactly supported cutoff
  $\chi$ equal to one near zero. For each fixed small $a>0$, the image
  lies in a target coordinate ball and the perturbation is homotopic
  to the constant relative to its boundary. Its signed integral is
  zero, but its rescaled absolute Jacobian integral is positive
  because the map has full rank near zero. Its fourth energy is
  $O(a^4)$ uniformly in $j$, so one can enforce (4) while the
  absolute Jacobian mass fails uniform integrability.

## Primary comparison and inputs

Robert Hardt and Tristan Rivière, *Connecting rational homotopy type
singularities*, Acta Math. 200 (2008), 15–83, DOI
`10.1007/s11511-008-0023-6`, already prove the corresponding
concentration result for a sphere domain under weak $W^{1,p}$
convergence. With $p=4$, target $S^4$, and the degree homomorphism
on $\pi_4(S^4)$, their graph consists of one vertex carrying
$\omega/V$, so the forms in Proposition 3.4(i) are normalized
Jacobian forms and the coefficients are integer degrees.
Part (iii) gives an energy lower bound with a positive
graph-dependent constant. Their proof uses bounded boundary slices
and extensions in the discussion of (3.22)–(3.25). The proof here
uses the stronger supplied convergence and smooth limit to obtain
the integer on each good geodesic ball by direct collar gluing.
The sharp coefficient in (1) follows from (2).
