**Result.** Gluing the punctured core of $(M,g_0)$ to a round chamber across a
conformally flat neck returns the conformal manifold $(M,[g_0])$ for every
neck size and every relative scale. The Yamabe constant, the minimizing
metrics and their relative pinching are those of $[g_0]$. In a minimizing
metric the chamber occupies a region of volume $O(\rho^4)$ and every function
that is constant on the core has Rayleigh quotient at least $c/\rho^2$, where
$\rho$ is the radius of the removed ball. Separately, changing a conformal
class inside a ball of radius $r$ raises its Yamabe constant by at most
$O(r/\log^2(1/r))$.

## The gluing

Let $(M,g_0)$ be a smooth closed Riemannian four-manifold that is flat on a
coordinate ball $\{|y|<2\rho\}$ centred at $p$. [E26][e26] supplies such a
metric on every smooth homotopy four-sphere; nothing else about $g_0$ is used
here, and the argument is the same in every dimension at least three. Let
$z$ be the stereographic coordinate of the unit round sphere centred at a
point $q$, so that $g_{\rm rd}=4|dz|^2/(1+|z|^2)^2$ and $z=\infty$ is the
antipode $q^*$. Fix $a>0$ and a reflection $R$ of $\mathbb R^4$, and put

\[
 \iota(y)=\frac{2a\rho\,Ry}{|y|^2},\qquad
 M_{\rho,a}=\bigl(M\setminus\{|y|\le\rho\}\bigr)\ \cup_{\iota}\
            \bigl(S^4\setminus\{|z|\le a\}\bigr),
\]

where $\iota$ identifies the collar $\{\rho<|y|<2\rho\}$ with the collar
$\{a<|z|<2a\}$. The map $\iota$ is an inversion composed with a reflection
and a dilation. It preserves orientation, exchanges the two boundary spheres
of the collar, and is conformal between the flat metrics $|dy|^2$ and
$|dz|^2$. Since $g_0=|dy|^2$ and $g_{\rm rd}$ is a positive multiple of
$|dz|^2$ on the collars, the classes $[g_0]$ and $[g_{\rm rd}]$ agree under
the identification and define a conformal class $C_{\rho,a}$ on
$M_{\rho,a}$. A partition of unity applied to the two conformal factors gives
smooth representatives that are homothetic to $g_0$ on the core, round on the
chamber and conformally flat on the neck. The parameter $a$ sets the size of
the chamber's hole, and the constant factor on the core sets the relative
scale.

**Proposition 1.** Define $\Psi:M\to M_{\rho,a}$ as the identity on
$M\setminus\{|y|\le\rho\}$, as $y\mapsto z=\iota(y)$ on $\{0<|y|<2\rho\}$,
and by $\Psi(p)=q^*$. Then $\Psi$ is a diffeomorphism and
$\Psi^*C_{\rho,a}=[g_0]$.

*Proof.* On the collar the two definitions give points that the gluing
identifies, so $\Psi$ is well defined. The formula $\iota$ maps
$\{0<|y|<2\rho\}$ diffeomorphically onto $\{|z|>a\}$. In the chart
$w=z/|z|^2$ at $q^*$ it reads $w=Ry/(2a\rho)$, which is linear, so the
extension over $p$ is smooth with smooth inverse. The two pieces of
$M_{\rho,a}$ are the images of $M\setminus\{|y|\le\rho\}$ and of
$\{|y|<2\rho\}$, which gives bijectivity. Finally $\Psi$ is the identity on
the first piece, and on the second it is conformal from $|dy|^2$ to
$|dz|^2$, hence to $g_{\rm rd}$. $\square$

The class does not depend on $a$, on the scale given to the core, or on the
interpolation in the neck. Different choices are different representatives of
$[g_0]$, pulled back by $\Psi$.

## What the minimizing representative does

**Corollary 2.** For all $\rho$ and $a$:

1. $Y(C_{\rho,a})=Y([g_0])$, and $\Psi$ carries the unit-volume Yamabe
   minimizers of $[g_0]$ isometrically onto those of $C_{\rho,a}$. Their
   scalar curvature, Laplace spectrum, five-eigenvalue gap $A$ and relative
   pinching $\epsilon=3\lambda_5/s-1$ do not depend on $\rho$ or $a$.
2. If $[g_0]$ is not the round class, then $Y([g_0])<Y(S^4)$ by the
   Aubin–Schoen inequality, and every minimizer has $A>0$ by the
   five-eigenfunction theorem of [E38][e38]. No choice in the gluing changes
   these numbers.
3. Let $g=u^2g_0$ be a unit-volume minimizer of $[g_0]$, with
   $0<u_-\le u\le u_+$ on $\{|y|\le2\rho_0\}$. For $\rho\le\rho_0$ the
   chamber and neck correspond under $\Psi$ to the ball $\{|y|<2\rho\}$, whose
   $g$-volume is at most $8\pi^2u_+^4\rho^4$.
4. Let $\phi$ be a Lipschitz function on $M$ that is constant on
   $M\setminus\{|y|<2\rho\}$ and not constant. Its Rayleigh quotient in the
   minimizing metric satisfies
   \[
    \frac{\int_M|d\phi|_g^2\,dV_g}{\int_M(\phi-\bar\phi)^2\,dV_g}
    \ \ge\ \frac{u_-^2}{u_+^4}\,\frac{j_{1,1}^2}{4\rho^2},
    \qquad j_{1,1}\approx3.8317 .
   \]

*Proof.* The Yamabe constant and the set of minimizers are defined by the
class, and $\Psi$ is a conformal diffeomorphism, which gives item 1. Item 2
quotes the two theorems. Item 3 is the Euclidean volume $\pi^2(2\rho)^4/2$
times $u_+^4$. For item 4 let $c$ be the constant value on the core. The mean
minimizes the $L^2$ distance to constants, so
$\int(\phi-\bar\phi)^2dV_g\le\int(\phi-c)^2u^4\,dy\le u_+^4\int_{|y|<2\rho}(\phi-c)^2dy$.
The function $\phi-c$ vanishes on the boundary of the ball. The first
Dirichlet eigenvalue of a Euclidean four-ball of radius $2\rho$ is
$j_{1,1}^2/(4\rho^2)$, where $j_{1,1}$ is the first positive zero of the
Bessel function $J_1$. In dimension four
$|d\phi|_g^2\,dV_g=u^2|d\phi|^2dy\ge u_-^2|d\phi|^2dy$. $\square$

The handoff asked for the two effects of rescaling the cap to be separated.
The conformal quotient does not change at all: a test function supported in
the core has the same Yamabe quotient at every scale, and the infimum of such
quotients is $Y([g_0])$, because removing a point does not change the Yamabe
constant. The $L^2$ mass does change. The five round coordinate functions of
the chamber have Rayleigh quotients close to the round value in the glued
representative. In the minimizing representative the same five functions, or
any other functions that are constant on the core, lie at frequency at least
$c/\rho^2$ by item 4, and the chamber carries volume $O(\rho^4)$ by item 3.
A family of metrics whose five chamber Rayleigh quotients are good therefore
says nothing about $3\lambda_5/s$ on the minimizers. This is the failure the
handoff asked to preserve, and here it is exact.

## A ball cannot raise the Yamabe constant

The next statement does not need the neck to be conformally flat.

**Proposition 3.** Let $g$ be a unit-volume Yamabe minimizer of its class on
a closed four-manifold, with $s_g=Y([g])\ge0$. Let $\kappa$ be such that
geodesic spheres and balls of radius $d\le R_0$ about $p$ satisfy
$|\partial B_d|\le2\pi^2d^3(1+\kappa d^2)$ and
$|B_d|\le\tfrac12\pi^2d^4(1+\kappa d^2)$. Let $C$ be any conformal class on
$M$ with a representative that equals $g$ outside $B_r(p)$, where
$r\le R_0^2$. Then

\[
 Y(C)\ \le\ \frac{Y([g])+24\pi^2(1+\kappa r)\,r/\log^2(1/r)}
                {\bigl(1-\tfrac12\pi^2r^2(1+\kappa r)\bigr)^{1/2}} .
\]

*Proof.* Put $R=\sqrt r$ and let $\chi$ be $0$ on $B_r$, $1$ outside $B_R$,
and $\log(d/r)/\log(R/r)$ in between, where $d$ is the $g$-distance to $p$.
The support of $\chi$ lies where the representative equals $g$, so its Yamabe
quotient in $C$ is the one computed with $g$. Its gradient energy is
\[
 \int|d\chi|^2\,dV_g\le\frac{1}{\log^2(R/r)}\int_r^R\frac{2\pi^2d^3(1+\kappa d^2)}{d^2}\,dd
 \le\frac{\pi^2R^2(1+\kappa R^2)}{\log^2(R/r)}
 =\frac{4\pi^2(1+\kappa r)\,r}{\log^2(1/r)} .
\]
Also $\int s_g\chi^2\le Y([g])$ and $\|\chi\|_4^2\ge(1-|B_R|)^{1/2}$. The
Yamabe quotient $\bigl(6\int|d\chi|^2+\int s_g\chi^2\bigr)/\|\chi\|_4^2$ is an
upper bound for $Y(C)$. $\square$

For $Y([g])<0$ the same test function gives
$Y(C)\le Y([g])(1-|B_R|)+24\pi^2(1+\kappa r)r/\log^2(1/r)$.

Consequently a sequence of classes on $M$ that agree with one fixed non-round
class outside balls shrinking to a point has
$\limsup Y\le Y([g_0])<Y(S^4)$. The cut-off minimizer of the core is the
competing test function of the handoff, and it wins by a fixed margin however
the inside of the small ball is filled. The sibling investigation records the
matching statement in the other direction, that a flat germ can be installed
with an arbitrarily small change of the Yamabe constant
([SEC-E25](../../01_sections_and_connections/notes/SEC_E25_20261124.md)).

## Scope

The class is unchanged only for the gluing above: flat germ, round chamber,
inversion on a conformally flat collar. A neck that is not conformally flat,
or a core metric that is changed on a set carrying a definite part of the
minimizer's $L^4$ mass, gives a different class, and Proposition 3 then says
only that the change cannot be confined to a small ball. Neither statement
produces or excludes a class on an unknown $M$ whose Yamabe constant is close
to $Y(S^4)$. The compactness of the set of minimizers in a non-round class is
not used.
