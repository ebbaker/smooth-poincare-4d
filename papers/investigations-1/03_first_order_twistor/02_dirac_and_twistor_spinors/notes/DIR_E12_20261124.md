## Setting and the two-form contraction

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth oriented Riemannian spin four-manifold,
$\psi\in\Gamma(S^+)$ smooth, and $T_X=P_X\psi=\nabla_X\psi+\tfrac14c(X)D\psi$.
The Hermitian spinor metric and $c(X)^2=-|X|_g^2$ are fixed. Real
self-dual two-forms act on $S^+$, and their action on $S^-$ vanishes.
The two-form action is $c(e^{ij})=c(e_i)c(e_j)$ for $i<j$, with $e^{ij}$
an orthonormal wedge basis. The superscript $+$ on $(d^\nabla T)^+$ is
the orthogonal Hodge projection in the base two-form slot.

In an oriented orthonormal coframe take the orthonormal self-dual basis

\[
 \omega_1=\frac{e^{12}+e^{34}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \omega_2=\frac{e^{13}-e^{24}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \omega_3=\frac{e^{14}+e^{23}}{\sqrt2}.
\]

Write $c_i=c(e_i)$ and $C_a=c(\omega_a)|_{S^+}$. The volume product
$c_1c_2c_3c_4$ acts as $-I$ on $S^+$ and $+I$ on $S^-$. Indeed
$c(*e^{1k})=-c_1c_k(c_1c_2c_3c_4)$ for $k=2,3,4$, so $c(\omega_a)$
acts as $\sqrt2 c_1c_{a+1}$ on $S^+$ and vanishes on $S^-$. Each
$C_a$ is skew-Hermitian, and the Clifford relations give

\[
 C_aC_b+C_bC_a=-4\delta_{ab}I,qquad
 c(\omega)^*c(\omega)|_{S^+}=2|\omega|^2I\quad(\omega\in\Lambda^+\text{ real}).
\]

Thus for $m(\sum_a\omega_a\otimes u_a)=\sum_a C_a u_a$, the adjoint is
$m^*v=-\sum_a\omega_a\otimes C_a v$, with $mm^*=6I$. The projection
in the request is $\Pi=I-m^*m/6$. The kernel of $m$ is orthogonal
to $\operatorname{im}m^*$.

## Exterior derivative and its Weyl term

Use $R(X,Y)=[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}$ and define the curvature
operator $\mathcal R$ by
$\langle\mathcal R(X\wedge Y),Z\wedge U\rangle=\langle R(X,Y)U,Z\rangle$.
It is positive for positive sectional curvature on a space form, and
$\mathcal R_{++}=W^++(s_g/12)I$. In these conventions the spin curvature is

\[
 R^S(X,Y)=\tfrac14\sum_{j,k}\langle R(X,Y)e_j,e_k\rangle c_jc_k
         =-\tfrac12 c(\mathcal R(X\wedge Y)).
\]

The first equality is Bär's formula for the Levi–Civita spin
connection. The second counts both ordered pairs for each $j<k$ and
converts the sign to the positive curvature-operator convention.
Bär's round eqs. (4)–(5) give $R^S(e_i,e_j)=-c_ic_j/2$ at curvature
one, consistent with this conversion. [Bär, §2, equations (3)–(5),
published p. 71](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jmath1948/48/1/48_1_69/_pdf#page=3).

Put $\xi=D\psi\in\Gamma(S^-)$. In a normal frame the exterior covariant
derivative has coefficients

\[
 (d^\nabla T)_{ij}=R^S(e_i,e_j)\psi
     +\tfrac14(c_j\nabla_i\xi-c_i\nabla_j\xi).
\]

For any real two-form $\omega$, Clifford multiplication satisfies
$[c(\omega),c_i]=2c(\iota_{e_i}\omega)$, as follows on each wedge basis
element. Consequently the second summand, evaluated on $\omega_a$, is

\[
 \tfrac14\sum_i c(\iota_{e_i}\omega_a)\nabla_i\xi
 =\tfrac18\sum_i[c(\omega_a),c_i]\nabla_i\xi
 =\tfrac18 C_a D\xi.
\]

Here $\nabla_i\xi$ is negative-chiral, so $c(\omega_a)\nabla_i\xi=0$.
The resulting term in $\Lambda^+\otimes S^+$ is $-m^*(D^2\psi)/8$.

For the remaining curvature term define

\[
 \mathcal W(\psi)=\sum_a\omega_a\otimes c(W^+\omega_a)\psi.
\]

The base-form projection restricts $\mathcal R$ to $\Lambda^+$ in the
input. The output component of $\mathcal R$ into $\Lambda^-$ acts
trivially on $\psi$, so the off-diagonal Ricci block drops out. The
curvature term is therefore $-\mathcal W(\psi)/2+(s_g/24)m^*\psi$.
If $W^+_{ba}=\langle W^+\omega_a,\omega_b\rangle$, then

\[
 m\mathcal W(\psi)=\sum_{a,b}W^+_{ba} C_a C_b\psi
                   =-2\operatorname{tr}(W^+)\psi=0
\]

by symmetry of the Weyl matrix and the Clifford anticommutator. This
gives the signed formula and its norm in the wedge normalization:

\[
 \boxed{\ \Pi(d^\nabla T)^+=-\tfrac12\mathcal W(\psi),\qquad
 |\Pi(d^\nabla T)^+|^2=\tfrac12|W^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2|\psi|^2.\ }
\]

For the norm, sum $|c(W^+\omega_a)\psi|^2=2|W^+\omega_a|^2|\psi|^2$
over the orthonormal $\omega_a$ and include the squared coefficient
$1/4$. An opposite curvature-operator sign changes the signed Weyl
formula accordingly and gives the same norm coefficient.

## The component tested by the adjoint

The complete self-dual derivative can be written using the formal
adjoint expression $P^*T=P^*P\psi=(3/4)D^2\psi-(s_g/4)\psi$ from
[DIR-E01, equation (2)](DIR_E01_20261124.md#4-fixed-metric-energy-and-principal-symbol):

\[
 (d^\nabla T)^+
   =-\tfrac12\mathcal W(\psi)+m^*\big(\tfrac{s_g}{24}\psi-\tfrac18D^2\psi\big)
   =-\tfrac12\mathcal W(\psi)-\tfrac16m^*(P^*T).
\]

In particular $m((d^\nabla T)^+)=-P^*T$. The adjoint expression measures
the component in $\operatorname{im}m^*$, orthogonal to the component
in $\ker m$ that carries $W^+$. As a direct check, $T$ has zero
one-form Clifford trace. At a normal frame, projecting to $\Lambda^+$
before Clifford contraction leaves that contraction unchanged on $S^+$.
Then

\[
 m((d^\nabla T)^+)=\sum_{i\ne j}c_i c_j\nabla_i T_j
                  =\sum_i\nabla_i T_i=-\nabla^*T.
\]

The full sum over $i,j$ vanishes by differentiating the zero trace,
and the diagonal has $c_i^2=-I$. Since $T$ is Clifford-traceless,
$P^*T=\nabla^*T$. On the round $K$ model the two scalar
terms above cancel for the chiral Killing projections since $s_g=12K$
and $D^2\psi=4K\psi$.

## Zeros and the recognition threshold

The pointwise identity holds at every zero, with both sides zero
before division. If $\psi$ has isolated transverse zeros, the ratio
on their complement has the smooth extension $|W^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2/2$.
Locally transversality means that the derivative of the section at
the zero is a real isomorphism. It implies $|\psi|$ is comparable to
distance from the zero, but the smooth extension follows from the
identity without a separate estimate of the vanishing rate.

If $M$ is a closed homotopy four-sphere, its isolated zero set
$Z_\psi$ is finite and has volume zero. The formula, including the
case $Z_\psi=\varnothing$, gives

\[
 \int_{M\setminus Z_\psi}\frac{|\Pi(d^\nabla T)^+|^2}{|\psi|^2}\,dV_g
 =\tfrac12 E_+(g).
\]

For such a supplied pair, [SH-E01](../../notes/SH_E01_20261124.md#supplied-metric-satisfying-strict-weyl-and-yamabe-bounds)
applies if the integral is strictly less than $2\pi^2$ and the same
conformal class has $Y(M,[g])>0$. The threshold follows from
$E_+(g)<4\pi^2$ with the operator Hilbert–Schmidt convention. The
identity expresses exactly which derivative a construction would
have to estimate. It supplies no bound on that derivative along a
produced family from the first-order error or from its adjoint alone.
