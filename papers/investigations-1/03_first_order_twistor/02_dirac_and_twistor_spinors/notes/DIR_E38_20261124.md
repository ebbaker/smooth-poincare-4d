## Assumptions and multiplicity bound

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected Riemannian four-manifold
of volume one. Use $\Delta_g=d^*d$, let $\lambda>0$ be its first
positive scalar eigenvalue, and denote the real first eigenspace
by $E_1$. Assume for every $\phi\in E_1$ that
\[
 \int_M \phi^4\,dV_g\le \frac{15}{7}\left(\int_M\phi^2\,dV_g\right)^2 .
 \tag{1}
\]
Assume also that every nonzero equality direction satisfies
\[
 \Delta_g\bigl(\phi^2-\|\phi\|_2^2\bigr)
   =\frac{5\lambda}{2}\bigl(\phi^2-\|\phi\|_2^2\bigr).
 \tag{2}
\]
These are the inputs of this packet. The multiplicity argument starts
from $E_1$ alone and uses neither an initial eigenmap nor a Yamabe
minimizing condition.

Set $d=\dim_{\mathbb R}E_1$ and choose a real $L^2(g)$-orthonormal
basis $u_1,\ldots,u_d$. Write $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_d)$ and $S=|u|^2$.
Then $\int_M S\,dV_g=d$. For a coefficient vector $c$ chosen with
uniform probability on $S^{d-1}$, $\phi_c=c\cdot u$ has
$\|\phi_c\|_2=1$. Orthogonal invariance gives
\[
 \mathbb E_c (c\cdot z)^4=\frac{3}{d(d+2)}|z|^4
 \qquad(z\in\mathbb R^d).
\]
For $d\ge2$, let $a=\mathbb E c_1^4$ and
$b=\mathbb E c_1^2c_2^2$. Rotating the first axis to
$(e_1+e_2)/\sqrt2$ gives $4a=2a+6b$, since sign symmetry cancels the
odd terms. Then $1=\mathbb E(\sum_i c_i^2)^2=da+d(d-1)b$
gives the stated constant. The same formula is immediate on $S^0$
when $d=1$.

Averaging (1), interchanging the two integrals and using volume one,
\[
 \frac{3d}{d+2}
 \ \le\ \frac{3}{d(d+2)}\int_M S^2\,dV_g
 \ =\ \mathbb E_c\int_M\phi_c^4\,dV_g
 \ \le\ \frac{15}{7}.
\]
The first inequality is Cauchy–Schwarz. Rearranging gives $d\le5$,
using only (1).

## Equality and the normalized basis map

If $d=5$, the entire chain is equality. In particular
$\int_M(S-5)^2\,dV_g=0$, hence $S=5$ everywhere by smoothness.
The continuous nonnegative function
$c\mapsto 15/7-\int_M\phi_c^4\,dV_g$ has integral zero in a measure
of full support, so every unit coefficient direction saturates (1).
Homogeneity gives equality for all of $E_1$, and (2) applies to
every nonzero $\phi$. Since $\Delta_g\phi=\lambda\phi$, the product
rule gives
\[
 2\lambda\phi^2-2|d\phi|_g^2
   =\Delta_g(\phi^2-\|\phi\|_2^2)
   =\frac{5\lambda}{2}(\phi^2-\|\phi\|_2^2).
\]
Thus $|d\phi|_g^2=(\lambda/4)(5\|\phi\|_2^2-\phi^2)$.
Polarization yields the pointwise identity
\[
 \langle d\phi,d\psi\rangle_g
     =\frac{\lambda}{4}(5\langle\phi,\psi\rangle_{L^2(g)}-\phi\psi),
 \qquad
 \langle du_i,du_j\rangle_g
     =\frac{\lambda}{4}(5\delta_{ij}-u_i u_j).
 \tag{3}
\]
This also covers linear combinations that vanish identically.

Let $U=u/\sqrt5:M\to S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ and let $h$ be the
unit round metric. For the pointwise adjoint
$dU^*:\mathbb R^5\to T_xM$ defined by $g$ and the Euclidean inner
product, (3) reads
\[
 dU\,dU^*=\frac{\lambda}{4}(I_5-UU^\top).
\]
The factor $I_5-UU^\top$ is the orthogonal projection onto
$T_{U(x)}S^4$. Thus $dU$ has rank four, and on that four-dimensional
image its four positive singular values are all $\sqrt{\lambda/4}$.
In particular $U^*h=(\lambda/4)g$. This gives a local diffeomorphism.
The image is open by local invertibility and closed by compactness,
so it is the entire connected sphere. A proper local diffeomorphism
is a covering, and the connected domain and simply connected target
make this one a diffeomorphism. In particular $M$ is orientable
without an orientation hypothesis. If orientations are chosen,
$\deg U=\pm1$. Writing $V=\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)$, volume one
gives $V=(\lambda/4)^2$. Hence $\lambda=4V^{1/2}$ and
$g=V^{-1/2}U^*h$.

## All first eigenmaps into the unit four-sphere

Let $F:M\to S^4$ be any smooth map whose five coordinates belong
to $E_1$. The condition that $F(M)$ spans $\mathbb R^5$ is
equivalent to the five coordinates being linearly independent as
functions. This gives $d\ge5$, and (1) then forces $d=5$. For this
dimension, every such eigenmap, with any span a priori, can be
written $F=AU$ for a real $5\times5$ matrix $A$. The sphere condition
says $U(x)^\top A^\top A U(x)=1$ for all $x$. Since the covering
argument already gave $U(M)=S^4$, the quadratic form of $A^\top A$
equals one on the entire unit sphere. Homogeneity extends the
identity to $z^\top A^\top A z=|z|^2$ for every $z\in\mathbb R^5$,
and polarization gives $A^\top A=I_5$. Consequently all
first eigenmaps into $S^4$ for $d=5$ are the orthogonal
postcompositions of $U$. They are surjective homothetic
diffeomorphisms. With domain and target oriented,
$\deg F=(\det A)\deg U\in\{+1,-1\}$.

More generally the dimension of $\operatorname{span}F(M)$ is the
rank of the map $\mathbb R^5\to E_1$ sending $c$ to $c\cdot F$.
A proper-span eigenmap lies in a proper great subsphere, is
nonsurjective, and has degree zero when orientations are given,
since it omits a point. Such a map under (1)–(2) entails $d\le4$.
Full span gives the algebraic multiplicity input; the
surjectivity conclusion uses the separate differential and
covering argument.

## Round calibration and scope for E14 limits

For the volume-one round model $g=V^{-1/2}h$, set $q=V^{1/2}$.
The five coordinate functions on $S^4$ have eigenvalue $4q$.
For any positive eigenvalue $\mu$ the integrated Bochner identity,
$\operatorname{Ric}_g=3qg$ and
$|\nabla^2 f|^2\ge(\Delta_g f)^2/4$ imply $\mu\ge4q$.
At $\mu=4q$ equality gives $\nabla^2 f=-q f g$, which determines
$f$ by its value and gradient at a single point along geodesics.
Thus the five coordinate functions form the entire first eigenspace.
For $\phi(x)=c\cdot x$, the coefficient sphere calculation gives
$\|\phi\|_2^2=|c|^2/5$ and $\int\phi^4\,dV_g=3|c|^4/35$.
Also $|d\phi|_g^2=q(|c|^2-\phi^2)$. The product rule now gives
$\Delta_g(\phi^2-\|\phi\|_2^2)=10q(\phi^2-\|\phi\|_2^2)$,
which is (2) since $\lambda=4q$. The round model therefore meets
both premises and attains the multiplicity bound. [E17 round calibration][e17].

For the supplied smooth unit-volume Yamabe minimizer on a
homotopy four-sphere, [E14][e14] obtains a smooth first-eigenmap
limit from a balanced degree-$+1$ near-zero sequence. If that
metric meets (1)–(2), a full-span limit recognizes the round
metric and the standard smooth sphere, with degree $+1$ or $-1$.
A degree-zero E14 limit in this setting must have proper span.
That alternative has $d\le4$ and so requires a nonround supplied
metric. If another argument recognizes that same metric as a
scaled pullback of the round metric, its eigenspace has dimension
five and proper span is excluded. Recognition of the smooth
manifold alone is compatible with a different round metric and
a nonround supplied metric. The existence or exclusion of a
proper-span limit on a nonround metric remains open here.
