On the DIR-E02 family, every sequence of normalized positive half-spinors
with twistor residual tending to zero loses all mass outside the magnified
ball in the fixed reference coordinates. If the base metric has positive
self-dual Weyl energy, that family admits no positive $C^2$ subsequential
metric limit even after oriented spin diffeomorphisms.

## 1. Mass estimate in the reference coordinates

Keep the smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian spin four-manifold
$(M,g)$ and center $p$ fixed. Assume the positive-chiral kernel of $P_g$
is zero. Use $S^+$ on which real self-dual two-forms act and
$c(X)^2=-|X|^2$. In the [DIR-E02 family](DIR_E02_20261124.md) the balls
$B_t$ are defined by the reference metric. The conformal multiplier
$u_r=a_r f_r$ has $f_r=r^{-1}$ on $B_r$, $f_r=1$ off $B_{2r}$ and
$1\le f_r\le r^{-1}$. We use $a_-\le a_r\le a_+$ with constants
$a_->0$ and $a_+<\infty$. Then $h_r=u_r^2g$ has unit volume.

Let $\Psi_r$ be any smooth positive half-spinor with
$\|\Psi_r\|_{L^2(h_r)}=1$ and put $\epsilon_r=\|P_{h_r}\Psi_r\|_{L^2(h_r)}$.
With the pointwise isometric conformal bundle map
$\beta_r:S_g^+\to S_{h_r}^+$, define
$v_r=u_r^{-1/2}\beta_r^{-1}\Psi_r$. The full operator covariance
checked in DIR-E02 yields

\[
  1=\int_M u_r^5|v_r|_g^2\,dV_g,\qquad
  \epsilon_r^2=\int_M u_r^3|P_gv_r|_g^2\,dV_g .
\]

The uniform lower bound $u_r\ge a_-$ gives
$\|P_gv_r\|_{L^2(g)}\le a_-^{-3/2}\epsilon_r$.
In [DIR-E01](DIR_E01_20261124.md), the compact-resolvent realization
of $P_g^*P_g$ has least eigenvalue $\mu(g)>0$ when this kernel is
zero. Hence $\|v_r\|_{L^2(g)}^2\le\mu(g)^{-1}\|P_gv_r\|_{L^2(g)}^2$.
The first-order elliptic estimate with injective symbol, or the
integrated identity

\[
  \|P_gv_r\|_2^2=\tfrac34\|\nabla^g v_r\|_2^2
                          -\tfrac1{16}\int_M s_g|v_r|^2\,dV_g,
\]

improves this to $\|v_r\|_{H^1(g)}\le C\epsilon_r$. Define the physical
spinor probability measure by $d\nu_r=|\Psi_r|_{h_r}^2\,dV_{h_r}$.
Since $u_r=a_r$ off $B_{2r}$,

\[
  \nu_r(M\setminus B_{2r})
  =a_r^5\int_{M\setminus B_{2r}}|v_r|_g^2\,dV_g
  \le \frac{a_+^5}{a_-^3\mu(g)}\,\epsilon_r^2 . \tag{1}
\]

For an actual lowest eigensection of $P_{h_r}^*P_{h_r}$,
$\epsilon_r^2=\mu(h_r)\le Cr^3$. If $\epsilon_r\to0$, (1) implies
$\nu_r\rightharpoonup\delta_p$. For a continuous test function, the
absolute error from its value at $p$ is bounded by its oscillation
from that value on $B_{2r}$ plus twice its supremum norm times the
right side of (1). No metric compactness enters these estimates.

On $B_r$ the unweighted base mass is related to the physical mass
by $\int_{B_r}|v_r|_g^2\,dV_g=a_r^{-5}r^5\nu_r(B_r)\le Cr^5$.
The annulus $B_{2r}\setminus B_r$ can carry the concentration measured
by (1). In fact a positive lower bound for $\nu_r(B_r)$ for **all**
almost-solutions would fail under the allowed E02 profiles. To see
this, choose a smooth radial $f_r=1+(r^{-1}-1)\eta(\rho/r)$ with
$0\le\eta\le1$, $\eta=1$ for $t\le1$ and on $[4/3,5/3]$, and
$\eta=0$ on $[9/8,5/4]$ and for $t\ge7/4$. Let $v_r$ before
normalization be the radial-parallel unit field of E02 multiplied by
a smooth radial cutoff of $\rho/r$. Choose the cutoff supported in
$(9/8,15/8)$, equal to one on $[5/4,7/4]$, with derivative supported
in $(9/8,5/4)\cup(7/4,15/8)$ where $f_r=1$. Its weighted squared
norm is at least $cr^{-1}$ from the second plateau. Its weighted
numerator is $O(r^2)$ from cutoff derivatives and $O(r^3)$ from the
radial field derivative $O(\rho)$, by the same normal-ball volume
bounds as E02. After normalization, the residual is $O(r^{3/2})$
and $\nu_r(B_r)=0$. This example is for arbitrary almost-solutions
under the permitted profiles. The physical $B_r$ mass of actual
lowest eigensections is not determined here.

## 2. Consequences of a hypothetical positive $C^2$ metric limit

Assume $E_+(g)=\int_M\|W_g^+\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV_g>0$, using
the Weyl block as an endomorphism of real self-dual two-forms.
$E_+(h_r)=E_+(g)$ because the Hodge star on two-forms is unchanged
by a positive conformal rescaling in dimension four, the Weyl
endomorphism scales by $u_r^{-2}$, and the volume scales by $u_r^4$.
An orientation-preserving diffeomorphism also preserves $E_+$.
It is continuous under $C^2$ convergence to a positive metric on
the compact base, since the tensor, norm and volume then converge
uniformly in fixed local frames. [Shared energy normalization](../../notes/SH_E01_20261124.md)

Suppose $k_j=F_j^*h_{r_j}\to k$ in $C^2$, with $r_j\to0$, $k$ positive
and $F_j$ oriented spin diffeomorphisms with chosen lifts. Pull back
the normalized lowest eigensections to $\psi_j\in\Gamma(S_{k_j}^+)$.
Their $L^2(k_j)$ norms are one and their twistor residuals tend to zero.
Using the integrated identity in §1 with $k_j$ in place of $g$ gives

\[
  \|\nabla^{k_j}\psi_j\|_{L^2(k_j)}^2
   \le\tfrac43\|P_{k_j}\psi_j\|_{L^2(k_j)}^2
       +\tfrac1{12}\|s_{k_j}\|_\infty .
\]

The scalar curvature bound follows from $C^2$ convergence. In spin
frames obtained from a smooth fixed background by the positive
symmetric comparison, the connection coefficients converge in $C^1$,
and the $H^1$ norms are uniformly equivalent by uniform metric
positivity and bounded coefficients. The spin lifts can be taken
on the same background spin structure. Rellich compactness yields
a subsequence with weak $H^1$ and strong $L^2$ limit $\psi$ in the fixed
bundle. The $L^2(k)$ norm is one, since the bundle comparisons are
pointwise isometries and the volume forms converge uniformly.
The coefficients of the first-order operators $P_{k_j}$ converge
uniformly to those of $P_k$, so their difference as maps $H^1\to L^2$
tends to zero. It follows from weak convergence and the vanishing
residuals that $P_k\psi=0$ weakly.

## 3. Weyl compatibility at $C^2$

Let $k$ be any positive $C^2$ metric in the same category and
$\psi\in H^1(S_k^+)$ a nontrivial weak twistor spinor. Both
$R_k$ and the Schouten endomorphism $A=(\operatorname{Ric}_k^\sharp
-s_kI/6)/2$ are continuous. Set $\chi=D_k\psi\in L^2(S_k^-)$.
The spin connection and all the leading coefficients of the relevant
first-order operators are at least $C^1$ in local frames. The
second-order identities used next act on $H^1$ fields in distributions.
They can be checked first on smooth fields and metrics, and passed to
the claimed regularity using smooth local approximations in $H^1$ for
the fields and in $C^2$ for the metric, with bundle identification as
in §2. Compositions of the first-order operators converge in
$H^{-1}_{\mathrm{loc}}$ on $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ fields, and curvature
and scalar multiplication converge on $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$ fields.
This uses only first derivatives of the connection.

In distributions $\nabla_X\psi=-c(X)\chi/4$. Taking the covariant
trace using Clifford compatibility gives
$\nabla^*\nabla\psi=D_k\chi/4$. Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz therefore gives
$D_k\chi=s_k\psi/3$. Commuting and contracting the original equation
gives

\[
 R^S(X,Y)\psi=-\tfrac14\big(c(Y)\nabla_X\chi-c(X)\nabla_Y\chi\big),
 \qquad
 -\tfrac12 c(\operatorname{Ric}_k^\sharp X)\psi
      =\tfrac14(2\nabla_X\chi-c(X)D_k\chi).
\]

The contraction is the same Clifford and Bianchi algebra as in
[DIR-E01, §2](DIR_E01_20261124.md). Thus
$\nabla_X\chi=-2c(A(X))\psi$ distributionally. In particular $\chi$
has weak first derivatives in $L^2_{\mathrm{loc}}$. The pair
$U=(\psi,\chi)$ is now in $H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and obeys
$\partial_i U=B_i U$ for continuous matrix functions $B_i$ in
local frames. In four dimensions the embedding
$H^1_{\mathrm{loc}}\subset L^4_{\mathrm{loc}}$ and the equation give
$U\in W^{1,4}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. On smaller coordinate balls, for
any finite $p>4$ one can use $W^{1,q}\subset L^p$ with $q<4$ close
enough to four. Applying the equation again yields
$U\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}$. Morrey's embedding makes $U$ continuous.
Its weak derivatives $B_iU$ are then continuous, so $U$ has a $C^1$
representative with these classical derivatives.

The spin curvature commutator above is consequently a continuous
identity. Substitution of $\nabla_X\chi=-2c(A(X))\psi$ cancels the
Schouten part of $R^S$ and gives $W^S(X,Y)\psi=0$. The real
$\Lambda^+$ action on $S^+$ is invertible for every nonzero
two-form in $\Lambda^+$, so $W_k^+=0$ wherever $\psi$ is nonzero.
The $C^1$ pair satisfies a linear parallel-transport ODE with
continuous coefficients along paths. Since $\psi$ is nontrivial,
uniqueness implies $\chi(x)\ne0$ at any zero $x$ of $\psi$.
In a local spin frame at such a zero, the derivative of $\psi$
is the injective real linear map $X\mapsto-c(X)\chi(x)/4$ with
norm $|X|_k|\chi(x)|/4$. The first-order expansion shows that
the zero is isolated. The nonzero locus is dense and $W_k^+=0$
everywhere by continuity. This argument derives the needed
$C^2$ version of the curvature compatibility. Friedrich supplies
the smooth equations and zero analysis. [Friedrich, pp. 59–63, equations (1.1), (2.1)–(2.2), Proposition 1, curvature remark and Proposition 2](https://dml.cz/dmlcz/701462)

Applied to the limit in §2, this forces $E_+(k)=0$, in contradiction
with $E_+(k)=E_+(g)>0$. The obstruction is invariant under the
specified diffeomorphisms and concerns this sequence. The argument
does not require the gauge-transformed mass to concentrate at a
specified point.

## 4. A base metric with positive Weyl energy on the standard sphere

In round normal coordinates at a point, replace the round metric by
$g_{\mathrm{rd}}+\delta b(x)(x^1)^2\,dx^2\otimes dx^2$, where $b=1$
near the origin and is supported in the coordinate ball. For small
nonzero real $\delta$ this is smooth and positive after extension
by the round metric. The perturbation has zero value and first jet
at the origin. Its only nonzero second derivative there among the
metric components is $\partial_1^2(g-g_{\mathrm{rd}})_{22}=2\delta$.
The normal-coordinate curvature formula therefore adds a nonzero
multiple $t$ of the rank-one curvature operator
$e^{12}\otimes e^{12}$ at that point. Its $\Lambda^+$ block is
$(t/2)\omega\otimes\omega$, where
$\omega=(e^{12}+e^{34})/\sqrt2$. Taking its trace-free part gives
eigenvalues $t/3,-t/6,-t/6$, so $W^+$ is nonzero there and
$E_+(g)>0$. The result of DIR-E01 then gives a zero positive twistor
kernel for this base metric.
