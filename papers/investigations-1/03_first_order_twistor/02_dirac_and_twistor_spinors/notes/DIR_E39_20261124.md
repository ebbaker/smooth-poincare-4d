The independent calculation gives
\[
\dim_{\mathbb R}\ker(\Delta_g-\lambda_1(g))\le 5
\]
on a supplied smooth, closed, connected Riemannian four-manifold
with a unit-volume Yamabe-minimizing metric \(g\) and \(s_g=3\lambda_1(g)\),
using \(\Delta_g=d^*d\). Equality forces \(g\) to be isometric to the
unit-volume round four-sphere. Five independent scalar first
eigenfunctions suffice under these hypotheses. The proof constructs
the recognizing map from them.

## Conformal test through fourth order

Write \(\lambda=\lambda_1(g)>0\) and \(s=3\lambda>0\). Set
\(\langle v\rangle=\int_M v\,dV_g\) and \(E_1=\ker(\Delta_g-\lambda)\)
on real functions. The four-dimensional Yamabe quotient at \(u>0\) is
\[
Y_g(u)=s\,\frac{A(u)}{B(u)^{1/2}},\qquad
A(u)=\langle u^2\rangle+\frac{2}{\lambda}\langle |du|^2\rangle,
\qquad B(u)=\langle u^4\rangle.
\]
Unit volume gives \(Y_g(1)=s\). Since \(A\) and \(B\) are positive,
the minimizing property is \(A(u)^2-B(u)\ge0\). Local minimality
among positive conformal factors also suffices for the following
tests. If one formulates the minimization at fixed volume, the scaled
factor \(B(u)^{-1/4}u\) gives the same quotient.

Fix any smooth mean-zero \(w\) and any \(\phi\in E_1\). Both signs of
\(t\) sufficiently near zero give \(u_t=1+t\phi+t^2w>0\).
Put \(m=\langle\phi^2\rangle\), \(q=\langle\phi^4\rangle\). The
mean-zero identities and \(\langle d\phi,dw\rangle_{L^2}=
\lambda\langle\phi w\rangle\) give the exact numerator
\[
A(u_t)=1+3mt^2+6\langle\phi w\rangle t^3
       +\left(\langle w^2\rangle+\frac2{\lambda}
          \langle|dw|^2\rangle\right)t^4 .
\]
The coefficients of \(B(u_t)\) in degrees two through four are
\(6m\), \(12\langle\phi w\rangle+4\langle\phi^3\rangle\), and
\(6\langle w^2\rangle+12\langle\phi^2w\rangle+q\).
Thus
\[
A(u_t)^2-B(u_t)
 =-4\langle\phi^3\rangle t^3+\mathcal Q_\phi(w)t^4+O(t^5),
\quad
\mathcal Q_\phi(w)=9m^2-q-12\langle\phi^2w\rangle
  +\frac4{\lambda}\langle |dw|^2\rangle-4\langle w^2\rangle .
\]
Nonnegativity for both signs gives \(\langle\phi^3\rangle=0\) and
\(\mathcal Q_\phi(w)\ge0\) for each fixed smooth mean-zero \(w\).
Polarization also gives \(\langle\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3\rangle=0\)
for \(\phi_1,\phi_2,\phi_3\in E_1\).

Take \(z=\phi^2-m\). Integration of \(\Delta_g\phi=\lambda\phi\)
against \(\phi^3\) gives \(3\langle\phi^2|d\phi|^2\rangle=\lambda q\).
Hence \(\langle |dz|^2\rangle=4\lambda q/3\) and
\(\langle\phi^2 z\rangle=\langle z^2\rangle=q-m^2\). It follows that
\[
0\le\mathcal Q_\phi(z)=25m^2-\frac{35}{3}q,\qquad
q\le\frac{15}{7}m^2 .
\tag{1}
\]
Equality makes \(z\) a minimizer of \(\mathcal Q_\phi\) among
mean-zero \(w\). Varying \(w=z+\varepsilon v\) for arbitrary smooth
mean-zero \(v\) gives
\[
0=\frac8{\lambda}\langle dz,dv\rangle_{L^2}
   -8\langle zv\rangle-12\langle\phi^2 v\rangle
 =\left\langle
    \left(\frac8{\lambda}\Delta_g z-20z\right)v
   \right\rangle.
\]
The factor paired with \(v\) is itself mean-zero and smooth, so
\(\Delta_g z=(5\lambda/2)z\). Conversely this equation implies
\(4\lambda q/3=(5\lambda/2)(q-m^2)\), the equality in (1).
The product rule gives the equivalent pointwise identity
\[
|d\phi|^2=\frac{\lambda}{4}(5m-\phi^2).
\tag{2}
\]

## Averaging and recognition

Let \((\phi_a)_{a=1}^d\) be a real \(L^2(g)\)-orthonormal basis of \(E_1\).
Write \(P=\sum_a\phi_a^2\), and average (1) over unit vectors
\(\xi\in S^{d-1}\subset\mathbb R^d\) with rotation-invariant probability
measure, using \(\phi=\sum_a\xi_a\phi_a\), for which \(m=1\). For any
vector \(X\in\mathbb R^d\), the spherical average of
\((\xi\cdot X)^4\) is \(3|X|^4/[d(d+2)]\). For \(d\ge2\), rotation
in a two-coordinate plane gives
\(\mathbb E\xi_1^4=3\mathbb E(\xi_1^2\xi_2^2)\), and the square of
\(\sum_a\xi_a^2=1\) fixes the coefficient. The same formula holds
directly for \(d=1\). Since \(\langle P\rangle=d\), it follows that
\[
\frac{3d}{d+2}
 \ \le\ \frac{3\langle P^2\rangle}{d(d+2)}
 \ \le\ \frac{15}{7},
\]
so \(d\le5\). At \(d=5\) equality in the first inequality gives
\(P=5\) everywhere by smoothness. Equality in the second forces
every direction to saturate (1), by continuity and full support of
the coefficient-sphere measure.

The map \(\Phi=(\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_5)/\sqrt5\) is then smooth and valued
in the unit round \(S^4\). Polarizing (2) gives
\[
\langle d\phi_a,d\phi_b\rangle_g
 =\frac{\lambda}{4}(5\delta_{ab}-\phi_a\phi_b).
\]
Equivalently \(d\Phi(d\Phi)^*=(\lambda/4)(I-\Phi\Phi^\top)\), the
positive multiple of orthogonal projection onto \(T_\Phi S^4\).
It follows that \(d\Phi:T_xM\to T_{\Phi(x)}S^4\) has rank four and
all four singular values are \(\sqrt{\lambda/4}\). Thus
\(\Phi^*h=(\lambda/4)g\), with \(h\) the unit round metric. A local
diffeomorphism from a compact source has open and closed image and
is a covering. Connectedness and simple connectivity of \(S^4\)
give a diffeomorphism. With \(V=\operatorname{Vol}_h S^4=8\pi^2/3\),
volume one forces \(g=V^{-1/2}\Phi^*h\),
\(\lambda=4V^{1/2}\), and \(s=12V^{1/2}\).
If an orientation is supplied on \(M\), the degree is \(+1\) or
\(-1\) according to the basis orientation.

This is sharp. In the unit-volume round scaling \(c h\) with
\(c=V^{-1/2}\), the five coordinate functions have eigenvalue \(4/c\).
The integrated Bochner identity with \(\operatorname{Ric}=3g/c\)
and the trace bound on the Hessian show \(\lambda_1\ge4/c\), so the
coordinates belong to \(E_1\). The round Yamabe comparison follows
by stereographic conformal covariance from Talenti's Euclidean
sharp \(m=4,p=2\) optimizer \((1+|x|^2)^{-1}\), which is the image
of a constant sphere test up to multiplication. [E17][e17] · [Talenti][talenti].

Whenever \(d=5\), any smooth first eigenmap \(F:M\to S^4\) has the
form \(F=L\Phi\) for a real \(5\times5\) matrix \(L\). Because \(\Phi\)
is onto, \(x^\top L^\top Lx=1\) on the unit sphere, so \(L^\top L=I\).
Thus every such eigenmap has full linear span and is a homothetic
diffeomorphism. Conversely, if a first eigenmap has full linear
span, its five coordinate functions are linearly independent in
\(E_1\), and the theorem applies without a prior degree hypothesis.
A proper-span eigenmap requires \(d\le4\) under these assumptions.
The existence of one on a nonround homotopy four-sphere remains
open in this calculation. For the balanced near-zero sequences of
[E14][e14], the supplied Yamabe minimizer and the spectral step give
\(s=3\lambda_1\) and a smooth first eigenmap. The theorem treats the
full-span case, or separately any case with \(d=5\). The existence
of the requisite spectral equality or such a sequence on an
arbitrary smooth homotopy four-sphere remains an additional premise.

## Primary literature comparison

Six targeted searches covered the CCR publication and erratum,
Obata's Hessian theorem, and combinations of Yamabe minimization,
first-eigenspace multiplicity, critical scalar curvature, and
fourth moments. The comparison below uses four primary works
and five inspected documents counting CCR's journal PDF and its
author preprint separately. The targeted results yielded a direct
antecedent for the cubic reduction and the classical Hessian
criterion, and no direct statement of (1) or of the sharp
five-dimensional rigidity at a Yamabe minimum. This is the result
of the bounded search.

- **Carlotto–Chodosh–Rubinstein, *Slowly converging Yamabe flows*.**
  Published *Geometry & Topology* **19** (2015), 1523–1568,
  DOI `10.2140/gt.2015.19.1523`. Inspected §2, pp. 1530–1533
  (linearization, Proposition 7, Definition 8, Lemma 9,
  Definition 10 and (4)), Proposition 13, p. 1534, §5.2.3
  Proposition 25 and proof, p. 1562, and Appendix A, pp. 1563–1564.
  In their convention \(\Delta_{\rm CCR}=-d^*d\). At a unit-volume
  constant-scalar-curvature metric on a smooth closed \(n\)-manifold,
  \(n\ge3\), the nullspace is that of
  \((n-1)\Delta_{\rm CCR}+s\). It equals the whole \(E_1\) in the
  supplied four-dimensional equality case. Proposition 7 constructs
  an analytic Lyapunov–Schmidt graph with zero first derivative of
  its correction and projected gradient zero in the orthogonal
  directions. Equation (4) and Appendix A identify the cubic
  derivative on the nullspace with the triple integral times
  \(-2(N-1)(N-2)s\), \(N=2n/(n-2)\). For \(n=4\) the third
  derivative is \(-12s\langle\phi^3\rangle\) in a single direction,
  agreeing with the coefficient \(-2s\langle\phi^3\rangle\) of
  \(t^3\) in \(Y_g(u_t)-s\) after dividing by \(3!\).
  The quartic above comes from testing the unreduced variational
  inequality with an explicit quadratic correction. Appendix A
  computes the cubic and discusses the dependence of higher
  reduced coefficients on more information about the reduction.
  The author preprint, arXiv:1401.3738v3, 26 February 2015,
  p. 7 (4) and p. 29 Appendix A, was also checked for notation
  and signs. [Journal PDF][ccr] · [Author preprint v3][ccr-preprint].
- **CCR, author erratum, 2 June 2016.** Inspected the single page.
  It replaces the nonintegrable exponent \(\theta=1/p\) in
  Proposition 13 by the existence of some \(\theta\in(0,1/p]\)
  for a nullspace of arbitrary positive dimension. The original
  asserted exponent applies in the one-dimensional case. The
  fourth-order test here uses neither exponent. [Author erratum][erratum].
- **A dimension detail in CCR's example.** Proposition 25 concerns
  the critical product \(S^1((n-2)^{-1/2})\times S^{n-1}(1)\) for
  \(n>2\). The product has \(s=(n-1)(n-2)\) and
  \(\lambda_1=n-2\), prior to unit-volume scaling. The proof's
  reference to a one-dimensional nullspace on p. 1562 should be
  read with care. On the full real eigenspace, the circle's sine
  and cosine at frequency \(\sqrt{n-2}\) are independent and the
  first sphere eigenvalue is \(n-1\), so the kernel is two-dimensional.
  Every combination of these circle modes has zero cubic integral.
  Nonzero kernel together with the unique critical point asserted
  by the period argument gives nonintegrability regardless of
  this dimension correction. The product case illustrates
  degeneracy at a nonround minimizer on its specified topology.
  For \(n=4\), the two modes give a sphere-valued eigenmap
  \((\cos(\sqrt2\,t),\sin(\sqrt2\,t),0,0,0)\) of span two on
  that product. Constant rescaling to volume one preserves
  the spectral equality and the eigenmap.
  [Journal PDF, Proposition 25 and proof][ccr-product].
- **Morio Obata, *Certain conditions for a Riemannian manifold to be
  isometric with a sphere*.** Inspected the published
  *J. Math. Soc. Japan* **14** (1962), 333–340, Theorem A on p. 334
  and Theorems 2 and 4–5 on p. 336.
  Theorem A recognizes a connected complete smooth Riemannian
  manifold of dimension \(n\ge2\) from a nonconstant smooth
  \(\phi\) satisfying \(\nabla^2\phi=-c^2\phi g\), with \(c>0\).
  Its sphere has radius \(1/c\). The pure scalar spectral criterion
  in the Einstein case uses the Einstein condition. Here the
  full Gram identity gives the homothety directly and implies the
  Obata equation with \(c^2=\lambda/4\) by pulling back a coordinate
  on the unit sphere. The sharp eigenspace bound above uses the
  Yamabe minimizing inequality and has no Einstein premise.
  [Obata][obata].
- **Giorgio Talenti, *Best constant in Sobolev inequality*.**
  Inspected the published *Ann. Mat. Pura Appl.* **110** (1976),
  353–372, p. 353, opening theorem and (1)–(3), for the Euclidean
  \(1<p<m\) sharp inequality and its optimizer. It supplies the
  round calibration at \(m=4,p=2\). [Talenti][talenti].
