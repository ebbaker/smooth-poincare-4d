## Supplied setting and the minimizing inequality

On the given smooth closed unit-volume Yamabe minimizer with constant
$s>0$, use $L=3\Delta_g/s$, $\Delta_g=d^*d$, and
$\Gamma(f,h)=(3/s)\langle df,dh\rangle_g$. For functions on $M$, all
integrals are taken with respect to $dV_g$. Set
$\|w\|_H^2=\int(w^2+\Gamma(w,w))$ and use its dual norm on $H^*$,
with the integral pairing for smooth functions. The real smooth $u_i$
for $1\le i\le5$ are orthonormal eigenfunctions of $L$ with eigenvalues
in $[1,1+\varepsilon]$. In each real unit direction
$\phi=\sum c_i u_i$, $|c|=1$, take as supplied
\[
 \|\phi\|_\infty\le C_0,\quad
 r=(L-1)\phi,\quad \|r\|_\infty\le C_0\varepsilon,\quad
 \left|\int \phi^4-\tfrac{15}{7}\right|\le C_0\sqrt\varepsilon.
\]
In particular $\int\phi=0$, $\int\phi^2=1$, and
$\delta=\int\Gamma(\phi,\phi)-1=\int\phi r$ lies in
$[0,\varepsilon]$ by orthonormality and the eigenvalue window.
Track $0<\varepsilon\le1$. Constants below depend only on the
supplied uniform bounds.

Dividing the positive-test Yamabe inequality by $s$ gives
\[
 \|u\|_4^2\le\int (u^2+2\Gamma(u,u)).
 \tag{1}
\]
For a real smooth $u$ of arbitrary sign, use the positive smooth
approximations $(u^2+\eta^2)^{1/2}$ to $|u|$. Their gradient squares
are bounded by $\Gamma(u,u)$, and as $\eta\downarrow0$ the left
side and the zeroth-order integrals converge to those in (1).
This gives the stated inequality for signed tests and
$\|u\|_4\le\sqrt2\|u\|_H$, hence the same embedding on the completion.

## The exact sign average and all remainder terms

Let $w$ be a real smooth mean-zero function. For
$u_\pm=1\pm t\phi+t^2w$, use the elementary quantities
\[
 a=3+2\delta,\qquad
 B=\int\bigl(\phi w+2\Gamma(\phi,w)\bigr)
   =3\int\phi w+2\int r w,\qquad
 J=\int\bigl(w^2+2\Gamma(w,w)\bigr),\qquad m_4=\int\phi^4 .
\]
The bracket on the right of (1) is exactly
$1+a t^2\pm2B t^3+J t^4$. The average of its squares has
coefficients $2a$, $a^2+2J$, $2aJ+4B^2$, and $J^2$ at
orders $2,4,6,8$, respectively, with constant term one.
Expanding and averaging $\int u_\pm^4$ gives coefficient $6$
at order two and the coefficients
$6\int w^2+12\int\phi^2w+m_4$,
$4\int w^3+6\int\phi^2w^2$, and $\int w^4$ at orders $4,6,8$.
All odd powers cancel in the average, including those involving
$r$ through $B$. Its exact deficit is therefore
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 0\le {}&4\delta t^2+
 \bigl(\mathcal C_\phi(w)+12\delta+4\delta^2\bigr)t^4\\
 &+\left(2aJ+4B^2-4\int w^3-6\int\phi^2w^2\right)t^6
   +\left(J^2-\int w^4\right)t^8,\\
 \mathcal C_\phi(w)={}&
 4\int\Gamma(w,w)-4\int w^2-12\int\phi^2w+9-m_4 .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]

The remainder in (2) is uniform on bounded $H$ sets. Indeed
$a\le5$, $|B|\le(\|\phi\|_2+2(\int\Gamma(\phi,\phi))^{1/2})\|w\|_H$,
and $J\le2\|w\|_H^2$. By (1) and unit volume,
$|\int w^3|\le\|w\|_4^3$ and $\int w^4\le4\|w\|_H^4$.
Also $\int\phi^2w^2\le C_0^2\|w\|_H^2$.
Thus for $\|w\|_H$ bounded and $|t|\le1$, the two last terms
of (2) are bounded in absolute value by $C t^6$.
Taking $t=\varepsilon^{1/4}$ and dividing (2) by $t^4$
gives
\[
 \mathcal C_\phi(w)\ge-C\sqrt\varepsilon
 \tag{3}
\]
since $\delta/t^2\le\sqrt\varepsilon$, $\delta\le\varepsilon$,
and $t^2=\sqrt\varepsilon$.

## Centered square, variation and dual bound

Put $b=\phi^2-1$. Its mean vanishes, and $\int b^2=\int\phi^2b=m_4-1$.
The chain rule and integration by parts give
\[
 \int\Gamma(b,b)
 =4\int\phi^2\Gamma(\phi,\phi)
 =\tfrac43\int\phi^3 L\phi
 =\tfrac43(m_4+\int\phi^3r).
\]
Consequently $\|b\|_H$ is uniformly bounded and
\[
 \mathcal C_\phi(b)
 =25-\tfrac{35}{3}m_4+\tfrac{16}{3}\int\phi^3r
 =O(\sqrt\varepsilon).
 \tag{4}
\]
The last term is $O(\varepsilon)$ by the supplied $L^\infty$ bounds
and unit volume. For smooth mean-zero $\zeta$ with $\|\zeta\|_H\le1$,
the exact quadratic expansion in $w=b+\tau\zeta$ gives
\[
 \mathcal C_\phi(b+\tau\zeta)
 =\mathcal C_\phi(b)
     +8\tau\left\langle(L-\tfrac52)b,\zeta\right\rangle
     +4\tau^2\int(\Gamma(\zeta,\zeta)-\zeta^2).
 \tag{5}
\]
In the linear term, $\int\phi^2\zeta=\int b\zeta$ because
$\int\zeta=0$. Its gradient pairing is the weak pairing with
$L b$. All $w=b+\tau\zeta$ for $|\tau|\le1$ are in the same
uniform $H$ ball, and the absolute value of the last coefficient
in (5) is at most four. Apply (3) to $\tau=\pm\varepsilon^{1/4}$
and use (4). This gives
\[
 \left|\left\langle(L-\tfrac52)b,\zeta\right\rangle\right|
 \le C\varepsilon^{1/4}.
\]
The residual pairs to zero with constants, by $\int b=0$ and
$L1=0$. Subtracting the mean from a general test decreases its
$H$ norm. Density thus gives
\[
 \big\|(L-\tfrac52)(\phi^2-1)\big\|_{H^*}
 \le C\varepsilon^{1/4}.
 \tag{6}
\]
At $\varepsilon=0$, (2) gives $\mathcal C_\phi(w)\ge0$ on
sending $t\to0$, (4) gives $\mathcal C_\phi(b)=0$, and the
linear coefficient in (5) vanishes by both signs of $\tau$.

## Polarization and all entries

The nonnegative Laplacian product rule gives
$L(\phi^2)=2\phi L\phi-2\Gamma(\phi,\phi)$. Thus
\[
 \Gamma(\phi,\phi)-\tfrac14(5-\phi^2)
 =\phi r-\tfrac12(L-\tfrac52)b .
 \tag{7}
\]
The first term on the right has $H^*$ norm $O(\varepsilon)$
by its $L^2$ norm, so the full left side is $O(\varepsilon^{1/4})$
in $H^*$. For diagonal entries use $\phi=u_i$. For $i\ne j$ take
one half the difference of (7) for
$\phi_\pm=(u_i\pm u_j)/\sqrt2$ to obtain the off-diagonal left
side $\Gamma(u_i,u_j)+u_i u_j/4$. Set $X_i=u_i/\sqrt5$.
Dividing all these entries by five yields
\[
 \left\|\Gamma(X_i,X_j)-\tfrac14(\delta_{ij}-X_iX_j)\right\|_{H^*}
 \le C\varepsilon^{1/4}\qquad (1\le i,j\le5).
\]
The estimate applies to constants as tests. The mean error in the
diagonal entry is exactly $(\lambda_i(L)-1)/5$, and it vanishes
for off-diagonal entries by orthonormality and the eigenvalue equations.
These are weak identities for the supplied moment pinching.
