## Result

Let \(M\) be any smooth connected closed oriented four-manifold and
\(\mu\) a supplied smooth positive density of total mass one. There is a
smooth degree \(+1\) map \(F:M\to S^4\subset\mathbb R^5\) with
\(\int_M F\,d\mu=0\). It can be chosen with nonnegative signed
Jacobian, so its absolute-Jacobian measure has a smooth finite
density. For any probability measure on \(S^4\), an atom of mass
\(\beta>1/2\) prevents conformal centering.

## Ball collapse and removal of the exterior atom

Choose a positively oriented chart with a closed coordinate ball \(B\)
of radius \(r_0\) in its interior. Write the unit target sphere with its
boundary orientation from \(\mathbb R^5\) and let \(q=(0,0,0,0,-1)\).
Use the radial map of [E05](DIR_E05_20261124.md#degree-and-twisted-index)
on the ball,
\[
F_0(r,\omega)=(\sin\theta(r)\,\omega,\cos\theta(r)).
\]
Take \(\theta=c r\) near zero for \(c>0\), with
\(\theta'(r)>0\) on \(0<r<r_0\) and \(\theta=\pi\) for \(r\ge r_0\).
Such a function is the integral of a smooth positive derivative on
\([0,r_0)\) that is constant near zero, is flat at \(r_0\), and has
integral \(\pi\). Smoothness at zero follows by writing the first four
components as \(\sin(cr)x/r\). At \(r=r_0\), \(F_0-q\) is flat, meaning
that all its derivatives vanish there. Extend \(F_0\) by \(q\) outside
\(B\). On \(B^\circ\) it is a diffeomorphism onto \(S^4\setminus\{q\}\).
At the center its derivative is \(cI\) into the oriented tangent
frame given by the first four axes at the north pole. Its signed
Jacobian is therefore positive throughout \(B^\circ\), and its
degree is \(+1\).

Write \(U=M\setminus B\) for the open exterior. Choose a smooth
function \(\rho\) on \(M\) with \(0\le\rho\le1\), zero on \(B\) and
positive on \(U\). For example, use \(e^{-1/t^2}\) in an outer collar
with collar coordinate \(t>0\) and join it smoothly to a positive
constant farther out. It is flat along \(\partial B\).

Take a finite collection of smaller charts whose union covers \(M\).
For each choose a bump function equal to one on the smaller chart
and supported in a larger coordinate chart. Use that bump and its
four products with the larger chart coordinates as globally defined
smooth functions, extending each by zero off the larger chart.
List the resulting functions as \(H_1,\dots,H_N\). At any \(x\in U\)
some smaller chart contains \(x\), and the corresponding differentials
of \(\rho H_i\) include \(d\rho\) and
\(x_jd\rho+\rho\,dx_j\), \(1\le j\le4\), in its coordinates.
They span \(T_x^*M\) since \(\rho(x)>0\).

For \(\mathbf a\) in a sufficiently small Euclidean ball about zero put
\[
\varphi_{\mathbf a}
=\epsilon\,\rho\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^N a_i H_i\right).
\]
The parameter ball can be chosen so that the parenthesis ranges in
\([1/2,3/2]\) on \(M\). Fix \(0<\epsilon<1\), which bounds
\(\varphi_{\mathbf a}\) by \(3/2<\pi\). For each \(x\in U\), the
condition \(d\varphi_{\mathbf a}(x)=0\) cuts out an affine subspace of
codimension four in the parameter space, by the spanning property.
It has Lebesgue measure zero. The zero-differential condition is
measurable on the product, so Fubini gives a small parameter
\(\mathbf a\) for which the critical set of \(\varphi_{\mathbf a}\)
in \(U\) has \(\mu\)-measure zero. Fix such a parameter and write
\(\varphi=\varphi_{\mathbf a}\).

Every level of \(\varphi|_U\) consequently has measure zero. Its
critical points lie in the single null critical set, and near each
regular point a level is contained in a smooth hypersurface by the
implicit function theorem. Second countability gives a countable
cover of this regular part by such neighborhoods. The smooth density
assigns zero measure to each of their hypersurfaces.

Let \(\gamma(t)=\cos(t)q+\sin(t)e_1\) for \(0\le t\le3/2\), where
\(e_1\) is the first coordinate vector in \(\mathbb R^5\).
This arc is embedded. Keep \(F_0\) on \(B\) and define \(F_1=\gamma(\varphi)\)
on \(U\). The two definitions agree to all orders at \(\partial B\),
so \(F_1\) is smooth. Replacing \(\varphi\) by \(s\varphi\) for
\(0\le s\le1\) gives a smooth homotopy from \(F_0\) to \(F_1\) with
the same ball. Thus \(F_1\) has degree \(+1\).
For each target value the preimage on \(B^\circ\) is at most a
singleton, on \(U\) it is empty or one level of \(\varphi\), and the
remaining preimages lie on the null seam \(\partial B\). Hence
\(\nu=(F_1)_*\mu\) is nonatomic. The differential of \(F_1\) has
rank at most one on \(U\), with zero determinant there and on the
seam. Its determinant remains positive on \(B^\circ\).

## Conformal centering and the atom obstruction

For \(a\) in the open unit ball of \(\mathbb R^5\), define
\[
T_a(y)=a+(1-|a|^2)\frac{y+a}{|y+a|^2},\qquad y\in S^4 .
\]
Expanding the squared norm using \(|y|=1\) gives \(|T_a(y)|=1\).
The inverse is \(T_{-a}\), by the inversion identity
\(T_a(y)-a=(1-|a|^2)(y+a)/|y+a|^2\). The formula is a composition
of translations, inversion and dilation, so its restriction to
the sphere is conformal. The path \(T_{sa}\) for \(0\le s\le1\)
starts at the identity and makes its degree \(+1\). Its round
length scale at \(y\) is \((1-|a|^2)/|y+a|^2\), from the derivative
of Euclidean inversion.

The barycenter \(b(a)=\int_{S^4}T_a(y)\,d\nu(y)\) is continuous in the
open ball. As \(a\to\zeta\in S^4\), the formula gives
\(T_a(y)\to\zeta\) at every \(y\ne-\zeta\). The exceptional point is
\(\nu\)-null and the integrand has norm one. Dominated convergence
extends \(b\) continuously to the closed ball with \(b(\zeta)=\zeta\)
on its boundary. If \(b\) had no zero, normalizing \(b/|b|\) would
give a retraction of the closed five-ball onto \(S^4\).
Such a retraction would factor the identity on \(H_4(S^4;\mathbb Z)\)
through \(H_4(\overline B^5;\mathbb Z)=0\), a contradiction.
Therefore some interior \(a_*\) has \(b(a_*)=0\). The map
\(F=T_{a_*}\circ F_1\) is smooth of degree \(+1\) and obeys
\(\int_M F\,d\mu=0\).

For any probability measure \(\nu_0\) with an atom \(\{y_0\}\) of
mass \(\beta>1/2\), and any conformal automorphism \(T\) of the sphere,
\[
\left|\int_{S^4}T(y)\,d\nu_0(y)\right|
\ \ge\ \beta |T(y_0)|-\int_{S^4\setminus\{y_0\}}|T(y)|\,d\nu_0(y)
\ =\ 2\beta-1\ >0 .
\]
In particular the unperturbed collapse cannot be centered by target
automorphisms when the exterior atom exceeds half the starting mass.

## Absolute Jacobian and scope

Identify \(\mu\) with its positive oriented four-form and write
\(F^*\omega_{\mathrm{rd}}=J_F\,\mu\) for the pullback of the oriented
round volume form. The determinant sign of \(F_1\) is nonnegative
and \(T_{a_*}\) is orientation preserving, hence \(J_F\ge0\).
Consequently \(|J_F|=J_F\) is smooth, including at the seam and on
the rank-deficient exterior. Its total mass is
\(\int_M|J_F|\,d\mu=\operatorname{Vol}_{\mathrm{rd}}(S^4)\) by degree.

Balance concerns the extrinsic mean for the supplied density. If a
domain metric \(g\) is also supplied, target postcomposition gives
\[
|d(T_a\circ F_1)|_{g,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2
=\left(\frac{1-|a|^2}{|F_1+a|^2}\right)^2
  |dF_1|_{g,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2 .
\]
This scale enters the potential of \(Y_f([g])\), while invariance of
that quotient under domain conformal changes concerns a fixed target
map. The centering argument establishes feasibility for one map.
It gives no variational control of that potential or uniform
integrability of absolute Jacobians for a sequence.
