Let $M$ be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere,
and fix any $p\in M$. Every smooth Riemannian metric $g_0$ that is
exactly Euclidean on a coordinate ball about $p$ admits a smooth
deformation to a metric $g$ with invertible full complex spin Dirac,
supported inside any prescribed disjoint nonempty open ball $U$.
In particular the same Euclidean tensor in the same chart is retained.

## Spin structure and index

The homotopy type gives $H^2(M;\mathbb Z/2)=H^1(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0$.
Thus $w_2(TM)=0$, and spin structures for the given orientation
form a torsor for the zero group $H^1(M;\mathbb Z/2)$. Use the unique
spin structure up to isomorphism through all metric changes. Its
restriction to a coordinate ball is the standard local spin structure.
[Ammann–Dahl–Humbert, §2.1, p. 929][adhspin].

Use $X\cdot X=-|X|^2$ and the positive complex half-spinor
bundle on which real self-dual two-forms act. For the untwisted
operator, $D_g^-=(D_g^+)^*$ with
$D_g^+:\Gamma(S_g^+)\to\Gamma(S_g^-)$.
The Atiyah–Singer spin index in **complex** dimensions is
\[
 \operatorname{ind}_{\mathbb C}D_g^+
 =\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker D_g^+-\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker D_g^-
 =\widehat A[M]=-\frac{\langle p_1(TM),[M]\rangle}{24}.
\]
The coefficient follows from $(z/2)/\sinh(z/2)=1-z^2/24+\cdots$
in each formal Pontryagin root.
The signature theorem gives $\langle p_1(TM),[M]\rangle=3\tau(M)$.
Here $H^2(M;\mathbb R)=0$, so $\tau(M)=0$ and
$\widehat A[M]=-\tau(M)/8=0$.
[Atiyah–Singer, §3.3, Theorem 4, p. 428][as] ·
[Hirzebruch, §4, p. 11, eq. (16)][hirz] ·
[Shared signature audit](../../notes/SH_E01_20261124.md#setting-and-normalization).

For comparison, the real $KO^{-4}(\mathrm{pt})\cong\mathbb Z$ index
counts the kernel difference in quaternionic dimensions. The
half-spin representations in dimension four are quaternionic, and
their Clifford intertwiners commute with that structure. Thus the
complex index is twice the quaternionic index with the compatible
generator, and $\alpha(M)=\widehat A[M]/2=0$ in that convention.
The relevant lower bound in Ammann–Dahl–Humbert is their explicit
full **complex** kernel bound $a(M)=|\widehat A[M]|$ in eq. (1).
[Hitchin, §4.2, remark 1 after Proposition 4.2, p. 41][hit] ·
[Ammann–Dahl–Humbert, §1, pp. 927–928][adh].

## Local deformation

To obtain an initial $g_0$ from a reference metric $g_{\mathrm{ref}}$,
choose a small oriented chart about $p$ and pull the Euclidean metric
$\delta$ back to it. Choose a smooth cutoff $\chi$ with $0\le\chi\le1$,
equal to one on a neighborhood of the closure of a smaller coordinate
ball and supported in the chart. Set
$g_0=(1-\chi)g_{\mathrm{ref}}+\chi\delta$ there and use
$g_{\mathrm{ref}}$ outside the chart. Smoothness and positive
definiteness follow from the support and convexity of positive
definite tensors. The chart can be chosen to leave room for a
disjoint ball.

For any supplied $g_0$ with the flat ball and any such $U$, choose
$q\in U$ and $r>0$ with the closure of the $g_0$-distance ball
$U_q(r)$ contained in $U$. One can also take $r$ below the injectivity
radius at $q$. Theorem 1.1 of Ammann–Dahl–Humbert applies to the
compact connected Riemannian spin manifold $(M,g_0)$ in dimension
four. It gives a smooth $D$-minimal $g$ equal to $g_0$ on
$M\setminus U_q(r)$. Consequently $\operatorname{supp}(g-g_0)$
is contained in $\overline{U_q(r)}\subset U$. In particular it retains
all metric and local spin-geometric data on a neighborhood of
the complement of $U$. A smooth deformation is given by
$(1-t)g_0+t g$ for $0\le t\le1$, whose endpoint is the asserted
$D$-minimal metric. [Ammann–Dahl–Humbert, Theorem 1.1, p. 928][adh].

Here $D$-minimality gives $\dim_{\mathbb C}\ker D_g=|\widehat A[M]|=0$.
The full self-adjoint elliptic Dirac operator has domain $H^1$ in
$L^2$ and discrete spectrum on the closed manifold. Trivial kernel
gives invertibility and a positive distance from zero to the spectrum
for this metric. The relative theorem uses no scalar-curvature sign
and gives no uniform positive lower bound for that distance across
the input manifolds or metrics. Its published Corollary 1.1 also
allows $g$ arbitrarily close to $g_0$ in any prescribed finite $C^k$
topology, $k\ge1$, with exactly the same exterior metric.
[Ammann–Dahl–Humbert, §1, pp. 927–928][adh].
