**Result.** On a closed four-manifold a unit-volume Yamabe metric with
$s=Y>0$ and $\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2<s^2/12-8\pi^2\chi(M)$ forces $M$ to be
diffeomorphic to $S^4$ or $\mathbb{RP}^4$. This is the theorem of Chang,
Gursky and Yang read at a Yamabe metric. $\mathbb{CP}^2$ and the necklace of
[E49][e49] sit exactly at equality. On a homotopy four-sphere the threshold is
$s^2/12-16\pi^2$, which is positive only for $Y>Y(S^4)/\sqrt2$ and tends to
$s^2/24$ as $Y\to Y(S^4)$. Relative pinching controls the sum
$\int|{\rm Hess}_0\phi|^2+\int{\rm Ric}_0(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)$ for the five
modes and neither term alone. The five-eigenfunction theorem holds for local
minimizers of the Yamabe functional, and a non-round local minimizer breaks
at most four independent conformal symmetries.

Conventions are those of the manuscript: $\Delta=d^*d$,
$E_\pm=\int|W^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2$, $|W|_{4\rm ind}^2=4(|W^+|_{\rm HS}^2+|W^-|_{\rm HS}^2)$,
${\rm Ric}_0$ the trace-free Ricci tensor, $Y(S^4)=8\sqrt6\,\pi$ and
$Y(S^4)^2=384\pi^2$.

## Gauss–Bonnet at a Yamabe metric

The Chern–Gauss–Bonnet formula in dimension four is
$8\pi^2\chi=\int(\tfrac14|W|_{4\rm ind}^2-\tfrac12|{\rm Ric}_0|^2+\tfrac1{24}s^2)\,dV$
([R104](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_DIRAC_B23_20261124.md),
equation (1.2)). With $\tfrac14\int|W|_{4\rm ind}^2=E_++E_-$ and the
signature formula $E_+-E_-=12\pi^2\tau$ of the manuscript, every metric on a
closed oriented four-manifold satisfies

\[
 E_+=2\pi^2(2\chi+3\tau)+\tfrac14\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2\,dV-\tfrac1{48}\int s^2\,dV .
\tag{1}
\]

On a homotopy four-sphere, at a unit-volume metric of constant scalar
curvature $s$,

\[
 E_+=\frac{Y(S^4)^2-s^2}{48}+\tfrac14\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2\,dV .
\tag{2}
\]

At a Yamabe minimizer $s=Y$. Three consequences follow.

1. $E_+\ge\bigl(Y(S^4)^2-Y^2\bigr)/48$ in every class with $Y\ge0$, with
   equality exactly when a minimizer is Einstein. This is the known bound
   $\int\sigma_2\le Y^2/24\le16\pi^2$ (R104, (1.7)), and the form used by the
   sibling investigation, where $E_+\le8\pi^2-\delta$ gives
   $Y\ge\sqrt{48\delta}$
   ([SEC-E36](../../01_sections_and_connections/notes/SEC_E36_20261124.md)).
2. Chang–Gursky–Yang's Theorem A states that a closed four-manifold with
   $Y>0$ and $\int|W|_{4\rm ind}^2<16\pi^2\chi$ is diffeomorphic to $S^4$ or
   $\mathbb{RP}^4$. By the Gauss–Bonnet formula the hypothesis reads, at a
   unit-volume Yamabe metric,
   \[
    \int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2\,dV<\frac{s^2}{12}-8\pi^2\chi(M).
   \tag{3}
   \]
   Their equality case, for $M$ not one of those two manifolds, is the class
   of the Fubini–Study metric or a quotient of the product $S^3\times S^1$
   (R104, Theorem 1.1). Both are visible in (3): for $\mathbb{CP}^2$,
   ${\rm Ric}_0=0$, $Y=12\sqrt2\,\pi$ and $Y^2/12=24\pi^2=8\pi^2\chi$; for the
   necklace, $\chi=0$ and $\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2=s^2/12$ by [E49][e49].
3. On a homotopy four-sphere (3) is
   $\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2<s^2/12-16\pi^2$. The right side is at most $16\pi^2$,
   is positive only when $Y>8\sqrt3\,\pi=Y(S^4)/\sqrt2$, and equals $s^2/24$
   at $Y=Y(S^4)$. Conversely, on a homotopy four-sphere that is not standard,
   every unit-volume Yamabe metric with $Y>0$ has
   $\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2\ge s^2/12-16\pi^2$.

**Comparison with the pointwise hypothesis.** Suppose
${\rm Ric}\ge(1-\delta)(s/4)g$ at a unit-volume Yamabe metric. The
eigenvalues of ${\rm Ric}_0$ are at least $-\delta s/4$ and sum to zero, so
$|{\rm Ric}_0|^2\le\tfrac34\delta^2s^2$, with the extreme case
$(3,-1,-1,-1)\delta s/4$. Then (3) holds as soon as

\[
 \delta^2<\frac19-\frac{32\pi^2\chi(M)}{3\,s^2},
\]

which on a homotopy sphere at $Y=Y(S^4)$ is $\delta<1/(3\sqrt2)\approx0.236$.
This uses no eigenvalue. The Aubry route of [E45][e45] needs both $\delta$
and the spectral pinching to be small and gives a Gromov–Hausdorff estimate
as well. The integral criterion needs the Euler characteristic and a Yamabe
constant above the stated level, and neither follows from a Ricci lower
bound alone: the Fubini–Study and product Einstein metrics on $\mathbb{CP}^2$
and $S^2\times S^2$ have $\delta=0$.

## Bochner identities for the pinched modes

Let $g$ have constant scalar curvature $s$ and $\Delta\phi=\lambda\phi$. Write
$\nabla^2\phi={\rm Hess}_0\phi-\tfrac14(\Delta\phi)g$. The integrated Bochner
formula $\int|\nabla^2\phi|^2+\int{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)=\int(\Delta\phi)^2$
and ${\rm Ric}={\rm Ric}_0+\tfrac s4g$ give

\[
 \int|{\rm Hess}_0\phi|^2+\int{\rm Ric}_0(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)
 =\frac\lambda4(3\lambda-s)\int\phi^2
 =\frac{s^2}{12}\,\nu(\nu-1)\int\phi^2,\qquad\nu=\frac{3\lambda}s ,
\tag{4}
\]

and the pointwise form, from $\operatorname{div}\nabla^2\phi=-\nabla\Delta\phi+{\rm Ric}(\nabla\phi)$,

\[
 \operatorname{div}{\rm Hess}_0\phi={\rm Ric}_0(\nabla\phi)-\tfrac14(3\lambda-s)\nabla\phi .
\tag{5}
\]

For the first five modes of a unit-volume Yamabe minimizer the right side of
(4) lies between $0$ and $\tfrac{s^2}{12}(1+\epsilon)\epsilon$. Relative
pinching therefore makes the sum of the two terms on the left small for each
mode. The first is nonnegative. The second has no sign, and no estimate
separates them. On the necklace the translation mode has $\nu=1$ and

\[
 \int|{\rm Hess}_0\phi|^2=-\int{\rm Ric}_0(\nabla\phi,\nabla\phi)
 =\frac{s^2}{12}\int\phi^2
\]

at every neck size, while the four transverse modes have the two terms in
a proportion that approaches $3:2$ for a thin neck, both of order $\epsilon$
([computed record](../numerics/E49_NECKLACE_LAW.txt)). So at pinching the
trace-free Hessian of a mode can stay of unit size in $L^2$, supported where
${\rm Ric}_0$ is large. Identity (5) shows why no bound for
$\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2$ follows: ${\rm Ric}_0$ applied to the five gradients is
a divergence of the trace-free Hessians, one derivative beyond what (4)
controls. The integral criterion (3) and relative pinching are independent
hypotheses, and the necklace has the second without the first.

## Local minimizers

The proof of the five-eigenfunction theorem in [E37][e37] and [E38][e38]
uses the minimizing inequality only along curves $1+t\phi+t^2w$ as $t\to0$,
for smooth $\phi$ and $w$: the sign of the second-order term gives
$s\le3\lambda_1$, the cubic term gives the vanishing triple products, and the
quartic term gives the moment bound and its equality case. The theorem
therefore holds for every unit-volume metric of constant scalar curvature at
which the Yamabe functional of the conformal class has a local minimum along
all such curves, in particular at a local minimum in the smooth topology:
$A(g)\ge0$, the first eigenspace at $s=3\lambda_1$ has dimension at most
five, and dimension five forces the round sphere. The near-equality estimates
of [E42][e42]–[E44][e44] use a finite step $t=\epsilon^{1/4}$ and the global
Sobolev inequality, and would need a uniform neighbourhood of minimality and
a bound for $s/Y$.

**Broken conformal symmetry.** Let $g$ be such a local minimizer and $X$ a
conformal Killing field, $\mathcal L_Xg=2\sigma g$ with
$\sigma=\tfrac14\operatorname{div}X$. As in [E49][e49], $\Delta\sigma=(s/3)\sigma$,
and $\sigma=0$ exactly when $X$ is Killing. So $X\mapsto\sigma$ embeds the
quotient of the conformal Killing fields by the Killing fields of $g$ in the
eigenspace of $s/3$. If the quotient is nonzero then $s/3$ is an eigenvalue,
hence equals $\lambda_1$, and the theorem gives

\[
 \dim\mathfrak{conf}(M,[g])-\dim\mathfrak{isom}(M,g)\le4
\]

unless $g$ is round. The necklace has the value one for every
$L>\sqrt2\,\pi$.
