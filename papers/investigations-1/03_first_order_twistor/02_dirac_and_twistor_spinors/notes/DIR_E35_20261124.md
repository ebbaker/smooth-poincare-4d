## The background and fixed perturbation

Work on the standard oriented smooth spin $S^4$, with Clifford square
$X\cdot X=-|X|^2$ and positive chirality acted on by real self-dual
two-forms. Fix a pole $p$ and oriented stereographic coordinates $x$
on $N=S^4\setminus\{p\}$. Choose a unit vector $a$, write $S_a$ for
reflection in $a^\perp$, and set $y=S_a x/|x|^2$ near the pole, so
$r=|y|=|x|^{-1}$. Fix a bounded open ball $U$ in the $x$-chart and a
positive smooth factor $\omega$ on $N$ equal to one near $\overline U$
and $|x|^2$ for $|x|$ large. Define $g_b=\omega^{-2}\delta_x$ on $N$.
It is exactly flat on $U$. Near the pole inversion gives
$\delta_x=r^{-4}\delta_y$ and $\omega=r^{-2}$, so $g_b=\delta_y$
extends smoothly and exactly flat across $p$. It is conformal to the
round metric with a smooth positive factor on all of $S^4$, since
$g_{\mathrm{rd}}=[2/(1+|x|^2)]^2\delta_x$ in the $x$-chart and
$2\omega/(1+|x|^2)=2/(1+r^2)$ near $p$. Thus $h_0=\omega^2g_b=\delta_x$
globally on $N$. The round Dirac kernel vanishes by the positive scalar
term in the integrated Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz identity. Conformal
Dirac covariance gives the same vanishing for $g_b$ under its smooth
compact conformal factor. The full self-adjoint compact Dirac operator
$D_b=D_{g_b}$ is therefore invertible.
[Lee–Parker, Appendix (A.2)][lp] · [Hitchin, Proposition 1.3][hitchin].

Reuse the exact tensor of [E11][e11], here denoted $b_n$. Choose the same
fixed nonzero real cutoff $\chi\in C_c^\infty(U)$ and put
$F_n=\chi\cos(nx^1)$, $H=(dx^2)^2-(dx^3)^2$,
$T=(dx^1)^2\owedge H$, and $C=2T-H\owedge\delta_x$. The tensor
$C_{i1j1}=H_{ij}$ is an algebraic Weyl tensor in the E11 convention. Set

\[
(b_n)_{ij}=-n^{-7/2}\sum_{k,l}C_{ikjl}\partial_k\partial_l F_n,
\qquad g_n=g_b+b_n,\qquad h_n=\omega^2g_n.
\]

Here $b_n$ extends by zero and is symmetric, transverse and trace-free
for $g_b$ by the algebraic curvature symmetries. In the $x$-chart
$h_n=\delta_x+b_n$ because $\omega=1$ on the support. By [E11][e11],
the coordinate bounds on $b_n$, $\partial b_n$, $\partial^2 b_n$ are
$O(n^{-3/2})$, $O(n^{-1/2})$, $O(n^{1/2})$, respectively. For a bounded
Lipschitz coefficient $f$, $[f]_\alpha\le C_\alpha\|f\|_\infty^{1-\alpha}
\operatorname{Lip}(f)^\alpha$ by taking the minimum of the two difference
bounds. For $\alpha=1/4$ this gives

\[
\|g_n-g_b\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}=O(n^{-1/4}),\qquad
\|g_n-g_b\|_{C^1}=O(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{1}
\]

All norms on compact data refer to a fixed atlas and $g_b$ when needed.
For sufficiently large $n$ the $g_n$ are smooth positive-definite,
uniformly bilipschitz to $g_b$, with volume tending to that of $g_b$,
uniform diameter bound and unchanged flat pole ball. The $h_n$ are
complete, with the same exact Euclidean end and the same marked chart.

## Fixed-bundle inverse and actual Green solutions

Send oriented $g_b$-orthonormal frames to $g_n$-orthonormal frames by
$A_n=(I+g_b^{-1}b_n)^{-1/2}$. Lift the path using $t b_n$ for $0\le t\le1$
starting at the identity; this defines a Hermitian chiral spin
identification $B_n:S_{g_b}\to S_{g_n}$, the identity off the support.
Set $\widehat D_n=B_n^{-1}D_{g_n}B_n$. In paired spin frames the
principal coefficient change consists of entries from $A_n-I$. The
zero-order coefficient change consists of the corresponding differences
of orthonormal connection forms, which depend smoothly on $g_n$ and
linearly on its first derivatives in a fixed local frame. Explicitly
these forms use $g_n(\nabla^{g_n}_X(A_ne_i),A_ne_j)$, with the Christoffel
coefficients and $dA_n$ as their only metric derivatives. The Clifford
matrices in the paired frames are fixed. Smooth matrix inversion and
square roots near the identity, and (1), now give

\[
\|\widehat D_n-D_b\|_{C^{1,\alpha}\to C^{0,\alpha}}=O(n^{-1/4}),
\qquad
\|\widehat D_n-D_b\|_{W^{1,4}\to L^4}=O(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{2}
\]

The latter norm only needs the supremum bounds of the first-order
operator coefficients. The fixed inverse $D_b^{-1}$ is bounded
$C^{0,\alpha}\to C^{1,\alpha}$ and $L^4\to W^{1,4}$. For instance,
apply compact elliptic Schauder and $W^{2,4}$ estimates to the inverse
of $D_b^2$ and then apply $D_b$, using its zero kernel and self-adjointness.
In each space, invert $I+D_b^{-1}(\widehat D_n-D_b)$ by a Neumann series.
Both resulting inverse norms stay bounded as $n\to\infty$. Smoothness of
each $g_n$ and elliptic regularity give smooth solutions on smooth input,
and these inverses agree there by uniqueness. In particular the compact
$D_{g_n}$ is invertible for large $n$.

Fix a unit constant positive spinor $\kappa$ in the global Euclidean
$x$ spin frame. Take the pole spin frame and lift as in [E27][e27]. With
$c=1/(2\pi^2)$, choose $\eta=-c^{-1}a\cdot\kappa$ in the negative compact
pole fiber. Thus $c\,a\cdot\eta=\kappa$ in the end frame. Let $Q$ be the
cutoff of $-c\,y\cdot\eta/r^4$ with cutoff supported in the fixed flat
pole ball and equal to one near $p$. It is common to all compact bundles
under the identifications. Distributionally $\widehat D_nQ=D_bQ=
\delta_p\eta+f$ for one fixed smooth $f$ supported in the pole annulus.
[E27, signed pole and orientation transition][e27] ·
[Ammann–Humbert–Morel, Remark 2.2 and Proposition 2.3][ahm].

Let $G_n=D_{g_n}^{-1}(\delta_p\eta)$, $G_b=D_b^{-1}(\delta_p\eta)$, and
$S_b=G_b-Q$. Their regular difference $u_n=B_n^{-1}G_n-G_b$ is smooth
and satisfies

\[
\widehat D_n u_n=-(\widehat D_n-D_b)S_b,
\qquad \|u_n\|_{C^{1,\alpha}}=O(n^{-1/4}),\qquad
\|u_n\|_{W^{1,4}}=O(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{3}
\]

Indeed, each regular part solves the smooth equation with right-hand
side $-f$, so the estimate follows from (2) and the bounded inverses.
On the fixed pole ball $D_b u_n=0$, and $D_b^2$ is the ordinary flat
componentwise positive Laplacian there. On a smaller fixed ball the
interior harmonic estimates give $\|u_n\|_{C^2}=O(n^{-1/2})$ from its
$L^4$ bound. This estimate uses fixed flat data near $p$.

## Nonvanishing, derivative estimates and compact jet

Set $\Psi_n=\omega^{-3/2}\beta_{g_n,h_n}G_n$, where $\beta$ denotes the
unitary conformal spin identification. It is positive chiral and
$D_{h_n}\Psi_n=0$. The corresponding Euclidean $\Psi_b$ is exactly
$\kappa$: its Green construction has this end limit by [E27][e27],
and the constant parallel spinor has the same limit and is unique in
the $O(R^{-3})$ class by compact removal. In particular the regular
part of $G_b$ on the pole ball vanishes where $Q$ is its singular pole.

The positive comparison from $h_0$ to $h_n$ uses the same $A_n$ as
above, since both compact metrics were multiplied by $\omega^2$. The
frame maps commute with this common conformal rescaling, and the
chosen paths lift the commuting relation. Use the resulting unitary
identification to write $\Psi_n$ in the $h_0$ bundle. In this comparison
its difference from $\kappa$ is $\omega^{-3/2}\beta_{g_b,h_0}u_n$.
On a fixed compact truncation of $N$, (3) bounds this difference by
$O(n^{-1/4})$ in $C^{1,\alpha}$ and $O(n^{-1/2})$ in $W^{1,4}$.
On the end, writing $R=|x|$ and $q=a\cdot(y/r)\cdot$, the difference
in the parallel $x$ frame is $R^{-3}q\,u_n(y(x))$. By the preceding
flat estimates, for $j=0,1,2$ one has

\[
\partial_x^j(\Psi_n-\kappa)=O(n^{-1/2}R^{-3-j}).
\tag{4}
\]

Consequently $\sigma_n=|\Psi_n|_{h_n}$ is bounded above and bounded
away from zero on all of $N$ for large $n$, with $\sigma_n\to1$ uniformly.
The compact field $\psi_n=\omega^{-1/2}\beta_{g_n,h_n}^{-1}\Psi_n=
\omega^{-2}G_n$ is smooth. Near the pole its exact expression is
$-c\,y\cdot\eta+r^4u_n(y)$. It has the fixed transverse jet
$\nabla_X^{g_n}\psi_n(p)=-c\,X\cdot\eta$ and only the zero at $p$.
The compact fields converge in $C^{1,\alpha}$ under $B_n$ to the
background compact field by (3) and smooth multiplication by
$\omega^{-2}$, which is $r^4$ near $p$. In particular their compact
$L^2$ norms converge to a positive limit.

In the paired $h_0,h_n$ frames the spin connection of $h_n$ is
$O(n^{-1/2})$ in supremum norm on the compact perturbation support,
and the Euclidean connection is flat elsewhere in these frames.
The compact-part $W^{1,4}$ estimate and the integrable end bound (4)
give global estimates in both exponents $p=2,4$

\[
\|\nabla^{h_n}\Psi_n\|_{L^2(h_n)}+\|\nabla^{h_n}\Psi_n\|_{L^4(h_n)}
=O(n^{-1/2}).
\tag{5}
\]

The $L^2$ estimate on the compact part follows from its uniformly
bounded volume and the $L^4$ estimate. Since $\Psi_n$ is harmonic,
$P_{h_n}\Psi_n=\nabla^{h_n}\Psi_n$. Twistor-weight invariance in [E07][e07],
restricted to the punctured manifold and using that its complement is
the sole zero, gives

\[
\int_N |\nabla^{h_n}\Psi_n|^2\,dV_{h_n}=O(n^{-1}),\qquad
J_{g_n}(\psi_n)^4=J_{h_n}(\Psi_n)^4
=\int_N \frac{|\nabla^{h_n}\Psi_n|^4}{\sigma_n^4}\,dV_{h_n}
=O(n^{-2}).
\tag{6}
\]

## Unit harmonic scale and the Weyl obstruction

Set $k_n=\sigma_n^{4/3}h_n$ and
$\xi_n=\sigma_n^{-1}\beta_{h_n,k_n}\Psi_n$ as in [E31][e31]. This gives
a smooth complete $k_n$, uniformly comparable to $h_n$, with
$D_{k_n}\xi_n=0$ and $|\xi_n|=1$. In the spin connection comparison,
the extra gradient terms are bounded by $C|d\sigma_n|$ because
$\sigma_n$ is uniformly bounded above and away from zero. For example,
use the conformal connection formula with $u=(2/3)\log\sigma_n$ and
differentiate the harmonic weight $e^{-3u/2}$. The norm derivative
satisfies $|d\sigma_n|\le|\nabla^{h_n}\Psi_n|$. Thus (5), also with
the uniformly comparable $k_n$ measure, gives
$\|\nabla^{k_n}\xi_n\|_{L^p(k_n)}=O(n^{-1/2})$ for $p=2,4$.
The unit harmonic identity gives $s_{k_n}=-4|\nabla^{k_n}\xi_n|^2$ by
Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz. It follows that

\[
\int_N |\nabla^{k_n}\xi_n|^2\,dV_{k_n}=O(n^{-1}),\qquad
J_{k_n}(\xi_n)^4=O(n^{-2}),\qquad
\|s_{k_n}\|_{L^2(k_n)}=O(n^{-1}).
\tag{7}
\]

On $U$, $g_b$ is exactly Euclidean in the same coordinates throughout
the support and cutoff. Therefore the local E11 computation is
unchanged, giving
$W(g_n)_{1212}=\tfrac14 n^{1/2}\chi\cos(nx^1)+O(n^{-1/2})$ on $U$.
The squared cosine integral against $\chi^2$ tends to
$\tfrac12\int_U\chi^2>0$. The signature relation on the compact standard
sphere gives $E_+(g_n)=\tfrac18\int |W(g_n)|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2\,dV_{g_n}$,
so $E_+(g_n)\ge c_1 n$ for all large $n$ and some $c_1>0$.
Four-dimensional chiral Weyl energy is conformally invariant on the
punctured manifold. Its compact integral is unchanged by removing the
pole, hence $E_+(h_n)=E_+(k_n)=E_+(g_n)$. By [E12][e12], for the unit
spinor the squared $L^2$ norm of the Weyl projection of
$d^\nabla(P_{k_n}\xi_n)$ is $E_+(k_n)/2$, so it diverges. Equations (6)–(7)
therefore fail to provide Weyl control even for the actual Green field
with fixed unit end datum and no interior zeros. The example belongs
to the standard sphere and tests the specified first-order controls.
