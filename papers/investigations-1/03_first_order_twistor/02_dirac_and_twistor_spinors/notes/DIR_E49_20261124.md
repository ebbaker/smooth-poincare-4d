**Result.** For $L>\sqrt2\,\pi$ the unit-volume Yamabe minimizer $g_L$ of
$S^1(L)\times S^3$ is an explicit Delaunay metric. Its first positive
eigenvalue is exactly $s/3$, and

\[
 \epsilon(g_L)=\frac{3\lambda_5}{s}-1=\tfrac{15}{16}(1-m)+O\bigl((1-m)^2\bigr),
 \qquad
 1-\frac{Y(g_L)}{Y(S^4)}=\tfrac38(1-m)+O\bigl((1-m)^2\log\tfrac1{1-m}\bigr),
\]

where $1-m\sim16e^{-L}$. So $\epsilon(g_L)\to0$ as $L\to\infty$ on a manifold
that is not a sphere. The five lowest modes have the round joint law in the
limit, as the near-equality theorem requires, while their image passes
arbitrarily close to the origin of $\mathbb R^5$ and the trace-free Ricci
tensor keeps $\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2=s^2/12$. The upper bound for $\epsilon$ and
the formula for $Y$ are closed forms. The coefficient $15/16$ as an equality
and the identification of $\lambda_5$ are numerical. The classification of
constant scalar curvature metrics in this class is imported from Schoen.

## The family

Let $g_0=dt^2+g_{S^3}$ on $S^1(L)\times S^3$ with the unit round $S^3$, so
$s_{g_0}=6$ and $|S^3|=2\pi^2$. For $g=u(t)^2g_0$ the conformal scalar
equation in dimension four is $s_g=u^{-3}(-6u''+6u)$. For $m\in(0,1)$ put

\[
 u_m(t)=\operatorname{dn}(x\,|\,m),\qquad x=\frac{t}{\sqrt{2-m}},\qquad m'=1-m .
\]

From $\operatorname{dn}''=(2-m)\operatorname{dn}-2\operatorname{dn}^3$ one
gets $u''=u-2u^3/(2-m)$, so $g_m=u_m^2g_0$ has constant scalar curvature
$s=12/(2-m)$. Its first integral is
$u'^2=u^2-u^4/(2-m)-m'/(2-m)$. The function $u_m$ has minimal period
$T(m)=2K(m)\sqrt{2-m}$, which increases from $\sqrt2\,\pi$ at $m=0$ to
infinity at $m=1$. With $I_{2j}=\int_0^K\operatorname{dn}^{2j}$ one has
$I_0=K$, $I_2=E$ and $(2j+1)I_{2j+2}=2j(2-m)I_{2j}-(2j-1)m'I_{2j-2}$, so
$I_4=\tfrac13\bigl(2(2-m)E-m'K\bigr)$. The volume is $4\pi^2\sqrt{2-m}\,I_4$
and the Yamabe energy of $g_m$ on the circle of length $T(m)$ is

\[
 Y(m)=s\,{\rm Vol}^{1/2}=24\pi\,(2-m)^{-3/4}\,I_4(m)^{1/2},\qquad
 Y(S^4)=8\sqrt6\,\pi .
\]

At $m=1$ the metric is the round sphere in cylindrical coordinates,
$u=\operatorname{sech}t$, and $Y=Y(S^4)$. At $m=0$ it is the product with
$L=\sqrt2\,\pi$. The maximum $u=1$ is the equator of a bulge of curvature
$1/(2-m)$ and the minimum $u=\sqrt{m'}$ is a neck, so $\sqrt{m'}$ is the
ratio of the neck radius to the bulge radius. From
$E=1+\tfrac14m'(\Lambda-1)+O(m'^2\Lambda)$ and
$K=\tfrac12\Lambda+O(m'\Lambda)$ with $\Lambda=\log(16/m')$,

\[
 I_4=\tfrac23\bigl(1+\tfrac34m'\bigr)+O(m'^2\Lambda),\qquad
 Y(m)^2=384\pi^2\bigl(1-\tfrac34m'\bigr)+O(m'^2\Lambda),\qquad
 T(m)=\Lambda+O(m'\Lambda).
\]

The logarithms cancel at first order in $I_4$. This gives the stated deficit
$\tfrac38m'$ and $m'\sim16e^{-L}$.

## Which metric minimizes

Schoen's analysis of this class gives the following
([R99](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_DIRAC_B23_20261124.md)).
A positive solution of the Yamabe equation on $S^1(L)\times S^3$ lifts to
$\mathbb R\times S^3\cong\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\}$ with singularities at
both ends, and the theorem of Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck makes it a
function of $t$. The positive periodic solutions of $u''=u-(s/6)u^3$ are the
constant and, up to scale and translation, the functions $u_m$. On a circle
of length $L$ the constant scalar curvature metrics in the class are
therefore the product and, for each $k\ge1$ with $L/k>\sqrt2\,\pi$, the
metric $u_m^2g_0$ with $T(m)=L/k$ wrapped $k$ times. Schoen leaves the
monotonicity of the period in the parameter unchecked. For dimension four the
computation record confirms it on a grid of 2,200 values of $m$ reaching
$m'=10^{-12}$.

A minimizer exists because the class is not round. It is stable, meaning
$3\lambda_1\ge s$. The product has $\lambda_1=(2\pi/L)^2<2=s/3$ once
$L>\sqrt2\,\pi$. For $k\ge2$ the translation mode found below is an
eigenfunction with $3\lambda=s$ and $2k\ge4$ zeros on the circle, and
Sturm oscillation for a periodic problem puts a nonconstant eigenfunction
with two zeros strictly below it. Both are unstable. Hence for
$L>\sqrt2\,\pi$ the minimizer is $g_L=g_{m(L)}$ with $T(m)=L$, normalized to
unit volume, unique up to rotation of the circle and the isometries of the
product. The computation record agrees: among the listed competitors the
one-period metric has the least energy at each of ten lengths between $4.45$
and $30$, and its even $k=0$ mode stays above the stability threshold.

## The spectrum

Write $\nu=3\lambda/s$, so that stability reads $\nu\ge1$ and the round
sphere has $\nu=1$ on its five coordinate functions. For
$\phi=f(x)Y_k(\theta)$, with $Y_k$ a spherical harmonic of degree $k$ on
$S^3$ of multiplicity $(k+1)^2$, the covariance
$L_g\phi=u^{-3}L_{g_0}(u\phi)$ of $L=6\Delta+s$ turns
$\Delta_g\phi=\lambda\phi$ into an equation for $h=\operatorname{dn}\cdot f$:

\[
 -h''+(2-m)(k+1)^2h=(4\nu+2)\operatorname{dn}^2(x\,|\,m)\,h
 \qquad\text{on }\mathbb R/2K\mathbb Z .
\tag{1}
\]

This is a Lamé equation with $N(N+1)=4\nu+2$. Three solutions are
elementary and were checked to thirty digits:

| Sector | $h$ | $\nu$ | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $k=0$, even | $\operatorname{dn}$ | $0$ | constants |
| $k=0$, odd | $\operatorname{sn}\operatorname{cn}$ | $1$ | $\phi=u'/u$, rotation of the circle |
| $k=1$, odd | $\operatorname{sn}\operatorname{cn}\operatorname{dn}$ | $5/2$ | continuation of the degree-two harmonics $x_5x_i$ |

The second row holds for a general reason. The field $\partial_t$ is
conformal Killing for $g_m$ and not Killing. For a conformal Killing field
$X$ on a metric of constant scalar curvature in dimension four, the function
$\sigma=\tfrac14\operatorname{div}X$ satisfies $\Delta\sigma=(s/3)\sigma$:
the flow of $X$ multiplies $g$ by $e^{2\tau\sigma+O(\tau^2)}$ and keeps the
scalar curvature equal to the same constant, and the linearization of
$s_{e^{2f}g}=e^{-2f}(s+6\Delta f-6|df|^2)$ is $6\Delta\sigma-2s\sigma$. So
the necklace satisfies $s=3\lambda_1$ for every $L>\sqrt2\,\pi$, with a
simple first eigenvalue when the other low modes lie above it.

The remaining low eigenvalues are the even $k=0$ mode $\nu_e(m)$, which rises
from $1$ at $m=0$ to $5/2$ at $m=1$, and the ground state $\nu_1(m)$ of the
$k=1$ sector, of multiplicity four, which falls from $3/2$ to $1$. All other
eigenvalues computed are at least $5/2$, on 93 values of $m$ between $0.002$
and $1-10^{-7}$ and in the sectors $k\le3$. The five lowest positive values are
therefore $1$ and the four smallest of $\{\nu_e,\nu_1,\nu_1,\nu_1,\nu_1\}$,
and in either order of $\nu_e$ and $\nu_1$

\[
 \epsilon(g_m)=\nu_1(m)-1,\qquad
 \tfrac35\epsilon\le\frac{A(g_m)}{s}\le\tfrac45\epsilon .
\]

**A closed upper bound.** In the variable $f$ the $k=1$ problem has Rayleigh
quotient
$\nu=\bigl(\int\operatorname{dn}^2f'^2+3(2-m)\int\operatorname{dn}^2f^2\bigr)/\bigl(4\int\operatorname{dn}^4f^2\bigr)$.
The trial function $f=\operatorname{dn}$, exact at $m=1$, together with
$\operatorname{dn}'^2=(1-\operatorname{dn}^2)(\operatorname{dn}^2-m')$ gives

\[
 0\le\epsilon(g_m)\le\frac{-I_6+4(2-m)I_4-m'I_2}{4I_6}-1
 =\tfrac{15}{16}m'+O(m'^2\Lambda),
\tag{2}
\]

using $I_6=\tfrac8{15}+\tfrac13m'+O(m'^2\Lambda)$. Given the identification
of $\lambda_5$, this proves $\epsilon(g_L)\to0$ with a rate.

**The first-order coefficient.** For fixed $x$,
$\operatorname{dn}^2(x|1-m')=\operatorname{sech}^2x+\tfrac12m'(\tanh^2x+x\tanh x\operatorname{sech}^2x)+O(m'^2)$.
Inserting this and $\nu=1+\epsilon$ in (1) with $k=1$ and pairing with the
limit solution $h_0=\operatorname{sech}^2$ on the line gives
$4\epsilon\int\operatorname{sech}^6=m'\bigl(4\int\operatorname{sech}^4-6\cdot\tfrac29\bigr)$,
that is $\epsilon=\tfrac{15}{16}m'$, with
$\int\operatorname{sech}^4=\tfrac43$ and $\int\operatorname{sech}^6=\tfrac{16}{15}$.
The effect of periodicity is of order $h_0(K)^2\sim m'^2$. This formal
calculation agrees with (2), and a thirty-digit evaluation gives
$(\nu_1-1)/m'=0.9375-0.3867\,m'$ at $m'=10^{-6}$ and $10^{-10}$.

between $1.19$ at $m=0$ and $1+\tfrac38m'$ near $m=1$.

## What the near-equality theorem sees

Normalize the five lowest eigenfunctions in $L^2$ of the unit-volume measure
and put $X=(u_1,\dots,u_5)/\sqrt5$. The measure is proportional to
$\operatorname{dn}^4dx\,d\theta$, and all the quantities below reduce to
integrals in $x$.

| $m'$ | $\epsilon$ | $\max_a\lvert q(a)-\tfrac{15}7\rvert$ | $\lVert\,\lvert X\rvert^2-1\rVert_2$ | $\sup\lvert X\rvert$ | $\inf\lvert X\rvert$ | $\int\Gamma(X,X)^2$ | Gram residual, $L^1$ / $L^2$ |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $10^{-1}$ | $9.0\times10^{-2}$ | $2.7\times10^{-1}$ | $1.2\times10^{-1}$ | $1.0278$ | $0.154$ | $1.1690$ | $4.0\times10^{-2}$ / $0.172$ |
| $10^{-2}$ | $9.3\times10^{-3}$ | $3.9\times10^{-2}$ | $1.8\times10^{-2}$ | $1.0026$ | $0.0494$ | $1.0535$ | $6.2\times10^{-3}$ / $0.202$ |
| $10^{-4}$ | $9.4\times10^{-5}$ | $4.3\times10^{-4}$ | $2.6\times10^{-4}$ | $1.00003$ | $0.0050$ | $1.0430$ | $7.4\times10^{-5}$ / $0.207$ |
| $10^{-6}$ | $9.4\times10^{-7}$ | $4.3\times10^{-6}$ | $3.2\times10^{-6}$ | $1.00000$ | $0.0005$ | $1.0429$ | $7.5\times10^{-7}$ / $0.207$ |

Here $q(a)=\int(\sum a_iu_i)^4$ was evaluated in the axial direction, a
transverse direction and their diagonal, and the Gram residual is
$\Gamma(X_5,X_5)-\tfrac14(1-X_5^2)$ for the axial coordinate. The fourth
moments tend to $15/7$ at the rate $4.57\,\epsilon$, the radial error tends
to zero, and the Wasserstein distance of the law of $X_5$ from the round law
of density $\tfrac34(1-t^2)$ falls to the discretization floor of the grid.
The manuscript's bounds $C\sqrt\epsilon$ and $C\epsilon^{1/4}$ hold with
room. The computation therefore agrees with the near-equality theorem on a
family that satisfies its hypotheses.

Three quantities do not converge, and they identify what the weak estimates
leave open.

1. $\inf|X|\approx\tfrac12\sqrt{m'}\to0$. The image of the neck is a thin
   tube along the axis of the sphere. Because the $k=1$ ground state is
   positive, $X$ never vanishes, and $X/|X|$ omits both poles, so it has
   degree zero as a map $S^1\times S^3\to S^4$: the bulge covers the sphere
   once and the neck covers it once more with the opposite orientation.
2. The Gram identity converges in $L^1$ and not in $L^2$. The squared energy
   density has $\int\Gamma(X,X)^2\to1.0429$. The excess
   $0.0429=2\pi^2\!\int\operatorname{sech}^8/(16\,|S^4|)$ is the conformally
   invariant fourth energy of the transition of $u'/u$ across the neck.
3. With $r$ the arclength along the axis, the metric is
   $dr^2+u^2g_{S^3}$, with radial and tangential sectional curvatures
   $\tfrac s{12}\mp c\,u^{-4}$, $c=m'/(2-m)$, and
   $|{\rm Ric}_0|^2=12c^2u^{-8}$. Hence
   \[
    \int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2\,dV=\frac{s^2\,{\rm Vol}}{12}
   \]
   for every $m$, and the radial Ricci curvature at the neck is
   $\tfrac s4-3/\bigl((2-m)m'\bigr)\to-\infty$. The density
   $12c^2u^{-4}$ concentrates in the neck. Aubry's Ricci hypothesis in
   [E45][e45] fails there, as it must.

## Consequences

Relative near equality in the five-eigenvalue inequality does not determine
the diffeomorphism type, the homotopy type or the fundamental group of a
closed four-manifold. On $S^1\times S^3$ the quantity $A(g)/s$ of unit-volume
Yamabe minimizers has infimum zero, not attained. The conclusions of the
near-equality theorem are close to the most that hold without a further
hypothesis: the law of five values converges, while pointwise rank, the
degree of $X/|X|$ and the $L^2$ norm of the Gram defect are affected by a
region of vanishing volume. The example says nothing about simply connected
manifolds. Its vanishing-volume region is a handle, and
$Y(S^1(L)\times S^3)\to Y(S^4)$, in agreement with Schoen and Kobayashi.
