## Setting

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian spin
four-manifold with $g$ exactly Euclidean in a coordinate ball centered at
$p$. Put $N=M\setminus\{p\}$ and $h=\omega^2g$ with $\omega$ smooth and
positive on $N$ and equal to $r^{-2}$ in the punctured flat ball, where
$r=|x|$. Inversion followed by a fixed reflection gives an oriented
Euclidean end chart with $R=r^{-1}$. This makes $h$ complete and flat
on the one end.

Use $X\cdot X=-|X|^2$ and the $S_h^+$ on which real self-dual two-forms
act. Fix a smooth ordinary harmonic spinor $\Psi\in\Gamma(S_h^+)$ with
$D_h\Psi=0$, unit constant limit $\Psi_\infty$ in the oriented flat spin
frame, and
\[
 u=\Psi-\Psi_\infty=O(R^{-3}),\qquad \nabla^h\Psi=O(R^{-4})
\]
on the end. Write $\beta:S_g^+\to S_h^+$ for the unitary conformal
identification on $N$ and take the twistor-weight field
$\psi=\omega^{-1/2}\beta^{-1}\Psi$. The packet assumes its smooth compact
extension with the prescribed jet at $p$. The extension and interior zero
set are unnecessary for the integral computation.

## Integrated Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz identity

For large $R$ let $\Omega_R$ be the compact region bounded by the
Euclidean sphere $S_R$ in the end, with outward normal $\nu=\partial_R$.
In the original compact chart it is $-\omega^{-1}\partial_r=-r^2\partial_r$.
The Riemannian untwisted identity in the stated Clifford convention is
$D_h^2=(\nabla^h)^*\nabla^h+s_h/4$. Setting $D_h\Psi=0$ and integrating
the real inner product against $\Psi$ gives
\[
 \int_{\Omega_R}\left(|\nabla^h\Psi|^2+
                   \tfrac14 s_h|\Psi|^2\right)dV_h
 = B(R),\qquad
 B(R)=\int_{S_R}\operatorname{Re}\langle\nabla^h_\nu\Psi,\Psi\rangle\,dA_h
     =\tfrac12\int_{S_R}\partial_R|\Psi|^2\,dA_h.
\]
Indeed the rough-Laplacian term equals the integral of
$|\nabla^h\Psi|^2$ minus the displayed boundary integral. This is the
integration-by-parts sign in the scalar-flat case of the Riemannian
spinorial mass argument. The $s_h/4$ term is retained for the present
metric. [Ammann–Humbert, *Positive Mass Theorem for the Yamabe Problem on
Spin Manifolds*, corrected 15 December 2005 PDF, proof of Theorem 2.2
through (2.3), PDF pp. 3–4](https://ammann.app.uni-regensburg.de/preprints/masse/masse_corr.pdf#page=3).

The direct bound on $B(R)$ is $O(R^{-1})$ from area $O(R^3)$, bounded
$\Psi$ and derivative $O(R^{-4})$. On the exact flat end one can also
determine its sign for each sufficiently large $R$. There the constant
spinor is parallel and $D_h^2u=0$ says that each flat component of
$u$ is harmonic. By the divergence theorem $\int_{S_R}\partial_R u\,dA_h$
is independent of $R$, and the derivative bound makes it tend to zero.
The cross term with $\Psi_\infty$ in $B(R)$ therefore vanishes. Applying
ordinary integration by parts to $u$ on an annulus and sending its outer
radius to infinity gives
\[
 B(R)=-\int_{\{|y|>R\}}|\nabla^h u|^2\,dV_h\le0,\qquad
 |B(R)|=O(R^{-4}).
\]
Here the outer annular boundary term is bounded by
$C R^3 R^{-4}R^{-3}$ at radius $R$, and the squared derivative tail
is integrable by $\int_R^\infty t^{-8}t^3\,dt$.

Thus $\nabla^h\Psi\in L^2(N,h)$ and $s_h|\Psi|^2$ is absolutely
integrable because $s_h$ is supported in the compact interior. The
end difference $u$ is itself $L^2$ there, by
$\int^\infty t^{-6}t^3\,dt<\infty$. The total $\Psi$ has infinite
$L^2(N,h)$ norm because $|\Psi|\to1$ and the Euclidean end has infinite
volume. The unit boundary amplitude normalizes the asymptotic datum.
The geometric ADM mass is zero because the Euclidean end metric has
vanishing first derivatives in an end Cartesian chart, so the metric
flux in its definition vanishes identically there.

## Weighted scalar criterion and zeros

Let $\mathcal S=\int_N s_h|\Psi|^2\,dV_h$. The exhaustion yields the
finite identity
\[
 \mathcal S=-4\int_N|\nabla^h\Psi|^2\,dV_h\le0.
\]
Parallelism is equivalent to $\mathcal S=0$, hence to $\mathcal S\ge0$.
A sufficient pointwise input is $s_h\ge0$. For the compact formulation
with $\Delta_g=d^*d$ on functions, the dimension-four scalar law is
$s_h=\omega^{-3}(6\Delta_g\omega+s_g\omega)$ on $N$. Since
$|\Psi|_h^2=\omega|\psi|_g^2$ and $dV_h=\omega^4dV_g$, this gives
\[
 \mathcal S=\int_N(6\Delta_g\omega+s_g\omega)\,
                              \omega^2|\psi|_g^2\,dV_g.
\]
The scalar expression vanishes in the punctured flat ball by
$\Delta_g r^{-2}=0$ there. The displayed Laplacian is classical on
$N$ and the integral has compact support in $N$.
[Ammann–Humbert, corrected PDF, conformal Laplacian convention on
p. 2 and scalar transformation in Theorem 2.2](https://ammann.app.uni-regensburg.de/preprints/masse/masse_corr.pdf#page=2).

If $\Psi$ is parallel its norm is constant on the connected $N$ and
equals one, and the pointwise Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz identity then
forces $s_h=0$. In this case the compact spinor vanishes precisely at
$p$, where $|\psi|_g=r$ in the punctured flat chart. If an interior zero
occurs, the asymptotic amplitude implies the spinor cannot be parallel,
so $\mathcal S<0$. In the general case no division by $|\Psi|$ was used,
and $\Psi$ is already nonzero sufficiently far out by its prescribed limit.

The bulk identity determines the nonpositive sign of $\mathcal S$. The
criterion still requires an independently derived nonnegative lower
bound for this same weighted integral, or a construction forcing the
scalar-flat case. The harmonic field and prescribed jet alone supply no
such lower bound. A bound of the form $\mathcal S\ge-\varepsilon$ would
give $\|\nabla^h\Psi\|_2^2\le\varepsilon/4$. This is an $L^2$ estimate
for the first derivative on the supplied data. A critical-norm or
curvature estimate would require additional control.

## Compact transport and conditional recognition

When the criterion is met, $P_h\Psi=0$ because $\nabla^h\Psi=0$.
The twistor kernel transports for $h=\omega^2g$ by
$\Psi=\omega^{1/2}\beta\psi$, so $P_g\psi=0$ on $N$ and on all of
$M$ by smooth extension. This is the twistor weight. In dimension four,
the compact Dirac-weight field is
$\omega^{3/2}\beta^{-1}\Psi=\omega^2\psi$, harmonic on $N$.
[Friedrich, *On the conformal relation between twistors and Killing
spinors*, published 1990, §1 (1.1) and the weight on pp. 59–60](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/701462/WSGP_09-1989-1_7.pdf#page=3) ·
[Ammann–Humbert, corrected PDF, Dirac weight in Theorem 2.2](https://ammann.app.uni-regensburg.de/preprints/masse/masse_corr.pdf#page=3).

The invariant quaternionic partner $j\Psi$ is also parallel and the
pair spans $S_h^+$ at each point. The positive spin connection is flat.
Its curvature block in the shared chirality sees
$W_h^++(s_h/12)I$ and the trace-free Ricci block, so this yields
$W_h^+=0$ and $\operatorname{Ric}_h=0$. Conformal transport gives
$W_g^+=0$ on $N$, extending to $p$ by smoothness of $g$.
If $M$ is a smooth homotopy four-sphere, the compact signature and
developing-map steps of SH-E01 now give a diffeomorphism to standard
$S^4$. This uses the exact recognition implication and its topology.
