Under the supplied hypotheses below, the joint law of the five
normalized coordinates converges weakly in $\mathbb R^5$ to normalized
round area on the unit $S^4$. The argument takes the weak Gram estimates
as input.

## Test norms and integration by parts

Index the smooth closed unit-volume Riemannian metrics by $\ell$.
Use $dV_\ell$ for the volume probability measure, $\Delta_\ell=d^*d$,
$L_\ell=3\Delta_\ell/s_\ell$ for the supplied positive scalar
normalization $s_\ell>0$, and
$\Gamma_\ell(v,w)=(3/s_\ell)\langle dv,dw\rangle_{g_\ell}$.
Let $H_\ell$ have norm
$\|v\|_{H_\ell}^2=\int(v^2+\Gamma_\ell(v,v))\,dV_\ell$.
The five real $L^2(dV_\ell)$-orthonormal eigenfunctions are
$u_{\ell,i}$, with $X_{\ell,i}=u_{\ell,i}/\sqrt5$ and
$L_\ell X_{\ell,i}=\nu_{\ell,i}X_{\ell,i}$, where
$1\le\nu_{\ell,i}\le1+\varepsilon_\ell$ and $\varepsilon_\ell\to0$.
The packet assumes $|X_\ell|\le C$ everywhere,
$\||X_\ell|^2-1\|_{L^2(dV_\ell)}\to0$, and
\[
 e_\ell:=\max_{i,j}
 \left\|\Gamma_\ell(X_{\ell,i},X_{\ell,j})
          -\tfrac14(\delta_{ij}-X_{\ell,i}X_{\ell,j})\right\|_{H_\ell^*}
 \longrightarrow 0 .
\]
For a fixed smooth function $F$ on $\mathbb R^5$, all composition
estimates may use sup norms on the common closed ball $K$ of radius
$\max(C,1)$. The Gram matrix $G_\ell=(\Gamma_\ell(X_{\ell,i},X_{\ell,j}))$
is positive semidefinite. Its trace has integral
$\sum_i\nu_{\ell,i}/5\le1+\varepsilon_\ell$ by integration by
parts and the $L^2$ norms. The chain rule and the trace bound
for the largest eigenvalue of a positive semidefinite matrix give
\[
 \|F(X_\ell)\|_{H_\ell}^2
 \le \|F\|_{L^\infty(K)}^2
       +(1+\varepsilon_\ell)\|\nabla_{\mathbb R^5}F\|_{L^\infty(K)}^2 .
\]
In particular the five tests $(\partial_jF)(X_\ell)$ have uniformly
bounded $H_\ell$ norms, using two fixed derivatives of $F$.
There is no constant from an embedding of the varying domains.

Integration by parts and the chain rule give
\[
 \nu_{\ell,i}\int X_{\ell,i}F(X_\ell)\,dV_\ell
   =\int\Gamma_\ell(X_{\ell,i},F(X_\ell))\,dV_\ell
   =\sum_j\int G_{\ell,ij}(\partial_jF)(X_\ell)\,dV_\ell .
\]
Replace each Gram entry by $(\delta_{ij}-X_{\ell,i}X_{\ell,j})/4$.
The error is bounded by $e_\ell$ times the sum of the five test norms.
On the left, replacing $\nu_{\ell,i}$ by one costs at most
$\varepsilon_\ell C\|F\|_{L^\infty(K)}$. Thus the claimed identity before
passage to the limit has error $O_F(e_\ell+\varepsilon_\ell)$.

## Identification of the limit

The probability laws $\mu_\ell=(X_\ell)_*dV_\ell$ are supported in $K$,
so every subsequence has a further weakly convergent subsequence. If
$\mu$ is such a limit, testing the continuous bounded function
$(|x|^2-1)^2$ on $K$ shows $\int(|x|^2-1)^2\,d\mu=0$.
Consequently $\mu$ is supported on the unit sphere. The limiting
integration-by-parts identity for every $F\in C^\infty(\mathbb R^5)$ is
\[
 4\int_{S^4} x_i F\,d\mu
   =\int_{S^4}\sum_{j=1}^5
             (\delta_{ij}-x_ix_j)\partial_jF\,d\mu,
 \qquad 1\le i\le 5 .
 \tag{1}
\]
The derivatives on the right use only the tangential differential
of the restriction of $F$. Round probability $\sigma$ satisfies (1).
Indeed $V_i(x)=e_i-x_ix$ is tangent to $S^4$. For a tangent vector
$\tau$, its tangential derivative is $-x_i\tau$, since the other
term $-\tau_i x$ is normal. Thus $\operatorname{div}_{S^4}V_i=-4x_i$,
and integration of $\operatorname{div}_{S^4}(F V_i)$ gives (1).

For a homogeneous polynomial $P$ of degree $m\ge1$, take
$F=\partial_iP$ in the $i$-th identity and sum. The two Euler
identities are $\sum_i x_i\partial_iP=mP$ and
$\sum_{i,j}x_ix_j\partial_{ij}P=m(m-1)P$. Therefore
\[
 m(m+3)\int_{S^4}P\,d\mu
    =\int_{S^4}\left(\sum_{i=1}^5\partial_{ii}P\right)d\mu .
 \tag{2}
\]
In particular every nonconstant homogeneous harmonic polynomial
has zero integral. For general $P$, the right side has degree
$m-2$ or is zero. Equation (2), induction by degree, and unit mass
give the same moments for $\mu$ and $\sigma$. The restrictions of
real polynomials contain the constants and separate points of
$S^4$, so they are dense in $C(S^4)$ by Stone–Weierstrass. Hence
$\mu=\sigma$. Since every subsequence has a further subsequence
with this unique limit, the full sequence $\mu_\ell$ converges
weakly to $\sigma$.

## Scope and evidence

For each fixed $a>0$ the radial premise directly gives
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}_{g_\ell}
   \big\{\,\big||X_\ell|^2-1\big|>a\,\big\}
 \le a^{-2}\||X_\ell|^2-1\|_{L^2(dV_\ell)}^2 .
\]
The conclusions require the supplied estimates. The zero infimum
alone supplies no exceptional-volume rate here. The law of values
and the tested weak Gram identity establish no pointwise Gram
comparison, strong gradient convergence, or smooth-topology
conclusion.
