## Normalization and iteration

Let $(M,g)$ be any smooth closed connected four-manifold of volume
one with $g$ a Yamabe minimizer of constant scalar curvature $s>0$.
Use the nonnegative Laplacian $\Delta_g=d^*d$ and define
\[
 L=\frac3s\Delta_g,\qquad
 \Gamma(v,w)=\frac3s\langle dv,dw\rangle_g,\qquad
 \|w\|_H^2=\int_M \bigl(w^2+\Gamma(w,w)\bigr)\,dV_g .
\]
All integrals below use $dV_g$. Let $u_i$ for $1\le i\le5$ be
real $L^2$-orthonormal eigenfunctions for the first five positive
eigenvalues of $L$, counted with multiplicity. Assume
$Lu_i=\nu_i u_i$ with $1\le\nu_i\le1+\varepsilon$ and
$0<\varepsilon\le1$. The input inequality is
\[
 \|v\|_4^2\le Q(v),\qquad Q(v)=\int_M(v^2+2\Gamma(v,v)).
 \tag{1}
\]
If initially stated for positive smooth tests, it extends to all
smooth $v$ by applying it to $(v^2+\alpha^2)^{1/2}$ and letting
$\alpha$ decrease to zero. Indeed this substitution increases the
square by $\alpha^2$ and has gradient energy at most that of $v$.
Density then extends (1) to $H$ since $Q(v)\le2\|v\|_H^2$.
In particular the sign-changing polynomial tests below satisfy
$Q(v)^2-\int_M v^4\ge0$ for every real $t$.

For an eigenfunction $u$ with eigenvalue $\nu\le2$, test its
equation against $u^{p-1}$ for even dyadic $p\ge2$. Integration
by parts with $L$ gives
\[
 (p-1)\int_M u^{p-2}\Gamma(u,u)=\nu\int_M |u|^p,\qquad
 \int_M\Gamma(u^{p/2},u^{p/2})
    =\frac{p^2\nu}{4(p-1)}\int_M |u|^p.
\]
Applying (1) to $u^{p/2}$ yields
\[
 \|u\|_{2p}^p
 \le \left(1+\frac{p^2\nu}{2(p-1)}\right)\|u\|_p^p
 \le (1+2p)\|u\|_p^p .
\]
The product $\prod_{j\ge1}(1+2^{j+1})^{1/2^j}$ is finite,
since its logarithm is bounded by a constant times
$\sum_{j\ge1}(1+j)2^{-j}$. Taking the dyadic exponents to infinity
gives $\|u_i\|_\infty\le C$. Thus for any unit $c\in\mathbb R^5$,
\[
 \phi=\sum_i c_i u_i,\qquad r=(L-1)\phi,\qquad
 \|\phi\|_2=1,\quad \int_M\phi=0,\quad
 \|\phi\|_\infty\le C,\quad \|r\|_\infty\le C\varepsilon.
 \tag{2}
\]
The last bound follows from
$r=\sum_i c_i(\nu_i-1)u_i$ and $\sum_i|c_i|\le\sqrt5$.
Also $\int_M\Gamma(\phi,\phi)=1+\delta$ for
$\delta=\sum_i c_i^2(\nu_i-1)=\int_M\phi r\in[0,\varepsilon]$.
These constants are uniform in the metric, scalar curvature,
eigenbasis and direction.

## Exact sign-averaged deficit

For real mean-zero $w\in H$, set $v_\pm=1\pm t\phi+t^2w$.
Write $q=\int_M\phi^4$ and
\[
 A=3+2\delta,\qquad B=\int_M\bigl(\phi w+2\Gamma(\phi,w)\bigr),
 \qquad J=Q(w).
\]
Then $Q(v_\pm)=1+A t^2\pm2Bt^3+Jt^4$.
Here $B=3\int_M\phi w+2\int_M rw$ by integration by parts,
so the mixed coefficient includes the eigenvalue residual even
when $\phi$ is a linear combination of different eigenfunctions.
The mean of $v_+^4$ and $v_-^4$ pointwise is
\[
 (1+t^2w)^4+6t^2\phi^2(1+t^2w)^2+t^4\phi^4 .
\]
The mean $\mathcal D(t,\phi,w)$ of the squared deficits
$Q(v_\pm)^2-\int_M v_\pm^4$ is consequently
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal D(t,\phi,w)
 &=4\delta t^2+
    \bigl(\mathcal C_\phi(w)+12\delta+4\delta^2\bigr)t^4\\
 &\quad+\left(2AJ+4B^2-4\int_M w^3-6\int_M\phi^2w^2\right)t^6
    +\left(J^2-\int_M w^4\right)t^8,\\
 \mathcal C_\phi(w)
 &=9-q+4\int_M\Gamma(w,w)-4\int_M w^2-12\int_M\phi^2 w .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3}
\]
The sign average cancels every odd term without a moment assumption.
For each fixed $K$, when $\|w\|_H\le K$, (1) gives
$\|w\|_4\le\sqrt2 K$, and $J\le2K^2$.
The residual formula for $B$, (2), and $\|w\|_1\le\|w\|_2\le K$
bound $B$ by a constant depending only on $K$. Also
$\bigl|\int_M w^3\bigr|\le\|w\|_4^3$ by volume one and
$\int_M\phi^2w^2\le\|\phi\|_\infty^2\|w\|_2^2$. Thus both
higher even coefficients are bounded by $C_K$, as is
$\mathcal C_\phi(w)$ itself. The remainder after order four is
bounded in absolute value by $C_K|t|^6$ for $|t|\le1$.
This estimate uses only the $H$ norm of $w$.

Take $t=\varepsilon^{1/4}$, so $0<t\le1$.
Since $\mathcal D(t,\phi,w)\ge0$, (3) gives
\[
 \mathcal C_\phi(w)
 \ge -4\delta/t^2-12\delta-4\delta^2-C_K t^2
 \ge -C_K'\sqrt{\varepsilon}
 \qquad(\|w\|_H\le K).
 \tag{4}
\]
The quadratic coefficient contributes $O(\varepsilon/t^2)$
after dividing by $t^4$. The even remainder contributes $O_K(t^2)$.

## Directional fourth moments and sum of squares

For $b=\phi^2-1$, $\int_M b=0$ and $\|b\|_H$ is universally
bounded. In fact $\|b\|_2^2=q-1$ is bounded by (2), and
$\int_M\Gamma(b,b)\le4\|\phi\|_\infty^2(1+\delta)$.
The exact energy identity follows by using $\phi^3$ in the equation
$L\phi=\phi+r$:
\[
 \int_M\Gamma(b,b)
 =4\int_M\phi^2\Gamma(\phi,\phi)
 =\frac43\int_M\Gamma(\phi,\phi^3)
 =\frac43\left(q+\int_M\phi^3 r\right).
\]
As $\int_M\phi^2 b=q-1$, this gives
\[
 \mathcal C_\phi(b)
    =25-\frac{35}{3}q+\frac{16}{3}\int_M \phi^3 r .
\]
The residual term is $O(\varepsilon)$ by (2).
Inserting the last identity into (4) yields
$q\le15/7+C_0\sqrt{\varepsilon}$ for every unit direction with one
universal $C_0$.

Write $u=(u_1,\ldots,u_5)$ and $S=\sum_i u_i^2$, so $\int_M S=5$.
On the coefficient sphere $S^4$ use uniform probability measure.
The moment formula of [E38][e38],
$\mathbb E_c(c\cdot z)^4=(3/35)|z|^4$, gives
\[
 \mathbb E_c\,q(c)
 =\frac3{35}\int_M S^2
 =\frac{15}{7}+\frac3{35}\int_M(S-5)^2.
\]
The directional upper bound then implies
$\int_M(S-5)^2\le (35/3)C_0\sqrt{\varepsilon}$.
For the lower bound in every individual direction, consider the
homogeneous quartic polynomial
\[
 P(c)=\left(\frac{15}{7}+C_0\sqrt{\varepsilon}\right)|c|^4
       -\int_M(c\cdot u)^4 .
\]
On the coefficient sphere it is nonnegative and its integral is at
most $C_0\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. The restrictions of homogeneous quartic
polynomials form a fixed finite-dimensional space on $S^4$.
The probability $L^1$ norm and sup norm are genuine norms there
because of continuity and full support. Their equivalence gives
$\|P\|_\infty\le C\sqrt{\varepsilon}$. Restoring the main constant
therefore yields, with a universal $C$,
\[
 \boxed{
 \sup_{|c|=1}\left|\int_M(c\cdot u)^4-\frac{15}{7}\right|
     \le C\sqrt{\varepsilon},\qquad
 \int_M\left(\sum_i u_i^2-5\right)^2\le C\sqrt{\varepsilon}.}
\]
In particular $X=u/\sqrt5$ satisfies
$\||X|^2-1\|_2\le C\varepsilon^{1/4}$, with a uniform bound on
$|X|$. The estimates require the supplied cluster and the normalized
Sobolev inequality. They provide estimates on the functions and their
moments without an existence or geometric compactness conclusion.
