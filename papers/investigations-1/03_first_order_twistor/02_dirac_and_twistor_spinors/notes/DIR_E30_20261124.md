## E23 · First variation and finite stress moments

Use a fixed supplied smooth volume-one metric on a smooth, closed,
connected, oriented Riemannian homotopy four-sphere. Let
\(\Delta=d^*d\), \(\lambda=\lambda_1(g)>0\), and \(s=3\lambda\).
Let \(F:M\to(S^4,h)\) be smooth, with \(\Delta F=\lambda F\) in
\(\mathbb R^5\) and \(|dF|^2=\lambda\). The target \(h\) has sectional
curvature one. No rank condition is needed. The sphere tension is
\(\tau(F)=-\Delta F+|dF|^2 F=0\).

For \(A=F^*h\), the divergence is
\(\operatorname{div}A=h(\tau(F),dF)+\tfrac12 d|dF|^2=0\).
The constant scalar curvature and contracted Bianchi identity give
\(\operatorname{div}\operatorname{Ric}=0\). Since \(\operatorname{tr} A=\lambda\)
and \(s=3\lambda\), both \(H=A-\lambda g/4\) and \(T=\operatorname{Ric}-3A\)
are trace-free and divergence-free.

At \(F\) the potential of \(L_{g,F}=6\Delta_g+s_g-3|dF|^2\) vanishes,
and \(u=1\) is the simple volume-one \(L^2\)-normalized ground state.
For a smooth metric path with initial tangent \(K\), differentiation
of the eigenvalue equation and integration against the unperturbed
volume give
\[
D_g\mu[K]=\int_M
\left[\nabla^j\nabla^i K_{ij}+\Delta\operatorname{tr}K
      -\langle\operatorname{Ric}-3A,K\rangle\right]dV_g
=-\int_M\langle T,K\rangle\,dV_g .
\]
Here \(D_g\Delta[K]1=0\) and the scalar curvature formula has the plus
sign on \(\Delta\operatorname{tr}K\) with the nonnegative Laplacian.
The calculation uses the simple isolated eigenpair. A small smooth
metric change preserves its smooth local branch and positivity of
the eigenfunction, hence it remains the ground state.

Writing \(D=|\nabla dF|^2\), the unit-round target term in the
harmonic-map Bochner formula is \(|A|^2-\lambda^2\). Constancy of
\(|dF|^2\) gives \(0=D+\langle\operatorname{Ric},A\rangle+|A|^2-\lambda^2\).
The trace terms sum to \(\lambda^2\), leaving
\(D+|H|^2=-\langle\operatorname{Ric}_0,H\rangle\), as in [E21][e21].
Thus \(D_g\mu[H]=\int_M(D+4|H|^2)\,dV_g\), also for the normalized
path since \(\operatorname{tr}H=0\). If this derivative vanishes, then
\(F^*h=(\lambda/4)g\). This is a local isometry from the scaled domain,
hence a covering by compactness, and a diffeomorphism by connectedness
and simple connectivity of the target. Conversely, a homothetic
diffeomorphism gives \(H=D=0\). For a nonhomothetic \(F\), the positive
derivative makes the eigenvalue positive for all sufficiently small
positive times on the indicated fixed-map path.

For a real orthonormal first-eigenspace basis \((\phi_a)_{a=1}^N\), put
\(K_{ab}=(d\phi_a\odot d\phi_b)_0\) for \(a\le b\). The \(N(N+1)/2\)
directions are fixed before choosing \(F\) and are first-order
volume-preserving. With \(F^I=\sum_a c_{Ia}\phi_a\) and
\(Q_{ab}=\sum_I c_{Ia}c_{Ib}\), the ordered-pair identity
\(H=\sum_{a,b}Q_{ab}K_{ab}\) includes both off-diagonal contributions.
For \(f_j\to F\) strongly in ambient \(W^{1,2}\), the estimate
\[
\|f_j^*h-F^*h\|_1
\le (\|df_j\|_2+\|dF\|_2)\|df_j-dF\|_2
\]
passes all moments of the stresses against these bounded tensors.
If the moments of \(\operatorname{Ric}_0-3(f_j^*h)_0\) go to zero,
then \(\int\langle T,H\rangle=0\) and the covering argument applies.
For the quantitative version, \(Q\) is positive semidefinite,
\(\operatorname{tr}Q=1\), and the symmetric matrix
\(\mathcal M_{ab}(F)=\int\langle T,K_{ab}\rangle\,dV_g\) gives
\(\int(D+4|H|^2)=-\operatorname{tr}(Q\mathcal M(F))
\le\|\mathcal M(F)\|_{\mathrm{op}}\). The stress then vanishes everywhere
in the exact equality case.

For a smooth variation of both metric and map, the derivative of a
simple lowest eigenvalue at a general smooth pair \((g,f)\), with
normalized ground state \(u\), is obtained by differentiating its
Rayleigh numerator and denominator at fixed \(u\). With
\(U=s_g-3|df|^2\), \(A_f=f^*h\), \(K=\dot g\), and \(Z=\dot f\)
tangent to the target, it is
\[
\begin{aligned}
\dot\mu={}&\int_M\Big[-6\langle K,du\otimes du\rangle
 +\big(\dot s+3\langle K,A_f\rangle
            -6\langle df,\nabla^f Z\rangle\big)u^2\\
 &\hspace{38mm}+\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}K
     \big(6|du|^2+(U-\mu)u^2\big)\Big]dV_g .
\end{aligned}
\]
At the exact pair \((g,F)\), the integral of the map derivative is
zero since \(\int\langle dF,\nabla^F Z\rangle=-\int h(\tau(F),Z)=0\)
and \(u=1\). At an approximating pair the \(u^2\)-weights and kinetic
terms matter, even for trace-free directions. The finite test is an
\(L^1\) argument about tensors. It requires the moments as input and
does not differentiate a supremum over degree-one or balanced maps.
The production of the moments from an optimizing sequence remains
the [B13][b13] premise.

## E24 · Total fourth-energy excess

Let \(M\) be any smooth, closed, oriented Riemannian four-manifold with
a fixed smooth metric. Fix the outward orientation of unit round
\(S^4\), \(V=8\pi^2/3\), and \(E_4(f)=\int |df|_{\mathrm{HS}}^4\,dV_g\).
Take smooth \(f_j\) of degree \(+1\) with strong ambient \(W^{1,2}\)
convergence to smooth sphere-valued \(F\). For finite energy liminf,
use an energy-realizing subsequence and set \(B=dF,\ G_j=df_j-dF\).
Then \(B\) is bounded, \(\|G_j\|_2\to0\), and \(\|G_j\|_4\) is bounded.
In particular \(\int|G_j|^3\le\|G_j\|_2\|G_j\|_4^2\to0\).

Expanding the four differential columns of
\(\det(f_j,(df_j)_1,\ldots,(df_j)_4)\) leaves the four-\(G_j\) term.
Each mixed term is \(o(1)\) in \(L^1\) by the \(L^1,L^2,L^3\) bounds.
In the four-\(B\) term alone, replacing \(f_j\) by \(F\) costs
\(O(\|f_j-F\|_1)\). For the pure \(G_j\) term, keep \(f_j\) in
the first column and project the other columns to \(f_j^\perp\).
Their product of four singular values has magnitude at most
\(|G_j|^4/16\) by arithmetic–geometric mean applied to their squares.
This avoids an estimate of \(\int |f_j-F||G_j|^4\), for which strong
\(W^{1,2}\) and a fourth-energy bound alone do not give \(o(1)\).
For the round dilations from [E25][e25], \(F=q\) is the limiting
constant and \(G_j=df_j\). Conformality and change of variables give
\(\int |f_j-q||G_j|^4=16\int_{S^4}|z-q|\,dV_h(z)>0\). In the same
example the determinant with first column \(q\) integrates to zero:
its density is \((q\cdot f_j)J_{f_j}\), and the average of \(q\cdot z\)
over the round target is zero.
The cross terms of the fourth-energy expansion also vanish in \(L^1\).
Integration of the two splits yields
\[
\liminf_j E_4(f_j)\ \ge\ E_4(F)+16V|1-\deg F|,
\qquad 16V=128\pi^2/3.
\]

The insertion check gives the same factor. On the stereographic core,
\(G_\epsilon^*h=b_\epsilon^2\delta\) with
\(b_\epsilon=2\epsilon/(\epsilon^2+r^2)\). The Hilbert–Schmidt fourth
power is \(16 b_\epsilon^4\) with Euclidean domain metric. In normal
coordinates, the metric and volume factor together incur \(O(r^2)\)
relative error. The \(b_\epsilon^4\,dx\) core integral tends to \(V\)
and the Euclidean tail is \(O((\epsilon/\rho)^4)\) for
\(\rho=\epsilon^{1/2}\). Cutoff derivatives in the bubble annulus
are \(O(\epsilon/\rho^2)\), and the background flattening has bounded
derivative. Their fourth-energy integrals are \(o(1)\). The
orientation-preserving target correction is \(O(\rho^4)\) from the
identity in \(C^1\), so it changes fourth energy by \(o(1)\) and
preserves degree. The background cost is \(E_4(F)\) and each positive
insertion adds \(16V\). The near-zero property follows from the
[checked E15 scalar-form estimate and balance correction][e15].
The unit-volume round example of a reflection and two distinct
positive insertions is balanced, has near-zero modified Yamabe value,
and has limiting total energy \(48V\), achieving the inequality for
\(\deg F=-1\). Round minimality uses Talenti's sharp case with
dimension four and exponent two. [E17][e17] · [Talenti][talenti].

For recognition, suppose separately that \(M\) is a homotopy
four-sphere and \(g\) is a supplied unit-volume Yamabe minimizer.
Suppose each degree-\(+1\) map is \(g\)-balanced and its modified
Yamabe value tends to zero. The constant test, Yamabe stability and
Poincaré give \(s=3\lambda_1\) and \(\|df_j\|_2^2\to\lambda_1\).
Every subsequence then has a further strong \(W^{1,2}\) cluster
subsequence by the gap above the first eigenspace. For these smooth
\(F\), \(|dF|^2=\lambda_1\) and \(E_4(F)=\lambda_1^2\).
Any nonzero degree gives a homothetic diffeomorphism by Listing
Theorem 3 (6). The domain is closed spin and dimension four. For
\(a_i=\sigma_i(dF)^2\), the required threshold
\(\sqrt{24\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k}\) is at most \(3\lambda_1=s\).
Both signs of nonzero degree qualify. [E14][e14] · [Listing][listing].

Consequently \(\liminf E_4(f_j)<\lambda_1^2+16V\) produces at least
one degree-\(+1\) limit. The same strict bound on \(\limsup\) gives
degree \(+1\) at every strong \(W^{1,2}\) cluster limit. The bound
\(\lambda_1^2+32V\) excludes degree \(-1\) below it, and retains
degree \(+1\) if degree zero has been excluded independently. At
\(\lambda_1^2+16V\), failure through a degree-zero limit remains
conditional on supplying the corresponding eigenmap. The round
example proves the price per unit of lost degree and recognizes
the smooth type despite orientation reversal.

## E25 · Signed atoms and the local cap

Under the fixed metric and strong smooth-limit hypotheses of E24,
add \(\sup_j E_4(f_j)<\infty\). Fix any joint weak-* limits \(\mu\)
of the positive energy measures and \(\kappa\) of the signed
Jacobian measures. The two \(L^1\) expansions above give
\(\nu=\mu-|dF|^4dV_g\ge0\) and, for
\(\eta=\kappa-F^*\omega\),
\[
\left|\int_M \phi\,d\eta\right|\le \tfrac1{16}\int_M|\phi|\,d\nu
\qquad(\phi\in C(M;\mathbb R)).
\]
Taking total variation gives \(16|\eta|\le\nu\). The original
pointwise inequality likewise gives \(16|\kappa|\le\mu\).

For each fixed center \(x\), take a subsequence with summable squared
\(L^2\) errors in map values, still with the given measure limits.
Coarea gives vanishing \(L^2\) errors on almost every slice
\(S_r=\partial B_r(x)\), \(0<r<\operatorname{inj}_g(x)\).
Fatou gives finite \(\liminf_j\int_{S_r}|d_\tau f_j|^4\) for almost
every such \(r\). On each such slice, select a bounded subsequence
and use the compact embedding \(W^{1,4}(S_r)\hookrightarrow C^0(S_r)\)
since the slice dimension is three. The uniform limit is \(F\)
by the \(L^2\) convergence. This extraction can depend on \(x,r\).
Disjointness of the slices excludes only countably many further
radii with \(\mu(S_r)>0\). For a remaining radius, convergence
of the signed masses on \(B_r(x)\) follows by approximating its
indicator by continuous functions with error confined to shrinking
closed neighborhoods of its boundary. The errors are controlled
by \(1/16\) of the positive energy masses of those neighborhoods,
whose limsups are bounded by \(\mu\) there. The boundary-null
condition makes those bounds tend to zero. The same argument
works for the limiting signed measure by \(16|\kappa|\le\mu\).

On a good slice, let \(\delta_j=\sup_{S_r}d_h(f_j,F)\). The short
geodesic collar contributes at most
\(C\delta_j\int_{S_r}(|d_\tau f_j|+|d_\tau F|)^3=o(1)\) in absolute
Jacobian mass. The collar orientation \(dt\wedge\operatorname{or}(S_r)\)
glues its initial end to \(B_r\) and final end to \(-B_r\).
Flattening each ball map in the normal boundary variable preserves
its volume integral by Stokes and permits smooth gluing. The signed
volume integral of the resulting map on the oriented double is in
\(V\mathbb Z\). Passing to the limit gives \(\eta(B_r(x))\in V\mathbb Z\).
Good radii increasing to a specified radius extend the result to all
open balls below the injectivity radius by continuity from below
of the finite signed measure. Shrinking the radius to zero gives
\(\eta(\{x\})\in V\mathbb Z\).

For \(A=\{x:|\eta|(\{x\})\ge V\}\), finiteness follows from the total
variation bound. On a compact subset of \(M\setminus A\), a finite
cover by half-balls with enlarged total variation \(<V\) makes the
vanishing of \(\eta(B_r(x))\) uniform for small \(r\). To infer
vanishing of the measure off \(A\), integrate these ball masses
against \(\phi\in C_c(M\setminus A)\), divide by Euclidean radius-\(r\)
ball volume, and use Fubini. The resulting average of \(\phi\) on
\(B_r(y)\) tends to \(\phi(y)\) uniformly on \(M\) by compactness
and the Riemannian small-ball volume asymptotic. This proves
\[
\eta=V\sum_\ell k_\ell\delta_{x_\ell},\qquad
\nu\ge16V\sum_\ell |k_\ell|\delta_{x_\ell},\qquad
\sum_\ell k_\ell=1-\deg F .
\]
The lower bound concerns the absolute value of each net signed
charge. The actual energy atom may carry excess, and the weak limit
of absolute Jacobians may have atoms even where the signed defect
cancels, as the examples in [E25][e25] show.

The strict local criterion \(\mu(\{x\})<16V\) for every \(x\) implies
\(\deg F=1\). The sequence-level cap
\[
\forall x\in M,\qquad
\lim_{r\downarrow0}\limsup_j\int_{B_r(x)}|df_j|^4\,dV_g<16V
\]
implies this for each joint weak-* limit by positive-measure
portmanteau on the open balls. Under the E24 Yamabe, balance and
near-zero hypotheses the cap and bounded fourth energy then give a
recognizing limit. The global budget bounds total excess and hence
the net loss \(16V|\sum_\ell k_\ell|\). The local estimate bounds
\(16V\sum_\ell|k_\ell|\) by the total excess and excludes each signed
atom under the strict cap, even with a larger total excess. A round
dilation shows sharpness of the local cap for the general theorem.
The balanced round reflection family with two distinct insertion
points also loses degree at equality in the atom cap and still
recognizes the sphere. Neither example supplies a degree-zero
balanced first eigenmap.
