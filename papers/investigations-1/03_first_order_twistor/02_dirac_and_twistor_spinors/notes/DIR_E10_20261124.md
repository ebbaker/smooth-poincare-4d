## Spinor scale and restricted Clifford trace

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian spin
four-manifold. Use the positive half-spin bundle on which real
self-dual forms act, the Levi–Civita spin connection and
$X\cdot X=-|X|^2$. Suppose the given smooth positive half-spinor
$\psi$ has a single zero $p$ with
$(\nabla_X^g\psi)(p)=X\cdot_g\eta$ for $0\ne\eta\in S_{g,p}^-$.
On $M^\circ=M\setminus\{p\}$ let
$\sigma=|\psi|_g$, $h=\sigma^{-4}g$, and
$\phi=\sigma^{-1}\beta\psi$, where $\beta$ is the unitary conformal
spin identification for $h=\sigma^{-4}g$. These fields are smooth
on $M^\circ$ and $|\phi|_h=1$. E07 gives
\[
|P_h\phi|_h=\sigma|P_g\psi|_g,\qquad
dV_h=\sigma^{-8}dV_g,\qquad
\|P_h\phi\|_{L^4(h)}=J_g(\psi).
\]
The conformal calculation is local and applies because $\sigma>0$
on the punctured manifold. The jet gives finite cost by E06.

Use real inner products on spinors, meaning the real parts of the
Hermitian pairings. At a point of $M^\circ$, write
$E=(\mathbb R\phi)^\perp\subset S_h^+$ and
$c:T^*M^\circ\otimes_{\mathbb R}S_h^+\to S_h^-$ for Clifford contraction.
In an $h$-orthonormal frame,
$c^*\xi=-\sum_i e^i\otimes(e_i\cdot_h\xi)$ by skew-adjointness of
Clifford action. Thus $cc^*=4I$. The adjoint of the restriction
$c_E$ to $T^*M^\circ\otimes E$ is $c_E^*=\pi_E c^*$, with the
projection applied to the spinor factor. The restriction
$c_{\mathbb R\phi}$ sends $\alpha\otimes\phi$ to
$\alpha^\sharp\cdot_h\phi$. This is a real isometry from a real
four-dimensional space onto $S_h^-$, by Clifford norm and polarization.
The domain is the orthogonal direct sum of the two restrictions, so
\[
 c_Ec_E^*=cc^*-c_{\mathbb R\phi}c_{\mathbb R\phi}^*
          =3I.
\]
The derivative $T=\nabla^h\phi$ lies in $T^*M^\circ\otimes E$ because
the spinor has constant unit length. The twistor projection is
$P_h\phi=(I-\frac14c^*c)T$. In particular,
\[
 |P_h\phi|_h^2=|T|_h^2-\tfrac14|c_E T|_h^2,\qquad
 |P_h\phi|_h\le|\nabla^h\phi|_h\le2|P_h\phi|_h.
\]
Both factors are sharp pointwise. A nonzero tensor in $\ker c_E$
attains the first equality. A nonzero tensor in $\operatorname{im}c_E^*$
satisfies $|c_E T|^2=3|T|^2$ and attains the second equality.
These tensors are allowed first derivatives of a unit spinor, since a
local field with the desired orthogonal derivative can be normalized
without changing that derivative at the point.

## Positive spin connection in the unit spinor frame

The invariant quaternionic structure $j$ on $S_h^+$ is antiunitary,
satisfies $j^2=-I$, and commutes with $\nabla^h$. It is built from the
standard $\mathrm{SU}(2)$ representation, which commutes with right
quaternionic multiplication as in E06. The ordered pair $(\phi,j\phi)$
is a global unitary spin frame with the invariant determinant.
Taking frame columns for covariant derivatives, write
$\nabla^h\phi=i\alpha\phi+b\,j\phi$ with a real one-form $\alpha$ and a
complex one-form $b$. Then
\[
A=\begin{pmatrix}
i\alpha&-\bar b\\
b&-i\alpha
\end{pmatrix}.
\]
Define the matrix norm on $\mathfrak{su}(2)$ by
$\langle U,V\rangle_{\mathrm{mat}}=-\frac12\operatorname{tr}(UV)$.
The squared norm of $A(X)$ is $\alpha(X)^2+|b(X)|^2$, the squared
norm of $\nabla^h_X\phi$. Summing on $h$-orthonormal tangent vectors
and integrating yields
\[
J_g(\psi)\le \|A\|_{L^4(h),\mathrm{mat}}\le 2J_g(\psi).
\]
The shared chiral connection convention uses the inner product
$-2\operatorname{tr}(UV)$. In that convention the connection norm
is twice the matrix norm here and the two bounds are $2J_g$ and
$4J_g$. The constants refer to the frame defined by the given spinor.

## Complete end and derivative orders

Take normal coordinates $x$ for $g$ at $p$, $r=|x|$, and a smooth local
unitary spin frame. With $\kappa=|\eta|_{g,p}>0$ and
$\omega=x/r$, Taylor's formula with smooth remainder gives
\[
\psi(r\omega)=r\,(\omega\cdot_{g,p}\eta)+r^2 B(r,\omega),\qquad
\sigma(r\omega)/r=\kappa+r\,q(r,\omega).
\]
Here $B$ and $q$ extend smoothly to $r=0$ in the polar variables.
For the second expression, use Clifford norm to give the leading
vector constant length $\kappa$, so its perturbation has a smooth
positive norm for small $r$. The metric coefficients in normal
coordinates satisfy $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(r^2)$.

Invert with $y=x/r^2$ and put $R=|y|=r^{-1}$. The derivative of
$x(y)$ is $R^{-2}Q(\omega)$, where $Q=I-2\omega\otimes\omega$ is
orthogonal. The two factors $R^{-2}$ in the tensor pullback cancel
the $r^{-4}$ in $h$. Thus the components in the $y$-chart are
\[
 h_{ij}(y)=\kappa^{-4}\delta_{ij}
             +R^{-1}H_{ij}(R^{-1},\omega),
\]
where $H$ is smooth down to polar radius zero. In particular the
$C^0$ error is $O(R^{-1})$. Differentiating in $y_i$ acts on a polar
coefficient via radial term $-R^{-2}\omega_i\partial_r$ and tangential
term $R^{-1}(\mathbf e_i-\omega_i\omega)\cdot\nabla_{S^3}$. Applying
this to the displayed form proves
\[
\partial_y^\gamma(h_{ij}-\kappa^{-4}\delta_{ij})
       =O_\gamma(R^{-1-|\gamma|})
\quad\text{for each fixed multi-index }\gamma .
\]
The constants depend on the smooth fixed compactifying data. A quadratic
spinor term can contribute to the order $R^{-1}$ coefficient.
The manifold has one end, and $h$ is uniformly equivalent to
$\kappa^{-4}\delta$ there. A curve reaching $R=\infty$ has infinite
$h$-length. The remaining region is compact with smooth positive
metric, so $h$ is complete.

## Exact case and derivative dependence

If $J_g(\psi)=0$, smoothness and the sharp inequality give
$P_h\phi=0$ and $\nabla^h\phi=0$ on the punctured manifold. Then
$j\phi$ is parallel and $A=0$ in this spin frame, so the positive
spin connection is flat with trivial holonomy. The curvature
of the Levi–Civita spin lift sees the $\Lambda^+$ rows of the
curvature operator because its positive spin representation is
faithful on the positive factor. Vanishing of these rows kills
$W_h^++(s_h/12)I$ and the trace-free Ricci block. Taking the trace
gives $s_h=0$, hence $\operatorname{Ric}_h=0$ and $W_h^+=0$.
This local calculation leaves $W_h^-$ as the other Weyl block.
On the E06 homotopy sphere the separate compact signature argument
applies to $g$, with $W_g^+=0$ by conformal transport and continuity
at $p$. It gives $W_g^-=0$, hence $W_h^-=0$ and full flatness in that
exact case.

At finite error the bound controls $A$ in $L^4$ and therefore
$A\wedge A$ in $L^2$. It supplies no estimate for $dA$ in terms of $J_g$.
For the fixed smooth input one can check more independently. The
prescribed jet gives $P_g\psi=O(r)$ and $\sigma=O(r)$, hence
$|A|_h=O(R^{-2})$ by covariance and the pointwise bound. The first
and second coordinate derivative estimates for $h$ give curvature
$O(R^{-3})$ on the end, hence $F_A\in L^2(h)$. Then
$dA=F_A-A\wedge A$ also belongs to $L^2(h)$ for these supplied data,
with no bound here on its norm by the relative defect.
The given factor $\sigma^4=(|\psi|_g^2)^2$ recovers the known smooth
compactification $\sigma^4 h=g$. Smoothness of $r^4h$ across the
added point is a separate property. For example the allowable local
quadratic term $\varepsilon x_1^2L(e_1)$ added to the linear jet
$L(x)=x\cdot_{g,p}\eta$ gives
$\sigma/r=\kappa(1+\varepsilon r\omega_1^3+O(r^2))$, so the first
radial coefficient of $r^4h$ has a cubic angular term that is not
the restriction of a linear form. The norm equivalence alone does
not supply compactifying data for a general end.
