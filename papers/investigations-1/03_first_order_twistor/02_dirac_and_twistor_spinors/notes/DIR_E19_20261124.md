## Setting and conclusions

Let \((M^4,g)\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere
with a fixed smooth positive-definite metric. On functions use
\(\Delta_g=d^*d\geq 0\), with first positive eigenvalue \(\lambda_1\).
The target is the unit round \(S^k\subset\mathbb R^{k+1}\), with \(k=2,3,4\).
For \(k=2,3\) take the inclusion in the unit \(S^4\) to be totally
geodesic. Let \(F\) be a supplied smooth map with
\(\Delta_g F=\lambda_1 F\) componentwise. The sphere constraint gives
\[
 0=\tfrac12\Delta_g |F|^2
   =\langle F,\Delta_g F\rangle-|dF|^2,
 \qquad |dF|^2=\lambda_1>0 .
\]
In this sign convention \(\Delta_gF=|dF|^2F\) is the harmonic-map
equation in ambient coordinates. Thus rank zero never occurs. The
proofs use only the fixed metric and eigenmap assumptions above.

| Target and condition | Necessary ranks |
| --- | --- |
| Great \(S^2\) | Both ranks 1 and 2 occur. Rank 2 is open dense. |
| Great \(S^3\) | Somewhere the rank is at most 2. The locus of rank at least 2 is open dense. |
| \(S^4\) and \(\deg F=0\) | Somewhere the rank is at most 3. The locus of rank at least 2 is open dense. If rank 4 occurs, the signed Jacobian takes both signs. |

## Rank one and a great circle

Suppose \(F\) has rank one throughout a nonempty open set. On a smaller
neighborhood the constant-rank theorem gives \(F=\gamma\circ u\), with
\(u\) a smooth submersion to an interval and \(\gamma\) a smooth curve
in the target parametrized by arclength. In ambient coordinates,
\[
 \gamma'(u)\Delta_g u-\gamma''(u)|du|^2
    =\lambda_1\gamma(u),\qquad |du|^2=\lambda_1 .
\]
Taking the inner product with \(\gamma'(u)\) gives \(\Delta_g u=0\)
because \(\langle\gamma',\gamma\rangle=0\) and
\(\langle\gamma',\gamma''\rangle=0\). Then
\(\gamma''=-\gamma\) throughout the parameter interval, since \(u\) is
onto that interval and \(|du|>0\). Its image lies in the fixed linear
two-plane \(P\) spanned by \(\gamma\) and \(\gamma'\) at one parameter.

For every vector \(n\) in a basis of \(P^\perp\), the scalar function
\(v=n\cdot F\) satisfies \(\Delta_gv=\lambda_1v\) and vanishes on the
neighborhood. Aronszajn's scalar unique-continuation theorem applies on
each relatively compact coordinate ball. In local coordinates the
principal coefficients of \(-\Delta_g\) are smooth and positive definite,
and the lower-order coefficients are bounded on such a ball. For
\(A=-\Delta_g\), \(|Av|^2=\lambda_1^2|v|^2\) supplies the inequality used
in the theorem. Smooth \(v\) has locally square-integrable derivatives
through order two. An open zero set supplies a zero of infinite order.
The set of points with an identically zero neighborhood is also closed:
any connected coordinate ball centered at a closure point contains
such a zero, so the theorem gives vanishing throughout that ball.
Connectedness yields \(v\equiv 0\) on \(M\) and hence
\(F(M)\subset S^k\cap P\). [Aronszajn, §1, (1.1), theorem and restrictions,
printed pp. 235–236][aronszajn].

Any smooth harmonic map from \(M\) to \(S^k\) whose image lies in a
great circle is constant. Indeed, for an orthonormal basis \(a,b\) of
the circle's plane, simple connectivity gives a global angle lift
\(\theta:M\to\mathbb R\) through \(t\mapsto a\cos t+b\sin t\). The lift
is smooth by local inverses of the covering. Taking the tangential
component of the harmonic-map equation gives \(\Delta_g\theta=0\).
Integration by parts on the closed domain gives
\(\int_M |d\theta|^2\,dV_g=0\), and connectedness makes \(\theta\) constant.
This contradicts the first eigenmap's positive energy and rules out
any open rank-one set. The set of rank at least two is open by the
nonvanishing of a two-minor. Its complement consists of rank-one points
and has empty interior, so the set is dense.

This argument also excludes a nonconstant smooth harmonic sphere-valued
map of rank at most one everywhere on this domain, allowing critical
points. A nonconstant map has a rank-one point and hence a rank-one
neighborhood. In the local factorization, replace \(\lambda_1\) by
\(|dF|^2=|du|^2\), which is positive there. The same scalar theorem
applies to \(\Delta_gv=|dF|^2v\) because the smooth potential is bounded
on every compact coordinate ball. The resulting global circle lift is
constant as before.

## Topological rank defects

**Great \(S^2\).** If \(F\) were a submersion, the smooth splitting would
be \(TM=K\oplus H\) with \(K=\ker dF\) and \(H=K^{\perp_g}\simeq F^*TS^2\).
The target orients \(H\), and the orientation of \(TM\) orients \(K\)
compatibly. Both oriented plane bundles have Euler class in
\(H^2(M;\mathbb Z)=0\). The Euler class product formula gives
\(e(TM)=e(K)e(H)=0\), while \(\langle e(TM),[M]\rangle=\chi(M)=2\).
Thus rank one occurs somewhere. The open dense locus established above
has rank two.

**Great \(S^3\).** A submersion gives the same splitting with \(H\)
of rank three and \(K\) of rank one. The target and source orientations
orient \(K\). Its positive unit vector is a smooth nowhere-zero tangent
field, impossible since \(\chi(M)=2\). Thus rank drops to at most two
somewhere.

**Target \(S^4\), degree zero.** Give the target its sphere orientation
and define \(F^*dV_{S^4}=J_F\,dV_g\). Full rank everywhere would make
\(J_F\) continuous, nowhere zero and of constant sign, contradicting
\[
 \int_M J_F\,dV_g=\deg(F)\operatorname{Vol}(S^4)=0 .
\]
The same identity implies that if \(J_F\) is nonzero anywhere, it has
positive and negative values on nonempty open sets. If the image is
assumed to be all of \(S^4\), rank four does occur. To see this, cover
\(M\) by finitely many compact coordinate cubes whose images lie in
target charts. If all derivatives were singular, subdivide each cube
into cubes of side \(r\). At the center of each small cube one target
direction is perpendicular to the range of the derivative. Uniform
continuity of the derivative and the linear approximation bound the
image in that direction by \(o(r)\), uniformly, and in the other three
directions by \(O(r)\). The \(O(r^{-4})\) resulting boxes cover the image
with total coordinate volume \(o(1)\). The image would have zero volume,
contrary to surjectivity. When only the target or the linear span of the
image is specified, the rank tests here leave \(J_F\equiv 0\) open.

## Pullback area and the Hodge star

For the great \(S^2\), let \(\omega\) be the oriented unit round area
form and \(\beta=F^*\omega\). Pullback gives \(d\beta=0\) and
\(\beta\wedge\beta=F^*(\omega\wedge\omega)=0\). Since
\(H^2_{\mathrm{dR}}(M)=0\), a smooth real one-form \(\alpha\) exists
with \(\beta=d\alpha\). The form is decomposable and is nonzero exactly
on the rank-two locus because \(\omega\) is a nonzero area form on each
target tangent plane.

Use the source orientation and metric for \(*_g\), and set
\(\beta^\pm=(\beta\pm *_g\beta)/2\). The middle-degree pointwise identity
\[
 0=\beta\wedge\beta
      =( |\beta^+|^2-|\beta^-|^2 )\,dV_g
\]
implies \(|\beta^+|^2=|\beta^-|^2=|\beta|^2/2\). Both chiral parts are
nonzero at each rank-two point. If \(d^*\beta=0\), then exactness and the
adjoint identity give
\(\|\beta\|_2^2=\langle d\alpha,\beta\rangle_{L^2}
=\langle\alpha,d^*\beta\rangle_{L^2}=0\), a contradiction. Thus \(\beta\)
is neither coclosed nor harmonic. In particular \(d(*_g\beta)\) is
nonzero somewhere.
