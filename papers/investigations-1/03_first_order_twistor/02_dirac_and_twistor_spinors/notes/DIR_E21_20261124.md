## Supplied geometry

Let $(M^4,g)$ be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented Riemannian
homotopy four-sphere with a supplied smooth unit-volume Yamabe minimizer.
With $Delta_g=d^*d$ nonnegative on functions, let $lambda=lambda_1(g)>0$
and suppose the scalar curvature is $s=3\lambda$. Let $F:M\to S^m$ be
smooth for $m\in\{2,3,4\}$ and let $h$ be the unit round metric. In the
ambient Euclidean space suppose $\Delta_g F=\lambda F$. Since $|F|^2=1$,
the scalar product rule gives $|dF|^2=\lambda$. These are the supplied
eigenmap hypotheses of [B12][b12], with the $S^4$ endpoint from [E14][e14]
and [E17][e17]. The pointwise calculation uses these equations and the
Riemannian metrics. Volume one fixes the normalization, and orientation
and spin enter the global comparison below.

Use $A=F^*h$, $A_0=A-\lambda g/4$, and
$\operatorname{Ric}_0=\operatorname{Ric}-(3\lambda/4)g$, with tensor norms
and contractions induced by $g$. Let $D=|\nabla dF|^2$, using the pullback
Levi–Civita connection of the sphere for the target index.

## Normal-frame derivation

Write $F_i=dF(e_i)$ at a point in a $g$-orthonormal normal frame. The
normal component of the ambient second derivative is $-A_{ij}F$,
so the tension field is $\tau(F)=-\Delta_g F+|dF|^2F=0$. Write
$R(X,Y)=[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}$ and
$\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\sum_i g(R^g(e_i,X)Y,e_i)$. The unit sphere has
$R^h(U,V)W=h(V,W)U-h(U,W)V$. The covariant derivative of $dF$ is symmetric
in its two domain entries. Differentiating this symmetry and commuting the
induced covariant derivatives gives, with second derivatives taken as tensors,

\[
\sum_i (\nabla_i\nabla_i dF)_j
=\nabla_j\tau(F)+\sum_i\bigl[R^h(F_i,F_j)F_i-dF(R^g(e_i,e_j)e_i)\bigr].
\]

Indeed, symmetry replaces $(\nabla_i\nabla_i dF)_j$ by
$(\nabla_i\nabla_j dF)_i$. The commutator acts by the pullback curvature on
the target slot and by negative domain curvature on the covector slot.
Contract the displayed identity against $F_j$ and sum in $j$. The
domain curvature gives $\langle\operatorname{Ric},A\rangle$, since
$-\sum_iR^g(e_i,e_j)e_i=\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}(e_j)$. The sphere term
gives $\sum_{i,j}(A_{ij}^2-A_{ii}A_{jj})=|A|^2-\lambda^2$.
Differentiating the squared norm of $dF$ yields

\[
 -\tfrac12\Delta_g|dF|^2
 = D+\langle\operatorname{Ric},A\rangle+|A|^2-\lambda^2=0.
\tag{1}
\]

Here $\langle\operatorname{Ric},A\rangle=3\lambda^2/4+
\langle\operatorname{Ric}_0,A_0\rangle$ and $|A|^2=\lambda^2/4+|A_0|^2$.
The trace parts cancel $\lambda^2$ exactly. Thus

\[
D+|A_0|^2=-\langle\operatorname{Ric}_0,A_0\rangle,
\qquad
D+\bigl|A_0+\tfrac12\operatorname{Ric}_0\bigr|^2
 =\tfrac14 |\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2.
\tag{2}
\]

In particular $D\le |\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2/4$ and
$|A_0|\le |\operatorname{Ric}_0|$. At a point with $\operatorname{Ric}_0=0$,
these equations give $\nabla dF=0$ and $A=(\lambda/4)g$.

## Rank restrictions at each point

At a point of rank $k$, let $a_1,\ldots,a_k>0$ be the nonzero eigenvalues
of $A^\sharp$, the squared singular values of $dF$. Then
$1\le k\le\min(4,m)$, $\sum_i a_i=\lambda$, and
$|A|^2\ge\lambda^2/k$ with equality exactly when all $a_i=\lambda/k$.
If $\rho=\operatorname{Ric}_{\min}$ is the least eigenvalue with respect to
$g$, then $\langle\operatorname{Ric},A\rangle\ge\rho\lambda$. Equation (1)
implies

\[
\rho \le \lambda-\frac{|A|^2+D}{\lambda}
\le \Bigl(1-\frac1k\Bigr)\lambda-\frac D\lambda.
\tag{3}
\]

Equality in the last bound for $\rho$ requires equal positive $a_i$ and
$\operatorname{im}(A^\sharp)$ contained in the $\rho$-eigenspace. This
follows by diagonalizing $A^\sharp$ in the contraction and using that
$\operatorname{Ric}-\rho g$ is positive semidefinite. At rank one,
$A=\lambda v^\flat\otimes v^\flat$ for a unit vector $v$, and the target
curvature term vanishes in (1). Hence

\[
\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)=-D/\lambda\le0.
\tag{4}
\]

At rank one, $\operatorname{Ric}(v,v)=0$ holds exactly when $\nabla dF=0$
at the point. Strictly positive Ricci excludes rank one. If Ricci is
positive semidefinite at a rank-one point, then $D=0$ and $v$ is in
the kernel of $\operatorname{Ric}^\sharp$.

For $k<4$, $|A_0|^2=|A|^2-\lambda^2/4\ge\lambda^2(1/k-1/4)>0$.
Cauchy–Schwarz in (2) gives the rank-drop bound

\[
|\operatorname{Ric}_0|
 \ge |A_0|+\frac{D}{|A_0|}
 \ge \lambda\sqrt{\frac1k-\frac14}.
\tag{5}
\]

Equality in the last bound holds exactly when $D=0$, the positive $a_i$
are equal, and $\operatorname{Ric}_0=-A_0$. For an orthogonal projector
$P$ of rank $k$, the pointwise matrix data $A^\sharp=(\lambda/k)P$,
$\operatorname{Ric}^\sharp=\lambda I-A^\sharp$, and $D=0$ attain the
algebraic bounds in (3) and (5). This calibration concerns the displayed
identities at one point.

## Conditional rank and recognition consequences

For an actual pointwise bound $\operatorname{Ric}\ge c g$, equation (3)
forces the listed rank when $c$ is strictly above the corresponding
threshold. Equation (5) gives a separate sufficient strict upper bound
on $|\operatorname{Ric}_0|$.

| Forced rank | Sufficient Ricci lower bound at the point | Sufficient trace-free Ricci bound at the point |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $\ge2$ | $c>0$ | $|\operatorname{Ric}_0|<\sqrt{3}\lambda/2$ |
| $\ge3$ | $c>\lambda/2$ | $|\operatorname{Ric}_0|<\lambda/2$ |
| $4$ | $c>2\lambda/3$ | $|\operatorname{Ric}_0|<\lambda/(2\sqrt{3})$ |

For the row forcing rank $\ge r$, if $c=(1-1/(r-1))\lambda$ and the
rank is below $r$, it must be exactly $r-1$. Equations (1) and (3) then
force $D=0$, equal positive $a_i$, and support of $A^\sharp$ in the
Ricci eigenspace of eigenvalue $c$.

For $m=4$, everywhere rank four makes $F$ a local diffeomorphism. Its
image is open and compact, hence all of $S^4$. Properness then gives a
covering. Connectedness of $M$ and simple connectivity of $S^4$ make
it a diffeomorphism of degree $+1$ or $-1$. In the supplied spin setting
this further gives $F^*h=(\lambda/4)g$ by Listing. In fact, for a smooth
nonzero-degree map from a closed spin four-manifold to the unit round
$S^4$, Theorem 3 requires $s\ge(24\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j)^{1/2}$.
Here $\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j=(\lambda^2-|A|^2)/2\le3\lambda^2/8$, so $s=3\lambda$
gives the bound, and the homothety scale follows by taking the trace.
[Listing, §1 trace comparison and Theorem 3, equation (6), p. 4][listing].

For a map into a great $S^m$ with $m\le3$, the inclusion in $S^4$ has rank
at most $m$ and the composite has degree zero. An everywhere Ricci lower
bound with $c>(1-1/m)\lambda$ excludes this lower target case. A proper
ambient linear span also bounds rank by its dimension minus one, whereas
a rank drop at a point gives only the pointwise conclusions above. The
recognition implication requires a supplied map of everywhere full rank
or, through Listing, a supplied map of nonzero degree. The Ricci bounds
and existence of a spectral-equality eigenmap on every homotopy four-sphere
remain additional premises.
