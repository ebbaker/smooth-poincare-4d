All five benchmarks hold in the selected conventions. The chiral round
fields solve the twistor equation and are eigenfields of $D^2$. The
nonzero ordinary Dirac eigenvalues on the lowest round modes belong to
full spinors. The implication from a nontrivial positive twistor spinor
to $W^+=0$ holds on the original smooth metric, including at its isolated

and round metric variations, respectively.

## Conventions and source scope

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian spin
four-manifold. Use the untwisted complex spin bundle
$S=S^+\oplus S^-$, its Levi–Civita connection $\nabla$, Hermitian norm,
and real Clifford action $c(X)^*=-c(X)$,
$c(X)c(Y)+c(Y)c(X)=-2g(X,Y)$. Real self-dual two-forms act on $S^+$.
Write $D=\sum_i c(e_i)\nabla_{e_i}$ and
$P_X=\nabla_X+c(X)D/4$. For curvatures use
$R(X,Y)=[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}$, so constant sectional
curvature $K$ has $R(X,Y)Z=K(g(Y,Z)X-g(X,Z)Y)$ and $s=12K$.
The chiral test is the first target in the [B01 decision](../brainstorm/notes/DIR_B01_TARGETS_20261124.md),
using the [shared conventions](../../brainstorm/notes/SH_B01_CONVENTIONS_20261124.md).

## 1. Unit-round Killing fields and their chiral projections

On the standard $S^4$ with $K=1$, Bär's equation (1) and Lemma 1 give
Killing spinors for either $\kappa=1/2$ or $\kappa=-1/2$, with
$\nabla_X\Psi=\kappa c(X)\Psi$. The proof exhibits the flat connection
$\widetilde\nabla_X=\nabla_X-\kappa c(X)$ on the simply connected
sphere, so the value of a Killing spinor for a fixed $\kappa$ can be
prescribed freely at one point. Clifford tracing gives
$D\Psi=-4\kappa\Psi$ and hence $P\Psi=0$.

The spin connection preserves chirality and both $D$ and $c(X)$ reverse
it. Thus $P$ preserves it. For $\psi_\pm=\operatorname{pr}_\pm\Psi$,
the Killing and Dirac equations give

\[
 \nabla_X\psi_\pm=\kappa c(X)\psi_\mp,\qquad
 D\psi_\pm=-4\kappa\psi_\mp,\qquad
 D^2\psi_\pm=4\psi_\pm,\qquad P\psi_\pm=0.
\]

If, for example, $\psi_+$ vanished identically, the first positive
equation and invertibility of $c(X)$ for real $X\ne0$ would force
$\psi_-=0$. Both projections of a nontrivial $\Psi$ are therefore
nontrivial. Choosing $\Psi(p)\in S_p^-\setminus\{0\}$ gives
$\psi_+(p)=0$ and $D\psi_+(p)=-4\kappa\Psi(p)\ne0$. This supplies a
smooth round example of a chiral twistor zero.

Every nonzero Dirac eigenvalue involves both chiralities because
$D:S^\pm\to S^\mp$. In particular $D\psi=\lambda\psi$ for a nonzero
pure-chirality field is possible only if $\lambda=0$. The integrated
Lichnerowicz identity

\[
 \|D\psi\|_2^2=\|\nabla\psi\|_2^2+
                  \frac14\int_M s|\psi|^2\,dV_g
\]

shows that the ordinary harmonic equation $D\psi=0$ has zero kernel
on the unit round sphere and on every closed spin metric with $s>0$.
For a full Dirac eigenfield, the round twistor energy equals
$(3\lambda^2/4-3)\|\psi\|_2^2$ by §4 below. Bär's Theorem 1 gives
$\lambda=\pm(2+k)$, $k=0,1,\ldots$, with multiplicity
$4\binom{k+3}{3}$ for each sign. The modes with $k=0$ have zero
twistor energy, while those with $k\ge1$ have strictly positive
twistor energy. This distinguishes the three equations on the same
metric. [Bär, author-hosted text, §2 equation (1), Lemma 1 and Theorem 1](https://www.math.uni-potsdam.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Prof-Geometrie/Dokumente/Publikationen/sphere.pdf)

## 2. Curvature compatibility and the positive Weyl block

Let $\psi\in\Gamma(S^+)$ be a smooth twistor spinor and put $\chi=D\psi$.
Define the Schouten endomorphism $A=(\operatorname{Ric}^\sharp-sI/6)/2$.
The equations for the pair are

\[
 \nabla_X\psi=-\tfrac14 c(X)\chi,\qquad
 \nabla_X\chi=-2c(A(X))\psi. \tag{1}
\]

For a convention check, in dimension $n=4$ the first equation gives
$\nabla^*\nabla\psi=D\chi/4$ by taking the covariant trace in a normal
frame. Lichnerowicz then gives $D\chi=s\psi/3$. Commuting the first
equation gives

\[
 R^S(X,Y)\psi=-\tfrac14\big(c(Y)\nabla_X\chi-c(X)\nabla_Y\chi\big).
\]

The spin curvature is
$R^S(X,Y)=\tfrac14\sum_{j,k}g(R(X,Y)e_j,e_k)c(e_j)c(e_k)$.
In $\sum_i c(e_i)R^S(X,e_i)$ the alternating three-Clifford term
vanishes by the first Bianchi identity and pair symmetry. The
single-Clifford terms give $-\tfrac12c(\operatorname{Ric}^\sharp X)$
by contracting $-\delta_{ij}c(e_k)+\delta_{ik}c(e_j)-\delta_{jk}c(e_i)$.
Contracting the twistor commutator instead gives
$\tfrac14(2\nabla_X\chi-c(X)D\chi)$. Equating these expressions and
using $D\chi=s\psi/3$ gives the second equation of (1).
Friedrich writes the same prolongation with the endomorphism $-A$.
[Friedrich, equation (1.1), p. 59, and equations (2.1)–(2.2), pp. 61–62](https://dml.cz/dmlcz/701462)

Write $R=W+R_A$, where

\[
 R_A(X,Y)Z=g(Y,Z)A(X)-g(X,Z)A(Y)
          +g(A(Y),Z)X-g(A(X),Z)Y.
\]

The spin action $R_A^S(X,Y)$ is
$\tfrac12(c(Y)c(A(X))-c(X)c(A(Y)))$ by the spin curvature formula and
symmetry of $A$. Substituting the second equation of (1) into the
twistor commutator gives exactly $R_A^S(X,Y)\psi$. Subtraction gives
$W^S(X,Y)\psi=0$. Equivalently, $\beta_{X,Y}\cdot\psi=0$ for the real
two-form $\beta_{X,Y}(U,V)=g(W(X,Y)U,V)$, since $W^S(X,Y)$ is one half
of this two-form action.

At a point where $\psi\ne0$, the $\Lambda^-$ part of $\beta_{X,Y}$
acts trivially on $S^+$ and the real $\Lambda^+$ action is, up to the
fixed Clifford normalization, the isomorphism onto
$\mathfrak{su}(S^+)$. This follows from the two
$\mathfrak{su}(2)$ factors of $\mathfrak{spin}(4)$ and the defining
two-dimensional complex representation on $S^+$ of the self-dual
factor. In a unitary frame, a trace-free skew-Hermitian endomorphism
has eigenvalues $it,-it$ with $t\in\mathbb R$ and both are nonzero
when the endomorphism is nonzero. Thus annihilation of the fixed
nonzero $\psi$ forces $(\beta_{X,Y})_+=0$ for all real $X,Y$.
The two-vectors $X\wedge Y$ span $\Lambda^2$, so
$\pi_+W=0$ as a map on two-forms. Its restriction to $\Lambda^+$
is $W^+$, which therefore vanishes at that point.

## 3. Prolongation at zeros

Equations (1) make $(\psi,\chi)$ a parallel section of the smooth
connection on $S^+\oplus S^-$ defined by

\[
 \mathcal T_X\binom{\psi}{\chi}
 =\binom{\nabla_X\psi+\tfrac14c(X)\chi}
        {\nabla_X\chi+2c(A(X))\psi}.
\]

If the pair were zero at one point, uniqueness for the linear
parallel-transport equation along every path from that point would
force the pair to vanish everywhere on the connected base. Therefore,
at any zero $p$ of a nontrivial $\psi$, $\chi(p)\ne0$. The same smooth
equations hold at $p$. With $u=|\psi|^2$, compatibility of $\nabla$
with the Hermitian pairing gives $du(p)=0$ and

\[
 \operatorname{Hess}_p u(X,Y)
 =2\operatorname{Re}\langle\nabla_X\psi,\nabla_Y\psi\rangle_p
 =\tfrac18|\chi(p)|^2g_p(X,Y).
\]

For the second equality one uses skew-adjointness and the Clifford
anticommutator. Thus in normal coordinates $x$ at $p$,
$u(x)=|\chi(p)|^2|x|^2/16+O(|x|^3)$, so $p$ is isolated.
The zero set is closed, hence finite by compactness. Its complement
is dense, and the smooth tensor $W^+$ vanishes everywhere by
continuity. The curvature conclusion extends using the original smooth
fields through each zero. Extending a punctured or singularly
conformally rescaled configuration would require its own hypotheses.
[Friedrich, Proposition 1 and the curvature remark, p. 62, and Proposition 2, p. 63](https://dml.cz/dmlcz/701462)

## 4. Fixed-metric energy and principal symbol

On $T^*M\otimes S^+$ the Clifford contraction into $S^-$ is
$C(a)=\sum_i c(e_i)a_i$ and its adjoint is
$(C^*\chi)_i=-c(e_i)\chi$. Since $CC^*=4I$, $\Pi=I-\tfrac14 C^*C$
is orthogonal projection onto the Clifford-traceless part and
$P=\Pi\nabla$. The pointwise orthogonal identity
$|P\psi|^2=|\nabla\psi|^2-\tfrac14|D\psi|^2$ and the untwisted
Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula give, on the closed base,

\[
 P^*P=\nabla^*\nabla-\tfrac14D^2
      =\tfrac34D^2-\tfrac14s I,\qquad
 \|P\psi\|_2^2=\tfrac34\|D\psi\|_2^2
               -\tfrac14\int_M s|\psi|^2\,dV_g. \tag{2}
\]

Here $D^2|_{S^+}=(D|_{S^+})^*(D|_{S^+})$. With the symbol convention
$\nabla\mapsto i\xi$, $\sigma_1(P)(\xi)v=i\Pi(\xi\otimes v)$. Its
squared norm is $|\xi|^2|v|^2-\tfrac14|c(\xi^\sharp)v|^2
=\tfrac34|\xi|^2|v|^2$. Polarization gives
$\sigma_2(P^*P)(\xi)=\tfrac34|\xi|^2I_{S^+}$. Equivalently this is
the symbol of the Dirac square in (2) multiplied by $3/4$.

For a supplied smooth $g$, the nonnegative self-adjoint elliptic
realization of $P^*P$ on $L^2(S^+)$ has compact resolvent by elliptic
estimates and the compact Sobolev embedding. Its lowest eigenvalue
is the Rayleigh infimum $\mu(g)$, attained by a smooth eigensection
by elliptic regularity. Integration in (2) gives
$\mu(g)=0$ precisely when there is a nontrivial twistor half-spinor
for this $g$. On the unit round sphere $(3/4)D^2-3I$ annihilates
the chiral Killing projections. The term $s/4$ in (2) is the
Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz term used by Friedrich–Kim. Their proof
works with an eigenspinor and a conformally modified equation on a
compact Riemannian spin manifold of dimension $n\ge2$. Only the
untwisted identity enters this fixed-metric calculation.
[Friedrich–Kim, arXiv:math/9906168v1, proof of Theorem 2.6, pp. 5–6](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/9906168)

## 5. Scope of the round TT benchmark

In the CME manuscript let $\mathcal D=(W^+)'_{g_0}$ and let
$\mathcal H=(A_g^+)'_{g_0}$ be the variation of the metric-induced
chiral connection in the symmetric-frame identification. Use its
round connection and tensor $L^2$ pairings and the Weyl block norm
as an endomorphism. On smooth transverse-traceless tensors for
the round metric of curvature $K>0$ the integrated compatibility
identity and the Bianchi intertwining give, with $L=\nabla^*\nabla$,

\[
 \mathcal H^*\mathcal H=\tfrac14(L+4K),\qquad
 \mathcal L_{\mathrm{conn}}\mathcal H=4\mathcal H(L+2K).
\]

The manuscript applies the Hessian chain rule at the critical round
connection and obtains

\[
 16\mathcal D^*\mathcal D=(L+2K)(L+4K),\qquad
 \operatorname{Hess}_{g_0}\mathcal E_+
 =2\mathcal D^*\mathcal D=\tfrac18(L+2K)(L+4K)
 \quad\text{on TT}.
\]

The round rough Laplacian preserves TT. With
$S_{abc}=\nabla_a h_{bc}+\nabla_b h_{ca}+\nabla_c h_{ab}$, the
CME gap proof integrates the cross term by parts and uses the TT
curvature commutator. It gives
$\|S\|^2=3\|\nabla h\|^2-24K\|h\|^2$. Hence the TT eigenvalues of $L$
are at least $8K$. At $K=1$ this implies
$\|\mathcal D h\|^2\ge(15/2)\|h\|^2$, or Hessian lower bound $15$
for $\mathcal E_+$ on this subspace. The bound requires no
identification of an eigentensor attaining $8K$.
[CME manuscript, metric Hessian factorization and TT spectral gap](../../../01_chiral_metric_evolution/manuscript/chiral_curvature_and_metric_evolution.tex)

This comparison controls the metric Weyl linearization on the
round TT subspace. For a coupled linearization at a round solution
$(g_0,\psi_0)$, the two variations would enter through
$P_{g_0}\dot\psi+(\partial_gP)_{g_0}[h]\psi_0$ in the chosen bundle
identification. The range of this map and the variable-metric
existence, uniform estimates and smooth attainment premises remain
open in this packet.
