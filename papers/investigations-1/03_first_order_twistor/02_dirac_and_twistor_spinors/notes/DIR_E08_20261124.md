## Result and conventions

Fix a smooth connected closed oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\),
a smooth positive-definite metric \(g\), and a smooth nonzero-degree
map \(f:M\to(S^4,g_{\mathrm{rd}})\), with the target of sectional
curvature one. The map stays fixed. Use \(\Delta_g=-\operatorname{div}_g\nabla\)
and set \(W_g=s_g-3|df|_{g,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2\), \(L_g=6\Delta_g+W_g\).
For real \(u\in H^1(M)\) write
\[
Q_g(u)=\int_M(6|du|_g^2+W_g u^2)\,dV_g,\qquad
Y=Y_f([g])=\inf_{u\ne 0}\frac{Q_g(u)}{\|u\|_{4,g}^2}.
\]
Smooth density and the continuous embedding \(H^1\hookrightarrow L^4\)
make this the [B07 quotient](../brainstorm/notes/DIR_B07_DEGREE_CONCENTRATION_20261124.md)
also when the infimum is taken over smooth functions. The fixed-map
identity is
\[
\max_{\substack{h\in[g]\ \mathrm{smooth}\\\operatorname{Vol}_h(M)=1}}
       \lambda_1(6\Delta_h+s_h-3|df|_{h,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2)=Y\le0 .
\]
If \(Y<0\), the unique maximizing factor with \(\int_M u^4\,dV_g=1\)
is the smooth positive quotient minimizer \(u\). The metric \(u^2g\)
has \(W_{u^2g}\equiv Y\). If \(Y=0\), every smooth unit-volume
representative maximizes the eigenvalue. The smooth positive quotient
minimizer still picks out a unique unit-volume representative with
\(W\equiv0\), the normalized round pullback by \(f\).

## Covariance and sign

For \(h=a^2g\) with \(a>0\) smooth, \(dV_h=a^4dV_g\) and
\(|df|_{h,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2=a^{-2}|df|_{g,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2\).
Combining the latter with the conformal Laplacian law in dimension four
gives
\[
L_h\phi=a^{-3}L_g(a\phi),\qquad
W_h=a^{-3}L_g a,\qquad
Q_h(\phi)=Q_g(a\phi),\qquad \|\phi\|_{4,h}=\|a\phi\|_{4,g}.
\]
The quadratic form identity extends by density. [Lee and Parker, 1987, §2, equations (2.6)–(2.7)](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf#page=7)

The [assessed E05 sign comparison](DIR_E05_20261124.md#spectral-sign-and-complete-equality-case)
uses a smooth positive ground state \(b\) of \(L_g\) and
\(W_{b^2g}=\lambda_1(L_g)b^{-2}\). The pointwise comparison applies
whenever \(W\ge0\). In dimension four, if \(\sigma_i\) are the metric
singular values of \(df\), Listing's threshold satisfies
\[
\sqrt{24\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2}
   \ \le\ 3\sum_i\sigma_i^2 .
\]
The domain is spin because \(H^2(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0\).
Listing's theorem therefore applies for the stipulated nonzero degree.
It makes \(f\) a homothety, which in this setting is a diffeomorphism
of degree \(+1\) or \(-1\), with \(W=0\). Thus \(\lambda_1(L_g)\le0\).
It equals zero precisely when \(f\) is a diffeomorphism and
\(g\) is conformal to \(f^*g_{\mathrm{rd}}\). In the converse direction
that pullback has \(W=0\) and covariance gives a positive kernel
function in every representative. [Listing, Theorem 3, equation (6), PDF p. 4](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=4)

A ground-state test gives \(Y\le0\). The infimum is finite by the
boundedness of \(W_g\) and the Hölder estimate for \(\|u\|_{2,g}\).
If \(\lambda_1(L_g)<0\) the same test gives \(Y<0\). If
\(\lambda_1(L_g)=0\), \(Q_g\) is nonnegative and its ground state has
zero form value, so \(Y=0\). These conclusions apply to every
representative because \(Y\) is conformally invariant.

## Attainment at negative value

Suppose \(Y<0\). Choose \(u_j\ge0\) with \(\|u_j\|_{4,g}=1\) and
\(Q_g(u_j)\to Y\), using \(|u_j|\) in the Sobolev space if necessary.
The constraint bounds \(\|u_j\|_{2,g}\) and then the form bounds
\(\|du_j\|_{2,g}\). After passing to a subsequence, \(u_j\rightharpoonup u\)
in \(H^1(M)\) and \(u_j\to u\) in \(L^2(M)\) by the compact subcritical
embedding. In particular \(u\ge0\). Bounded \(W_g\) gives convergence
of the potential term and weak lower semicontinuity gives
\(Q_g(u)\le Y<0\). The weak limit is nonzero. Weak \(L^4\) lower
semicontinuity gives \(\|u\|_{4,g}\le1\). By the infimum and its negative
sign,
\[
Y\ \le\ Y\|u\|_{4,g}^2\ \le\ Q_g(u)\ \le\ Y .
\]
Therefore the full critical norm survives. Also \(Q_g(u)=Y\), so the
convergence of the potential and form values implies convergence of the
gradient norms. This yields strong \(H^1\) convergence along the
subsequence. [Lee and Parker, §2, Theorem 2.2(a)–(b), p. 45](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf#page=9)

The Euler equation at this minimizer with \(\int_Mu^4\,dV_g=1\) is
\[
L_g u=Y u^3
\]
weakly, with the multiplier verified by testing with \(u\).
To gain boundedness at the critical exponent, put \(A=6\Delta_g+1\)
and choose \(C\ge\max(0,\sup_M(1-W_g))\). Since \(u\ge0\) and
\(Y u^3\le0\), \(Au\le Cu\) weakly. Solve \(A v=Cu\) by coercivity
on \(H^1\). The right side lies in \(L^4\). First the equation with
\(v\in H^1\hookrightarrow L^4\) yields \(v\in W^{2,4}\) by global
elliptic regularity, so \(v\) is bounded. Testing \(A(u-v)\le0\)
with \((u-v)_+\) gives
\[
6\|d(u-v)_+\|_{2,g}^2+\|(u-v)_+\|_{2,g}^2\le0 .
\]
This test is valid in \(H^1\) because the cubic term is in \(L^{4/3}\).
Thus \(0\le u\le v\) almost everywhere. The now-bounded right side
of \(6\Delta_g u=Y u^3-W_g u\) gives \(W^{2,p}\) for every finite
\(p>1\), followed by smoothness through Sobolev and Schauder bootstrap.
Choose \(m\ge0\) with \(6m\ge\sup_M(W_g-Yu^2)\). Then
\((\Delta_g+m)u\ge0\). The strong maximum principle on connected \(M\)
excludes a zero minimum of this nonnegative nontrivial solution.
[Lee and Parker, §2, Theorems 2.2(c) and 2.5–2.6, pp. 45–46](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf#page=10)

## Spectral maximum, equality and curvature

At \(Y=0\), take a smooth strictly positive ground state \(u\) with
\(\|u\|_{4,g}=1\). It minimizes \(Q_g\) and solves \(L_gu=0\).
Thus for both signs we have a smooth positive \(u\) solving
\(L_gu=Y u^3\), and \(h_*=u^2g\) has volume one and \(W_{h_*}=Y\).
Its operator is \(6\Delta_{h_*}+Y\), with first eigenvalue \(Y\).
For any other \(h=a^2g\) of volume one, test \(u/a\) in the first
eigenvalue quotient. The numerator is \(Q_g(u)=Y\) and the
denominator is \(\int_M u^2a^2\,dV_g\). By Cauchy–Schwarz this
denominator lies in \((0,1]\). Consequently
\[
\lambda_1(L_h)\ \le\ \frac{Y}{\int_Mu^2 a^2\,dV_g}\ \le\ Y.
\]
At \(Y<0\), equality requires the denominator to be one, hence \(a=u\)
by equality in Cauchy–Schwarz and the volume normalization. This also
makes the positive quotient minimizer unique up to positive scaling
before normalization. The scalar curvature satisfies
\(s_{h_*}=3|df|_{h_*,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2+Y\) everywhere. Thus the
deficit is strictly negative, and the scalar curvature itself is \(Y\)
on any open set where \(f\) is constant.

At \(Y=0\), covariance makes \(Q_h\ge0\) for every \(h\in[g]\) and
gives \(L_h(u/a)=0\), so every normalized representative attains the
maximum. The positive quotient minimizer is unique up to scale
because in the \(h_*\) representative every kernel function is
harmonic and hence constant. Consequently \(h_*\) is the unique
unit-volume metric with \(W=0\). If \(k=f^*g_{\mathrm{rd}}\) and
\(g=w^2k\), it is given by the factor
\(u=\operatorname{Vol}_k(M)^{-1/4}w^{-1}\), and
\(h_*=\operatorname{Vol}_k(M)^{-1/2}k\). It is round up to scale and
satisfies \(s_{h_*}=3|df|_{h_*,g_{\mathrm{rd}}}^2>0\).
In any representative \(a^2k\) with nonconstant \(a\), the
deficit is \(6a^{-3}\Delta_k a\). The Laplacian of a nonconstant
function on this connected closed manifold is nonzero and has
integral zero, so the deficit takes both signs.

In either case, a unit-volume representative with constant deficit
\(q\) has first eigenvalue \(q\le0\). On that representative
\(Q_h(\phi)\ge q\|\phi\|_{4,h}^2\) by Hölder, with equality for
constants. Consequently \(q=Y\), and normalizing to volume one
exhausts the constant-deficit metrics up to scale.

A fixed \(f\) constant on a nonempty open set cannot be a
diffeomorphism. Thus \(Y<0\) for that pair and the displayed bound
separates the entire volume-one spectral maximum from zero in the
fixed class. This closes the variational attainment question for
one pair \(([g],f)\). It leaves the size of the gap across varying
conformal classes or maps undetermined.
