Fix a smooth closed connected oriented spin homotopy four-sphere and
**one supplied** smooth unit-volume Yamabe-minimizing metric $g$ of
constant scalar curvature $s$. The target is the oriented unit round
sphere $(S^4,h)\subset\mathbb R^5$. Write $v_4=\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)$
and $\Delta_g=d^*d$ with first positive scalar eigenvalue $\lambda_1$.
Set $V_f=3|df|_{g,h}^2$ and let $Y_f([g])$ be the infimum of the form
$Q_f(u)=\int_M(6|du|^2+(s-V_f)u^2)\,dV_g$ divided by $\|u\|_4^2$ over
nonzero $H^1$ tests. The balance constraint is $\int_M f\,dV_g=0$.
The supremum $S_{\mathrm{bal}}$ is taken over smooth balanced degree
$+1$ maps, with $g$ fixed throughout.

## 1. Necessity and the Jacobian hypothesis

For a smooth real mean-zero $v$, $1+tv$ is positive for small $t$.
The ordinary Yamabe numerator is
$s+t^2(s\|v\|_2^2+6\|dv\|_2^2)$ and the square root in the
denominator is $1+3t^2\|v\|_2^2+O(t^3)$. Thus minimality forces
$6\|dv\|_2^2-2s\|v\|_2^2\ge0$. Taking a first eigenfunction yields
$s\le3\lambda_1$ with no prior scalar-sign assumption. For a
balanced map, sum the Poincaré inequalities on its five coordinates
and use $\|f\|_2=1$. The constant test in $Y_f$ gives
\[
 Y_f\le s-3\|df\|_2^2\le s-3\lambda_1\le0.
\]
If $Y_{f_j}\to0$, then $s=3\lambda_1>0$ and
$0\le\|df_j\|_2^2-\lambda_1\le -Y_{f_j}/3$.
Let $\Pi$ project coordinatewise onto the first positive eigenspace
and let $\lambda_*>\lambda_1$ be the next distinct eigenvalue.
The higher modes satisfy
\[
 \|(1-\Pi)f_j\|_{H^1}^2
 \le\frac{1+\lambda_*}{\lambda_*-\lambda_1}
       \big(\|df_j\|_2^2-\lambda_1\|f_j\|_2^2\big).
\]
There are no constant modes. Boundedness of $\Pi f_j$ in the
finite-dimensional eigenspace gives a convergent subsequence, and
the bound upgrades it to ambient strong $W^{1,2}$ convergence to a
smooth $F$. The sphere constraint passes by strong $L^2$ and
continuity. Then $\Delta_gF=\lambda_1F$ and the scalar product
identity for $\Delta_g|F|^2=0$ gives $|dF|^2=\lambda_1$ everywhere.
These steps need no Jacobian hypothesis. [E14](DIR_E14_20261124.md).

Write $f^*dV_h=J_f\,dV_g$. The signed Jacobian is the determinant
of the five ambient columns $f,df(e_1),\ldots,df(e_4)$ in a positively
oriented local orthonormal frame. The strong $W^{1,2}$ limit gives
convergence of these columns in measure and hence $J_{f_j}\to J_F$
in measure. The sufficient uniform integrability uses the
**absolute** densities relative to the fixed $dV_g$. It requires
bounded $L^1$ norms and
$\sup_j\int_A|J_{f_j}|\,dV_g\to0$, uniformly over measurable $A$
with $\operatorname{Vol}_g(A)\to0$. This is stronger than a bound on
total absolute mass. To upgrade the convergence, split at
$|J_{f_j}-J_F|>\delta$. The set has volume tending to zero and its
integral is controlled by uniform integrability and absolute
continuity for $J_F$. On the complement the integral is at most
$\delta$ since the volume is one. Signed integrals now converge,
giving $\deg F=+1$. No converse to this uniform-integrability
criterion is used for an individual sequence.

For squared singular values $a_1,\ldots,a_4$ of $dF$,
$\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k\le(3/8)(\sum_i a_i)^2$. Thus
$\sqrt{24\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k}\le3|dF|^2=s$. This is Listing's
pointwise scalar bound in Theorem 3 for a closed spin source
and a smooth nonzero-degree map to the unit round sphere in
the same even dimension. The homothety conclusion applies to
either sign of nonzero degree. Here degree $+1$ fixes orientation,
and the scale is $F^*h=(\lambda_1/4)g$.
[Listing, §1 trace definitions and Theorem 3, eq. (6)](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=4).

## 2. Converse, critical gluing and balance correction

Suppose $s=3\lambda_1$ and a smooth sphere-valued first eigenmap $F$
is supplied. Its eigenvalue equation gives balance by integration
and $V_F=s$ pointwise. Nonzero degree implies homothety by the same
scalar bound, so $\deg F\in\{-1,0,1\}$. For $\deg F=1$, the constant
sequence $F$ works. For $k=1-\deg F\in\{1,2\}$, take $k$ disjoint
positively oriented normal balls at fixed points $p_i$, with
$\rho=\epsilon^{1/2}$ and $R=\epsilon^{1/4}$ and $4\rho<R$.

Flatten $F$ smoothly to $F(p_i)$ inside $B_{3\rho}(p_i)$,
retaining $F$ off $B_{4\rho}(p_i)$. In target normal coordinates
the coordinate function is $O(\rho)$ on the cutoff annulus, and
its derivative is bounded. Multiplying by the cutoff keeps a
uniform derivative bound and is a homotopy. Insert in $B_{2\rho}$
a rotated, positively oriented inverse stereographic bubble with
core conformal factor $b_\epsilon=2\epsilon/(\epsilon^2+|x|^2)$.
Cut it off smoothly near the south pole, then rotate that pole to
$F(p_i)$ by an orientation-preserving isometry. In the south chart
the cutoff applies to $\epsilon x/|x|^2$, giving the derivative
bound $C\epsilon/\rho^2$ in the transition. Each insertion is
constant at the seam and has degree $+1$ when extended by this
constant. Equivalently the original north pole has one regular
positive preimage before target rotation. The flattened map had
zero Jacobian there. Thus the joined map $G_\epsilon$ has
$\deg G_\epsilon=\deg F+k=1$. Its squared derivative integral on
a core is $O(\epsilon^2(1+\log(\rho/\epsilon)))$ and on the cutoff
annulus is $O(\epsilon^2)$. Bounded-derivative parts of the
flattening occupy volume $O(\rho^4)$, so $G_\epsilon\to F$
strongly in $W^{1,2}$, and its uncorrected mean is $O(\rho^4)$.
[E09 local model](DIR_E09_20261124.md) · [E15](DIR_E15_20261124.md).

For a local test on $B_R(p_i)$ the model potential is
$P_\epsilon=12b_\epsilon^2$, with $\|P_\epsilon\|_{L^2(dx)}\le C$.
Differentiation gives $\Delta_\delta b_\epsilon=2b_\epsilon^3$,
so $\int(6|dv|_\delta^2-P_\epsilon v^2)\,dx\ge0$ on
$H^1_0(B_R)$ by the positive ground-state identity. The model tail
outside $B_\rho$ has $L^2$ norm $O((\epsilon/\rho)^2)$, by integrating
$O(\epsilon^4 r^{-8})r^3\,dr$ and taking the square root. The actual
cutoff annulus has the same $L^2$ order since its potential is
$O(\epsilon^2/\rho^4)$ over volume $O(\rho^4)$. On the core, the
metric error times the bounded model $L^2$ norm is $O(R^2)$.
The bounded potential on the rest of $B_R$ and the scalar term
have $L^2$ size $O(R^2)$. With $J=\sqrt{\det(g_{ab})}$ this gives
\[
 \|J V_{G_\epsilon}-P_\epsilon\|_{L^2(B_R,dx)}
     +\|Js\|_{L^2(B_R,dx)}
 \le C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2\big).
\]
It also gives a global uniform $L^2$ bound on $V_{G_\epsilon}$.
Multiply the complete nonnegative Euclidean form by the kinetic
comparison factor $1-CR^2$. The resulting $O(R^2)$ extra term
is paired with $P_\epsilon v^2$ and bounded by Hölder. There is no
error proportional to an uncontrolled test gradient. The local
lower bound is therefore
$-C(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2)\|v\|_4^2$.

Use the square partition $\sum_i\chi_i^2+\eta^2=1$ on the disjoint
balls, with cosine and sine cutoffs in the logarithmic interval
between $4\rho$ and $R$. Each relevant gradient has fourth power
of its $L^4$ norm $O(\ell^{-3})$, for
$\ell=\log(R/(4\rho))$, by integrating $O(\ell^{-4})\,dr/r$.
Where $\eta$ is nonzero, $G_\epsilon=F$ and $s-V_{G_\epsilon}=0$.
Thus the exterior localized form is nonnegative. Expanding the
gradient squares yields the localization error bounded by
$C\ell^{-3/2}\|u\|_4^2$. The local bounds sum because $k\le2$.
Consequently the global lower bound for $Q_{G_\epsilon}$ is
$-C(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2+\ell^{-3/2})\|u\|_4^2$,
uniform over the entire unit $L^4$ sphere.

To rebalance, take $\Phi_b$ to be the time-one flow on the target
of $X_b(y)=b-(b\cdot y)y$. It is orientation-preserving and conformal
since the spherical Hessian of $b\cdot y$ is $-(b\cdot y)h$.
The derivative at $b=0$ of the mean map for $F$ equals
$A=I-\int FF^\top\,dV_g$. If $A$ had a nontrivial null vector,
$F$ would take values in its two antipodal directions. Connectedness
and balance exclude this, so $A$ is positive definite.
For small $b$, smooth target dependence gives the uniform derivative
error $O(|b|+\|G_\epsilon-F\|_1)$ relative to $A$. Hence the map
$b\mapsto b-A^{-1}\int\Phi_b(G_\epsilon)\,dV_g$ is a contraction
on a small ball of radius $2|A^{-1}\int G_\epsilon\,dV_g|$ for
small $\epsilon$. It fixes $b_\epsilon^{\mathrm{bal}}=O(\rho^4)$,
with the zero parameter itself a solution when the initial mean
vanishes. Postcompose by $\Phi_{b_\epsilon^{\mathrm{bal}}}$ to
obtain $f_\epsilon$. The conformal factor squared differs uniformly
from 1 by $O(\rho^4)$, so the potential changes by
$O(\rho^4)\|V_{G_\epsilon}\|_2$ in $L^2$. Hölder preserves the lower
bound up to $o(1)\|u\|_4^2$. Balance supplies the upper bound zero.
The degree remains $+1$, and the $C^1$-small target correction
preserves the strong $W^{1,2}$ limit.

The signed Jacobian measures of these maps converge to
$F^*dV_h+v_4\sum_i\delta_{p_i}$. On the complement of the flattening
balls the map is unchanged before correction. The flattening
contributes $O(\rho^4)$ in total variation and on insertion balls
the signed integrals are exactly $v_4$ each. Total variations stay
bounded by $|J_f|\le |df|^4/16$ and the uniform potential bound.
The target volume factor changes the measures by $o(1)$ in total
variation. Each shrinking $B_{2\rho}(p_i)$ still has signed integral
$v_4$ after this correction, giving the failure of absolute-Jacobian
uniform integrability when $k>0$.

This proves that $S_{\mathrm{bal}}=0$ exactly when the scalar equality
and an eigenmap hold for the supplied $g$. Any individual balanced
degree-$+1$ map attaining zero saturates the constant-test and
Poincaré bounds, and is a positive-degree homothety by the first
check. Such homotheties are balanced by round volume symmetry and
attain zero by the vanishing potential defect. Exact attainment and
the eigenmap criterion are separate statements.

## 3. Round calibration and feasibility

Take $g=c h$ on standard $S^4$, with $c=v_4^{-1/2}$. In stereographic
coordinates the round Yamabe quotient converts to
$6\|dv\|_2^2/\|v\|_4^2$ with $v=2u/(1+|x|^2)$. For smooth sphere
tests this passage is justified by decay and cutoff in the Euclidean
homogeneous Sobolev space. Constants on the sphere give the sharp
Euclidean optimizer of Talenti for dimension four and gradient
exponent two. Hence the round metric, and the unit-volume scaling,
meet the supplied Yamabe-minimizer hypothesis.
[Talenti, printed p. 353, opening Theorem, eqs. (1)–(3)](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/talenti.sobolev.pdf#page=1).

Here $s=12/c$ and $\lambda_1=4/c$: the coordinates have that
eigenvalue, and the first check gives the matching lower bound.
The restriction of a single ambient orthogonal reflection has
degree $-1$ and is a first eigenmap. Two positive bubbles produce
the verified balanced degree-$+1$ sequence with $Y_{f_\epsilon}\to0$
and strong $W^{1,2}$ limit of degree $-1$. Its signed measure limit is
$-dV_h+v_4\delta_{p_1}+v_4\delta_{p_2}$ with the domain identified
with the standard sphere. The identity on the same metric attains
zero. On this round input a degree-zero eigenmap is excluded:
the integrated Bochner formula for eigenvalue $4/c$ gives equality
in $|\nabla^2u|^2\ge(\Delta_g u)^2/4$, hence
$\nabla^2u=-(u/c)g$. This equation determines the eigenfunction by
its value and gradient at one point, so the eigenspace has at
most five dimensions and the five coordinates span it. Then an
eigenmap is $x\mapsto Ax$ for a real $5\times5$ matrix, and its
sphere constraint gives $A^\top A=I$. No degree-zero example on
a nonround homotopy sphere is claimed by the reviewed work.

The E16 construction establishes nonemptiness. Its radial collapse
maps the ball interior diffeomorphically onto the punctured target
with positive sign. On the exterior a rank-at-most-one perturbation,
flat along the seam, makes the target pushforward of the supplied
probability density nonatomic. The finite-parameter differential
spanning argument and Fubini make the exterior critical set null,
and the regular parts of level sets are null hypersurfaces. The
conformal centering maps then extend the barycenter continuously to
the closed parameter ball with boundary value the identity, since
each exceptional antipodal target point is null. The no-retraction
theorem forces an interior zero. This gives one balanced degree-$+1$ map
with smooth nonnegative Jacobian. A target pushforward atom of mass
$\beta>1/2$ obstructs balancing by any target conformal map since
the mean has length at least $2\beta-1$. These target atoms are for
$f_*dV_g$ on $S^4$. The atoms causing loss of degree in E15 are of
the signed Jacobian measure on $M$. Existence of one balanced map
gives no bound on near-optimal potentials or on the absolute
Jacobians of an optimizing sequence. [E16](DIR_E16_20261124.md).
