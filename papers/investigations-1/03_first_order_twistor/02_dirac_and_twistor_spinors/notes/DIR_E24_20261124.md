## Global lower bound on a fixed metric

Let $(M,g)$ be a fixed smooth closed oriented Riemannian four-manifold.
Orient the unit round target $(S^4,h)\subset\mathbb R^5$ by the outward
unit normal, and let $V=\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)=8\pi^2/3$. For a
smooth sphere-valued map $f$, use the unnormalized fourth energy
$E_4(f)=\int_M |df|_{\mathrm{HS}}^4\,dV_g$. If smooth degree-$+1$
maps $f_j$ converge strongly in ambient $W^{1,2}$ to a smooth
sphere-valued map $F$, then

\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \liminf_{j\to\infty} E_4(f_j)
 \ \ge\ E_4(F)+16V\,|1-\deg F|.}
\]

When the left-hand side is finite, work along a subsequence realizing
it. Put $B=dF$, $U_j=df_j-B$, and write $\|\cdot\|_p$ for the
$L^p(g)$ norm using the ambient Hilbert–Schmidt fiber norm on
differentials. Smoothness of $F$ gives $B\in L^\infty$. The energy
bound gives $\sup_j\|U_j\|_4<\infty$, while $\|U_j\|_2\to0$. In
particular $\int |U_j|\to0$, $\int |U_j|^2\to0$, and

\[
 \int_M |U_j|^3\,dV_g \le \|U_j\|_2\|U_j\|_4^2\longrightarrow0 .
\]

In a positively oriented local $g$-orthonormal frame
$(e_1,\dots,e_4)$, put
$J_f=\det(f,df(e_1),\dots,df(e_4))$, so
$\int_M J_f\,dV_g=V\deg f$. Expand $J_{f_j}$ in its four
differential columns, using $df_j=B+U_j$ and retaining the normal
$f_j$. Every term with one, two, or three $U_j$ columns is
pointwise bounded by $C|U_j|^m$ for its respective $m$, by the
unit length of $f_j$ and boundedness of $B$. These terms vanish
in $L^1$. In the term with four $B$ columns, replacing $f_j$
with $F$ incurs an $L^1$ error at most $C\|f_j-F\|_1=o(1)$.
These estimates are frame independent and give

\[
 V(1-\deg F)
 =\int_M \det(f_j,U_j(e_1),\ldots,U_j(e_4))\,dV_g+o(1).
\]

In this last determinant the projection $P_j$ onto $f_j^\perp$
leaves the result unchanged when applied to each $U_j$ column.
Let $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_4$ be the singular values of
$P_j U_j:T_xM\to f_j(x)^\perp$. The unit length of $f_j$ makes
the absolute determinant precisely $\prod_i\sigma_i$. Therefore

\[
 \left|\det(f_j,U_j(e_1),\ldots,U_j(e_4))\right|
 =\prod_i\sigma_i
 \le \left(\frac{\sum_i \sigma_i^2}{4}\right)^2
 \le \frac{|U_j|^4}{16}.
\]

To split the energy, expand
$(|B|^2+2\langle B,U_j\rangle+|U_j|^2)^2$. After removing
$|B|^4$ and $|U_j|^4$, each term contains between one and three
factors of $U_j$ and is $o(1)$ in $L^1$ by the same estimates.
Thus $E_4(f_j)=E_4(F)+\|U_j\|_4^4+o(1)$. Taking absolute values
in the degree difference and passing to the chosen subsequence
proves the claimed bound. The infinite-liminf case is immediate.
The proof uses only the unit sphere constraint, the signed degree
formula, the two norm estimates, and smoothness of the limit.

## Balanced near-zero sequences and recognition

For this application let $M$ be a smooth closed connected oriented
homotopy four-sphere and $g$ one supplied smooth unit-volume Yamabe
minimizer of constant scalar curvature $s$. In particular $M$ is
spin since $H^2(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0$. Let $\lambda_1>0$ be the first
positive eigenvalue of $\Delta_g=d^*d$. Balance means
$\int_M f\,dV_g=0$ in $\mathbb R^5$, and the modified value is

\[
 Y_f([g])
 =\inf_{u\in H^1(M)\setminus\{0\}}
 \frac{\int_M(6|du|^2+(s-3|df|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2)u^2)\,dV_g}
 {\|u\|_4^2}.
\]

For any sequence of smooth balanced degree-$+1$ maps with
$Y_{f_j}([g])\to0$, the constant test, Yamabe second variation and
Poincaré inequality give
$Y_{f_j}\le s-3\|df_j\|_2^2\le s-3\lambda_1\le0$,
forcing $s=3\lambda_1$ and $\|df_j\|_2^2\to\lambda_1$.
For every subsequence the spectral gap above the first eigenspace
supplies a further subsequence converging strongly in $W^{1,2}$
to a smooth sphere-valued limit $F$ satisfying
$\Delta_g F=\lambda_1 F$ componentwise. The sphere constraint gives
$|dF|^2=\lambda_1$ pointwise, so $E_4(F)=\lambda_1^2$. This spectral
step is the part of [E14][e14] and [E17][e17] that precedes the
Jacobian condition.

At such a limit, $s=3|dF|^2$. For the squared singular values
$a_i$ of $dF$, the inequality
$\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k\le (3/8)(\sum_i a_i)^2$ gives
$s\ge (24\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k)^{1/2}$.
Listing's sphere rigidity theorem applies for either sign of nonzero
degree. The source is closed and spin, the target is unit round of
the same even dimension, and this is the pointwise curvature bound.
The theorem yields the homothety $F^*h=(\lambda_1/4)g$. The homothety
is a covering because $M$ is compact, and a diffeomorphism because
$M$ is connected and $S^4$ simply connected. Thus the only possible
degrees are $+1,0,-1$. [Listing, §1, Theorem 3, eq. (6), PDF p. 4][listing].

Take a subsequence realizing the energy liminf and extract its smooth
first-eigenmap limit as above. The global bound gives the following
lower bounds on that subsequence's limiting energy.

| Degree of $F$ | Lower bound on the limiting energy | Consequence of the limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $+1$ | $\lambda_1^2$ | Orientation-preserving recognizing homothety |
| $0$ | $\lambda_1^2+16V$ | Listing's nonzero-degree hypothesis is unavailable |
| $-1$ | $\lambda_1^2+32V$ | Orientation-reversing recognizing homothety |

Consequently $\liminf_j E_4(f_j)<\lambda_1^2+16V$ forces
a degree-$+1$ recognizing limit on the chosen subsequence. The
same strict threshold with a limsup instead applies to every strong
$W^{1,2}$ cluster limit. A degree-$-1$ limit is also a diffeomorphism
recognizing the standard smooth type. The approximating maps still
have degree $+1$, with two units of degree lost in the limit. If
a separate argument excludes degree zero, then $\lambda_1^2+32V$
is the strict threshold for retaining degree $+1$ by this estimate.
The per-unit cost is $16V=128\pi^2/3$.

## Fourth energy of the finite E15 recovery family

Suppose $s=3\lambda_1$ and a smooth sphere-valued first eigenmap
$F$ is supplied. For its degree $d\in\{-1,0,1\}$, the [E15][e15]
construction uses $k=1-d$ positive degree insertions. If $k=0$,
take $f_\epsilon=F$, so $E_4(f_\epsilon)=\lambda_1^2$.
For $k=1,2$, at each chosen point take positively oriented normal
coordinates with $r=|x|$, set $\rho=\epsilon^{1/2}$, and flatten
$F$ to $F(p_i)$ by radius $3\rho$, matching $F$ at
radius $4\rho$. In target normal coordinates the cutoff multiplies
a function of size $O(\rho)$ and first derivative $O(1)$ on
the flattening region. Thus the flattening derivative stays $O(1)$
and contributes $O(\rho^4)$ to fourth energy. The region
$2\rho\le r\le3\rho$ is constant.

On the inserted bubble core $r\le\rho$, the unbalanced joined map
$G_\epsilon$ pulls back the target metric to $b_\epsilon^2\delta$
in these coordinates, where $b_\epsilon=2\epsilon/(\epsilon^2+r^2)$.
The rotation taking the south pole to $F(p_i)$ is an
orientation-preserving isometry. Since $g_{ab}=\delta_{ab}+O(r^2)$,
the fourth-energy density on this core is

\[
 |dG_\epsilon|^4\,dV_g
 = b_\epsilon^4\big(\operatorname{tr}_g\delta\big)^2
    \sqrt{\det g}\,dx
 =16b_\epsilon^4(1+O(r^2))\,dx .
\]

The inverse stereographic map identifies $b_\epsilon^4\,dx$
with the round volume on the target minus one point. Its integral
over $\mathbb R^4$ is $V$, and its tail $r\ge\rho$ is
$O((\epsilon/\rho)^4)$, by integrating $C\epsilon^4 r^{-5}$
radially. Therefore the core contributes
$16V+O(\rho^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^4)$. In $\rho\le r\le2\rho$,
the target south-pole coordinate is
$\kappa(r/\rho)\epsilon x/r^2$ for a fixed smooth cutoff $\kappa$.
Its derivative is bounded by $C\epsilon/\rho^2=O(1)$, with
the same bound in the Riemannian metrics. Fourth energy there is
$O((\epsilon/\rho)^4)=O(\rho^4)$.
[E09, smooth degree-one maps and local estimates][e09].

The complement of the flattening balls contributes $E_4(F)+O(\rho^4)$
by smoothness of $F$. The insertions change the degree by $+k$
because the flattened map was constant on each bubble ball and
each bubble extends by a constant with degree $+1$.
The uncorrected mean differs from the mean of $F$ by $O(\rho^4)$
because only the shrinking balls were modified. The E15 correction
postcomposes by an orientation-preserving target conformal flow
$\Phi_{b_\epsilon^{\mathrm{bal}}}$, with
$|b_\epsilon^{\mathrm{bal}}|=O(\rho^4)$, and makes the mean
exactly zero. The derivative of the mean map at $F$ and parameter
zero is $I-\int_M FF^\top\,dV_g$, positive definite because
connectedness and balance exclude a map with image in two antipodal
points. This is the invertibility behind the $O(\rho^4)$ estimate.
Writing $\Phi_b^*h=a_b^2h$, one has $\|a_b^4-1\|_\infty\le C|b|$
near zero. Thus for $f_\epsilon=\Phi_{b_\epsilon^{\mathrm{bal}}}
\circ G_\epsilon$,

\[
 |E_4(f_\epsilon)-E_4(G_\epsilon)|
 \le C\rho^4 E_4(G_\epsilon)=o(1).
\]

The corrected maps are smooth, balanced and of degree $+1$.
They converge strongly in $W^{1,2}$ to $F$: the changes before
correction have shrinking support and bounded fourth energy, and
the target correction converges to the identity in $C^1$.
For the near-zero property, E15's local positive ground-state
comparison and logarithmic localization give a lower bound on
the unbalanced scalar form by
$-C(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2+\ell^{-3/2})\|u\|_4^2$, with
$R=\epsilon^{1/4}$ and $\ell=\log(R/(4\rho))$.
The target correction changes $3|dG_\epsilon|^2$ by $o(1)$
in $L^2$ using the bounded fourth energy just checked. Hölder
preserves the lower bound up to $o(1)\|u\|_4^2$, and the balanced
upper bound is zero. Thus $Y_{f_\epsilon}([g])\to0$, and

\[
 \lim_{\epsilon\downarrow0} E_4(f_\epsilon)
   =\lambda_1^2+16V(1-d)
   =E_4(F)+16V\,|1-d|.
\]

The degree-zero row of the energy table is attained by this
construction whenever such an eigenmap is supplied. Its existence
on a homotopy sphere remains an open premise here. Sharpness of
the recognition threshold at the one-unit budget is conditional on
that premise. On round $S^4$ with $g=V^{-1/2}h$,
$s=12V^{1/2}$ and $\lambda_1=4V^{1/2}$, as follows from the
round coordinate eigenfunctions and the Yamabe second variation.
Round minimality follows from stereographic conformal covariance
and the sharp Euclidean Sobolev optimizer given by Talenti.
The identity attains the $+1$ row and a round reflection followed
by the two E15 insertions realizes the $-1$ row at total energy
$48V=128\pi^2$. This actual example proves sharpness of the
global coefficient $16V$. [Talenti, printed p. 353, opening
Theorem and eqs. (1)–(3)][talenti] · [E17 round calibration][e17].

The inequality controls the total energy excess in terms of the net
degree difference. Quantization of individual defect atoms would
require separate analysis. The recognition conclusion still requires
an actual balanced near-zero sequence on a supplied minimizing metric
with the stated energy budget.
