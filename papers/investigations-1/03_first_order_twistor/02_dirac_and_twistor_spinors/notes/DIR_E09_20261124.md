Fix a smooth closed connected oriented spin homotopy four-sphere
$(M,g)$ with arbitrary smooth positive-definite metric. Let $h$ be the
unit round metric on the oriented $S^4$, with scalar curvature $12$.
Use $\Delta_g=d^*d$. For smooth maps $f:M\to S^4$ of degree $+1$, put

\[
 V_f=3|df|_{g,h}^2,\qquad
 Q_f(u)=\int_M\big(6|du|_g^2+(s_g-V_f)u^2\big)\,dV_g,
 \qquad Y_f([g])=\inf_{u\in H^1(M)\setminus\{0\}}
 \frac{Q_f(u)}{\|u\|_{L^4(g)}^2}.
\]

Write $Q_0$ for the untwisted scalar form with zero potential, and
$Y=Y([g])$ for its infimum with the same normalization. Smooth tests
give the same infima by density and the Sobolev embedding. Then

\[
 \boxed{\quad\sup_{f\text{ smooth},\ \deg f=+1}Y_f([g])=\min\{Y,0\}.\quad}
\]

The supremum is attained precisely when there is an orientation-preserving
conformal diffeomorphism $(M,[g])\to(S^4,[h])$. The maximizing maps are
exactly those diffeomorphisms. Such a class has $Y>0$.

## Upper bound

For every supplied map, $V_f\ge0$ gives $Y_f\le Y$. By DIR-E05,
$\lambda_1(6\Delta_g+s_g-V_f)\le0$. Its smooth positive ground state
has $Q_f\le0$, so $Y_f\le0$. The only rigidity input is DIR-E05's
fixed-pair application of Mario Listing, *Scalar Curvature on Compact
Symmetric Spaces*, [arXiv:1007.1832](https://arxiv.org/abs/1007.1832).

## Smooth degree-one maps

Fix $p\in M$ and positively oriented normal coordinates around $p$.
For $0<\epsilon<1$ sufficiently small take the two shrinking radii
$\rho=\epsilon^{1/2}$ and $R=\epsilon^{1/4}$, with $2\rho<R$ inside
the normal chart. On the Euclidean chart set $r=|x|$ and

\[
 F_\epsilon(x)=
 \left(\frac{2\epsilon x}{\epsilon^2+r^2},
       \frac{\epsilon^2-r^2}{\epsilon^2+r^2}\right),
 \qquad b_\epsilon(x)=\frac{2\epsilon}{\epsilon^2+r^2}.
\]

This inverse stereographic map goes from $\mathbb R^4$ to the unit
sphere minus the south pole. Choose the target coordinates so that the
first four coordinate vectors are positively oriented at the north
pole. Then $dF_\epsilon(0)$ is orientation-preserving.

On $r\le\rho$ set $f_\epsilon=F_\epsilon$. In the target chart around
the south pole, with that pole at $z=0$, the map $F_\epsilon$ is
$z=\epsilon x/r^2$. For $r\ge\rho$ in the domain chart replace this by
$z=\kappa(r/\rho)\epsilon x/r^2$, where $\kappa$ is a smooth cutoff
equal to $1$ up to $1$ and to $0$ from $2$ onward. The resulting map
extends by the south pole outside $B_{2\rho}$. It is smooth across the
two cutoff radii. The north pole has its unique preimage at $p$, with
positive derivative, so $\deg f_\epsilon=+1$.

## Local potential and metric estimates

Let $P_\epsilon=3|dF_\epsilon|_{\delta,h}^2=12b_\epsilon^2$, since
$F_\epsilon^*h=b_\epsilon^2\delta$ and the dimension is four. Here
$\delta$ is the Euclidean normal-coordinate metric. The Euclidean
Laplacian with our sign is $\Delta_\delta=-\sum_i\partial_i^2$.
Direct radial differentiation gives $\Delta_\delta b_\epsilon=2b_\epsilon^3$.
Thus $(-6\sum_i\partial_i^2-P_\epsilon)b_\epsilon=0$ with
$b_\epsilon>0$. For smooth compactly supported $v$, substitution of
$v=b_\epsilon w$ and integration by parts yield

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^4}(6|dv|_\delta^2-P_\epsilon v^2)\,dx
 =6\int_{\mathbb R^4} b_\epsilon^2|d(v/b_\epsilon)|_\delta^2\,dx
 \ge0.
\]

For each fixed $\epsilon$ the inequality extends by density to
$H^1_0(B_R)$ extended by zero in the chart. The scale-critical bound
$\|P_\epsilon\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^4)}\le C$ follows by $x=\epsilon y$.
For $r\ge\rho$ one has $P_\epsilon\le C\epsilon^2/r^4$, so
$\|P_\epsilon\|_{L^2(r\ge\rho)}\le C(\epsilon/\rho)^2$.

On $\rho\le r\le2\rho$, differentiating the cutoff and the south
coordinate $\epsilon x/r^2$ gives $|df_\epsilon|_{g,h}\le C\epsilon/\rho^2$.
The annulus has volume $O(\rho^4)$, so the actual potential there
has $L^2$ norm at most $C(\epsilon/\rho)^2$. It vanishes outside
$B_{2\rho}$. In $B_\rho$ the exact stereographic pullback and the
normal-coordinate bound $g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(r^2)$ give
$V_{f_\epsilon}=P_\epsilon(1+O(r^2))$. If $J=\sqrt{\det(g_{ij})}$,
it follows that

\[
 \|J V_{f_\epsilon}-P_\epsilon\|_{L^2(B_R,dx)}
 \le C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2\big).
\]

The same estimate holds without the $J$ factor. Also
$\|Js_g\|_{L^2(B_R,dx)}\le CR^2$ because the smooth scalar curvature
is bounded and the coordinate volume is $O(R^4)$.

For $v\in H^1_0(B_R)$, normal coordinates bound the local kinetic
energy below by $(1-CR^2)6\int|dv|_\delta^2\,dx$. To make its error
uniform in $v$, keep that factor on the full Euclidean quadratic
form. By the two $L^2$ estimates above and the equivalence of the
$L^4$ norms on $B_R$,

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 Q_{f_\epsilon}(v)
 &\ge (1-CR^2)\int(6|dv|_\delta^2-P_\epsilon v^2)\,dx
       -CR^2\int P_\epsilon v^2\,dx
       -C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2\big)\|v\|_{L^4(g)}^2\\
 &\ge -C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2\big)\|v\|_{L^4(g)}^2 .
 \end{aligned}
\]

The second line uses the nonnegative Euclidean form, $1-CR^2>0$,
and the bounded $L^2$ norm of $P_\epsilon$. The estimate applies with
arbitrary gradients of $v$.

## Logarithmic localization and the three signs

Let $\ell=\log(R/(2\rho))\to\infty$. Choose a smooth function
$\Theta(t)\in[0,\pi/2]$ with value $0$ for $t\le0$ and $\pi/2$ for
$t\ge1$. On the transition annulus let
$\theta(r)=\Theta(\log(r/(2\rho))/\ell)$. Extend it constantly inside
and outside. Set $\chi=\cos\theta$ and $\eta=\sin\theta$.
Then $\chi^2+\eta^2=1$, $\chi u\in H^1_0(B_R)$, and $\eta=0$ on the
support of $V_{f_\epsilon}$ except at its outer boundary where the
potential vanishes. The bound $|d\theta|_g\le C/(\ell r)$ gives

\[
 \|d\chi\|_{L^4(g)}^4+\|d\eta\|_{L^4(g)}^4
 \le C\ell^{-4}\int_{2\rho}^R\frac{dr}{r}=C\ell^{-3}.
\]

Expanding gradients and using $\chi\,d\chi+\eta\,d\eta=0$ gives

\[
 Q_{f_\epsilon}(u)=Q_{f_\epsilon}(\chi u)+Q_0(\eta u)
 -6\int_M(|d\chi|^2+|d\eta|^2)u^2\,dV_g .
\]

The final integral is at most $C\ell^{-3/2}\|u\|_{L^4(g)}^2$ by
Hölder. With $e_\epsilon=C(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2+\ell^{-3/2})\to0$,
the local estimate yields

\[
 Q_{f_\epsilon}(u)\ge Y\|\eta u\|_{L^4(g)}^2
                       -e_\epsilon\|u\|_{L^4(g)}^2.
\]

If $Y>0$ the exterior term is nonnegative. It vanishes from the lower
bound when $Y=0$. If $Y<0$ then $\|\eta u\|_4\le\|u\|_4$ bounds it
below by $Y\|u\|_4^2$. Consequently

\[
 \min(Y,0)-e_\epsilon\le Y_{f_\epsilon}([g])\le\min(Y,0).
\]

This proves the claimed supremum using the same family for all three
signs. The lower estimate is uniform over $\|u\|_{L^4(g)}=1$.

## Degree concentration and attainment

The map is eventually constant in a neighborhood of each point other
than $p$. On $B_\rho$, the signed degree measure is exactly
$f_\epsilon^*dV_h=b_\epsilon^4\,dx$. Its integral there tends to
$\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)$ by the rescaling $x=\epsilon y$. The
absolute integral contributed by the annulus is
$O((\epsilon/\rho)^4)$ by the derivative estimate. Thus as measures on
$M$,

\[
 f_\epsilon^*dV_h\rightharpoonup\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)\,\delta_p.
\]

The potential also concentrates at critical size. The same rescaling
and the $L^2$ comparison give
$V_{f_\epsilon}^2\,dV_g\rightharpoonup144\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)\,\delta_p$.
In particular, the small $L^2$ error concerns the difference from the
Euclidean bubble potential, whose $L^2$ norm stays positive.

If $Y\ge0$ and a fixed $f$ attains the supremum, $Y_f=0$, so $Q_f$
is nonnegative on all tests. Hence $\lambda_1\ge0$ and DIR-E05 makes
$f$ an orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphism. Conversely every
such map gives $\lambda_1=0$ with smooth positive eigenfunction, so
$Q_f\ge0$ and $Y_f=0$. A class conformally diffeomorphic to round has
$Y>0$: the round form $6\|du\|_2^2+12\|u\|_2^2$ controls $\|u\|_4^2$
by the compact Sobolev inequality, and $Y$ is conformally invariant.
Thus for $Y=0$ the supremum is never attained.

For $Y<0$, there is a positive ordinary Yamabe minimizer by a direct
argument. A nonnegative sequence normalized by $\|u_j\|_4=1$ with
$Q_0(u_j)\to Y$ is bounded in $H^1$, since
$Q_0(u_j)\ge6\|du_j\|_2^2-\|(s_g)_-\|_2$. A subsequence converges
weakly in $H^1$ and strongly in $L^2$ to a nonnegative $u$ with
$\|u\|_4\le1$. Weak lower semicontinuity gives
$Y\|u\|_4^2\le Q_0(u)\le Y<0$, so $\|u\|_4=1$ and $Q_0(u)=Y$.
The weak equation is $6\Delta_g u+s_gu=Y u^3$. Testing with
$(u-K)^+$ for $K>0$ and $|Y|K^2>\|(s_g)_-\|_\infty$ shows $u\le K$.
Elliptic regularity then gives smoothness and the Harnack inequality
applies to $6\Delta_g u+(s_g-Yu^2)u=0$, giving $u>0$. For any
fixed smooth degree-one $f$, $V_f$ is positive somewhere, so

\[
 Y_f([g])\le Q_f(u)=Y-\int_M V_f u^2\,dV_g<Y.
\]

This proves nonattainment in the negative case. Each constructed
$f_\epsilon$ is constant on an open set and is excluded from equality
also when $Y\ge0$. The degree concentration and supremum concern a
single fixed smooth manifold and conformal class. Achieving the
supremum by a genuine map in a positive class still requires the
recognizing conformal diffeomorphism.
