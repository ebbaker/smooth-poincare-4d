## Geometry and the two spectral defects

Fix a smooth closed connected oriented spin homotopy four-sphere $M$ and
a smooth unit-volume Riemannian Yamabe minimizer $g$ of constant scalar
curvature $s$, of any sign. Write $\Delta_g=d^*d$ on functions and
$0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots$ for its positive eigenvalues counted
with multiplicity. Use the assessed B20 theorem as specified in the
[frozen request][request]: at $s=3\lambda_1$ the first eigenspace has
dimension at most five, with dimension five exactly when $g$ is
homothetically round. The finite trace argument below uses the theorem
only to recognize the zero-defect case.

For a smooth degree-$+1$ map $f:M\to S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$, take the
unit round metric $h$ on the oriented target and require
$\int f\,dV_g=0$. Put $C_f=\int ff^\top dV_g$, so $C_f$ is symmetric
positive semidefinite of trace one. We use the fixed-map modified Yamabe
value of [E08][e08],

\[
Y_f([g])=\inf_{0\ne u\in H^1(M)}
\frac{\int_M(6|du|_g^2+(s-3|df|_{g,h}^2)u^2)\,dV_g}
{(\int_M|u|^4\,dV_g)^{1/2}} .
\]

Write $\mathcal D(g)=3\lambda_1-s$ and
$\mathcal B(g)=\sum_{i=2}^5(\lambda_i-\lambda_1)$.
Both are nonnegative. For the first, take a real first eigenfunction
$v$ with zero mean and $\|v\|_2=1$ in the ordinary Yamabe quotient.
The test $1+tv$ is positive for small $|t|$. The numerator is
$s+(6\lambda_1+s)t^2$ and the denominator is $1+3t^2+O(t^3)$.
Minimality at $1$ implies $6\lambda_1-2s\ge0$. This gives
$\lambda_1\ge s/3$ directly in every scalar-curvature sign.
[E14, Yamabe second variation][e14].

## L² orthonormalization and the finite trace bound

Suppose $C_f\ge\delta I$ with $0<\delta\le1/5$. The components of
$w=C_f^{-1/2}f$ form five real $L^2(g)$-orthonormal mean-zero functions.
Define the symmetric matrix $Q_{ab}=\int\langle dw_a,dw_b\rangle_g dV_g$.
For each $z\in\mathbb R^5$, the mean-zero function $z\cdot w$ has
squared norm $|z|^2$. Poincaré gives $Q\ge\lambda_1 I$.

For the Ky Fan lower trace bound, expand in a real orthonormal eigenbasis
$(e_\ell)_{\ell\ge1}$ of the mean-zero functions. Put
$p_\ell=\sum_{a=1}^5 |\langle w_a,e_\ell\rangle_{L^2}|^2$. Bessel's
inequality gives $0\le p_\ell\le1$ and Parseval gives $\sum p_\ell=5$.
The diagonal trace is $\operatorname{tr}Q=\sum_\ell\lambda_\ell p_\ell$.
Subtracting $\sum_{i=1}^5\lambda_i$ and using $\sum p_\ell=5$, the
difference is

\[
\sum_{\ell>5}(\lambda_\ell-\lambda_5)p_\ell
+\sum_{i=1}^5(\lambda_5-\lambda_i)(1-p_i)\ge0.
\]

Since $f=C_f^{1/2}w$ and the target is isometrically embedded in
$\mathbb R^5$, the energy is $\int |df|^2 dV_g=\operatorname{tr}(C_fQ)$.
The matrices $C_f-\delta I$ and $Q-\lambda_1I$ are positive semidefinite,
so the trace of their product is nonnegative by cyclicity and a positive
square root. Thus

\[
\int_M |df|^2\,dV_g
\ge \lambda_1+\delta(\operatorname{tr}Q-5\lambda_1)
\ge \lambda_1+\delta\mathcal B(g).
\]

Applying the unit constant test to the coupled quotient gives the
explicit upper bound

\[
\boxed{\displaystyle
Y_f([g])\le s-3(1-5\delta)\lambda_1-3\delta\sum_{i=1}^5\lambda_i
=-\mathcal D(g)-3\delta\mathcal B(g)\le0.}
\tag{1}
\]

Only balance and the moment bound enter this estimate. The degree
condition enters the normalized class and the orientation of its round
optimizer. An individual degree-$+1$ map is onto, so $C_f$ is positive
definite: for each nonzero $z$ some image point has $z\cdot f\ne0$, which
by continuity holds over a set of positive volume. Uniform positivity
along sequences is a separate constraint.

## Exact isotropy and recognition

At $\delta=1/5$ the trace identity gives $C_f=I/5$. The exact normalized
class consists of smooth balanced degree-$+1$ maps with this covariance.
Its supremum is bounded above by $s-(3/5)\sum_{i=1}^5\lambda_i$. If $g$
is nonround, at least one of $\mathcal D(g),\mathcal B(g)$ is positive:
their simultaneous vanishing would give $s=3\lambda_1$ and at least five
first eigenfunctions, hence multiplicity five and roundness by the
supplied B20 theorem. Thus the strict negative upper bound for this fixed
metric is $-[\mathcal D(g)+(3/5)\mathcal B(g)]$. It applies to each map
and, with the extended-real convention for an empty class, to the
supremum. The argument gives a bound on a nonround supremum.

If $g$ is homothetically round, take an orientation-preserving
homothety $F:M\to(S^4,h)$ and write $g=cF^*h$ with
$c=\operatorname{Vol}(S^4,h)^{-1/2}$. A target reflection may be composed
with an initially orientation-reversing homothety to obtain degree $+1$.
The pushforward of $dV_g$ is normalized round measure, so symmetry gives
$\int F=0$ and $C_F=I/5$. Then $s=12/c$ and $|dF|^2=4/c$, and the modified
potential vanishes pointwise. The quotient is nonnegative and equals zero
on constants. This proves that the normalized supremum is zero exactly
in the homothetically round case, where it is attained by a degree-$+1$
homothety. The same conclusion applies for any fixed $\delta>0$ in (1).
The round Yamabe normalization is the one checked in [E17][e17] using
the stereographic sharp Sobolev calibration. [Talenti, p. 353][talenti].

In particular, without presupposing any map,

\[
\frac35\sum_{i=1}^5\lambda_i-s=\mathcal D(g)+\frac35\mathcal B(g)\ge0,
\tag{2}
\]

with equality exactly for a homothetically round supplied minimizer.
For the converse in (2), the five coordinate functions on the unit
sphere have Laplace eigenvalue $4$, and the scale $c$ changes this
to $4/c$. Their spherical Hessian is $-F^a h$ in the unit metric.
Since $\lambda_1\ge s/3=4/c$, the first five positive eigenvalues
are all $4/c$. At equality, the B20 theorem and the supplied scalar
minimization hypothesis are the recognition inputs.

## Near-zero sequences and dependence on the metric

For a fixed $g$, take smooth balanced degree-$+1$ maps $f_j$ with
$Y_{f_j}([g])\to0$, with or without a uniform covariance lower bound.
Ordinary Poincaré and the unit constant test give
$Y_{f_j}\le s-3\int|df_j|^2\le s-3\lambda_1\le0$. Hence
$\mathcal D(g)=0$ and the energy excess satisfies

\[
0\le\int|df_j|^2\,dV_g-\lambda_1\le -Y_{f_j}([g])/3\longrightarrow0.
\]

Let $\Pi$ be the scalar first-eigenspace projection applied to each
component and $\lambda_*>\lambda_1$ the next distinct eigenvalue.
On the orthogonal complement among mean-zero functions the coefficient
inequality $1+\lambda\le \frac{1+\lambda_*}{\lambda_* -\lambda_1}
(\lambda-\lambda_1)$ gives $\|(1-\Pi)f_j\|_{W^{1,2}}\to0$. A subsequence
of the bounded finite-dimensional projections converges to a smooth
first eigenmap $F$ and the convergence is strong in ambient $W^{1,2}$.
The constraint $|F|=1$ follows from $L^2$ convergence and continuity.
This is the spectral argument of [E14][e14] and [E17][e17]. For the
Gram matrices the simpler estimate

\[
\|C_{f_j}-C_F\|\le (\|f_j\|_{L^2}+\|F\|_{L^2})\|f_j-F\|_{L^2}
=2\|f_j-F\|_{L^2}
\]

gives convergence along every such subsequence from the $L^2$ estimate
alone. An $L^4$ bound on $df_j$ or uniform absolute-Jacobian integrability
would be additional information.
If the limit covariance is positive definite, (1) gives roundness using
a common lower bound on the covariance along that subsequence. If $g$
is nonround, then $\mathcal B(g)>0$ at this scalar equality. Applying (1)
to each $\delta_j=\lambda_{\min}(C_{f_j})>0$ gives the whole-sequence bound

\[
\delta_j\le\frac{-Y_{f_j}([g])}{3\mathcal B(g)}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Every subsequential Gram limit is then singular, consistent with the
first eigenspace having dimension at most four by B20.

For a fixed round $g$, its first eigenspace has dimension five and the
five coordinates of the homothety form a basis. Any sphere-valued first
eigenmap has the form $AF$ for a real $5\times5$ matrix $A$. The sphere
constraint and surjectivity of $F$ give $A^\top A=I$, so every possible
limit has covariance $I/5$. The compactness argument then gives
$C_{f_j}\to I/5$ along the whole sequence. An individual degree-$+1$
map attaining zero has first-eigenfunction coordinates by the same
Poincaré equality, so its orthogonal matrix preserves orientation.
For merely balanced sequences the [E17 reflection and bubble example][e17]
exhibits loss of degree even with convergence of the limiting Gram matrix.

For varying smooth unit-volume minimizers $g_j$ with a uniform fixed
$\delta>0$ (including exact isotropy), (1) instead implies
$\mathcal D(g_j)\to0$ and $\mathcal B(g_j)\to0$ if $Y_{f_j}([g_j])\to0$.
The same numerical conclusion follows from a uniform limiting lower
bound on the covariance matrices. There is no fixed spectral projection
or higher-mode gap in that argument. Obtaining a limiting smooth critical
metric, or constructing such sequences on arbitrary homotopy spheres,
would require further inputs. For a fixed conformal class the inequality
uses the supplied minimizing representative to define the moments and
spectral gap. Uniformity over varying representatives requires additional
information on their geometry.
