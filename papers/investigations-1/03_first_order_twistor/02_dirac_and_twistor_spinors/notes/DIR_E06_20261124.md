## Result

Let \(M\) be a smooth, connected, closed, oriented homotopy four-sphere
with a fixed smooth positive-definite metric \(g\). Use its unique spin
structure, \(X\cdot X=-|X|_g^2\), and \(S^+\) the complex rank-two
half-spin bundle on which self-dual two-forms act. Fix \(p\in M\) and
\(0\ne\eta\in S_p^-\). There is a smooth section \(\psi\) of \(S^+\)
with exactly one zero at \(p\), transverse, and
\[
(\nabla_X\psi)(p)=X\cdot\eta\qquad (X\in T_pM).
\]
It has finite [B06](../brainstorm/notes/DIR_B06_RELATIVE_DEFECT_20261124.md)
cost \(J_g(\psi)=\bigl(\int_{M\setminus Z(\psi)} |P_g\psi|^4/|\psi|^4\,dV_g\bigr)^{1/4}\).
The construction uses the given metric and a single coordinate ball.

## Chern number and local index

The underlying real bundle \(S^+_{\mathbb R}\) has its complex orientation,
so \(e(S^+_{\mathbb R})=c_2(S^+)\). The adjoint representation of the
positive \(\mathrm{SU}(2)\) factor identifies \(\Lambda^+\) with
\(\mathfrak{su}(S^+)\). If \(x,-x\) are the formal Chern roots of \(S^+\),
its complexified adjoint has roots \(2x,0,-2x\). With
\(p_1(V)=-c_2(V\otimes\mathbb C)\), this gives
\(p_1(\Lambda^+)=-4c_2(S^+)\).
For an oriented orthogonal splitting of the tangent representation into
two real planes with Euler roots \(a,b\), the torus of plane rotations fixes
one axis of \(\Lambda^+\) and rotates the remaining plane by the sum of
the two rotation angles. Thus
\[
p_1(\Lambda^+)=(a+b)^2=p_1(TM)+2e(TM).
\]
These splitting calculations can be made rationally, which suffices for
the integral characteristic numbers in dimension four.

Hirzebruch's signature formula for smooth closed oriented four-manifolds
gives \(\langle p_1(TM),[M]\rangle=3\tau(M)=0\). The tangent Euler
number is \(\chi(M)=2\), hence
\[
\big\langle c_2(S^+),[M]\big\rangle=-1.
\]
[Hirzebruch, 1971, §4, p. 11, equation (16)](https://hirzebruch.mpim-bonn.mpg.de/id/eprint/84/1/signature%20theorem.pdf)

For the local sign, identify an oriented orthonormal tangent frame with
\((1,i,j,k)\) in \(\mathbb H\). Write the spin action on vectors as
\(v\mapsto a v b^{-1}\) for unit quaternions \((a,b)\). Realize the
half-spin spaces as copies of \(\mathbb H\), complex via right
multiplication by \(i\), with action
\((s_+,s_-)\mapsto(a s_+,b s_-)\). Use the equivariant Clifford action
\[
c(v)(s_+,s_-)=(v s_-,-\bar v s_+),\qquad c(v)^2=-|v|^2.
\]
For the self-dual form represented by \(1\wedge i+j\wedge k\), the
Clifford action \(c(1)c(i)+c(j)c(k)\) is left multiplication by
\(2i\) on the first summand and vanishes on the second. The first
summand is therefore the designated \(S^+\). Its complex basis
\((1,j)\) orders the real axes as \((1,i,j,-k)\), which reverses
the tangent-frame orientation. The jet \(v\mapsto v\eta\) is right
multiplication by a nonzero quaternion, orientation preserving in the
real quaternionic orientation since \(\mathbb H\setminus\{0\}\) is
connected. With the target's complex orientation the jet has determinant
sign \(-1\). The corresponding unit sphere map has degree \(-1\)
with the outward boundary orientation on the ball and the complex
orientation on the target ball.

## Construction and collar matching

Take a positively oriented normal coordinate chart at \(p\) from the
chosen tangent frame, and a smooth spin frame on it agreeing with the
frame at \(p\). In that trivialization, let \(\psi_B(x)=x\cdot\eta\),
where the displayed Clifford multiplication is the fixed model on
\(\mathbb R^4\). Take a closed ball \(B\) inside the chart with
room for an outer collar. The model section is nonzero away from \(p\)
and has the asserted covariant first jet, since the connection term
vanishes at its zero.

Put \(N=M\setminus\mathring B\). Excision identifies \(H_*(M,N;\mathbb Z)\)
with \(H_*(B,\partial B;\mathbb Z)\). The fundamental class maps to
the relative orientation generator in degree four, so the exact
sequence gives \(\widetilde H_*(N;\mathbb Z)=0\). Van Kampen with collar
thickenings and the simply connected \(S^3\) intersection gives
\(\pi_1(N)=0\). As a smooth compact manifold with boundary, \(N\) has
CW type. Hurewicz and Whitehead then give contractibility. In particular
\(S^+|_N\) has a smooth unitary trivialization. One obtains a smooth
frame by smoothing a continuous trivialization on the compact complement
and orthonormalizing.

In this exterior frame let \(f:\partial N\to S^3\) be the direction of
\(\psi_B\). To compute its degree, extend \(\psi_B\) smoothly using a
cutoff on the outer collar and perturb in the interior of \(N\) to make
the extension transverse to zero. For this temporary section the signed
sum of zeros in \(N\) equals \(\deg f\), with \(\partial N\) outward
oriented. Indeed, in the exterior trivialization the normalized extension
maps \(N\) with small balls around the zeros removed to \(S^3\).
Its oriented boundary consists of \(\partial N\) and the negatives of
the outward-oriented small spheres. Their images therefore give
\(\deg f\) minus the signed sum of local degrees as a boundary in
homology. Globally the signed sum is the Euler number \(-1\), and
the zero at \(p\) contributes \(-1\). Consequently \(\deg f=0\).
The opposite orientations of \(\partial N\) and \(\partial B\) in
this computation account for the change of boundary.

Maps \(S^3\to S^3\) of degree zero are nullhomotopic. Keep the original
direction on a smaller exterior collar and use a nullhomotopy on the
rest of the collar to join it to a constant. The direction on any slice
of the original collar has the same degree, so this gives a continuous
unit section on \(N\), smooth on the smaller collar. In the exterior
trivialization, smooth it relative to a still smaller collar as an
\(\mathbb R^4\)-valued map, using uniform error less than \(1/2\),
and normalize. Extend the positive length smoothly by using a cutoff
on \(\log|\psi_B|\) in the original collar and exponentiating.
The resulting section equals \(\psi_B\) on a collar of the seam and
is nonvanishing throughout \(N\). Gluing to \(\psi_B\) on \(B\)
preserves smoothness and gives the unique prescribed zero. This
argument uses the homotopy-sphere hypothesis and no classification
by diffeomorphism type.

## Cost at the zero

The convention is \(P_X\psi=\nabla_X\psi+\tfrac14X\cdot D_g\psi\),
with \(D_g=\sum_{j=1}^4 e_j\cdot\nabla_{e_j}\) in an orthonormal frame.
At \(p\) the prescribed jet gives
\[
D_g\psi(p)=\sum_{j=1}^4e_j\cdot e_j\cdot\eta=-4\eta,
\qquad P_g\psi(p)=0.
\]
Writing \(r=|x|\), transversality gives \(c r\le|\psi(x)|\le C r\)
near \(p\) for positive constants \(c,C\), and smoothness gives
\(|P_g\psi(x)|\le C' r\). The fourth power of the quotient is bounded
on the punctured ball. It is also bounded on the compact complement
of a smaller ball, where the denominator has a positive minimum.

For any smooth section with a transverse zero at \(p\), its first
jet \(A:X\mapsto(\nabla_X\psi)(p)\) gives \(P_g\psi(p)=0\) precisely
when \(A(X)=X\cdot\eta'\) for some \(\eta'\in S_p^-\), necessarily
nonzero. In one direction take \(\eta'=-D_g\psi(p)/4\); in the other,
take the Clifford trace as above. For an incompatible jet,
\(|P_g\psi|\) is bounded above and bounded below by a positive constant
near the zero. The fourth-power integrand is then comparable to \(r^{-4}\).
The smooth positive volume density in four dimensions gives a
logarithmically divergent radial integral \(\int_0^\epsilon dr/r\).
Matching the local index alone therefore does not ensure finite cost.
