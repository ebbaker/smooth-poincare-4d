## Statement and support

Let $(M^4,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian manifold
of volume one and let $f:M\to S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ be a supplied
smooth map of degree $+1$. The target has its unit round metric and
outward orientation. There is a smooth orientation-preserving
diffeomorphism $T:M\to M$, isotopic to the identity, such that
$F=f\circ T$ still has degree $+1$ and satisfies

\[
\int_M F\,dV_g=0,\qquad \int_M FF^{\mathsf T}\,dV_g=I/5 .
\tag{1}
\]

Nonzero degree makes $f$ surjective. Otherwise it factors through the
complement of a target point, with zero top-dimensional homology.
Consequently $\mu=f_*dV_g$ is a probability measure of full support.
For every nonempty open set $O\subset S^4$, its preimage is nonempty
and open, so it has positive Riemannian volume. This property permits
atoms if fibers have positive volume.

## Moment space and a strictly convex functional

Use $E=\mathbb R^5\oplus\operatorname{Sym}_0(5)$ with the Euclidean
and Frobenius pairings, where $\operatorname{Sym}_0(5)$ is the space
of real symmetric trace-free matrices. Its dimension is $5+15-1=19$.
Define

\[
\Phi(y)=(y,yy^{\mathsf T}-I/5).
\]

For $b=(a,A)\in E$ the corresponding linear functional on $\Phi$
is $\langle b,\Phi(y)\rangle=a\cdot y+y^{\mathsf T}Ay$.
If it is constant on $S^4$, evaluating at $y$ and $-y$ gives $a=0$.
A quadratic form constant on the unit sphere is a multiple of $I$,
and the trace-free condition gives $A=0$. This proves full affine
span and hence full linear span. The mean of $\Phi$ under the round
probability measure is zero by antipodal symmetry and rotation
invariance, using $\operatorname{tr}(yy^{\mathsf T})=1$.
Every nonzero functional therefore takes both positive and negative
values on $\Phi(S^4)$. By finite-dimensional separation, $0$ is in
the interior of the convex hull of $\Phi(S^4)$.

For the fixed $\mu$ put

\[
L(b)=\log Z(b),\qquad Z(b)=\int_{S^4}\exp\langle b,\Phi(y)\rangle\,d\mu(y).
\]

Since $\Phi$ is bounded on the compact target, differentiation under
the integral on bounded parameter sets gives

\[
dL_b(c)=\mathbb E_{\mu_b}\langle c,\Phi\rangle,\qquad
d^2L_b(c,c)=\operatorname{Var}_{\mu_b}(\langle c,\Phi\rangle),\qquad
d\mu_b=Z(b)^{-1}e^{\langle b,\Phi\rangle}\,d\mu .
\]

The tilted measure still has full support. A continuous function with
zero variance for this measure is constant on the target. The affine
span calculation then gives $c=0$, so $L$ is strictly convex.

For coercivity, $H(u)=\max_{y\in S^4}\langle u,\Phi(y)\rangle$ is
continuous and positive on the unit sphere of $E$. Let $c_0>0$ be
its minimum. For each unit $u$, choose a maximizing target point.
Joint continuity gives a neighborhood of $u$ in the parameter unit
sphere and a nonempty open target set $O_u$ on whose product the
pairing is at least $c_0/2$. Choose a finite cover of the parameter
sphere by such neighborhoods, and let $m_f>0$ be the least $\mu$
mass among their target sets. For every unit $u$ and $r\ge0$,

\[
L(ru)\ge r c_0/2+\log m_f .
\]

The lower bound is uniform in direction for this map. Coercivity
and continuity give a minimizer $b_*$, unique by strict convexity.
Its derivative vanishes. Thus the positive smooth domain density

\[
\rho(x)=Z(b_*)^{-1}e^{\langle b_*,\Phi(f(x))\rangle}
\]

obeys $\int_M\rho\,dV_g=1$ and
$\int_M\Phi(f(x))\rho(x)\,dV_g(x)=0$.

## Volume transport and orientation

The two positive smooth volume forms $dV_g$ and $\rho\,dV_g$
have equal total volume on the same closed connected manifold.
Moser's theorem supplies an orientation-preserving smooth
diffeomorphism $T$ with

\[
T^*(\rho\,dV_g)=dV_g,\qquad T_*dV_g=\rho\,dV_g .
\tag{2}
\]

In modern pullback notation, if the diffeomorphism is first chosen
with pullback of $dV_g$ equal to $\rho\,dV_g$, use its inverse as
$T$. Moser's §1 specifies the orientation-preserving transformation
and the theorem continuing onto p. 287 states that equal total
volume is sufficient. [Moser, §1, pp. 286–287][moser].

One can obtain (2) in this direction by taking
$\omega_t=(1-t)dV_g+t\rho\,dV_g$. The difference
$(\rho-1)dV_g$ is exact, since integration identifies the top
de Rham group of a closed connected oriented manifold with
$\mathbb R$. Choose a smooth $3$-form $\alpha$ with
$d\alpha=(\rho-1)dV_g$, and let $X_t$ be determined by
$\iota_{X_t}\omega_t=-\alpha$. Each $\omega_t$ is positive.
The smooth flow $T_t$ exists on $[0,1]$ by compactness, and
Cartan's formula gives
$\frac{d}{dt}T_t^*\omega_t=T_t^*(d\alpha+d\iota_{X_t}\omega_t)=0$.
Thus $T=T_1$, with $T_0=\mathrm{id}$, satisfies (2) and is isotopic
to the identity. Change of variables gives
$\int_M\Phi(f\circ T)\,dV_g=\int_M\Phi(f)\rho\,dV_g=0$,
which is precisely (1). Composition multiplies degrees and
$\deg T=+1$.

## Derivative and support bounds

For a fixed map the smooth positive density has a positive minimum,
and the diffeomorphism and its inverse have bounded derivatives on
compact $M$. The chain rule and (2), with Hilbert–Schmidt norm on
$df$ and operator norm on $dT$, give for $p\ge1$

\[
\int_M |d(f\circ T)|^p\,dV_g
\le \|dT\|_{L^\infty,\mathrm{op}}^p\int_M|df|^p\rho\,dV_g .
\tag{3}
\]

The constants are map-dependent. In particular, the determinant
of the inverse is $\det_g dT^{-1}(x)=\rho(x)$.
To see the effect of localization, fix $q\in S^4$ and write
$U=\{x:f(x)\ne q\}$, with $\varepsilon=\operatorname{Vol}_g(U)>0$.
For the normalized map let $\beta$ be the mass of the atom at $q$.
By (1), the scalar coordinate $X=q\cdot F$ has
$\mathbb E[X]=0$ and $\mathbb E[X^2]=1/5$. Its contribution on the
fiber over $q$ to the nonnegative square $(X+1/5)^2$ gives

\[
\beta(6/5)^2\le\mathbb E[(X+1/5)^2]=6/25,\qquad \beta\le1/6 .
\]

The set on which $F\ne q$ is $T^{-1}(U)$, which has volume
$\int_U\rho\,dV_g=1-\beta\ge5/6$. Consequently,

\[
\sup_M\rho\ge 5/(6\varepsilon),\qquad
\|dT^{-1}\|_{L^\infty,\mathrm{op}}\ge (5/(6\varepsilon))^{1/4}.
\]

The compactly supported radial collapse underlying [E16][e16]
allows this estimate for arbitrarily small $\varepsilon$.
For the exponential construction,
$\sup\rho\le e^{2\|\Phi\|_\infty |b_*|}$, so the required tilt
can also become unbounded as $\varepsilon$ decreases. Thus the
construction establishes feasibility on each supplied metric,
with no sequence-uniform transport or energy estimate from (3).
In particular, changing sampling density by a domain diffeomorphism
can change the potential $3|df|^2$ of the coupled scalar problem.

The support premise is satisfied for every map in the packet. A
loss of support could prevent this method in a different class.
For instance a pairing nonnegative on the supported image and
positive on some open subset of the support would give strictly
positive mean for every positive tilt. On the present class,
positive reweighting and diffeomorphism transport preserve full
target support.
