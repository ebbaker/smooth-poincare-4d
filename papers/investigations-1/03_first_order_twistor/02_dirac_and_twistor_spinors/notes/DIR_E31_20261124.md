## Unit harmonic scale

Let $(N^4,h)$ be a connected, smooth, complete, oriented Riemannian spin
manifold without boundary, with one end isometric to the exterior of a ball
in Euclidean $\mathbb R^4$. Use the ordinary Levi–Civita Dirac operator,
$X\cdot X=-|X|^2$ and $\Delta=d^*d$ on functions. Fix a smooth nowhere-zero
harmonic spinor $\Psi$ of one chirality with $\Psi=\Psi_\infty+O_2(R^{-3})$
in a parallel spin frame on the end, and $|\Psi_\infty|=1$.
Here $O_2(R^{-b})$ includes coordinate derivatives of order $j\le 2$
bounded by $O(R^{-b-j})$ in that frame or chart.

For a change from $g_0$ to $\widetilde g=e^{2u}g_0$, let $\beta$ be
the unitary conformal spin identification induced by sending each
oriented orthonormal frame to its $e^{-u}$ rescaling. It preserves
chirality. The four-dimensional formula is

\[
D_{\widetilde g}(e^{-3u/2}\beta\chi)
  =e^{-5u/2}\beta D_{g_0}\chi .
\tag{1}
\]

In Hitchin's notation the target line of the Dirac operator also changes
trivialization. In orthonormal metric frames this contributes $e^{-u}$
in addition to the input weight in (1).
[Hitchin, §1.4, Proposition 1.3, pp. 10–11][hitchin].

Set $\sigma=|\Psi|_h$. The unique positive conformal factor for which the
harmonic transport of this spinor has unit norm is

\[
k=\sigma^{4/3}h,\qquad
\xi=\sigma^{-1}\beta_{h,k}\Psi,\qquad D_k\xi=0,\qquad |\xi|_k=1 .
\]

Indeed the harmonic weight is $e^{-3u/2}$, so unit norm imposes
$e^u=\sigma^{2/3}$. Smoothness and absence of zeros on the compact
part, and $\sigma=1+O_2(R^{-3})$ on the end, bound $\sigma$ above and away
from zero. Thus $k$ is smooth and uniformly equivalent to the complete
$h$. In the original Euclidean coordinates $k_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O_2(R^{-3})$.
In the corresponding conformal spin frame
$\xi=\xi_\infty+O_2(R^{-3})$ with unit $\xi_\infty$.
The connection term from the conformal factor is $O(R^{-4})$,
so $\nabla^k\xi=O(R^{-4})$ and $V=|\nabla^k\xi|_k^2=O(R^{-8})$.

For an ordinary harmonic spinor $\chi$ the Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz
identity and the derivative of its norm give

\[
\tfrac12\Delta|\chi|^2=-|\nabla\chi|^2-\tfrac14s|\chi|^2.
\tag{2}
\]

Applied to $\xi$ this gives $s_k=-4V\le0$ pointwise. In particular the
scalar curvature is integrable. The ADM flux of $k$ vanishes since its
metric derivatives are $O(R^{-4})$ and the coordinate sphere area is
$O(R^3)$. The same conclusion holds for the eventually Euclidean $h$.
[Lee–Parker, Appendix equation (A.2)][lp-sl] ·
[Definition 8.2, equation (8.10)][lp-adm].

## Conformal Dirichlet energy

Let $v$ be smooth and positive with $v=1$ off a compact set. Denote
$g'=v^{-4}k$ and $\Psi'=v^3\beta_{k,g'}\xi$. The metric is complete and
agrees with $k$ on the end. By (1), $D_{g'}\Psi'=0$ and $|\Psi'|_{g'}=v^3$.
The connection formula with $u=-2\log v$, $A=\operatorname{grad}_k\log v$
and a $k$-orthonormal frame $(e_i)$ gives

\[
\nabla^{g'}_{v^2e_i}\Psi'
 =v^5\beta_{k,g'}
  \big(\nabla^k_{e_i}\xi+e_i\cdot_k A\cdot_k\xi+4k(e_i,A)\xi\big).
\tag{3}
\]

In fact the spin lift of the conformal frame connection adds
$-\frac12 X\cdot_k\operatorname{grad}_k u\cdot_k-\frac12X(u)$
for a fixed vector $X$. Differentiating the spinor weight contributes
$-\frac32X(u)$, which gives the displayed scalar coefficient in (3).
For $A_i=k(e_i,A)$ the cross term with $\nabla^k\xi$ vanishes when
summed in $i$. The $A_i\xi$ part vanishes since $|\xi|=1$, and the
$e_i\cdot_k A\cdot_k\xi$ part contracts against $-D_k\xi$ by
skew-adjointness. Clifford norm and
$\operatorname{Re}\langle e_i\cdot_k A\cdot_k\xi,\xi\rangle=-A_i$
give
$\sum_i|e_i\cdot_k A\cdot_k\xi+4A_i\xi|^2
=(4+16-8)|A|_k^2=12|A|_k^2$.
Since $dV_{g'}=v^{-8}dV_k$, the resulting density and total energy are

\[
|\nabla^{g'}\Psi'|^2_{g'}\,dV_{g'}
    =(v^2V+12|dv|_k^2)\,dV_k,\qquad
E(v)=\int_N(v^2V+12|dv|_k^2)\,dV_k .
\tag{4}
\]

The integrals converge by the decay of $V$ and compact support of $dv$.
Taking $v=\sigma^{1/3}$ also recovers $(h,\Psi)$, using a zero-mass
$O_2(R^{-3})$ variation.
[Hitchin, §1.4, connection comparison and Clifford trace][hitchin].

## Scalar-curvature and boundary check

Write $g'=w^2k$ with $w=v^{-2}$. The scalar transform is
$s_{g'}=w^{-3}(6\Delta_k w+s_k w)$. With
$\Delta_k(v^{-2})=-2v^{-3}\Delta_k v-6v^{-4}|dv|_k^2$, this gives

\[
s_{g'}|\Psi'|^2_{g'}\,dV_{g'}
   =(s_kv^2-12v\Delta_k v-36|dv|_k^2)\,dV_k .
\tag{5}
\]

Let $N_R$ be the compact truncation with boundary the coordinate sphere
$S_R$, and let $\nu_k$ point outward. Put
$B_R(v)=\int_{S_R}v\partial_{\nu_k}v\,dS_k$. The convention
$\Delta_k=d^*d$ gives
$\int_{N_R}v\Delta_k v\,dV_k=\int_{N_R}|dv|_k^2\,dV_k-B_R(v)$.
Hence (5) and $s_k=-4V$ give

\[
-\tfrac14\int_{N_R}s_{g'}|\Psi'|^2_{g'}\,dV_{g'}
   =\int_{N_R}(v^2V+12|dv|_k^2)\,dV_k-3B_R(v).
\]

Integrating (2) also gives

\[
\int_{N_R}\big(|\nabla^{g'}\Psi'|^2_{g'}
             +\tfrac14s_{g'}|\Psi'|^2_{g'}\big)\,dV_{g'}
=\tfrac12\int_{S_R}\partial_{\nu_{g'}}(v^6)\,dS_{g'}
=3B_R(v).
\tag{6}
\]

The last equality uses $\nu_{g'}=v^2\nu_k$ and $dS_{g'}=v^{-6}dS_k$.
The two boundary contributions cancel already on $N_R$. Each
vanishes for all large $R$ when $v=1$ on the end.
[Lee–Parker, equation (1.1)][lp-scal] ·
[Appendix equation (A.2)][lp-sl].

## An end with an inverse-square term

For the comparison, allow $v=1+aR^{-2}+O_2(R^{-2-\epsilon})$ with
constant $a$ and $\epsilon>0$. This keeps smooth completeness.
The leading inverse-square term is Euclidean harmonic on the end.
The scalar formula then gives
$s_{g'}=O(R^{-4-\min(\epsilon,2)})$, so the weighted scalar integral
converges absolutely. The Dirichlet density in (4) is also integrable.
With $\omega_3=\operatorname{Vol}(S^3)=2\pi^2$,
$B_R(v)\to-2a\omega_3$. The scalar integration-by-parts term in
$-\frac14\int s_{g'}|\Psi'|^2$ tends to $+6a\omega_3$ and the
spinor term in (6) to $-6a\omega_3$.

Use the normalized ADM convention

\[
m_{\rm ADM}(\gamma)=
\frac1{6\omega_3}\lim_{R\to\infty}
\int_{S_R}(\partial_j\gamma_{ij}-\partial_i\gamma_{jj})
          \nu_\delta^i\,dS_\delta .
\tag{7}
\]

For $\gamma=g'$, the leading term of $\gamma_{ij}-\delta_{ij}$
is $-4aR^{-2}\delta_{ij}$ and the remainder is
$O_2(R^{-2-\min(\epsilon,1)})$. The leading radial flux density
is $-3\partial_R(1-4aR^{-2})=-24aR^{-3}$. Thus
$m_{\rm ADM}(g')=-4a$ and the limit in (6) is
$\frac32\omega_3m_{\rm ADM}(g')$. Lee–Parker's equation (8.10)
uses $m_{\mathrm{LP}}=6m_{\rm ADM}$ in dimension four, so the
coefficient agrees with $\omega_3m_{\mathrm{LP}}/4$ in their
appendix boundary formula. The identity here follows directly
from the supplied harmonic field and its decay.
[Lee–Parker, Definition 8.2 and equation (8.10)][lp-adm] ·
[Appendix, concluding identity][lp-witten].

## Critical relative cost and the E10 scale

Use [E07's relative twistor functional][e07]
$J_\gamma(\chi)^4=\int |P_\gamma\chi|^4/|\chi|^4\,dV_\gamma$
on nowhere-zero fields. Since $P_\gamma\chi=\nabla^\gamma\chi$
for a harmonic field, (4) gives the exact relation

\[
J_{g'}(\Psi')^4
 =\int_N\big(V+12|d\log v|_k^2\big)^2\,dV_k,\qquad
J_k(\xi)^4=\int_N V^2\,dV_k .
\tag{8}
\]

For $(h,\Psi)$ this uses $v=\sigma^{1/3}$ and adds
$\tfrac43|d\log\sigma|_k^2$ inside the square. These critical
costs are finite by smooth zero-free data and decay, including
for the inverse-square comparison. Within this supplied harmonic
transport, (8) gives $J_{g'}(\Psi')\ge J_k(\xi)$, with equality
only for $v\equiv1$ when $v\to1$. This comparison concerns $J$.
Equation (4) is the distinct quadratic energy for B16.

The [E10 twistor-weight normalization][e10] applied to $(h,\Psi)$
instead gives $q=\sigma^{-4}h$ and
$\phi=\sigma^{-1}\beta_{h,q}\Psi$. This uses $e^{u/2}$ for a metric
factor $e^{2u}$, the twistor rather than the harmonic weight. The
same construction on compact data matches this $q$ and $\phi$
whenever $(h,\Psi)$ is their twistor-weight transport. In terms
of $k$ the two unit sections are related by
$q=(\sigma^{4/3})^{-4}k$ and $\phi=\beta_{k,q}\xi$. Harmonic
transport to $q$ gives $\sigma^4\phi$, so
$D_q\phi=-4\,d\log\sigma\cdot_q\phi$, where a one-form acts by
its $q$-dual vector. Thus the unit section in E10's scale is
harmonic exactly when $\sigma$ is constant. E07 covariance
gives $J_q(\phi)=J_h(\Psi)$, and E10's pointwise comparison
in the unit frame gives
$J_h(\Psi)\le\|\nabla^q\phi\|_{L^4(q)}\le2J_h(\Psi)$.
[Friedrich, equation (1.1) and conformal paragraph, pp. 59–60][friedrich].
