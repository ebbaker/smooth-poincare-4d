## Setting

Fix a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\) and a
smooth positive-definite metric \(g\) of volume one. Use
\(\Delta_g=d^*d\) on functions and \(\lambda=\lambda_1(g)>0\). For the
energy result \(F:M\to(S^4,h)\) is a supplied smooth map to the unit
round target with \(\Delta_gF=\lambda F\) in ambient coordinates. Thus
\(\int_M F\,dV_g=0\) and \(|dF|^2=\lambda\), by integration and the
product rule for \(\Delta_g|F|^2\). The bundle and curvature calculations
apply to any smooth \(F\). The first-eigenmap result uses only the fixed
metric and eigenmap hypotheses, including when \(s_g=3\lambda\).

Use \(\mathbb H^2\) as a right quaternionic vector space with Hermitian
product \(v^*w\). The group \(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) acts on unit columns by
right multiplication. Denote the Hopf projection by \(\mathcal H\).
The real coordinates \(\mathcal H(z)=(q,t)\in\mathbb H\oplus\mathbb R\)
and the projector \(P\) are
\[
 q=2z_1\overline z_2,\qquad t=|z_1|^2-|z_2|^2,\qquad
 P=zz^*=\tfrac12(I+T(q,t)),\qquad
 T(q,t)=\begin{pmatrix}t&q\\\overline q&-t\end{pmatrix}.
\]
The image has \(|q|^2+t^2=1\). Conversely, \(T(q,t)^2=I\) there and
\((I+T)/2\) is the quaternionic rank-one orthogonal projector. Thus
these coordinates identify \(\mathbb{HP}^1\) with the unit sphere.
Orient the target outward in the ordered coordinates
\((q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3,t)\) with quaternion basis \((1,i,j,k)\).
Use \(\langle X,Y\rangle=-\operatorname{Re}(XY)\) for imaginary
quaternions, and take exterior products of distinct orthonormal
covectors to have unit norm.

## Projection and two-dimensional dilation

Write \(L\) for the tautological quaternionic line and use its projected
connection \(\nabla=P\,d\) from the trivial ambient bundle. For a local
unit frame \(z\) of \(F^*L\) let \(a=z^*dz\) and \(b=(I-P)dz\). Then
\(a\) is imaginary by differentiating \(z^*z=1\), and \(dz=za+b\)
is an orthogonal splitting. Acting on \(z\), the derivative \(dP(X)\)
gives \(b(X)\). Since \(T\) is linear and
\(T(v)^2=|v|^2 I\) for \(v\in\mathbb R^5\), one has
\[
 |b(X)|^2=\tfrac14|dF(X)|^2,\qquad
 |dz|^2=\tfrac14|dF|^2+|a|^2 .
\]
Thus the differential of \(\mathcal H\) doubles horizontal lengths
and multiplies horizontal two-dimensional areas by four.

The covariant derivative of \(z\xi\) is \(z(d\xi+a\xi)\). Its curvature
is \(F_a=da+a\wedge a\), where the wedge uses quaternionic multiplication.
Directly, \(da=dz^*\wedge dz\) and the vertical term of this wedge
is \(-a\wedge a\), so \(F_a=b^*\wedge b\). At a point choose a unit
column in the quaternionic complement and identify \(b(X),b(Y)\) with
quaternions \(u,v\) in that frame. Then
\[
 F_a(X,Y)=\overline u v-\overline v u
         =2\operatorname{Im}(\overline u v),\qquad
 |F_a(X,Y)|=\tfrac12|dF(X)\wedge dF(Y)|_h .
\]
The norm identity follows from
\(|\operatorname{Im}(\overline u v)|^2=|u|^2|v|^2-
(\operatorname{Re}(\overline u v))^2\) and the factor four for areas.
For the four singular values of \(dF\), including zeros, this gives
\[
 |F_a|^2=\tfrac14\sum_{r<s}\sigma_r^2\sigma_s^2
          \leq \tfrac{3}{32}|dF|^4 .
\]
Indeed, for \(x_r=\sigma_r^2\),
\(2\sum_{r<s}x_rx_s=(\sum x_r)^2-\sum x_r^2
\leq\tfrac34(\sum x_r)^2\). Equality holds pointwise precisely when
all four squared singular values coincide. For a degree-zero
first eigenmap the integrated inequality is strict. Equality in
the integral would force equality everywhere by continuity, giving
rank four everywhere and a Jacobian of one nonzero sign on the
connected domain.

## Unit-sphere calibration and the lift obstruction

For the identity of the unit target, at the north pole take
\(z=(1,0)\) and the oriented orthonormal tangent basis
\((1,i,j,k)\) in the \(q\) coordinates. If \(X\) is a target tangent
quaternion, its horizontal lift is \((0,\overline X/2)\). The curvature
\(\Omega\) of \(L\) therefore has value
\(\tfrac12\operatorname{Im}(X\overline Y)\) on \(X,Y\). In the dual
coframe \(e^0,\ldots,e^3\) this is
\[
 \Omega=-\tfrac12\left[
 i(e^{01}+e^{23})+j(e^{02}-e^{13})+k(e^{03}+e^{12})\right] ,
 \qquad e^{rs}=e^r\wedge e^s .
\]
It is self-dual in the specified target orientation. The left action
of \(\mathrm{Sp}(2)\) on \(\mathbb H^2\) preserves \(P\,d\) and acts
transitively on the lines. Its conjugation of \(T\) preserves the real
Euclidean norm, as seen from \(T^2\). The action on the target sphere
preserves orientation because \(\mathrm{Sp}(2)\) is connected, as
follows from its transitive action on \(S^7\) with connected stabilizer
\(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\). Hence
\(\Omega\) is self-dual globally and \(|\Omega|^2=3/2\), with
\(\int_{S^4}|\Omega|^2\,dV_h=4\pi^2\).

View the line as complex rank two by right multiplication by \(i\).
Then left multiplication is the defining complex representation of
\(\mathrm{Sp}(1)=SU(2)\) and
\(\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C}(XY)=2\operatorname{Re}(XY)\).
The degree-four term of \(\det(I+i\Omega/(2\pi))\) is
\((8\pi^2)^{-1}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)\),
since the trace of \(\Omega\) vanishes. The convention is curvature
\(\nabla^2\) and the trace here is the complex matrix trace.
[Ballmann, Appendix A, (A.33)–(A.35) and Remark A.37, pp. 130–131][ballmann].
By self-duality and the trace pairing,
\[
 \operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb C}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)
  =-2|\Omega|^2\,dV_h=-3\,dV_h,\qquad
 \int_{S^4} c_2(L,\nabla)=-1 .
\]
Here \(\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)=8\pi^2/3\). For any \(F\) the
trace four-form is \(-3F^*dV_h\) by pullback. Reversing the target
orientation exchanges the Hodge chiralities and changes the sign of
the Chern number on the target. In the present orientations
\(\langle c_2(F^*L),[M]\rangle=-\deg F\) by naturality.

The unit sphere bundle \(S(F^*L)\) is the pulled-back principal
\(\mathrm{Sp}(1)\)-bundle. A compact smooth four-manifold has a
four-dimensional CW structure. Because its fiber \(S^3\) is
two-connected, a section extends through the three-skeleton. The
remaining obstruction lies in \(H^4(M;\mathbb Z)\), with constant
coefficients since the transition action preserves fiber orientation.
It is the Euler class up to sign, which is the top Chern class of the
underlying complex rank-two bundle with its complex orientation.
There are no higher cells. The obstruction vanishes exactly when
the Chern number vanishes, since \(H^4(M;\mathbb Z)\cong\mathbb Z\)
is detected by \([M]\). [Hatcher, §§3.2–3.3, Props. 3.13, 3.19 and
3.22, and the primary obstruction construction][hatcher].
A continuous unit section can be approximated uniformly by smooth
ambient columns, projected to the smooth line and normalized. This
gives a smooth lift \(z:M\to S^7\), and \(z\) trivializes the principal
bundle by right multiplication. Thus the bundle is trivial exactly
when \(\deg F=0\).

## Every lift and the equality case

Use \(P=(I+T(F))/2\) on \(M\). Integration gives
\(\int_M P\,dV_g=I/2\). For a smooth lift of a degree-zero eigenmap,
write \(m=\int_M z\,dV_g\). Each constant unit \(v\in\mathbb H^2\)
satisfies
\[
 \int_M |v^*z-v^*m|^2\,dV_g=\tfrac12-|v^*m|^2.
\]
For \(m\ne0\), take \(v=m/|m|\), so \(|m|^2\leq 1/2\).
The scalar Poincaré inequality applied to the eight real coordinates
of \(z\), combined with the derivative splitting, gives
\[
 \int_M |a|^2\,dV_g\ \geq\ \lambda(3/4-|m|^2)\
 \geq\ \lambda/4 .
\]
Equality in the first step alone is equivalent to
\(\Delta_g z=\lambda(z-m)\) componentwise. Equality in the final
bound requires the mean equality too. That gives
\(v^*z=v^*m=1/\sqrt2\) everywhere. The identity
\[
 2v^*Pv-1=\mathcal H(v)\cdot F
\]
follows by multiplying the two projector matrices and taking the real
part of the quaternionic trace, since
\(\operatorname{Re}\operatorname{tr}_{\mathbb H}(vv^*zz^*)=|v^*z|^2\).
Therefore the image lies in the great \(S^3\) perpendicular to
\(\mathcal H(v)\), and \(z=\sqrt2Pv\).

Conversely, given an eigenmap with image in such a great \(S^3\),
choose a constant unit \(v\) whose Hopf image is a unit normal.
The lift \(z=\sqrt2 Pv\) has unit length and mean \(v/\sqrt2\).
Its coordinates are affine-linear in \(F\), giving Poincaré equality.
Moreover \(|dz(X)|^2=|dF(X)|^2/2\) by the square identity for \(T\), so
this lift has \(|a|^2=\lambda/4\) pointwise. This shows sharpness
conditional on a supplied eigenmap into the great subsphere.

For a fixed full-span \(F\), define
\[
 B(F)=\max_{|v|=1}\int_M |P(x)v|\,dV_g(x).
\]
The maximum exists by continuity and compactness. Since
\(\int |Pv|^2=1/2\), Cauchy–Schwarz bounds it by \(1/\sqrt2\).
Equality at a maximizing \(v\) requires the norm to be constant,
which implies \(\mathcal H(v)\cdot F\equiv0\) by the projector identity.
Thus \(B(F)<1/\sqrt2\) for a full-span map. For every lift,
\(|m|=\max_{|v|=1}|v^*m|\leq B(F)\), as \(|v^*z|=|Pv|\). It follows
that the strict lower bound
\(\int|a|^2\geq\lambda(3/4-B(F)^2)>\lambda/4\) holds uniformly over
the lifts of this fixed \(F\). The estimate supplies no gap independent
of \(F\) over the full-span class.

## Gauge and the remaining input

Under \(z\mapsto zu\) with \(u:M\to\mathrm{Sp}(1)\) smooth,
\[
 a\mapsto u^{-1}au+u^{-1}du,\qquad
 F_a\mapsto u^{-1}F_a u .
\]
The curvature norm is gauge invariant. The one-form equation one
could apply in a co-closed gauge would be
\(da=F_a-a\wedge a\). Thus the scalar Hodge comparison for coexact
one-forms would control \(a\) by \(da\), whose right side also contains
a quadratic function of \(a\). In the circle case both the quadratic
term and the conjugation are absent. The quaternionic identities
give no corresponding linear inversion from curvature to the
connection. A bound
\(\inf_u\int_M|u^{-1}au+u^{-1}du|^2\,dV_g\leq\lambda/4\)
would rule out the fixed full-span eigenmap by the strict lower
bound. Establishing any such curvature-to-connection estimate is
the new premise returned to [B13][b13].
