## Supplied geometry and Green expansion

Let $(M^4,g)$ be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented Riemannian spin
manifold with invertible full Dirac operator $D_g$ and an oriented exactly
flat ball about $p$. Use the shared positive half-spin bundle acted on by
the self-dual two-forms, $X\cdot X=-|X|^2$, and skew-Hermitian Clifford
action by real tangent vectors. Choose an oriented Euclidean isometry
$x$ on the ball with $x(p)=0$ and a spin lift of its parallel frame.
Extend $0\ne\eta\in S^-_{g,p}$ constantly in this frame. Put $r=|x|$ and
$c=1/|S^3|=1/(2\pi^2)$. Normalize the point source so that testing it against
a smooth spinor pairs $\eta$ with the test spinor's value at $p$.

In the flat chart, $D_g=\sum_i e_i\cdot\partial_i$. The locally integrable
spinor $E(x)=-c\,x\cdot\eta/r^4$ is harmonic off the origin. Its sign and
coefficient follow by writing $E=D_g[(c/(2r^2))\eta]$ in the chart, with
the scalar function understood distributionally. The scalar Laplacian
$D_g^2=-\sum_i\partial_i^2$ applied to $c/(2r^2)$ gives $\delta_0$, since
the outward flux of its negative gradient on a radius $r$ sphere is
$c r^{-3}|S^3|r^3=1$. Therefore $D_g E=\delta_p\eta$ locally.

Cut off $E$ by a smooth $\chi$ supported in the flat chart and equal to
one near $p$. Then $D_g(\chi E)=\delta_p\eta+f$ with smooth $f$ supported
where $d\chi$ is nonzero. Invertibility and elliptic regularity give a
smooth $D_g^{-1}f$. Thus the unique compact distributional Green solution is

\[
G_\eta=\chi E-D_g^{-1}f,
\qquad
G_\eta(x)=-c\,\frac{x\cdot\eta}{r^4}+\gamma(x),
\qquad D_g\gamma=0\quad\text{near }p,
\tag{1}
\]

where $\gamma$ is smooth across $p$. The full Dirac operator anticommutes
with chirality, and hence its inverse also anticommutes. The negative
source produces $G_\eta\in S_g^+$ off $p$, consistent with the odd Clifford
factor of its pole. The regular term in (1) is likewise positive chiral.

Ammann–Humbert–Morel use the point-source convention in Definition 2.1.
Remark 2.2 and Proposition 2.3 give the negative pole in (1), with their
regular term scaled by $|S^3|$ relative to $\gamma$. The v2 proof of
Proposition 2.3 sets $D\zeta=-\Psi$ for the smooth residual $\Psi$ and then
subtracts $\zeta$. With that definition the residual cancels upon adding
$\zeta$. The formula $\chi E-D_g^{-1}f$ above directly verifies the sign. The
cutoff argument uses only flatness on the chosen ball and full compact
invertibility. [Ammann–Humbert–Morel, §§2.1–2.2, pp. 3–4][ahm].

## Exact Euclidean end and its spin frame

On $M^\circ=M\setminus\{p\}$ fix smooth $\omega>0$ equal to $r^{-2}$ near
$p$ and set $h=\omega^2g$. Let $\beta:S_g\to S_h$ be the Hermitian isometry
induced by $e_i\mapsto\omega^{-1}e_i$ on paired oriented orthonormal frames.
It preserves chirality and satisfies
$X\cdot_h\beta\xi=\omega\beta(X\cdot_g\xi)$. The Dirac conformal law is

\[
D_h(\omega^{-3/2}\beta\xi)=\omega^{-5/2}\beta D_g\xi.
\tag{2}
\]

In these paired frames, the conformal spin connection differs by
$-\tfrac12 X\cdot_g U\cdot_g-\tfrac12 X(\log\omega)$, where
$U=\operatorname{grad}_g\log\omega$. Its Clifford trace in the
$h$-orthonormal frame gives
$D_h(\beta\xi)=\omega^{-1}\beta(D_g\xi+\tfrac32 U\cdot_g\xi)$.
Differentiating the weight cancels this extra term, giving (2).
[Ammann–Humbert–Morel, §2.1, p. 3][ahm] · [Paired-frame calculation in E07][e07].
Consequently

\[
\Psi=\omega^{-3/2}\beta G_\eta\in\Gamma(S_h^+),\qquad D_h\Psi=0
\quad\text{on }M^\circ.
\tag{3}
\]

For a fixed unit vector $a$ in the Euclidean model, write
$S_a v=v-2\langle a,v\rangle a$ and set

\[
y=S_a x/r^2,\qquad R=|y|=r^{-1},\qquad u=x/r.
\]

The derivative is $D_xy=r^{-2}S_aS_u$. It is orientation-preserving because
it is the product of the inversion reflection and the fixed reflection
$S_a$. It takes the $h$-orthonormal $x$-frame $r^2e_i$ to $S_aS_u e_i$ in
the Euclidean $y$-frame, so $h$ is exactly Euclidean in this exterior chart.
The compact complement and the complete Euclidean exterior give a complete
metric on $M^\circ$.

With the negative Clifford square, $S_v(w)=v\cdot w\cdot v$ for any unit
vector $v$. The even element $q(u)=a\cdot u\cdot\in\operatorname{Spin}(4)$
projects to $S_a S_u$, since conjugation by $q(u)$ gives that composition.
The product is unitary and preserves both half-spin spaces. Choose the
sign of the parallel $y$ spin frame so the conformally paired $x$ frame
is the $y$ frame multiplied on the right by $q(u)$. In the associated
spinor coordinates the change from the paired $x$-frame to the $y$-frame
therefore multiplies components by $q(u)$. Then (1) and (3) give

\[
\Psi_y(y)=q(u)\bigl(-c\,u\cdot\eta+r^3\gamma(x)\bigr)
=c\,a\cdot\eta+R^{-3}q(u)\gamma\bigl(S_a y/R^2\bigr).
\tag{4}
\]

The limit $\Psi_\infty=c\,a\cdot\eta$ is a positive parallel spinor in the
chosen exterior frame and $|\Psi_\infty|=c|\eta|_{g,p}$. In that frame,
the odd factor $a\cdot$ converts the negative compact source fiber to
the positive end fiber. The actual frame transition is even and preserves
positive chirality. Multiplying the chosen lift by $-1$ changes both
end components and the written limit by the same sign. As $\eta$ varies,
$\eta\mapsto c\,a\cdot\eta$ gives a complex-linear isomorphism to the
positive parallel end data.

Equation (4) yields $\Psi_y-\Psi_\infty=O(R^{-3})$. In the exterior frame
$u=S_a y/R$, so $\partial_y q(u)=O(R^{-1})$, and
$\partial_y x=O(R^{-2})$. Smoothness of $\gamma$ yields
$\partial_y\Psi_y=O(R^{-4})$. The coordinate derivative here is also the
covariant derivative in the constant $y$ spin frame. The boundary
amplitude in (4) is fixed by the pole. The regular Green coefficient
$\gamma(0)$ contributes at order $R^{-3}$. The ADM surface integral of
$h$ vanishes on the exact Euclidean exterior.

## Compact removal and the compact twistor weight

Suppose two smooth $D_h$-harmonic spinors have this same end constant
and have $O(R^{-3})$ differences from it. For their difference $V$, use
the inverse Dirac weight $v=\omega^{3/2}\beta^{-1}V$, which is compact
$D_g$-harmonic off $p$ by (2). Since $\beta$ is unitary, $v$ is bounded
near $p$. Test the equation against cutoff spinors vanishing in a small
ball of radius $\epsilon$, with cutoff derivative bounded by
$C/\epsilon$ on an annulus of volume $O(\epsilon^4)$. The contribution
from that derivative is $O(\epsilon^3)$ and tends to zero. The bounded
extension is therefore $D_g$-harmonic in distributions across $p$ and
smooth by elliptic regularity. Compact invertibility gives $v=0$, hence
$V=0$. This proves uniqueness in the stated asymptotic class.

For transport of a field with the twistor weight the same isometry uses
$\psi\mapsto\omega^{1/2}\beta\psi$ from $g$ to $h$. This is the weight
checked in [E07][e07] and [E10][e10]. The corresponding compact
field for (3) is

\[
\psi=\omega^{-1/2}\beta^{-1}\Psi=\omega^{-2}G_\eta
=-c\,x\cdot\eta+r^4\gamma(x)\quad\text{near }p.
\tag{5}
\]

The scalar factor $r^4=(\sum_i x_i^2)^2$ is smooth across the origin.
Thus (5) defines a smooth compact positive half-spinor with exact jet

\[
\psi(p)=0,\qquad (\nabla^g_X\psi)(p)=-c\,X\cdot_g\eta,
\qquad D_g\psi(p)=4c\,\eta.
\]

The smooth error vanishes through third order. The norm of the linear
term equals $c|\eta|r$ by the Clifford relation, so $p$ is a simple zero
and is the only zero in a sufficiently small compact ball. The linear
term is a flat twistor spinor, since $D_g(x\cdot\eta)=-4\eta$ in the flat
frame. In particular the error gives $P_g\psi=O(r^3)$ near $p$.
These calculations use the twistor weight to transport the ordinary
harmonic field and establish its local jet. Any other zeros of the compact field
correspond to zeros of $\Psi$ on $M^\circ$, and they can occur only in
a compact subset away from the end. Their exclusion or local structure
requires further information.
