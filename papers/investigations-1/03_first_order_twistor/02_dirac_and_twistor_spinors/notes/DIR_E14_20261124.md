## Conditional statement

Let \(M\) be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented spin homotopy
four-sphere and let \(g\) be a supplied smooth positive-definite
unit-volume Yamabe-minimizing metric of constant scalar curvature \(s\).
Use \(\Delta_g=d^*d\ge0\) and let \(h\) be the metric of the oriented
unit round \(S^4\subset\mathbb R^5\). Write \(\lambda_1>0\) for the first
positive eigenvalue of \(\Delta_g\). For smooth \(f:M\to S^4\), set

\[
Y_f([g])=\inf_{0\ne u\in H^1(M)}
\frac{\int_M(6|du|_g^2+(s-3|df|_{g,h}^2)u^2)\,dV_g}
{\left(\int_M|u|^4\,dV_g\right)^{1/2}},
\qquad f^*dV_h=J_f\,dV_g.
\]

Suppose smooth maps \(f_j:M\to S^4\) have degree \(+1\), satisfy
\(\int_M f_j\,dV_g=0\) in \(\mathbb R^5\) and obey \(Y_{f_j}([g])\to0\).
Suppose also that their absolute Jacobian densities \(|J_{f_j}|\) are
uniformly integrable. Then \(s=3\lambda_1>0\). A subsequence converges
strongly in \(W^{1,2}(M;\mathbb R^5)\) to a smooth sphere-valued map \(F\)
with \(\deg F=1\) and \(|dF|_{g,h}^2=\lambda_1\) everywhere. In fact \(F\)
is an orientation-preserving homothetic diffeomorphism, so \(g\) is a
constant multiple of \(F^*h\) and \(M\) has standard smooth type.

## The Yamabe and Poincaré inequalities

Use the ordinary Yamabe quotient \(Y_0(g;u)\) with zero potential.
Because \(g\) minimizes in its conformal class and has volume one,
\(u=1\) minimizes \(Y_0(g;u)\) among smooth positive \(u\), with value \(s\).
For real smooth \(v\) of zero mean, take \(u_t=1+tv>0\) for sufficiently
small \(|t|\).
The numerator equals \(s+t^2(6\|dv\|_2^2+s\|v\|_2^2)\), and the
denominator is \(1+3t^2\|v\|_2^2+O(t^3)\). Hence

\[
Y_0(g;1+tv)=s+t^2\bigl(6\|dv\|_2^2-2s\|v\|_2^2\bigr)+O(t^3).
\]

The quadratic coefficient is nonnegative. Applying it to a first
eigenfunction gives \(s\le3\lambda_1\), for any sign of \(s\).
For a balanced smooth \(f\), the five component functions have zero
mean. Applying Poincaré to them and using \(|f|^2=1\) and volume one
gives \(\int_M|df|^2\,dV_g\ge\lambda_1\). Testing the modified quotient
with \(u=1\) now yields

\[
 Y_f([g])\le s-3\int_M|df|^2\,dV_g\le s-3\lambda_1\le0.
\]

The sequence tending to zero forces equality in the last inequality.
Connectedness gives \(\lambda_1>0\), so \(s=3\lambda_1>0\). The same chain gives

\[
0\le \int_M|df_j|^2\,dV_g-\lambda_1
    \le -Y_{f_j}([g])/3\longrightarrow 0.
\]

## Spectral convergence and the limit

Let \(\Pi\) be the \(L^2\)-orthogonal projection onto the first positive
eigenspace of \(\Delta_g\), applied coordinatewise. Let \(\lambda_*>\lambda_1\)
be the next distinct eigenvalue. Smoothness and compactness of the fixed
metric give the discrete spectrum and finite-dimensional eigenspaces.
There is no constant component because of balance. For eigenvalues
\(\lambda\ge\lambda_*\) one has
\(1+\lambda\le(1+\lambda_*)(\lambda-\lambda_1)/(\lambda_* -\lambda_1)\).
Expansion in the orthonormal eigenbasis therefore gives

\[
\|(1-\Pi)f_j\|_{W^{1,2}(M;\mathbb R^5)}^2
\le\frac{1+\lambda_*}{\lambda_* -\lambda_1}
\left(\int_M |df_j|^2\,dV_g-\lambda_1\|f_j\|_2^2\right)
\longrightarrow0.
\]

The projected maps are bounded in a finite-dimensional space. A
subsequence converges there to a smooth vector of eigenfunctions \(F\),
and the preceding estimate gives strong \(W^{1,2}\) convergence along
the same subsequence. Since \(|f_j|=1\), strong \(L^2\) convergence gives
\(|F|=1\) almost everywhere and hence everywhere by continuity. In
particular \(F\) is a smooth map into the unit sphere. Componentwise
\(\Delta_g F=\lambda_1 F\), and the product identity for the nonnegative
Laplacian gives

\[
 0=\tfrac12\Delta_g|F|^2=\langle F,\Delta_g F\rangle-|dF|^2
   =\lambda_1-|dF|^2.
\]

This is the pointwise scalar comparison \(s=3|dF|^2\) and also gives
\(Y_F([g])=0\) by the constant test and the nonnegative gradient term.

## Jacobians and smooth recognition

Uniform integrability here means \(\sup_j\int_M |J_{f_j}|\,dV_g<\infty\)
and, with \(A\) ranging over measurable sets,

\[
\lim_{\eta\downarrow0}
\sup_j\sup_{\operatorname{Vol}_g(A)\le\eta}
       \int_A|J_{f_j}|\,dV_g=0.
\]

In a local positively oriented \(g\)-orthonormal frame \((e_i)_{i=1}^4\),
the signed density is \(J_f=\det(f,df(e_1),\ldots,df(e_4))\) in ambient
\(\mathbb R^5\), with orientation chosen to match the sphere volume form.
Strong \(W^{1,2}\) convergence gives convergence in measure of the
components and their first derivatives in each frame. The continuous
polynomial determinant gives \(J_{f_j}\to J_F\) in measure on \(M\).
Equivalently, every further subsequence has an almost-everywhere
convergent subsubsequence for the components and derivatives, hence
for the determinant. A finite local cover gives the assertion globally.

Vitali convergence now gives \(\|J_{f_j}-J_F\|_{L^1(g)}\to0\). To see
the role of the absolute densities, bound the integral of the difference
on the set where it is at most a fixed small threshold by that threshold
times the total volume. The measure of the other set tends to zero.
Uniform integrability bounds the contribution of \(|J_{f_j}|\) there,
and absolute continuity of the integral bounds that of the smooth
\(|J_F|\). Integrating the signed densities therefore gives

\[
\deg(F)\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)=\int_M J_F\,dV_g
=\lim_j\int_M J_{f_j}\,dV_g=\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4).
\]

The smooth \(F\) has degree \(+1\). If \(\sigma_i\) are the four singular
values of \(dF\), Listing's Theorem 3 in dimension four requires
\(s\ge\sqrt{24\sum_{i<k}\sigma_i^2\sigma_k^2}\). For \(a_i=\sigma_i^2\),
\(\sum_{i<k}a_i a_k=((\sum_i a_i)^2-\sum_i a_i^2)/2
\le 3(\sum_i a_i)^2/8\). Hence \(s=3\sum_i\sigma_i^2\) implies the
required bound everywhere. The theorem applies to the closed spin
domain and smooth nonzero-degree map to the unit sphere of the same
even dimension, with conclusion a homothety. The degree fixes the
orientation and the diffeomorphism has \(F^*h=(\lambda_1/4)g\).
[Listing, Theorem 3, eq. (6), PDF p. 4][listing] · [DIR-E05 comparison][e05].

## Sufficient derivative control and the two models

The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives
\(|J_f|=\prod_i\sigma_i\le |df|^4/16\). Thus a uniform
\(L^{4+\delta}(g)\) bound on \(df_j\) for any \(\delta>0\) yields
a uniform \(L^{1+\delta/4}(g)\) bound on the absolute Jacobians. In
particular, Hölder bounds their integrals on \(A\) by a constant times
\(\operatorname{Vol}_g(A)^{\delta/(4+\delta)}\). An \(L^4(g)\) bound on
\(df_j\) yields a uniform \(L^1(g)\) bound on the absolute Jacobians.
The bubble below shows the missing uniform small-set condition.

**The E09 bubble.** In the [E09 family][e09], \(f_\epsilon\) equals the
south pole \(q\) off a ball \(B_{2\rho}\) with \(\rho=\epsilon^{1/2}\).
The uniform \(L^2\) bound on \(3|df_\epsilon|^2\) there is a uniform
\(L^4\) derivative bound. The positive Jacobian mass in \(B_\rho\)
tends to \(\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)\) as its domain volume tends to
zero, which violates uniform integrability. The maps in fact converge
strongly in \(W^{1,2}\) to \(q\) since their variations are supported
on sets of vanishing volume and \(\|df_\epsilon\|_4\) is bounded.
This example concerns the general critical derivative bound and also
fails the balance hypothesis. For \(m_\epsilon=\operatorname{Vol}_g(B_{2\rho})\),
any target conformal diffeomorphism \(T\) leaves the complement mapped
to one point \(T(q)\). The mean of \(T\circ f_\epsilon\) has magnitude at
least \(1-2m_\epsilon>0\) for small \(\epsilon\). Even the unconstrained
near-zero E09 values in nonnegative Yamabe classes therefore supply no
balanced sequence from this family. In general a target conformal
change \(T^*h=e^{2\omega}h\) sends the potential \(3|df|^2\) to
\((e^{2\omega}\circ f)3|df|^2\), so preserving the modified quotient
under such a change would require a separate argument.

**The unit-volume round identity.** On standard \(S^4\) orient domain
and target alike. Write \(V=\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)\),
\(\alpha=V^{1/2}\) and \(g=\alpha^{-1}h\). The round metric is Yamabe
minimizing in its own class. In stereographic coordinates with
\(h=b^2\delta\) and \(b(x)=2/(1+|x|^2)\), its Yamabe quotient is the
Euclidean quotient \(6\|d(bu)\|_2^2/\|bu\|_4^2\) by the conformal
scalar law. For smooth spherical \(u\), \(bu=O(|x|^{-2})\) and
\(d(bu)=O(|x|^{-3})\) at infinity, so cutoff approximation justifies
the Euclidean inequality and the integration by parts there.
Talenti's sharp inequality in dimension four with gradient exponent
two is attained by \(b\), proving round minimality at \(u=1\).
[Talenti, §0 Theorem, eqs. (1)–(3), p. 353][talenti]. The constant
rescaling keeps minimality and gives volume one. The identity is
balanced by antipodal symmetry and has \(s=12\alpha\),
\(|d\operatorname{id}|_{g,h}^2=4\alpha\), \(Y_{\operatorname{id}}([g])=0\)
and constant Jacobian \(J_{\operatorname{id}}=\alpha^2=V\). The five
coordinates satisfy \(\Delta_h x^a=4x^a\), from their spherical Hessian
\(-x^a h\), so \(4\alpha\) is an eigenvalue for \(g\). The inequality
\(s\le3\lambda_1\) then confirms \(\lambda_1=4\alpha\). The constant
sequence of identity maps meets all hypotheses.
