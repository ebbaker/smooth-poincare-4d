The estimates and identification in [E42][e42]–[E44][e44] pass this
review. The connection to [E41][e41] requires relative spectral
pinching. A vanishing scalar gap in the units of the original metric
gives that premise when the positive scalar curvatures are bounded
away from zero, or when the gap divided by scalar curvature tends to
zero.

## Supplied class and the link to E41

Let $g$ be a smooth unit-volume Yamabe-minimizing metric on a smooth
closed connected four-manifold, with scalar curvature $s>0$. Use
$L=3d^*d/s$, $\Gamma(v,w)=(3/s)\langle dv,dw\rangle_g$ and
$\|w\|_H^2=\int(w^2+\Gamma(w,w))\,dV_g$. Choose any real
$L^2(dV_g)$-orthonormal eigensystem $u_1,\ldots,u_5$ for the first
five positive eigenvalues $\nu_i$ of $L$, counted with multiplicity.
Write $\varepsilon=3\lambda_5/s-1=\nu_5-1$ for the relative pinching,
where the $\lambda_i$ are the corresponding eigenvalues of $d^*d$.
The minimizing inequality gives $\nu_1\ge1$ by second variation.
For uniform estimates take $0<\varepsilon\le1$.

On E41's balanced map class with covariance $C_f\ge\delta I$ for
fixed $0<\delta\le1/5$, the constant test and trace bound give
$-Y_f\ge D+3\delta B$, where $D=3\lambda_1-s\ge0$ and
$B=\sum_{i=2}^5(\lambda_i-\lambda_1)\ge0$. Denote the dimensionful
five-eigenvalue gap by $A=D+(3/5)B$. For $s>0$,

\[
\frac{A}{s}=\frac15\sum_{i=1}^5(\nu_i-1),
\qquad
\frac{A}{s}\le\varepsilon\le\frac{5A}{s}
\le\frac{-Y_f}{\delta s}.
\tag{1}
\]

The last inequality follows from $5A=5D+3B$ and
$5\delta\le1$. Thus $A\to0$ gives the required relative limit
if $\inf s>0$. Alternatively $A/s\to0$ directly suffices and
is equivalent to $\varepsilon\to0$. The map condition $Y_f/s\to0$
also suffices under the stipulated uniform $\delta$. Raw $Y_f\to0$
with uniform $\delta$ gives $A\to0$ by E41. That estimate alone does
not bound $A/s$ as $s\downarrow0$. This is an interface condition
for E42–E44, consistent with the absolute conclusion of E41.
It gives no example of a geometric sequence with $s\downarrow0$.

## Dyadic iteration and the quartic estimate

In dimension four the Yamabe quotient has gradient coefficient $6$
and power $4$ in its denominator norm. Dividing the minimizing
inequality at volume one by its value $s$ gives
$\|v\|_4^2\le Q(v)=\int(v^2+2\Gamma(v,v))$.
For smooth signed $v$, apply the positive-test inequality to
$\sqrt{v^2+\alpha^2}$ and let $\alpha\downarrow0$. Its gradient
energy is at most the energy of $v$. The inequality thus holds for
real signed tests, and density gives the continuous inclusion
$\|v\|_4\le\sqrt2\|v\|_H$ on $H$.
[Lee–Parker, (1.4)–(1.5) and §3][lp].

For each eigenfunction $u$ and any dyadic $p\ge2$, test the
eigen-equation with $u^{p-1}$ and apply the inequality to $u^{p/2}$.
Both powers are smooth even where $u=0$. The chain rule gives

\[
\int\Gamma(u^{p/2},u^{p/2})
  =\frac{\nu p^2}{4(p-1)}\|u\|_p^p,\qquad
\|u\|_{2p}\le
  \left(1+\frac{\nu p^2}{2(p-1)}\right)^{1/p}\|u\|_p .
\]

Since $\nu\le2$ the factor is bounded by $(1+2p)^{1/p}$.
The product over $p=2^j$, $j\ge1$, converges by summability
of $j2^{-j}$. Starting at $\|u\|_2=1$ yields a universal
$L^\infty$ bound. For $\phi=\sum_i c_i u_i$ with $|c|=1$,
orthogonality gives $\int\phi=0$, $\|\phi\|_2=1$ and
$\eta=\int\Gamma(\phi,\phi)-1=\int\phi r\in[0,\varepsilon]$,
where $r=(L-1)\phi$. The bounds
$\|\phi\|_\infty\le C$ and $\|r\|_\infty\le C\varepsilon$
follow from $\sum_i|c_i|\le\sqrt5$.

For mean-zero $w$, put $v_\pm=1\pm t\phi+t^2w$, $q=\int\phi^4$,
$a=3+2\eta$, $J=Q(w)$ and $B_w=\int(\phi w+2\Gamma(\phi,w))$.
The sign average of $Q(v_\pm)^2-\int v_\pm^4$ is the even polynomial

\[
\begin{aligned}
 4\eta t^2
 &+\left(\mathcal C_\phi(w)+12\eta+4\eta^2\right)t^4\\
 &+\left(2aJ+4B_w^2-4\int w^3-6\int\phi^2w^2\right)t^6
  +\left(J^2-\int w^4\right)t^8 ,\\
 \mathcal C_\phi(w)&=9-q+4\int\Gamma(w,w)-4\int w^2-12\int\phi^2w .
\end{aligned}
\tag{2}
\]

To check the expansion, the quadratic form on each sign is
$1+at^2\pm2B_wt^3+Jt^4$. The fourth-power average is the integral of
$(1+t^2w)^4+6t^2\phi^2(1+t^2w)^2+t^4\phi^4$.
Cauchy–Schwarz in $H$ bounds $B_w$, including its residual term
$2\int rw$. The embedding bounds $\int w^4$ and, by unit volume,
$|\int w^3|$. The $\phi^2w^2$ term is bounded by
$\|\phi\|_\infty^2\|w\|_2^2$. Thus for a fixed bound $K$ on
$\|w\|_H$, the terms of order six and eight contribute at most
$C_K|t|^6$ for $|t|\le1$. Nonnegativity of (2) and the choice
$t=\varepsilon^{1/4}$ give $\mathcal C_\phi(w)\ge-C_K\sqrt\varepsilon$.
After division by $t^4$ the relevant errors are
$O(\varepsilon/t^2)$ and $O_K(t^2)$.

For $b=\phi^2-1$, one has $\int b=0$, $\int b^2=q-1$ and
$\int\Gamma(b,b)=(4/3)\int\phi^3 L\phi$ by the chain rule.
In particular $\|b\|_H$ is uniformly bounded, and

\[
\mathcal C_\phi(b)=25-\frac{35}{3}q+\frac{16}{3}\int\phi^3r .
\tag{3}
\]

Since the residual term is $O(\varepsilon)$, this yields the
directional upper bound $q\le15/7+C\sqrt\varepsilon$.
For an independent check of the directional average, rotational
invariance on the coefficient unit $S^4$ gives
$\mathbb E c_1^4=3\mathbb E c_1^2c_2^2$. Expand
$(\sum_i c_i^2)^2=1$ to obtain $\mathbb E c_1^4=3/35$.
Thus for $S=\sum_i u_i^2$ the average of $q$ is
$(3/35)\int S^2=15/7+(3/35)\int(S-5)^2$. This proves
$\int(S-5)^2=O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$. The nonnegative quartic
$(15/7+C\sqrt\varepsilon)|c|^4-q(c)$ has coefficient-sphere
$L^1$ norm $O(\sqrt\varepsilon)$. Equivalence of $L^1$ and
$L^\infty$ norms in the fixed space of spherical quartics yields
the lower bound $q\ge15/7-C'\sqrt\varepsilon$ in every direction.
All constants here are independent of the metric, $s$, basis,
and direction.

## Scaled dual estimate and composite tests

The two-sided bound for $q$ and identity (3) give
$|\mathcal C_\phi(b)|\le C\sqrt\varepsilon$.
For a real smooth mean-zero $\zeta$ with $\|\zeta\|_H\le1$,
apply (2) to the uniformly $H$-bounded
$w=b+\tau\zeta$, $|\tau|\le1$. The change in $\mathcal C_\phi$ is

\[
8\tau\langle (L-\tfrac52)b,\zeta\rangle
+4\tau^2\int(\Gamma(\zeta,\zeta)-\zeta^2).
\]

For each sign of $\tau=\varepsilon^{1/4}$, the absolute quadratic
error is at most $4\sqrt\varepsilon$. Together with the lower
bound from (2), the signs give
$\|(L-\tfrac52)b\|_{H^*}\le C\varepsilon^{1/4}$.
The residual annihilates constants since $b$ has mean zero and
$L1=0$. Subtracting the mean decreases the $H$ norm, and smooth
tests are dense. This gives the full dual estimate, including
tests with nonzero mean. The signed test convention matters here.
No $L^\infty$ estimate on $\zeta$ is needed.

The nonnegative Laplacian product rule rewrites the residual as
$\Gamma(\phi,\phi)-(5-\phi^2)/4
=\phi r-\tfrac12(L-\tfrac52)b$. Here the product $\phi r$
has $L^2$, hence $H^*$, norm $O(\varepsilon)$. Taking $\phi=u_i$
or the half-difference for $\phi=(u_i\pm u_j)/\sqrt2$,
and setting $X_i=u_i/\sqrt5$, proves

\[
\left\|\Gamma(X_i,X_j)-\tfrac14(\delta_{ij}-X_iX_j)\right\|_{H^*}
 \le C\varepsilon^{1/4} .
\tag{4}
\]

The constant-test pairing of the left-hand error before taking
its norm is $(\nu_i-1)/5$ on the diagonal and zero off it.
This matches the chosen normalization exactly.

Across the sequence, the value vectors $X$ lie in a common
closed ball $K\subset\mathbb R^5$ by the dyadic estimate.
Their Gram matrix $G=(\Gamma(X_i,X_j))$ is positive semidefinite
pointwise and $\int\operatorname{tr}G=\sum_i\nu_i/5\le1+\varepsilon$.
For any fixed smooth function $F$ on $\mathbb R^5$, the chain rule
gives

\[
\|F(X)\|_H^2\le \sup_K|F|^2+
              (1+\varepsilon)\sup_K|\nabla_{\mathbb R^5}F|^2 .
\tag{5}
\]

Applied to each $\partial_jF$, this proves validity of the
composite tests in (4) with only two fixed target derivatives.
There is no pointwise upper bound on $G$ in this step.
Integration by parts for $LX_i=\nu_i X_i$ and (4)–(5) give an
error $O_F(\varepsilon^{1/4}+\varepsilon)$ in

\[
4\int X_i F(X)\,dV_g
 =\int\sum_j(\delta_{ij}-X_iX_j)(\partial_jF)(X)\,dV_g .
\tag{6}
\]

## Limiting law and recognition scope

Take any sequence in this class with $\varepsilon\to0$, allowing
the manifolds, metrics, and eigenbases to vary. The value laws
$(X)_*dV_g$ are supported in $K$. Every subsequential weak limit
$\mu$ is supported on the unit $S^4$ by
$\||X|^2-1\|_2=O(\varepsilon^{1/4})$. Passing to the limit in (6)
uses only continuous bounded tests on $K$. On the sphere
the tangent fields $V_i=e_i-x_i x$ have divergence $-4x_i$,
since the tangent derivative in the direction $\tau$ is
$-x_i\tau$. This proves the limiting identity for round probability.

To check uniqueness without a density assumption on $\mu$, take
$F=\partial_iP$ in the $i$-th identity, and sum, for each homogeneous
polynomial $P$ of degree $m\ge1$. Euler's identities give

\[
m(m+3)\int P\,d\mu =
 \int\sum_i\partial_{ii}P\,d\mu .
\]

The right-hand side has degree $m-2$ or vanishes. Since the
coefficient on the left is positive, induction from the total
mass proves equality of all polynomial moments with round
probability. Polynomials separate points and are uniformly dense
in continuous functions on $S^4$, so the limit is uniquely
normalized round area. Compactness and uniqueness then give weak
convergence of the full sequence.

For an individual smooth metric at exact pinching, the same
estimates apply with arbitrarily small positive $\varepsilon$.
Then $|X|=1$ and (4) is an identity of smooth functions, hence
$dX\,dX^*=(s/12)(I-XX^{\mathsf T})$. At every point $dX$ has
rank four and $X^*h=(s/12)g$ for the unit sphere metric $h$.
The image of this local diffeomorphism is open and closed. Properness
gives a covering, and connectedness of the domain and simple
connectivity of the sphere make it a diffeomorphism. For the varying
metrics the estimates give the law of values and a weak tested
differential identity. The radial estimate also bounds the volume
of $\{\,\big||X|^2-1\big|>a\,\}$ by $C a^{-2}\sqrt\varepsilon$
for each fixed $a>0$. A smooth metric limit, strong gradient
convergence, pointwise differential invertibility, and smooth
recognition require further hypotheses.
