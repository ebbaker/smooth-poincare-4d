Fix a smooth closed oriented Riemannian spin four-manifold $(M,g)$ with
a fixed spin structure, and $p\in M$. Let $S_g^+$ be the half-spin bundle on
which real self-dual two-forms act. Use the Clifford convention
$X\cdot_g X=-|X|_g^2$ and
$P_g\psi(X)=\nabla^g_X\psi+X\cdot_g D_g\psi/4$. Put
\[
\mu(h)=\inf_{0\ne\phi\in C^\infty(S_h^+)}
  \frac{\|P_h\phi\|_{L^2(h)}^2}{\|\phi\|_{L^2(h)}^2}.
\]
There are smooth unit-volume metrics $h_r\in[g]$ with
$0\le\mu(h_r)\le C r^3$. The specified trial field has a Rayleigh
quotient of order $r^3$ and its normalized mass converges weakly
to $\delta_p$ in the fixed reference coordinates. The infimum over the
unit-volume metrics in $[g]$ is zero, with exact attainment precisely
when $\ker P_g$ contains a nonzero positive half-spinor.

## 1. Metric scale and transport

Take a fixed normal ball centered at $p$. Write $\rho=d_g(p,\cdot)$ on it
and $B_t=\{\rho<t\}$. Take $0<r<1$ sufficiently small that $B_{4r}$ lies
in the ball. Choose smooth $1\le f_r\le r^{-1}$, identically $r^{-1}$ in
$B_r$ and identically 1 off $B_{2r}$. Such functions follow from a smooth
cutoff in $\rho/r$ that is constant near the endpoints of the transition
and near $p$. Put
\[
 u_r=a_r f_r,\qquad h_r=u_r^2g,\qquad
 a_r=\left(\int_M f_r^4\,dV_g\right)^{-1/4}.
\]
The normal-coordinate volume density is bounded above and below by
positive constants. In particular, $\operatorname{Vol}_g(B_t)\asymp t^4$
for small $t$. Since
\[
 \operatorname{Vol}_g(M)\le\int_M f_r^4\,dV_g
       \le \operatorname{Vol}_g(M)+r^{-4}\operatorname{Vol}_g(B_{2r}),
\]
$a_r$ stays between two positive constants. All constants in the proof
are independent of $r$ and of the choice of $f_r$ under the stated
bounds. The notation $\asymp$ means upper and lower bounds by positive
constant multiples for sufficiently small $r$.

For $h=u^2g$, the positive frame rescaling $e_i\mapsto u^{-1}e_i$ lifts
using the fixed spin structure to the norm-preserving bundle
identification $\beta:S_g^\pm\to S_h^\pm$. Clifford multiplication obeys
$(u^{-1}X)\cdot_h\beta\psi=\beta(X\cdot_g\psi)$. The dual coframes
$e^i\mapsto u e^i$, together with $\beta$, give the isometric tensor
identification $\mathcal B_u:T^*M\otimes S_g^+\to T^*M\otimes S_h^+$,
which preserves the Clifford-trace-free subbundles.

The kernel weight is $u^{1/2}$. Friedrich's published §1, eq. (1.1) and
the paragraph spanning pp. 59–60, gives this when its factor
$\lambda$ in $\bar g=\lambda g$ is taken to be $u^2$.
[Digitized published copy, pp. 59–60](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/701462/WSGP_09-1989-1_7.pdf#page=2).
The proof here also needs the covariance on arbitrary test fields. With
$q=\log u$, the conformal Levi-Civita formula in the two identified
orthonormal frames changes the connection coefficients
$g(\nabla_X^g e_i,e_j)$ by $e_i(q)g(X,e_j)-g(X,e_i)e_j(q)$. Applying
the spin connection matrix $\tfrac14\sum_{ij}\omega_{ij}e_i\cdot_g e_j\cdot_g$
and differentiating $u^{1/2}$ gives
\[
 \nabla^h_{u^{-1}X}(u^{1/2}\beta\psi)
   =u^{-1/2}\beta\bigl(\nabla^g_X\psi
               -\tfrac12 X\cdot_g\operatorname{grad}_g q\cdot_g\psi\bigr).
\]
Clifford contraction gives
$D_h(u^{1/2}\beta\psi)=u^{-1/2}\beta(D_g\psi+2\operatorname{grad}_g q\cdot_g\psi)$.
The gradient terms therefore cancel in $P_h$, yielding
$P_h(u^{1/2}\beta\psi)=u^{-1/2}\mathcal B_u(P_g\psi)$.
Since $dV_h=u^4dV_g$,
\[
 \|P_h(u^{1/2}\beta\psi)\|_{L^2(h)}^2
     =\int_M u^3|P_g\psi|_g^2\,dV_g,\qquad
 \|u^{1/2}\beta\psi\|_{L^2(h)}^2
     =\int_M u^5|\psi|_g^2\,dV_g .
\tag{1}
\]

## 2. The field and the two orders

Parallel transport a unit $s_p\in S_{g,p}^+$ radially to obtain a smooth
unit field $s$ on the normal ball. In the resulting radial spin frame
the connection matrix $A_i$ obeys $x^i A_i=0$ and
$A_i(x)=\int_0^1 t x^j F_{ji}(tx)\,dt$, where $F$ is its curvature.
Indeed, $x^j F_{ji}=x^j\partial_j A_i+A_i$ in this gauge. Smooth
curvature bounds give $|\nabla^g s|\le C\rho$ and hence
$|P_g s|\le C\rho$, using the orthogonal Clifford-trace-free projection.

Choose a smooth radial $0\le\chi_r\le1$ equal to 1 through $B_{2r}$,
supported in $B_{4r}$, with $|d\chi_r|\le C/r$. Extend
$\psi_r=\chi_r s$ by zero. Define the transported field
$\Phi_r=u_r^{1/2}\beta_r\psi_r$ and set
$N_r=\|P_{h_r}\Phi_r\|_{L^2(h_r)}^2$,
$D_r=\|\Phi_r\|_{L^2(h_r)}^2$. Formula (1) gives
\[
 c r^{-1}\le D_r\le C r^{-1}.
\tag{2}
\]
The lower bound integrates $u_r^5|\psi_r|^2=a_r^5r^{-5}$ on $B_r$.
For the upper bound, the integral on $B_{2r}$ is at most
$C r^{-5}\operatorname{Vol}_g(B_{2r})$. On $B_{4r}\setminus B_{2r}$
it is at most $C\operatorname{Vol}_g(B_{4r})=O(r^4)$ because $u_r=a_r$.

On $B_{2r}$, which includes the conformal transition, the numerator
contribution is at most
\[
 C r^{-3}\int_{B_{2r}}\rho^2\,dV_g\le C r^3 .
\]
On $A_r=B_{4r}\setminus B_{2r}$ the conformal factor is constant and
$P_g(\chi_r s)=\chi_r P_gs+\Pi_g(d\chi_r\otimes s)$, where $\Pi_g$ is the
orthogonal projection on the Clifford-trace-free part. Each point has
$|\Pi_g(d\chi_r\otimes s)|^2=\tfrac34 |d\chi_r|^2$, since the squared
norm of the removed trace is $|(d\chi_r)^\sharp\cdot_g s|^2/4$.
The cutoff bound and the annular volume give an upper bound $C r^2$ for
the weighted annular integral. For the lower bound, on every radial ray
the cutoff changes from 1 to 0 between $2r$ and $4r$. Cauchy–Schwarz gives
$\int_{2r}^{4r}|\partial_\rho\chi_r|^2\,d\rho\ge 1/(2r)$, so the polar
volume density comparable to $\rho^3$ gives
$\int_{A_r}|d\chi_r|^2\,dV_g\ge c r^2$. Apply
$|A+B|^2\ge |A|^2/2-|B|^2$ with $A=\Pi_g(d\chi_r\otimes s)$ and
$B=\chi_r P_gs$. The integral of $|B|^2$ over $A_r$ is $O(r^6)$.
Consequently
\[
  N_r\asymp r^2,\qquad
  \|P_{h_r}(\Phi_r/\sqrt{D_r})\|_{L^2(h_r)}^2
          =N_r/D_r\asymp r^3 .
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the normalized trial residual has norm of order $r^{3/2}$, and
$0\le\mu(h_r)\le N_r/D_r\le C r^3$. The argument requires no scalar
curvature sign, and applies to any fixed smooth base metric in the
stated category. A smooth homotopy four-sphere is spin by
$H^2(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0$.

## 3. Trial mass, eigenvectors and exact attainment

For the normalized field $\widehat\Phi_r=\Phi_r/\sqrt{D_r}$ its
probability measure in the original coordinates is
\[
  |\widehat\Phi_r|_{h_r}^2\,dV_{h_r}
     =D_r^{-1}u_r^5|\psi_r|_g^2\,dV_g.
\]
It has mass at least $c>0$ in $B_r$ by the estimates on numerator and
denominator of this mass fraction in (2). It has mass $O(r^5)$ off
$B_{2r}$ and vanishes off $B_{4r}$. The inner transition
$B_{2r}\setminus B_r$ can contribute to the mass. For each continuous
test function, the error from its value at $p$ is bounded by its maximum
oscillation on $B_{4r}$. This proves the stated weak convergence.

For a fixed smooth $h$, the Clifford projection gives principal symbol
$\tfrac34|\xi|_h^2 I$ for $P_h^*P_h$. Compact elliptic spectral theory
gives a smooth first eigenspinor on the closed manifold. The proof of
(3) uses a Rayleigh test. It bounds the first eigenvalue from above and
establishes mass concentration for $\widehat\Phi_r$ only. In particular,
it supplies no estimate comparing $\widehat\Phi_r$ with the first
eigenspace as $r$ varies.

The covariance above is invertible for every fixed smooth positive
$u$, and thus identifies the positive chiral kernels of $P_g$ and
$P_{u^2g}$. If the class has a nonzero kernel, $\mu(h)=0$ for every
unit-volume representative by normalization of a kernel element. If the
class has zero kernel, fixed-metric ellipticity and nonnegativity give
$\mu(h)>0$ for every smooth representative. In that case (3) proves
nonattainment of the zero infimum within $[g]$. The result leaves existence
of a conformal class with a nonzero kernel on an arbitrary homotopy
four-sphere open.
