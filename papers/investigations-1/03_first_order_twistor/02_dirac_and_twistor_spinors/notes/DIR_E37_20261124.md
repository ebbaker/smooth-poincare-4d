## Fixed minimizing inequality

Let $(M^4,g)$ be any smooth closed connected Riemannian manifold with
$\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)=1$. Write $\Delta=d^*d$ and let $\lambda>0$
be its first positive scalar eigenvalue. Assume $s=3\lambda$ is the
constant scalar curvature and $1$ minimizes the Yamabe quotient of
$[g]$. Thus for smooth positive $u$,
\[
 \frac{\int_M(6|du|^2+s u^2)\,dV_g}{(\int_M u^4\,dV_g)^{1/2}}\ge s,
 \qquad
 \left[\int_M\left(u^2+\tfrac2\lambda|du|^2\right)dV_g\right]^2
 \ge\int_M u^4\,dV_g .
\]
Squaring is valid because the two sides of the unsquared version
are nonnegative. This is the assumed minimizing inequality on a
single supplied metric. The $s=3\lambda$ situation is the one
identified in [E14](DIR_E14_20261124.md#the-yamabe-and-poincaré-inequalities)
and [E17](DIR_E17_20261124.md#1-necessity-and-the-jacobian-hypothesis).

## Cubic and quartic coefficients

Take any real first eigenfunction $\phi$, any real smooth mean-zero
$w$, and $u=1+t\phi+t^2w$. The eigenfunction also has mean zero and
$u>0$ for both signs of sufficiently small $t$. Write
$m_j=\int_M\phi^j\,dV_g$, $b=\int_M\phi w\,dV_g$, and
$J=\int_M(w^2+2|dw|^2/\lambda)\,dV_g$. Integrals in the rest
of the calculation use $dV_g$. The exact first bracket is
\[
 1+3m_2t^2+6bt^3+Jt^4
\]
because $\int\langle d\phi,dw\rangle=\lambda b$. With
$z=t\phi+t^2w$, the squared deficit is therefore the exact polynomial
\[
 (1+3m_2t^2+6bt^3+Jt^4)^2
     -1-6\int z^2-4\int z^3-\int z^4.
\]
Its terms through order four are
\[
 -4m_3t^3+\mathcal C_\phi(w)t^4,\qquad
 \mathcal C_\phi(w)=
 \frac4\lambda\int|dw|^2-4\int w^2-12\int\phi^2w+9m_2^2-m_4 .
\]
Nonnegativity at both signs of $t$ implies $m_3=0$. Dividing by
$t^4$ and letting $t\to0$ then gives $\mathcal C_\phi(w)\ge0$
for every smooth mean-zero $w$. The first eigenspace is linear.
The coefficient of $abc$ in
$\int(a\phi_1+b\phi_2+c\phi_3)^3=0$ is
$6\int\phi_1\phi_2\phi_3$, so all triple products of real first
eigenfunctions integrate to zero.

## Centered-square test and equality

For $w_0=\phi^2-m_2$, integration by parts gives
\[
 3\int\phi^2|d\phi|^2=\int\phi^3\Delta\phi=\lambda m_4,
 \qquad
 \int|d(\phi^2)|^2=\tfrac{4\lambda}{3}m_4.
\]
Also $\int w_0^2=\int\phi^2w_0=m_4-m_2^2$. Inserting these
terms gives precisely
\[
 \mathcal C_\phi(w_0)=25m_2^2-\tfrac{35}{3}m_4\ge0,
 \qquad
 m_4\le\tfrac{15}{7}m_2^2 .
\]

For any smooth mean-zero $\zeta$, the exact variation in $w$ is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \mathcal C_\phi(w+\varepsilon\zeta)
 &=\mathcal C_\phi(w)
 +\varepsilon\left(
      \tfrac8\lambda\int\langle dw,d\zeta\rangle
      -8\int w\zeta-12\int\phi^2\zeta
   \right)\\
 &\quad+\varepsilon^2\left(\tfrac4\lambda\int|d\zeta|^2-4\int\zeta^2\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The pure $\varepsilon^2$ coefficient is nonnegative by Poincaré.
If the moment bound saturates, then $w_0$ is a global minimum
of $\mathcal C_\phi$ with value zero, so its linear coefficient
vanishes for all $\zeta$. Using the mean-zero constraint and
$\int w_0=\int\Delta w_0=0$ yields
\[
 (\Delta-\lambda)w_0=\tfrac{3\lambda}{2}w_0,\qquad
 \Delta(\phi^2-m_2)=\tfrac{5\lambda}{2}(\phi^2-m_2).
\]
Conversely, this equation implies
$(5\lambda/2)(m_4-m_2^2)=\int|dw_0|^2=(4\lambda/3)m_4$,
so it is equivalent to saturation. For nonzero $\phi$,
the equivalent pointwise gradient identity in the equality case is
$|d\phi|^2=(\lambda/4)(5m_2-\phi^2)$, by the product rule.

## Unit-volume round calibration

Let $h$ be unit round on $S^4$ and $g=c h$ where
$c=\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)^{-1/2}$. The constant function
minimizes the round Yamabe quotient. In stereographic coordinates
for $h$, put $\rho(x)=2/(1+|x|^2)$ and $U=\rho u$. The identity
$\Delta_{\mathbb R^4}\rho=2\rho^3$ with nonnegative Laplacian gives
\[
 \int_{S^4}(6|du|_h^2+12u^2)\,dV_h
 =6\int_{\mathbb R^4}|dU|^2\,dx,\qquad
 \int_{S^4}u^4\,dV_h=\int_{\mathbb R^4}U^4\,dx.
\]
For the energy identity expand $|d(\rho u)|^2$ and integrate
$\langle d\rho,d(\rho u^2)\rangle$ by parts. The boundary
term vanishes for smooth sphere tests by the decay of $\rho$
and $d\rho$. The sharp Euclidean Sobolev theorem with dimension
four and gradient exponent two applies by cutoff, and the
function $U=\rho$ from $u=1$ attains equality. Constant scaling
preserves the four-dimensional Yamabe quotient.
[Talenti, *Best Constant in Sobolev Inequality*, 1976, printed
p. 353, opening theorem, eqs. (1)–(3)](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/talenti.sobolev.pdf#page=1).

Here $s=12/c$ and $\lambda_1=4/c$. The coordinate restrictions
on $S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ have eigenvalue $4/c$. The integrated
Bochner identity with $\operatorname{Ric}_g=3g/c$ and
$|\nabla^2 f|^2\ge(\Delta f)^2/4$ gives the matching lower
bound for every nonzero eigenvalue. At $\lambda_1$, equality
gives $\nabla^2 f=-(f/c)g$. Along geodesics the equation determines
$f$ from its value and gradient at one point, so the five coordinate
restrictions span the first eigenspace.
[E17, round calibration](DIR_E17_20261124.md#3-round-calibration-and-feasibility).

For $\phi(x)=a\cdot x$, the volume-one measure is the uniform
probability measure on the unit sphere. A single coordinate has
density $\tfrac34(1-t^2)$ on $[-1,1]$ from the latitude volume
element. Rotating $a$ to a coordinate direction gives
$m_2=|a|^2/5$ and $m_4=3|a|^4/35$, which attain the ratio $15/7$
for $a\ne0$. Also $|d\phi|_g^2=(|a|^2-\phi^2)/c$, so
$\Delta(\phi^2-|a|^2/5)=(10/c)(\phi^2-|a|^2/5)$, as required
by $\tfrac52\lambda_1$.
