## Homogeneous Sobolev inequality and a positive lower bound

On the given $N$, there is $S<\infty$ such that
$\|\phi\|_{L^4(k)}\le S\|d\phi\|_{L^2(k)}$ for $\phi\in C_c^\infty(N)$.
To see this, cut off to the end with a fixed function transitioning
across a fixed coordinate annulus $A$. The end contribution is bounded
by $C(\|d\phi\|_2+\|\phi\|_{L^2(A)})$ by uniform metric comparability and
the Euclidean dimension-four Sobolev inequality for compactly supported
functions. The annular $L^2$ term is bounded by $C\|d\phi\|_2$ by integrating
outward on each ray and using
\[
 |\phi(r\theta)|^2\le
 \left(\int_r^\infty|\partial_t\phi(t\theta)|^2t^3\,dt\right)
 \left(\int_r^\infty t^{-3}\,dt\right).
\]
Integrate this inequality over the fixed annulus. On a connected smooth
compact truncation $K$ containing $A$, the usual compact Poincaré
inequality relative to the average $\phi_K$ gives
$\|\phi\|_{L^2(K)}\le C(\|d\phi\|_{L^2(K)}+\|\phi\|_{L^2(A)})$.
Indeed, $|\phi_K|\operatorname{Vol}(A)^{1/2}$ is bounded by
$\|\phi\|_{L^2(A)}+\|\phi-\phi_K\|_{L^2(K)}$. The compact part of
$\phi$ is then bounded in $L^4$ by a finite partition into coordinate
charts and the same Euclidean Sobolev inequality, with the derivative
of the cutoffs paid for by $\|\phi\|_{L^2(K)}$.
[Talenti, *Best Constant in Sobolev Inequality*, 1976, printed p. 353, opening theorem (1), with $m=4$, $p=2$](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/talenti.sobolev.pdf#page=1).

Write $D^{1,2}(N)$ for the completion of $C_c^\infty(N)$ in the
gradient norm. The inequality identifies it with functions in
$L^4(N,k)$ with weak gradient in $L^2(N,k)$. For the reverse inclusion,
truncate with smooth exhaustion cutoffs $\chi_R$ equal to one through
radius $R$ and zero beyond $2R$, $|d\chi_R|\le C/R$. The shell error
$\|u\,d\chi_R\|_2$ is at most $C\|u\|_{L^4(R<|x|<2R)}$ by Hölder
and the volume bound. The other cutoff error is the gradient tail.
Local smoothing of the compactly supported cuts completes the argument.

If $V\not\equiv0$, choose a relatively compact ball $B$ of volume
$b>0$ on which $V\ge c>0$. For every $v\in1+D^{1,2}(N)$,
\[
 b^{1/2}\le\|1-v\|_{L^2(B)}+\|v\|_{L^2(B)}
 \le b^{1/4}S\|dv\|_2+c^{-1/2}\left(\int_N V v^2\,dV_k\right)^{1/2}.
\]
Hence $Q(v)\ge b/(b^{1/2}S^2/12+c^{-1})>0$.
If $V=0$, the constant competitor $v=1$ gives zero.

## Relaxed minimizer and approximation

On $D^{1,2}(N)$ the bilinear form
$12\int\langle dw,d\eta\rangle+\int Vw\eta$ is bounded and coercive.
Indeed, $V\in L^1\cap L^2\cap L^{4/3}$ and the homogeneous Sobolev
inequality bounds the potential term. The functional $\eta\mapsto
\int V\eta$ is bounded as well. Lax–Milgram supplies a unique $w$
for that right-hand side, and $v_*=1-w$ is the unique minimizer of
$Q$ over $1+D^{1,2}(N)$. For all $\eta$ in that space of variations,
\[
 12\int_N\langle dv_*,d\eta\rangle\,dV_k+\int_N Vv_*\eta\,dV_k=0,
 \qquad 12\Delta_k v_*+Vv_*=0.
\]
The differential equation holds smoothly by local elliptic regularity.
Truncating $v_*$ to $[0,1]$ preserves the affine space and cannot
increase either term in $Q$. Uniqueness implies $0\le v_*\le1$.
The strong maximum principle for $\operatorname{div}_k\nabla -V/12$
gives $v_*>0$ since $v_*\equiv0$ would violate $1-v_*\in L^4$.
If $V$ is nonzero then $w>0$ everywhere by the strong minimum principle
for $\Delta_k w=Vv_*/12\ge0$. In this case $v_*<1$ everywhere, so the
relaxed optimum belongs to the original compact-variation class only
when $V=0$, where it equals one.

The smooth functions $v_R=1-\chi_R w\ge v_*>0$ equal one off compact
sets, and $v_R-1\to -w$ in $D^{1,2}$ by the cutoff estimate.
The potential term is continuous under this convergence by Hölder.
It follows that the compact-class infimum equals the relaxed minimum,
denoted $P=Q(v_*)$. Testing the equation with $w$ gives
the identity $12\int |dv_*|^2=\int Vv_*(1-v_*)$, so
\[
 P=\int_N Vv_*\,dV_k,\qquad
 q:=P/12=\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{S_R}\partial_{\nu_k} v_*\,dA_k .
\]
For the flux, integrate the equation on the compact truncation
bounded by $S_R$, with outward normal $\nu_k$. The finite-radius
flux is exactly $\int_{N_R} Vv_*/12\,dV_k$. In particular $q>0$
when $V\not\equiv0$.

## Leading coefficient and derivative estimate

Let $f=Vv_*/12$ and $w=1-v_*$. Then $\Delta_k w=f=O(R^{-8})$.
On the exterior of a large fixed sphere,
$\Delta_k R^{-2}=O(R^{-7})$ and
$\Delta_k(R^{-2}-R^{-3})=3R^{-5}+O(R^{-7})$. This uses the
nonnegative Laplacian and $k-\delta=O_2(R^{-3})$. A sufficiently
large multiple $B_0$ of $R^{-2}-R^{-3}$ therefore satisfies
$\Delta_k B_0\ge f$ there and dominates $w$ on a collar of the
inner sphere. Test the inequality for $w-B_0$ with
$(w-B_0)_+$, extended by zero through that collar. The test is
in $D^{1,2}$ since $w$ and the exterior barrier have $L^4$ values
and $L^2$ gradients. Approximation by cutoffs as above justifies
its use on the unbounded exterior. This gives
$\int |d(w-B_0)_+|^2\le0$. Thus $w=O(R^{-2})$.

On fixed nested annuli rescaled by $R$, the principal coefficients
are uniformly elliptic and uniformly Lipschitz and the first-order
coefficients, including the rescaling, are $O(R^{-3})$.
For a smooth $z$ with $\Delta_k z=F$, the interior $W^{2,p}$ estimate
for $p>4$ and Morrey embedding give on the smaller annulus
\[
 R\|Dz\|_\infty+R^{2-4/p}\|D^2z\|_{L^p(dx)}
 \le C\left(\|z\|_{\infty,\mathrm{larger}}+
                 R^2\|F\|_{\infty,\mathrm{larger}}\right).
\]
Here the derivatives and the $L^p$ norm on the left are Euclidean in
the end coordinates. To apply the usual principal-part interior
estimate one absorbs the uniformly small first-order term in the
rescaled interior estimate by interpolation on nested annuli.
Applied to $w$, this gives $Dw=O(R^{-3})$. The annular estimate also
supplies the weighted second-derivative bounds used below.
[Chiarenza–Frasca–Longo, *$W^{2,p}$-Solvability of the Dirichlet Problem for Nondivergence Elliptic Equations with VMO Coefficients*, 1993, Theorem 4.1, eq. (4.4), printed p. 850](https://www.karlin.mff.cuni.cz/~kaplicky/pages/pages/2010z/ChiaFraLon1993.pdf#page=10).

For the coefficient, write $\rho=\sqrt{\det k}$ and
$a^{ij}=\rho k^{ij}$. With a fixed exterior cutoff equal to one
at large radius, extend the cut-off $w$ to a smooth $W$ on
$\mathbb R^4$. On its large-radius region,
\[
 \Delta_0 W=\rho f+\partial_i\big((a^{ij}-\delta^{ij})\partial_j w\big),
 \qquad \Delta_0=-\sum_i\partial_i^2 .
\]
Cutting off the displayed sources and absorbing the compactly
supported commutator into the scalar source gives on $\mathbb R^4$
$\Delta_0 W=F_0+\partial_i G_i$, with $F_0=O(R^{-8})$ and
$G=O(R^{-6})$. The scalar source has charge $\int F_0\,dx=q$.
Indeed the integral of $\partial_i G_i$ on a large Euclidean
ball tends to zero, and the Euclidean flux
$-\int_{S_R}\partial_R W\,dA_0$ differs from the metric flux of
$-dw$ by $O(R^3 R^{-3}R^{-3})=O(R^{-3})$.

The Euclidean fundamental solution for $\Delta_0$ is
$\Gamma(x)=(2\omega_3)^{-1}|x|^{-2}$, where
$\omega_3=|S^3|=2\pi^2$. This constant gives unit outward flux of
$-d\Gamma$. Set $U=\Gamma*F_0+\sum_i(\partial_i\Gamma)*G_i$.
For $|x|=R$ the integral of $(\Gamma(x-y)-\Gamma(x))F_0(y)$ over
$|y|\le R/2$ is $O(R^{-3})$ using the first moment of $F_0$.
For $|y|>R/2$, split into $|x-y|\le R/2$ and its complement.
On the former the kernel integral is $O(R^2)$ and the source bound
is $O(R^{-8})$. On the latter the kernel is $O(R^{-2})$ times
a source tail $O(R^{-4})$. The subtracted constant term on the
tail is also $O(R^{-6})$. For the $G$ convolution the near-origin
part is $O(R^{-3})\|G\|_1$. The tail bound follows from
$|\partial\Gamma|\lesssim |x-y|^{-3}$ and $G=O(R^{-6})$,
giving $O(R^{-5})$ in each of the two tail regions.
Therefore $U=(q/(2\omega_3))R^{-2}+O(R^{-3})$. The difference
$W-U$ is harmonic distributionally on all of $\mathbb R^4$ and
decays to zero, so it vanishes by Weyl's lemma and Liouville.

Put $A=q/(2\omega_3)=P/(24\omega_3)$. The residual
$z=w-A R^{-2}$ satisfies $z=O(R^{-3})$ and $\Delta_k z=O(R^{-7})$.
Applying the rescaled estimate to it gives
$Dz=O(R^{-4})$ and
$\|D^2 z\|_{L^p(R<|x|<2R)}=O(R^{-5+4/p})$.
In particular, with no derivative decay assumed for $V$,
\[
 v_*=1-\frac{P}{24\omega_3} R^{-2}+O_1(R^{-3}).
\]

## ADM normalization and the admissible optimizer

Every compact-variation metric $v^{-4}k$ equals $k$ on its exterior
and has zero ADM mass by $k-\delta=O_2(R^{-3})$. For the relaxed
metric $\widehat k=v_*^{-4}k$, the established derivative expansion
gives $\widehat k_{ij}=(1+4A R^{-2})\delta_{ij}+O_1(R^{-3})$.
It is complete as $v_*\le1$. On the end its metric error is in
$W^{2,p}_{-\tau}$ for any $1<\tau<2$, by dyadic summation of
the annular second-derivative bounds. Its scalar curvature
\[
 s_{\widehat k}
 =v_*^6\big(6\Delta_k(v_*^{-2})+s_kv_*^{-2}\big)
 =-3Vv_*^4-36v_*^2|dv_*|_k^2
\]
is integrable, since $v_*\to1$, $V=O(R^{-8})$, and $dv_*=O(R^{-3})$.
This verifies the decay and curvature hypotheses of Bartnik's
mass invariance theorem. In the same chart the flux integrand for
the leading scalar metric term is $-3\partial_R(1+4A R^{-2})$.
Thus the raw flux is $24\omega_3 A=P$. Dividing by the usual
$2(4-1)\omega_3=6\omega_3$ gives the normalized mass
\[
 m_{\mathrm{ADM}}(\widehat k)=4A=\frac{P}{6\omega_3}.
\]
[Bartnik, *The Mass of an Asymptotically Flat Manifold*, 1986, §4, (4.4), (4.6), Theorem 4.2, pp. 680–681](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/bartnik.mass.pdf) ·
[Baldauf–Ozuch, *Spinors and Mass on Weighted Manifolds*, §1.2 after (0.5), mass normalization](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00220-022-04420-y).

The E31 spinor boundary flux at the optimizer is $P/4$. Indeed
$\int_{S_R}v_*\partial_{\nu_k}v_*\to q$ by the first-derivative
bounds and $v_*\to1$. Equation (6) of E31 gives three times that
flux for the transported harmonic spinor. Since its squared norm
is $v_*^6$ and $dV_{\widehat k}=v_*^{-8}dV_k$, the displayed scalar
formula gives the corresponding weighted scalar integral $-3P$,
consistent with the spinor Dirichlet integral $P$.

For $V\not\equiv0$, compact competitors approximate the optimal
energy but the optimizer itself carries positive mass. The bound
and the mass relation concern this fixed geometry and supplied
spinor.
