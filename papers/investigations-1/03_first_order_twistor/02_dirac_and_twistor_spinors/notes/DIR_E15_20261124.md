Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented spin homotopy
four-sphere with a supplied unit-volume Yamabe minimizer $g$. Write $s$
for its constant scalar curvature and $\lambda_1>0$ for the first
positive eigenvalue of $\Delta_g=d^*d$. Write $h$ for the unit round
metric on the oriented $S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$. A smooth map $f$ is
balanced when $\int_M f\,dV_g=0$. For a smooth $f$ set

\[
 V_f=3|df|_{g,h}^2,\qquad
 Q_f(u)=\int_M(6|du|_g^2+(s-V_f)u^2)\,dV_g,
 \qquad Y_f([g])=\inf_{u\in H^1(M)\setminus\{0\}}
                 \frac{Q_f(u)}{\|u\|_{L^4(g)}^2}.
\]

Let $S_{\rm bal}$ be the supremum of $Y_f([g])$ over smooth balanced
degree-$+1$ maps. For a supplied $g$ as above, $S_{\rm bal}=0$ precisely
when $s=3\lambda_1$ and a smooth sphere-valued first eigenmap exists.
The maps attaining the value zero are exactly the orientation-preserving
homotheties to $(S^4,h)$.

## Unit first eigenmap and possible degrees

Assume $s=3\lambda_1$ and let $F:M\to S^4$ be a smooth first eigenmap.
Thus $\Delta_g F=\lambda_1 F$ in $\mathbb R^5$, allowing identically
zero coordinates. Integration gives balance and
$\tfrac12\Delta_g|F|^2=\lambda_1-|dF|^2=0$, so $V_F=s$ everywhere.
If $\deg F\ne0$, Listing's rigidity theorem applies through E05 since
$s=3|dF|^2$. It makes $F$ a homothety and thus a diffeomorphism with
degree $\pm1$. Listing's theorem uses nonzero degree of either sign.
[Author PDF, §1, Theorem 3, equation (6), PDF p. 4](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=4).

Set $k=1-\deg F\in\{0,1,2\}$. For $k=0$ take the constant family
$f_\epsilon=F$. It is balanced of degree $+1$, with $Q_F(u)=6\|du\|_2^2$
and $Y_F=0$. For $k=1,2$, choose distinct fixed points $p_i$, $1\le i\le k$,
and positively oriented normal coordinates at each. Set
$\rho=\epsilon^{1/2}$ and $R=\epsilon^{1/4}$. For small $\epsilon$ the
balls $B_R(p_i)$ are disjoint and $4\rho<R$.

## Flattening, bubbles and degree

For each $i$, express $F$ on $B_{4\rho}(p_i)$ by
$F(x)=\exp_{F(p_i)}\zeta_i(x)$ in target geodesic coordinates. Here
$|\zeta_i(x)|\le C|x|$ and $|d\zeta_i|\le C$ uniformly. Replace
$\zeta_i$ by $\beta(|x|/\rho)\zeta_i$, where $\beta$ is smooth, zero up
to $3$ and one from $4$ onward. This yields a smooth map $\widetilde F$
that is constant equal to $F(p_i)$ on $B_{3\rho}(p_i)$ and agrees with
$F$ outside all $B_{4\rho}(p_i)$. Multiplying by the interpolation
between $1$ and $\beta$ gives a smooth homotopy. The derivative of the
flattened map remains bounded since $|\zeta_i|=O(\rho)$ wherever the
cutoff derivative is $O(1/\rho)$.

On $B_{2\rho}(p_i)$ insert the E09 inverse-stereographic bubble of scale
$\epsilon$, equal to the south pole at and outside the cutoff radius
$2\rho$, followed by an orientation-preserving orthogonal target
rotation taking the south pole to $F(p_i)$. Each rotated bubble is
constant outside radius $2\rho$ and has degree $+1$ when extended
globally by that constant. The insertion matches the flattened map
smoothly. Call the joined map $G_\epsilon$. On each insertion ball the
flattened map has zero Jacobian. Integration of the signed target
volume form over the modified balls thus gives

\[
 \deg G_\epsilon=\deg\widetilde F+k=\deg F+k=1.
\]

The maps differ from $F$ only in the union of $B_{4\rho}(p_i)$, of
volume $O(\rho^4)$, and the flattened derivatives outside the inserted
bubbles are bounded. On each bubble core the Euclidean conformal factor
is $b_\epsilon=2\epsilon/(\epsilon^2+|x|^2)$. Since the core has
$|dG_\epsilon|^2\le Cb_\epsilon^2$, its derivative square integral is
bounded by $C\epsilon^2(1+\log(\rho/\epsilon))\to0$. The E09 annular
derivative bound $C\epsilon/\rho^2$ gives $O(\epsilon^2)$ for the square
integral there. Hence $G_\epsilon\to F$ strongly in ambient $W^{1,2}$.
The mean error satisfies $\big|\int_MG_\epsilon\,dV_g\big|=O(\rho^4)$.

## Uniform lower bound for the modified scalar form

On each $B_R(p_i)$ put $P_\epsilon=12b_\epsilon^2$, the Euclidean
bubble potential. Use the normal volume factor $J=\sqrt{\det(g_{ab})}$.
The E09 core and cutoff estimates, the uniform derivative bound on
the flattened or unchanged part, and the small volume of $B_R(p_i)$
give

\[
 \|JV_{G_\epsilon}-P_\epsilon\|_{L^2(B_R(p_i),dx)}
 \le C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2\big),
 \qquad \|Js\|_{L^2(B_R(p_i),dx)}\le CR^2.
\]

In detail, on $r\le\rho$ the metric error of $JV_{G_\epsilon}$ is
$O(R^2)P_\epsilon$, with $\|P_\epsilon\|_2$ bounded independently of
$\epsilon$. On $\rho\le r\le2\rho$ the cutoff potential has $L^2$
norm $O((\epsilon/\rho)^2)$. On $2\rho\le r\le R$ the actual
potential is uniformly bounded and has $L^2$ norm $O(R^2)$, and the
model tail on $r\ge\rho$ has $L^2$ norm $O((\epsilon/\rho)^2)$.
These estimates also give $\sup_\epsilon\|V_{G_\epsilon}\|_{L^2(g)}<\infty$.

For $v\in H^1_0(B_R(p_i))$, comparison of the kinetic term with
the Euclidean metric gives a factor $1-CR^2$. As in E09, keep that
factor on the entire nonnegative Euclidean form with potential
$P_\epsilon$. This is the form for which $b_\epsilon>0$ is a zero
solution, $-6\sum_a\partial_a^2 b_\epsilon=P_\epsilon b_\epsilon$.
The remaining kinetic comparison error appears as at most
$CR^2\int P_\epsilon v^2\,dx\le CR^2\|v\|_4^2$. Together with the
$L^2$ potential and scalar errors, this yields

\[
 Q_{G_\epsilon}(v)\ge-C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2\big)\|v\|_4^2.
\]

This bound holds with arbitrary test gradients. Localize with
$\chi_i=\cos\theta_i$ on $B_R(p_i)$, supported there and equal to one
on $B_{4\rho}(p_i)$, with $\theta_i$ rising smoothly on the logarithmic
scale from $0$ to $\pi/2$. Take $\eta=\sin\theta_i$ in the $i$th ball
and $1$ elsewhere. Then $\sum_i\chi_i^2+\eta^2=1$. For
$\ell=\log(R/(4\rho))$, each cutoff gradient has fourth power of
its $L^4$ norm bounded by $C\ell^{-3}$, by integrating
$C\ell^{-4}r^{-4}$ in dimension four. On the support of $\eta$,
$G_\epsilon=F$ and $s-V_F=0$, so $Q_{G_\epsilon}(\eta u)\ge0$.
Expanding gradients in the partition gives

\[
 Q_{G_\epsilon}(u)=\sum_i Q_{G_\epsilon}(\chi_i u)
  + Q_{G_\epsilon}(\eta u)
  -6\int_M\Big(\sum_i|d\chi_i|^2+|d\eta|^2\Big)u^2\,dV_g.
\]

Hölder's inequality and $k\le2$ give a lower bound
$-C(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2+\ell^{-3/2})\|u\|_4^2$. This is uniform
over the unit $L^4$ sphere of tests.

## Exact balance and convergence

For $b\in\mathbb R^5$ near zero let $\Phi_b:S^4\to S^4$ be the
time-one flow of $X_b(y)=b-(b\cdot y)y$. This is a smooth family with
$\Phi_0=\operatorname{id}$. The vector field is the gradient of
$b\cdot y$ on the unit sphere, whose Hessian is $-(b\cdot y)h$, so
its flow is conformal and orientation-preserving. Write
$\Phi_b^*h=a_b^2h$. Compactness and smooth dependence give
$\|a_b^2-1\|_{C^0}+\|a_b^4-1\|_{C^0}\le C|b|$ for small $b$.

Set $\mathcal B_G(b)=\int_M\Phi_b(G)\,dV_g$. The derivative at zero
for $F$ is

\[
 A=D_b\mathcal B_F(0)=I-\int_M FF^\top dV_g,
 \qquad v^\top Av=\int_M\big(|v|^2-(v\cdot F)^2\big)\,dV_g.
\]

If $v\ne0$, vanishing would force $F$ everywhere into the pair of
antipodal points $\{v/|v|,-v/|v|\}$. The domain is connected and
balance excludes a constant, so $A$ is positive definite. The smooth
dependence of $D_b\Phi_b(y)$ on both variables and the compact target
give the uniform bound

\[
 \|D_b\mathcal B_{G_\epsilon}(b)-A\|
 \le C\big(|b|+\|G_\epsilon-F\|_{L^1(g)}\big).
\]

Choose a fixed small parameter ball so that for sufficiently small
$\epsilon$ the map $b\mapsto b-A^{-1}\mathcal B_{G_\epsilon}(b)$ is
$1/2$-Lipschitz there. It maps the closed ball of radius
$2|A^{-1}\mathcal B_{G_\epsilon}(0)|$ into itself for small $\epsilon$.
The contraction gives a solution $b_\epsilon^{\rm bal}=O(\rho^4)$ to
$\mathcal B_{G_\epsilon}(b)=0$, with $b=0$ available when the radius
is zero. Let $f_\epsilon=\Phi_{b_\epsilon^{\rm bal}}\circ G_\epsilon$.
It has exact mean zero and the same degree $+1$. The potential changes
by at most

\[
 \|V_{f_\epsilon}-V_{G_\epsilon}\|_2
 \le C|b_\epsilon^{\rm bal}|\|V_{G_\epsilon}\|_2=o(1),
\]

by the conformal factor of the target map. This bounds the change in
the Rayleigh quotient uniformly for $\|u\|_4=1$. The preceding lower
bound and E05's upper bound give $Y_{f_\epsilon}([g])\to0$. When $k>0$
there is a nonempty constant annulus $2\rho<r<3\rho$ in each insertion
ball after the target correction, so E05 yields $Y_{f_\epsilon}<0$
for every $\epsilon$. The
$C^1$ convergence $\Phi_{b_\epsilon^{\rm bal}}\to\operatorname{id}$,
viewing target maps in $\mathbb R^5$, preserves strong $W^{1,2}$
convergence of $f_\epsilon$ to $F$.

Write $dv_h$ for the target's oriented volume form and $v_4$ for its
total volume. For $G_\epsilon$, the signed Jacobian measure equals
$F^*dv_h$ off the flattening balls, the bounded-derivative flattening
regions contribute $O(\rho^4)$ in total variation, and the E09 bubbles
converge as degree measures to $v_4\delta_{p_i}$. Also
$\int|G_\epsilon^*dv_h|\le\tfrac{1}{16}\int|dG_\epsilon|^4$ by the
singular-value arithmetic-geometric mean inequality, so the total
variation is uniformly bounded. The target volume factor $a_b^4$
changes these measures by $o(1)$ in total variation. Hence

\[
 f_\epsilon^*dv_h\rightharpoonup F^*dv_h+v_4\sum_{i=1}^k\delta_{p_i}.
\]

For $k>0$, the absolute Jacobian densities are not uniformly
integrable. Indeed one ball $B_{2\rho}(p_i)$ has volume tending to
zero and carries signed integral $v_4$, since each rebalance composed
with the individual bubble is still a degree-one map constant off
that ball. Its absolute integral is at least $v_4$.

## The zero supremum and its maximizing maps

[E14's assessed necessity](DIR_E14_20261124.md#spectral-convergence-and-the-limit)
starts with $Y_f\le s-3\lambda_1\le0$ for a balanced map at a supplied
unit-volume Yamabe minimizer. If $S_{\rm bal}=0$, the constant test
forces equality $s=3\lambda_1$ and the spectral gap above the first
eigenspace yields a smooth sphere-valued first eigenmap from a
maximizing sequence. The construction above proves the converse,
irrespective of the degree of that eigenmap. Equality of the supremum
therefore gives the stated eigenmap condition, with uniform integrability
of absolute Jacobians a separate premise for retaining degree in a
limit.

If a smooth balanced degree-one map $f$ attains zero, both bounds in
$0=Y_f\le Q_f(1)\le s-3\lambda_1\le0$ are equalities. Equality in
Poincaré for its five mean-zero coordinate functions puts each in the
first eigenspace or makes it zero. The sphere constraint gives
$|df|^2=\lambda_1$ everywhere. Then $s=3|df|^2$ and degree $+1$ make $f$
an orientation-preserving homothety by Listing. Conversely such a
homothety pulls back the round volume form up to a constant, so it
is balanced by round symmetry, and $s=3|df|^2$ gives $Y_f=0$ by
testing constants in the nonnegative form. This characterizes
attainment of the zero value on the supplied metric.

## Round orientation-reversal test and source scope

Take $M=S^4$ and $g=c h$ with $c=v_4^{-1/2}$ for volume one. The round
metric minimizes the Yamabe quotient in its conformal class. For this
benchmark one can see the minimization by stereographic pullback: the
four-dimensional scalar form becomes $6\int_{\mathbb R^4}|dv|^2$ with the
$L^4$ norm unchanged after multiplying a sphere test by the stereographic
factor. Smooth sphere tests give functions in the homogeneous Sobolev
space by cutoff. The constant sphere test becomes a multiple of
$(1+|x|^2)^{-1}$, which attains the Euclidean sharp inequality.
[Talenti, *Best constant in Sobolev inequality*, Ann. Mat. Pura Appl. **110**
(1976), pp. 353–372, opening theorem, (1)–(3), p. 353](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/talenti.sobolev.pdf).

Here $s=12/c$ and $\lambda_1=4/c$. The ambient linear coordinates
have eigenvalue $4/c$. The integrated Bochner identity with
$\operatorname{Ric}=3g/c$ and $|\nabla^2 u|^2\ge(\Delta u)^2/4$
gives the matching lower bound for any positive eigenvalue. The
restriction of a reflection of $\mathbb R^5$ is a balanced degree
$-1$ first eigenmap $F$. The two-bubble construction gives balanced
degree-$+1$ maps with $Y_{f_\epsilon}\to0$ and strong $W^{1,2}$ limit $F$.
Their signed Jacobian measure has diffuse part $-dv_h$ and two positive
atoms of mass $v_4$ each. Their absolute Jacobians fail uniform
integrability despite the critical $L^4$ derivative bound. The
identity map on the same input attains zero. A degree-zero first
eigenmap on any supplied nonround homotopy sphere remains a conditional
input to this proof.
