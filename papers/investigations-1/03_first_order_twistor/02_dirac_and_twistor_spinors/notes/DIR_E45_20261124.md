# SP4-FOT-DIR-E45 · Aubry’s smooth sphere theorem under an added Ricci bound

24 November 2026, America/New_York. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with
OpenAI Codex assistance. Fixed E routing in [request v1][request] was

Let \((M^4,g)\) be smooth, closed and connected, with a supplied
unit-volume Riemannian Yamabe-minimizing metric of constant scalar
curvature \(s>0\). Use the nonnegative scalar Laplacian
\(\Delta_g=d^*d\), with eigenvalues counted with multiplicity and
\(\lambda_0(g)=0\). Set \(\epsilon=3\lambda_5(g)/s-1\). Assume the
additional pointwise quadratic-form inequality
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_g\ \ge\ (1-\delta)\frac{s}{4}g,\qquad
0\le\delta<1.
\tag{1}
\]
If \(a_4>0\) is the threshold from Aubry’s Theorem 1 in dimension four,
a sufficient condition for \(M\) to be diffeomorphic to standard smooth
\(S^4\) is
\[
\frac{4(\epsilon+\delta)}{1-\delta}<a_4 .
\tag{2}
\]

## Primary theorem and eigenvalue indexing

Erwann Aubry, *Pincement sur le spectre et le volume en courbure de Ricci
positive*, *Annales scientifiques de l’École Normale Supérieure*,
4e série **38** (2005), no. 3, 387–405, DOI
`10.1016/j.ansens.2005.01.002`. The journal PDF on Numdam was
inspected on 24 November 2026. The introduction, printed p. 387,
specifies \(0=\lambda_0<\lambda_1\le\lambda_2\le\cdots\) counted
with multiplicity. On printed p. 388, Theorem 1 assumes a complete
Riemannian \(n\)-manifold with
\(\operatorname{Ric}\ge(n-1)g\). It gives positive dimension-dependent
constants \(a_n,C_n,\beta_n\) such that if the first \(n\) positive
eigenvalues are strictly below \(n+a_n\), then
\[
d_{\mathrm{GH}}\big((M,g),(S^n,h)\big)
\ \le\ C_n(\lambda_n(g)-n)^{\beta_n},
\]
with \(h\) unit round, and \(M\) is diffeomorphic to \(S^n\).
Here \(a_n\) denotes the paper’s \(\varepsilon(n)\). The source
gives the constants by existence in the theorem statement.
The dimension-four hypothesis uses \(\lambda_4\).
[Aubry, introduction and Theorem 1][aubry].

Printed pp. 401–402, §6 and Proposition 19, were also checked.
Under the paper’s Ricci hypothesis, for sufficiently small
\(r=\lambda_n-n\) they give
\(\lambda_{n+1}\le n+C(n)r^{1/2}\). The text combines that estimate
with Proposition 12 and the Cheeger–Colding differentiable
stability theorem cited in the introduction. Thus the proof’s
intermediate use of a fifth eigenvalue in dimension four requires
only the first four positive eigenvalues as independent inputs.
The erratum mentioned in the paper’s abstract and references is
Petersen’s. Aubry’s own Theorem 1 is the source used here.
[Aubry, Proposition 12 and §6][aubry].

## Scaling and threshold

Put \(b=(1-\delta)s/12>0\) and \(k=bg\). Constant scaling preserves
the Levi–Civita connection and the covariant Ricci tensor.
The inverse metric in the scalar Laplacian scales by \(b^{-1}\),
so
\[
\operatorname{Ric}_k=\operatorname{Ric}_g\ge 3k,\qquad
s_k=b^{-1}s=\frac{12}{1-\delta},\qquad
\lambda_i(k)=b^{-1}\lambda_i(g).
\]
The metric \(k\) is complete because it is smooth and \(M\) is closed.

The constant conformal factor minimizes the ordinary Yamabe
quotient in the supplied volume-one normalization. Its coefficient
at order \(t^2\) along \(1+t\phi\), for a real mean-zero first
eigenfunction \(\phi\), is
\( (6\lambda_1(g)-2s)\|\phi\|_{L^2(g)}^2\ge0\).
Thus \(\lambda_1(g)\ge s/3\), as in [E14][e14]. Monotonicity of the
eigenvalues and the definition of \(\epsilon\) yield
\[
\frac{4}{1-\delta}
\ \le\ \lambda_i(k)\
\le\ \frac{4(1+\epsilon)}{1-\delta}
\qquad(1\le i\le5).
\]
In particular \(\epsilon\ge0\) and
\(\lambda_4(k)-4\le 4(\epsilon+\delta)/(1-\delta)\).
Condition (2) therefore supplies the strict upper pinching
hypothesis of Aubry’s theorem. Equivalently,
\(\epsilon+(1+a_4/4)\delta<a_4/4\). A sufficient symmetric smallness
condition is \(\epsilon+\delta<a_4/(4+a_4)\). On the rescaled
manifold the quantitative conclusion gives
\[
d_{\mathrm{GH}}\big((M,k),(S^4,h)\big)
\ \le\ C_4\left(\frac{4(\epsilon+\delta)}{1-\delta}\right)^{\beta_4}.
\]
The differentiable conclusion transfers to \(M\) with its original
metric. The analogous distance estimate for \(g\) compares it to
\((S^4,b^{-1}h)\) and multiplies the displayed upper bound by
\(b^{-1/2}\), since distances scale by the square root of the
metric factor. [Aubry, Theorem 1][aubry].

## What the Ricci premise supplies

With \(m=s/4\), let \(r_1,\dots,r_4\) be the pointwise eigenvalues of
the \(g\)-self-adjoint Ricci endomorphism. Then
\(\sum_i r_i=4m\) and \(r_i\ge(1-\delta)m\).
The lower bound on the other three gives
\[
(1-\delta)m\le r_i\le (1+3\delta)m .
\]
In particular \(\delta=0\) forces \(\operatorname{Ric}_g=(s/4)g\);
for general \(\delta\), the operator norm of the trace-free Ricci
tensor relative to \(g\) is at most \(3\delta s/4\).
For the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, write
\(x_i=r_i-(1-\delta)m\ge0\), so \(\sum_i x_i=4\delta m\).
Then \(\sum_i (r_i-m)^2=\sum_i x_i^2-4\delta^2m^2
\le12\delta^2m^2\), giving the pointwise bound
\(|\operatorname{Ric}_{0,g}|_g\le\sqrt{3}\,\delta s/2\).

At exact \(\epsilon=0\), Yamabe stability gives
\(\lambda_1=\cdots=\lambda_5=s/3\). The [B20][b20] quartic argument
and [E38][e38] equality case, independently checked in [E39][e39],
give the unit-volume round metric directly, using five independent
first eigenfunctions under the stated minimizing hypothesis.
With positive pinching the comparison
above gives smooth recognition and metric-space closeness under
(1). The [B22][b22] weak Gram and joint-law tests concern a
different strength of geometric data. No implication from them
to the pointwise Ricci premise is used, and production of the
required \(g\) with small pinching on an arbitrary smooth homotopy
four-sphere remains an open premise.
