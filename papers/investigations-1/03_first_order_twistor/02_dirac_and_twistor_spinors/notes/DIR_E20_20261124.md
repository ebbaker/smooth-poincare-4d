Use closed connected smooth oriented Riemannian products, the nonnegative
scalar Laplacian $\Delta=d^*d$, its first positive eigenvalue $\lambda_1$,
and the outward-oriented unit target $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$. Its area
form $\omega$ integrates to $4\pi$. The round equatorial inclusion
$S^2\hookrightarrow S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ appends two zero coordinates.
For a source of volume $V$, scaling $g$ to $V^{-1/2}g$ gives volume one
and multiplies $s_g$, $\lambda_1$, and $|dF|^2$ by the same factor
$V^{1/2}$. All spectral comparisons below are made before this common
scaling. Both products are spin: $S^3\times S^1$ is parallelizable, and
each oriented $S^2$ has even Euler class and hence vanishing $w_2$.
There is no chirality dependence in the scalar computations.

## 1. Berger three-sphere times a short circle

On the unit $S^3\subset\mathbb C^2$ let $V_H(z)=iz$ generate the Hopf
action with period $2\pi$. Take the round horizontal metric on
$\ker\alpha$, where $\alpha=\operatorname{Im}(z^*dz)$, and assign
$V_H$ length $t>0$. Thus $g_t=g_{m hor}+t^2\alpha^2$. The exterior
circle is $S^1_L=\mathbb R/L\mathbb Z$ with circumference $L$ and
metric $dr^2$. Set
\[
 H(z)=\left(2\Re(\bar z_1z_2),,2\Im(\bar z_1z_2),\,
                   |z_1|^2-|z_2|^2\right),\qquad
 F(z,r)=H(z).
\]
The three components of $H$ are restrictions of Euclidean harmonic
homogeneous quadratics, so their round eigenvalue is $8$. They are
invariant under $V_H$. The co-metric changes only in the vertical
direction and $dV_{g_t}=t\,dV_{m round}$. Since $V_H$ preserves the
round volume, the scalar operator is
$\Delta_{g_t}=\Delta_{m round}-(t^{-2}-1)V_H^2$.
Thus $\Delta F=8F$ on the product as well.

Under the standard identification with $SU(2)$ that takes $z$ to the
matrix with columns $z$ and $(-\bar z_2,\bar z_1)^T$, $V_H$ is the
left-invariant field generated by $X_1=\operatorname{diag}(i,-i)$.
Lauret's orthonormal basis $\{aX_1,bX_2,cX_3\}$ gives
$g_t=g(t^{-1},1,1)$, with $g(1,1,1)$ the unit round metric. For $t\le1$,
Theorem 1.1 gives $\min(2+t^{-2},8)$. The full Berger formula in
(3.8) and Proposition 3.9 gives $2+t^{-2}$ in the $k=1$ modes for
every $t>0$, so $t>1$ cannot make $F$ first either. The product
spectrum consists of sums of factor eigenvalues, with the first
positive circle eigenvalue $(2\pi/L)^2$. Accordingly $F$ is a first
eigenmap precisely when $0<t\le1/\sqrt6$ and $0<L\le\pi/\sqrt2$.
[Lauret v3, eqs. (1.2) and (2.5), Theorem 1.1, eq. (3.8) and Proposition 3.9](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.04259).

The same parameter convention in Lauret's scalar formula gives
\[
 s_{g_t\oplus dr^2}
  =4(t^{-2}+2)-2(t^2+2t^{-2})=8-2t^2 .
\]
In particular take $t=1/3$ and $L=\pi/2$. The first circle eigenvalue is
$16$, and the values on the product are $\lambda_1=8$ and $s=70/9$.
Throughout the first-eigenmap range, $0<s<3\lambda_1=24$. The constant
conformal factor is a critical point and its Yamabe Hessian is positive
on all mean-zero directions by the second variation in E14. Global
Yamabe minimality at the explicit length $L=\pi/2$ is unverified here.
[Lauret v3, Proposition 2.3, eq. (2.8)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.04259#page=7) ·
[E14, Yamabe second variation](DIR_E14_20261124.md#the-yamabe-and-poincaré-inequalities).

The projection $z$ is a global lift. At $z=(1,0)$ use the real and
imaginary vectors in the $z_2$ direction. They give $d\alpha=2\,dx_2
\wedge dy_2$, and $dH$ multiplies both positively oriented horizontal
lengths by two. Both forms are invariant under $SU(2)$, with the
induced action on the target by rotations. On the Hopf circle
$\alpha(V_H)=1$ and $\iota_{V_H}d\alpha=0$ by invariance. On the
exterior circle both $\alpha$ and its derivative are pulled back
from the $S^3$ factor, so
$\alpha(\partial_r)=d\alpha(\partial_r,\cdot)=0$. Consequently
$d\alpha=F^*\omega/2$ on the whole product. In particular $F$ has
rank two everywhere, its nonzero singular values are $2,2$, and
$|dF|^2=8$.

For the connection estimate take orthonormal horizontal one-forms
$e^1,e^2$ oriented by $F^*\omega$ and set $e^3=t\alpha$, $e^4=dr$.
The product orientation uses this order. Then
$d\alpha=2e^1\wedge e^2$, $*d\alpha=2t\alpha\wedge dr$, and
$d(*d\alpha)=4t\,e^1\wedge e^2\wedge e^4$.
Since $d(e^1\wedge e^2)=0$, $d(*\alpha)=0$ as well. Thus
$d^*d\alpha=4t^2\alpha$ and $d^*\alpha=0$. This makes $\alpha$ a
coexact one-form of eigenvalue $4t^2$, giving
$\lambda_{m coex}^{(1)}\le4t^2=4/9$ at $t=1/3$.
Directly $|\alpha|^2=t^{-2}$ and $|d\alpha|^2=4$ in this metric.
The lift also satisfies $|dz|^2=2+t^{-2}=|dF|^2/4+|\alpha|^2$.
There is an additional harmonic direction $dr$. Rephasing the lift
by $e^{2\pi i k r/L}$ adds $(2\pi k/L)dr$ to $\alpha$ without
altering the curvature or co-closed condition. Here
$H^2(S^3\times S^1;\mathbb Z)=0$ but
$\pi_1(S^3\times S^1)=\mathbb Z$, so the homotopy-sphere
and $H^1=0$ premises fail.

### Global Yamabe minimization for sufficiently small $L$

At fixed $t=1/3$, there is also a nonexplicit short-length range in
the same product family where the product metric is Yamabe minimizing.
Let $d\mu_t=dV_{g_t}/\operatorname{Vol}(S^3,g_t)$ and
$d\mu_L=d\mu_t\,dr/L$. The three-dimensional spherical interpolation
inequality at $p=4$ is
$\|v\|_4^2-\|v\|_2^2\le (2/3)\int|dv|_{\rm round}^2d\mu_t$.
The round and Berger probability measures coincide and shortening the
Hopf direction increases the co-metric, so the same bound holds with
$g_t$ in the integrand.
[Dolbeault–Esteban–Kowalczyk–Loss, published §1, p. 99, eq. (1.1)](https://repositorio.uchile.cl/bitstream/handle/2250/126455/Sharp%20Interpolation.pdf?isAllowed=y&sequence=1).

For a smooth real $u$ on the product, let $v$ be its circle mean and
$w=u-v$. Both the squared $L^2(d\mu_L)$ norm and the Dirichlet energy
split as sums for $v$ and $w$. Moreover
\[
 \|v+w\|_4^2\le \|v\|_4^2+3\|w\|_4^2 .
\]
Indeed $\phi(\tau)=(\int (v+\tau w)^4d\mu_L)^{1/2}$ has
$\phi'(0)=0$ from the zero fiber mean of $w$, and
$\phi''(\tau)\le 6\|w\|_4^2$ by Hölder on its positive second
derivative term. If $v\ne0$, $v+\tau w$ cannot vanish identically
because its circle mean is $v$. The case $v=0$ satisfies the bound
directly.

For $0<L\le 1$ extend $w$ periodically in $r$ and multiply it by
$\chi(r/L)$ with $\chi$ supported in $(-1,2)$ and equal to one on
$[0,1]$. The result $W$ defines a smooth function on the fixed
$S^3_t\times S^1_4$ using the length-four circle, since its support
lies in $(-L,2L)$ of length at most three. The ordinary Sobolev
embedding for this fixed compact four-dimensional metric gives a
constant $C_t$ with
\[
 \left(\int |W|^4\,dV_{g_t}\,dr\right)^{1/2}
  \le C_t\int (|dW|^2+|W|^2)\,dV_{g_t}\,dr.
\]
The left side is at least $(V_t L)^{1/2}\|w\|_{4,\mu_L}^2$ with
$V_t=\operatorname{Vol}(S^3,g_t)$. The right side is at most a fixed
constant times
$V_t L\big(\int |dw|^2d\mu_L+(1+L^{-2})\int |w|^2d\mu_L\big)$:
the support covers three periods and the cutoff derivative is
$O(L^{-1})$. The circle Poincaré inequality gives
$\int|w|^2d\mu_L\le (L/(2\pi))^2\int|\partial_rw|^2d\mu_L$.
Absorbing $V_t^{1/2}$ into the fixed constant yields
\[
 \|w\|_{4,\mu_L}^2\le C'_t\sqrt L\int |dw|^2d\mu_L .
\]
Choosing $L$ sufficiently small to have $3C'_t\sqrt L\le2/3$
gives $\|u\|_4^2\le\|u\|_2^2+(2/3)\int|du|^2d\mu_L$.
The inequality extends to $H^1$ by smooth approximation. Since
$6>(2/3)s$, the normalized Yamabe numerator $6\int|du|^2d\mu_L+
s\|u\|_2^2$ is at least $s\|u\|_4^2$, strictly for nonconstant
$u$. Converting the quotient to the geometric measure multiplies it
by $(V_t L)^{1/2}$. Thus constants give the global minimum for
sufficiently small $L$, with the Hopf lift and first-eigenmap features
above. The estimate supplies no numerical threshold for $L$.

## 2. Product of round two-spheres

Let $g$ be the product on $S^2(a)\times S^2(b)$, with $a,b$ radii.
Write $F(x,y)=x/a$. The eigenvalues are
$k(k+1)/a^2+\ell(\ell+1)/b^2$, $k,\ell\ge0$, with the tensor
eigenspace dimension $(2k+1)(2\ell+1)$ for each pair. Coincident
eigenvalues add those dimensions. The coordinates of $F$ use
$(k,\ell)=(1,0)$ and are first exactly when $a\ge b$.
This gives the equality comparison
\[
 s=\frac{2}{a^2}+\frac{2}{b^2}=3\lambda_1
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad b=\frac{a}{\sqrt2}
 \qquad (a\ge b).
\]
At this choice $\lambda_1=2/a^2$ and $s=6/a^2$. The map is rank
two everywhere, with nonzero singular values $1/a,1/a$ and
$|dF|^2=\lambda_1$. One can take $a=1$ before normalization, or
$a=(8\pi^2)^{-1/4}$ for volume one.
[de Lima–Piccione–Zedda, §4.2, product and round-factor spectra](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.anihpc.2011.10.005.pdf#page=12).

Let $u=[\omega/(4\pi)]$ be the positive integral generator on the
target. For the Hopf bundle viewed as the unit circle bundle of
the tautological complex line, its Euler class is $-u$ in these
orientations. In local unit frames the connection is $d+i\alpha$
and its first Chern form is $(i/2\pi)i\,d\alpha$, giving this sign.
The pullback class restricts to $-u$ under the oriented
identification $S^2(a)\times\{y\}\simeq S^2$. It is minus the
first generator of $H^2(S^2\times S^2;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^2$.
Any smooth lift would give $d(z^*\alpha)=F^*\omega/2$ on the
product. The integral of its right side on this first factor
is $2\pi$, contradicting Stokes. This model has $\pi_1=0$
and Euler characteristic $4$, while the homotopy-sphere
condition would give $H^2=0$ and Euler characteristic $2$.

The equality $s=3\lambda_1$ gives a nonnegative conformal Hessian
with kernel the first eigenspace on mean-zero variations. Global
Yamabe-minimizing status is **unverified**. In the primary product
analysis of de Lima, Piccione and Zedda, their metric parameter is
$b^2/a^2$, so the desired case is $1/2$. It is the first
degeneracy and bifurcation instant in §4.2 for $n=2$ by
Corollary 4.8 and the displayed $\lambda_i(n)$ formula. The
uniqueness result in Proposition 4.9 covers an open subset
of $(1/2,2)$ containing $1$. Neither result supplies global
minimality at $1/2$.
[de Lima–Piccione–Zedda, Corollary 4.8 and §4.2, Proposition 4.9](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1016/j.anihpc.2011.10.005.pdf#page=12).

## Consequence for B12

Both maps are balanced by integration of their first-eigenfunction
equations. Viewed as maps to $S^4$, they have identically zero
four-dimensional Jacobian and degree zero. The Berger example
calibrates the lift and curvature coefficient with $s<3\lambda_1$
and nontrivial fundamental group. The two-sphere product calibrates
$s=3\lambda_1$ and rank two with a nontrivial Hopf pullback class.
Both ordinary Yamabe constants are positive by $s>0$ and the
Sobolev inequality on each fixed product. The modified Yamabe
potential defect $s-3|dF|^2$ is strictly negative on the first
model and vanishes on the second. On the second, the modified
quotient has minimum zero at constants regardless of the ordinary
Yamabe-minimizing status. Each example's non-spherical topology is
already visible in its fundamental group or integral cohomology.
Their rank-two maps supply no smooth recognition implication and
give no example satisfying all B12 premises. The two source/target
scale conventions agree with those premises.
