## Domain and zeros

Let $M$ be a connected, smooth, closed, oriented Riemannian spin
four-manifold with smooth positive-definite metric $g$. Use the half-spin
bundle $S_g^+$ on which real self-dual two-forms act, the Levi–Civita spin
connection and $X\cdot_g X=-|X|_g^2$. Let
$P^g_X\psi=\nabla^g_X\psi+\frac14 X\cdot_g D_g\psi$ act on smooth positive
half-spinors. The extended domain $\mathcal A_g$ consists of smooth
$\psi\not\equiv0$ with all zeros transverse, allowing an empty zero set.
Each transverse zero is isolated since $S_g^+$ has real rank four.
Compactness and closedness of $Z(\psi)$ give finitely many zeros. Set
\[
J_g(\psi)=
\left(\int_{M\setminus Z(\psi)}
\frac{|P_g\psi|_g^4}{|\psi|_g^4}\,dV_g\right)^{1/4}
\in[0,+\infty].
\]

Near a transverse zero $p$, the real derivative is invertible, so in a
smooth local trivialization $|\psi|_g$ is bounded above and below by
positive multiples of $r=d_g(p,\cdot)$ on a sufficiently small ball.
If $P_g\psi(p)\ne0$, its norm has a positive lower bound on a smaller
ball, and the fourth power of the ratio has a lower bound proportional
to $r^{-4}$. The four-dimensional volume has radial order $r^3dr$, so the
integral diverges logarithmically. If $P_g\psi(p)=0$, smoothness gives
$|P_g\psi|_g=O(r)$, and the ratio is bounded near $p$. Off small
neighborhoods of the zeros the ratio is bounded by compactness. Thus
finite cost is equivalent on $\mathcal A_g$ to $P_g\psi(p)=0$ at every
zero, or equivalently to the B06 first jet
$\nabla^g_X\psi(p)=X\cdot_g a_p$ with
$a_p=-D_g\psi(p)/4\ne0$. The same results below apply if one restricts
the domain to those jets from the outset.

## Conformal invariance

For smooth $f>0$ and $h=f^2g$, let $\beta$ be the fiberwise unitary
spin identification induced by sending an oriented $g$-orthonormal
frame $(e_i)$ to the $h$-orthonormal frame $(f^{-1}e_i)$. It preserves
chirality, with $X\cdot_h\beta\eta=f\beta(X\cdot_g\eta)$.
Friedrich's equation (1.1) and the conformal paragraph spanning pp. 59–60
give the operator convention and kernel weight $\lambda^{1/4}$ for
$\bar g=\lambda g$. With $\lambda=f^2$ this is $f^{1/2}$.
[Friedrich, published copy, §1](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/701462/WSGP_09-1989-1_7.pdf#page=2)

Here is the off-kernel transformation for the covector-valued operator.
Write $u=\log f$, $U=\operatorname{grad}_g u$ and $\psi_h=f^{1/2}\beta\psi$.
The Levi–Civita conformal formula gives the change of oriented orthonormal
connection forms
$\omega^h_{ij}(X)-\omega^g_{ij}(X)=e_i(u)g(X,e_j)-g(X,e_i)e_j(u)$
in the paired frames, with $\omega^g_{ij}(X)=g(\nabla_X^g e_i,e_j)$.
The spin lift multiplies this skew array against $e_i\cdot_g e_j$ and
sums with factor $1/4$, giving
$-\frac12 X\cdot_g U\cdot_g-\frac12 X(u)$.
The last term cancels the derivative of $f^{1/2}$. Taking the Clifford
trace in the $h$-frame gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
\nabla_X^h\psi_h
 &= f^{1/2}\beta\big(\nabla_X^g\psi-\tfrac12 X\cdot_g U\cdot_g\psi\big),\\
D_h\psi_h
 &= f^{-1/2}\beta\big(D_g\psi+2U\cdot_g\psi\big),\\
(P_h\psi_h)(X)
 &= f^{1/2}\beta\big((P_g\psi)(X)\big).
\end{aligned}
\]
In the respective orthonormal frames this gives
$|P_h\psi_h|_h=f^{-1/2}|P_g\psi|_g$ and
$|\psi_h|_h=f^{1/2}|\psi|_g$. The ratio scales by $f^{-1}$ and its
fourth power cancels $dV_h=f^4dV_g$. The map $\psi\mapsto\psi_h$ is
invertible, preserves the zero set and multiplies the derivative at
a zero by the fiberwise isomorphism $f^{1/2}\beta$. The same transformation
preserves $P\psi(p)=0$ and therefore the finite-cost domain. Consequently
$J_h(\psi_h)=J_g(\psi)$ as extended real numbers.

Constant nonzero complex multiples of a spinor leave $J_g$ unchanged.
One may impose $\|\psi\|_{L^2(g)}=1$ using the reference metric, or
renormalize after conformal transport. When $h=c^2g$ for a constant
$c>0$, the ratio has scale $c^{-1}$ and the volume scale is $c^4$.

## Elliptic estimate and best reference constant

Use a fixed smooth representative $g$ and define its chiral kernel
$K_g=\ker(P_g:H^1(S_g^+)\to L^2(T^*M\otimes S_g^+))$.
The principal symbol of $P_g$, omitting the common factor $\mathrm{i}$,
projects $\xi\otimes v$ orthogonally to the kernel of Clifford
contraction. For $c(\alpha\otimes v)=\alpha^\sharp\cdot_g v$,
$cc^*=4I$ and $|c(\xi\otimes v)|^2=|\xi|_g^2|v|_g^2$.
Thus $|\sigma(P_g)(\xi)v|^2=\frac34|\xi|_g^2|v|_g^2$ and
$P_g^*P_g$ is elliptic. Its integrated Gårding estimate and density give
\[
\|v\|_{H^1(g)}\le A_g\big(\|P_gv\|_{L^2(g)}+\|v\|_{L^2(g)}\big).
\]
Elliptic regularity for $P_g^*P_g$ gives $K_g\subset C^\infty$.
Suppose $K_g=0$. Failure of an $L^2$ inverse bound would give
$\|v_j\|_2=1$ and $\|P_gv_j\|_2\to0$. The estimate bounds $v_j$ in
$H^1$. Rellich gives a subsequence strongly convergent in $L^2$
and weakly convergent in $H^1$ to a spinor of $L^2$ norm one.
Continuity of $P_g:H^1\to L^2$ puts the limit in $K_g$, a contradiction.
Substituting the $L^2$ inverse bound into the elliptic estimate yields
$\|v\|_{H^1(g)}\le B_g\|P_gv\|_{L^2(g)}$.

The continuous critical embedding $H^1\hookrightarrow L^4$ on a
compact smooth four-manifold gives an embedding constant $S_g$.
This is the scalar local four-dimensional Sobolev inequality applied
componentwise in finitely many bundle charts with a partition of unity.
The compactness step uses the embedding into $L^2$. Define the best
reference constant
\[
C_g=\sup_{0\ne v\in H^1(S_g^+)}
\frac{\|v\|_{L^4(g)}}{\|P_gv\|_{L^2(g)}}\in(0,S_g B_g].
\]
For finite-cost $\psi\in\mathcal A_g$ extend $r_\psi=|P_g\psi|_g/|\psi|_g$
by zero on $Z(\psi)$. Since this is a measure-zero set,
$|P_g\psi|_g=r_\psi|\psi|_g$ almost everywhere. Hölder gives
\[
\|P_g\psi\|_{L^2(g)}\le J_g(\psi)\|\psi\|_{L^4(g)},\qquad
J_g(\psi)\ge C_g^{-1}.
\]
The bound also holds for infinite cost. The inverse of $C_g$ is the
optimal constant of the intermediate $L^4$–$L^2$ inequality, so this
argument supplies a lower bound for $J_g$. By conformal transport the
same $C_g^{-1}$ applies to every admissible spinor on every smooth
representative of $[g]$.

## Zero infimum and uniformity across classes

For a nontrivial solution in $K_g$, Friedrich's prolongation makes
$(\psi,D_g\psi)$ a parallel pair. Connectedness and uniqueness of parallel
transport give $D_g\psi(p)\ne0$ at a zero. At that point the twistor
equation gives $\nabla_X^g\psi(p)=-\frac14 X\cdot_g D_g\psi(p)$.
Its norm on $X$ is $|X|_g|D_g\psi(p)|_g/4$, so the real derivative is
an isomorphism between two real four-dimensional spaces. This verifies
the admissible transversality and the prescribed jet. Any such solution
attains $J_g=0$. Conversely, the fixed-reference gap excludes zero
infimum when $K_g=0$. A spinor attaining zero satisfies $P_g\psi=0$
everywhere by smoothness and density of the complement of its zeros.
Thus the infimum over the whole class is zero exactly when $K_g\ne0$,
and is then attained. The convention for an empty admissible domain
is $\inf\varnothing=+\infty$.
[Friedrich, §2, equation (2.2), Propositions 1–2](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/701462/WSGP_09-1989-1_7.pdf#page=4)

For a family of kernel-free reference representatives a common
positive gap follows if $\sup_g C_g<\infty$. Sufficient control is
uniform $S_g$ and $B_g$, or uniform Sobolev and elliptic estimate
constants together with a uniform $L^2$ inverse bound for $P_g$.
For example, a compact smooth family on a fixed spin manifold
consisting of kernel-free metrics has such control. After smooth
pointwise spin bundle and tensor identifications, nearby metric norms
are uniformly equivalent and $P_g$ is continuous in operator norm
$H^1\to L^2$. The inverse estimate at one metric absorbs the operator
difference on a small neighborhood. A finite cover provides uniform
inverse and Sobolev constants. This argument requires every limit in
the compact family to be kernel-free.
