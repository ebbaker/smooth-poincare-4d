Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian homotopy
four-sphere with its spin structure, let $h$ be the unit round metric on
the oriented $S^4$, and fix a smooth $f:M\to S^4$ of degree $+1$. Write
$V_{g,f}=3|df|_{g,h}^2=3\operatorname{tr}_g(f^*h)$ and
$L_{g,f}=6\Delta_g+s_g-V_{g,f}$, where $\Delta_g=d^*d\ge0$. Then

\[
 \lambda_1(L_{g,f})\le 0,\qquad
 \lambda_1(L_{g,f})=0\ \Longleftrightarrow\
 f\text{ is a diffeomorphism and }g=w^2 f^*h
 \text{ for a smooth }w>0.
\]

The pointwise inequality $s_g\ge V_{g,f}$ holds exactly in the subclass
with $w$ constant, for which $s_g=V_{g,f}$ everywhere.

## Degree and twisted index

Choose an oriented embedded closed coordinate ball inside a chart. In
polar coordinates $(r,\omega)$ on this ball, map to
$(\sin\theta(r)\,\omega,\cos\theta(r))\in S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$.
Take $\theta=r$ near zero, smoothly increasing with positive derivative
until it reaches $\pi$, and constant $\pi$ on a collar. Extend by the
south pole outside the ball. Smoothness at zero follows from smoothness
of the even radial functions $\sin(r)/r$ and $\cos(r)$. The collar gives
smoothness at the ball boundary. Orient the chart against the target
orientation near the north pole so that a regular value there has one
positive preimage. This supplies the stated degree on every $M$ before
making any metric choice.

Use $c(X)^2=-|X|^2$ and $S^+$ on which self-dual two-forms act. Write
$E^\pm=f^*S_h^\pm$, with the pulled-back target spin connections, and let
$D^{E^\pm,+}:\Gamma(S_g^+\otimes E^\pm)\to\Gamma(S_g^-\otimes E^\pm)$
be the domain-chiral operators. Both tangent bundles are spin, so the
spinor bundle for $TM\oplus f^*TS^4$ identifies with $S_g\otimes f^*S_h$.
The domain Clifford action acts in the first factor. Each separate target
chirality is parallel. The product grading has positive subbundle
$(S_g^+\otimes E^+)\oplus(S_g^-\otimes E^-)$ and the two opposite
combinations form its negative subbundle. Listing uses this grading in
[§2, eq. (7), and §3, PDF pp. 5 and 11](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=11).

For the signs, in an oriented orthonormal frame the complex volume
operator is $-c_1c_2c_3c_4$, with eigenvalue $+1$ on $S^+$. Indeed,
$c(e^{12}+e^{34})=c_1c_2(1-c_1c_2c_3c_4)$. With formal Euler roots
$x_1,x_2$ of the oriented two-planes, the two circle rotations act by the
weights $\pm(x_1+x_2)/2$ on $S^+$ and $\pm(x_1-x_2)/2$ on $S^-$.
Expanding the exponential characters yields
$\operatorname{ch}_{[4]}(S^\pm)=(p_1\pm 2e)/4$. On $TS^4$, $p_1=0$
by stable triviality and $\langle e,[S^4]\rangle=2$. On $M$,
$\langle\widehat A_{[4]}(TM),[M]\rangle=-\tau(M)/8=0$
by the four-dimensional signature formula. The chiral Dirac index is
therefore

\[
 \operatorname{ind}D^{E^\pm,+}
 =\langle\widehat A(TM)\operatorname{ch}(E^\pm),[M]\rangle
 =\pm\deg f=\pm1.
\]

The product-graded index is their difference, $2$, in agreement with
Listing's $\chi(S^4)\deg f$ formula in [§3, PDF p. 11, and the Euler-class
version in §4, PDF p. 14](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=14).
Thus there is a smooth nonzero kernel element in $S_g^+\otimes E^+$ and
one in $S_g^-\otimes E^-$, using the adjoint domain-chiral operator in
the latter case. These conclusions hold for every smooth $g$ and every
smooth degree-one $f$. Normalize a nonzero twisted spinor by its
$L^2(g)$ norm if desired. The index guarantees neither a pointwise
nonzero spinor nor pointwise control of $df$ or $s_g$.

On a constant patch of $f$ the twist is flat, and a constant target-spinor
frame gives local ordinary harmonic spinor equations for the components.
The pullback connection on all of $M$ still enters the global equation.
The untwisted chiral index is zero here, and the untwisted Dirac kernel
vanishes for metrics with $s_g>0$ by the spin Lichnerowicz identity.
The ordinary twistor equation involves a different operator on $S_g^+$.
See [the branch's untwisted comparison](../../brainstorm/notes/DIRAC_AND_PROOF_ARCHITECTURE_20261124.md#1-three-different-uses-of-dirac-operators).

## Listing's scalar bound and pointwise equality

Let $\sigma_1,\ldots,\sigma_4\ge0$ be the singular values of $df$ in
the metrics $g,h$. Put $a_i=\sigma_i^2$, $T=\sum_i a_i$ and
$U=\sum_{i<j}a_i a_j$. The wedge basis $e_i\wedge e_j$ with $i<j$ is
orthonormal, so $U=\operatorname{tr}_g((f^*h)^{\Lambda^2})$ and $T=|df|^2$.
Listing's cutoff in Theorem 3, (6), is $b=\sqrt{24U}$. Indeed

\[
 U=\frac{T^2-\sum_i a_i^2}{2}\le\frac38T^2,\qquad b\le 3T=V_{g,f}.
\]

Equality in the algebraic comparison occurs precisely when all $a_i$
coincide, including the all-zero case. At a regular point where
$f^*h=\alpha g$ for $\alpha>0$, $T=4\alpha$, $U=6\alpha^2$ and
$b=V_{g,f}=12\alpha$. Listing's threshold is sharp on this model.
The theorem applies to smooth maps from closed spin manifolds to the
unit sphere of equal even dimension at least four, with nonzero degree
and $s_g\ge b$ everywhere. It makes $f$ a homothety. Here the given
degree specifies an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. If
$f^*h=\alpha g$ with $\alpha>0$ constant, then $s_g=12\alpha=V_{g,f}$.
[Theorem 3, eq. (6), PDF p. 4, and the trace identity (15), PDF p. 7](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=4).

The Bochner normalization can also be checked before using rigidity.
Listing writes $(D^{f^*S_h})^2=\nabla^*\nabla+s_g/4+\mathcal R_f$,
where $\mathcal R_f=\sum_{i<j}c(e_i)c(e_j)\otimes R^{f^*S_h}_{e_i,e_j}$.
The unit-sphere curvature term in Proposition 2, (11), is
$\mathcal R_f\ge-\tfrac12\sqrt{6U}\,I=-bI/4$. It vanishes at every
point where $\operatorname{rank}(df)\le1$. Inserting a nonzero twisted
kernel element into (21) gives

\[
 0\ge\int_M\Bigl(|\nabla\Psi|^2+\tfrac14(s_g-b)|\Psi|^2\Bigr)\,dV_g.
\]

With $s_g\ge V_{g,f}\ge b$, both terms are nonnegative. Thus $\Psi$ is
parallel of positive constant norm and $s_g=V_{g,f}=b$ everywhere.
A normalized such spinor has norm $\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)^{-1/2}$.
The index, Bochner formula and curvature bound here are exactly those
for the pulled-back target connection. [Listing, eqs. (8), (11), (12),
and (21)–(22), PDF pp. 5–7 and 12–13](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=12).

For an independent check of the global step for the stronger bound
$s_g\ge V_{g,f}$, the equal singular values give $f^*h=\alpha g$ with
$\alpha=T/4$ smooth and nonnegative. Where $\alpha>0$ the pulled-back
metric has scalar curvature $12$. Since $s_g=12\alpha$, the conformal
scalar formula gives $\Delta_g\sqrt\alpha=0$ on that open set. Therefore
$2\alpha\Delta_g\alpha+|d\alpha|^2=0$ there, and the latter identity
holds on its complement because $\alpha$ and $d\alpha$ vanish there.
Integration yields $3\int_M|d\alpha|^2\,dV_g=0$. Degree $+1$ ensures
$\alpha>0$ at some point. Hence $\alpha$ is a positive constant, $f$ is
a local diffeomorphism everywhere, and compactness makes it a covering
of the simply connected target. This argument does not require a priori
smoothness of $\sqrt\alpha$ at a zero.

## Spectral sign and complete equality case

Since $V_{g,f}$ is smooth even where $df$ drops rank, $L_{g,f}$ has a
smooth strictly positive lowest eigenfunction $u$ with real eigenvalue
$\lambda=\lambda_1(L_{g,f})$. This is the scalar variational ground
state on a connected closed manifold. The Rayleigh minimizer can be
taken nonnegative. Elliptic regularity and the Harnack inequality give
smoothness and strict positivity. For $\tilde g=u^2 g$ the four-dimensional
scalar law and inverse-metric scaling give

\[
 s_{\tilde g}=u^{-3}(6\Delta_g u+s_g u),\qquad
 V_{\tilde g,f}=u^{-2}V_{g,f},\qquad
 s_{\tilde g}-V_{\tilde g,f}=\lambda u^{-2}.
\]

The sign of the conformal Laplace term follows by writing $u=e^\varphi$
and using $s_{u^2g}=u^{-2}(s_g+6\Delta_g\varphi-6|d\varphi|_g^2)$.
If $\lambda\ge0$, the pointwise comparison applies to $(\tilde g,f)$.
It gives $\tilde g=c f^*h$ for a constant $c>0$ with pointwise equality.
Consequently $\lambda=0$ and $g=w^2 f^*h$ with $w=\sqrt c/u>0$.

Conversely, if $f$ is a diffeomorphism and $g=w^2 f^*h$, the same
law with $u=w^{-1}$ gives $L_{g,f}u=0$ since $u^2g=f^*h$. The first
eigenfunction can be chosen positive, so its inner product with $u$ is
strictly positive. Self-adjointness implies that its eigenvalue is
zero. Thus for every other supplied degree-one pair the eigenvalue is
strictly negative. In particular, a fixed degree-one map with a
nonempty constant patch has $\lambda_1<0$ for every smooth metric on
its domain, including on standard $S^4$.

For the unit round pair $(h,\operatorname{id})$, $s_h=V_{h,\operatorname{id}}=12$
and $L_{h,\operatorname{id}}=6\Delta_h$ with constant ground state.
For a conformal round pullback pair $g=w^2 k$ with $k=f^*h$ and $f$ an
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism, the ground state is a positive
multiple of $w^{-1}$, and

\[
 s_g-V_{g,f}=6w^{-3}\Delta_k w.
\]

If $w$ is nonconstant, this deficit takes both signs since $\int_M\Delta_k w\,dV_k=0$
and the only harmonic functions on a connected closed manifold are
constant. Hence spectral equality can occur for a supplied metric that
fails the pointwise inequality. Equivalently, spectral equality says
that the pointwise inequality can be realized somewhere in the
supplied conformal class for the same $f$. For $\lambda_1<0$ the
eigenfunction instead gives a conformal representative with strictly
negative deficit everywhere.

The universal kernel is an unconditional topological input. The
nonnegative spectral test is a rigidity criterion whose realization
requires an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism. It supplies no
construction of such a map from the smooth degree-one collapse.
