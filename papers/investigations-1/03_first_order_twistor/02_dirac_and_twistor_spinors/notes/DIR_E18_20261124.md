## Statement

Let $(M,g)$ be a smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian four-manifold
with volume one, $H^1(M;\mathbb R)=0$ and $H^2(M;\mathbb Z)=0$. In particular
these cohomological hypotheses hold on a smooth homotopy four-sphere.
Suppose $F:M\to S^2$ is smooth, the target has unit radius, and
$\Delta F=\lambda F$ componentwise for $\lambda=\lambda_1(g)>0$ and
$\Delta=d^*d$ on functions. Integration gives $\int_M F\,dV_g=0$.
The product rule for $\Delta |F|^2=0$ gives $|dF|^2=\lambda$.
Then the first coexact one-form Hodge eigenvalue satisfies
\[
 \lambda_{\mathrm{coex}}^{(1)}
 \le \frac{\lambda}{16(3/4-|m|^2)} < \frac{\lambda}{4},
 \qquad m=\int_M z\,dV_g ,
\]
where $z:M\to S^3$ is the Coulomb lift below and $|m|$ is independent
of its constant phase. The metric criterion
$\lambda_{\mathrm{coex}}^{(1)}\ge\lambda/4$ therefore excludes this
target for a first eigenmap.

## Hopf normalization and the Coulomb lift

Use the phase action $z\mapsto e^{it}z$ on the unit $S^3\subset\mathbb C^2$.
Fix the outward orientation on the unit $S^2\subset\mathbb R^3$, its area
form $\omega$, and
\[
 H(z)=(2\operatorname{Re}(\overline z_1z_2),
       2\operatorname{Im}(\overline z_1z_2), |z_1|^2-|z_2|^2),
 \qquad \alpha=\operatorname{Im}(z^*dz).
\]
For $w\in T_zS^3$, the vertical component is $\alpha(w)iz$ and the
horizontal space is $z^*w=0$. At $z=(1,0)$ on a horizontal vector
$(0,w_2)$, the differential is $(2\operatorname{Re}w_2,
2\operatorname{Im}w_2,0)$. Moreover,
$d\alpha=2\sum_{j=1}^2 dx_j\wedge dy_j$ restricted to $S^3$ for
$z_j=x_j+iy_j$. On the horizontal basis $(0,1),(0,i)$ it equals $2$,
while $H^*\omega$ equals $4$. Both two-forms annihilate the vertical
direction. The identities hold throughout $S^3$ by transitivity of
$SU(2)$, which preserves $\alpha$ and acts on the target by rotations
via its conjugation action on traceless Hermitian matrices. Thus for a
lift $H\circ z=F$, with $a=z^*\alpha$ denoting pullback of the one-form,
\[
 |dz|^2=\tfrac14|dF|^2+|a|^2,\qquad
 da=\tfrac12 F^*\omega.
\]
Reversing the target orientation changes the sign in the curvature
formula relative to its oriented area form.

The pullback circle bundle is the unit-circle bundle of the pulled-back
tautological complex line over $\mathbb{CP}^1$. Its first Chern class
vanishes in $H^2(M;\mathbb Z)$, so the line bundle is topologically
trivial, using classification by $c_1$ on a space of CW type. A continuous
unit section can be approximated uniformly in the ambient trivial
$\mathbb C^2$ by a smooth map. Orthogonal projection to the pulled-back
smooth line and normalization give a smooth unit section $z_0$.
[Hatcher, *Vector Bundles and K-Theory*, v2.2 (November 2017), §3.1, Prop. 3.10, p. 86](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/VBKT/VB.pdf).

Writing $a_0=z_0^*\alpha$, set $z=e^{i\phi}z_0$ with the real smooth
solution of $\Delta\phi=-d^*a_0$ of mean zero. The right side is
orthogonal to constants by adjointness, so the scalar Poisson equation
has such a solution on the closed connected domain. The gauge law
$a=a_0+d\phi$ gives $d^*a=0$. Two Coulomb primitives differ by a closed
and co-closed one-form, which vanishes since $H^1(M;\mathbb R)=0$.
In the Hodge decomposition this $a$ is coexact. Any two lifts differ
by a smooth phase $u:M\to S^1$, whose connection increment is
$-i u^{-1}du$. If both lifts are Coulomb, the increment vanishes by
uniqueness of the primitive, so $u$ is constant.
[Dyatlov, *Lecture notes for 18.155*, 10 December 2022, §17.3.4, Thms. 17.15 and 17.17, pp. 250–252](https://math.mit.edu/~dyatlov/18.155/155-notes.pdf).

## Balance and strictness of the lift mean bound

With $P=zz^*$ the Hermitian rank-one projector, the Hopf convention gives
\[
 P=\tfrac12
 \begin{pmatrix}1+F_3&F_1-iF_2\\F_1+iF_2&1-F_3\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad \int_M P\,dV_g=I/2 .
\]
For every constant unit vector $v\in\mathbb C^2$,
\[
 \int_M |v^*z-v^*m|^2\,dV_g=\tfrac12-|v^*m|^2 .
\]
For $m\ne0$ use $v=m/|m|$, obtaining $|m|^2\le1/2$.
Equality would give $v^*z=v^*m$ everywhere by smoothness, hence
$2v^*Pv-1=0$. Since $2v^*Pv-1=H(v)\cdot F$, the image would lie
in a great circle.

Independently, any smooth great-circle-valued eigenmap on a closed
simply connected domain can be expressed in that plane as $e^{i\theta}$
for a global smooth real function $\theta$. More generally
$H^1(M;\mathbb R)=0$ suffices because for the circle-valued coordinate
$f$ the closed real form $-if^{-1}df$ is then exact and a constant
phase can be absorbed in $\theta$. The eigenvalue equation reads
\[
 \lambda= e^{-i\theta}\Delta(e^{i\theta})
         = |d\theta|^2+i\Delta\theta .
\]
Its imaginary part gives $\Delta\theta=0$, so $\theta$ is constant
by integration by parts. The real part contradicts $\lambda>0$.
This argument excludes great-circle maps before any estimate on
coexact forms. In particular the mean bound is strict.

## Energy, curvature and equality

The scalar Poincaré inequality for the four real components of the
Coulomb lift gives
\[
 \tfrac{\lambda}{4}+\|a\|_2^2
  =\|dz\|_2^2\ge \lambda(1-|m|^2),\qquad
 \|a\|_2^2\ge\lambda(3/4-|m|^2)>\tfrac{\lambda}{4}.
\]
At any point let $\sigma_1,\sigma_2$ be the two singular values of
$dF$ with zero included at rank drop. The norm on two-forms is the
one for which wedges of distinct orthonormal one-forms are unit.
Then
\[
 |F^*\omega|=\sigma_1\sigma_2
 \le \tfrac12(\sigma_1^2+\sigma_2^2)=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}.
\]
Equality holds exactly at $\sigma_1=\sigma_2$, which here requires
rank two and both singular values equal to $\sqrt{\lambda/2}$.
Consequently $\|da\|_2^2=\|F^*\omega\|_2^2/4\le\lambda^2/16$.
Since $a$ is coexact and nonzero, the Hodge Rayleigh quotient is
$\|da\|_2^2/\|a\|_2^2$ and bounds $\lambda_{\mathrm{coex}}^{(1)}$
from above. This proves the claimed strict comparison. Equality in
the preliminary comparison with $\lambda/4$ would require equality
in all three estimates, including $|m|^2=1/2$. Equality in the
curvature estimate alone would require equal singular values
everywhere, by continuity. Equality in the Hodge step alone would
require $a$ to lie in the first coexact one-form eigenspace.

Equality in the scalar Poincaré step would give
$\Delta z=\lambda(z-m)$. In fact, under the Coulomb condition this
can only happen with $m=0$. To see this, use the local product rule
$0=d^*a=\operatorname{Im}(z^*\Delta z)$, since the quadratic derivative
term is real. If $m\ne0$, then $u=(m/|m|)^*z$ is real and
$\Delta u=\lambda(u-|m|)$. The function $2u^2-1=H(m/|m|)\cdot F$
also satisfies the $\lambda$-eigenvalue equation. Subtracting the
two expressions for $\Delta(2u^2-1)$ gives
\[
 |du|^2=\tfrac{\lambda}{2}
   \big((u-|m|)^2+\tfrac12-|m|^2\big)>0.
\]
This contradicts the existence of a maximum on $M$. In the case
$m=0$ the stated mean-dependent estimate already reads
$\lambda_{\mathrm{coex}}^{(1)}\le\lambda/12$.

At the supplied Yamabe spectral equality $s_g=3\lambda$, the same
strict comparison reads $\lambda_{\mathrm{coex}}^{(1)}<s_g/12$,
and the pointwise area bound is $|F^*\omega|\le s_g/6$.
The Hopf estimate uses only the fixed eigenmap and the given topology.
At the Yamabe endpoint an independent lower Hodge bound of $s_g/12$
would exclude the great-$S^2$ alternative. The availability of such
a lower bound on the supplied metrics remains a separate premise.
