## Supplied geometry and stress tensors

Let $(M^4,g)$ be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented Riemannian
homotopy four-sphere with supplied smooth unit-volume metric. Use
$\Delta_g=d^*d$ and $\lambda=\lambda_1(g)>0$. Assume $s=3\lambda$
and let $F:M\to S^4\subset\mathbb R^5$ be smooth with
$\Delta_g F=\lambda F$ and $|dF|^2=\lambda$. The round target metric $h$
has sectional curvature one. These assumptions include maps whose
image lies in a lower-dimensional great sphere. The variation below
uses the supplied identities and leaves $F$ fixed.

Set $A=F^*h$, $H=A-\lambda g/4$ and
$T=\operatorname{Ric}-3A=\operatorname{Ric}_0-3H$. For a symmetric tensor
$K$ write $(\operatorname{div}K)_j=\nabla^i K_{ij}$, and use the $g$-norm
for domain tensors. Since $\operatorname{tr}_g A=\lambda$, both $H$ and
$T$ have zero trace. Let $F_i=dF(e_i)$ in a normal orthonormal frame.
The round target has tension field $\tau(F)=-\Delta_g F+|dF|^2F=0$.
Using symmetry of $\nabla dF$ in its domain entries gives

\[
(\operatorname{div}A)_j
= h(\tau(F),F_j)+\sum_i h(F_i,(\nabla dF)_{ji})
= h(\tau(F),F_j)+\tfrac12\nabla_j|dF|^2=0.
\]

The contracted Bianchi identity gives $\operatorname{div}\operatorname{Ric}=\tfrac12 ds=0$.
Thus $\operatorname{div}H=\operatorname{div}T=0$, and both tensors are
transverse-traceless.

## First variation at the constant ground state

Consider $g_t=g+tK$ for any fixed smooth symmetric $K$ and sufficiently
small $|t|$, and write $L_t=6\Delta_{g_t}+U_t$, where
$U_t=s_{g_t}-3|dF|^2_{g_t,h}$. Let $\mu(t)$ be the lowest eigenvalue.
At $t=0$, $U_0=0$ and $L_0$ has simple ground state $1$ of eigenvalue
zero because $M$ is connected. A smooth eigenpair through this state
follows from the implicit function theorem on Hölder spaces, normalizing
$\int_M u_t\,dV_g=1$. For $0<\alpha<1$, the derivative of the eigenpair
equations in $C^{2,\alpha}(M)\times\mathbb R$ is

\[
(v,b)\longmapsto \left(6\Delta_g v-b,\,\int_M v\,dV_g\right)
\in C^{0,\alpha}(M)\times\mathbb R.
\]

It is invertible by solving the zero-mean Poisson equation after choosing
$b$ by integrating the first component. The resulting $u_t$ is strictly
positive for small $|t|$ by continuity. Testing the equation for $u_t$
against $v^2/u_t$ for smooth real $v$ gives

\[
\int_M \bigl(6|dv|_{g_t}^2+(U_t-\mu(t))v^2\bigr)dV_{g_t}
=6\int_M u_t^2|d(v/u_t)|_{g_t}^2\,dV_{g_t}\ge0.
\]

Consequently this smooth eigenvalue is the bottom of the spectrum.
The identity $\Delta_{g_t}1=0$ for every $t$ means that differentiation
at the base state gives $6\Delta_g\dot u+\dot U=\dot\mu$. Integrate
against the fixed $dV_g$, using unit volume, to get
$\dot\mu=\int_M\dot U\,dV_g$.

For the scalar variation, differentiating metric compatibility and zero
torsion gives the connection variation

\[
\dot\Gamma^k_{ij}=\tfrac12 g^{k\ell}
  (\nabla_iK_{j\ell}+\nabla_jK_{i\ell}-\nabla_\ell K_{ij}).
\]

The Ricci contraction varies by
$\dot{\operatorname{Ric}}_{ij}=\nabla_k\dot\Gamma^k_{ij}-\nabla_j\dot\Gamma^k_{ik}$.
Taking its trace gives $\nabla^j(\nabla^i K_{ij})+\Delta_g\operatorname{tr}_g K$.
The contraction by the inverse metric adds $-\langle\operatorname{Ric},K\rangle$,
so

\[
\dot s=-\langle\operatorname{Ric},K\rangle
 +\nabla^j(\nabla^i K_{ij})+\Delta_g\operatorname{tr}_g K.
\]

Since $A$ is fixed, the derivative of $|dF|^2_{g_t,h}$ is
$-\langle A,K\rangle$. The divergence and Laplacian terms integrate
to zero on the closed manifold. Therefore

\[
\dot\mu[K]=-\int_M\langle T,K\rangle\,dV_g.
\tag{1}
\]

For $K=H$, the divergence and trace terms already vanish pointwise.
Write $D=|\nabla dF|^2$. The normal-frame Bochner identity in [E21][e21]
gives $D+|H|^2=-\langle\operatorname{Ric}_0,H\rangle$. Hence

\[
\dot U[H]=-\langle T,H\rangle=D+4|H|^2,
\qquad
\dot\mu[H]=\int_M(D+4|H|^2)\,dV_g.
\tag{2}
\]

For a trace-free $K$ the first volume derivative is
$\tfrac12\int_M\operatorname{tr}_gK\,dV_g=0$. The volume-normalized
path $\operatorname{Vol}_{g_t}(M)^{-1/2}g_t$ has the same tangent $K$ and
the same first derivative. The given $H$ therefore furnishes an admissible
direction even if nearby metrics are required to have volume one.

If $\dot\mu[H]=0$, then $H=0$ and $D=0$ everywhere by smoothness. Thus
$F^*h=(\lambda/4)g$ with $\lambda>0$. The map is a local isometry after
scaling the domain and is a diffeomorphism by the [E21 covering argument][e21].
The converse holds for a homothetic diffeomorphism, for which $H=D=0$.
If $F$ is nonhomothetic, (2) shows that for all sufficiently small
$t>0$ the perturbed metric has $\mu(t)>0$ with $F$ fixed. The metric path
varies the Riemannian metric freely in direction $H$, with optional
volume normalization. Existence of an extremizer for a metric or map
optimization is outside the calculation.

## A fixed finite moment test for a strongly convergent sequence

Choose a real $L^2(g)$-orthonormal basis $\phi_1,\ldots,\phi_N$ of the
first positive scalar eigenspace. This is a finite choice determined
by the supplied metric. For $1\le a\le b\le N$ take the smooth
trace-free tensors

\[
K_{ab}=(d\phi_a\odot d\phi_b)_0,
\qquad
\eta\odot\zeta=\tfrac12(\eta\otimes\zeta+\zeta\otimes\eta).
\]

Each is an admissible first-order volume-preserving metric direction,
using the same normalization as above. They are defined before any
choice of limiting $F$. Suppose smooth $f_j:M\to S^4$ converge strongly
to the supplied eigenmap in ambient $W^{1,2}(M;\mathbb R^5)$. Assume
the explicit finite moment condition

\[
\mathcal M_{ab}(f_j):=
\int_M\bigl\langle\operatorname{Ric}_0-3(f_j^*h)_0,K_{ab}\bigr\rangle\,dV_g
\longrightarrow0
\quad\text{for all }1\le a\le b\le N.
\tag{3}
\]

In the Euclidean embedding of the sphere, the pullback tensor is the
sum of $df_j^I\otimes df_j^I$ over the five components. The difference
of the pullbacks satisfies

\[
\|f_j^*h-F^*h\|_{L^1}
\le (\|df_j\|_{L^2}+\|dF\|_{L^2})\|df_j-dF\|_{L^2}\longrightarrow0.
\]

Trace-free projection with respect to the fixed metric preserves
this convergence. Thus the stresses converge in $L^1$ to $T$, and
(3) implies $\int_M\langle T,K_{ab}\rangle dV_g=0$ for the entire
family. For each component write $F^I=\sum_a c_{Ia}\phi_a$. If
$Q_{ab}=\sum_I c_{Ia}c_{Ib}$, then $H=\sum_{a,b}Q_{ab}K_{ab}$, where
the tensors are extended by symmetry to ordered pairs. This gives
$\int_M\langle T,H\rangle dV_g=0$. By (2) the limit is a homothetic
diffeomorphism. Its Ricci tensor is $3F^*h$, because the metric is a
constant multiple of a pullback of the unit round metric. In particular
$T=0$ pointwise, so the sequence of stresses converges to zero as
distributions against every smooth symmetric test tensor.

More quantitatively, $Q$ is positive semidefinite with trace
$\sum_I\|F^I\|_2^2=1$, so (2) gives
$\int_M(D+4|H|^2)\,dV_g=-\operatorname{tr}(Q\mathcal M(F))\le
\|\mathcal M(F)\|_{\mathrm{op}}$. The criterion tests moments of the
tensors themselves. It uses strong $W^{1,2}$ as in [E14][e14] and
[E17][e17], and requires the additional hypothesis (3). Any argument
that obtains these moments from an optimizing degree-one sequence
requires a separate result for [B13][b13].
