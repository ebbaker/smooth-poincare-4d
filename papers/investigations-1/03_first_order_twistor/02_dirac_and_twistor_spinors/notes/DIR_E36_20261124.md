## Fixed compact problem

Let \((M^4,g)\) be a supplied smooth closed connected oriented spin
homotopy four-sphere with a positive-definite metric, a flat ball
about \(p\), and invertible full Dirac operator \(D_g\). Use metric
spin connections, \(c_g(X)^2=-|X|_g^2\), and \(S_g^+\) the chirality
on which real self-dual two-forms act. Write \(K(x,y)\) for the full
inverse kernel with respect to \(dV_g\). Its chiral blocks use the
target chirality as the first subscript, so the supplied Green map is
\(G(x,p)=K_{+-}(x,p):S_{g,p}^-\to S_{g,x}^+\) for \(x\ne p\).
For \(\eta\in S_{g,p}^-\), let \(G_\eta=K(\cdot,p)\eta\), with
\(D_gG_\eta=\delta_p\eta\).

Use the explicit [E06 quaternionic model][e06]: in an oriented
orthonormal frame the tangent space is \(\mathbb H\) in the orientation
\((1,i,j,k)\). The spin action of \((a,b)\in\mathrm{Spin}(4)\) on the
two copies \(S^\pm\cong\mathbb H\) is \((as_+,bs_-)\), and the complex
structure on each copy is right multiplication by \(i\). The Clifford
action is
\[
 c(v)(s_+,s_-)=(v s_-,-\overline v\,s_+).
\]
In this model the self-dual form \(1\wedge i+j\wedge k\) acts as left
multiplication by \(2i\) on \(S^+\) and vanishes on \(S^-\), which
fixes the chirality of the model.

## Source symmetry, scales and costs

On both summands define \(J(s)=s j\). It preserves chirality, is
antiunitary and complex-antilinear since \(ij=-ji\), and satisfies
\(J^2=-I\). More generally right multiplication \(R_q(s)=s q\) for
any fixed quaternion \(q\) defines a global real-linear bundle map
because the spin action is on the left. It is parallel and commutes
with Clifford multiplication by every real vector, by associativity
in the displayed action. Consequently it commutes with \(D_g\) and
with the twistor operator, acting also on the spinor factor of its
target. This explicitly verifies the four-dimensional structure used
in [Ammann–Humbert–Morel, Remark 2.13, printed p. 6][ahm-q].

In particular \(D_g(G_\eta q)=\delta_p(\eta q)\) distributionally.
Uniqueness gives \(G_{\eta q}=G_\eta q\). Each fiber \(S_{g,p}^-\)
is a quaternionic line, so any two unit sources satisfy
\(\eta'=\eta q\) for a unique \(|q|=1\). Therefore
\[
 |G_{\eta'}(x)|=|G_\eta(x)|\quad (x\ne p).
\]
For each \(x\ne p\), \(G(x,p)\) is either zero or a positive scalar
times a quaternionic-linear isometry. In particular a zero for one
nonzero source at \(x\) is a zero for every source there. Equality of
the derivative norms for unit sources follows from parallelism of
\(R_q\).

In the [E27 fixed end construction][e27], take \(h=\omega^2g\),
\(\omega=r^{-2}\) near \(p\), with unitary conformal identification
\(\beta:S_g\to S_h\). The fields are
\(\Psi_\eta=\omega^{-3/2}\beta G_\eta\) on \(M\setminus\{p\}\) and
\(\psi_\eta=\omega^{-2}G_\eta\) compactified by the twistor weight.
The identification \(\beta\) commutes with \(R_q\) because it takes
paired spin frames to one another without changing spinor coordinates.
Thus the same rotation relates \(\Psi_{\eta'}\) to \(\Psi_\eta\) and
\(\psi_{\eta'}\) to \(\psi_\eta\). The metric in which the
twistor-transported field has unit norm is, on the common nonzero set,
\[
 |\psi_\eta|_g^{-4}g=|\Psi_\eta|_h^{-4}h ,
\]
independent of the unit source. The norms of \(P_g\psi_\eta\) and
\(\nabla^h\Psi_\eta\) likewise agree pointwise for all unit sources.
Consequently the relative costs, with the zero sets omitted, agree
including the value \(+\infty\). In [E29][e29], finiteness is equivalent
to absence of interior zeros, so either every unit source has finite
cost or every one has infinite cost.

For the oriented flat end frame of E27 with the chosen reflection
unit vector \(a\), the boundary constant is
\(\Psi_{\infty,\eta}=c\,a\cdot\eta\), where \(c=1/|S^3|=1/(2\pi^2)\).
The map \(\eta\mapsto\Psi_{\infty,\eta}\) is \(c\) times a
quaternionic-linear isometry from \(S_{g,p}^-\) to the parallel
positive end fields. Changing the unit source gives all boundary
constants of norm \(c\), related by the same right unit
quaternion. A common rescaling by \(c^{-1}\) gives unit boundary
norm and preserves zero sets and relative costs.

## Adjoint relation and chirality on swapping the points

The full inverse of the self-adjoint invertible compact \(D_g\)
is self-adjoint on \(L^2\). The adjoint kernel is \(K(y,x)^*\) with
the same volume measure. Equality of the distribution kernels and
their smoothness off the diagonal give \(K(x,y)^*=K(y,x)\) for
\(x\ne y\). This is the inverse argument of [Ammann–Humbert–Morel,
Prop. 2.8, printed p. 5][ahm-adjoint], whose stated flat-open-set
conclusion also follows globally from the supplied inverse.
The full operator anticommutes with chirality, as does its inverse,
so
\[
 K_{+-}(x,p)^*=K_{-+}(p,x):S_{g,x}^+\longrightarrow S_{g,p}^- .
\]
Thus vanishing of the whole fixed-pole negative-source block at
\(x\) is equivalent to vanishing of the swapped **positive-source**
block at \(p\). A comparison with \(K_{+-}(p,x)\), the negative-source
block after swapping, would require a further chirality identification.
The quaternionic symmetry used here preserves both chiralities.

## Signed interior count

By E27, \(\psi_\eta=-c\,x\cdot\eta+O(r^4)\) in the oriented flat
ball, with smooth error. Its derivative at \(p\) is
\(v\mapsto-c\,v\eta\). Right multiplication by a nonzero quaternion
preserves the standard real quaternionic orientation since
\(\mathbb H\setminus\{0\}\) is connected. The complex orientation
on \(S^+\), however, is \((1,i,j,-k)\) from its complex basis
\((1,j)\), so the pole index is \(-1\). [E06][e06] gives the Euler
number \(\langle e(S^+_{\mathbb R}),[M]\rangle
=\langle c_2(S^+),[M]\rangle=-1\) in these orientations.

Suppose all interior zeros are transverse. They are finite because
the zero set of the smooth compactified section is compact and all
its zeros, including \(p\), are isolated. At an interior zero, the
positive scalar multiplier relating \(G_\eta\) and \(\psi_\eta\),
and the complex-linear conformal identification to \(\Psi_\eta\),
preserve the local index. The Euler count thus yields
\[
 \sum_{x\ne p,\ G_\eta(x)=0}\operatorname{ind}_x(G_\eta)
 =\langle c_2(S^+),[M]\rangle-\operatorname{ind}_p(\psi_\eta)
 =-1-(-1)=0 .
\]
The number of transverse interior zeros of index \(+1\) equals
the number of index \(-1\).

## Two flat ordinary Dirac jets

Use \(e_0=1,e_1=i,e_2=j,e_3=k\) as the oriented orthonormal frame
on flat \(\mathbb R^4\) with coordinate functions \(x_\mu\). For a
positive spinor \(s\), the ordinary Dirac operator has negative
component \(D^+s=-\sum_{\mu=0}^3\overline e_\mu\,\partial_\mu s\).
For the real-linear diagonal field
\(s(x)=\sum_\mu d_\mu x_\mu e_\mu\) its Clifford trace is the
quaternion \(-\sum_\mu d_\mu\), since
\(\overline e_\mu e_\mu=1\). The two choices below give homogeneous
solutions with invertible real derivatives at their only zero:

| Positive field | Diagonal coefficients | Clifford trace | Determinant in quaternionic axes | Index in complex fiber orientation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(s_+(x)=x_0+x_1i+x_2j-3x_3k\) | \(1,1,1,-3\) | \(0\) | \(-3\) | \(+1\) |
| \(s_-(x)=x_0+x_1i-x_2j-x_3k\) | \(1,1,-1,-1\) | \(0\) | \(+1\) | \(-1\) |

Both fields take values in \(S^+\). The subscripts distinguish
their index signs, and the sign reversal between the last two
columns is the change from \((1,i,j,k)\) to \((1,i,j,-k)\) in the
target. These local examples show that the ordinary Dirac equation
alone allows either transverse index. The signed total therefore
does not exclude paired zeros of the actual fixed-pole Green field.
No transversality or existence of a zero-free pole is inferred.
The question of varying the pole or metric remains at [B19][b19].
