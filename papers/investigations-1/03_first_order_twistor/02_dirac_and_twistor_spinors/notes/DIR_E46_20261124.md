## 1. Green transport, simple zero and the zero-mass flux

For the supplied compact \(g\) and its flat pole chart \(x\), let
\(\eta\in S^-_p\setminus\{0\}\). Set \(c=\omega_3^{-1}\).
The outward flux of \(-d(c/(2r^2))\) on compact-chart spheres is one.
Consequently \(D_g(-c\,x\cdot\eta/r^4)=\delta_p\eta\) in the chart,
by applying \(D_g^2=-\sum_i\partial_i^2\) to \(c\eta/(2r^2)\).
Cutting off the pole creates a smooth residual \(f\). Subtracting
\(D_g^{-1}f\) gives the compact Green solution with its positive-chiral
expansion \(G_\eta=-c\,x\cdot\eta/r^4+\gamma(x)\).
This also resolves the sign slip already identified in the proof of
Proposition 2.3 in the cited v2 source, where the correction satisfying
\(D\zeta=-f\) must be added to the cutoff field.
[E27](DIR_E27_20261124.md) ·
[Ammann–Humbert–Morel, §2.2, Definition 2.1, Remark 2.2 and Proposition 2.3][ahm].

For \(h=\omega^2g\), Clifford tracing the paired-frame connection adds
\(\tfrac32\,d\log\omega\cdot\) before the factor \(\omega^{-1}\).
This gives
\(D_h(\omega^{-3/2}\beta G_\eta)=\omega^{-5/2}\beta D_gG_\eta\).
Applying the twistor projection instead gives kernel transport with
weight \(\omega^{1/2}\). Thus the end harmonic field
\(\Psi=\omega^{-3/2}\beta G_\eta\) corresponds by the twistor transport
to the compact field
\[
\psi=\omega^{-1/2}\beta^{-1}\Psi=\omega^{-2}G_\eta
=-c\,x\cdot\eta+r^4\gamma(x).
\]
In the last formula \(\omega=r^{-2}\). The real derivative
\(-c\,X\cdot\eta\) is an isomorphism onto \(S_p^+\), since its norm is
\(c|\eta|\,|X|\) and both real dimensions are four. Also
\(D_g\psi(p)=4c\,\eta\), and \(P_g\psi=O(r^3)\) because the linear term
solves the flat twistor equation. The compact extension has a
transverse zero at \(p\) and no further zero near \(p\). Interior zeros
of the end field remain possible for the general supplied data.
[E27](DIR_E27_20261124.md) ·
[Hitchin, §1.4, Proposition 1.3][hitchin] · [Friedrich, §1, pp. 59–60][friedrich].

The orientation-preserving inversion \(y=S_a x/r^2\) has rotation
part \(S_aS_{x/r}\), with even spin lift \(q=a\cdot(x/r)\cdot\).
Then \(q(-c\,(x/r)\cdot\eta)=c\,a\cdot\eta\). The end error
\(u=\Psi-\Psi_\infty\) is \(R^{-3}q\,\gamma(x(y))\), so
\(u=O(R^{-3})\) and \(\nabla^h u=O(R^{-4})\) in the parallel end frame.
Uniqueness in that decay class follows by transporting a difference
back with the inverse Dirac weight. Its compact representative is
bounded near \(p\), and a cutoff on an annulus of radius \(\epsilon\)
has distributional error bounded by \(O(\epsilon^3)\). Elliptic
regularity and compact invertibility eliminate the difference.
[E27](DIR_E27_20261124.md).

For a large end sphere \(S_R\) oriented out of the compact truncation
\(N_R\), integration of \(D_h^2\Psi=0\) gives
\[
\int_{N_R}\left(|\nabla^h\Psi|^2+\frac{s_h}{4}|\Psi|^2\right)dV_h
=B(R),\qquad
B(R)=\frac12\int_{S_R}\partial_R|\Psi|^2\,dA_h .
\]
On the Euclidean end, \(u\) is componentwise harmonic and its
componentwise derivative flux is constant in \(R\). The bound
\(O(R^3R^{-4})\) forces that flux to vanish, including the term in
\(B(R)\) paired with \(\Psi_\infty\). Exterior integration by parts for
\(u\) then gives
\[
B(R)=-\int_{\{|y|>R\}}|\nabla^h u|^2\,dV_h=O(R^{-4}),\qquad B(R)\le0.
\]
The geometric ADM integral vanishes because \(h\) is exactly Euclidean
there. The compact Green regular coefficient is different data, entering
the spinor at order \(R^{-3}\). The limiting identity is
\(\int s_h|\Psi|^2=-4\int|\nabla^h\Psi|^2\). A lower bound
\(\int s_h|\Psi|^2\ge0\), such as \(s_h\ge0\), is sufficient for
parallelism and the compact chiral twistor conclusion. For arbitrary
supplied data the identity supplies the opposite weak inequality.
Exact recognition on a homotopy four-sphere retains the compact
signature and developing-map hypotheses.

## 2. Unit harmonic scale, coefficient 12 and relaxed mass

This check starts with the supplied nowhere-zero harmonic spinor and
unit end amplitude. Put \(\sigma=|\Psi|_h\), \(k=\sigma^{4/3}h\) and
\(\xi=\sigma^{-1}\beta_{h,k}\Psi\). Then \(D_k\xi=0\), \(|\xi|_k=1\),
and the pointwise Schrödinger–Lichnerowicz formula gives
\(s_k=-4V\) for \(V=|\nabla^k\xi|^2\). On the end \(k-\delta=O_2(R^{-3})\)
and \(V=O(R^{-8})\). For \(g'=v^{-4}k\) and
\(\Psi'=v^3\beta_{k,g'}\xi\), put \(A=\operatorname{grad}_k\log v\).
Using the \(g'\)-orthonormal frame \(v^2 e_i\), the derivative in the
paired spin frame is
\[
\nabla^{g'}_{v^2 e_i}\Psi'
=v^5\beta_{k,g'}\big(\nabla^k_{e_i}\xi+
e_i\cdot A\cdot\xi+4A_i\xi\big).
\]
The cross term with \(\nabla^k\xi\) vanishes in the sum by the constant
norm and \(D_k\xi=0\). The new squared term is
\((4+16-8)|A|^2=12|A|^2\), using
\(\operatorname{Re}\langle e_i\cdot A\cdot\xi,\xi\rangle=-A_i\).
Since \(|\Psi'|=v^3\) and \(dV_{g'}=v^{-8}dV_k\), this yields
\[
|\nabla^{g'}\Psi'|^2=v^{10}(V+12|d\log v|^2),\qquad
Q(v)=\int_N (Vv^2+12|dv|^2)\,dV_k .
\]
For the same harmonic transport \(P_{g'}\Psi'=\nabla^{g'}\Psi'\), and
the critical relative integral is
\(\displaystyle J_{g'}(\Psi')^4=\int_N(V+12|d\log v|^2)^2\,dV_k\).
Thus \(v\equiv1\) minimizes the critical cost among the stated
end-normalized transports. Its criterion concerns a different
functional from \(Q\). [E31](DIR_E31_20261124.md) ·
[Hitchin, pp. 10–11][hitchin] · [Lee–Parker, equation (A.2)][lp].

With \(B_R(v)=\int_{S_R}v\partial_{\nu_k}v\,dA_k\), the transformed
spinor boundary term is \(3B_R(v)\). The conformal scalar law gives
\[
 s_{g'}|\Psi'|^2dV_{g'}
 =(-4Vv^2-12v\Delta_k v-36|dv|^2)dV_k .
\]
Integration by parts gives \(-\tfrac14\int_{N_R}s_{g'}|\Psi'|^2
=Q_{N_R}(v)-3B_R(v)\). For \(v=1+aR^{-2}+O_2(R^{-2-\epsilon})\)
the limits are \(B_R\to-2a\omega_3\) and \(m_{\rm ADM}=-4a\). The latter
uses \(g'_{ij}=(1-4aR^{-2})\delta_{ij}+O_1(R^{-2-\min(\epsilon,1)})\)
in the normalized flux with denominator \(6\omega_3\).
[E31](DIR_E31_20261124.md) ·
[Lee–Parker, equations (1.1) and (8.10) and Appendix][lp].

In the fixed \(k\), the homogeneous Sobolev bound follows from the flat
end cutoff and a fixed compact Poincaré inequality, with the cutoff
annulus controlled by integrating along rays. When \(V\not\equiv0\)
and \(V\ge c_0>0\) on a ball of volume \(b\), it gives
\[
 b^{1/2}\le b^{1/4}S\|dv\|_2+c_0^{-1/2}
       \left(\int Vv^2\,dV_k\right)^{1/2}
\]
for \(v\in 1+D^{1,2}\). This gives a fixed positive lower bound for
\(Q\). The coercive quadratic form gives the relaxed minimizer \(v_*\),
with \(0<v_*\le1\) after truncation and the maximum principle.
If \(V\not\equiv0\), then \(1-v_*>0\) everywhere by the superharmonic
minimum principle. Cutoffs of \(1-v_*\) yield positive
compact-variation test functions whose \(Q\) approaches the optimal
value \(P_*=Q(v_*)\).
[E32](DIR_E32_20261124.md) · [Talenti, theorem (1)][talenti].

Testing \(12\Delta_kv_*+Vv_*=0\) against \(1-v_*\) gives
\(P_*=\int Vv_*\) and integration over \(N_R\) gives
\(\int_{S_R}\partial_{\nu_k}v_*=\int_{N_R} Vv_*/12\).
For \(w=1-v_*\), the barrier \(C(R^{-2}-R^{-3})\) works because
\(\Delta_k(R^{-2}-R^{-3})=3R^{-5}+O(R^{-7})\). Hence \(w=O(R^{-2})\).
Rescaled interior \(W^{2,p}\) estimates, with \(p>4\), then give
\(Dw=O(R^{-3})\). The Euclidean equation after cutoff has scalar
source \(O(R^{-8})\) with total charge \(P_*/12\) and divergence source
with vector coefficient \(O(R^{-6})\). This uses the metric error
times \(Dw\). The scalar source has a finite first moment, and the
divergence term has a convolution kernel of order \(R^{-3}\).
Splitting at radius \(R/2\), the distant source contributions near the
evaluation point have bounds \(O(R^{-6})\) and \(O(R^{-5})\),
respectively. The only \(R^{-2}\) coefficient is therefore the scalar
charge times the Euclidean fundamental-solution coefficient
\(1/(2\omega_3)\). Subtracting this leading term and applying the
annular estimate again gives
\[
v_*=1-A_*R^{-2}+O_1(R^{-3}),\qquad A_*=\frac{P_*}{24\omega_3}.
\]
The residual second derivatives have annular \(L^p\) bound
\(O(R^{-5+4/p})\). These bounds give weighted \(W^{2,p}_{-\tau}\)
decay for \(v_*^{-4}k-\delta\) with \(1<\tau<2\).
[E32](DIR_E32_20261124.md) ·
[Chiarenza–Frasca–Longo, Theorem 4.1, equation (4.4)][cfl].

The leading metric term for \(\widehat k=v_*^{-4}k\) is
\(4A_*R^{-2}\delta\), with raw flux \(24\omega_3A_*=P_*\). Thus
\[
m_{\rm ADM}(\widehat k)=\frac{P_*}{6\omega_3},\qquad
 \lim_{R\to\infty} 3B_R(v_*)=\frac{P_*}{4},\qquad
 \int_N s_{\widehat k}|\Psi'|^2\,dV_{\widehat k}=-3P_* .
\]
The last identity also follows from
\(s_{\widehat k}=-3Vv_*^4-36v_*^2|dv_*|^2\), which is integrable
with the established decay. The leading flux computation is in the
fixed chart, with coordinate independence supplied by the weighted
decay and integrable scalar curvature. The compact variations agree
with \(k\) on the end and have zero ADM mass. The relaxed optimum
has positive mass when \(V\) is nonzero. If \(V=0\), \(v_*=1\) and
both fluxes vanish.

## 3. Actual oscillatory Green fields and the scale infimum

In the fixed E35 family on standard \(S^4\), the leading compact metric
perturbation is \(n^{-3/2}\chi\cos(nx^1)H\), with a remainder
\(O(n^{j-5/2})\) after \(j\le2\) derivatives. This gives the
\(C^1\) size \(O(n^{-1/2})\) and \(C^{1,1/4}\) size \(O(n^{-1/4})\)
by interpolating the uniform first- and second-derivative bounds.
The fixed-bundle coefficient change of the Dirac operator has
exactly these upper bounds in \(W^{1,4}\to L^4\) and
\(C^{1,1/4}\to C^{0,1/4}\), respectively. Composing with the bounded
background inverse yields uniformly bounded perturbed inverses by a
Neumann series. In particular the equal Dirac pole on the common
flat compact ball cancels before taking either norm. If
\(S_b\) is the regular background field after subtracting a common
cutoff pole, the Green difference \(u_n\) solves
\(\widehat D_nu_n=-(\widehat D_n-D_b)S_b\).
It inherits \(W^{1,4}\) size \(O(n^{-1/2})\) and
\(C^{1,1/4}\) size \(O(n^{-1/4})\). The noncompact harmonic fields
are therefore the actual compact Green transports.
[E35](DIR_E35_20261124.md).

On the common flat pole ball, \(u_n\) is Dirac harmonic and
componentwise harmonic. Interior estimates bound its first two
derivatives and value by \(O(n^{-1/2})\) on a smaller fixed ball from
its \(L^4\) norm. After the end frame rotation and the Dirac weight,
the end difference from the same unit \(\kappa\) is
\(O_2(n^{-1/2}R^{-3})\). On a fixed compact truncation the fields
converge uniformly to \(\kappa\), so they are zero-free globally on the
punctured manifold for large \(n\). Their compact twistor transports
retain the sole transverse zero at \(p\). Combining the interior
\(W^{1,4}\) estimate, \(O(n^{-1/2})\) connection coefficients and the
end decay gives \(O(n^{-1/2})\) for the spinor covariant derivative
in both \(L^2\) and \(L^4\). The same orders hold in the unit harmonic
scale by \(|d\sigma_n|\le|\nabla^{h_n}\Psi_n|\) and uniform positive
norm bounds. The resulting energy is \(O(n^{-1})\), \(J^4=O(n^{-2})\),
and \(\|s_{k_n}\|_{L^2}=O(n^{-1})\).
[E35](DIR_E35_20261124.md) · [E31](DIR_E31_20261124.md).

The leading linearized curvature is
\(\frac12 n^{1/2}\chi\cos(nx^1)T\). The Euclidean Weyl projection
sends \(T\) to \(C/2\). The nonlinear error in the Weyl tensor is
\(O(|b_n||\partial^2 b_n|+|\partial b_n|^2)=O(n^{-1})\) and the
cutoff derivative error is \(O(n^{-1/2})\). Thus the \(1212\)
coordinate component is \(\frac14 n^{1/2}\chi\cos(nx^1)+O(n^{-1/2})\).
Its squared \(L^2\) leading term has a positive coefficient at
order \(n\), since the squared cosine integral tends to
\(\frac12\int\chi^2\). The upper bound is also \(O(n)\) in squared
norm because the metric is conformally flat off the fixed support.
The compact signature vanishes, so
\(E_+=\frac18\int |W|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2\) in operator Hilbert–Schmidt
normalization. Conformal invariance carries the same energy to
\(h_n\) and \(k_n\). In the E12 Weyl projection of
\(d^\nabla(P_{k_n}\xi_n)\), the factor \(1/2\) on Clifford multiplication
and the squared action \(2|\alpha|^2\) of a self-dual two-form
\(\alpha\) on a unit positive spinor give squared \(L^2\) norm
\(E_+(k_n)/2\). Hence \(\|d^\nabla P\|\) in this projection grows
at order \(n^{1/2}\) in \(L^2\) while both first derivative
norms of the actual fields tend to zero. This is a simultaneous test
on the same fixed end of the standard sphere.
[E11](DIR_E11_20261124.md) · [E12](DIR_E12_20261124.md) · [E35](DIR_E35_20261124.md).

For E34, pullback by \(\Phi_t\) and the length homothety \(t\) preserve
the boundary spinor with the chosen unitary spin identification.
Since \(\Phi_t(y)=y/t\) on the fixed exterior, the new tensor there
is again Euclidean, and the field is \(\Psi(y/t)\). Near the compact
pole the inverse Dirac transport is \(t^3G_\eta(tx)\), so its leading
pole and source are fixed. The derivative length scales by \(t^{-1}\)
and the volume density by \(t^4\). The quadratic energy and weighted
scalar integral thus scale by \(t^2\). The critical integral has
degree zero under this rescaling and is invariant on the transported
nonzero locus, including as an extended nonnegative integral.
The fixed identity core shrinks by volume factor \(t^4\).
The resulting zero infimum for the supplied marked class therefore
comes from a degenerating scale family for every positive-energy
input. Smooth attainment would still require a parallel spinor.
On the Euclidean transition annulus a radial map with radial function
\(\lambda_t(R)\) gives radial and tangential length factors
\(t\lambda_t'(R)\) and \(t\lambda_t(R)/R\). These differ where the cutoff
increases for \(t<1\). This puts the marked scale family outside the
pointwise conformal variations of a single metric used in E32.
