Let $M$ be a fixed smooth closed oriented Riemannian spin four-manifold
with a fixed spin structure. Suppose smooth positive metrics $h_j$
converge uniformly and strongly in $W^{1,4}$ in fixed charts to a
**smooth positive metric** $g=h_\infty$. Use Clifford multiplication
$X\cdot_h X=-|X|_h^2$ and $S_h^+$ on which real self-dual two-forms act.
Write $P_h\psi(X)=\nabla^h_X\psi+\tfrac14 X\cdot_h D_h\psi$. After the
positive-symmetric spin and all covector identifications below, the
operators converge in $H^1(g)\to L^2(g)$ norm. If smooth spinors satisfy
$\|\psi_j\|_{2,h_j}=1$ and either $\|P_{h_j}\psi_j\|_{2,h_j}\to0$ or
$J_{h_j}(\psi_j)\to0$ on the relative-defect domain of E07, their
transports have a subsequence converging strongly in $H^1(g)$ to a
smooth positive twistor half-spinor of $L^2(g)$ norm one. The same
conclusion applies to smooth unit first eigenspinors when
$\mu(h_j)\to0$, since their squared residual is $\mu(h_j)$.

## 1. Bundle comparison and coefficients

Define $H_j=g^{-1}h_j$, so $h_j(X,Y)=g(H_j X,Y)$, and let
$b_j=H_j^{-1/2}$. It takes a $g$-orthonormal frame $(e_i)$ to an
$h_j$-orthonormal frame $(b_je_i)$. Lift from the identity through
$H_j^{-t/2}$ for $0\le t\le1$ in the fixed spin cover of the oriented
frame bundle. The lift gives the unitary spin identification
$\beta_j:S_g^\pm\to S_{h_j}^\pm$ with
$(b_jX)\cdot_{h_j}\beta_jv=\beta_j(X\cdot_g v)$. Together with
$\alpha\mapsto\alpha\circ b_j^{-1}$ on covectors it gives an isometry
$\mathcal B_j:T^*M\otimes S_g^+\to T^*M\otimes S_{h_j}^+$. This
intertwines the Clifford trace-free projections. These are the
Bourguignon–Gauduchon positive-symmetric identifications, using
Propositions 1.1 and 1.6 and §2 equation (1).
[Author-proofread Schwahn translation, pp. 3, 6, 8–9](https://www.igt.uni-stuttgart.de/dokumente/schwahn/SpineursVariations_EN.pdf).

Set $K_j(X)=\nabla^{h_j}_X-\nabla^g_X$ on vectors. The connection
of $h_j$ transported to $g$ has connection difference
\[
\begin{aligned}
A_j(X)Y
 &=b_j^{-1}\nabla^{h_j}_X(b_jY)-\nabla^g_X Y\\
 &=b_j^{-1}\big((\nabla^g_X b_j)Y+K_j(X)b_jY\big).
\end{aligned}
\]
This is the transported connection appearing in the proof of
Bourguignon–Gauduchon Theorem 3.1, equation (14), and Remark 3.2(ii).
Both connections in the difference are $g$-metric, so $A_j(X)$
is $g$-skew. [Author-proofread Schwahn translation, p. 14](https://www.igt.uni-stuttgart.de/dokumente/schwahn/SpineursVariations_EN.pdf#page=14).

All subsequent norms without subscripts use $g$. Uniform convergence and
compactness bound $H_j$ uniformly above and away from zero for large $j$.
Smoothness of the positive matrix power on this uniformly bounded
positive set, applied in a $g$-orthonormal frame parallel at the point
of differentiation, gives
\[
 \|b_j-I\|_\infty\le C\|h_j-g\|_\infty,\qquad
 \|\nabla^g b_j\|_4\le C\|\nabla^g(h_j-g)\|_4 .
\]
The covariant difference on the right tends to zero by the assumed
chart convergence and smoothness of $g$. The Koszul identity for two
torsion-free connections reads
\[
 2h_j(K_j(X)Y,Z)
 =(\nabla_X^g h_j)(Y,Z)+(\nabla_Y^g h_j)(X,Z)
  -(\nabla_Z^g h_j)(X,Y).
\]
Uniform control of $h_j^{-1}$ yields
$\|K_j\|_4\le C\|\nabla^g(h_j-g)\|_4$. Thus
$\|A_j\|_4\le C\|\nabla^g(h_j-g)\|_4$. The lift to spin connections is
$\widetilde\nabla^j=\beta_j^{-1}\nabla^{h_j}\beta_j=\nabla^g+
\rho_{\rm spin}(A_j)$, where $\rho_{\rm spin}$ is the infinitesimal
spin representation. Its connection coefficient difference has the
same $L^4$ bound since the representation is a bounded fiberwise
linear map.

Define $\widetilde P_j=\mathcal B_j^{-1}P_{h_j}\beta_j$.
In the paired orthonormal frames its covariant derivative part is the
$T^*M\otimes S_g^+$ tensor $X\mapsto\widetilde\nabla^j_{b_jX}v$.
Its trace-free projection $\Pi_g$ is fixed because $\mathcal B_j$
intertwines the Clifford trace. Consequently
\[
 (\widetilde P_j-P_g)v
   =\Pi_g\big[X\mapsto \nabla^g_{(b_j-I)X}v+
                           \rho_{\rm spin}(A_j(b_jX))v\big] .
\]
The principal coefficient difference is uniformly small, and the
lower-order difference is small in $L^4$. Hölder and the dimension-four
embedding $H^1(g)\hookrightarrow L^4(g)$ give
\[
 \|(\widetilde P_j-P_g)v\|_2
 \le C\left(\|h_j-g\|_\infty+\|\nabla^g(h_j-g)\|_4\right)\|v\|_{H^1(g)}
   =:\varepsilon_j\|v\|_{H^1(g)},\quad \varepsilon_j\to0 .
\tag{1}
\]
In particular, (1) uses the critical product $L^4\cdot L^4\subset L^2$
without requiring a compact embedding into $L^4$.

## 2. Absorption, mass, and the limiting equation

The volume ratios $w_j=dV_{h_j}/dV_g=\sqrt{\det H_j}$ converge uniformly
to 1. Norms computed for $\beta_j v$ and for $\mathcal B_j T$ in $h_j$
are those of $v,T$ weighted by $w_j$ in their respective integrals.
This gives uniform norm equivalences for $L^2,L^4$. In particular,
$v_j=\beta_j^{-1}\psi_j$ satisfies $\|v_j\|_2\to1$, since
$1=\int_M |v_j|_g^2w_j\,dV_g$.

The fixed smooth operator $P_g$ has
$\sigma(P_g)(\xi)^*\sigma(P_g)(\xi)=\tfrac34|\xi|_g^2I$ by the
Clifford projection computation in [E07](DIR_E07_20261124.md#elliptic-estimate-and-best-reference-constant).
The elliptic estimate is
$\|v\|_{H^1(g)}\le C_g(\|P_gv\|_2+\|v\|_2)$.
Substitute $\widetilde P_j v$ and absorb $C_g\varepsilon_j\|v\|_{H^1(g)}$
for large $j$. This gives a uniform estimate
\[
 \|v\|_{H^1(g)}\le C_0(\|\widetilde P_jv\|_2+\|v\|_2).
\tag{2}
\]
In the residual case, norm equivalence gives
$\|\widetilde P_j v_j\|_2\to0$ directly, and (2) bounds $v_j$ in $H^1$.

For the relative defect, use
\[
 J_h(\psi)=
 \left(\int_{M\setminus Z(\psi)} (|P_h\psi|_h/|\psi|_h)^4\,dV_h\right)^{1/4}
\]
on the domain of nonzero smooth chiral fields with transverse zeros,
allowing an empty zero set. The zeros have measure zero. Extending the
ratio by zero on this set and applying Hölder gives
$\|P_{h_j}\psi_j\|_{2,h_j}\le
J_{h_j}(\psi_j)\|\psi_j\|_{4,h_j}$ as in
[E07](DIR_E07_20261124.md#elliptic-estimate-and-best-reference-constant).
For the finite-cost tail of the sequence, the norm equivalences and
Sobolev embedding give
\[
 \|\widetilde P_j v_j\|_2\le C J_{h_j}(\psi_j)\|v_j\|_{H^1(g)}.
\]
Insert this estimate into (2) and absorb for large $j$ as
$J_{h_j}(\psi_j)\to0$. Then $\|v_j\|_{H^1(g)}$ remains bounded and
$\|\widetilde P_j v_j\|_2\to0$ also in this case.

In either case (1) yields $P_gv_j\to0$ in $L^2$. By Rellich, a
subsequence converges weakly in $H^1$ and strongly in $L^2$ to $v$.
The strong $L^2$ convergence preserves norm one. The bounded operator
$P_g:H^1\to L^2$ sends the weak limit to the weak residual limit,
so $P_gv=0$ on all of $M$ in the distributional sense. Applying the
fixed elliptic estimate to $v_j-v$ also gives strong $H^1$
convergence. Elliptic regularity for $P_g^*P_g$ with the **assumed
smooth** coefficients now yields $v\in C^\infty(S_g^+)$. The whole
equation therefore holds at the zeros as well.

On a connected $M$, a zero of $v$ has $D_gv\ne0$ there, by uniqueness
of the parallel pair $(v,D_gv)$. Hence $X\mapsto-\tfrac14X\cdot_g D_gv$
is the derivative at the zero. It is a real linear isomorphism by the
Clifford norm identity and equality of the real dimensions. In
particular the limit is admissible for the transverse-zero domain
and has $J_g(v)=0$. On a disconnected base, this argument applies on
each component carrying a nonzero limiting spinor. These assertions
use Friedrich's prolongation in §2, eq. (2.2) and the following
parallel-pair argument, pp. 61–62, with the zero discussion in
Proposition 2 on p. 63. [Published digitized pages](https://dml.cz/bitstream/handle/10338.dmlcz/701462/WSGP_09-1989-1_7.pdf#page=4).

## 3. Gauges and remaining hypotheses

- Simultaneous pullback of metric and spinor by an orientation-preserving
  diffeomorphism preserving the fixed spin structure, with a spin lift
  chosen, preserves chirality, norms, $\mu$, and $J$ by naturality and
  change of variables. It also transports the zero set and its first
  jets. The two choices of lift on a connected component differ by sign
  and give the same hypotheses. For a varying sequence of diffeomorphisms
  the metric convergence must hold **after** pullback.
- In the $J$ case one may also replace $h$ by $k=f^2h$ for any smooth
  $f>0$ and transport using $f^{1/2}\beta$, then multiply by a positive
  constant to restore $L^2(k)$ norm one. Both transformations preserve
  $J$ and the finite-cost zero domain by [E07](DIR_E07_20261124.md#conformal-invariance).
  Before renormalizing, squared residual and squared norm in the new
  metric are the old integrals weighted by $f^3$ and $f^5$,
  respectively. Thus general sequence-dependent conformal changes
  have no Rayleigh-residual invariance. Convergence in the $J$ case
  is required of the resulting representatives.

The coefficient argument is conditional on the existence of
representatives with the stated strong convergence to a smooth
positive limit. A global construction must supply these representatives
or a different attainment mechanism. For the $J$ route, it must
also supply a sequence with $J\to0$. The coefficient estimate
does not yield independent geometric bounds or infer metric smoothness
from weak $W^{1,4}$ convergence.
