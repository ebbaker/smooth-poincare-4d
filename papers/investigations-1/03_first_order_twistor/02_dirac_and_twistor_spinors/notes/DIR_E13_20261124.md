## Shared coefficient and rigidity

Fix a smooth compact connected oriented spin Riemannian four-manifold
$(M,g)$ without boundary. Denote the unit round target metric by $h$.
For a smooth nonzero-degree $f:M\to S^4$, put $V=3|df|_{g,h}^2$,
$L=6\Delta_g+s_g-V$ with $\Delta_g=d^*d$, and let $Q_f$ be the form
of $L$. Write $Y_f=\inf Q_f(u)/\|u\|_4^2$, and $Y$ for the same
infimum with $V=0$.

At a point set $T=\sum_i \sigma_i^2$ and
$U=\sum_{i<j}\sigma_i^2\sigma_j^2$ for the metric singular values of
$df$. Since $2U=T^2-\sum_i\sigma_i^4\le 3T^2/4$, Listing's threshold
in dimension four is $\sqrt{24U}\le 3T=V$. For a smooth positive
ground state $b$ with eigenvalue $\lambda$, conformal covariance under
$\widetilde g=b^2g$ gives $s_{\widetilde g}-V_{\widetilde g,f}
=b^{-3}L b=\lambda b^{-2}$. If $\lambda\ge0$, the threshold applies
on the closed spin domain with the same nonzero-degree map. Listing's
homothety conclusion gives $\widetilde g=c f^*h$ with $c>0$, hence
$s_{\widetilde g}=12/c=V_{\widetilde g,f}$ and $\lambda=0$. The
homothety is a diffeomorphism since a connected compact local
diffeomorphism onto the simply connected target is a covering with
one sheet. Conversely, a conformal diffeomorphism gives $L=6\Delta$
in the representative $f^*h$, and conformal covariance of the form
gives a zero first eigenvalue in every representative. Degree $+1$
selects the orientation-preserving case. E05 imports precisely this
rigidity step. [Listing 2010, trace definitions and Theorem 3, (6), p. 4](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=4)

This yields $\lambda_1\le0$, hence $Y_f\le0$ by a ground-state test.
It also gives $Y_f=0$ exactly at $\lambda_1=0$, since a negative
eigenvalue gives a strictly negative test and a zero eigenvalue gives
a nonnegative form with kernel. Nonnegativity of $V$ separately gives
$Y_f\le Y$.

## 1. Fixed-map maximum (E08)

For any one smooth bounded potential in this calculation, let
$q=\inf Q_f(u)/\|u\|_4^2<0$. A nonnegative minimizing sequence with
unit $L^4$ norm is bounded in $H^1$ since the $L^2$ norm is bounded
and the potential is bounded. Its weak $H^1$ limit $u$ along a
subsequence has strong $L^2$ convergence. Thus $Q_f(u)\le q<0$,
and $q\|u\|_4^2\le Q_f(u)$ with $\|u\|_4\le1$ forces
$\|u\|_4=1$ and $Q_f(u)=q$. At the minimizer,
$6\Delta_g u+(s_g-V)u=q u^3$. Testing with $(u-K)_+$ for
$|q|K^2>\|(s_g-V)_-\|_\infty$ bounds $u$ by $K$. The resulting
bounded right hand side gives $W^{2,p}$ for finite $p$ and then
smoothness by elliptic bootstrap. The maximum principle gives
strict positivity on connected $M$. This also proves the ordinary
negative Yamabe minimizer claim below by taking $V=0$. For $q=0$
in the fixed-map problem, the smooth positive ground state is a
quotient minimizer after $L^4$ normalization.

Use $q=Y_f\le0$ and denote its normalized positive minimizer by $u$.
The metric $g_*=u^2g$ has volume one and deficit
$s_{g_*}-V_{g_*,f}=q$, because the conformal weight of $V$ is $-2$.
The first eigenvalue there is $q$. For any other factor $a>0$
with $\int_M a^4\,dV_g=1$, test $u/a$ in its spectral quotient.
The numerator remains $Q_f(u)=q$ and the denominator is
$D=\int_M u^2a^2\,dV_g\in(0,1]$ by Cauchy–Schwarz. Therefore
$\lambda_1(L_{a^2g,f})\le q/D\le q$. At $q<0$ equality forces
$D=1$ and $a=u$. At $q=0$, covariance transports both the kernel
and form nonnegativity, so every unit-volume representative
maximizes. If $q=0$, the representative with pointwise deficit
zero is unique after volume normalization, either by the homothety
statement or by uniqueness of the positive ground state up to
scaling. This verifies E08's sign and both equality cases, including
the distinction between a spectral maximizer and a metric with a
constant deficit. [Frozen E08](DIR_E08_20261124.md)

## 2. Supremum over degree-one maps (E09)

The shared estimates give $Y_f\le\min(Y,0)$ for every smooth map of
degree $+1$. The supplied lower-bound family uses one positively
oriented normal chart, with $r=|x|$ and
$\epsilon\ll\rho=\epsilon^{1/2}\ll R=\epsilon^{1/4}$. On $r\le\rho$
it is the inverse stereographic map

\[
 F_\epsilon(x)=
 \left(\frac{2\epsilon x}{\epsilon^2+r^2},
       \frac{\epsilon^2-r^2}{\epsilon^2+r^2}\right).
\]

Its differential at the north-pole preimage $x=0$ is $2/\epsilon$
times the chosen orientation-preserving identification. On the
annulus $\rho\le r\le2\rho$ the cut-off south coordinate
$z=\kappa(r/\rho)\epsilon x/r^2$ has size $O(\epsilon/\rho)$ and
derivative $O(\epsilon/\rho^2)$. Taking $\kappa=1$ up to $1$ and
$\kappa=0$ from $2$ gives smooth matching to $F_\epsilon$ and to
the constant south pole. This finite south coordinate chart misses
the north pole. The north pole is therefore a regular value with
one positive preimage and the map has degree $+1$.

Set $b_\epsilon=2\epsilon/(\epsilon^2+r^2)$ and
$P_\epsilon=12b_\epsilon^2$. The exact flat pullback is
$F_\epsilon^*h=b_\epsilon^2\delta$, so $3|dF_\epsilon|^2_{\delta,h}
=12b_\epsilon^2$. Direct differentiation with
$\Delta_\delta=-\sum_i\partial_i^2$ gives
$\Delta_\delta b_\epsilon=16\epsilon^3/(\epsilon^2+r^2)^3
=2b_\epsilon^3$. For compactly supported tests, integration by parts
with this positive solution gives

\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^4}(6|dv|^2-P_\epsilon v^2)\,dx
   =6\int_{\mathbb R^4}b_\epsilon^2|d(v/b_\epsilon)|^2\,dx
   \ge0 .
\]

The inequality extends to $H^1_0(B_R)$ by density for fixed
$\epsilon$. The scale change $x=\epsilon y$ gives
$\int b_\epsilon^4\,dx=8\pi^2/3=\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)$ and
$\|P_\epsilon\|_2^2=144\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)$. From
$P_\epsilon\lesssim\epsilon^2/r^4$ at $r\ge\rho$ one gets tail
$L^2$ norm $O((\epsilon/\rho)^2)$. The cutoff annulus has the same
order for the actual $V_\epsilon=3|df_\epsilon|_{g,h}^2$ by the
derivative and the $O(\rho^4)$ volume. On $B_\rho$ the metric
and volume errors are $O(r^2)$. For $J=\sqrt{\det g}$, these imply

\[
 \|JV_\epsilon-P_\epsilon\|_{L^2(B_R)}
 \le C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2\big),\qquad
 \|Js_g\|_{L^2(B_R)}\le CR^2 .
\]

To preserve a bound uniform in test gradients, set
$\gamma=1-CR^2>0$ from the kinetic lower bound
$Jg^{-1}\ge\gamma I$. Add and subtract $\gamma P_\epsilon v^2$
in the local form. The part
$\gamma\int(6|dv|^2-P_\epsilon v^2)\,dx$ is nonnegative.
The difference of the potential densities, including
$(1-\gamma)P_\epsilon$, is $O(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2)$ in $L^2$.
Hölder and local norm equivalence now give
$Q_{f_\epsilon}(v)\ge-C(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2)\|v\|_4^2$ for
$v\in H^1_0(B_R)$.

With $\ell=\log(R/(2\rho))$, the stated cutoff
$\theta=\Theta(\log(r/(2\rho))/\ell)$ is constant before $2\rho$
and after $R$. For $\chi=\cos\theta,\ \eta=\sin\theta$, one has
$\chi^2+\eta^2=1$ and $\eta=0$ on the potential support.
The radial derivative bound $|d\theta|\le C/(\ell r)$ gives
$\|d\theta\|_4^4=O(\ell^{-3})$ by integration against $r^3\,dr$.
The IMS cross term cancels since
$\chi\,d\chi+\eta\,d\eta=0$. Its error is
$6\int_M|d\theta|^2u^2\,dV_g
\le C\ell^{-3/2}\|u\|_4^2$. Combining local and exterior forms yields

\[
 Q_{f_\epsilon}(u)\ge
 Y\|\eta u\|_4^2 -
 C\big(R^2+(\epsilon/\rho)^2+\ell^{-3/2}\big)\|u\|_4^2.
\]

For $Y\ge0$ the first term is nonnegative. For $Y<0$ it is at
least $Y\|u\|_4^2$. All three error terms vanish for the frozen
scales. This recovers $\sup_{\deg f=+1}Y_f=\min(Y,0)$ uniformly
over $L^4$-normalized tests.

The core signed pullback volume is $b_\epsilon^4 dx$ exactly.
Its integral tends to the target volume and the absolute annular
contribution is $O((\epsilon/\rho)^4)$. All support shrinks to
the chart center $p$. The signed degree measure converges weakly
to $\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)\delta_p$. The $L^2$ potential comparison
and $\sup_{B_R}|J-1|\to0$ give convergence of total potential-square
mass to $144\operatorname{Vol}_h(S^4)$ by Cauchy–Schwarz on the
difference of squares. Shrinking support gives the analogous
weak convergence of $V_\epsilon^2dV_g$ to this multiple of $\delta_p$.

If $Y\ge0$ a maximizing map must have $Y_f=0$ and thus be an
orientation-preserving conformal diffeomorphism by the shared
rigidity. Such a map attains zero and gives $Y>0$ by Sobolev
control of the round positive scalar form. The $Y=0$ case is
unattained. If $Y<0$, use the positive normalized ordinary Yamabe
minimizer from the negative direct argument in §1. A degree-one
map is nonconstant, so its smooth potential is positive on some
open set. Testing with that minimizer gives
$Y_f\le Y-\int_M V_f u^2\,dV_g<Y$, proving nonattainment. The
family members with constant patches never realize a maximizing
map. [Frozen E09](DIR_E09_20261124.md)

The local construction uses orientation and closedness for the
degree, and compactness and smoothness for the estimates and
attainment argument. Spin is used for Listing's theorem. No homology,
signature or simple-connectivity hypothesis on the domain enters.
In particular, E05's individual chiral indices used $\tau(M)=0$,
but this part of E05 is not invoked. The generic spin-domain theorem
uses the product index $\chi(S^4)\deg f$, which does not require
$\tau(M)=0$. The proof retains the given target, dimension and
smoothness. [Listing 2010, §3 product index, p. 11](https://home.mathematik.uni-freiburg.de/mlisting/scalar_symmetric.pdf#page=11)
