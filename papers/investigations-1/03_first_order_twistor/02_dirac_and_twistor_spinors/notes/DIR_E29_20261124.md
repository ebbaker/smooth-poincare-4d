## Local divergence

Let \((N,h)\) be a smooth connected oriented Riemannian spin
four-manifold with positive-definite metric. Use the metric spin
connection and \(c_h(\xi)^2=-|\xi|_h^2\). Let \(\Psi\) be a smooth
nontrivial chiral spinor with \(D_h\Psi=0\). For every zero \(q\)
and every neighborhood \(U\) of \(q\),
\[
 \int_{U\cap\{\Psi\ne0\}}
 \frac{|\nabla^h\Psi|_h^4}{|\Psi|_h^4}\,dV_h=+\infty.
\]
The local proof applies to any smooth section of a bundle with a
metric connection, at a zero in the closure of its nonzero locus.

To obtain this density hypothesis for the spinor, work in a smooth
spin frame on a relatively compact coordinate ball. The coordinate
principal part of \(D_h^2\) is
\(-h^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j I\) by the Clifford anticommutation
relations. Since \(D_h^2\Psi=0\), its real component vector \(v\)
satisfies inequalities
\[
 |A v_a|^2\ \leq\ C\sum_b\bigl(|\partial v_b|^2+|v_b|^2\bigr),
 \qquad A=h^{ij}\partial_i\partial_j ,
\]
using the same scalar elliptic \(A\) for all components. The principal
coefficients are smooth and positive definite, the lower-order
matrices are bounded on the chosen ball, and \(v\) has locally
square-integrable derivatives through order two. Aronszajn's
system extension of the scalar theorem implies vanishing on the
ball if the components vanish on an open subset of it. A zero
neighborhood thus propagates across the connected domain by
overlapping coordinate balls. Since \(\Psi\) is nontrivial, its
nonzero set is dense. This application uses only the open-set
consequence of unique continuation. [Aronszajn, §1, (1.1) and
restrictions 1°–2°, pp. 235–236, and Remark 3, p. 248][aronszajn].

## Clipped logarithms and radial means

Take a precompact Euclidean coordinate ball \(B\) centered at
\(q=0\), with closure in the neighborhood under test. On \(B\)
Euclidean and metric gradient norms and measures are uniformly
equivalent. Put \(f=|\Psi|_h\). Smoothness gives \(f(x)\leq C_0|x|\)
near zero for a constant \(C_0>0\). On the nonzero locus
\(|df|_h\leq|\nabla^h\Psi|_h\), by differentiation of the norm with
the metric connection. Assume the stated integral on \(B\) is finite.
For integers \(K>0\) consider the locally Lipschitz functions
\[
 u_K=\log\!\left(\max(f,e^{-K})\right).
\]
The clipping makes \(u_K\) locally constant on \(\{f<e^{-K}\}\),
including at zeros. On \(\{f>0\}\) the Lipschitz chain rule gives
\(|d u_K|_h\leq |\nabla^h\Psi|_h/f\) almost everywhere. Thus the
Euclidean \(\|\nabla u_K\|_{L^4(B)}\) are bounded uniformly in \(K\).

Since \(q\) is in the closure of the nonzero locus, a smaller ball
\(A_0\subset B\) exists on which \(f\) has a strictly positive lower
bound. For large \(K\), \(u_K=\log f\) is bounded on \(A_0\). Let
\((u_K)_B\) denote its Lebesgue mean. The ball Poincaré inequality
bounds \(\|u_K-(u_K)_B\|_{L^4(B)}\) uniformly. Restricting to the anchor
ball gives
\[
 |A_0|^{1/4}|(u_K)_B|
 \leq \|u_K\|_{L^4(A_0)}
      +\|u_K-(u_K)_B\|_{L^4(A_0)} .
\]
The right side is uniformly bounded. Consequently \(u_K\) is bounded
in \(W^{1,4}(B)\). Fatou's lemma for \(|u_K|^4\) shows that
\(\{f=0\}\) has Lebesgue measure zero and \(u=\log f\) lies in
\(L^4(B)\), interpreted almost everywhere. On \(\{f>0\}\),
\(|u_K|\leq|u|\), so dominated convergence gives strong \(L^4\)
convergence. Weak compactness in \(W^{1,4}\) then gives
\(u\in W^{1,4}(B)\). In particular the measure-zero conclusion
has followed from the assumed finite cost.

Write the spherical mean as \(m(r)=\fint_{S^3}u(r\theta)\,d\theta\)
for almost every \(r\). On intervals bounded away from zero it has a
locally absolutely continuous representative. Indeed, the weak radial
derivative on each polar annulus can be averaged over the unit sphere
to give the weak derivative of the mean there. Jensen's inequality
then bounds \(|m'(s)|^4\) by \(\fint_{S^3}|\partial_s u(s\theta)|^4
\,d\theta\). Fix a radius \(r_0\) with finite mean inside the ball.
Integrating the derivative and using Hölder with the polar weight
\(s^3\,ds\) gives
\[
 |m(r)-m(r_0)|
 \leq |S^3|^{-1/4}
       \|\nabla u\|_{L^4(\{r<|x|<r_0\})}
       \bigl(\log(r_0/r)\bigr)^{3/4}.
\]
On the other hand, \(u(x)\leq\log(C_0|x|)\) almost everywhere for
small \(|x|\). Fubini gives \(m(r)\leq\log(C_0r)\) for almost every
small \(r\). Hence \(m(r_0)-m(r)\) grows at least linearly in
\(\log(r_0/r)\) along radii tending to zero. This contradicts the
last bound because \(\|\nabla u\|_{L^4(B)}<\infty\). The argument
works in every sufficiently small ball and uses no isolation,
transversality or finite-order hypothesis on the zero.

## Transport from the flat end

Now take a smooth closed connected oriented spin four-manifold
\((M,g)\), with \(g\) exactly flat on a ball at \(p\), and let
\(N=M\setminus\{p\}\) with \(h=\omega^2 g\). Here \(\omega\) is
smooth and positive on \(N\) and equals \(r^{-2}\) near \(p\), with
\(r\) the flat coordinate radius. Assume the flat end and spinor
hypotheses of [E28 request v1][e28]. In an oriented parallel end frame
and radius \(R=r^{-1}\), these are
\[
 D_h\Psi=0,\qquad |\Psi_\infty|=1,\qquad
 \Psi-\Psi_\infty=O(R^{-3}),\qquad
 |\nabla^h\Psi|_h=O(R^{-4}).
\]
Assume also the stated smooth compact extension of the twistor-weight
field. The end is Euclidean. For all sufficiently large \(R\),
\(|\Psi|\geq 1/2\) and there are no zeros there. The tail from radius
\(R\) outward satisfies
\[
 \int_{\{R'\geq R\}} \frac{|\nabla^h\Psi|_h^4}{|\Psi|_h^4}\,dV_h
   \leq C\int_R^\infty (R')^{-16}(R')^3\,dR'
   =O(R^{-12}) .
\]

Use the chirality-preserving, unitary conformal identification
\(\beta:S_g\to S_h\), and set \(\psi=\omega^{-1/2}\beta^{-1}\Psi\)
on \(N\). For the covector-valued twistor operator
\((P_g\psi)(X)=\nabla^g_X\psi+\frac14X\cdot_gD_g\psi\), the conformal
identities are
\[
 |\Psi|_h=\omega^{1/2}|\psi|_g,\qquad
 (P_h\Psi)(X)=\omega^{1/2}\beta((P_g\psi)(X)),\qquad
 |P_h\Psi|_h=\omega^{-1/2}|P_g\psi|_g .
\]
The second identity is for the same tangent vector \(X\), and the
third uses metric orthonormal frames. The full local off-kernel
calculation is [E07][e07], with the kernel-weight convention from
[Friedrich, §1, eq. (1.1) and the conformal paragraph, pp. 59–60][friedrich].
Since \(dV_h=\omega^4dV_g\) and \(P_h\Psi=\nabla^h\Psi\), on
\(N\cap\{\Psi\ne0\}\) there is the exact density identity
\[
 \frac{|P_g\psi|_g^4}{|\psi|_g^4}\,dV_g
   =\frac{|\nabla^h\Psi|_h^4}{|\Psi|_h^4}\,dV_h .
\]
The equality holds as an equality of integrals with possible value
\(+\infty\). It is applied on the punctured manifold, where \(\omega\)
is smooth and positive, and the point \(p\) is omitted from the
compact relative integral.

In flat compact coordinates the asymptotics and spinor weight give
\[
 |\psi|_g=r(1+O(r^3)),\qquad
 |P_g\psi|_g=r^{-1}|P_h\Psi|_h=O(r^3),\qquad
 |P_g\psi|_g/|\psi|_g=O(r^2).
\]
In particular the compact twistor ratio is integrable to the fourth
power near the pole, with cost to the fourth power \(O(\epsilon^{12})\)
in a radius-\(\epsilon\) ball. By the assumed smooth extension,
\(\psi(p)=0\), its real derivative is an isometry by the leading
norm \(r\), and \(P_g\psi(p)=0\). The jet is therefore
\((\nabla^g_X\psi)(p)=X\cdot_g\eta\) with
\(\eta=-D_g\psi(p)/4\) a unit spinor of opposite chirality. This is the
prescribed type of transverse zero for the compact twistor ratio.
The compact covariant-derivative ratio with \(\nabla^g\) in the
numerator instead diverges at \(p\) by the local section lemma.

Every additional zero of \(\psi\) is an interior zero of \(\Psi\) and
has infinite cost by the local result. Conversely, if \(\Psi\) is
nowhere zero on \(N\), the ratio is bounded on the compact region
left after removing a sufficiently far end, and the tail is finite.
Thus the global relative cost is finite exactly when the only
compact zero is the distinguished \(p\). Vanishing cost would imply
\(\nabla^h\Psi=0\) and, by the density identity and smoothness,
\(P_g\psi=0\) on \(M\). This argument gives neither a nonvanishing
guarantee for the boundary solution nor an implication from finite
positive cost to parallelism. Those conclusions require further
input at [B15][b15].
