Let $A$ be the intrinsic Dirac operator of the unit round $S^3$ in the normal convention $L=\partial_t+A$; $|A|\ge\kappa=3/2$. For a homogeneous harmonic field on $[0,L]\times S^3$ with positive incoming data $x$ at zero and negative data $y$ at $L$, $u(t)=e^{-t|A|}x+e^{-(L-t)|A|}y$. The two summands are orthogonal in the slice $L^2$. For any finite family, whose size may depend on $L$, at the middle slice
$$
 \int_{S^3}\sum_a |u_a(L/2)|^2\le
 e^{-\kappa L}\sum_a(\|x_a\|_2^2+\|y_a\|_2^2).
$$
Consequently any joint pointwise lower bound $c^2$ on the whole neck satisfies $c^2\operatorname{vol}(S^3)\le e^{-3L/2}\sum_a(\|x_a\|^2+\|y_a\|^2)$. Thus a uniform floor requires exponentially growing *total squared trace norm* in this gauge. A single lowest eigenspinor gives the sharp decay in slice norm. Two orthogonal sign subspaces need not be pointwise orthogonal. This integral test avoids pointwise cancellation assumptions. The claim is about product-gauge incoming data. One must first convert a physical pole or core normalization into these data. Scaling all fields multiplies the trace budget and the differentiated defect by the same squared scalar.
