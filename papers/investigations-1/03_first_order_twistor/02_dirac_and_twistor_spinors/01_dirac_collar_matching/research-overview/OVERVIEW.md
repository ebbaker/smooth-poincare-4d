## Question and role in smooth recognition

On a supplied smooth oriented Riemannian spin collar, what bounds on the metric or its slice operators suffice for a length-uniform Dirac inverse? Can that inverse match the **actual spinors on the adjoining pieces**, with enough normalization and derivative control to say something about curvature on the original homotopy four-sphere? This branch develops the sufficient conditions and tests the gluing. The [extraction branch](../../../01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/research-overview/OVERVIEW.md) seeks collars realizing the conditions. The [neck geometry branch](../../../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/research-overview/OVERVIEW.md) can use a quantitative curvature contribution or a criterion for a cut. Both can ask for a reformulation if the geometric data available on a sequence are weaker. [Dirac §§2.1–2.3](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L155) · [Parent first-order manuscript §3.2](../../../manuscript/first_order_twistor_and_smooth_poincare.tex#L320) · [Shared proof route](../../../brainstorm/notes/COLLAR_PROGRAM_AND_SPC4_20261125.md)

The eventual source is an arbitrary smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\) with its spin structure. The model problem is local and uses a supplied \(I\times S^3\), specified spin identification and conformal factor. The fixed-strip estimate is useful at a single cut. If the application is a long neck, all constants and the final energy budget must be tracked through \(|I|\to\infty\). The collar may have nonzero Weyl curvature. The flat germ used to specify an exact Green pole is at the puncture of the capped manifold. The Green construction also uses an invertible compact Dirac operator. Exact conformally flat insertion is a separate special case. [Dirac §4.1, canonical boundary data](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L516) · [§10.1, flat insertion](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L1970)

## Three successive questions

1. **A boundary inverse.** On \(h_0=dt^2+\gamma_{S^3(1)}\) write \(L_0=\partial_t+A\) after fixing the normal Clifford convention. Prescribe \(P_+u(a)\) and \(P_-u(b)\), the positive and negative spectral traces at the two ends. The reference gap is \(3/2\). Audit the \(L^2\)-to-\(H^1\) inverse and \(H^{1/2}\) trace lifting uniformly for \(|I|\ge2\). Test its stability when \(h-h_0\) is small in \(L^\infty\) and its first derivative is uniformly small in slab \(L^4\). A second criterion could use varying slice operators and an explicit low-mode analysis. [Bär, Theorem 1](https://www.math.uni-potsdam.de/fileadmin/user_upload/Prof-Geometrie/Dokumente/Publikationen/sphere.pdf) · [Dirac §3.3, coefficient comparison](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L489) · [Original collar calculation](../brainstorm/notes/RELAXING_COLLAR_HYPOTHESES_20261125.md)
2. **Actual traces.** The admissible end data are traces of interior solutions on the cap and core. Compare these spaces with the graph of the collar solution map, accounting for complementary normal orientations, any kernels or poles, and the conformal weights in the physical metric. The first target is a uniform inverse for the matching equation on a specified family, possibly after finitely many kernel or compatibility conditions. A round fixed-projection inverse alone leaves this comparison open. [Dirac §§4.1–4.3](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L516) · [The geometric data to request](../../../01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/research-overview/OVERVIEW.md)
3. **Curvature or smooth global attainment.** For positive half-spinors \(\psi_a\) put \(T_a=P_g\psi_a\), where \(P_g\) is the twistor operator. The exact identity in §2.3 gives
   \[
   |\Pi(d^\nabla T_a)^+|^2=\tfrac12 |W_g^+|_{\rm HS}^2|\psi_a|^2.
   \]
   If \(\sum_a|\psi_a|^2\ge c^2>0\), a bound on the total integral of the squared differentiated defects bounds \(E_+\) on that region. Spinors can decay in the middle of a product neck. Small first-order errors alone allow the oscillatory Green fields of §4.4. An alternative endpoint is a normalized, nonzero exact twistor field on the same smooth \(M\), under the hypotheses of §3.3. For instance, after spin identification, require uniform and strong \(W^{1,4}\) convergence of the metrics to a smooth positive metric on \(M\), unit \(L^2\) spinor norm and \(\|P_{g_i}\psi_i\|_2\to0\). An exact field may have zeros. [§2.3, projected derivative](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L276) · [§4.4, counterexample](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L780) · [§3.3, conditional attainment](../../manuscript/dirac_twistor_and_yamabe_rigidity.tex#L489)

For SPC4 the local output must enter a global argument. Bounds on \(E_+\) that cover the full remainder and vanish after summation suffice for the one-chirality energy identity in §12.4. A full tensor bound on a particular collar requires the other chirality as well. An attained positive-chiral twistor field on \(M\) instead yields \(W^+=0\), hence smooth recognition. The parent DIR project also has a distinct spectral/Yamabe route. Its round equality criterion and integral Ricci test are global, and the Delaunay examples on \(S^1\times S^3\) delimit interpretations of relative pinching.
