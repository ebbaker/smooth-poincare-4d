## What can now be located on the original manifold

Let \(M\) be a fixed smooth oriented homotopy four-sphere. Work with smooth Riemannian volume-one physical Yamabe minimizers \(g_i\) in the stated band \(4\pi^2\le E_+(g_i)=E_-(g_i)\le8\pi^2-\delta\); the chiral norm is Hilbert–Schmidt on real \(\Lambda^\pm\). Pass to \(Y_i=s_{g_i}\to y\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\), \(E_+(g_i)\to e\). No exact Bach equation is needed to locate volume. The result applies in particular when \(\int |B_{g_i}|dV_{g_i}\to0\).

**For every \(\epsilon,\zeta>0\), a subsequence has a uniformly finite collection of disjoint smoothly embedded standard four-balls carrying together at least \(1-\epsilon\) of the physical volume, each with physical full-curvature energy below \(\zeta\).** The balls may be chosen on arbitrarily small fixed scales in the normalized positive Ricci representative described below. Their positions persist from prescribed regular points of one actual distance limit. The number depends on the tolerances and the subsequence. Their boundaries are genuine smooth three-spheres in \(M\). Neither the curvature energy of their complement nor the critical first derivatives of their pullback metrics are controlled by this statement.

The scale is determined by the physical probability \(\mu_i=dV_{g_i}\). In a smooth conformal positive Ricci representative, rescale distances so that the least radius of a ball carrying half of \(\mu_i\) equals one. Call the resulting metric \(k_i\) and a maximizing center \(p_i\). Bishop comparison bounds the conformally invariant fourth-power Dirichlet integral of a logarithmic cutoff by \(2\pi^2/\log^3(R/r)\). The physical Yamabe inequality then bounds the fraction of physical volume on its inner plateau when the support has physical mass at most one half. Iteration controls small-ball mass at every center and distant tails about \(p_i\). A finite cover of the median ball gives noncollapse of \(k_i\) on every bounded window. Finally \(g_i=w_i^2k_i\) and \(R_{k_i}>0\) imply \(-6\Delta_{k_i}w_i\le Y_iw_i^3\). The small-ball bound absorbs the critical potential in a first power test. Iteration gives \(w_i\le C_D\) on \(B_{k_i}(p_i,D)\), hence \(\mu_i\le C_D^4dV_{k_i}\). The constants here are local in the normalized positive Ricci distance. [Proof and finite-ball construction](../01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/notes/E16_POSITIVE_RICCI_VOLUME_MARKING_20261125.md) · [Independent capacity check](../01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/reviews/CAPACITY_MARKING_CHECK_20261125.md) · [Density and smooth-embedding check](../01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/reviews/POSITIVE_RICCI_DENSITY_CHECK_20261125.md).

In the resulting pointed noncollapsed Ricci limit, \(\mu_i\) has a limiting probability with locally bounded density with respect to \(\mathcal H^4\). Thus physical volume lies at four-dimensional metric regular points and misses the atoms of any bounded curvature measure there. At a point of positive physical density first take \(i\to\infty\) at fixed continuity radii, then shrink the radii. The physical ball mass divided by \(\omega_4r^4\) in these **positive Ricci distances** has a positive limit, while the physical curvature energy in a fixed multiple of the ball tends to zero. Almost-maximal \(k_i\)-volume gives a smooth local ball marking on the original source by the Cheeger–Colding embedding theorem. Vitali selection followed by finite transfer gives the result above. There is as yet no proof that one of these spheres separates two prescribed positive **chiral** masses, or that their individual markings extend to a common closed base. ## What a whole first-order marking would give

Suppose \(N\) is a smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere with finitely many smooth marked points, and one compatible smooth embedding compares each whole truncation in \(M\). In a chosen conformal gauge write \(\Phi_i^*g_i=u_i^2m_i\). It suffices that \(m_i\to h\) locally uniformly and strongly in \(W^{1,4}\), that \(h\) extend smoothly on \(N\), and that \(\|R_{m_i}\|_{L^2}\) be bounded on each fixed compact. Scalar curvature here belongs to \(m_i\); the source controls it automatically only in physical gauges up to homothety. The Euler transgression and the conformally invariant Weyl budget bound the full comparison curvature locally. Both chiral Weyl matrices then converge strongly in \(L^2_{\rm loc}\) by testing the first variation with a cutoff times \(m_i-h\). A fixed second test gives \(B_h=0\).

If one fixed compared compact carries nonzero limiting chiral energy, the closed strict Bach gap gives \(E_+(h)>4\pi^2\). Splicing first to \(h\) at fixed truncation and then capping its ordinary punctures leaves every exterior below the \(4\pi^2\) recognition threshold, hence \(N\cong M\) with the stated orientation. At \(e=I^+(M)\) equality \(e=E_+(h)\) rules out evacuation: evacuation would cost at least \((Y_{\rm rd}^2-y^2)/48>0\). A positive fixed-compact physical volume also detects nonroundness when \(y<Y_{\rm rd}\). In a whole \(k_i\)-comparison as above, almost Euclidean Bishop volume at the ordinary punctures rules out deep complementary exits; volume saturation in that gauge also covers the round Yamabe threshold. A round base can still have a curvature residual. In the saturated branch the recovered physical factor converges strongly locally in \(W^{1,p}\) for each \(p<4\) and in finite \(L^q\). The exponent \(p>3\) controls the one-dimensional size of the exceptional sets on which lengths might shorten. With the marked exterior diameters and physical noncollapse this gives measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of the **whole physical sources** to the smooth unit-volume Yamabe base. At minimizing energy for the nonround base, Gauss–Bonnet gives equality of total physical trace-free Ricci norms as well. Testing the conformal Ricci relation identifies their weak limit. The physical scalar, trace-free Ricci and both Weyl energy measures then have no residual on exhaustion. This does not establish smooth convergence of the physical pullbacks at marked ordinary points or elsewhere in the given coordinates.
