**Judgment.** On a minimizing sequence in the stated band, a supplied **nonround conformal base** already gives a smooth unit-volume Yamabe minimizer on the original smooth type, physical convergence on each fixed truncation, measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence of the whole source and zero curvature defect measures. ## What an evacuated exterior must carry

Use [EXTRACT-E09](../01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/notes/E09_CONFORMAL_BASE_VOLUME_DICHOTOMY_20261125.md). The physical metrics \(g_i\) are smooth unit-volume positive Yamabe minimizers on a fixed smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\), with \(Y_i\to y>0\), \(E_i\to e\le8\pi^2-\delta\) and \(\int|B_{g_i}|\to0\). Here \(E_i=E_+(g_i)\) uses the Hilbert–Schmidt norm on real \(\Lambda^+\). The smooth closed homotopy-sphere conformal base \((N,[h])\) has finitely many smooth marked points. Require one oriented compatible comparison of every whole compact truncation. Write \(e_N=E_+(h)\). Suppose physical volume vanishes on every fixed compared compact. Evacuation requires at least one puncture since an embedding of the whole closed base would cover \(M\).

Use the one-face exteriors and positive-Yamabe caps of [NECK-E09](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E09_FINITE_PUNCTURE_TRANSFER_20261125.md). First choose a finite buffer length giving positive cap sign. Fix sufficiently small disjoint normal radii \(b\) and take \(i\to\infty\) before \(b\downarrow0\). Pass to a subsequence for the finitely many exterior masses along a countable nested exhaustion. Let \(\lambda_j\) be the limiting physical volume of the \(j\)-th exterior. Its value is independent of the fixed radius, since each intervening compared annulus evacuates. Write \(q_j\) for its limiting raw chiral energy after exhaustion. Then \(\sum_j\lambda_j=1\) and \(\sum_jq_j=e-e_N\).

Choose a smooth cutoff \(\chi_j\) equal to one throughout the deep exterior and zero near the cap splice and on the core. Its transition is on one fixed compared annulus on the retained side. There \(\|d\chi_j\|_{L^4(g_i)}\) is bounded by conformal invariance and smooth comparison. Hölder gives \(\int|d\chi_j|^2dV_i\to0\) since its transition has vanishing physical volume. The physical integrals of \(\chi_j^2\) and \(\chi_j^4\) both tend to \(\lambda_j\). Applying the original source Yamabe inequality to each cutoff yields
\[
  y\sqrt{\lambda_j}\le y\lambda_j.
\]
Thus exactly one marked exterior has limiting volume one, already using the positive source Yamabe inequality and evacuation.

Let \(Y_{ij,b}\) be the Yamabe constant of the capped exterior. On the support of \(\chi_j\) its metric is conformal to the physical metric. Transport the test by this conformal factor, extending by zero across the splice. For \(\lambda_j>0\) the same numerator bound gives \(\limsup_iY_{ij,b}\le y\sqrt{\lambda_j}\). On a closed positive-Yamabe homotopy-sphere cap in its own volume-one Yamabe metric, Gauss–Bonnet and signature give
\[
  48E_+ =384\pi^2-Y_{ij,b}^{\,2}+12\int|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2.
\]
The cap's chiral energy is bounded above by the raw exterior energy plus the squared splice error, which vanishes in the iterated limit. With \(Y_{\rm rd}^2=384\pi^2\), this proves
\[
  q_j\ \ge\ 8\pi^2-\frac{y^2\lambda_j}{48}\quad(\lambda_j>0),
  \qquad
  e-e_N\ \ge\ \frac{Y_{\rm rd}^2-y^2}{48}.
\]
Even the per-exterior energy bound alone precludes two positive-volume exits in the strict band, since their combined cost would be at least \(16\pi^2-y^2/48\ge8\pi^2\). All physical integrals above precede cap normalization. The Gauss–Bonnet normalization is [Chang–Gursky–Yang, (0.4)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf#page=3).

The Green-function argument gives an independent coefficient check. If the normalized factors \(u_i/c_i\), \(c_i=u_i(x_0)\to0\), converge smoothly on fixed compacts to a positive sum \(w\) of Green functions with \(\ell\ge1\) poles, then \(w^2h\) is scalar-flat with \(\ell\) asymptotically Euclidean ends. Its Euler characteristic is \(2-\ell\), and each end subtracts one further unit from the interior Gauss–Bonnet integral. Thus \(D_X=4e_N+32\pi^2(\ell-1)\). Constant rescaling on fixed compared compacts gives \(D_X\le\lim_iD_i=4e+y^2/12-32\pi^2<32\pi^2\). This forces \(\ell=1\) and reproduces the residual bound. The pole lies at the volume-carrying exit. Indeed a cutoff equal to one outside a fixed collar of that exit and zero deep inside it has support of vanishing physical volume. Its gradient square integral is \(O(c_i^2)\) by smooth normalized convergence on the transition. The original Yamabe inequality absorbs the scalar term and gives \(\|\chi\|_4^2=O(c_i^2)\). Hence the \(c_i^{-2}g_i\)-volume of the region where \(\chi=1\) stays bounded. A pole anywhere in that region gives infinite limiting volume on exhaustion. [Lee–Parker, Lemma 6.4 and Theorem 6.5, end expansion in conformal normal coordinates](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf#page=29)

## Consequences for extraction

Compactly supported conformal tests give \(Y([h])\ge y\), and the conformally invariant Bach density gives \(B_h=0\). For nonround \([h]\), sharp Yamabe rigidity gives \(y\le Y([h])<Y_{\rm rd}\). The closed Bach gap gives \(e_N>4\pi^2\). Its proof uses smooth recognition at or below the Weyl threshold before invoking the Bach-flat rigidity theorem on standard \(S^4\). Hence \(e-e_N<4\pi^2\), and the marked caps give an oriented diffeomorphism \(N\cong M\). The transferred class gives \(I^+(M)\le e_N\). [Schoen, Theorem 2](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen.yamabe.pdf#page=15) · [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorems A and B](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf#page=3) · [Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1](https://web.math.princeton.edu/~chang/21Chang-Chen-nz.pdf) · [SEC §12.1](../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L4357)

If \(e=I^+(M)\), then \(e=e_N\) and the evacuation inequality contradicts \(y<Y_{\rm rd}\). EXTRACT-E09 gives a positive smooth limiting conformal factor, \(Y([h])=y\) and physical volume saturation. The [EXTRACT-E10 metric and curvature argument](../01_sections_and_connections/02_dirac_collar_extraction/notes/E10_METRIC_LIMIT_AND_CURVATURE_ATOMS_20261125.md) then applies. In its notation the weights of the two Weyl, trace-free Ricci and full four-index curvature defects are \(e_j,e_j,4e_j,16e_j\). Their common Weyl sum vanishes. More generally, if \(e-I^+(M)<(Y_{\rm rd}^2-y^2)/48\), physical recovery still follows and the common Weyl sum is at most \(e-I^+(M)\). The uniform margin \(\kappa_\delta\) of [SEC §12.2](../01_sections_and_connections/manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L4550) suffices here since the nonround exact base belongs to the same fixed band.

For a round physical base the [marked cap restart](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E19_ROUND_BASE_SINGLE_PUNCTURE_20261125.md) still carries the nonstandard minimizing type and its chiral energy through one shrinking exterior. At \(y=Y_{\rm rd}\), a complete nonflat smooth ALE comparison satisfying SEC §11 would already contradict sharp Yamabe rigidity of its positive smooth compactification. The rescaling at fixed chiral mass therefore tests whether curvature survives in a limit to which that exclusion applies. The [MATCH-E14 estimate](../02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/01_dirac_collar_matching/notes/E14_WEAK_CHART_BACH_DEFECT_20261125.md) uses a supplied chart in the physical metric up to constant scaling. A rescaling by a varying conformal factor requires a new bound on full curvature in that gauge. The open geometrical step is to obtain the actual whole-truncation comparisons or separating charts. Classification of a surviving closed nonround minimizer is a further step toward smooth sphere recognition.
