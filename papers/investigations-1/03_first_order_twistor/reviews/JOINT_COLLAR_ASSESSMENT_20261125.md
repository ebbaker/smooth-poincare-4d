## Same-manifold result

Let $M$ be a fixed smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere and $E_+=\int|W^+|_{\rm HS}^2$. Consider smooth, volume-one, positive Yamabe minimizers $g_i$, with $E_+(g_i)\to e$ in $[4\pi^2,8\pi^2-\delta]$, $\delta>0$, and $\int_M |B_{g_i}|\,dV_i\to0$. Suppose one nonflat, smooth, complete ALE rescaling satisfies SEC §11, including differentiated end decay and oriented smooth comparison on whole compact truncations. Then its compactification provides a metric on the **original smooth $M$** with
$$
 M\cong\widehat X,\qquad
 4\pi^2< E_+(\widehat X)=e_X\le e-\kappa_\delta,\qquad
 Y(\widehat X)>0,\qquad B_{\widehat X}=0 .
$$
Here $Y(\widehat X)$ denotes the Yamabe constant of the supplied compactified class. With $I^+(M)$ the infimum of $E_+$ over positive Yamabe classes on this smooth type, even a sequence with $e-I^+(M)<\kappa_\delta$ excludes that bubble. For exact minimization the strict residual $e_X<e$ already suffices. The constant $\kappa_\delta$ is inherited from the compactness argument for exact closed critical classes in SEC §12.2; no effective value was computed. [Proof](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E02_E05_SAME_TYPE_DESCENT_20261125.md) · [Independent proof check](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/reviews/INDEPENDENT_DESCENT_AND_SELECTION_20261125.md)

**Why the smooth type follows.** Take the ball around the added point in the *smooth compactification's* normal coordinates. Its boundary and both buffers transfer through one full-core embedding. At fixed buffer length choose the radius small, then the source index large. The capped exterior still has positive Yamabe sign and its energy is at most $e-e_X+\varepsilon<4\pi^2$. Chang–Gursky–Yang recognize that capped exterior as standard. The core cap is the particular smooth coordinate ball used to define the cut. Reassembly along the marked boundary gives $M\cong\widehat X\#S^4$. The proof neither classifies $\widehat X$ as standard nor needs control of where the exterior energy went. [Cap costs](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E01_TWO_CAP_AUDIT_20261125.md) · [Marked smooth cut](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E03_EMBEDDED_CUT_20261125.md) · [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A and Remark 2](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf#page=3)

**Smooth base with finitely many punctures.** Suppose instead that a smooth positive-Yamabe closed component $N$ minus finitely many smooth points admits simultaneous oriented smooth conformal comparison on entire compact truncations. If $0\le e-E_+(N)<4\pi^2$, the same construction at every puncture recognizes every capped exterior. The incidence calculation gives one exterior per boundary sphere. The original smooth type is $N$. Thus on a minimizing sequence $E_+(N)=e$, and the entire exterior chiral energy vanishes in the order $i\to\infty$ followed by exhaustion. Here accounting is a consequence of comparison, strict exterior budget and minimization. In particular this applies to a positive-energy smooth closed Bach-flat base above the $4\pi^2$ closed gap in the fixed band. Smooth marked compact comparison is essential. ## What a weaker cut can do

A fixed buffered **embedded** Euclidean cone chart, initially with link $S^3/\Gamma$, suffices for an inexpensive cut when the source metric after constant scaling is $m=\delta_{\rm eucl}+v$, with small $\|v\|_\infty+\|Dv\|_4$ and bounded intrinsic $\|\mathrm{Rm}_m\|_2$. The source band supplies the last bound in the physical gauge. In the all-lowered Riemann tensor the coefficients of the second derivatives of $m$ are constant. Comparing the interpolated Riemann tensors before taking the chiral part trades a chart Hessian for $\|v\|_\infty\|\mathrm{Rm}_m\|_2$. If the splice previously carried chiral energy $q$, its **increase** is at most $2\sqrt q\,\Delta+\Delta^2$ with $\Delta\to0$. The Yamabe form comparison uses integration by parts and first derivatives on fixed buffers. Control of the $L^1$ Bach cost after the cut still has a separate fourth-order hypothesis. [First-order splice, including group normalizations](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E07_FIRST_ORDER_CAP_20261125.md) · [Critical-Hessian intermediate estimate](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E06_WEAK_CAP_20261125.md)

An initially unknown finite group can be identified **on this one collar**. Straighten the middle to an exact round product in the closed source. If the resulting total $E_+$ is below $9\pi^2$, the link is $S^3$. Hillman's finite-group list and the smooth spin filling obstruction leave only the quaternion group to test. Its round link has odd-signature eta invariant of magnitude $3/4$. The two complementary signatures vanish. The Atiyah–Patodi–Singer formula forces $E_+-E_-=+9\pi^2$ on one side, exceeding the available total $E_+$. In the source band there is uniform room for this interpolation. This argument identifies a supplied space-form link; it does not construct the first annular chart or place its slice between two particular masses. [Single-collar quotient test](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E08_QUOTIENT_LINK_TEST_20261125.md) · [Primary source records](../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_SEC_20261124.md)

For a finite set of such spherical cuts, suppose all capped pieces have positive Yamabe constants and the total energy including splice increases is below $8\pi^2$. All but at most one are standard. If $M$ has nonstandard smooth type, the remaining piece carries that type. Write $\gamma_*$ for the allowance on this surviving piece. The chiral energy on **all other raw pieces of $M$** satisfies
$$
 E_+(g;M\setminus P_*)\le E_+(g)-I^+(M)+\gamma_* .
$$
A genuinely balanced split across a cheap cut is impossible along a minimizing sequence on a nonstandard candidate. This statement requires no prior ALE component, approximate Bach equation or no-neck theorem. In particular, if any positive class on a nonstandard candidate has $E_+<8\pi^2$, a positive-class minimizing sequence in a fixed band can already be used for this cut test. Obtaining additional compactness or small Bach error for it is separate. [Finite-cut argument](../01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/notes/E10_NONSPLITTING_MINIMIZERS_20261125.md)

### Actual boundaries and the differentiated defect

On actual exact-product-ended pieces with positive-Yamabe cylindrical compactifications, the closed Dirac inverse constructs their Cauchy trace spaces as bounded graphs. On a length-$L$ round cylinder the norm of the composed return operator is at most $\|G_L\|\|G_R\|e^{-3L}$. This covers two real interiors, with the genuine affine trace of a prescribed pole as input. On the original closed positive-Yamabe metric, one can also correct arbitrary $H^{1/2}$ jumps at two protected near-product seams by solving a single compact Dirac equation. Its bound is uniform in the local seam gauges. Regular corrections preserve a prescribed pole strength. [Real core graphs](../02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/01_dirac_collar_matching/notes/E06_ACTUAL_CORE_GRAPHS_20261125.md) · [Global jump problem](../02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/01_dirac_collar_matching/notes/E07_GLOBAL_JUMP_SOLVER_20261125.md)

For the actual regular ball and flat-pole cap in the round conformal class, the pole excites a single lowest mode. Its cylinder midpoint amplitude is $e^{-3L/4}$ times its incoming amplitude. The incoming amplitude for a fixed physical source varies as the cap radius to the power $-3/2$. Thus moving the cap while holding its physical source fixed can reverse the apparent decay. More generally, a uniform combined incoming budget on the product forces the joint squared spinor floor to be $O(e^{-3L/2})$. For global regular jump corrections there is also an upper bound in the conformally invariant $L^{8/3}$ norm. Exponential weights extend interior attenuation to the supplied varying families with a uniform inverse. These are upper bounds on regular fields or fields with measured end data. ## Remaining global alternatives

The next discriminating test is to construct, or exhibit a sequence obstructing, the **one separating first-order cone chart** in the balanced block above. One should keep the full Riemann bound in the physical gauge and verify both quantile masses are outside the splices on opposite sides. The link group is now a conclusion of the energy test. The exact Bach-flat theorem of Chang–Qing–Yang supplies a benchmark with its inner sphere-area bound and geodesic component conditions. A small $L^1$ Bach norm by itself has not supplied those estimates on the approximately critical sequence. [CQY Theorem 3.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0508621) · [Joint continuation](../continuation-notes/AFTER_JOINT_COLLAR_RESEARCH_20261125.md)

Even universal success for balanced separated radii would leave concentration at one scale and the genuinely closed case. A positive closed Bach-flat minimizer on $M$ above $4\pi^2$ can survive all the present arguments, for instance as the compact endpoint of the conditional analysis. A strict energy-lowering variation on that same smooth type, a rigidity theorem in the surviving class, or a nonzero global twistor field in a smooth nondegenerate gauge would change that conclusion. The exact product also has $F_i\wedge F_j=0$; the chiral reconstruction route still requires compatible global definite data where it is used. Finally, constructing a positive subcritical class or an alternative recognizing metric on every smooth homotopy sphere remains the entry problem. All the results here are analytical research deductions at their stated hypotheses.
