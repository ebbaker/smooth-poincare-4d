On one common subsequence let a closed smooth oriented connected homotopy sphere $(N,h)$ minus finitely many smooth points $p_1,\ldots,p_k$ have smooth *physical* comparison on entire compact truncations. In particular this applies to $h=u^2h_0$ after the nonzero conformal factor conclusion of [E09](E09_CONFORMAL_BASE_VOLUME_DICHOTOMY_20261125.md). The argument below proves a measured Gromov–Hausdorff and curvature-measure statement about the **original** metrics.

**No long thin exterior.** For fixed small $b$, let $K_b=N\setminus\bigcup_j B_h(p_j,b)$ use disjoint normal coordinate balls. Write $K_{i,b}$ for its physical comparison image. The compact complement has one piece $P_{ij,b}$ attached to each sphere. Indeed all those embedded two-sided spheres separate in $M_i$ since $H_1(M_i)=0$. The core is connected. A path within an exterior joining any two of its incident faces would give a loop meeting one separating sphere once. Every exterior touches the core by connectedness. Thus every exterior has exactly one face. Smooth comparison makes the intrinsic diameter of each boundary sphere at most $C b+o_i(1)$. Since $\mathrm{vol}_h(N)=1$ by E06,
$$
 \lim_i\sum_j\mathrm{vol}_{g_i}(P_{ij,b})=\sum_j\mathrm{vol}_h B_h(p_j,b)\le Ck b^4.
$$
By [NECK-E11](../../01_neck_geometry/notes/E11_BALANCED_BLOCK_AND_NONCOLLAPSE_20261125.md), $\mathrm{vol}_{g_i}B(x,r)\ge c r^4$ for $r\le r_0$. If an interior point lies at ambient distance $d$ from the single boundary of $P$, its ball of radius $\min(d/2,r_0)$ lies in $P$. When the volume bound above is small, $d\le 2(\mathrm{vol}(P)/c)^{1/4}$. A minimizing exit arc to the boundary stays in $P$. Connect two such exit points along the boundary to obtain
$$
 \limsup_i\max_j\mathrm{diam}_{\rm intrinsic}(P_{ij,b},g_i)\le C' b.
$$
All constants here may depend on the smooth limit and its finite puncture set. The bound applies to closures of the whole pieces, regardless of their smooth fillings.

**Metric comparison.** In $M_i$ a minimizing curve between two points of $K_{i,b}$ can be rerouted into the core at cost at most the sum of the intrinsic diameters of the boundary spheres. For each closed exterior visited, replace the subarc from its first to its last boundary contact by a boundary path. Endpoints in the core and the one-face property justify the replacement. Replacements do not enter any other open exterior. The same construction for the omitted balls in $N$ compares its ambient distance with intrinsic distance in $K_b$. At fixed $b$, the two intrinsic core metrics are $(1+o_i(1))$-bilipschitz under the comparison and have bounded diameter. Hence the distortion between the ambient distances on the two matched cores is $O(kb)+o_{i,b}(1)$. Both cores are $O(b)+o_{i,b}(1)$ dense in the corresponding spaces. This gives Gromov–Hausdorff convergence by first taking the source index to infinity, then $b\downarrow0$. One can define Borel approximate maps $M_i\to N$ by the inverse comparison on the core and the marked point $p_j$ on the open exterior. Their jump across each boundary is at most $b$ in the target. Choose $b_i\downarrow0$ sufficiently slowly for all fixed-compact comparisons. Smooth density convergence on each fixed core and vanishing exterior volume prove weak convergence of the pushed-forward physical volumes to $dV_h$. Together these yield measured Gromov–Hausdorff convergence. This conclusion allows an exterior of nonstandard capped smooth type to shrink to a point.

**Curvature at the marked points.** Pass to a subsequence for the finitely many total masses on each member of a countable nested exhaustion. Equivalently take weak limits of the finite curvature measures under the approximate maps above. Away from the designated points these agree with the limit's smooth densities. There are numbers $e_j\ge0$ such that on $N$
$$
 \begin{aligned}
 |W_{g_i}^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2dV_i &\rightharpoonup |W_h^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2dV_h+\sum_j e_j\delta_{p_j},\\
 |\operatorname{Ric}_{0,g_i}|^2dV_i &\rightharpoonup |\operatorname{Ric}_{0,h}|^2dV_h+4\sum_j e_j\delta_{p_j},\\
 |\mathrm{Rm}_{g_i}|_{\rm tens}^2dV_i &\rightharpoonup |\mathrm{Rm}_h|_{\rm tens}^2dV_h+16\sum_j e_j\delta_{p_j}.
 \end{aligned}
$$
To identify the weights, work on each $P_{ij,b}$. Its physical normal-coordinate face, viewed on the scale $b$, has Cartesian spin Chern–Simons term $O(b^4)+o_{i,b}(1)$ for either chirality by E06. Outward orientation reverses its sign if needed. Its volume is $O(b^4)+o_{i,b}(1)$. [MATCH-E12](../../../02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/01_dirac_collar_matching/notes/E12_RELATIVE_CHIRAL_CHARGE_20261125.md) forces the relative integer to vanish for sufficiently small fixed $b$ and sufficiently large $i$. Therefore the two Weyl atoms are equal, and each equals one quarter of the trace-free Ricci atom. The full four-index decomposition is $|\mathrm{Rm}|^2=4(|W^+|^2_{\rm HS}+|W^-|^2_{\rm HS})+2|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2+s^2/6$. Bounded scalar and vanishing exterior volume yield the coefficient $16$. There is no atom in the scalar-square measure. Total mass gives $\sum_j e_j=e-E_+(N)$. These measure statements require neither a cone chart inside the exterior nor small pointwise curvature there.

With approximate criticality and a subround, volume-bearing base on a fixed minimizing sequence, E08/E09 already give $N\cong M$ and $\sum e_j=0$. ## A curvature scale independent of pointwise spikes

In the single-atom case with $e>0$, fix $0<\eta<e$. On the original physical manifolds set $f_i(r)=\max_x\int_{B_{g_i}(x,r)}|W^+|^2$. At fixed $i$ the integrand is smooth, each distance sphere has zero four-volume, and compactness and dominated convergence give joint continuity in $(x,r)$ (including $r=0$). Thus $f_i$ is continuous and nondecreasing. The smallest radius $\rho_i$ with $f_i(\rho_i)=\eta$ exists for large $i$, and a maximizing center $x_i$ exists. The diameter estimate and point concentration imply $\rho_i\to0$: first choose a fixed deep puncture truncation whose exterior mass exceeds $\eta$ eventually and whose diameter is $O(b)$, then let $b$ decrease. Under the approximate maps the centers tend to the atom, since any closed set at positive distance from the atom has vanishing chiral mass in the round-base case. For $g_i'=\rho_i^{-2}g_i$ a unit ball now carries precisely $\eta$ and no unit ball carries more. In the round-base regime $s(g_i')\to0$, the global full curvature remains bounded, ball noncollapse persists at each fixed scale, and the normalized Sobolev inequality tends to the one with sharp round Yamabe coefficient on compactly supported comparisons. Choosing the mass scale absorbs no pointwise-curvature hypothesis. Positive mass on each source ball gives positive mass on a limit only with justified convergence or tightness against curvature concentration. The analogous normalization for full Riemann mass does not force a Weyl lower bound on its chosen ball.
