**End frames.** At inner AE radius \(A\) and smooth exterior
normal-coordinate radius \(b\), the physical local scales are
\(\lambda_i A\) and \(b\), respectively. Separate oriented
symmetric Cartesian spin frames give a boundary Chern–Simons
error in either chirality bounded in the source limsup by
\(C(A^{-2\alpha}+|P|b^4)\). The matrix connection and
Chern–Simons integrals are unchanged by the constant physical
normalizations. The frames need no common extension through
the residual.

**Physical volume.** For a smooth removable exterior compared
in the unmodified physical Yamabe metrics, the limit has scalar
curvature \(y=\lim Y_i>0\). Passing the Yamabe inequality to
fixed compact tests and cutting off its finite omitted points
gives \(y\sqrt{\operatorname{Vol}(N)}\le y\operatorname{Vol}(N)\).
Physical volume exhaustion bounds that volume by one, hence
\(\operatorname{Vol}(N)=1\) and \(Y([h_N])=y\). The whole
uncovered physical volume vanishes as the truncations are
exhausted after the source limit.

**Charge consequence.** For sufficiently far inner and small
outer truncations, the band and volume put each signed
\(Q_\pm\) in \([-8\pi^2+\delta/2,8\pi^2-\delta/2]\).
With boundary error below \(\delta/2\) the relative integer
is zero. At fixed truncations the signed charge can still
equal a nonzero boundary term. In the iterated limit it
vanishes and the chiral Weyl deficit equals one quarter
of the physical trace-free Ricci deficit. A slow diagonal
can realize the same estimates. This gives an accounting
criterion for every residual component, even without a
common cylinder. It does not imply the individual nonnegative
deficits vanish. A comparison using nonconstant conformal
factors gives Weyl energy in its images, but needs separate
physical volume, Ricci and boundary-connection controls.
