Let \(A=\{r\le|x|\le R\}\) and \(A^*=\{r/2<|x|<2R\}\) in \(\mathbb R^4\) with \(R\ge2r\), and let \(g=\delta+v\) be a smooth Riemannian metric on \(A^*\). Put \(\varepsilon=\|v\|_{L^\infty(A^*)}\), \(K=\|D^2v\|_{L^2(A^*)}\) and \(\mathfrak B=\int_{A^*}|B_g|_g\,dV_g\), with Euclidean norms and derivatives for \(v\) and the Bach tensor normalized as in the manuscript, equation `var:bach`. (i) Test the first variation of the full Weyl functional at \(g\) against \(\eta^4v\) for a radial cutoff \(\eta\) built from one fixed profile at scale \(r\) and one at scale \(R\). Decide whether \(\int_A|W_g|^2\,dV_g\) is bounded by a function of \((\varepsilon,K,\mathfrak B)\) that tends to zero as \(\varepsilon\to0\) with \(K\) and \(\mathfrak B\) bounded, with a constant independent of \(R/r\). Every term must be estimated; a factor \(\log(R/r)\) is failure. State which hypotheses of the handoff are used, in particular whether a bound for \(\|Dv\|_{L^4}\), smallness of \(\mathfrak B\), or vanishing derivative energy on the two cutoff shells is needed. (ii) Decide whether the dependence on \(K\) can be removed under a gauge condition on \(v\). (iii) Give examples showing which terms of the bound cannot be dropped, including a high-frequency perturbation on a fixed ball and a metric with components homogeneous of degree zero. (iv) State what the estimate does not give: say which norm of the curvature on adjacent dyadic annuli would produce the chart, and compare with the scalar function \(c\log|x|\). Method: direct proof with the structure \(W_g=\mathfrak A(g)[D^2g]+\mathfrak Q(g)[Dg,Dg]\), integration by parts for the gradient term, absorption. Completion: a proved estimate with its exact hypotheses, a counterexample inside the frozen hypotheses, or the single term that is not controlled. Escalate to B34 if the mechanism has to change. Do not construct the chart from the fixed-margin hypotheses and do not treat quotient necks.
