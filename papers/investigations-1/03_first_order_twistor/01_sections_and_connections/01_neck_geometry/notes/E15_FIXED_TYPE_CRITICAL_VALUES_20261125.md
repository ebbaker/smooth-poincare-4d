Let $M$ be a fixed smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere. Define
$$
\mathcal A_M=\{E_+([g]):Y([g])>0,\ B_g=0,\ 0<E_+([g])<8\pi^2\},\qquad a_M=\inf\mathcal A_M.
$$
Metrics and conformal classes here are smooth Riemannian. The positive-class infimum $I^+(M)$ allows noncritical classes and may be strictly smaller. Use Hilbert–Schmidt curvature-operator norm. Write $\kappa_\delta>0$ for the uniform same-type drop with upper energy $8\pi^2-\delta$ from SEC §12 and NECK-E02. [Exact alternatives](../../manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L4433) · [Same-type drop and order of cutting](E02_E05_SAME_TYPE_DESCENT_20261125.md)

**Compactness.** If $\mathcal A_M$ is nonempty, $4\pi^2<a_M<8\pi^2$ and $a_M$ is attained on $M$. Fix an upper endpoint $b\le8\pi^2-\delta$ with $b<a_M+\kappa_\delta$. All volume-one Yamabe minimizers representing $\mathcal A_M\cap[a_M,b]$ form a sequentially compact family up to oriented diffeomorphisms. Indeed, pass to a subsequence with energies converging to $e$. The closed gap places them in the band of the exact compactness alternative. In the curvature blow-up alternative, smooth ALE compactification and the marked cap argument produce an element of $\mathcal A_M$ at most $e-\kappa_\delta<a_M$. The smooth compact alternative yields convergence on the original type to an exact positive minimizer of its own Yamabe class with energy $e$. Apply the same argument first to energies approaching $a_M$, choosing $\delta$ from one strictly subcritical upper bound and discarding a finite prefix. Strictness at $4\pi^2$ follows by applying the closed gap to the attaining metric. No one-parameter flow or attainment over noncritical competitors enters this argument.

**Local constancy of the value.** Fix any smooth closed Bach-flat $g$ on $M$. With $0<\alpha<1$, use the trace-free Bach gauge $P_g^B:C^{4,\alpha}(S^2_0)\to C^{0,\alpha}(S^2_0)$ of Gursky–Viaclovsky, measured relative to this fixed $g$. Their Theorem 2.11 gives an elliptic linearization and represents any sufficiently close Bach-flat metric, up to a diffeomorphism and conformal factor, by a small zero at $g$. The map in (2.59) is real analytic in its *tensor argument*: covariant derivatives in a fixed background are bounded between the indicated Hölder spaces, the inverse metric is analytic on the open positive cone, and products and fixed trace projection are continuous multilinear operations. No analytic dependence on the spatial variable or on the choice of gauge transformation is required. Split off the finite-dimensional kernel $K$ and choose a bounded finite-dimensional complement to the image of the elliptic derivative. Projecting onto its closed image and using the analytic implicit function theorem gives an analytic graph $h=k+v(k)$, $k\in K$, containing all small zeros. The map $v$ takes values in a Banach complement of $K$. This argument also applies if the dimensions of the kernel and the chosen complement to the range are described separately. [Gursky–Viaclovsky, §2.3, (2.59), Theorem 2.11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1105.4648)

On the whole finite-dimensional graph, $F(k)=E_+(g+k+v(k))$ is real analytic by the same inverse-metric argument, including the Hodge projection and the volume density. At a parameter representing a Bach-flat metric, the unrestricted first variation vanishes in every metric direction, hence $dF(k)=0$. The finite-dimensional analytic gradient inequality at $0$ gives $|F(k)-F(0)|^\theta\le C|dF(k)|$ locally for some $0<\theta<1$ (the constant-function case is immediate). Every sufficiently close Bach-flat metric has exactly $E_+(g)$, even if the moduli space has positive dimension or singularities. Energy equality follows by diffeomorphism and conformal invariance when returning to the original metric. Only the forward implication from actual critical metrics to zeros is needed. [Finite-dimensional gradient inequality, Feehan, arXiv:1708.09775v13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1708.09775v13) · [Independent reduction check](../reviews/E15_CRITICAL_VALUES_CHECK_20261125.md)

**Consequences.** $\mathcal A_M\cap[a_M,b]$ is finite under the strict compactness margin above. Otherwise choose representatives of pairwise distinct values, pass to a smooth subsequential limit on $M$, then apply local constancy, a contradiction. In particular there is a non-effective $\epsilon_M>0$ with $\mathcal A_M\cap[a_M,a_M+\epsilon_M)=\{a_M\}$. To choose it, first fix $\delta$ with $a_M<8\pi^2-\delta$, then choose $b>a_M$ inside both upper bounds and use finiteness. More generally any interior accumulation of distinct exact values in a fixed band forces a nonflat smooth ALE rescaling of representatives on a subsequence and an exact closed class on the same $M$ lower by at least $\kappa_\delta$. There is no claimed finite spectrum in the whole band. A compact space of critical metrics may have infinitely many metrics of the same energy. An isolated critical *value* may be attained at a saddle. Only a minimizer over **all** positive classes is subject to unconditional variational nonnegativity. No smooth type is recognized by isolation alone.
