## Proper and fully covered comparison from protected local geometry

Use the oriented smooth Riemannian unit-volume positive Yamabe sources, the same attaining center \(x_i\), and the two entire marks of E53. Take \(a_i/\rho_i\to0\) and \(\rho_i/b_i\to0\); the physical diameter bound then gives \(\rho_i\to0\). Put \(h_i=\rho_i^{-2}g_i\) and \(d_i=d_{h_i}(x_i,\cdot)\). The small-ball lower bound \(\operatorname{Vol}_{h_i}B(q,s)\ge\nu s^4\) is uniform for \(s\le1\) at large \(i\). As in the E53 radial test, let \(J=(1/16,2L)\), with \(L\) fixed from the anchor and ellipticity thresholds. On one physical band \(\Omega_i=\{a_0<d_i<b_0\}\) with \(0<a_0<\inf J<\sup J<b_0<\infty\), assume

\[
\operatorname{Vol}_{h_i}(\Omega_i)\le C,\qquad
\sup_{\Omega_i}|\operatorname{Rm}_{h_i}|_{\rm tens}\le K,
\qquad \int_{\Omega_i}|\operatorname{Rm}_{h_i}|_{\rm tens}^2\,dV_{h_i}\longrightarrow0 .
\]

Locally finite weak convergence of the radial pushforward of the whole physical volumes on a slightly wider band gives the first premise by testing with a compact cutoff equal to one on \([a_0,b_0]\). It holds on E63's selected scale because \(P_i\le C\). The pointwise sectional bound is an additional hypothesis.

Fix \(a_0<a_1<\inf J<\sup J<b_1<b_0\) and take \(X_i=\{a_1\le d_i\le b_1\}\) with the ambient source metric restricted to \(X_i\). Its diameter is at most \(2b_1\). ## A genuine polar example on the terminal side of a throat

Let \(t\in[0,2]\) be the polar coordinate from the north pole on a smooth rotationally symmetric standard \(S^4\). Fix \(0<\delta<1/8\) and let \(\epsilon\downarrow0\), \(k=\epsilon\). On \(|t-1|\le 2\delta\), put \(g_\epsilon=dt^2+u_\epsilon(t)^2\gamma_{S^3(1)}\), where

\[
u_\epsilon(t)^2=\epsilon^2+(1-2k\epsilon^2)\frac{\sin^2(\sqrt{k}(t-1))}{k}.
\]

Use a fixed smooth round-fiber profile away from the central band, equal to \(|t-1|\) on the overlap with a fixed cutoff for \(2\delta\le |t-1|\le 3\delta\), equal to \(t\) near \(0\), and equal to \(2-t\) near \(2\). This profile is positive off the poles and throat, and interpolation blends it with \(u_\epsilon\) through the overlap. The inner profile converges smoothly to \(|t-1|\) there, giving uniform bounds on transition derivatives. Distance from the north pole is exactly \(t\), because lengths dominate radial variation and each meridian realizes that bound. Equality for \(0<t<2\) gives a unique meridian. The angular differential has positive determinant, so all directions survive until the south pole at \(t=2\). The angular Jacobian is \(u_\epsilon^3\) in the unit angular measure.

On the exact band, \(v=u_\epsilon^2\) satisfies \(v''+4kv=2\). The scalar curvature is \(6(1-v''/2)/v=12k>0\) and the first integral \(m=u_\epsilon^2(1-(u_\epsilon')^2-ku_\epsilon^2)=\epsilon^2(1-k\epsilon^2)\) gives sectional curvatures \(K_{\rm tan}=k+m/u_\epsilon^4\) and \(K_{\rm rad}=k-m/u_\epsilon^4\). In full tensor norm \(|\operatorname{Rm}|^2=24(k^2+m^2/u_\epsilon^8)\). Since \(u_\epsilon\asymp(\epsilon^2+(t-1)^2)^{1/2}\), the throat contribution to the total critical integral is bounded, using \(m^2\int u_\epsilon^{-5}\,dt=O(1)\). The same integral is \(O(\epsilon^2)\) for \(\sqrt\epsilon\le t-1\le\delta\). The transitions and fixed caps give a global bound on the full curvature integral and scalar curvature. The complete metric is conformal to round through the change of radial variable \(d\log R=dt/u_\epsilon(t)\) and the Euclidean polar expression, with the conformal factor extending at both exact polar caps. It has the round Yamabe constant as a conformal class. Its scalar varies on the closed source and its chiral Weyl measures vanish.

Uniform two-sided ball-volume ratios hold at all centers for small radii \(r\). The profiles are uniformly Lipschitz. When \(u_\epsilon(t)\ge c r\), the ball contains a polar product box with radial width comparable to \(r\) and angular radius comparable to \(r/u_\epsilon(t)\) capped at a fixed angular constant. Choose the widths small enough that the warp is comparable to \(u_\epsilon(t)\) throughout the box and each point is reached by a radial segment followed by an angular path of total length below \(r\). The resulting volume is bounded below by a constant times \(r^4\). If \(u_\epsilon(t)<cr\) with \(c\) small, the center is within \(O(cr)\) of one of the zeros \(0,1,2\) of the limiting profile. Move radially by at most \(r/2\) to find the center of a subball of radius \(r/2\) whose warp is at least a fixed multiple of \(r\), then use the same box. For the upper bound, when the warp is at most a sufficiently large constant times \(r\), integrate the whole angular measure over \(|t'-t|<r\), using the Lipschitz bound. Otherwise paths of length \(r\) stay where the warp is comparable to \(u_\epsilon(t)\), with angular length bounded by \(Cr/u_\epsilon(t)\). Integrating this angular cap over the radial interval gives the upper bound \(O(r^4)\). The bounds extend to \(r\le1\) by monotonicity and bounded total volume.

Set \(s_\epsilon=\log(1+\sqrt\epsilon)\) and \(l=\log(1+\delta)\). The full curvature integral on the pair interval tends to zero. Physical pushforward volume tends on fixed outer trims to \(\operatorname{Vol}(S^3)(t-1)^3\,dt\), and weakly as a finite measure on the full interval extended by zero toward \(t=1\). Since \(p_\epsilon=(u_\epsilon/t)^3\) is angle-independent and the same directions survive, the exact forward moment is

\[
Q_\epsilon(s_\epsilon,l)=\operatorname{Vol}(S^3)\,\frac{p_\epsilon(l)^2}{p_\epsilon(s_\epsilon)}\asymp\epsilon^{-3/2}.
\]

The log separation is bounded. On any trim \(1<c<d<1+\delta\), the unshifted excess \(w_\epsilon=3tu_\epsilon'/u_\epsilon-3\) tends smoothly to \(3/(t-1)\). Its integral against physical volume tends to the strictly positive integral of \(3\operatorname{Vol}(S^3)(t-1)^2\) there. The unshifted defect has nonvanishing negative mass, while the flat limit satisfies the shifted distance equation with radius \(t-1\). This uses the limiting apex value \(1\). The earlier radius approaches the shifted apex and has no fixed inner buffer in the low-curvature terminal interval. This example shows that finite physical annular volumes, uniform ball ratios and vanishing critical full curvature on an unprotected pair interval do not imply the forward moment bound or the unshifted defect bound. It concerns a standard sphere with zero chiral charges and no constant positive scalar on the whole source. It leaves open selection of an admissible long logarithmic interval and radial saturation in the actual minimizing sequence.
