## Assumptions and estimates

Let \(g\) be a smooth Riemannian metric on a smooth closed oriented
homotopy four-sphere \(M\) with \(Y_0=Y([g])\ge y_0>0\).
For a fixed \(\ell\ge1\) assume an oriented embedded annulus with
coordinates \(y\) on
\[
 A_\ell=\{e^{-\ell-2}<|y|<e^{\ell+2}\}\subset\mathbb R^4,
 \qquad t=\log|y|.
\]
Assume the pullback of the source has a smooth conformal representative
\(m=\delta+v\) on \(A_\ell\), with Euclidean norms
\[
 a=\|v\|_{L^\infty(A_\ell)}\le\tfrac14,\qquad
 K=\|D^2v\|_{L^2(A_\ell)},\qquad
 \varepsilon=\sqrt{a(K+1)}\le1 .
\]
For \(j=1,2\) put \(x=|t|\) on the corresponding retained side
and make the cap with a fixed cutoff \(\theta(x)\) equal to zero near
\(x=0\) and one near \(x=1\) and beyond. Use the exact E01 cap
and the normalized collar representative
\[
 |y|^{-2}(\delta+\theta(x)v)
\]
on the splice \(T_j=\{0<x<1\}\) in that side. On the rest of the
retained side \(U_j\), keep the source conformal class by extending
the representative \(|y|^{-2}m\) globally as in E01. Let
\(\widehat g_j\) be these capped metrics. Let \(q_j=E_+(g;T_j)\)
and \(q=q_1+q_2\), with the inherited orientation and Hilbert–Schmidt
norm on real \(\Lambda^+\). Define \(A_j\) as the positive-chiral
energy of the corresponding splice in \(\widehat g_j\). Then
\[
 \begin{split}
 E_+(\widehat g_j)&=E_+(g;U_j)+A_j,\qquad
 A_j^{1/2}\le q_j^{1/2}+C_W\varepsilon,\\
 (A_1+A_2)^{1/2}&\le q^{1/2}+C_W\varepsilon .
 \end{split}                                                    \tag{1}
\]
Once \(C_\ell\varepsilon\le\tfrac12\), each side satisfies
\[
 Y([\widehat g_j])\ge
 \min\{Y_0,(1-C_\ell\varepsilon)Y_{\rm rd}\}
       -C_I\ell^{-3/2},
 \qquad Y_{\rm rd}=8\sqrt6\,\pi.                                  \tag{2}
\]
The constants \(C_W,C_I\) depend only on the fixed profiles
and model. The form comparison constant \(C_\ell\) may depend on
the chosen finite \(\ell\). These constants are independent of
the physical scale and the geometry outside the supplied chart.
The assumptions are smoothness of each metric and uniform
bounds in the displayed norms. The argument uses no pointwise
bound on its first or second derivatives.

## Projected Weyl comparison

For any smooth positive metric matrix \(n\) in the oriented
\(y\) coordinates, the positive square root defines the oriented
isometry \(n^{-1/2}:(\mathbb R^4,\delta)\to(\mathbb R^4,n)\).
Use it to pull the covariant Weyl tensor into the fixed Euclidean
frame, then use a fixed orthonormal basis of Euclidean
\(\Lambda^+\) to express its positive-chiral block as a matrix
\(\mathbf W_+(n)\). The oriented isometry intertwines the two
Hodge stars. In particular this prescription includes the change
of positive subbundle with \(n\). Set
\[
 \mathcal F(n)=(\det n)^{1/4}\mathbf W_+(n).
\]
Its Euclidean matrix norm satisfies
\(\|\mathcal F(n)\|_{L^2(T,dy)}^2=E_+(n;T)\) for each region \(T\)
in the chart.

For \(n=\delta+w\), write the four-index Weyl tensor as a
linear function of \(D^2w\) plus a quadratic function of \(Dw\),
with smooth coefficients in \(n\). Applying the frame change,
fixed projection and density factor above gives the same structure
\[
 \mathcal F(\delta+w)
   =\mathcal A(\delta+w)[D^2w]
      +\mathcal P(\delta+w)[Dw,Dw]
\]
with smooth coefficients in the positive definite matrix. These
coefficients and their first derivatives are uniformly bounded
on the indicated neighborhood of \(\delta\). Let
\(n=\delta+\theta v\) on the two central splices. Since
\(\theta v\) remains in that neighborhood, the product rule
and mean-value theorem give
\[
 \big|\mathcal F(n)-\theta\mathcal F(m)\big|
 \le C\big(a|D^2v|+|Dv|^2+|Dv|+a\big).                            \tag{3}
\]
For the principal term, the coefficient change multiplies
\(\theta D^2v\), and differentiating \(\theta v\) twice yields terms
bounded by \(C(|Dv|+a)\). For the quadratic term use
\(|D(\theta v)|^2\le C(|Dv|^2+a^2)\). The cutoff derivatives are
bounded on the fixed central Euclidean annulus. The estimate
uses the same oriented chart for both splices, so the sign of
the chiral block agrees with the source on both sides. [SEC §14.3,
structure formula and Euler relation](../../manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L5466)

The localized interpolation in SEC §14.3 gives, on an interior
subannulus where a compactly supported cutoff \(\eta=1\),
\[
 \|Dv\|_4^2\le C_\eta a^2+8aK.
\]
For completeness, put \(Z=\|\eta Dv\|_4\). Integrating
\(\int\eta^4|Dv|^4\) by parts in one factor \(Dv\) bounds it by
\(4a\|D\eta\|_4 Z^3+4aK Z^2\) for \(0\le\eta\le1\).
Dividing if \(Z>0\) and applying the scalar quadratic bound
gives \(Z^2\le32a^2\|D\eta\|_4^2+8aK\).
For (3) choose \(\eta\) identically one on
\(e^{-1}\le |y|\le e\), with support in a larger annulus of fixed
radii in every \(A_\ell\). Hölder gives
\(\|Dv\|_2\le\operatorname{Vol}(T_1\cup T_2)^{1/4}
\|Dv\|_4\) on the splices. Consequently the \(L^2\) norm of the
right-hand side of (3) on both splices is bounded by
\[
 C\left[aK+a(K+a)+\sqrt{a(K+a)}+a\right]\le C_W\varepsilon .
\]
The fixed-frame norm, \(\theta\le1\), and the triangle inequality
give (1), using conformal invariance of the positive-chiral
density to undo \(|y|^{-2}\) and the source gauge. The exact
cap is conformally flat. Subtracting the two discarded incoming
splice energies gives the net bound
\[
 \sum_j E_+(\widehat g_j)-E_+(g)
 \le 2C_W\varepsilon\sqrt q+C_W^2\varepsilon^2.
\]
For a small absolute added-energy budget, use the stronger
information in (1) that \(q\) is small. The estimates apply
to negative chirality with its corresponding incoming energies.
[SEC §14.3, interpolation lemma](../../manuscript/twistor_sections_and_chiral_weyl_curvature.tex#L5539)

## Scalar-density estimate on the comparison cap

For either side, let \(b=b_\infty\) be the fixed reference cap
and its semi-infinite unit round product extension. The exact
model gives, for compactly supported \(w\),
\[
 Q_b(w)=\int(6|dw|_b^2+R_b w^2)\,dV_b,\qquad
 Q_b(w)\ge c_b\|w\|_{H^1(b)}^2,\qquad
 Q_b(w)\ge Y_{\rm rd}\|w\|_{4,b}^2 .
\]
The first inequality uses the positive lower scalar bound,
and the second is the round Yamabe inequality by the conformal
Euclidean identification of \(b\). [E01, model and form](E01_TWO_CAP_AUDIT_20261125.md#yamabe-localization-on-each-side)

Extend the capped metric from the cap, splice and buffer to
a smooth metric \(k=b+e\) on the comparison manifold by multiplying
its perturbation on the product end by an outer cutoff equal
to one through \(x=1+\ell\), zero near \(x=\ell+3/2\).
Set \(e=0\) on the exact cap and beyond the outer cutoff.
The extension is positive by convexity. On the product,
\(e=\theta\beta |y|^{-2}v\) in the pulled-back coordinates, where
\(\beta\) is the outer cutoff. Thus
\[
 \|e\|_{L^\infty(b)}\le a,\qquad
 d:=\|\nabla_b e\|_{L^4(b)}\le C_\ell\big(\sqrt{a(K+a)}+a\big).
                                                                    \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(b=|y|^{-2}\delta\) on each side of the product under
\(x=|t|\), which gives the pointwise zeroth-order comparison.
On the fixed finite chart used for the derivative estimate,
the conformal coordinate change, its first derivatives and the
two cutoff derivatives give the bound by \(C_\ell(\|Dv\|_4+a)\).
Use SEC interpolation with a cutoff equal to one on the entire
support of the comparison perturbation in \(A_\ell\). Reflection
of the longitudinal coordinate on the inner side affects none of
the scalar form comparisons. In particular (4) does not require a
second pointwise derivative bound.

Write \(\mu=dV_k/dV_b\), \(P^{ij}=\mu k^{ij}\), and
\(C^p{}_{ij}=(\nabla_k-\nabla_b)^p{}_{ij}\). The uniform metric
comparison gives
\[
 |P-b^{-1}|\le C|e|,\qquad
 |C|+|\nabla_b P|\le C|\nabla_b e|.
\]
The Ricci difference formula is
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}(k)_{ij}-\operatorname{Ric}(b)_{ij}
 =\nabla_p C^p{}_{ij}-\nabla_j C^p{}_{ip}
  +C^p{}_{pq}C^q{}_{ij}-C^p{}_{jq}C^q{}_{ip},
\]
with \(\nabla=\nabla_b\). Contracting with \(P\) and applying
the product rule to the two differentiated terms gives
\[
 \mu R_k-R_b=\operatorname{div}_b V+H,\qquad
 V^p=P^{ij}C^p{}_{ij}-P^{ip}C^q{}_{iq},\qquad
 |H|\le C(|e|+|\nabla_b e|^2).
\]
Here the term from the background Ricci contraction is
\((P^{ij}-b^{ij})\operatorname{Ric}(b)_{ij}\). The differentiated
coefficient terms and the connection products are quadratic in
the first derivative. All estimates are in the reference metric.

Consequently, for each smooth compactly supported real \(w\),
integration by parts with no boundary term and Hölder give
\[
 \begin{split}
 |Q_k(w)-Q_b(w)|
 &\le C\left(
  a\|w\|_{H^1(b)}^2
  +d\,\|w\|_{4,b}\|dw\|_{2,b}
  +d^2\|w\|_{4,b}^2\right)\\
 &\le C(a+d+d^2)Q_b(w)
 \le C_\ell\varepsilon Q_b(w).
 \end{split}                                                       \tag{5}
\]
For the first inequality, the gradient density difference is
\(O(|e|)\), and \(\int(\operatorname{div}_b V)w^2\,dV_b
=-2\int w\,dw(V)\,dV_b\). The second inequality uses the two
model lower bounds for \(Q_b\). The factor \(a+d+d^2\) is at
most \(C_\ell\varepsilon\) for \(\varepsilon\le1\).
The \(L^4\) norm comparison is
\(\|w\|_{4,k}^2\le(1+Ca)\|w\|_{4,b}^2\). Absorb this
in the choice of \(C_\ell\). Then (5) yields
\[
 Q_k(w)\ge (1-C_\ell\varepsilon)Y_{\rm rd}\|w\|_{4,k}^2.
\]

## Localization and strict margins

Use the cutoff pair \(\chi,\xi\) of E01 with \(\chi^2+\xi^2=1\)
and derivatives supported in the unchanged product buffer
\(1<x<1+\ell\). For any test \(u\) on the capped piece,
\(\chi u\) extends to the source with the same Yamabe form and
\(L^4\) norm. The test \(\xi u\) extends to the exact
comparison manifold with metric \(k\), as its support avoids
the outer cutoff. Thus the two localized lower coefficients
are \(Y_0\) and \((1-C_\ell\varepsilon)Y_{\rm rd}\).
On the buffer, \((1-a)h_0\le\widehat g_j\le(1+a)h_0\), which
suffices for both the cutoff gradient bound and the volume bound:
the sum of squared gradients is \(O(\ell^{-2})\) and the buffer
has volume \(O(\ell)\). The exact IMS identity and Hölder
therefore give the error \(C_I\ell^{-3/2}\|u\|_4^2\).
The \(L^2\) triangle inequality for \(|u|^2\) and the positivity
of the smaller localized coefficient give (2). The scaling
is the same as in E01, since the cutoff coefficient has length
weight \(-2\), its \(L^2\) norm is scale invariant, and the
numerator and denominator in the Yamabe quotient have the same
weight. [E01, localization and scaling](E01_TWO_CAP_AUDIT_20261125.md#yamabe-localization-on-each-side)

Let \(y_*=\min\{y_0,Y_{\rm rd}/2\}>0\) and fix
\(\ell\ge1\) with \(C_I\ell^{-3/2}\le y_*/2\).
Choose \(\varepsilon\) small enough to have
\(C_\ell\varepsilon\le1/2\) for that length.
For any prescribed energy margin \(m_E>0\), asking that
\[
 \sqrt{q}+C_W\varepsilon\le\sqrt{m_E/2}
\]
gives total absolute added positive-chiral energy at most
\(m_E/2\). Both capped Yamabe constants are then at least
\(y_*/2\). The analytic criterion applies, for example, to a
sequence with bounded \(K\), \(a\to0\) and \(q\to0\) on the
required supplied fixed-width charts. Weyl energy and the
Yamabe sign require only these controls. A fourth-order
Bach estimate and a construction of the chart from small
annular energy are separate geometric requirements. [B01
revision](../brainstorm/notes/DECISIONS_JOINT_COLLAR_20261125.md)
