\subsection{Flat comparisons on a protected physical band}
\label{subsec:protected-physical-window}

\begin{proposition}[Compact comparison on a physical window]
\label{prop:protected-physical-window}
Use the complete marks of \eqref{eq:anchored-marks} and take
\(a_i/\rho_i\to0\) and \(\rho_i/b_i\to0\). In particular
\(\rho_i\to0\) by the physical diameter bound. Put
\(h_i=\rho_i^{-2}g_i\) and \(\widehat d_i=d_{h_i}(x_i,\cdot)\).
Take \(J=(1/16,2L)\) as in Proposition~\ref{prop:radial-distance-test}
and a larger fixed interval \((a_0,b_0)\) with
\(0<a_0<\inf J<\sup J<b_0\). On the full physical band
\(\Omega_i=\{a_0<\widehat d_i<b_0\}\) suppose
\[
 \vol_{h_i}(\Omega_i)\le C,\qquad
 \sup_{\Omega_i}|\Rm_{h_i}|\le K,\qquad
 \int_{\Omega_i}|\Rm_{h_i}|^2\,dV_{h_i}\longrightarrow0.
\]
After a subsequence there is a nonempty smooth oriented flat
four-manifold \((U,h)\) with a proper radial limit \(r:U\to J\).
Every compact subset has a neighborhood with oriented smooth
source embeddings, physical metric convergence in \(C^0\cap W^{1,4}\),
and locally uniform convergence of \(\widehat d_i\) to \(r\).
Before restriction to a connected component of \(U\), the comparison
on a buffered closed radial strip covers the full source strip of
strictly smaller radial range for all large \(i\).

If the scales lie in the wider low-curvature block of
Proposition~\ref{prop:radial-distance-test} and the full radial
defect tends to zero on compact strips of \(J\) at the same
scales, the conclusion of that proposition holds.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Choose \(a_0<a_1<\inf J<\sup J<b_1<b_0\). Equip the compact sets
\(X_i=\{a_1\le\widehat d_i\le b_1\}\) with the restricted ambient
physical distances. Their diameters are at most \(2b_1\).
For each small fixed \(s\), disjoint distance balls of radius
\(s/2\) at an \(s\)-separated family in \(X_i\) lie in \(\Omega_i\).
The physical Yamabe small-ball floor gives a uniform upper bound on
the family size from \(\vol_{h_i}(\Omega_i)\le C\). Compact metric
space convergence and the \(1\)-Lipschitz bound on \(\widehat d_i\)
give a subsequential limit \(X\) with continuous radial function \(r\).
The inverse image \(U=r^{-1}(J)\) has proper radial function into \(J\).
It is nonempty by minimizing segments from \(x_i\) reaching the
median exterior.

On each smaller physical trim the sectional bound holds on uniformly
buffered source balls. With the ball-volume floor it gives a uniform
injectivity bound by the local geodesic-loop estimate and the
conjugate-radius bound. Local harmonic radius then gives uniformly elliptic
charts bounded in \(W^{1,p}\) for fixed \(p>8\)
\cite[Theorem~4.3 and preceding discussion]{CheegerGromovTaylor1982}
\cite[Theorem~0.3 and the local-domain remarks]{AndersonCheeger1992Harmonic}.
In these charts the reduced Ricci equation has bounded Ricci term and
an \(L^{p/2}\) quadratic term. Interior estimates first give
\(W^{2,p/2}\) bounds and subsequential \(C^{1,\beta}\) convergence
for small \(\beta>0\), and then local weak \(W^{2,q}\) bounds
for finite \(q\). Ellipticity and first exit from a chart imply
that suitably trimmed chart images contain a fixed ambient ball at
their centers. On a sufficiently small trim the same estimate keeps
short distance comparisons inside the chart. Thus a countable family
of these limiting charts identifies the open set \(U\) in \(X\).
The all-lowered curvature formula passes to distributions in these
coordinates, while the physical curvature integrals tend to zero.
The reduced Ricci equation with zero Ricci term gives smoothness by
elliptic bootstrapping. The limits of harmonic transition maps are
smooth and the approximating transitions converge in \(C^2\) on
smaller overlaps by interior scalar estimates. The resulting manifold
has the induced orientation.

For clarity, the buffered whole-compact markings can be assembled
by the finite-atlas construction of
\cite[Theorem~3.1 and its proof]{BelegradekKapovitch2000Finiteness}.
On a finite covering of a compact trim, use an embedding by
cutoff-coordinate pairs \((\chi_a,\chi_a z_a)\). Form the same pairs
in the individual source charts, with the cutoffs supported inside
the chart buffers. In the overlaps the assembled source map is
\(C^2\)-close to the limit embedding in the corresponding
coordinates. Tubular projection to the limit embedding defines
local diffeomorphisms on a neighborhood of the trim. Their local
inverses agree where both are defined for large \(i\). Two candidate
images for the same point approach the same point in the metric-space
limit and therefore lie in one common buffered source chart by the
first-exit bound. There the projection map is locally injective by
its \(C^1\) closeness to the identity. The inverses give smooth
oriented source embeddings and \(C^1\) metric convergence on compact
subsets. For any omitted source points in a strictly smaller closed
radial strip, compact metric-space convergence would yield an
accumulation point in the limit trim. The local inverse there
contains those points for large \(i\), proving the full coverage.
Every connected component of \(U\) is closed in \(U\), so restricting
to one preserves properness. With vanishing full radial defect the
compactly supported tests of Proposition~\ref{prop:radial-distance-test}
now apply on that component.
\end{proof}

A locally finite weak limit of the full physical radial volume
measures on a slightly wider interval supplies the volume premise
by testing a compact radial cutoff. The curvature and volume premises
also hold after the selection in
Proposition~\ref{prop:critical-radial-defect}, provided the protected
pointwise curvature bound holds on the selected scale. In that case
this proposition supplies the proper comparison and the original
cut has both complete marks off the closed trim. The pointwise bound
and the radial estimate are separate inputs.

For the shifted volume test of
Proposition~\ref{prop:radial-volume-test}, apply the volume comparison
on the full limit strip before selecting a connected component.
The buffered embeddings capture the two entire tested physical
annuli. If the positive equality hypotheses of that test hold,
its nonnegative shifted defect vanishes on the union of the tested
limit strips. A nonempty component then gives the marked spherical
comparison under the remaining hypotheses of that test.
