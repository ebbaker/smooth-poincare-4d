## The finite protected forward estimate

Assume that $|\sec_{h_i}|\le K$ on the wider physical band and keep the same radial center. Use positive normalized physical radii $a_0<t_0<s<t<b_0$. Along every direction surviving to $t$, the polar mean curvature $m(t)=(\log J(t,\vartheta))'$ satisfies
\[
 m' \le 3K-\frac{m^2}{3}.
\]
The comparison with the scalar solution blowing up at $t_0$ gives $m(\xi)\le3\sqrt K\,\coth(\sqrt K(\xi-t_0))$ for $t_0<\xi\le t$. This can be justified by starting slightly to the right of $t_0$, where the comparison solution dominates the finite ray value, and taking that start point down to $t_0$. With $K=0$ read the right side as $3/(\xi-t_0)$. Integrating,
\[
 \frac{J(t,\vartheta)}{J(s,\vartheta)}
 \le
 \left(\frac{\sinh(\sqrt K(t-t_0))}
            {\sinh(\sqrt K(s-t_0))}\right)^3.
\]
For $K=0$ the ratio is $((t-t_0)/(s-t_0))^3$. Multiplying by $(s/t)^3$ gives the corresponding ratio for $p$. Thus a fixed positive separation between $t_0$ and all tested $s$ gives a uniform raywise bound in a fixed compact radial band. The same estimate applied on an earlier fixed interval $[s_0,s_1]$, with $t_0<s_0<s_1$ strictly below the tested $t$, makes $J(u,\vartheta)\ge cJ(t,\vartheta)$ there for all directions surviving to $t$. Integrating in $u$ and then in those directions bounds the polar area at $t$ from the overall band volume. This provides $P(\log t)\le C'$ and $Q(\log s,\log t)\le C''$ with fixed margins. No lower density is assumed on nonsurviving directions. The comparison is centered in the protected band, and its constants do not give the E63 bound on a growing interval of logarithmic length. The finite estimate also does not make the unshifted defect small. Its possible displaced apex remains relevant even in a flat limit.

## Check of the polar example

On the central band put $x=t-1$, $v=u_\epsilon^2$ and $k=\epsilon$. Differentiating the formula for $v$ gives $v''+4kv=2$. The radial and angular sectional curvatures are $k-m/u_\epsilon^4$ and $k+m/u_\epsilon^4$, where $m=\epsilon^2(1-k\epsilon^2)$ is verified at $x=0$ and is constant by the scalar equation. In dimension four there are three planes of each kind, giving full tensor norm squared $24(k^2+m^2/u_\epsilon^8)$. The squared integral on the whole throat is bounded since $\epsilon^4\int(\epsilon^2+x^2)^{-5/2}dx=O(1)$. On $\sqrt\epsilon\le x\le\delta$ it is $O(\epsilon^2)$. The profile is uniformly Lipschitz and bounded below away from the three limiting zeros. The product box proof yields two-sided ball ratios, including centers with warp much smaller than ball radius by a radial displacement of at most half that radius. The polar endpoints are smooth since the profiles there are exactly $t$ and $2-t$. Distance from the north pole equals $t$, and equality in the radial length comparison leaves a unique minimizing meridian away from the poles. The nonzero warp before the south pole makes the computed Jacobian the pre-cut polar Jacobian.

At the earlier terminal-side radius $t=1+\sqrt\epsilon$, the angular Jacobian is of order $\epsilon^{3/2}$. At $t=1+\delta$ it has a positive limit. The normalized forward moment diverges as $\epsilon^{-3/2}$ although the curvature integral on the intervening pair tends to zero. The pair has bounded logarithmic separation, and its earlier radius has no fixed buffer inside the low-curvature terminal side. On any fixed terminal trim separated from the throat the curvature goes smoothly to zero, and $t\Delta t-3\to3/(t-1)$. This gives nonvanishing negative unshifted defect there even on a fixed protected band. The flat model satisfies the shifted equation with shift equal to the displaced apex value $t=1$.
