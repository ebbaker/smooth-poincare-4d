## Results in context

The manuscript first shows that the optimized differentiated Frobenius defect of three local orthogonal twistor sections measures the chiral Weyl energy of a fixed metric, with bounds `(2/9) E₊ ≤ I(g) ≤ 4 E₊`. Given a round marked ball, an arbitrarily small change outside it can preserve the energy while making the full linearization of the chiral curvature map surjective. This starts an exact local radial contraction. At the original metric there is a separate sign criterion, and the spherical transmission calculation identifies opposite-chirality curvature forced by a prescribed cap variation.

The standard-sphere examples reach the sharp, unattained marked Yamabe-zero infimum `E₊ = 8π²` in the limit, with total Bach norm tending to zero and radial strain diverging. Below that wall, at a fixed positive margin and positive Yamabe sign, a supplied smooth nonflat ALE blow-up with its full core embedded has one Euclidean end. It carries strictly more than `4π²` per chirality and leaves a positive energy remainder. A supplied compact Sobolev gauge gives strong Weyl convergence on the original manifold. The closed critical-value result concerns exactly Bach-flat metrics. If a positive critical value below `8π²` exists, the least such value is attained and strictly exceeds `4π²`. The closed gap uses Chang–Gursky–Yang's Theorems A and B and Chang–Chen's Theorem 2.1, with the full tensor integral equal to `8E₊` on a homotopy sphere. ## What Claude added

**Annuli in one chart (§14.3).** Let \(g=\delta+v\) on a slightly enlarged annulus \(A^*\), with \(\varepsilon=\|v\|_\infty\) small, \(K=\|D^2v\|_2\), and \(\mathfrak B=\int_{A^*}|B_g|\,dV_g\). Testing the first variation with a cutoff times \(v\) gives
\[
\mathcal W_A \le C\varepsilon\bigl[\mathfrak B+(K+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon K)\bigr],
\qquad
\mathcal W_A=\int_A |W_g|_{\rm tens}^2\,dV_g.
\]
The leading second-jet terms agree in \(W'_g[v]\) and \(W_g\). Integration by parts controls the quadratic first-derivative term, and the two cutoff profiles have scale-invariant derivative norms in four dimensions. This is why the constants are independent of the annulus length. In a Euclidean transverse traceless chart the flat Weyl symbol controls \(D^2v\) after cutoff, giving \(\mathcal W_A\le C(\varepsilon\mathfrak B+\varepsilon^2)\) without a separate bound on \(K\). [Annular derivation](../notes/SEC_E59_20261124.md)

**Diffeomorphism current (§§14.1, 14.4).** The first variation has bulk term \(-4\langle B,h\rangle\) and a divergence term \(\operatorname{div}\mathfrak b(W;h)\). Substituting \(h=L_Xg\) gives a divergence-free current. Cutting off \(X\) on one side of a closed two-sided slice shows its flux vanishes on that slice, including when the slice does not bound in the full manifold. For a transverse conformal Killing field the boundary-form term vanishes, so \(B=0\) forces \(W=0\) near such slices. In particular, Bach-flat cones with closed cross-section are locally conformally flat. For nearly conical necks the integrated identity bounds their energy by the Bach norm and a weighted defect involving \((L_Xg)_0\) and its first derivative. [Conservation derivation](../notes/SEC_E61_20261124.md)

**Flat linearization (§§14.2, 14.5).** In the flat model \(B'=-\tfrac14\Delta^2\) on transverse traceless fields. The distributional argument excludes Bach-flat homogeneous modes with nonzero linearized Weyl curvature in \(-2<\operatorname{Re}\mu<2\), including powers of \(\log\rho\). Examples at \(\mu=\pm2\) have nonzero curvature. This identifies the first exponents. A uniform decay estimate for arbitrary solutions on finite annuli would require a further argument. For compactly supported perturbations \(h\), flat divergence-freeness permits integration by parts in the logarithmic potential for \(B'(h)\), giving the explicit bounds \(\|h^{\rm TT}\|_\infty\le(2\pi^2)^{-1}\|B'(h)\|_1\) and \(\|W'(h)\|_2^2\le\pi^{-2}\|B'(h)\|_1^2\). The energy estimate transfers to the round sphere at the linearized level. [Linear derivation](../notes/SEC_E60_20261124.md)

## Corrections to the draft

1. **State the error terms in the abstract and outlook.** Both summarize the one-chart estimate as a multiple of \(\varepsilon\mathfrak B\). The theorem includes a derivative term in a general chart and a \(C\varepsilon^2\) term in the transverse traceless chart. The quadratic end mode in §14.3 has energy of order \(\varepsilon^2\) and Bach norm of order at most \(\varepsilon^2\), so a bound by \(C\varepsilon\mathfrak B\) alone would fail on that example. For a sequence, the general estimate gives vanishing neck energy if \(\varepsilon_i\to0\) and \(K_i\), \(\mathfrak B_i\) stay bounded. In the transverse traceless estimate, a fixed small amplitude and vanishing Bach norm only bound the remainder by \(C\varepsilon^2\). The sharpness examples justify the two powers in the transverse traceless estimate and the need for a Bach term in the general bound.
2. **Specify the flat divergence in §15.** The covariant tensor \(B_{g,ij}\) acquires a term schematically of size \(v*DB_g\) when its divergence is taken with the Euclidean metric. The expression involving only first derivatives of \(g\) and undifferentiated Bach curvature holds for the mixed tensor density. From \(\nabla_i B^i{}_j=0\) and symmetry,
   \[
   \partial_i\bigl(\sqrt{\det g}\,B^i{}_j\bigr)
   =\tfrac12\sqrt{\det g}\,B^{ik}\partial_jg_{ik}.
   \]
   This is the coordinate conservation law to use in a nonlinear version of the potential argument.
3. **Distinguish the two endpoint norms in §15.** The flat estimate \(\|D^2h^{\rm TT}\|_{L^{2,1}}\lesssim\|B'(h)\|_1\) follows by applying Hernandez–Spector's Theorem 1.1 with \(d=4\), \(\alpha=2\) to each divergence-free column and then using the boundedness of Riesz transforms on \(L^{2,1}\). Continuity of \(h^{\rm TT}\) comes from the endpoint Sobolev embedding of \(W^{2,(2,1)}\). The spatial sum \(\sum_j\|W\|_{L^2(A_j)}\) over annuli and the Lorentz norm of \(W\) measure different distributions. For example, a function of unit height on equal-volume subsets of \(N\) annuli has annular sum proportional to \(N\) and Lorentz norm proportional to \(\sqrt N\). A comparison for neck curvatures requires additional geometric information. [Hernandez–Spector, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2008.05639)
4. **Use \(|s|\) in the round-sphere expansion of the Bach norm.** The proof of the two-sided ray limit uses \(\mathfrak B(g+sh)=|s|\,\|B'_g[h]\|_1+O(s^2)\). The squared limit in the corollary is correct.

There is also a short improvement to the flat potential constants. Since \(\int\beta=0\) for \(\beta=B'(h)\) with compact support and flat divergence zero, the bounded kernel formula can be written as \(h^{\rm TT}(x)=(4\pi^2)^{-1}\int\beta(y)(I-2\hat w\otimes\hat w)\,dy\), where \(\hat w=(y-x)/|y-x|\). The matrix on the right is a reflection and preserves the Hilbert–Schmidt norm under multiplication. This gives \(\|h^{\rm TT}\|_\infty\le(4\pi^2)^{-1}\|\beta\|_1\). The same energy pairing gives \(\|W'(h)\|_2^2\le(2\pi^2)^{-1}\|\beta\|_1^2\). Via the draft's stereographic argument, the corresponding nondegenerate round-ray bound for \(\mathfrak B^2/E_+\) is \(16\pi^2\).

## Implications for the global argument

The annular and flux results rule out energy retention under explicit geometric control. The missing implication is that bounded \(L^2\) curvature and vanishing \(L^1\) Bach norm in the fixed positive-Yamabe band supply that control, or otherwise give an energy identity. This is the issue for the next neck analysis. Extraction of smooth concentration limits and retention of the starting smooth manifold are additional premises in the relevant compactness arguments.

The Einstein calibration gives a separate constraint on the target. For a positive Einstein metric on the standard sphere, Gauss–Bonnet gives \(Y^2=48(8\pi^2-E_+)\). Gursky's gap puts any non-round example in \((16\pi^2/3,8\pi^2)\), and it would be Bach-flat. A positive lower bound for \(\mathfrak B/E_+\) on the whole band for every fixed margin therefore implies positive Einstein uniqueness on \(S^4\). Akutagawa–Endo–Seshadri's Theorem 1.2 strengthens the lower endpoint by an unspecified positive amount. The argument for an energy identity can allow a genuine closed critical class to carry energy. [Akutagawa–Endo–Seshadri, Theorems 1.1–1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.10305) · [Gursky–Malchiodi, §1](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.07257v1)
