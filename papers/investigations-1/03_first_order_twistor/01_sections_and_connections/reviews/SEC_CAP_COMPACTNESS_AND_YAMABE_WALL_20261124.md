On the standard smooth four-sphere, among conformal classes with Yamabe constant zero that admit a representative equal to the prescribed round metric on \(K\), the infimum of \(E_+\) is \(8\pi^2\). ## What a minimizing sequence would have to retain

Installing a prescribed round cap leaves \(\inf E_+\) unchanged. It also leaves the infimum over \(Y>0\) unchanged if that class is nonempty. Akutagawa–Botvinnik–Kobayashi–Seshadri, Proposition 4.1, supplies a flat conformal germ with arbitrarily small changes to the Yamabe constant and full Weyl energy. A local isotopy and a positive conformal rescaling give the exact marking on the original smooth manifold. Signature converts their four-index integral to \(8E_+\). The construction does not put an unknown exterior into round coordinates. [Marked approximation](../notes/SEC_E25_20261124.md) · [Primary passage record](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_SEC_20261124.md)

For an ASD cap, exterior stationarity for \(E_+\) forces the whole metric to be conformally round. Indeed its variational derivative in the conventions used is \(-B/2\). Stationarity makes the Bach tensor zero outside the cap, and the ASD metric in the cap is also Bach-flat. Smooth matching gives a global Bach-flat metric. A local analytic positive representative of its conformal class makes the chiral Hodge projection analytic. Vanishing of \(W^+\) propagates from the cap through connected charts. Signature then gives \(W^-=0\), and the developing map in SH-E01 identifies the smooth sphere. In particular, retaining a genuinely nonflat ASD cap makes *every smooth extension* admit an exterior-supported decreasing variation. The size of this decrease depends on the extension. [Stationarity proof](../notes/SEC_E24_20261124.md) · [Shared recognition audit](../../notes/SH_E01_20261124.md)

This gives a conditional route to recognition without knowing the minimizing value. Suppose smooth diffeomorphisms of the original \(M\) and conformal factors give a uniformly positive subsequence of marked minimizing metrics converging strongly in \(C^4\), and the moved cap interiors contain a common nonempty open set. The equality of infima allows variations on the whole manifold when passing to the limit. The limit is Bach-flat with a Weyl-flat open set, and finite-regularity development and smoothing yield a smooth diffeomorphism to \(S^4\). The same conclusion holds for such a compact subsequence with \(\int|B|\,dV\to0\). In the minimizing case this also forces the infimum to be zero. Neither the gauges nor the survival of the cap have been constructed for arbitrary \(M\). [Compactness criterion](../notes/SEC_E31_20261124.md)

## How low total Bach curvature can coexist with large Weyl curvature

Join two copies of a one-centre Taub–NUT coordinate ball of increasing Hopf-base radius \(R\), giving them opposite orientations in the standard double \(S^4\). Each core is Ricci-flat with one chiral energy approaching \(8\pi^2\). After rescaling lengths by \(R^{-1}\), the connecting annulus has Hopf circles of length \(O(R^{-1})\). Unwrapping the fiber locally gives uniform metric derivatives through order four while the annular volume is \(O(R^{-1})\). The neck contributes \(O(R^{-1})\) to each chiral energy and to the conformally invariant \(\int|B|\,dV\). An ordinary marked round ball can be installed in a common fixed core germ with an additional \(O(\varepsilon^4)\) Weyl cost and \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) Bach cost. [Double and transition bounds](../notes/SEC_E29_20261124.md) · [Marking costs](../notes/SEC_E30_20261124.md)

The neck supports both signs. A concave reparametrization of the Taub–NUT slices with positive outward mean and positive intrinsic scalar curvature gives a closed nonnegative-scalar-curvature metric, strictly positive on a middle interval. Its conformal class has \(0<Y\le C R^{-1/2}\). A different smooth neck contains a long collapsed Hopf product above a distorted \(S^2\) whose conformal-Laplacian test has negative integral. A separated test gives \(-C R^{-1/2}\le Y\le-cR^{-1/2}\). Both constructions retain the common large cores and the small total Bach curvature. ## The exact zero-sign infimum

Put the two necks on a common identity-marked double before interpolating. Before marking, the endpoint metrics have uniformly bounded coefficients, four derivatives and inverses in the same rescaled, locally unwrapped neck coordinates. Their convex interpolation keeps these bounds and the exact metric in both end collars. Fix one core germ containing the round marking for each family, choose the flattening radius small enough for the positive endpoint at that \(R\), and perform the same modification along the entire path. For a preassigned extended chart one may first move a slightly shrunken extended ball into the core by a fixed diffeomorphism and use the smaller chart for the support of the marking. The negative test is then disjoint from that modification. The lowest conformal-Laplacian eigenvalue changes sign continuously. At a zero, a positive eigenfunction gives a smooth scalar-flat representative. The Weyl and Bach integral estimates hold uniformly along the interpolation, including at any such zero. Diffeomorphism and conformal invariance carry the integrals through the marking without any derivative bound for the marking map.

For any homotopy four-sphere and any Yamabe-zero class, signature and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet in the unit-volume scalar-flat representative \(\widehat g\) give
\[
 E_+=8\pi^2+\tfrac14\int_M |\mathrm{Ric}_{\widehat g}|^2\,dV_{\widehat g}.
\]
Equality requires a Ricci-flat representative. It would be Bach-flat and would retain an open conformally flat set from the marked round ball. Analytic continuation and the developing map would make the class conformally round, which has positive Yamabe constant. This proves strictness for every marked zero-sign class. On the standard sphere the constructed sequence approaches equality, has \(\int|B|\,dV\to0\), and its unit-volume scalar-flat representatives satisfy \(\int|\mathrm{Ric}|^2\,dV\to0\). The scalar-flat representative is generally conformally flat on the cap while a different representative gives the exact round marking.

The unmarked zero-sign infimum on standard \(S^4\) is also \(8\pi^2\). Its attainment would be equivalent to the existence of a smooth Ricci-flat metric on that sphere; this research does not settle that existence question. The sequence minimizes in the zero-sign problem. The unrestricted marked infimum on standard \(S^4\) is zero. No same-manifold positive \(C^4\)-compact subsequence in conformal gauges can retain a common cap interior along this zero-sign sequence, by the Bach-norm form of E31. ## Available estimates strictly below the threshold

Suppose a positive-Yamabe class with \(E_+\le8\pi^2-\delta\) has already been found on \(M\). Its unit-volume Yamabe metric has \(Y\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\) and a uniform inhomogeneous Sobolev inequality. In a generally different unit-volume metric of constant \(\sigma_2(A)\), with \(A=(\mathrm{Ric}-Rg/6)/2\), the CGY equation gives
\[
 \sigma_2(A)=\tfrac12(8\pi^2-E_+),\qquad
 \lambda_{\min}(\mathrm{Ric})\ \ge\ 2\,\frac{\sigma_2(A)}{\sigma_1(A)}.
\]
An independent upper bound \(R\le\Lambda\) in this second representative gives \(\mathrm{Ric}\ge(6\delta/\Lambda)g\), bounded diameter and a lower bound for ball volumes. The scalar bound in Yamabe gauge does not supply this input in the second gauge. These estimates concern the interval up to \(8\pi^2\). Smooth recognition already follows at \(E_+<4\pi^2\) with positive \(Y\). No positive class or energy margin is assumed for the unknown sphere.
