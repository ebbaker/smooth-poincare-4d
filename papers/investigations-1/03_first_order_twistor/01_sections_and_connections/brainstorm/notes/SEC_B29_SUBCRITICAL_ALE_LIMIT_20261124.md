Suppose the invariant $\int|B|/E_i$ tends to zero. Test only a supplied smooth oriented pointed rescaling with scale tending to zero, then separately suppose the complete smooth limit has finitely many positive-order differentiably ALE ends and that full compact truncations embed by the convergence. The marking can shrink. No regularity or compactness follows here from small $L^1$ Bach alone.

First select the scalar-flat Bach-flat limit, sharply track its two chiral energies, inherited Yamabe inequality, and embedded topology. On each end, try the Euclidean quotient Yamabe upper bound. Compare that bound to Chern–Gauss–Bonnet on the entire truncation and in the closed source, retaining the trace-free Ricci term on both sides. Test whether they exclude multiple ends. On a remaining spherical link use the actual smooth embedding restriction and signature defect before attributing either chirality to the limit. For the trivial link examine the one-point compactification theorem in the primary literature and its exact regularity requirements. Independently compare a putative closed Bach-flat class to a shrinking round mark, including endpoint and first-eigenvalue issues. These are tests of specified candidates, with the converse explicitly conditional on the closed class's existence.
