The [full-domain submersion](../../notes/SEC_E03_20261124.md) provides a local right inverse Q only where L* is injective. To turn the local result into an actual finite estimate, distinguish the size of Q on the one required residual from its norm on nonlinear errors. In the Banach chart X=C^{k+2,α}, Y=C^{k,α}, fix the compatible bundle data and a right inverse at h=0. Take a closed ball radius ρ about zero, let H bound the derivative Lipschitz norm of F there, and put η=‖QW_r‖_X. Use h↦−tQW_r−Q(F(h)−F(0)−Lh) on im Q. Test the explicit threshold η+‖Q‖Hρ²/2≤ρ and ‖Q‖Hρ<1. This can succeed on every reference satisfying its verifiable analytic inequalities. It has an unconditional test class near the known round sphere, where one can choose a reference-independent complementary domain to avoid a spurious blowup at a drop of conformal symmetries.

E04 checks the estimate with common norms and, at a fixed high grade, uniform constants for sufficiently near-round smooth references on standard S⁴. Positivity, volume, the right inverse and its smooth dependence must be stated in the chart actually used. The quantities are allowed to depend on r. The unknown is how to reach such a ball on an arbitrary M; small L² curvature alone gives no strong norm bound. An example where the radial forcing is orthogonal to almost-null adjoint tests would also distinguish the two sizes. The exact endpoint and the finite-time CGY alternative are assessed separately.
