A change of reference shifts both the center and the prescribed section. An independent test for the original radial source uses genuine global kernel tensors near longitudinal coordinate variations in the actual exterior. For a vector field X of size 1/N with an order-one, principally longitudinal derivative, start from e=KX and subtract p=L*G_P Le to obtain k=e−p in the exact kernel. The curvature correction Lp=Le has order-one oscillations although p itself tends to zero in H¹. Thus a mixed Hessian term with a gauge direction can survive in the cokernel. A compact gauge direction in a round patch misses this term. This is a fixed-chart question and makes no assumption about neighboring metrics.

The proposed weak limit of b(F''[k,k]) at an exterior probe is a *positive* average of b against the pointwise tensor W. The sign follows heuristically from subtracting two mixed terms from a single pure-gauge term. E43 tests the actual sign, chart rotation and weak error. E44 tests the independent finite-dimensional implication: if the projected quadratic observables have such limits, when do they give a regular signed zero with a finite list of genuine, smooth kernel tensors? The dual certificate for failure of this particular cone test is an adjoint field pairing pointwise with W with one sign. Finite numerical sampling cannot certify the condition. This comparison can supply a local chord centered at the singular reference, or can delimit the oscillations even if a nearby reference is regular. No universal assertion about the signs of exterior pairings is assumed.
