## Fixed data and asserted lift

Let \(M\) be one smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere. Let \(r\)
be a supplied smooth positive-definite real Riemannian metric which
agrees, by an oriented identification, with the model round metric
on an embedded coordinate ball \(O\). Let \(K\Subset O\) be the
protected closed smooth coordinate subball. Use \(\nu=dV_r\),
\(V=S^2_0T^*M\), and \(B=S^2_0\Lambda_r^+\), the real trace-free
self-adjoint endomorphisms with curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt
pairing. ASD means \(W^+=0\). In the E03 full trace-free chart set

\[
 g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot),\qquad h^\sharp=r^{-1}h,\qquad
 F_r(h)=I_hW^+_{g_h}I_h^{-1},\qquad L=dF_r(0),
\]

where \(I_h\) is induced by the positive symmetric tangent isometry
\(e^{-h^\sharp/2}\) from \(r\) to \(g_h\). Adjoint and orthogonality
are with respect to \(r,\nu\). The baseline \(F_r(0)=W_r^+\) vanishes
on \(O\). Fix an integer \(s\ge5\) and let \(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\).
There are finitely many smooth source directions vanishing on \(K\).
After including them, every sufficiently small \(y\in H^s(B)\)
has a unique small lift in the specified complement, giving
\(g_h\) in \(H^{s+2}\), and smooth \(g_h\) when \(y\) is smooth.
[B18][b18] · [E03, fixed chart and (3)–(4)][e03]

## Exterior restriction and the two Gram matrices

By E03 the fourth-order \(P=LL^*\) is elliptic with symbol
\(|\xi|_r^4 I_B/16\). Its kernel equals \(\mathcal C\) by the
adjoint identity and consists of smooth sections. The generalized
inverse \(G_P\) maps \(H^s(B)\) boundedly to
\(U_s=H^{s+4}(B)\cap\mathcal C^\perp\), with
\(PG_P=I-\pi_{\mathcal C}\), the orthogonal complement projection.
Indeed, Fredholmness gives closed range. A distribution in its
annihilator solves \(P^*Z=PZ=0\) and is smooth by elliptic
regularity, so the range is exactly the stated orthogonal
complement. The resulting restricted inverse is bounded.
For the round sphere the matching full adjoint has zero kernel.
The latter follows from round unobstructedness with the
bundle and norm comparison of E02. [E03][e03] · [E02, round
inverse][e02] · [Melrose, Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose] ·
[Gover–Gursky, §1.1, pp. 295–296][gg]

Suppose \(Z\in\mathcal C\) vanishes on \(M\setminus K\). On \(O\)
the local coefficients of \(L\) and \(L^*\) identify with those
of the round linearization and formal adjoint: the metric, volume
and fiber pairings agree in the whole local neighborhood.
Transplant \(Z\) by the round isometry and extend by zero to
the rest of \(S^4\). It is zero in the collar outside \(K\) before
extension and the resulting section is smooth and solves the
round adjoint equation everywhere. Round injectivity forces
\(Z=0\). This gives injective restriction of \(\mathcal C\) to
the actual exterior using only the round adjoint theorem.

Choose \(w\ge0\) smooth, zero on \(K\) and strictly positive on
\(M\setminus K\). For example, in the exterior collar use
\(\exp(-1/d^2)\) with an outward collar coordinate \(d>0\),
extend by zero inside and interpolate positively to \(1\)
farther out. The support closure is allowed to meet \(\partial K\);
all derivatives of \(w\) are zero there. If a collar of zero
source is required, choose a slightly larger closed subball
\(K'\Subset O\) containing \(K\) in its interior and repeat
the restriction argument with \(K'\) in place of \(K\).

Take a reference \(L^2\)-orthonormal real basis \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\)
of \(\mathcal C\). Write

\[
 \mathcal W c=\sum_i c_i wZ_i,\qquad
 b(f)_i=\langle Z_i,f\rangle_{L^2(r)},\qquad
 \Gamma_{ij}=\langle Z_i,wZ_j\rangle_{L^2(r)}.
\]

Thus \(b\mathcal W=\Gamma\). For \(Z_\alpha=\sum_i\alpha_i Z_i\),
\(\alpha^\mathsf{T}\Gamma\alpha=\int_M w|Z_\alpha|^2\,dV_r\).
If this vanishes, continuity and positivity of \(w\) give
\(Z_\alpha=0\) on the exterior, and the transplant result
gives \(\alpha=0\). The ordinary Gram matrix of the weighted
sections \(wZ_i\), with entries
\(\langle wZ_i,wZ_j\rangle_{L^2(r)}\), is positive definite by
the same argument with \(w^2\). The coefficient equation uses
\(\Gamma\), the Gram pairing with a single factor of \(w\).
When \(m=0\) all moment and correction rows are empty.

## Graded split inverse and signed coefficients

For \(u\in U_s\) take \(h=L^*u\in H^{s+2}(V)\) and define

\[
 \Phi(u,c)=F_r(L^*u)-F_r(0)+\mathcal W c,
 \qquad
 \Phi:U_s\times\mathbb R^m\longrightarrow H^s(B).
 \tag{1}
\]

The curvature map is smooth at these grades by Sobolev products
and composition applied to the second-order metric differential
expression. The two derivatives in \(L^*\) and the two in
\(F_r\) account for the four-derivative gap in (1). Its
derivative at the origin is \(P\dot u+\mathcal W\dot c\). For
a target increment \(y\), the unique derivative inverse is

\[
 \dot c=\Gamma^{-1}b(y),\qquad
 \dot u=G_P\!\bigl(y-\mathcal W\Gamma^{-1}b(y)\bigr).
 \tag{2}
\]

Indeed the right side given to \(G_P\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal C\).
This inverse is bounded with the displayed grades, making the
Banach inverse function theorem applicable. On neighborhoods of
the origins there is a smooth inverse \(y\mapsto(u(y),c(y))\).
Writing \(h(y)=L^*u(y)\), it gives

\[
 F_r(h(y))=F_r(0)+y-\mathcal Wc(y),\qquad
 c(y)=\Gamma^{-1}b(y)+O(\|y\|_{H^s}^2).
 \tag{3}
\]

The derivatives of \(u(y)\) and \(h(y)\) are respectively the
second expression in (2) and its image under \(L^*\).
The exterior term in the realized Weyl target is
\(-\mathcal Wc(y)\). In particular the radial increment
\(y=-tF_r(0)\) has
\(c(y)=-t\Gamma^{-1}b(F_r(0))+O(t^2)\) for sufficiently small
\(|t|\), with the reference held fixed. [E03, normal operator
and inverse grades][e03]

## Smoothness in space and interface matching

For a smooth \(y\) the solution of (1) is spatially smooth
at the same low-norm size threshold. To verify this, adjoin
the moment row \(b(u)=0\) to (1) and use the full space
\(H^{s+4}(B)\times\mathbb R^m\), with target
\(H^s(B)\times\mathbb R^m\). At zero the derivative sends
\((v,d)\) to \((Pv+\mathcal Wd,b(v))\). For output \((z,a)\)
the inverse gives \(d=\Gamma^{-1}b(z)\) and
\(v=G_P(z-\mathcal Wd)+\sum_i a_i Z_i\). By continuity this
derivative remains invertible at the sufficiently small solution.

Choose a smooth family of coordinate changes equal to translations
on a smaller framed ball and the identity outside a larger one,
and let \(T_\eta\) be their component pullbacks on \(B\). Conjugate
the differential row by \(T_\eta\), leaving the scalar variable
unchanged, and take \(b(T_\eta^{-1}v)\) as the moment row on
the translated unknown \(v\). The spatial operator with \(L^*\)
inserted is differential of order at most four with smooth
background coefficients. The conjugated differential row depends
smoothly on \((\eta,v,c)\) at the same Sobolev grades by the chain
rule and Sobolev composition. In the integral row, change
variables to transfer the pullback to the smooth basis, reference
bundle pairings and density. This gives smooth dependence on
\((\eta,v)\) for that row as well. The target is
\((T_\eta y,0)\), smooth in \(\eta\) for smooth \(y\).
The implicit function theorem at \((u(y),c(y))\) shows that the
translated solution varies smoothly in the same Sobolev spaces:
\((T_\eta u(y),c(y))\) is the unique nearby pair by its strong
continuity and the transformed equation. On the smaller ball,
differentiation in the translation parameter gives all spatial
weak derivatives of \(u(y)\) in \(H^{s+4}\). Local Sobolev
embedding now proves smoothness of \(u(y)\) and \(g_{h(y)}\).
This uses the spatial translation argument in E03 with the
additional finite moment row accounted for. [E03, spatial
regularity of the normal slice][e03]

The metric is positive and \(dV_{g_h}=e^{\operatorname{tr}_r h/2}\nu
=\nu\) pointwise. The compatible chiral connection is the transported
\(\Gamma^+(g_h)\). On \(K\), (3) fixes the transported Weyl
block to \(y|_K\), because \(F_r(0)\) and the correction both
vanish there. The correction also has zero jets along \(\partial K\).
The metric and compatible connection are each globally defined
on the given \(M\), so their two restrictions agree in shared
charts and fixed bundle transitions at the interface, with
opposite outward normals. These are curvature source constraints.
The solution may deform the metric on \(K\) and its interface.

## Exact balance and the role of kernel directions

Set \(N(h)=F_r(h)-F_r(0)-Lh\). Taking moments of (1) on the
solved slice yields the exact identity

\[
 \Gamma c(y)=b\!\left(y-N(h(y))\right).
 \tag{4}
\]

The uncorrected target \(F_r(0)+y\) is attained by the
nearby solution in this slice precisely when the right side
of (4) vanishes. The linear test is \(b(y)=0\); the nonlinear
test retains the term \(b(N(h(y)))\). The baseline is the
fixed reference section even when its exterior value is
nonzero. Reaching a global zero block would require the increment
\(-F_r(0)\) to lie in the solvability neighborhood and pass
the nonlinear balance for the selected lift.

The bounded linear projection \(L^*G_P L\) on \(H^{s+2}(V)\)
has range \(L^*U_s\) and kernel \(\ker L\). Thus the full trace-free
chart allows free linear kernel directions \(k\), whereas
(1) fixes \(k=0\). With \(h=k+L^*u\), the same local augmented
inverse can be applied with a small \(k\) as parameter, since
its derivative in \((u,c)\) at zero is still (2). An
uncorrected solution with such \(k\) must meet the balance
with its own \(N(k+L^*u)\). The slice test neither determines
whether another \(k\) makes those moments vanish nor integrates
arbitrary linear kernel vectors into uncorrected solutions.

## Topology of the actual exterior

Let \(A=M\setminus\operatorname{int}K\). Its boundary is
\(\partial K\simeq S^3\) with a collar. It is connected since
the boundary is connected and any additional component would
be a nonempty open and closed subset of \(M\) disjoint from
the boundary. Use open collar thickenings of \(A,K\) in \(M\)
whose intersection retracts onto \(S^3\). Van Kampen gives
\(\pi_1(A)\simeq\pi_1(M)=0\) because \(K\) and the intersection
are simply connected. Mayer–Vietoris gives vanishing reduced
homology in degrees one and two and the exact segment

\[
 0\longrightarrow H_4(A)\longrightarrow H_4(M)
  \longrightarrow H_3(S^3)\longrightarrow H_3(A)
  \longrightarrow 0 .
\]

The middle map sends the oriented fundamental class of \(M\)
to the orientation class of \(\partial K\) up to sign.
It is the restriction to \(H_4(M,A)\simeq H_4(K,\partial K)\)
followed by the relative boundary map, so it is an isomorphism.
Both \(H_4(A)\) and \(H_3(A)\) vanish, and higher homology
vanishes by dimension. The smooth compact exterior has CW type.
Its map to a point is a homology isomorphism between
simply connected CW types, so the homology form of Whitehead
gives contractibility. The analytic lift uses \(A\) with its given
smooth structure and boundary marking. [Hatcher, Theorem 1.20,
§2.2, Corollary 4.33][hatcher]
