## Setup and the compact correction test

Let \(r\) be the unit round metric on the closed oriented smooth
\(S^4\). In oriented stereographic coordinates on the complement
of the south pole write \(r=\Omega^2\delta\), with \(\delta\) the
Euclidean metric and \(\Omega(x)=2/(1+|x|^2)\). The equator is
\(|x|=1\) and the northern cap is \(|x|<1\). Use real Riemannian
metrics, ASD convention \(W^+=0\) and curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt pairing on \(B_g^\pm=S^2_0\Lambda_g^\pm\).
For any oriented Euclidean orthonormal coframe \(e^0,e^1,e^2,e^3\),
write \(\omega_i^\pm=(e^0\wedge e^i\pm e^j\wedge e^k)/\sqrt2\)
for cyclic \((i,j,k)\) in \((1,2,3)\). The corresponding round
forms in the stereographic chart are \(\Omega^2\omega_i^\pm\).
Here the flat frames are constant in the chosen coordinates.
The linearized operators \(D_{\pm,g}\) are derivatives of \(W_g^\pm\)
in the corresponding positive symmetric transported frames,
at \(g=r\) or \(g=\delta\). Both backgrounds have zero full Weyl
curvature. [B16 setup][b16] · [Shared conventions][common] · [E02][e02]

Use the prescribed source
\[
 f_{\mathrm{flat}}(x)=\chi(|x|)Z,\qquad Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2),
 \qquad
 \chi(\rho)=\begin{cases}
 \exp(-1/(1-4\rho^2)),&\rho<1/2,\\
 0,&\rho\ge1/2,
 \end{cases}
\]
and \(f_{\mathrm{round}}=\Omega^{-2}f_{\mathrm{flat}}\) in the
induced positive round frame, extended by zero at the pole.
At the flat metric \(D_{+,\delta}\) has constant coefficients and
only second derivatives. In particular its formal adjoint on
any Euclidean open set kills the parallel Weyl field \(Z\).
Equivalently, pair its second derivative terms with \(Z\) and
integrate twice by parts for a compactly supported metric
variation. Thus a necessary condition for a compactly supported
correction in the northern flat chart is that the source pair
to zero with \(Z\). In this case the pairing is
\[
 \int_{\mathbb R^4}\langle f_{\mathrm{flat}},Z\rangle_{\rm HS}\,dx
 =6\int_{\mathbb R^4}\chi(|x|)\,dx>0 .
 \tag{1}
\]
There is no compactly supported metric variation giving this
flat source. The same argument rules out a variation supported
in the round northern cap by the conformal weight below. There
is no restriction to TT in (1). The constant mode is the flat
local adjoint test, as in the collar double divergence in E17.
[E17, (1)–(2)][e17] · [Gover–Gursky, §1.1 formal adjoint][gg]

## Conformal transport and the global metric gauge

For \(h\) a covariant metric variation on the sphere use
\(u=\Omega^{-2}h\) in the flat coordinates. The Hodge star on
two-forms in four dimensions is unchanged by positive
conformal scaling. The Weyl tensor with all indices covariant
scales by the square of the scale factor, hence its curvature
endomorphism scales by the inverse square. Applying this to
\(r+t h\) and \(\delta+t u=\Omega^{-2}(r+t h)\), and using the
induced chiral frames, gives the exact linearized identity
\[
 D_{\pm,\delta}u=\Omega^2 D_{\pm,r}h .
 \tag{2}
\]
Starting at vanishing Weyl ensures there is no change from
the derivative of a frame used to identify the targets.
Equation (2) holds without transporting the round divergence
condition into a Euclidean TT condition. For compactly
supported test metrics it also shows that the local round
adjoint mode corresponding to \(Z\) is \(\Omega^{-2}Z\) in
the round frame. Indeed pairing (2) against \(Z\) with \(dx\)
is equivalent to pairing \(D_{+,r}h\) against \(\Omega^{-2}Z\)
with \(dV_r=\Omega^4dx\). The latter local adjoint mode grows
in round norm at the missing pole and supplies no smooth
global cokernel field. [E02, conformal comparison (9)][e02] ·
[Shared Weyl normalization][recognition]

By E02's round TT kernel and the round ASD unobstructedness,
\(D_{+,r}|_{\mathrm{TT}_r}\) is bijective from smooth TT metric
variations to smooth \(B_r^+\) fields. On Sobolev grades the
inverse is \(D_{+,r}^*(D_{+,r}D_{+,r}^*)^{-1}\) with gain two.
Each \(D_{\pm,r}\) kills pure conformal and diffeomorphism
variations because its background Weyl block is zero. On trace-free
variations the global splitting into TT and \(\mathcal K_r X\)
uses the closed elliptic operator \(\mathcal K_r^*\mathcal K_r\),
with \(\mathcal K_r X=(\mathcal L_Xr)_0\). Hence any choice of
metric solution of the positive equation differing by one of
these gauges yields the same opposite Weyl field. The
conformal Killing stabilizer occurs in the vector potential,
whereas the TT solution itself is unique. An orientation-reversing
round isometry interchanges \(+\) and \(-\) and preserves TT,
so the same statement holds for the negative block.
[E02, §5][e02] · [E03, TT decomposition][e03] ·
[Gover–Gursky, §1.1, pp. 295–296 and the round example][gg]

## The full flat symbol and its relative sign

Use the positive-sectional-curvature convention
\(\mathcal R_{ab,cd}=\langle R(e_a,e_b)e_d,e_c\rangle\) and
first replace each derivative by the corresponding real
covector component. At \(\xi=\ell e^0\ne0\), a TT symbol
tensor has \(h_{0a}=0\) and trace-free \((h_{ij})\) on
\(\xi^\perp\). The flat curvature derivative gives
\(\sigma_\xi(\mathcal R')_{0i,0j}=-\ell^2 h_{ij}/2\).
Every entry with an index pair omitting \(0\) vanishes. Both
chiral compressions therefore have matrix
\(-\ell^2(h_{ij})/4\) in the respective \(\omega_i^\pm\).
Their trace is zero. The scalar correction consequently gives
no change and the traceless Ricci term is in the off-diagonal
curvature blocks. Thus on the TT symbol fiber
\[
 \sigma_\xi(D_{+,\delta})h=-\tfrac{|\xi|^2}{4}(h_{ij}),\qquad
 \sigma_\xi(D_{-,\delta})h=-\tfrac{|\xi|^2}{4}(h_{ij}) .
 \tag{3}
\]
With Fourier derivatives \(i\xi\) both signs change together.
[E02, (5)][e02] · [E16 flat curvature, (1)][e16] ·
[Gover–Gursky, curvature blocks before Definition 1.1][gg]

For \(\xi\ne0\), let \(R_\xi=I-2|\xi|^{-2}\xi\otimes\xi^\sharp\)
act on covectors, the reflection that negates \(\xi\) and fixes
\(\xi^\perp\). Put \(C_\xi=(\Lambda^2 R_\xi)|_{\Lambda_\delta^+}\)
with range \(\Lambda_\delta^-\). Orientation reversal anticommutes
with the Hodge star on two-forms, so this is an isometric
identification. When \(\xi=\ell e^0\), it sends \(\omega_i^+\)
to \(-\omega_i^-\). The minus sign cancels in conjugation of
endomorphisms. Define the fiber map
\[
 M(\xi)A=C_\xi A C_\xi^{-1},\qquad A\in B_\delta^+ .
 \tag{4}
\]
Both curvature symbols are orthogonally natural, with the
chiralities interchanged under orientation reversal. An
orientation-preserving rotation takes \(e^0\) to any specified
unit \(\xi\), so (3) proves the relative symbol formula with
(4) on each TT fiber. In the full trace-free fiber, the
complement of TT is \(\operatorname{im}\sigma_\xi(\mathcal K_\delta)\).
Both symbols annihilate that complement by diffeomorphism and
conformal invariance. Consequently their full kernels agree
and are exactly that complement. A further trace direction
lies in both kernels on the unrestricted symmetric fiber.
Writing \(d_\pm(\xi)\) for the Fourier symbols, the identity is
\[
 d_-(\xi)=M(\xi)d_+(\xi),\qquad \xi\ne0 .
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular \(M(\xi)\) is the relative symbol with positive
sign, is even in \(\xi\), has degree zero, and acts isometrically
for the operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Its inverse and adjoint
are conjugation by \(C_\xi^{-1}\). In frequency variables one
also has \(d_-^*(\xi)M(\xi)=d_+^*(\xi)\). Since \(d_+(\xi)\)
is onto for \(\xi\ne0\), the Euclidean formal adjoint has no
nonzero \(L^2\) null field on all of \(\mathbb R^4\). The constant
adjoint test in (1) is a local test against compact variations.

One useful polynomial presentation follows from the projection
onto the subspace of two-forms containing the direction \(\xi\).
On all two-forms,
\(\Lambda^2 R_\xi=I-2|\xi|^{-2}\xi\wedge\iota_{\xi^\sharp}\).
Thus \(B(\xi)=|\xi|^2 C_\xi\) is the homogeneous quadratic map
\(-2\pi_-\xi\wedge\iota_{\xi^\sharp}|_{\Lambda_\delta^+}\), where
\(\pi_-\) is projection to \(\Lambda_\delta^-\). In fixed frames
\[
 |\xi|^4 M(\xi)A=B(\xi)A B(\xi)^* .
 \tag{6}
\]
This determines the quartic polynomial numerator with all
normalizations. The radial evaluation is a separate packet.

## Exact round operator, zero frequency and the prescribed integral

Let \(h=(D_{+,r}|_{\mathrm{TT}_r})^{-1}f\) for any smooth round
source \(f\). Since \(|u|_\delta=|h|_r\), its flat metric
representative \(u=\Omega^{-2}h\) is bounded in the parallel
coordinates and defines a tempered distribution. The two
curvature fields \(D_{\pm,\delta}u=\Omega^2 D_{\pm,r}h\) are
in \(L^2(\mathbb R^4)\). In fact multiplication by \(\Omega^2\)
in the induced orthonormal frames gives an onto isometry
\(\mathcal C_\pm:L^2(B_r^\pm,dV_r)\to L^2(B_\delta^\pm,dx)\),
since \(dV_r=\Omega^4dx\) and the omitted point has measure
zero. Fourier transforming (5) as a distributional identity
away from zero gives
\[
 \widehat{D_{-,\delta}u}(\xi)=M(\xi)\widehat{D_{+,\delta}u}(\xi)
 \quad \text{on }\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\} .
\]
Both sides are \(L^2\) fields, so they agree as \(L^2\) fields
on all of \(\mathbb R^4\). A discrepancy supported at the
origin cannot be represented by a nonzero \(L^2\) field. A
value for \(M\) at a single frequency need never be specified.
In particular this argument imposes no vanishing average
requirement on a smooth compactly supported flat source.

Writing \(\mathscr F\) for the Euclidean Fourier transform,
the exact global opposite response operator is therefore
\[
 \mathcal T_r:=D_{-,r}(D_{+,r}|_{\mathrm{TT}_r})^{-1}
     =\mathcal C_-^{-1}\mathscr F^{-1} M\mathscr F\mathcal C_+ .
 \tag{7}
\]
It extends to an onto isometry of real round \(L^2\) target
spaces by Plancherel, since \(M\) is pointwise orthogonal
and even. The adjoint is the inverse, given on smooth fields
by \(D_{+,r}(D_{-,r}|_{\mathrm{TT}_r})^{-1}\). The derivation
starts from the smooth global metric lift. It permits use
of the Euclidean transformation to compute the curvature
without choosing a Euclidean TT metric inverse at the zero
frequency, or supposing that an arbitrary flat gauge
compactifies as a smooth round metric variation. The response
of every smooth global round source in (7) extends smoothly
across the pole because it is the opposite curvature of the
original smooth lift. Equal jets across the equator come from
this one global metric variation and the chosen gluing of
the two caps. No boundary condition is imposed independently
on the southern cap.

Use the Fourier convention \(\widehat q(\xi)=\int e^{-ix\cdot\xi}q(x)\,dx\).
For the specified seed the flat response and the corresponding
round response in the induced negative frame are exactly
\[
 G_-(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^4}\int_{\mathbb R^4}
    e^{ix\cdot\xi}\,\widehat\chi(\xi) C_\xi Z C_\xi^{-1}\,d\xi,
 \qquad (\mathcal T_r f_{\mathrm{round}})(x)=\Omega(x)^{-2}G_-(x).
 \tag{8}
\]
Here \(\widehat\chi\) is the radial transform of \(x\mapsto\chi(|x|)\).
The integral is absolutely convergent, and the factor \(\widehat\chi\)
is Schwartz, so spatial derivatives can be taken inside it.
On the southern cap it is used for \(|x|\ge1\), with the pole
value interpreted by the smooth round extension above. In
the fixed flat frames \(G_-(x)=O(|x|^{-4})\) follows from that
extension and the \(\Omega^2\) weight. Equation (6) also gives
\(\Delta^2 G_-\) as the differential operator with quartic
symbol \(B(\xi) Z B(\xi)^*\) applied to \(\chi\), so the flat
field is biharmonic outside the support of \(\chi\).

There is a nonzero southern response. Since \(\widehat\chi(0)>0\),
the multiplier product \(\widehat\chi(\xi)M(\xi)Z\) has differing
directional limits at zero frequency. At \(\xi=e^0\), \(M(\xi)Z\) has the same
ordered diagonal as \(Z\). At \(\xi=(e^0+e^1)/\sqrt2\), the reflection
sends \(e^0\) to \(-e^1\), \(e^1\) to \(-e^0\), and fixes
\(e^2,e^3\). Its induced map sends \(\omega_1^+\) to
\(-\omega_1^-\), \(\omega_2^+\) to \(\omega_3^-\), and
\(\omega_3^+\) to \(-\omega_2^-\), yielding
\(\operatorname{diag}(1,-2,1)\) after conjugation. If \(G_-\) vanished
outside any bounded set, it would be integrable and have a
continuous Fourier transform. On every punctured neighborhood
the transform must agree with the continuous multiplier product,
which has the two different limits just computed. This is
impossible. In particular \(G_-\) is nonzero somewhere for \(|x|>1\).
The argument uses the mean of the source and does not evaluate
the radial profile in closed form.
