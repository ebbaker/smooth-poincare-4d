## Obstruction for the conformal core

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere.
Fix an embedding into \(M\) of a flat product \(U=(-a,a)\times T^3\),
with \(T^3=\mathbb R^3/\Gamma\) for a translation lattice and flat
product metric \(g_U=ds^2+\sum_i d\theta_i^2\) in Euclidean cover
coordinates. Identify \(U\) with its image. Take a based loop
\(\gamma\) in a constant-\(s\) slice representing a primitive
vector \(v=\ell e\in\Gamma\), where \(\ell>0\) and \(e\) is unit.
An embedding and smooth extension of this product metric as reference
data are available by the framed torus and cap construction of [E11][e11].
The flat endpoint of the local inverse in [E15][e15] gives this type of
conformal data. The following obstruction applies to any embedding
with the prescribed core.

Develop the universal cover \((-a,a)\times\mathbb R^3\) by its
inclusion in \(\mathbb R^4\subset S^4\), where the inclusion into
the conformal sphere denotes the stereographic chart. With the
equivariance convention
\(\operatorname{dev}(\gamma x)=\rho(\gamma)\operatorname{dev}(x)\),
the Möbius holonomy is the nonidentity translation \(x\mapsto x+v\).
The Levi-Civita linear holonomy of the flat metric around
\(\gamma\) is the identity because the parallel Euclidean
frame descends under translations. The Möbius action is a conformal
invariant up to conjugacy. Its translation length in this chart
uses the chosen Euclidean scale. [Schoen–Yau, pp. 47, 49–50][sy]

Suppose a smooth Riemannian metric \(g\) on \(M\) had \(W_g^+=0\)
and \([g|_U]=[g_U]\). The closed signature formula in the
curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt normalization is
\(12\pi^2\tau(M)=E_+(M,g)-E_-(M,g)\). Since \(\tau(M)=0\), the
supposed ASD metric would satisfy \(W_g=0\). It would thus give
a locally conformally flat structure on all of \(M\) with a single-valued
developing map on the simply connected \(M\). The restriction of
that map to \(U\), pulled back to the universal cover of \(U\),
would be a developing map for \([g_U]\) and would be invariant
under its deck group. Uniqueness up to a single Möbius transformation
would make its holonomy at \(\gamma\) conjugate to the computed
nonidentity translation. The contradiction excludes \(g\).
Equivalently, the deck generator of \(U\) maps to the identity of
\(\pi_1(M)\), so it would have trivial holonomy under any restriction
of a global flat conformal structure. This conclusion only needs
preservation of the conformal class on the core.
[SH-E01 signature and developing argument][she01] · [Schoen–Yau §1][sy]

## Tractor holonomy in a fixed metric scale

Now suppose \(g\) is any smooth Riemannian extension equal to \(g_U\)
on \(U\). Fix the standard normal tractor connection
\(\nabla^{\mathcal T}\) of \([g]\). In the splitting of the
three tractor slots by \(g\), trivialize density weights by the
metric scale and write \((\sigma,\mu,\rho)\), with covector
\(\mu\). Let \(P=(\operatorname{Ric}-\tfrac16 s_g g)/2\) be the
Schouten tensor, where \(s_g\) is scalar curvature. The connection
formula is

\[
 \nabla^{\mathcal T}_X
 \begin{pmatrix}\sigma\\\mu\\\rho\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
  X\sigma-\mu(X)\\
  \nabla_X\mu+\rho X^\flat+\sigma P(X,\cdot)\\
  X\rho-\langle P(X,\cdot),\mu\rangle_g
 \end{pmatrix}.
\]

Here \(X^\flat=g(X,\cdot)\). In particular the normal connection
is determined by the conformal class. In the flat scale on \(U\),
the three ordinary derivatives in the parallel splitting reduce
to coordinate derivatives and \(P=0\). Along the unit tangent
\(e\) to the positive loop, the constant connection matrix sends
\((\sigma,\mu,\rho)\) to \((-\mu(e),\rho e^\flat,0)\).
Parallel transport after length \(\ell\) is therefore

\[
 T_\ell(\sigma,\mu,\rho)=
 \bigl(\sigma+\ell\mu(e)-\tfrac12\ell^2\rho,\,
       \mu-\ell\rho e^\flat,\ \rho\bigr).
\]

With the null-ray parametrization
\(x\mapsto[(-|x|^2/2,x^\flat,1)]\) this acts as \(x\mapsto x-\ell e\).
It is the inverse of the previous developing action in the stated
equivariance convention. Both have nontrivial holonomy. The tractor
metric in this splitting has quadratic form \(2\sigma\rho+|\mu|_g^2\).
Use instead the positive norm

\[
 |(\sigma,\mu,\rho)|_{\mathsf H_g}^2
 =\sigma^2+|\mu|_g^2+\rho^2
\]

on each fibre. At the basepoint \(p=\gamma(0)\), the unit tractor
\((0,0,1)\) gives the bound

\[
 \|T_\ell-I\|_{\mathsf H_g(p)}\geq
 \delta_\ell,\qquad
 \delta_\ell=\sqrt{\ell^2+\ell^4/4}>0.
\]

This bound depends on the chosen scale and positive norm.
Conjugating the translation by a Euclidean dilation rescales
its translation parameter.
[Curry–Gover, (22), Theorem 3.10 and Proposition 3.12][cg]

## Filling inequality

Choose a smooth based contraction \(F:[0,1]^2\to M\) with parameters
\(u,t\), with
\(F(0,t)=p\), \(F(1,t)=\gamma(t)\) and \(F(u,0)=F(u,1)=p\).
The loop is null-homotopic by simple connectivity. A smooth spanning
disc can be obtained by relative smoothing of a continuous spanning
disc. Composing it with contraction of the disc boundary to the
marked boundary point by straight segments gives such a based
homotopy. It is also enough to use piecewise smooth transport
and integrate the identity on the smooth pieces.

Let \(S_u(t_2,t_1)\) be tractor transport from \(F(u,t_1)\) to
\(F(u,t_2)\) along the \(t\)-curve. Suppose these segment maps have
operator norms at most \(B\) in their endpoint \(\mathsf H_g\) norms,
in both directions, for all \(u,t_1,t_2\). For a fixed smooth
metric and smooth contraction this holds for some finite \(B\).
For example, in the splitting, the difference of the tractor
connection and the positive-metric-compatible componentwise
Levi-Civita connection in direction \(X\) has operator norm bounded
by \(|X|_g+|P(X,\cdot)|_g\), by separating the two indicated
off-diagonal pairs in (22). Integrating the norm evolution in
either direction gives

\[
 B\leq
 \exp\left(\sup_u\int_0^1
       \bigl(|\partial_tF|_g+
             |P(\partial_tF,\cdot)|_g\bigr)\,dt\right)
\]

for the least uniform segment bound. In particular uniform bounds
on the lengths of the homotopy curves and on \(|P|\) along the
filling are sufficient for a uniform choice of \(B\).

Write \(\Omega\) for the curvature of \(\nabla^{\mathcal T}\),
with the commutator sign \([\nabla_u,\nabla_t]\) on the
pulled-back bundle. For a fixed tractor \(z\) at \(p\) let
\(Z(u,t)=S_u(t,0)z\). Then
\(\nabla_tZ=0\), \(\nabla_u Z(u,0)=0\), and
\(\nabla_t\nabla_u Z=-\Omega(\partial_u F,\partial_t F)Z\).
Integration in \(t\) and the constant endpoint fibres give

\[
 \frac{d}{du}S_u(1,0)=
 -\int_0^1 S_u(1,t)\,
    \Omega(\partial_u F,\partial_t F)\,
    S_u(t,0)\,dt .
\]

This uses the transports from the initial endpoint to \(F(u,t)\)
and from \(F(u,t)\) back to the basepoint along the terminal
segment. The latter is the inverse of the transport from the
final endpoint backwards along that segment. Set
\(J_2^gF=|\partial_uF\wedge\partial_tF|_g\) and define the curvature
comass \(|\Omega|_{\mathsf H_g,*}\) as the supremum of
\(\|\Omega(X,Y)\|_{\mathsf H_g}\) over \(|X\wedge Y|_g=1\).
Since \(S_0(1,0)=I\) and \(S_1(1,0)=T_\ell\), the formula implies

\[
 \|T_\ell-I\|_{\mathsf H_g(p)}
 \leq B^2\int_{[0,1]^2}
          |\Omega|_{\mathsf H_g,*}(F)\,J_2^gF\,du\,dt .
\]

In the same splitting, with \(C_{ab c}=\nabla_aP_{bc}-\nabla_bP_{ac}\)
and \(C_{XY}=C(X,Y,\cdot)\), the curvature is

\[
 \Omega(X,Y)(\sigma,\mu,\rho)
 =\bigl(0,\ W_{XY}\mu+\sigma C_{XY},\
             -\langle C_{XY},\mu\rangle_g\bigr).
\]

Here \(W_{XY}\) is the endomorphism of covectors from the full
Weyl tensor. Thus \(|\Omega|_{\mathsf H_g,*}\) is bounded above
by \(|W|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}+|C|_*\), where \(|C|_*\) is the supremum
of \(|C_{XY}|_g\) over \(|X\wedge Y|_g=1\). The Weyl bound uses the
norm of \(W(X\wedge Y)\) as a two-form, which dominates the operator
norm of its skew action on covectors. The two Cotton terms together
have operator norm \(|C_{XY}|_g\) in the positive splitting.
Both tensors vanish on the core. Only the part of the filling
outside \(U\) with nonzero two-dimensional Jacobian can contribute.
[Curry–Gover, (32) and Proposition 4.1][cg]

If the area with multiplicity satisfies
\(\int_{F^{-1}(M\setminus U)} J_2^gF\leq A_*\)
for \(0<A_*<\infty\), then

\[
 \sup_{F([0,1]^2)\setminus U}
       \bigl(|W|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}+|C|_*\bigr)
 \ \geq\ \sup_{F([0,1]^2)\setminus U}|\Omega|_{\mathsf H_g,*}
 \ \geq\ \frac{\delta_\ell}{B^2 A_*}.
\]

If \(\ell\) and the basepoint norm are fixed and \(B,A_*\) have
uniform upper bounds, this is a uniform positive lower bound on
the stated curvature quantity along such fillings. The area and
transport hypotheses are additional geometric controls. The
two-dimensional integral includes Cotton curvature and imposes
no four-dimensional \(E_+\) threshold. The exact exclusion returns
to B15 as an obstruction to preserving the flat conformal core
at a global ASD endpoint. The quantitative estimate leaves the
four-dimensional energy and CGY criteria open.
