## 1. Fixed double, collar maps and common geometry

In the \(\ell=1\) one-centre metric the Hopf base radius is
\(r=|z|^2/2\) in smooth nut coordinates \(z\in\mathbb C^2\). Thus the
balls \(r\le R\) are standard coordinate balls, including at the nut.
Each boundary is marked by the same unit direction \(z/|z|\).
The two latitude caps of the standard sphere have precisely these
boundary direction markings and their complement is a product.
Joining the two coordinate balls by the marked cylinder therefore
gives the standard smooth sphere. The two core orientations, each
computed with its own outward normal, are opposite for the same
cylinder angular coordinates. The two copies supply opposite Weyl
chiralities. [E07 nut model][e07] · [E29 fixed double][e29] ·
[Cherkis–Hurtubise, §2.1, (1) and footnote 1][ch]

On a fixed model ball of radius one the E29 radial identification is
\(r\mapsto r[1+(R-1)\zeta(r)]\) with fixed smooth nondecreasing
\(\zeta\) vanishing near \([0,1/2]\) and equal to one near the boundary.
Its derivative is at least one for \(R\ge1\). The map is smooth
and identical near the nut, is radial with positive derivative,
and is the identity in the normalized collar coordinate \(u=r/R\)
on the image side of the seam. The unscaled metric is exactly one
fixed Taub–NUT germ on the same model inner ball for every \(R\)
and every interpolation parameter. The coefficients on the rest
of a fixed abstract ball may depend on this radial identification.
The core integrals use the isometry onto the actual Taub–NUT balls.
No uniform derivative estimate for the full radial identifications
is required. [E29 radial identifications][e29] · [E37 common double][e37]

For the scaled core write \(V_R(u)=1+(2Ru)^{-1}\) and
\(D_R(u)=\int_1^u\sqrt{V_R(v)}\,dv\). In the E34 neck coordinate
\(a\) its shell-distance profile is \(a+1\) near the left seam
and \(1-a\) near the right seam. In the common coordinate \(t\)
set \(u_-=1+t+T\), \(u_+=1+T-t\) near the respective seams.
Then the prescribed \(a_R\) in E37 has derivative
\(\sqrt{V_R(u_\pm)}\) at both ends, because the minus sign in the
right endpoint prescription cancels \(u_+'=-1\). The slice
coordinate is exactly \(D_R(u_\pm)\), so the pullback metric in
each end collar is precisely
\[
 V_R(u_\pm)(dt^2+u_\pm^2\pi^*g_{S^2})
       +R^{-2}V_R(u_\pm)^{-1}\eta^2 .
\]
It agrees with the negative join's end germ and with the core
continuation in \(u\) at the appropriate seam. The increasing axial
map and fixed angular marking preserve orientation. Viewed in the
same \(u\)-charts the maps on the two sides fit the identity germ,
so the pullbacks and metric matching agree to all orders.

The E37 derivative extension has well-defined positive bounds.
Choose fixed end widths \(\delta\) with \(\sqrt{3/2}\,\delta<1/4\)
and small enough that the integral of \(b_-d_-+b_+d_+\) is below
one. The products may be taken zero off their respective fixed
collar supports. In \(a_R'=b_-d_-+b_+d_++\gamma_R(1-b_--b_+)\),
the coefficient \(\gamma_R\) has a positive lower and a finite
upper bound depending only on the cylinder and cutoffs. All four
derivatives of \(a_R'\) are uniformly bounded. Its integral is two,
so the left and right endpoint germs have the prescribed endpoint
values as well as their derivatives. The positive derivative
bound gives a uniformly \(C^5\)-controlled inverse on the fixed
interval. Pulling back a covariant metric costs the first
derivative of the axial map and leaves four controlled metric
derivatives. [E34 end germs][e34] · [E35 common parameter][e35]

For the estimates write \(\eta=d\phi+\alpha\) on fixed relatively
compact base gauges, using the same sections of the same Hopf
fibration for both endpoints. In the lifted local coordinate
\(\zeta=\phi/R\), the fiber factor is
\(R^{-1}\eta=d\zeta+R^{-1}\alpha\). Both metrics have uniform
positive lower matrix bounds and uniform \(C^4\) bounds in these
coordinates, including the reparametrized axial coefficient of
the positive metric. The convex interpolation is of these full
matrices with a constant weight at each path parameter. Thus its
pointwise lower bound and all its coordinate derivative bounds
through order four hold uniformly. Inverse differentiation then
gives the same bounds needed to control \(\mathrm{Rm}\),
\(\nabla\mathrm{Rm}\) and \(\nabla^2\mathrm{Rm}\) in intrinsic norms.
The upstairs local determinant is uniformly bounded and the
fiber has quotient length \(2\pi/R\) in \(\zeta\). Finite base
integration and fixed axial length give volume \(O(R^{-1})\)
in the scaled neck. These local curvature estimates descend
because the lifts are local isometries. They do not assert a
uniform injectivity radius in the circle quotient. After the
normal cutoff and marking only the integrated estimates in E37
are used. [E29 scaled neck][e29] · [E34 positive bounds][e34] ·
[E35 negative bounds][e35]

The scaled neck thus contributes \(O(R^{-1})\) to each
\(E_\pm\) and \(\int |B|\). The Bach formula has two
derivatives of Schouten and a quadratic curvature term, so
Ricci-flatness makes it zero on the cores. For the contributing
core, E07's squared Weyl density and shell volume give
\(96\pi^2\int_0^{2R}x/(1+x)^5\,dx\). Integration gives
exactly E37 (5), with limit \(8\pi^2\) and error \(O(R^{-3})\).
This matches the complete core normalization in Atiyah–LeBrun.
The restoring homothety preserves both integrals. For general
smooth \(v>0\), \(B_{v^2g}=v^{-2}B_g\) as a covariant tensor, so
its norm scales by \(v^{-4}\). The two chiral Weyl
operator norms scale by \(v^{-2}\), so their squares and the
Bach norm density each cancel the volume factor \(v^4\) exactly.
Pullback invariance is by tensoriality and change of variables.
No uniform estimates on derivatives of the marking maps or
conformal multipliers enter these equalities.
[E07 integral][e07] · [Atiyah–LeBrun, §5, (5.11)][al] ·
[Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1, (1.1) and weight][bgim]

## 2. Both signs and the shared marking

For E34, the shape trace of the scaled Taub–NUT shells in outward
unit distance \(s\) is
\(H_s=V_R^{-1/2}(2/u-1/(4Ru^2V_R))>0\) on the range \(1\le u\le2\).
The connection-metric slice has scalar curvature \(2/a^2-c^2/(8a^4)>0\)
because the horizontal curvature correction is minus three
quarters of the vertical bracket norm squared and each mixed
sectional curvature contributes one quarter. Substitution of
\(\gamma_{\sigma(t)}\) into the normal Riccati and tangential
Gauss trace gives
\((1-\sigma'^2)\operatorname{Scal}(\gamma_\sigma)-2\sigma''H_\sigma\)
because the original Taub–NUT scalar curvature vanishes.
This is nonnegative with strict positivity on the plateau.
A connected closed metric with nonnegative scalar curvature and
positivity on an open set has strictly positive lowest conformal
Laplacian eigenvalue, by the compact Rayleigh minimizer and the
Dirichlet form. Compact Sobolev comparison then gives positive
Yamabe constant for each \(R\) using its fixed-metric spectral gap.
Pullback and constant scaling preserve the claimed sign.
[E34 sign proof][e34] · [Ebert–Frenck, Lemma 2.4][ef]

For E35, with \(\Delta_0=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{grad}\) on
the base sphere and \(v=k\Delta_0(u^2)\), the two-dimensional
curvature law and integration by parts give
\(\int(6|du|_h^2+s_hu^2)=C_0-2k\int(\Delta_0u^2)^2<0\)
for the chosen large fixed \(k\), with the appropriate base
volume in each integral. In the central product the circle
curvature contributes \(-|F|^2/(2R^2)\) to the scalar, and its
volume factor is \(2\pi/R\) after fiber integration. The axial
separated test has derivative-to-mass ratio of order \(L^{-2}\),
so the fixed choice of \(L\) makes its full numerator strictly
negative for every \(R\). Its denominator is positive and the
support lies in the reserved central product. This verifies
the required strictly negative Yamabe endpoint.
[E35 negative test][e35] · [Shankar–Tapp–Tuschmann, §3, constant-fiber curvature][stt]

For an arbitrary prescribed \(\psi:B_{1+d}\to S^4\), first
choose \(0<d'<d\). The radial flow in the original chart is a
global orientation-preserving diffeomorphism and maps
\(\psi(\overline B_{1+d'})\) into an arbitrarily small
neighborhood of \(\psi(0)\). A fixed orientation-preserving
round rotation sends that center to a chosen common core point.
Taking the compression small once places the indicated compact
set inside the common unscaled core. Pull this path back by the
one fixed placement map, so the marked center has the same
metric germ in the same coordinates for all \(R,b\). No assumption
on the topology of the complement of \(\psi(\overline B_1)\)
enters this construction. For clarity, run the *second*
E25 marking within \(\psi(B_{1+d'})\), using an inner radius
\(1+d''\) with \(0<d''<d'\). That makes the support separation
from the pulled-back neck explicit. This is an expositional
qualification of the marking paragraph of E37.
[E25 transition germ and radial compression][e25]

In common unscaled normal coordinates the metric error from
flat is \(q=O(|x|^2)\), \(Dq=O(|x|)\). The local flat replacement
has coordinate derivative bound \(O(\varepsilon^{2-j})\) for
\(0\le j\le4\) on \(B_{2\varepsilon}\), hence Riemann curvature
bounded and its second covariant derivative \(O(\varepsilon^{-2})\).
The volume there is \(O(\varepsilon^4)\). This gives the two
replacement costs \(O(\varepsilon^4)\) for each \(E_\pm\) and
\(O(\varepsilon^2)\) for \(\int|B|\), with constants fixed by
the shared germ. The cutoff leaves the unscaled positive
endpoint's scalar curvature unchanged off this patch and the
scalar density differs there by at most a fixed local bound.

For fixed \(R\), let \(Q,D\) be the positive endpoint numerator
and denominator. Its scalar curvature is nonnegative so \(Q\)
controls the Dirichlet integral. Uniformly over test functions,
\(|Q_\varepsilon-Q|\le a(Q+D)\) and \(D_\varepsilon\le(1+a)D\)
with \(a=C_R\varepsilon^2\). The metric and volume errors give
the Dirichlet comparison. Hölder on the region of volume
\(O(\varepsilon^4)\) gives the scalar term \(O(\varepsilon^2)D\).
Using \(Q\ge Y_hD\), this yields
\(Q_\varepsilon\ge[Y_h-a(1+Y_h)]D\) for \(a<1\). Thus one
chooses \(\varepsilon(R)\le\varepsilon_*/R\) also below the
fixed starting radius and small enough that
\(a(1+Y_h)\le Y_h/2\). This works even when the original
lowest eigenvalue or Yamabe constant is arbitrarily small as
\(R\) increases. The same radius works for the negative endpoint
because its separated test is supported in the untouched neck.
The same cutoff data, subsequent germ-matching diffeomorphism
and conformal multiplier can be used for all interpolation
parameters, as the replaced germ is shared. For fixed \(R\)
this gives a smooth parameter path. The two sign classes and
the energy and Bach bounds are preserved by the marking
invariance already checked. The resulting path retains uniform
\(O(R^{-1})\) Weyl and Bach integral errors.

[E34 quotient comparison][e34] · [E35 test support][e35]

## 3. Exact class zero, normalization and attainment

Fix \(R\). At any path parameter, shift the numerator by
\(K_0\|v\|_{L^2}^2\) for \(K_0\) exceeding the reference scalar
potential bound by one. This shifted form is coercive on the
common \(H^1\) domain. A sufficiently close path parameter has
a relatively close shifted form by metric, volume and scalar
continuity, and its \(L^2\) denominator is also relatively
close. Taking infima of the Rayleigh quotients after this
comparison proves continuity of \(\lambda_1+K_0\), hence of
\(\lambda_1\). The Yamabe minimizer is a positive smooth
unit-volume factor with \((6\Delta+s)u=Yu^3\) in the positive
Laplacian convention. Substitution \(v=u(v/u)\) gives
\(Q(v)=6\int u^2|d(v/u)|^2+Y\int u^2v^2\), by integration
by parts. This is coercively positive in \(L^2\) for \(Y>0\),
nonnegative with a zero test \(u\) for \(Y=0\), and has a
negative test \(u\) for \(Y<0\). The crossing therefore produces
an interior parameter of exact Yamabe zero and a smooth
unit-volume scalar-flat representative \(k_R\).
[E10 sign and wall][e10] · [Schoen, §3 Theorem 3][schoen] ·
[Lee–Parker, §2, (2.6)–(2.7)][lp]

For any oriented smooth closed homotopy four-sphere, the
Euler and signature data are \(\chi=2,\tau=0\). In the full
four-index convention the Euler integrand is
\(\tfrac14|W|^2-\tfrac12|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2+s^2/24\).
The full Weyl integral is four times the sum of the two
operator Hilbert–Schmidt energies. Signature gives their
equality. With \(s_k=0\) the rearrangement is exactly
\[
 \int|\operatorname{Ric}_k|^2,dV_k=4(E_+-8\pi^2).
\]
Thus equality is equivalent to a Ricci-flat representative.
Conversely a Ricci-flat representative has Yamabe constant
zero by the nonnegative Dirichlet form and a constant test.
On the standard sphere the construction supplies the upper
bound for the marked infimum, and therefore also for the
unmarked infimum. The same lower bound applies to both.
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107, Remark 2 and (0.4)][cgy] ·
[SH-E01 signature and norm accounting][sh]

In the marked class equality would make the marked metric
Bach flat by covariance from the Ricci-flat representative.
The round cap supplies an open set of full Weyl zero. The
four-dimensional obstruction tensor has the same zero
equation as Bach. Lassas–Liimatainen Theorem 1.4 applies on
the connected smooth boundaryless Riemannian manifold and
propagates full Weyl vanishing. This contradicts the equality
energy \(8\pi^2\). This argument uses E24 in its applicable
smooth marked case and can use the primary theorem directly
because both chiralities vanish on the round patch. It
establishes strict inequality on every admissible marked
metric on any homotopy sphere. Unmarked attainment on the
standard sphere is equivalent to existence of a smooth
Ricci-flat metric there. On an arbitrary homotopy sphere
existence of marked Yamabe-zero classes and sharpness of the
bound remain separate existence questions.
[E24 continuation][e24] · [Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorem 1.4][ll]

## 4. Limits and the cap-surviving implication

The invariant squared Weyl integrals and the invariant
total Bach norm pass exactly from \(g_R\) to \(k_R\). The scalar-flat
identity then gives \(\int|\operatorname{Ric}_{k_R}|^2\to0\),
while the Bach norm tends to zero by the uniform construction
bound. The marked representative has scalar curvature 12 on
the exact unit round cap. The scalar-flat representative is
conformally flat on that cap, which is the cap property used
for this representative. There is no stronger curvature
regularity claim for either sequence.

If same-manifold gauges have a uniformly positive strong
\(C^4\) limit, then the usual curvature expressions and the
uniform lower matrix bound give continuity of the Bach tensor
and its density. The vanishing total norm forces a zero Bach
tensor of the limit. A common nonempty open subset in the
pulled-back cap interiors supplies full Weyl vanishing there
by \(C^2\) continuity. E31's small-Bach variant applies with
that finite regularity. For the contradiction itself one needs
only its propagation step. A positive \(C^4\) metric lies in
\(C^{3,\beta}\) for \(0<\beta<1\), and the obstruction-flat
identity and full-Weyl open set meet the hypotheses of
Lassas–Liimatainen's continuation theorem. The limit is
Weyl-flat. Conformal pullback preserves the two equal chiral
energies, with an orientation reversal exchanging them. Their
limit under \(C^2\) convergence is therefore both zero and
\(8\pi^2\), a contradiction. The same argument for \(k_R\)
absorbs its conformal factor into the gauge. It rules out the
specified positive regular compactness with a surviving open
cap. It does not resolve a collapsed limit, a drifting cap,
or unmarked compactness. [E31 small-Bach variant][e31] ·
[Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorems 1.3(c), 1.4][ll]
