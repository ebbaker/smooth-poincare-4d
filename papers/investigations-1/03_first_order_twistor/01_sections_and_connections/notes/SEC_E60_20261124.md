Everything in this note concerns the linearization at the flat metric of \(\mathbb R^4\), except the corollary on the round sphere and the last section. No statement about the nonlinear Bach tensor is proved here.

## Results

1. **No free slowly varying mode.** Let \(h\) be smooth on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\) and homogeneous of degree \(\mu\) up to a polynomial in \(\log|x|\), with \(-2<\operatorname{Re}\mu<2\). If the linearized Bach tensor \(B'(h)\) vanishes on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\), then the linearized Weyl tensor \(W'(h)\) vanishes there (Theorem 3). The first exponents at which \(W'(h)\ne0\) is possible are \(\mu=\pm2\), with ten-dimensional families that are written explicitly.
2. **The logarithmic field is a cone.** For a constant symmetric trace-free \(H\), the field \(H\log|x|\) is not a solution: \(B'(H\log|x|)\ne0\). It differs by a Lie derivative of the flat metric from \(-s_H\), where \(s_H=(x\otimes Hx+Hx\otimes x)/(2|x|^2)\) is homogeneous of degree zero. The coefficient of the logarithm depends on the gauge: it is \(1\) for \(H\log|x|\), \(\tfrac59\) for the divergence-free trace-free representative, and \(0\) for \(-s_H\). Per unit of \(\log|x|\) the Weyl energy is \(\tfrac{20\pi^2}{9}|H|^2\) and the \(L^1\) Bach norm is between \(42.6\,|H|\) and \(44.3\,|H|\) in the cases computed.
3. **Bound by the \(L^1\) Bach norm.** For compactly supported \(h\),
\[
 \|h^{TT}\|_{L^\infty}\le\frac1{2\pi^2}\,\|B'(h)\|_{L^1},\qquad
 \int|W'(h)|^2dx\le\frac1{\pi^2}\,\|B'(h)\|_{L^1}^2,
\]
where \(h^{TT}\) is the divergence-free trace-free part (Theorem 5 and Corollary 6). The same energy inequality holds for linearized fields on the round four-sphere (Corollary 7).
4. **Limits.** The amplitude bound is attained up to a factor about four by the cones of item 2, for which both sides grow linearly in the length. The nonlinear version is not proved. The last section names the term that prevents it.

## Conventions and four facts

For a symmetric tensor field \(h\) on an open subset of \(\mathbb R^4\),
\[
 R'_{ijkl}=\tfrac12(\partial_j\partial_kh_{il}+\partial_i\partial_lh_{jk}-\partial_i\partial_kh_{jl}-\partial_j\partial_lh_{ik}),\qquad
 \operatorname{Ric}'_{jl}=R'_{ijil},\qquad A'=\tfrac12\bigl(\operatorname{Ric}'-\tfrac16R'\delta\bigr),
\]
\[
 W'_{ijkl}=R'_{ijkl}-(A'_{ik}\delta_{jl}+A'_{jl}\delta_{ik}-A'_{il}\delta_{jk}-A'_{jk}\delta_{il}),\qquad
 B'_{ij}=\partial_k\partial_lW'_{ikjl}=\Delta A'_{ij}-\partial_i\partial_j\operatorname{tr}A' .
\]
These are the linearizations at \(\delta\) of the tensors of E24 and of the manuscript. Norms are Euclidean, \(|W'|^2=W'_{ijkl}W'_{ijkl}\), and \(|h|\) is the Frobenius norm. Write \(\rho=|x|\) and \(\nu=x/\rho\).

- **F1.** \(W'\) and \(B'\) vanish on \(L_Z\delta+f\delta\) for every vector field \(Z\) and function \(f\). \(B'(h)\) is trace free and divergence free for every \(h\).
- **F2.** At a frequency \(\xi\ne0\) split \(\hat h=\hat h^{TT}+\hat\xi\otimes w+w\otimes\hat\xi+\tfrac13\varphi(\delta-\hat\xi\otimes\hat\xi)\) as in the proof of Corollary 2 of E59. Then \(\widehat{B'(h)}=-\tfrac14|\xi|^4\hat h^{TT}\) and \(|\widehat{W'(h)}|^2=\tfrac12|\xi|^4|\hat h^{TT}|^2\). On divergence-free trace-free fields, \(\operatorname{Ric}'=-\tfrac12\Delta h\), \(R'=0\) and \(B'=-\tfrac14\Delta^2h\).
- **F3.** \(\Delta^2W'(h)=-4\,W'(B'(h))\) for every distribution \(h\). Both sides are constant-coefficient operators of order six. Their symbols agree on \(\hat h^{TT}\) by F2, and both vanish on the other parts of \(\hat h\) by F1; the symbol of \(B'(h)\) is itself divergence free and trace free, so \(W'\) sees all of it.
- **F4.** \(\int|W'(h)|^2dx=-2\int\langle B'(h),h\rangle dx\) for compactly supported or Schwartz \(h\). This is F2 and Plancherel's theorem. It is also the second variation of the Weyl functional at the flat metric, since the gradient is \(-4B\).

F1, F3 and F4 are confirmed in exact rational arithmetic on random polynomial and Gaussian tensors (checks 4 and 6 below).

## The logarithmic field and the cones \(\delta+c\,s_H\)

Let \(H\) be a constant symmetric trace-free matrix and \(H_{\nu\nu}=H(\nu,\nu)\).

**Proposition 1.** On \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\),
\[
 \operatorname{Ric}'(H\log\rho)=-\rho^{-2}(\nu\otimes H\nu+H\nu\otimes\nu),\qquad R'(H\log\rho)=-2\rho^{-2}H_{\nu\nu},
\]
\[
 B'(H\log\rho)=\rho^{-4}b_H(\nu),\qquad
 b_H=-\tfrac43H+\tfrac43(\nu\otimes H\nu+H\nu\otimes\nu)-\tfrac83H_{\nu\nu}\delta+8H_{\nu\nu}\,\nu\otimes\nu ,
\]
\[
 \rho^4|W'(H\log\rho)|^2=2|H|^2-4|H\nu|^2+\tfrac43H_{\nu\nu}^2 .
\]
Moreover \(b_H\nu=\tfrac{20}3H_{\nu\nu}\nu\), and the means over the unit sphere are
\[
 \oint_{S^3}b_H\,d\sigma=0,\qquad \oint_{S^3}b_H\nu\otimes\nu\,d\sigma=\tfrac59\cdot2\pi^2\,H,\qquad
 \kappa_1:=\oint_{S^3}\rho^4|W'(H\log\rho)|^2d\sigma=\tfrac{20\pi^2}9|H|^2 .
\]

Proof. \(\partial_a\partial_b\log\rho=\rho^{-2}P_{ab}\) with \(P=\delta-2\nu\otimes\nu\), so \(R'=-\tfrac12\rho^{-2}\,P\owedge H\), where \((a\owedge b)_{ijkl}=a_{ik}b_{jl}+a_{jl}b_{ik}-a_{il}b_{jk}-a_{jk}b_{il}\). The product \(\delta\owedge H\) has no Weyl part, so \(W'=\rho^{-2}\,\mathrm{Weyl}((\nu\otimes\nu)\owedge H)\). For \(T=a\owedge b\) one has \(|T|^2=4|a|^2|b|^2+4\langle a,b\rangle^2-8\operatorname{tr}(a^2b^2)\) and \(\operatorname{Ric}(T)=(\operatorname{tr}a)b+(\operatorname{tr}b)a-ab-ba\). With \(a=\nu\otimes\nu\) and \(b=H\): \(|T|^2=4|H|^2+4H_{\nu\nu}^2-8|H\nu|^2\), \(|\operatorname{Ric}(T)|^2=|H|^2-2|H\nu|^2+2H_{\nu\nu}^2\), and the scalar is \(-2H_{\nu\nu}\). The Weyl part has \(|W|^2=|T|^2-2|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2-\tfrac16R^2\), which gives the third formula. The spherical means of \(|H\nu|^2\) and \(H_{\nu\nu}^2\) are \(\tfrac14|H|^2\) and \(\tfrac1{12}|H|^2\), so the mean of the density is \((2-1+\tfrac19)|H|^2=\tfrac{10}9|H|^2\) and \(\kappa_1=2\pi^2\cdot\tfrac{10}9|H|^2\). The Ricci formulas follow by contraction. The formula for \(b_H\) is \(\Delta A'-\partial\partial\operatorname{tr}A'\) for \(A'=\tfrac12(\operatorname{Ric}'-\tfrac16R'\delta)\). It was obtained by differentiation and is confirmed as an identity of rational functions in check 1, together with \(b_H\nu=\tfrac{20}3H_{\nu\nu}\nu\). The means of \(\nu\otimes H\nu\), \(H_{\nu\nu}\) and \(H_{\nu\nu}\nu\otimes\nu\) are \(\tfrac14H\), \(0\) and \(\tfrac1{12}H\), which gives the two means of \(b_H\). \(\square\)

**Proposition 2 (three representatives of one class).** With \(Z=\tfrac12(\log\rho)Hx\),
\[
 H\log\rho+s_H=L_Z\delta .
\]
Hence \(W'(s_H)=-W'(H\log\rho)\) and \(B'(s_H)=-B'(H\log\rho)\). The field
\[
 h^{TT}_H=\tfrac59H\log\rho-\tfrac7{18}s_H-\tfrac1{18}H_{\nu\nu}\,\nu\otimes\nu+\tfrac19H_{\nu\nu}\,\delta
\]
is divergence free and trace free and differs from \(H\log\rho\) by \(L_Y\delta+f\delta\), with \(Y=-\tfrac29(\log\rho)Hx+\tfrac1{72}H_{\nu\nu}x\) and \(f=\tfrac1{12}H_{\nu\nu}\). Constant multiples of \(H\) may be added to it.

Proof. \(\partial_iZ_j=\tfrac12\rho^{-2}x_i(Hx)_j+\tfrac12(\log\rho)H_{ij}\), which gives the first identity. For the second, \(L_{(\log\rho)Hx}\delta=2H\log\rho+2s_H\) and \(L_{H_{\nu\nu}x}\delta=4s_H-4H_{\nu\nu}\nu\otimes\nu+2H_{\nu\nu}\delta\). The divergences are \(\operatorname{div}(H\log\rho)=\rho^{-1}H\nu\), \(\operatorname{div}s_H=\rho^{-1}(2H\nu-H_{\nu\nu}\nu)\), \(\operatorname{div}(H_{\nu\nu}\nu\otimes\nu)=3\rho^{-1}H_{\nu\nu}\nu\) and \(\operatorname{div}(H_{\nu\nu}\delta)=2\rho^{-1}(H\nu-H_{\nu\nu}\nu)\). Writing the class as \(H\log\rho+a_1L_{(\log\rho)Hx}\delta+a_3L_{H_{\nu\nu}x}\delta+a_4H_{\nu\nu}\delta\), the trace and the two components of the divergence vanish exactly when \(2a_1+8a_3+4a_4=0\), \(1+6a_1+12a_3+2a_4=0\) and \(2a_1+20a_3+2a_4=0\), that is \(a_1=-\tfrac29\), \(a_3=\tfrac1{72}\), \(a_4=\tfrac1{12}\). Check 2 confirms both statements exactly. \(\square\)

Consequences.

- \(\delta+c\,s_H\) has Cartesian components homogeneous of degree zero, so it is a cone, and for small \(c\) it is the metric that the logarithmic field \(-cH\log\rho\) describes in a different chart. The chart map is \(x\mapsto\exp(\tfrac12c\log|x|\,H)x\) to first order in \(c\). The deviation of the cone from \(\delta\) is \(c\sup|s_H|=c\,\|H\|_{\rm op}\), uniformly along the neck.
- In the chart where the perturbation is divergence free and trace free, the same cone appears with the logarithm \(\tfrac59cH\log\rho\). The divergence-free trace-free gauge is therefore a poor gauge on a long conical neck: its amplitude grows linearly in the length although the metric is as close to flat as \(c\) in its own chart. Theorem 1 of E59 makes no gauge assumption and applies in the cone's chart.
- For the cone, the leading-order densities of E59 are \(e_2=\kappa_1=\tfrac{20\pi^2}9|H|^2\) and \(b_1=\kappa_2(H):=\oint_{S^3}|b_H|d\sigma\). The pairing that proves E59 is exact at this order: \(\langle b_H,s_H\rangle=b_H(\nu,H\nu)=\tfrac{20}3H_{\nu\nu}^2\), whose mean is \(\tfrac59|H|^2\), so \(2\oint\langle b_H,s_H\rangle d\sigma=\kappa_1\). ## Homogeneous solutions

**Theorem 3.** Let \(\mu\in\mathbb C\) with \(-2<\operatorname{Re}\mu<2\), and let
\[
 h=\rho^\mu\sum_{m=0}^N(\log\rho)^mh_m(\nu)
\]
with \(h_m\) smooth on \(S^3\) with values in symmetric matrices. If \(B'(h)=0\) on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\), then \(W'(h)=0\) on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\).

Proof. Since \(\operatorname{Re}\mu>-4\), \(h\) is locally integrable and tempered on \(\mathbb R^4\). Let \(T=B'(h)\) as a distribution on \(\mathbb R^4\). It is supported at the origin, so \(T=\sum_{|\alpha|\le M}c_\alpha\partial^\alpha\delta_0\) with constant symmetric matrices \(c_\alpha\).

*Scaling.* For \(\lambda>0\), \(h(\lambda x)=\lambda^\mu\sum_{j=0}^N(\log\lambda)^jh^{(j)}(x)\) with fields \(h^{(j)}\) of the same form and \(h^{(0)}=h\). Since \(B'\) is homogeneous of order four and \((\partial^\alpha\delta_0)(\lambda x)=\lambda^{-4-|\alpha|}\partial^\alpha\delta_0\),
\[
 \sum_\alpha c_\alpha\lambda^{-|\alpha|}\partial^\alpha\delta_0=\lambda^\mu\sum_j(\log\lambda)^jB'(h^{(j)})\qquad\text{for all }\lambda>0 .
\]
The functions \(\lambda^{-k}\), \(k=0,1,\dots\), and \(\lambda^\mu(\log\lambda)^j\) are linearly independent unless \(\mu=-k\) and \(j=0\). So \(T=0\) unless \(\mu\) is a nonpositive integer, and then only the terms with \(|\alpha|=-\mu\) remain. In the strip this leaves \(\mu=0\) and \(\mu=-1\).

*Divergence.* \(B'\) maps into divergence-free fields identically, so \(\partial_iT_{ij}=0\). For \(\mu=0\), \(T=S\delta_0\) and \(S_{ij}\partial_i\delta_0=0\) gives \(S=0\). For \(\mu=-1\), \(T=S^a\partial_a\delta_0\) and \(S^a_{ij}\partial_i\partial_a\delta_0=0\) gives \(S^a_{ij}+S^i_{aj}=0\); with the symmetry in \(ij\) this is \(S^a_{ij}=-S^i_{aj}=-S^i_{ja}=S^j_{ia}=S^j_{ai}=-S^a_{ji}=-S^a_{ij}\), so \(S=0\). Hence \(B'(h)=0\) on \(\mathbb R^4\).

*Conclusion.* By F3, \(\Delta^2W'(h)=0\) on \(\mathbb R^4\). \(W'(h)\) is tempered, so its Fourier transform is supported at the origin and \(W'(h)\) is a polynomial. On \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\) it equals a smooth function bounded by \(C\rho^{\operatorname{Re}\mu-2}(1+|\log\rho|)^N\), which tends to zero at infinity. The polynomial is zero. \(\square\)

The same argument on the lines \(\operatorname{Re}\mu=\pm2\) leaves only \(\mu=\pm2\).

**Proposition 4 (the exponents \(\pm2\)).** Let \(\mathsf W\) be a constant tensor with the symmetries of a Weyl tensor.

- \(q_{ij}=-\tfrac13\mathsf W_{ikjl}x_kx_l\) is divergence free and trace free, and \(R'[q]=\mathsf W\). So \(W'(q)=\mathsf W\) and \(B'(q)=0\).
- \(p_{ij}=\mathsf W_{ikjl}x_kx_l\rho^{-4}=-\tfrac12\mathsf W_{ikjl}\partial_k\partial_l\log\rho\) is divergence free and trace free, \(B'(p)=0\) on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\), \(W'(p)\ne0\), and on \(\mathbb R^4\)
\[
 B'(-\pi^{-2}p)=\mathsf W_{ikjl}\,\partial_k\partial_l\delta_0 .
\]

Proof. The first item is a direct computation with the Bianchi identity. For the second, \(x_kx_l\rho^{-4}=\tfrac12(\delta_{kl}\rho^{-2}-\partial_k\partial_l\log\rho)\) and \(\mathsf W\) is trace free in \(kl\). The divergence \(-\tfrac12\mathsf W_{ikjl}\partial_i\partial_k\partial_l\log\rho\) vanishes by antisymmetry in \(ik\), also as a distribution. In \(\mathbb R^4\), \(\Delta\log\rho=2\rho^{-2}\) and \(\Delta\rho^{-2}=-4\pi^2\delta_0\), so \(\Delta^2\log\rho=-8\pi^2\delta_0\), and F2 gives \(B'(p)=-\tfrac14\Delta^2p=-\pi^2\mathsf W_{ikjl}\partial_k\partial_l\delta_0\). \(\square\)

The inversion \(x\mapsto x/\rho^2\), with the conformal weight of a metric perturbation, exchanges the two families. Check 5 confirms the two items exactly, including the constant \(\pi^{-2}\) in weak form against Gaussian test tensors. The table of kernel dimensions (check 9) finds, in the ranges it covers, no solution with \(W'\ne0\) at \(\mu\in\{-1,0,1\}\) and exactly ten at each of \(\mu=\pm2\).

*Reading.* For the quantity \(W'\), which does not see the gauge, the linearized Bach equation on the cone has no exponent in the open strip \(|\operatorname{Re}\mu|<2\). At \(\mu=\pm2\) the density \(\rho^4|W'|^2\) is proportional to \(\rho^{\pm4}\). The expected consequence is that the Weyl energy per unit of \(\log\rho\) of an unforced solution on a long annulus decays like \(e^{-4d}\) in the logarithmic distance \(d\) to the nearer end. That statement needs the completeness of the homogeneous solutions modulo the kernel of \(W'\), which is not proved here. The term \(\varepsilon^2\) of Corollary 2 of E59 is the nonlinear trace of these end modes.

## The bound by the \(L^1\) norm of the linearized Bach tensor

**Theorem 5.** Let \(\beta\) be a smooth compactly supported field of symmetric matrices on \(\mathbb R^4\) with \(\partial_i\beta_{ij}=0\). Then for every \(x\),
\[
 \frac1{2\pi^2}\int\log|x-y|\,\beta_{ij}(y)\,dy=-\frac1{2\pi^2}\int\beta_{ik}(y)\,\hat w_k\hat w_j\,dy,\qquad \hat w=\frac{y-x}{|y-x|},
\]
and the absolute value of the left side, as a matrix, is at most \((2\pi^2)^{-1}\|\beta\|_{L^1}\).

Proof. Fix \(x\) and \(j\) and let \(\psi(y)=(y-x)_j\log|y-x|\). Then \(\partial_k\psi=\delta_{jk}\log|y-x|+\hat w_j\hat w_k\), and \(\psi\) and its gradient are locally integrable. Since \(\beta\) is divergence free, \(\int\beta_{ik}\partial_k\psi\,dy=0\), which is the identity. The bound follows from \(|(\beta\hat w)\otimes\hat w|\le|\beta|\). \(\square\)

The logarithm is unbounded, and convolution with it does not map \(L^1\) to \(L^\infty\). The identity replaces the logarithm by a bounded kernel at the cost of the divergence-free condition. This is the mechanism of the estimates of Bourgain–Brezis and Van Schaftingen for divergence-free \(L^1\) fields. The bound that follows is of the type of the estimate \(\sup|D^{k-n}u|\le C\|A(D)u\|_{L^1}\) of Bousquet–Van Schaftingen (their Theorem 3) for elliptic canceling operators of order \(k\ge n\): the symbol of \(B'\) has as image the divergence-free trace-free matrices at each frequency, and these subspaces intersect in zero. \(B'\) is not elliptic on all symmetric tensors, so that theorem is not applied here; the proof is direct and gives the constant. The sources were read at the level stated in the source record, and nothing is imported from them.

**Corollary 6.** Let \(h\) be smooth, symmetric and compactly supported on \(\mathbb R^4\), and let \(h^{TT}\) be its divergence-free trace-free part, defined by the splitting of F2. Then \(h^{TT}=(2\pi^2)^{-1}\log|\cdot|*B'(h)\), and
\[
 \|h^{TT}\|_{L^\infty}\le\frac{1}{2\pi^2}\|B'(h)\|_{L^1},\qquad
 \int|W'(h)|^2dx\le\frac1{\pi^2}\|B'(h)\|_{L^1}^2,\qquad
 \int|W'^{\pm}(h)|^2dx\le\frac1{8\pi^2}\|B'(h)\|_{L^1}^2 ,
\]
the last in the Hilbert–Schmidt norms of the manuscript.

Proof. Let \(\beta=B'(h)\) and \(u=(2\pi^2)^{-1}\log|\cdot|*\beta\). Since \(\Delta^2\log\rho=-8\pi^2\delta_0\), \(\Delta^2u=-4\beta\), and \(\Delta^2h^{TT}=-4\beta\) by F2. So \(u-h^{TT}\) is a tempered biharmonic field, hence a polynomial. Both terms tend to zero at infinity: \(h^{TT}\) because its Fourier transform is integrable, and \(u\) by Theorem 5, since \(\hat w\to-x/|x|\) uniformly on the support of \(\beta\) and \(\int\beta\,dy=0\). So \(u=h^{TT}\), and Theorem 5 gives the first inequality. By F4 and F2, \(\int|W'(h)|^2=-2\int\langle\beta,h^{TT}\rangle\le2\|\beta\|_{L^1}\|h^{TT}\|_{L^\infty}\). For the last inequality, \(|W'|^2=4(|W'^+|^2+|W'^-|^2)\), and the two chiral energies are equal: \(\int(|W^+|^2-|W^-|^2)dV\) is unchanged by compactly supported changes of the metric, and its second derivative at the flat metric is \(2\int(|W'^+|^2-|W'^-|^2)dx\). \(\square\)

*Sharpness.* For \(\beta=c\,\rho^{-4}b_H\) on a long annulus, the right side of the identity of Theorem 5 grows by \(-(2\pi^2)^{-1}c\oint b_H\nu\otimes\nu\,d\sigma=-\tfrac59cH\) per unit of \(\log\rho\) between the point \(x\) and the outer end, in agreement with the coefficient \(\tfrac59\) of Proposition 2. The bound grows by \((2\pi^2)^{-1}c\,\kappa_2\approx2.2\,c|H|\) per unit. So the amplitude bound is attained up to a factor about four along cones, and it cannot be improved to a bound by the Bach norm of a bounded part of the neck. The energy inequality is not attained on cones: there the energy is linear in the length and the right side is quadratic.

**Corollary 7 (round sphere).** Let \(g\) be the round metric on \(S^4\) and \(h\) a smooth symmetric tensor field. Then
\[
 \int_{S^4}|W'_g[h]|_g^2\,dV_g\le\frac1{\pi^2}\Bigl(\int_{S^4}|B'_g[h]|_g\,dV_g\Bigr)^2 .
\]
Consequently, along the ray \(g+sh\) with \(W'_g[h]\ne0\), \(\ \liminf_{s\to0}\mathfrak B(g+sh)^2/E_+(g+sh)\ge8\pi^2\).

Proof. Fix \(p\in S^4\). Subtracting \(L_Xg\) for a vector field \(X\) with a suitable two-jet at \(p\) changes neither linearization, because \(W_g=0\) and \(B_g=0\), and achieves \(h(p)=0\) and \(Dh(p)=0\): in normal coordinates the first derivatives \(t_{kij}=\partial_kh_{ij}(p)\) are matched by \(\partial_i\partial_kX_j=\tfrac12(t_{kij}+t_{ikj}-t_{jik})\). Then \(|h|\le Cd^2\) and \(|Dh|\le Cd\) near \(p\), with \(d\) the distance to \(p\). Let \(\chi_\theta\) be a cutoff equal to one on the ball of radius \(\theta/2\) about \(p\), supported in the ball of radius \(\theta\), with \(|D^a\chi_\theta|\le C\theta^{-a}\), and \(h_\theta=(1-\chi_\theta)h\). The commutator terms of \(B'_g\) carry \(a\ge1\) derivatives of the cutoff and \(b\le4-a\) derivatives of \(h\) on a ball of volume \(C\theta^4\), and are at most \(C\theta^{4-a+\max(2-b,0)}\le C\theta^2\) in \(L^1\). Those of \(W'_g\) are bounded pointwise, hence \(O(\theta^2)\) in \(L^2\). So both sides for \(h_\theta\) converge to those for \(h\). In stereographic coordinates from \(p\), \(g=u^2\delta\) and \(h_\theta=u^2\tilde h\) with \(\tilde h\) smooth and compactly supported. From \(W_{u^2\hat g}=u^2W_{\hat g}\) and \(B_{u^2\hat g}=u^{-2}B_{\hat g}\), linearized at \(\hat g=\delta\), \(W'_g[u^2\tilde h]=u^2W'_\delta[\tilde h]\) and \(B'_g[u^2\tilde h]=u^{-2}B'_\delta[\tilde h]\), and both integrals are unchanged. Corollary 6 applies to \(\tilde h\). For the last statement, \(E_+(g+sh)=s^2\int|W'^+_g[h]|^2dV_g+O(s^3)\) with equal chiral parts, and \(\mathfrak B(g+sh)=s\|B'_g[h]\|_{L^1}+O(s^2)\). \(\square\)

The corollary is a statement about rays through the round metric. It says nothing about the band of energies of the fixed-margin problem.

## What the linear results say about a neck, and what stops the nonlinear argument

The linear picture is the following. An unforced solution carries no Weyl energy in the middle of a long annulus: there is no exponent in the strip. A forced solution can carry energy along the whole annulus, as the cones do, and then the forcing is spread along the whole annulus too. In the divergence-free trace-free gauge the amplitude is at most the total forcing divided by \(2\pi^2\), and the energy is at most the square of the total forcing divided by \(\pi^2\). The logarithm of B34 does occur in that gauge, with a coefficient tied to the forcing, and it does not occur in the cone's own chart.

For the nonlinear Bach tensor in a chart \(g=\delta+v\), write \(B_g=B'(v)+N(v)\). After a cutoff, Theorem 5 would need the \(L^1\) norm of the divergence-free field \(\eta B_g-\eta N(v)+[B',\eta]v\). The total Bach norm controls the first term. The remainder contains \(v*D^4v\) and \(Dv*D^3v\), which are not bounded in \(L^1\) by the energy. Integrating by parts onto the kernel leaves
\[
 \int\frac{|v|\,|D^2v|+|Dv|^2}{|x-y|^{2}}\,dy ,
\]
and \(|x-y|^{-2}\) lies in weak \(L^2\) of \(\mathbb R^4\), so this is bounded by \(\varepsilon\) times the Lorentz \(L^{2,1}\) norm of \(D^2v\) and not by its \(L^2\) norm. On a neck the integral is the \(\ell^1\) sum over dyadic annuli that E59 identified. The splitting also loses the cancellation: \(\eta B_g\) alone is not divergence free for the flat metric, since its flat divergence is of the form \(Dv*B_g\), and the logarithmic potential of an \(L^1\) field that is not divergence free is not bounded. The term that prevents the nonlinear amplitude estimate is therefore the quasilinear top-order part of \(N(v)\), together with the failure of the flat divergence-free condition for \(B_g\) by a term \(Dv*B_g\) that is not integrable under the available hypotheses.

If an estimate \(\sup|v|\le C(\mathfrak B+\text{end data})\) were available in a divergence-free trace-free chart on a neck, Corollary 2 of E59 would give a neck energy of at most \(C(\mathfrak B+\text{end data})^2\). This is a conditional statement.

A lead for the next task. At the linear level the Lorentz exponent is available. Theorem 1.1 of Hernandez–Spector states \(\|I_\alpha F\|_{L^{d/(d-\alpha),1}}\le C\|F\|_{L^1}\) for divergence-free \(F\in L^1(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)\), \(d\ge2\), \(0<\alpha<d\). The rows of \(B'(h)\) are such fields. With \(d=4\), \(\alpha=2\), \(D^2h^{TT}=-4D^2\Delta^{-2}B'(h)\), and the boundedness of Riesz transforms on \(L^{2,1}\), this gives \(\|D^2h^{TT}\|_{L^{2,1}}\le C\|B'(h)\|_{L^1}\). The theorem was read at the level of its statement and this deduction has not been checked against the paper beyond that. It shows that the linear theory lives in the space \(W^{2,(2,1)}\), which embeds in the continuous functions and is stable under the products \(v*D^2v\). What the nonlinear problem lacks is a version of the divergence-free condition for \(B_g\) that survives the change from \(\delta\) to \(g\). The identity \(\operatorname{div}_g(B(X,\cdot)^\sharp)=\tfrac12\langle B,L_Xg\rangle\) of [E61](SEC_E61_20261124.md) is the candidate.
