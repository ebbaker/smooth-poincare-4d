## Statement

Let \(M\) range over smooth closed connected oriented homotopy
four-spheres and use smooth positive-definite Riemannian metrics.
Write \(Y(M,[g])\) for the conformal-class Yamabe constant and
\[
 E_\pm(g)=\int_M |W_g^\pm|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\,dV_g .
\]
The blocks have equal energies by the signature formula. In the
full four-index norm the Weyl integral is \(8E_+(g)\). Define
\[
 \mathcal A=\{\, E_+(g)\in(0,8\pi^2):
           B_g=0,\quad Y(M,[g])>0,\quad M\text{ as above}\,\},
 \qquad a=\inf\mathcal A .
\tag{1}
\]
If \(\mathcal A\ne\varnothing\), then \(a\in\mathcal A\) and
\(4\pi^2<a<8\pi^2\). In unit-volume Yamabe minimizer gauges any
sequence in this class with \(E_+(g_i)\to a\) has a subsequence
converging smoothly via orientation-preserving diffeomorphisms
to such an attaining metric. In particular, the endpoint
\(a=4\pi^2\) is impossible. For the empty set we use \(a=+\infty\).

The sharp closed test of [E54][e54] gives the pointwise lower bound
\(E_+(g)>4\pi^2\) in (1). The inputs are Chang–Gursky–Yang
Theorems A and B, including the equality alternatives, and
Chang–Chen Theorem 2.1 on the standard smooth sphere. In this norm
their Bach-flat roundness threshold \(32\pi^2\) for the full
four-index Weyl integral is \(4\pi^2\) per block. The smooth
identification required by Chang–Chen, including at equality,
is part of E54. This yields \(a\ge4\pi^2\) if the class is
nonempty, with strictness at the infimum still to prove.
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorems A and B, pp. 107–108][cgy] ·
[Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1, p. 385][cc]

## Normalized minimizing sequence

Assume \(\mathcal A\ne\varnothing\). Since any member lies below
\(8\pi^2\), we have \(a<8\pi^2\). Choose a sequence \((M_i,g_i)\)
representing values \(E_i=E_\pm(g_i)\to a\), and discard a finite
prefix so that \(E_i\le8\pi^2-\delta\) for a single \(\delta>0\).
Choose a smooth unit-volume Yamabe **minimizer** in each class.
This uses existence in the smooth closed Yamabe problem. The
four-dimensional Bach equation and both Weyl integrals are
conformally invariant in the required sense. Consequently the
new metrics remain exact Bach-flat and have the same \(E_i\).
In these gauges \(R_i=Y_i=Y(M_i,[g_i])\) and
\[
 Y_i\|f\|_{L^4(g_i)}^2
   \le 6\|\nabla f\|_{L^2(g_i)}^2+Y_i\|f\|_{L^2(g_i)}^2
\tag{2}
\]
for all smooth \(f\) and by density for \(H^1\) tests. The
minimizer property supplies the inequality with \(R_i=Y_i\).
[Lee–Parker, Theorems 3.2–3.3 and §7][lp] ·
[Chang–Qing–Yang, Lemma 2.1, (2.1)–(2.2)][cqy]

Write \(T_i=\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}-\tfrac14R_i g_i\),
\(Y_{\rm rd}=8\sqrt6\,\pi\) and \(Y_*=\sqrt{48\delta}\).
Chern–Gauss–Bonnet with \(\chi(M_i)=2\) and the sharp
Yamabe upper bound give
\[
 \begin{split}
  Y_i^2 &= Y_{\rm rd}^2-48E_i+12\|T_i\|_2^2,\\
  Y_* &\le Y_i\le Y_{\rm rd},\qquad \|T_i\|_2^2\le4E_i,\\
  \int_{M_i}|{\rm Rm}_{g_i}|^2_{\text{four-index}}\,dV_{g_i}
      &= 8E_i+2\|T_i\|_2^2+Y_i^2/6 .
 \end{split}
\tag{3}
\]
Thus the Yamabe constants have a uniform positive lower bound,
the full curvature integrals have a uniform upper bound, and
\(b_1(M_i)=0\) for every \(i\). After a subsequence \(Y_i\to
y\ge Y_*\). Taking square roots in (2) gives a uniform
Sobolev constant in the precise normalization
\[
 \|f\|_4
 \le \sqrt{6/Y_*}\|\nabla f\|_2+
                \operatorname{Vol}^{-1/4}\|f\|_2 .
\tag{4}
\]
The coefficient on the volume term in (4) is one. The
inequality holds at unit volume and after any constant
metric rescaling, with that metric's volume.
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, (0.4)][cgy] ·
[Tian–Viaclovsky, (1.2) and §3.1][tv]

### Uniform diameter and noncollapse

Put \(V_x(r)=\operatorname{Vol}_{g_i}(B_r(x))\). Use a Lipschitz
cutoff in (2) equal to one on \(B_{r/2}(x)\), supported in
\(\overline{B_r(x)}\) and with slope at most \(2/r\).
For \(0<r\le1\),
\[
 Y_* V_x(r/2)^{1/2}\le (24+Y_{\rm rd})r^{-2}V_x(r).
\]
Hence \(U_x(r)=V_x(r)/r^4\) satisfies
\(U_x(r)\ge c\,U_x(r/2)^{1/2}\), where
\(c=Y_*/[4(24+Y_{\rm rd})]\). Iteration gives
\(U_x(r)\ge c^{2(1-2^{-n})}U_x(r/2^n)^{2^{-n}}\).
For each fixed \(i,x\) the final factor tends to one by smooth
small-ball asymptotics. Therefore
\[
 V_x(r)\ge c^2r^4\qquad(0<r\le1)
\tag{5}
\]
uniformly for all \(i,x\). Along a diameter-realizing minimizing
geodesic, take centers at spacing \(2r_0\) for \(r_0=1/4\).
The open \(r_0\)-balls are pairwise disjoint. Equation (5) and
unit volume bound their number and hence the diameter independently
of \(i\). For the metric \(A_i g_i\), (5) also holds for
\(r\le \sqrt{A_i}\), by the length and volume rescaling laws.
This uses a small-ball bound on the closed unit-volume sequence
and a rescaled bound on each fixed radius.

## Exact compactness alternative

Chang–Qing–Yang §4, equations (4.1)–(4.4), starts with closed
smooth four-manifolds, exact Bach-flatness, a uniform positive
**conformal-class** Yamabe constant, \(b_1(M_i)=0\) and a uniform
full curvature integral. Equations (4.1)–(4.4) are satisfied
by (2)–(3). Their Yamabe-gauge regularity and neck inputs are
Lemma 2.1, Theorem 2.4 and Theorem 3.1. The first concentration
scale in (4.5)–(4.6) is the smallest radius carrying a fixed
small curvature integral. When this radius shrinks, their first
smooth limit is a complete nonflat scalar-flat Bach-flat leaf
with finite total curvature and a single ALE end (Definition
4.1). A uniform lower bound on the relevant small-energy radius
gives uniform local curvature estimates. Their separate compact
orbifold statement in Theorem 5.1 fixes another closed volume
normalization, attainable by a fixed rescaling. For the
present argument the necessary convergence includes actual smooth
embeddings of the entire compact truncation of a leaf.

Here is a maximum-curvature implementation of the same alternative.
It makes the complete core embeddings in the concentrating case and
the global diffeomorphisms in the bounded case explicit.
[Chang–Qing–Yang, §2, §3, §4 and Theorem 5.1][cqy]

Whenever the sequence under discussion has uniformly bounded
pointwise curvature and either the original scale or a rescaling
\(A_i\ge1\), (5) and the sectional-curvature bound give a
uniform injectivity radius lower bound. Bishop–Gromov comparison
from the Ricci lower bound gives a uniform \(O(r^4)\) upper
bound for ball volumes at small fixed radius. It also gives a
uniform positive diameter lower bound since the total volume is
at least one. For all centers, choose a radius less than half
that lower bound and small enough to make the \(L^2\) curvature
in the ball uniformly small. Equation (4) and Tian–Viaclovsky
Theorem 4.1 then give bounds for all covariant curvature
derivatives after shrinking the ball. Their §2.1, (2.4)–(2.5),
records the Ricci elliptic equation for constant-scalar-curvature
Bach-flat metrics, to which the estimates apply. These bounds and
the injectivity-radius bound give smooth pointed Cheeger–Gromov
convergence to a complete smooth manifold on a subsequence.
We orient the connected limit so the comparison embeddings
preserve orientation after passing to a subsequence.
[Cheeger–Gromov–Taylor, Theorem 4.7][cgt] ·
[Tian–Viaclovsky, §2.1 and Theorem 4.1][tv] ·
[Andrews–Hopper, §8.2–8.3 and Theorem 8.8][ah]

If \(A_i=\max_{M_i}|{\rm Rm}_{g_i}|\to\infty\), take a
maximizing point \(x_i\) and consider \(\tilde g_i=A_i g_i\).
The length scale is \(A_i^{-1/2}\), the maximum curvature
is one and \(R_{\tilde g_i}=Y_i/A_i\to0\). The smooth pointed
limit \((X,h,x)\) is connected, complete, scalar-flat, exact
Bach-flat and has \(|{\rm Rm}_h(x)|=1\). It has finite \(L^2\)
full curvature by (3) and smooth convergence on compact sets.
The rescaled volumes \(A_i^2\to\infty\) force it to be noncompact.
Indeed a compact smooth limit would embed as a whole into
\(M_i\) and, by openness and closedness, exhaust each connected
\(M_i\), forcing bounded rescaled volume. Smooth convergence
gives positive limiting full curvature integral on some fixed
small ball at \(x\). Its comparison images lie in balls with
shrinking original radii, exhibiting a concentration in the
sense of Chang–Qing–Yang for a smaller fixed energy threshold.

For this particular choice of blow-up scale, the end conclusion
follows from Tian–Viaclovsky's pointed Theorem 1.1. Its hypotheses
(1.8)–(1.11) are a uniform normalized Sobolev constant, uniform
full \(L^2\) curvature, a positive lower volume bound and bounded
source \(b_1\). These hold for the rescaled sequence by (3)–(4),
\(\operatorname{Vol}_{\tilde g_i}=A_i^2\) and \(b_1(M_i)=0\).
The remark after Theorem 1.3 extends this theorem to
constant-scalar-curvature Bach-flat metrics with order-zero ALE
ends. The pointed convergence is Gromov–Hausdorff and identifies
its limit with the above smooth pointed limit. The noncompact case
has finitely many ends, by the argument in §7 using Theorem 5.2.
The order-zero condition following (1.6) gives
\(h_{ab}-\delta_{ab}=o(1)\) and
\(\partial^\beta h_{ab}=o(r^{-|\beta|})\) for every \(|\beta|\ge1\)
on each lifted end. The Betti bound in this theorem concerns
the source manifolds. Ache–Viaclovsky Definition 1.1
and Theorem 1.2 give differentiable ALE order \(n-2k=2\) for
the smooth scalar-flat Bach-flat limit (\(n=4,\ k=1\)), in new
end coordinates and through all derivatives.
[Tian–Viaclovsky, (1.5)–(1.11), remark after Theorem 1.3,
Theorem 5.2 and §7][tv] ·
[Ache–Viaclovsky, Definition 1.1, (1.7) and Theorem 1.2][av]

### Full core and the admissible critical value

For every fixed compact subset of \(X\), the comparison map
is a smooth embedding of a neighborhood for all sufficiently
large \(i\). This applies to each full compact truncation,
including all end links in the same comparison map. Passing
the rescaled (2) on compact tests eliminates the \(Y_i/A_i\)
term and gives
\[
 y\|f\|_{L^4(h)}^2\le6\|\nabla f\|_{L^2(h)}^2,\qquad
 E_\pm(h)\le a,\qquad
 \int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_h|^2\,dV_h
 \le \liminf_i\|T_i\|_2^2 .
\tag{6}
\]
The two energy inequalities follow by exhausting \(X\) by
compact sets, with the scale invariance of the relevant integrals
on the source. This is the transfer calculation of [E49][e49].
For the last bound the limiting scalar curvature is zero, so
the limiting trace-free Ricci tensor is the full Ricci tensor.

The end test of [E50][e50] applies to (3), (6), the
positive-order ends and a simultaneous full-core embedding.
It forces one end. The spherical link test of [E51][e51]
then forces the group of that end to be trivial, using
\(E_\pm(h)\le a<8\pi^2\), the zero core signature and the
embedding into a smooth integral homology four-sphere.
The E50 proof uses the homotopy-sphere invariants and one
actual embedding at a time. E51 allows a varying ambient
homology four-sphere. Thus varying the smooth type of the
\(M_i\) introduces no new topological hypothesis in either test.

Use [E52][e52] as the following scoped lemma. For a smooth
complete scalar-flat Bach-flat four-manifold with one AE end of
positive differentiable order through five derivatives, its
harmonic conformal factor near infinity and the removable
singularity theorem give a smooth Bach-flat conformal
one-point compactification \((\widehat X,\widehat h)\).
The order-two end just obtained supplies this differentiability.
E52 uses the harmonic factor to give a scalar-flat punctured
inverted metric before applying removability. The extension
is smooth in the resulting cap atlas. Bach-flatness at the
added point follows by smoothness. Capacity zero of the point
and conformal covariance give
\(\widehat Y=Y(\widehat X,[\widehat h])=Y_c(X,h)\ge y>0\),
where \(Y_c\) is the infimum of the Yamabe quotient on compactly
supported tests. Both chiral energies are preserved in the
compactification. These facts give the metric conditions in (1).
[Ache–Viaclovsky, Definition 1.6, Theorem 1.8 and Remark 7.2][av] ·
[E52, compactification and Yamabe comparison][e52]

For the topological condition, use an actual smooth embedding
of a truncation with boundary \(S^3\) into one \(M_i\).
Mayer–Vietoris gives integral homology balls on both sides.
Van Kampen across the simply connected link makes each
fundamental group a free factor of the trivial ambient group.
Capping the connected core by the smooth ball chart supplied by
removability therefore gives a smooth simply connected integral
homology sphere, hence a homotopy four-sphere. Also
\(\chi(X)=1\) since \(X\) retracts onto that core. The admissibility
of the capped smooth type follows from these intrinsic properties.
The smooth type of the ambient complement is undetermined.
[E52, topology of the compactification][e52]

Let \(e_X=E_+(h)=E_-(h)\), with equality from the signature
of the compactification. The scalar-flat AE Euler formula
has boundary limit one in Euler-characteristic normalization.
With \(\chi(X)=1\) this gives
\[
 \int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_h|^2\,dV_h=4e_X.
\tag{7}
\]
If \(e_X=0\), both Weyl blocks, the Ricci tensor and scalar
curvature vanish, forcing a flat metric contrary to
\(|{\rm Rm}_h(x)|=1\). Thus \(0<e_X\le a<8\pi^2\), and
the cap is a member of (1). Equations (3), (6) and (7) give
the strict residual estimate of [B31][b31] and [E54][e54]:
\[
 Y_{\rm rd}^2-48(a-e_X)\le y^2\le\widehat Y^2<Y_{\rm rd}^2,
 \qquad
 a-e_X\ \ge\ \frac{Y_{\rm rd}^2-\widehat Y^2}{48}>0 .
\tag{8}
\]
The strict Yamabe bound uses the nonzero Weyl of the
compactification, which excludes conformal roundness.
The inequality concerns the integral budgets of the full
core and the original sequence. The cap now has an admissible
value \(e_X<a\), contradicting the infimum. Hence no minimizing
sequence as above can have an unbounded-curvature subsequence.
[Schoen, Theorem 2, p. 493, and sharp equality case][schoen] ·
[Lee–Parker, Theorems 3.2–3.3][lp] ·
[E54, single-component strict residual][e54]

### Bounded curvature and attainment

With \(\sup_i\max|{\rm Rm}_{g_i}|<\infty\), apply the same
local estimates and injectivity-radius bound in the original
unit-volume gauges. The smooth pointed convergence also gives
pointed Gromov–Hausdorff convergence. The uniform diameter bound
therefore gives a smooth compact connected limit
\((M_\infty,g_\infty)\). A comparison embedding of all of
\(M_\infty\) into \(M_i\) is open and closed and hence a
diffeomorphism for large \(i\). Thus \(M_\infty\) is a smooth
homotopy four-sphere, and the limit has unit volume,
\(B_{g_\infty}=0\), \(R_{g_\infty}=y\), and \(E_\pm(g_\infty)=a\).
Passing (2) to the limit for every smooth test gives
\(Y(M_\infty,[g_\infty])\ge y\), and the constant test gives
equality. This verifies that the limit is still in a Yamabe
minimizer gauge with positive class Yamabe constant.
Since \(a\ge4\pi^2>0\), the limit lies in (1). If \(a=4\pi^2\)
the closed sharp test E54 would exclude this limit. We conclude
\(a>4\pi^2\).

## The enlarged excluded interval and its scope

Set \(a_*=\min\{a,8\pi^2\}\) and
\[
 \varepsilon=\tfrac12(a_*-4\pi^2)>0 .
\tag{9}
\]
The proof gives a common excluded interval
\([4\pi^2,4\pi^2+\varepsilon]\) in each of the following senses.

- There is no exact closed critical value in this interval
  over any smooth homotopy four-sphere. If \(\mathcal A\) is
  empty, the same exclusion holds for the entire positive
  strictly subcritical interval.
- In the compact-gauge regime, suppose an almost-Bach-flat
  sequence has a smooth, or smoothly regularized, closed
  positive-Yamabe Bach-flat limit on a smooth homotopy four-sphere,
  with strong convergence of Weyl and limiting source level
  \(e_*\). For \(e_*\in[4\pi^2,4\pi^2+\varepsilon]\), the limit would
  lie in (1), a contradiction. These are the sufficient compact-gauge
  output hypotheses of the [B32/E57 setup][e57-request].
  If \(\mathcal A\) is empty, this applies to all \(0<e_*<8\pi^2\).
- In the supplied ALE regime, suppose a nonflat complete smooth
  pointed limit has the scale, convergence, source Yamabe
  minimizers and strict uniform energy margin in [E49][e49],
  finitely many differentiable positive-order ALE ends, and the
  full-core embeddings used above. Require the limiting
  scalar-flat Bach equation from E49 and the differentiability
  needed by E52. The same argument gives an admissible capped
  value \(e_X\) and \(e_* > e_X\ge a\), using (8) with \(e_*\)
  in place of \(a\). This excludes (9)'s interval and also
  excludes \(e_*=a\) when \(a\) is finite. An empty \(\mathcal A\)
  excludes such a limit for any \(0<e_*<8\pi^2\) with the stated
  hypotheses. The argument for this supplied regime uses no
  exact Bach-flat compactness of the source metrics.

The width in (9) is an existence conclusion and is non-effective.
Attainment minimizes the numerical value among positive
subcritical closed Bach-flat metrics of positive Yamabe class.
It gives no unrestricted Weyl-functional minimizer, stability
claim, existence of a positive critical value, or concentration
or gauge theorem for general almost-Bach-flat sequences. The
strict residual estimate makes no assertion about where the
remaining source energy lies.
