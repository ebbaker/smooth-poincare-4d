## Result

For every smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere (M), there
is a smoothly embedded closed ball (B) and a smooth positive reference
metric equal to one-centre Taub–NUT on a neighbourhood of (B). Every smooth
Riemannian metric (g) on (M) that retains this conformal metric on (B)
satisfies

\[
 E_+(M\setminus B,g)=E_+(M,g)=E_-(M,g)\ \geq\ \frac{11}{2}\pi^2.
\]

Here (E_\pm(U,g)=\int_U |W^\pm_g|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV_g), using the
curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm on \(\Lambda^\pm\) and the
hyperkähler orientation on the core. Thus \(W^+=0\) on the core. The
quantity retained is the conformal metric on a specified finite ball.

## The smooth model and its core energy

Use the Hopf map (q:\mathbb C^2\to\mathbb R^3), with
\(t_1+it_2=z_1z_2\) and \(t_3=(|z_2|^2-|z_1|^2)/2\). Put
\(\rho^2=|z_1|^2+|z_2|^2\), \(r=|q(z)|=\rho^2/2\), and fix
\(\ell>0\). The circle has period \(2\pi\). With \(g_{S^2}\) the unit
round metric and angular tensors pulled back by the Hopf fibration, the
one-centre Gibbons–Hawking metric is

\[
 V=\ell+\frac{1}{2r},\qquad
 g_{\rm TN}=V(dr^2+r^2g_{S^2})+V^{-1}\eta^2.
\]

Choose the sign of the Hopf connection \(\eta\) so that \(d\eta=*dV\)
in the Euclidean base orientation. This is the hyperkähler Taub–NUT model
in Cherkis–Hurtubise, §2.1, equation (1) and the period in footnote 1.
In the hyperkähler orientation the three parallel Kähler forms span
\(\Lambda^+\). Hyperkähler holonomy gives Ricci-flatness, and the flat
induced connection on \(\Lambda^+\) gives \(W^+=0\). [Cherkis–Hurtubise][ch]

Write \(\eta=\alpha/\rho^2\), where \(\alpha\) is, up to sign, the
smooth one-form \(\operatorname{Im}(\bar z_1\,dz_1-\bar z_2\,dz_2)\).
The Euclidean metric \(g_0\) in Hopf coordinates is
\(d\rho^2+\rho^2(\eta^2+g_{S^2}/4)\). With \(x=2\ell r=\ell\rho^2\),
substitution gives

\[
 g_{\rm TN}=(1+x)g_0-\frac{\ell(2+x)}{1+x}\alpha^2.
\]

This expression is smooth at \(z=0\) and equals \(g_0\) there. The
metric is positive away from the nut by its Gibbons–Hawking form. Its
radial length to infinity is infinite since \(V\geq\ell\). For every
\(R>0\) the set \(B_R=\{r\leq R\}\) is the closed coordinate ball
\(\rho\leq\sqrt{2R}\), and \(r=R\) is a regular smooth three-sphere.

For the energy, introduce the unit radial coordinate \(u\) by
\(du=\sqrt V\,dr\). The slice metric is
\(a(u)^2 g_{S^2}+c(u)^2\eta^2\), where \(a=r\sqrt V\) and
\(c=V^{-1/2}\). In the orthonormal radial–slice splitting the shape
operator of the slices for the outward normal has entries
\(a'/a,a'/a,c'/c\), with prime denoting \(d/du\). These directions are
preserved under radial parallel transport, since the horizontal and
vertical distributions of the slice metric are independent of \(u\).
The normal Riccati equation then gives the radial curvature matrix with
entries \(-a''/a,-a''/a,-c''/c\). In particular, the vertical radial
sectional curvature is

\[
 c'=\frac{1}{4r^2V^2}=\frac{1}{(1+x)^2},\qquad
 K=-\frac{c''}{c}=\frac{4\ell}{(1+x)^3}.
\]

Use the curvature-operator sign with positive sectional curvature on
the round sphere. Since the full curvature operator is supported in
\(\Lambda^-\), projecting each radial unit bivector into \(\Lambda^-\)
and multiplying by \(\sqrt{2}\) gives an orthonormal basis for that
block. Thus its entries are twice those of the radial curvature matrix.
The two horizontal entries agree, and \(W^-\) is trace free, so the
eigenvalues are \(-K,-K,2K\). Consequently
\( |W^-|_{\rm HS}^2=6K^2=96\ell^2/(1+x)^6\).

The fibre and sphere integration factors are \(2\pi\) and \(4\pi\).
The shell volume is \(8\pi^2 V r^2\,dr=\pi^2\ell^{-2}x(1+x)\,dx\).
Writing \(X=2\ell R\) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 E_-(B_R,g_{\rm TN})
 &=96\pi^2\int_0^X \frac{x}{(1+x)^5}\,dx\\
 &=8\pi^2\left(1-\frac{4}{(1+X)^3}+\frac{3}{(1+X)^4}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]

The total is \(8\pi^2\) by exhaustion. It agrees with Atiyah–LeBrun
§5, equation (5.11), where the orientation interchanges the two Weyl
blocks, and with their direct ALF Gauss–Bonnet calculation in
equations (5.16), (5.18) and the Taub–NUT sentence following (5.18).
Their §2 norm statement is \(|W|^2=\tfrac14 W_{ijkl}W^{ijkl}\), so the
normalizations agree. The explicit core is \(R=(2\ell)^{-1}\), giving
\(E_-(B_R,g_{\rm TN})=11\pi^2/2>4\pi^2\). This \(R\) is a Hopf base
radius. [Atiyah–LeBrun][al]

## Extension and the signature constraint

Fix (R=(2\ell)^{-1}) and any (R'>R). Choose an orientation-preserving
embedding \(i\) of a neighbourhood of \(B_{R'}\) into a coordinate chart
of \(M\) by a sufficiently small linear dilation in oriented coordinates.
Set \(B=i(B_R)\). Given any smooth background Riemannian metric \(g_b\)
on \(M\), choose a smooth cutoff \(\chi\) supported in
\(i(\operatorname{int}B_{R'})\) and equal to one on a neighbourhood of \(B\).
The convex combination \(g_{\rm ref}=\chi i_*g_{\rm TN}+(1-\chi)g_b\)
on the image of \(i\), extended by \(g_b\), is smooth and positive. The chart
and cutoff use the smooth structure and orientation of \(M\).

For any smooth global extension \(g\) of the prescribed conformal metric,
write \(i^*g=e^{2f}g_{\rm TN}\) on the interior of \(B_R\). In four
dimensions the Hodge splitting on two-forms is unchanged by this rescaling,
and each Weyl block as an operator scales by \(e^{-2f}\) while the volume
scales by \(e^{4f}\). Thus \(E_+(B,g)=0\) and
\(E_-(B,g)=11\pi^2/2\). The sphere boundary has zero four-volume.

For the smooth metric on the entire closed oriented \(M\), the signature
formula in the same convention is

\[
 12\pi^2\tau(M)=E_+(M,g)-E_-(M,g).
\]

The intersection form is zero for a homotopy sphere, so \(\tau(M)=0\).
Additivity and nonnegativity now give
\(E_+(M\setminus B,g)=E_-(B,g)+E_-(M\setminus B,g)\geq 11\pi^2/2\).
The formula applies on the closed manifold. Gover–Gursky, pp. 293–294,
write the identity using the four-index norm and coefficient \(48\pi^2\).
Division by four gives the operator-norm form above, as in the assessed
shared recognition note. [Gover–Gursky][gg] · [SH-E01][she01]

## Consequences and scope

- **Exact ASD.** Any retained ASD region of positive \(W^-\) energy
  already forces nonzero \(W^+\) somewhere on the complement in a smooth
  global metric of signature zero. The selected Taub–NUT core has positive
  energy on every nonempty open subset by the calculation above.
- **Strict CGY energy.** CGY Theorem A, equation (0.3) and Remark 2 use
  the four-index norm. At \(\chi(M)=2\), their bound is
  \(\int_M |W|^2_{(0,4)}dV<32\pi^2\). This integral equals
  \(4(E_++E_-)=8E_+\) here. The selected core therefore forces it to be
  at least \(44\pi^2\), exceeding the strict threshold. Retaining only
  a sufficiently small neighbourhood of the nut leaves positive core
  energy but the computed energy tends to zero as \(R\downarrow 0\).
  [Chang–Gursky–Yang][cgy]
- **Support.** Arbitrary changes throughout the complement still obey
  the lower bound. If the proposed extension is ASD outside a measurable
  transition set \(T\subset M\setminus B\), that set must carry the same
  lower bound on \(E_+\). In particular a family with such sets shrinking
  to zero metric volume must have \(\sup_T |W^+|_{\rm HS}\) unbounded.
  Signature gives the total exterior lower bound. Localization uses the
  stated support hypothesis.
- **Yamabe.** CGY also needs positive Yamabe constant of the same global
  conformal class. The Yamabe sign remains unevaluated in this test.
  The energy obstruction holds for any sign. [Chang–Gursky–Yang][cgy]

This is an obstruction for a construction that retains the specified
conformal core. Solvability with freely chosen or alterable core data
remains a separate question.
