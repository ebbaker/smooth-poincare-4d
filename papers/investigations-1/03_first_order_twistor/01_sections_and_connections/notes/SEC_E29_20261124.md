## Result

On the standard smooth oriented \(S^4\) there is a family of smooth
positive Riemannian metrics \(g_R\), \(R\geq1\), containing two exact
balls of radius \(R\) in the Hopf base coordinate of one-centre
Taub–NUT. The two balls have opposite Taub–NUT orientations relative
to the global orientation. In the curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt convention and with \(B_g\) the Bach tensor,

\[
 \int_{S^4}|B_{g_R}|_{g_R}\,dV_{g_R}=O(R^{-1}),\qquad
 E_+(g_R)=E_-(g_R)=8\pi^2+O(R^{-1}).
\]

The construction retains on each side the exact inner ball with
Hopf base radius \(1/2\). In particular \(E_\pm(g_R)\geq11\pi^2/2\).
This is the positive-energy test selected in [B19][b19].

## Exact cores and smooth marked double

Use the smooth one-centre Taub–NUT metric on \(\mathbb C^2\) in
[E07][e07] with \(\ell=1\). Away from the nut, \(r=|z|^2/2\)
is the Hopf base radius and

\[
 g_{\rm TN}=\left(1+\frac{1}{2r}\right)(dr^2+r^2\pi^*g_{S^2})
      +\left(1+\frac{1}{2r}\right)^{-1}\eta^2 ,
\]

where \(\pi:S^3\to S^2\) is the Hopf projection to the unit sphere
and \(\eta\) has fibre period \(2\pi\) in its normalized circle
coordinate. This metric is Ricci-flat and hyperkähler. In its
hyperkähler orientation \(W^+=0\). The closed region \(r\leq R\)
is the standard coordinate ball of Euclidean radius \(\sqrt{2R}\),
with smooth nut and with boundary marked by \(z/\sqrt{2R}\in S^3\).
[E07][e07] · [Cherkis–Hurtubise, §2.1, (1) and footnote 1][ch]

Join two copies of this ball by \(N=[-1,1]\times S^3\) using the
same boundary marking at both ends. The two balls and the cylinder
are the standard smooth \(S^4\). Indeed, the two latitude caps of
the round \(S^4\) are smooth coordinate \(D^4\)'s with their boundaries
marked by the unit directions in the first four ambient coordinates.
Their complement is a product between those marked latitude spheres.
Collar reparametrizations give the same marked gluing as here.
Choose the global orientation to agree with the hyperkähler orientation
on the left ball. The identity marking and opposite outward normal
directions across the cylinder force the opposite orientation on
the right copy. To place the family on one fixed marked sphere,
start with the two \(R=1\) balls and use the identity on the
cylinder. On each ball send \(r\) to
\(r[1+(R-1)\zeta(r)]\) with the angular marking fixed, where
\(\zeta\) is a smooth nondecreasing cutoff that vanishes near
\([0,1/2]\) and equals one near \(r=1\). The radial derivative is
positive. The map is the identity near the inner ball and nut,
and matches the identity in \(s=r/R\) coordinates near the
new seam. Thus these radial maps and the cylinder marking
give smooth identifications preserving the inner balls.

On the balls put the scaled metric
\(\widehat g_R=R^{-2}g_{\rm TN}\). In \(s=r/R\), near \(s=1\) and
its continuation past the boundary, this is

\[
 V_R(s)(ds^2+s^2\pi^*g_{S^2})+R^{-2}V_R(s)^{-1}\eta^2,
 \qquad V_R(s)=1+\frac{1}{2Rs}.
\]

Let \(\chi(t)\) be a smooth even cutoff with \(0\leq\chi\leq1\),
equal to zero for \(|t|\leq1/4\) and one for \(|t|\geq3/4\).
Set \(\sigma(t)=2-\chi(t)|t|\) on \([-1,1]\).
It is smooth, takes values in \([1,2]\), and equals \(2-|t|\)
near both ends. Take the following actual neck metric:

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 A_R(t)&=V_R(\sigma(t)),&
 H_R(t)&=\sigma(t)^2 V_R(\sigma(t)),&
 C_R(t)&=V_R(\sigma(t))^{-1},\\
 \widehat g_R|_N
   &= A_R(t)\,dt^2+H_R(t)\pi^*g_{S^2}
          +R^{-2} C_R(t)\eta^2.
 \end{aligned}
\]

Near the left seam the exact matching coordinates satisfy \(s=t+2\).
Near the right seam they satisfy \(s=2-t\). On either side
\(ds^2=dt^2\) in the matching neighbourhood where the formula is
the Taub–NUT continuation. The seam thus agrees to every order.
On the middle interval all three coefficients are constant, so the
metric there is a product. The longitudinal coefficient is
\(A_R=V_R(\sigma)>0\) even where the radius profile has
\(\sigma'=0\). Consequently the neck is smooth and positive
throughout. Define \(g_R=R^2\widehat g_R\) globally, so the two
original Taub–NUT metrics are exact on their closed cores and
continue as Taub–NUT on an open neighbourhood of each core.

## Uniform local geometry and volume of the scaled neck

The coefficient functions \(A_R,H_R,C_R\), their inverses and
their derivatives through order four are uniformly bounded on
the fixed neck interval for \(R\geq1\). This follows directly
from the fixed smooth \(\sigma\), its range \([1,2]\), and
\(1\leq V_R\leq3/2\) there. To see that these estimates give
intrinsic curvature bounds despite the collapsing circle, choose
finitely many relatively compact charts on the base sphere with
local smooth sections of the Hopf fibration on larger charts.
In the induced gauge \(\eta=d\phi+\alpha\), the base coefficients
of \(g_{S^2}\) and the one-form \(\alpha\) have uniform \(C^4\)
bounds on that fixed finite cover. Unwrap the circle locally on
each chart and change coordinate to \(\psi=\phi/R\). The lifted
metric becomes

\[
  A_R\,dt^2+H_R g_{S^2}
      +C_R(d\psi+R^{-1}\alpha)^2 .
\]

It is uniformly comparable to the coordinate Euclidean metric:
the diagonal coefficients in the triangular coframe
\((dt,dx^1,dx^2,d\psi+R^{-1}\alpha)\) and the base metric
have uniform positive lower bounds and uniform upper bounds.
The triangular change and its inverse are uniformly bounded.
All four coordinate derivatives of the metric and its inverse
are uniformly bounded. Christoffel and curvature formulas in
these coordinates then bound \(|\operatorname{Rm}|\),
\(|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|\) and \(|\nabla^2\operatorname{Rm}|\)
uniformly. They are bounds on the original metric because the
lift is a local isometry. This argument uses a local covering
calculation with bounds independent of the shrinking circle
period in \(\psi\). The seam formulas extend to small fixed
matching neighbourhoods, so the estimates cover the seam
points as well.

The neck volume density uses a base horizontal block in dimension
two and a one-dimensional circle block. In absolute-density notation
it is

\[
 R^{-1}\sqrt{A_RC_R}\,H_R\,dt\,
       |\pi^*dA_{S^2}\wedge\eta| .
\]

Here \(A_RC_R=1\) and
\(\int_{S^3}|\pi^*dA_{S^2}\wedge\eta|=4\pi\cdot2\pi=8\pi^2\).
Thus
\(\operatorname{Vol}(N,\widehat g_R)
=8\pi^2R^{-1}\int_{-1}^1 H_R(t)\,dt=O(R^{-1})\).

## Bach norm and both chiral energies

Use the Bach convention from [E24][e24]. In terms of the
Schouten tensor \(P=\tfrac12(\operatorname{Ric}-\tfrac16 s_g g)\),
the Bach formula has two contracted second derivatives of \(P\)
and the term \(P^{kl}W_{kijl}\). Ricci-flatness gives \(P=0\)
identically on the cores, so their Bach tensors are zero.
On the neck the preceding bounds yield
\(|B_{\widehat g_R}|_{\widehat g_R}
\leq C(|\nabla^2\operatorname{Rm}|+|\operatorname{Rm}|^2)\leq C'\).
Hence \(\int|B_{\widehat g_R}|_{\widehat g_R}\,dV_{\widehat g_R}
=O(R^{-1})\). [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1, (1.1)][bgim]

For any constant homothety \(g'=\lambda^2 g\) in dimension four,
\(B_{g'}=\lambda^{-2}B_g\) as a covariant two-tensor.
This follows from the displayed Schouten structure: the
Schouten tensor and connection are unchanged, the derivative
terms gain one inverse metric factor, and the Weyl term gains
two inverse metric factors and a factor \(\lambda^2\) from
the covariant Weyl tensor. Thus \(|B_{g'}|_{g'}=\lambda^{-4}|B_g|_g\)
and \(dV_{g'}=\lambda^4 dV_g\). The operator norms of the Weyl
blocks scale by \(\lambda^{-2}\), so their squared integrals
are invariant as well. These identities give the same integral
bounds in \(g_R\).

On the left core only the minus chirality contributes, and on
the right only the plus chirality contributes. On each contributing
core the exact cut-off energy in [E07][e07] is

\[
 8\pi^2\left(1-\frac{4}{(1+2R)^3}+\frac{3}{(1+2R)^4}\right).
\]

It tends to \(8\pi^2\), in agreement with the complete Taub–NUT
operator-norm integral of Atiyah–LeBrun, after aligning orientations.
On the scaled neck \(|W^\pm|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}\) is bounded by
the curvature estimate. Each neck energy is nonnegative and
\(O(R^{-1})\). This proves the two limits. Equality of the total
chiral energies holds for each \(R\) by the closed signature
identity on \(S^4\). For each exact inner \(r\leq1/2\) ball
the displayed cap formula gives \(11\pi^2/2\) in its nonzero
chirality. This yields the stated lower bound on the whole
metrics. [Atiyah–LeBrun, §2 norms and §5, (5.11), (5.18)][al] ·
[SH-E01][she01]

The construction establishes positive limiting Weyl energy with
arbitrarily small total Bach norm on this particular smooth sphere.
The result addresses that pair of bounds alone. The Yamabe sign,
minimizing status, hypotheses for same-manifold smooth compactness
and subcritical variational questions remain outside this test.
