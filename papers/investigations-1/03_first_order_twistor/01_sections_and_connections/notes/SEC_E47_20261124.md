## Setting and result

Use one smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\) with a
supplied smooth positive Riemannian \(r\), unit round on an oriented
embedded round ball \(O\), and a closed protected subball \(K\Subset O\).
Write \(\nu=dV_r\), \(E=\Lambda_r^+\), \(V=S^2_0T^*M\) and
\(B=S^2_0E\) with the real curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt pairing.
Use the full fixed-volume chart
\[
 g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot),\qquad
 F(h)=I_h W_{g_h}^+ I_h^{-1},\qquad h^\sharp=r^{-1}h,\qquad
 I_h=\Lambda^2(e^{-h^\sharp/2})^*\big|_{\Lambda^+_{g_h}}.
\]
Set \(W=F(0)\), \(L=DF(0)\), \(H=D^2F(0)\),
\(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\), \(P=LL^*\), and \(Q=L^*G_P\) for
the orthogonal generalized inverse \(G_P\) of \(P\). Pairings,
adjoints and Sobolev norms use \(r,\nu\). Write
\(b(f)(Z)=\int_M\langle Z,f\rangle\,d\nu\) for \(Z\in\mathcal C\).
The normal operator has elliptic symbol \(|\xi|_r^4 I_B/16\),
so \(\mathcal C\) is finite-dimensional and smooth. Fix an integer
\(s\ge5\) for the nonlinear E44 implication.
[E03, (1)–(4)][e03] · [E27, fixed data and normal inverse][e27]

In a relatively compact exterior coordinate patch, take a smooth
phase \(\phi\) with \(n=\nabla_r\phi\) of unit length on the patch.
Such a coordinate is furnished locally by signed distance to a
smooth hypersurface with the prescribed normal. Let \(\beta\) be a
real compactly supported smooth amplitude and \(u\) an arbitrary
smooth real tangent polarization there. Extend
\(X_N=N^{-1}\beta\sin(N\phi)u\) by zero. Define
\[
 e_N=\mathcal K X_N=(\mathcal L_{X_N}r)_0,\quad
 p_N=QLe_N,\quad k_N=e_N-p_N.
\]
The tensor \(k_N\) is a smooth global kernel direction. With
\(d=\langle u,n\rangle\) and
\(A=\iota(\operatorname{skew}(u\otimes n^\flat))\), where \(\iota\)
is infinitesimal pullback on \(E\), the unhalved Hessian satisfies
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 b(H[k_N,k_N])(Z)\longrightarrow
 \tfrac12\int_M\beta^2
 \left(\tfrac{d^2}{4}\langle Z,W\rangle
              +\langle[A,Z],[A,W]\rangle\right)d\nu .}
 \tag{1}
\]
For any fixed finite family with disjoint compact supports of the
cutoffs, the off-diagonal projected Hessian limits vanish as the
frequencies tend independently to infinity. In particular,
\(u=n\) gives \(+\tfrac18\int\beta^2\langle Z,W\rangle\,d\nu\)
in (1). With a general submersive phase and a unit normalized
normal, the amplitude in (1) is \(|d\phi|_r\beta\).

## Derivative of the polar identification

For this calculation fix a smooth vector field \(X\) with flow
\(\varphi_a\), put \(y=\varphi_a(x)\), \(J_a=\varphi_a^*\nu/\nu\),
and let \(\Psi_a(h)\) be the chart coordinate of
\(J_a^{-1/2}\varphi_a^*g_h\). This metric has volume \(\nu\)
pointwise. Write \(R_h=e^{h^\sharp/2}\) and
\[
 B_{a,h}=J_a^{-1/4}R_h(y)\,d\varphi_a(x),\qquad
 O_{a,h}=B_{a,h}(B_{a,h}^*B_{a,h})^{-1/2}.
 \tag{2}
\]
The adjoint in (2) is for the two reference tangent metrics. In
fact \(B_{a,h}^*B_{a,h}=e^{\Psi_a(h)^\sharp}\), so \(O_{a,h}\)
is the same symmetric comparison of normalized pullback metrics
used in E42. It is an oriented \(r\)-isometry. On self-dual two-forms
naturality gives
\[
 F(\Psi_a(h))(x)=J_a^{1/2}O_{a,h}^*
           F(h)(y)(O_{a,h}^*)^{-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]
For the scale factor, the fully covariant Weyl tensor of
\(\rho g\) equals \(\rho W_{ijkl}(g)\). Raising a pair of indices
for the curvature operator multiplies this by \(\rho^{-2}\),
and the middle-degree Hodge star stays fixed. The normalized metric
scale is \(\rho=J_a^{-1/2}\), giving \(\rho^{-1}=J_a^{1/2}\)
in (3). [Fefferman–Graham, §6 after (6.7), covariant Weyl weight][fg] ·
[E42, (2)–(4)][e42]

Compare the tangent spaces by parallel transport along \(a\mapsto y\).
Let \(D=\nabla X\), with \(Dv=\nabla_vX\), \(S=\operatorname{sym}D\),
\(\Omega_0=\operatorname{skew}D\), and \(\theta=\operatorname{tr}D\).
The derivative of \(d\varphi_a\) at zero in these frames is \(D\).
Writing \(B_{a,h}=O_{a,h}R_{a,h}\) with \(R_{a,h}\) its positive
symmetric right factor, we have \(O_{0,h}=I\), \(R_{0,h}=R_h\).
The derivative \(\Omega(h)=\partial_a O_{0,h}\) is skew. Since
\(\partial_a R_{0,h}\) is symmetric, skew subtraction of the derivative
of the product gives
\[
 \Omega(h)R_h+R_h\Omega(h)
       =\partial_a B_{0,h}-(\partial_a B_{0,h})^*
       =R_hD-D^*R_h .
 \tag{4}
\]
Here \(\partial_a B_{0,h}=\nabla_XR_h+R_hD-\theta R_h/4\);
the first and last summands are symmetric. Differentiating (4)
in \(h\) at zero uses \(D_hR_0[j]=j^\sharp/2\) and gives
\[
 2D_h\Omega(0)[j]+\tfrac12(\Omega_0j^\sharp+j^\sharp\Omega_0)
    =\tfrac12(j^\sharp D-D^*j^\sharp),\qquad
 D_h\Omega(0)[j]=\tfrac14[j^\sharp,S]
              =\tfrac18[j^\sharp,e^\sharp],
 \quad e=\mathcal K X .
 \tag{5}
\]
The scalar trace removal in \(e^\sharp=2S-\theta I/2\) commutes with
\(j^\sharp\). Formula (5) depends only on the value of \(j\) and
the first jet of \(X\), and is zero for \(j=e\).

On target sections put
\[
 T_X f=\nabla_X f+\tfrac12\theta f+[\iota(\Omega_0),f],\qquad
 C_X(j)=\big[\iota(\tfrac14[j^\sharp,S]),W\big] .
\]
The induced pullback generator is
\((\iota(A_T)\omega)(v,w)=\omega(A_Tv,w)+\omega(v,A_Tw)\)
for a tangent skew endomorphism \(A_T\).
Differentiate (3) in \(a\) to get
\[
 DF(h)\,\partial_a\Psi_0(h)
  =\nabla_X F(h)+\tfrac12\theta F(h)+[\iota(\Omega(h)),F(h)] .
\]
At \(h=0\), \(\partial_a\Psi_0(0)=e\) and \(Le=T_X W\).
Differentiating once in \(h\) at zero gives
\(H[e,j]+L(D_h\partial_a\Psi_0(0)[j])=T_X Lj+C_X(j)\).
The term in the range of \(L\) drops from every old adjoint row.
Thus the exact paired identity in this fixed chart is
\[
 b(H[e,j])(Z)=\langle Z,T_XLj+C_X(j)\rangle_{L^2(r)} .
 \tag{6}
\]
In particular (5) removes \(C_X(e)\) exactly. No derivative of \(j\)
appears in the rotation error \(C_X(j)\).

## Projection and high-frequency limit

For any smooth \(X\), \(LQLe=Le\) by the normal inverse and
\(Le\perp\mathcal C\). Therefore taking \(p=QLe\) and \(k=e-p\)
in (6) gives
\[
 b(H[k,k])(Z)
 =\langle T_XZ,T_XW\rangle_{L^2(r)}
      -2\langle Z,C_X(p)\rangle_{L^2(r)}+b(H[p,p])(Z).
 \tag{7}
\]
Indeed the combination \(H[e,e]-2H[e,p]\) contributes
\(-T_X^2W-2C_X(p)\) in the adjoint pairing. The divergence theorem on
the closed manifold shows \((\nabla_X)^*=-\nabla_X-\theta\).
Commutation with a skew endomorphism is skew-adjoint for
Hilbert–Schmidt, so \(T_X^*=-T_X\) and the minus sign in
front of \(T_X^2\) becomes the positive sign in (7).

Apply (7) to \(X_N\). Its first derivative is
\(\beta\cos(N\phi)u\otimes n^\flat+O(N^{-1})\) in \(L^\infty\),
and \(X_N=O(N^{-1})\) there. Hence for each fixed smooth target \(f\),
\[
 T_{X_N} f=\beta\cos(N\phi)\big(\tfrac d2 f+[A,f]\big)
                   +O_f(N^{-1}) \quad \text{in }L^\infty .
 \tag{8}
\]
The leading term has \(H^{-1}\) norm \(O(N^{-1})\) by pairing
with an \(H^1\) test and integrating
\(\cos(N\phi)=N^{-1}n(\sin(N\phi))\) by parts on the compact
amplitude support. This uses \(n(\phi)=1\), and every derivative of
the nonoscillating factors is smooth and fixed. Thus \(Le_N\) is
\(O(1)\) in \(L^2\) and \(O(N^{-1})\) in \(H^{-1}\).
The closed elliptic estimate and Fredholm alternative for \(P\)
give \(Q:H^\ell(B)\to H^{\ell+2}(V)\) bounded for
\(\ell=0,-1\). At \(\ell=-1\) the annihilating rows still use the
smooth fields \(\mathcal C\) by distributional elliptic regularity.
Consequently \(p_N\) is bounded in \(H^2\) and
\(\|p_N\|_{H^1}=O(N^{-1})\). Smoothness follows from the smooth right
side and the same elliptic equation. [E03, normal operator][e03] ·
[Melrose, Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose]

The pointwise rotation error in (7) is bounded in the paired norm
by \(C\|\nabla X_N\|_{L^\infty}\|p_N\|_{L^2}\).
The paired Hessian is bounded on \(H^1\times H^1\) for every fixed
smooth \(Z\). In the frozen chart the differential expression is
quasilinear in second metric derivatives, and its second variation
contains only terms \(p\nabla^2q,\ q\nabla^2p,\ \nabla p\nabla q\)
and terms of lower total order with fixed smooth coefficients.
Integrating the double-derivative terms by parts once against \(Z\)
gives \(|b(H[p,q])(Z)|\le C\|p\|_{H^1}\|q\|_{H^1}\).
In particular both error terms in (7) vanish. [E03, fixed
chart and local curvature expression][e03]

Inserting (8) for \(Z\) and \(W\), the leading integrand in (7)
has factor \(\beta^2\cos^2(N\phi)\). The expression multiplying that
factor is
\[
 \tfrac{d^2}{4}\langle Z,W\rangle+\langle[A,Z],[A,W]\rangle,
\]
since the two \(dA\) cross terms cancel by the skew-adjointness of
\([A,\cdot]\). The oscillating part of
\(\cos^2(N\phi)=\tfrac12(1+\cos(2N\phi))\) vanishes on integration
by parts. This proves (1).

For different probes \(i,j\), polarize (7) using \(X_i+X_j\).
The corrections \(e,p,k\) and the operators \(T_X\) are linear
in \(X\). The mixed products of the two \(T\) terms vanish because
their supports are disjoint, since \(T_X\) uses only \(X,\nabla X\).
The other rotation and Hessian terms are bounded by the product
estimates just proved and tend to zero with independent frequencies.
Since \(\mathcal C\) is finite-dimensional, all the convergence is
also convergence as vectors of \(\mathcal C^*\).

## Normalization and the dual form

At a point fix an oriented orthonormal tangent frame
\((n,e_1,e_2,e_3)\) and the orthonormal positive frame
\[
 \omega_i=\tfrac1{\sqrt2}\big(n^\flat\wedge e_i^\flat
                           +*(n^\flat\wedge e_i^\flat)\big).
\]
Use the standard \(SO(3)\) generator \(J_i\omega_j=\varepsilon_{ijk}\omega_k\),
which rotates its two-plane at unit speed and satisfies
\(\|J_i\|_{\mathrm{HS}}=\sqrt2\). For \(u=e_1\),
\(\operatorname{skew}(e_1\otimes n^\flat)\) sends
\(n\mapsto e_1/2\) and \(e_1\mapsto-n/2\). Its pullback on two-forms
fixes \(\omega_1\) infinitesimally and sends
\(\omega_2\mapsto-\omega_3/2\), \(\omega_3\mapsto\omega_2/2\).
The cyclic permutations give \(A=-\tfrac12\sum_i u_iJ_i\)
for \(u=\sum_i u_i e_i\) transverse to \(n\). Thus the simple
tangent plane angle acts at that same angular speed on the
self-dual rotation plane. The factor \(1/2\) comes from the skew
part of the rank-one tangent map. This explicit action accounts
for the normalization without a spinor lift. All \(A\in\mathfrak{so}(E)\)
are attainable at fixed \(n\), and the longitudinal component \(d n\)
can be added independently. [Itoh, p. 155 after (2.4)][itoh]

For \(Z\in\mathcal C\) define the symmetric matrix in this standard
generator basis at \(x\) by
\[
 D_Z(x)_{ij}=\tfrac12\left(
    \langle[J_i,Z],[J_j,W]\rangle+
    \langle[J_j,Z],[J_i,W]\rangle\right)_x.
 \tag{9}
\]
If \(A=\sum a_iJ_i\), its quadratic form is
\(a^{\mathsf T}D_Z a=\langle[A,Z],[A,W]\rangle_x\). This description
and positive semidefiniteness are unchanged by oriented orthonormal
changes of frame. The cross-product realization \(J_i v=\omega_i\times v\)
gives \(\sum_i J_i^2=-2I\) and
\(\sum_i J_i M J_i = M^{\mathsf T}-(\operatorname{tr}M)I\), by contracting
two alternating symbols. For symmetric trace-free \(M\) this yields
the Casimir in this normalization,
\[
 -\sum_i[J_i,[J_i,M]]=6M.
\]
Since \([J_i,\cdot]\) is skew-adjoint,
\[
 \boxed{\operatorname{tr}D_Z(x)=6\langle Z(x),W(x)\rangle.}
 \tag{10}
\]
The longitudinal part of (1) is therefore already nonnegative in
any row with \(D_Z(x)\) positive semidefinite. Equivalently, the
longitudinal functional at \(x\) is a positive multiple of the sum
over three transverse generators at that point.

For an oriented orthonormal eigenbasis of \(W\), write
\(W=\operatorname{diag}(w_1,w_2,w_3)\) and \(Z=(z_{ij})\).
With \(c_i=w_j-w_k\) for cyclic \((i,j,k)\), (9) becomes
\[
 (D_Z)_{ii}=2c_i(z_{jj}-z_{kk}),\qquad
 (D_Z)_{ij}=3w_k z_{ij}\quad(i,j,k\text{ distinct}).
 \tag{11}
\]
For example \([J_1,W]_{23}=c_1\),
\([J_2,W]_{31}=c_2\), \([J_1,Z]_{31}=z_{12}\) and
\([J_2,Z]_{23}=-z_{12}\). Thus \((D_Z)_{12}=(c_2-c_1)z_{12}\),
and \(c_2-c_1=3w_3\) by trace-freeness. The rest follow cyclically.
This also verifies the off-diagonal signs of (11).

## Positive span and the one-sided radial lift

Let \(\mathcal D\subset\mathcal C^*\) be the positive hull of the
diagonal limiting probe vectors in (1), allowing every exterior
support and polarization in the packet. Equivalently, for the
quadratic Taylor coefficient one can use their halves. Then
\[
 \mathcal D\ne\mathcal C^*
 \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \exists\, 0\ne Z\in\mathcal C\ \text{such that}\
   D_Z(x)\succeq0\quad\text{for every }x\in M\setminus K .
 \tag{12}
\]
If \(D_Z\succeq0\) everywhere, (10) gives nonnegative longitudinal
terms and (9) nonnegative transverse terms at every \(x\).
The row is thus nonnegative on \(\mathcal D\). If \(D_Z\) has a
negative direction at a point, choose the transverse polarization
there that realizes it. Extend it smoothly on a smaller coordinate
patch with a unit-gradient phase and \(u\perp n\). By continuity it
is still strictly negative on a smaller open patch. A nonzero bump
supported there gives a negative limiting vector in that row.
This proves the exact dual description.

To rule out a proper positive hull with trivial dual, suppose there
is no such nonzero \(Z\). For every unit \(Z\) choose an exterior
point and transverse generator with strictly negative evaluation.
These strict half-spaces cover the unit sphere of \(\mathcal C\).
A finite subcover leaves a positive uniform margin \(\delta\)
in the maximum of the negatives. Repeated centers can be perturbed
to distinct exterior points after smoothly extending each choice
of local frame and polarization. By continuity the finite evaluation
vectors change by less than \(\delta/4\) in the dual norm when
the perturbations are sufficiently small. Choose disjoint small
coordinate balls and bumps of \(L^2\) norm one around these distinct
centers. By further shrinking, the averaged vectors differ from
the point vectors by less than \(\delta/4\) in the same norm,
with the fixed scalar in (1) incorporated into the margin.
Testing both signs of each unit row shows the convex hull of
these finitely many averaged vectors contains a ball about zero,
by the finite-dimensional support-function argument in E44.
They therefore positively span \(\mathcal C^*\).
This proof uses only the available strictly signed evaluations
and works directly with a disjoint configuration. It establishes
(12) without requiring the original positive hull to be closed.
[E44, finite positive hull and disjoint probes][e44]

If \(\mathcal C\ne0\) and the right-hand condition in (12) fails,
apply E44's finite-dimensional coefficient perturbation argument
to the polarized probe vectors. For the finite disjoint family let
\(v_j\) be half the \(j\)-th Hessian limit. The limiting quadratic
map is \(\sum_j a_j^2v_j\), because all mixed Hessian limits vanish.
Since the \(v_j\) positively span the target, write
\(-b(W)-\sum_jv_j\) as a nonnegative combination of them. This gives
a solution of the signed balance at which every \(a_j>0\). The
derivative columns \(2a_jv_j\) span. Coefficient convergence in (1)
and the finite-dimensional implicit function theorem give a regular
zero at sufficiently large *fixed* finite frequencies. E44/E42
then solve the exact scaled moment equation and the range equation
in the original chart. The resulting spatially smooth family,
jointly smooth in \((x,\tau)\), satisfies
\(F(h_\tau)=(1-\tau^2)W\) and \(h_0=0\). Thus \(t=\tau^2\) gives
an exact small one-sided lift for \(0\le t<\epsilon^2\), with
\(\epsilon\) depending on the supplied data and the chosen finite
probes. The case \(\mathcal C=0\) uses the normal inverse directly
and gives a branch smooth in \(t\).
[E44, transfer of a regular zero and exact moment equations][e44] ·
[E42, scaled equation and spatial regularity][e42]

For a nonzero \(Z\) as in (12) there are two cases.

- If \(D_Z(x)\) is nonzero somewhere in the exterior, its trace is
  positive at that point and nonnegative everywhere in the exterior.
  By (10), continuity and \(W=0\) on \(K\), \(b(W)(Z)>0\).
  Every limiting mixture evaluates nonnegatively on \(Z\), whereas
  the contracting target \(-b(W)(Z)\) is strictly negative.
  For any fixed disjoint configuration the same balance fails on
  bounded amplitude sets at all sufficiently large frequencies,
  by coefficient convergence. If every selected diagonal row
  is strictly positive, coefficient convergence makes the finite
  row positive definite, excluding all amplitudes for that
  configuration. Zero diagonal coefficients in a semidefinite limit
  leave possible finite-frequency effects at unbounded amplitudes.
- If \(D_Z\equiv0\) in the exterior, (10) gives \(b(W)(Z)=0\)
  and every polarized limiting mixture vanishes on \(Z\). The
  limiting scalar equation is then identically satisfied and
  its amplitude derivative is zero in that row. The test loses
  limiting rank. Finite-frequency rank and balance in that row
  require separate information.

These statements classify only the one-phase probe cone and its
disjoint limiting mixtures. They give neither a universal sign
exclusion for actual solutions of the adjoint PDE nor an obstruction
to general kernel families, finite-frequency corrections or other
scales. The constructive implication stays on a small interval in
a supplied fixed reference chart. [B27][b27] · [E44, scope of
one-sign tests][e44]
