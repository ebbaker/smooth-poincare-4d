## Hypotheses and conclusion

Let \(M\) be the smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere of B29,
and \(g_i\) smooth real positive Riemannian unit-volume Yamabe minimizers
on \(M\) with class constants \(Y_i>0\). Use curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt norms, so the common source energies are
\(E_i=E_+(g_i)=E_-(g_i)\) and the full four-index Weyl integral is
\(8E_i\). Use the source margin \(E_i\le 8\pi^2-\delta\),
\(\delta>0\). The lower endpoint \(4\pi^2\) of B29 plays no role in
this calculation. [SH-E01, signature and norm conversion][sh]

Suppose positive scales \(\lambda_i\to0\) give a supplied complete,
connected, smooth oriented pointed limit \((X,g)\) of
\(\widehat g_i=\lambda_i^{-2}g_i\), with smooth convergence under
orientation-preserving embeddings on neighborhoods of every fixed
compact truncation for all sufficiently large \(i\). Suppose the
entire exterior of some compact set in \(X\) consists of \(k\ge1\)
product ALE ends \((\mathbb R^4\setminus B)/\Gamma_j\), where
\(\Gamma_j\subset SO(4)\) is finite and acts freely on \(S^3\).
Write \(q_j=|\Gamma_j|\) and \(q_{\max}=\max_j q_j\). In the
lifted end coordinates assume
\(\partial^\beta(g_{ab}-\delta_{ab})=O(r^{-\alpha_j-|\beta|})\)
for \(\alpha_j>0\) and \(0\le|\beta|\le2\).
These are the decay orders used below. [Tian–Viaclovsky, (1.3)–(1.4)][tv]

For any common subsequence with \(e=\lim E_i\) and \(y=\lim Y_i\),
the claimed inequality holds in the following chain:
\[
\begin{aligned}
 (8\pi^2-e)+\frac{E_+^X+E_-^X}{2}
        +4\pi^2\Bigl(k-2+\sum_{j=1}^k q_j^{-1}\Bigr)
  &\le \frac{y^2}{48}\\
  &\le\frac{8\pi^2}{q_{\max}} .
\end{aligned}
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(E_\pm^X=\int_X |W_g^\pm|_{\rm op,HS}^2\,dV_g\).
Consequently \(k=1\). This conclusion concerns the complete
supplied smooth limit. It requires no Bach-flat equation.

## Topology of the entire embedded truncation

Choose a sufficiently large compact truncation \(A=X_R\)
bounded by all \(k\) oriented links \(L_j=S^3/\Gamma_j\). It is
connected and is a deformation retract of \(X\) by collapsing
each end along its product coordinate. A single smooth embedding
of \(A\) into \(M\) suffices for the following topology test.
Identify \(A\) with its image and let \(C=\overline{M\setminus A}\).
Use collar thickenings so that Mayer–Vietoris has the intersection
\(L=\bigsqcup_j L_j\) up to homotopy. Each link is a rational
homology three-sphere, using transfer through its finite oriented
cover by \(S^3\). All groups in this paragraph have rational
coefficients. Because \(H_1(M)=0\) and \(M\) is connected, the
degree-zero part is
\[
 0\longrightarrow\mathbb Q^k
      \longrightarrow\mathbb Q\oplus H_0(C)
      \longrightarrow\mathbb Q\longrightarrow0 .
\]
Thus \(C\) has \(k\) components. Each meets at least one link
since a closed component without boundary would be open and
closed in \(M\). Every link meets just one complementary component
by its connected collar. Therefore each complementary component
meets exactly one link, and each link separates that component
from the connected remainder. Since \(H_1(L)=H_2(L)=0\) and
\(H_1(M)=H_2(M)=0\), Mayer–Vietoris yields
\(H_d(A)\oplus H_d(C)=0\) for \(d=1,2\).
[Hatcher, §2.2, pp. 149–150][hatcher]

Poincaré–Lefschetz duality gives \(H_3(A,\partial A)=H^1(A)=0\)
and \(H_4(A)=H^0(A,\partial A)=0\), with the equalities
understood as natural isomorphisms. The boundary map from
\(H_4(A,\partial A)=\mathbb Q\) into \(H_3(\partial A)=\mathbb Q^k\)
sends its relative fundamental class to the sum of the induced
oriented boundary classes. The exact sequence of the pair gives
\(b_3(A)=k-1\). It follows that \(\chi(X)=\chi(A)=2-k\)
and \(\tau(X)=\tau(A)=0\), where the signature is that of the
pairing on the image of compactly supported degree-two
cohomology in ordinary degree-two cohomology. That image
vanishes because \(H^2(X;\mathbb R)=0\). The calculation is
rational and allows integral torsion. [Hatcher, Theorem 3.43,
p. 254][hatcher] · [E166 full-core scope][e166]

## Source identities and the limit on compact sets

With \(T_i=\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}-Y_i g_i/4\), closed
Chern–Gauss–Bonnet gives
\[
16\pi^2=2E_i-\tfrac12\int_M |T_i|^2\,dV_{g_i}
                 +Y_i^2/24,
\qquad
\frac{Y_i^2}{48}=(8\pi^2-E_i)+\frac14\int_M |T_i|^2\,dV_{g_i}.
\tag{2}
\]
The Yamabe minimizer has constant scalar curvature \(Y_i\) at
volume one. The scalar and Ricci coefficients in (2) use the
ordinary norms on scalars and symmetric two-tensors. The four-index
Weyl coefficient is \(1/4\), which converts to the sum of the two
operator energies. Also \(Y_i\le Y_{\rm rd}=8\sqrt6\,\pi\),
the value on the unit round sphere, and \(\delta>0\) in (2)
gives \(Y_i\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\). Thus the limits \(e,y\) can be
taken on a common further subsequence of any subsequence
realizing the stated smooth limit. [CGY, (0.4) and Remark 2,
p. 107][cgy] · [Schoen, §3, Theorem 3 and proof][schoen] ·
[Lee–Parker, §3][lp] · [E36, (1)–(3)][e36]

Under this constant metric rescaling the \(L^2\) integrals
of either Weyl block and of trace-free Ricci are unchanged.
The scalar curvature of \(\widehat g_i\) is \(\lambda_i^2Y_i\),
so \(R_g=0\) by smooth convergence. For each fixed \(R\),
pull back \(\widehat g_i\) to a neighborhood of \(X_R\).
Taking \(i\to\infty\) with \(R\) fixed gives convergence of the
curvature integrals on \(X_R\), which are bounded by their
whole-source integrals by nonnegativity and the embedding.
Then \(R\to\infty\) by monotone convergence to obtain
\[
 E_\pm^X\le e,\qquad
 \frac14\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_g|^2\,dV_g
 \le \frac{y^2}{48}-(8\pi^2-e).
\tag{3}
\]
In particular the full Ricci integral is controlled for this
scalar-flat limit. Equation (2) fixes the limit of the
whole-source \(T_i\) integrals along the same subsequence.
Energy loss away from the compared compact regions is permitted.

## An escaping annular test on each end

For any nonzero \(\phi\in C_c^\infty(X)\) use its pushforward in
the Yamabe quotient of \(\widehat g_i\), extending by zero.
The comparison embedding is defined on a neighborhood of a
compact truncation containing the support in its interior.
The Yamabe constant is unchanged by scale and the scalar term
of the quotient is \(\lambda_i^2Y_i\int\phi^2\) with the
pulled-back metric. Taking \(i\to\infty\) for this fixed test
gives
\[
 y\le Y_c(X,g):=\inf_{0\ne\phi\in C_c^\infty(X)}
         \frac{6\int_X |d\phi|_g^2\,dV_g}
              {(\int_X|\phi|^4\,dV_g)^{1/2}} .
\tag{4}
\]
The quotient applied to arbitrary real functions is the same
as the positive Yamabe infimum on the source by using absolute
values and positive smooth approximation. The dimension-four
coefficient is \(6\). [Lee–Parker, §1, (1.3)–(1.5)][lp]

On the Euclidean cover take \(U(x)=(1+|x|^2)^{-1}\) and smooth
radial cutoffs supported in \(\epsilon<|x|<2L\), equal to one
on \(2\epsilon\le |x|\le L\), with gradient bounds of order
\(\epsilon^{-1}\) and \(L^{-1}\) on the respective transitions.
The cutoff profiles \(U_{\epsilon,L}\) tend to \(U\) in
\(\dot H^1\) and \(L^4\) as \(\epsilon\downarrow0\),
\(L\uparrow\infty\). Squared Dirichlet errors from the cutoff
gradients are \(O(\epsilon^2)\) at the inner annulus and
\(O(L^{-2})\) at the outer annulus. The remaining errors follow
from integrability of \(dU\) and \(U^4\). Directly
\(\int U^4=\pi^2/6\) and \(\int |dU|^2=4\pi^2/3\), giving
quotient \(8\sqrt6\,\pi\), sharp by Talenti's Euclidean theorem
for dimension \(m=4\) and exponent \(p=2\).
[Talenti, pp. 353–354, (1)–(3)][talenti] ·
[Lee–Parker, §3, Theorem 3.3][lp]

Fix \((\epsilon,L)\) and use \(U_{\epsilon,L}(x/R)\) on end
\(j\), extended by zero. It descends because it is radial, and its
support escapes along that end as \(R\to\infty\). In the dilated
coordinates the end metrics \(R^{-2}D_R^*g\), \(D_R(x)=Rx\),
converge uniformly to Euclidean on the fixed annulus. The
quotient is invariant under this uniform metric scaling in
dimension four. On the quotient annulus the numerator integral
is \(1/q_j\) times the cover integral and so is the integral
inside the square root in the denominator. Therefore the
quotient for this sequence tends to \(q_j^{-1/2}\) times the
Euclidean quotient of \(U_{\epsilon,L}\). After this limit take
the cutoff profile to \(U\). Equivalently, given a sequence
of approximating profiles one can take \(R\) large enough for
both the quotient error and the inverse inner support radius
to tend to zero. Each such test is already admissible in (4)
on the entire \(X\). This proves
\[
 y\le Y_c(X,g)\le\frac{Y_{\rm rd}}{\sqrt{q_j}}
 \quad\text{for every }j.
\tag{5}
\]
The \(q_j^{-1/2}\) factor is exact for the end-at-infinity
constant. Any end-supported function lifts to a
\(\Gamma_j\)-invariant compact Euclidean test extended by zero.
Outside coordinate radius \(R\), the lifted Dirichlet and \(L^4\)
integrals are uniformly within multiplicative \(1+O(R^{-\alpha_j})\)
of the Euclidean ones, and \(R_g=0\). The Euclidean sharp lower
bound then gives the matching lower limit for the infimum
over tests supported outside radius \(R\) in that end.
The whole-manifold \(Y_c\) can be smaller.

## Chern–Gauss–Bonnet and the end count

The curvature decays as \(O(r^{-2-\alpha_j})\) on each end,
and the four-dimensional volume element is comparable with
the Euclidean one there. Consequently all quadratic curvature
integrals in Chern–Gauss–Bonnet converge absolutely. Apply the
boundary formula on \(X_R\) with outward normals pointing
toward increasing radius. On each link the rescaled annulus
has metric converging in \(C^2\) to the Euclidean annulus.
The Euler boundary integral is invariant under uniform metric
scaling and continuous in these metric jets, as its terms
use the curvature and second fundamental form. Its limit is
the contribution of the Euclidean unit sphere with outward
normal divided by \(q_j\). The sign and value in the \(8\pi^2\)
normalization are \(+8\pi^2/q_j\), since the unit Euclidean
ball has Euler characteristic one and zero Euler interior
density. This yields, with \(R_g=0\),
\[
8\pi^2\Bigl(\chi(X)-\sum_j q_j^{-1}\Bigr)
    =E_+^X+E_-^X-\frac12\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_g|^2\,dV_g .
\tag{6}
\]
This is also the scalar-flat case of Tian–Viaclovsky (7.2),
with its operator Weyl norm. [Kasue, (3.5) and boundary
integrands, p. 44][kasue] · [Tian–Viaclovsky, §7, (7.2)][tv]

Insert \(\chi(X)=2-k\) into (6) and use (3) to get the first
inequality in (1). For the second, use (5) with an end of order
\(q_{\max}\) and \(y>0\), together with \(Y_{\rm rd}^2=384\pi^2\).
For \(k\ge2\), the left side of (1) minus its upper bound is at
least
\[
 \delta +4\pi^2\bigl(k-2+k/q_{\max}\bigr)
             -8\pi^2/q_{\max}
   =\delta+4\pi^2(k-2)(1+q_{\max}^{-1})>0 .
\tag{7}
\]
Here \(E_+^X+E_-^X\ge0\) and every \(q_j^{-1}\ge q_{\max}^{-1}\).
This is the contradiction. It is insensitive to cancellations
among end contributions in a signature formula. The topological
signature vanishes by the earlier argument, and no equality of
the two limiting Weyl energies is used.

## Schwarzschild calibration at the algebraic wall

For \(a>0\), \(g=(1+a/r^2)^2g_{\rm Eucl}\) on
\(\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\}\) is the four-dimensional Riemannian
Schwarzschild example in Tian–Viaclovsky after Theorem 1.3,
with their \(m=3a\). The positive conformal multiplier
\(u=1+a/r^2\) is Euclidean harmonic off zero, so the metric
is scalar-flat and conformally flat. The inversion
\(x\mapsto a x/|x|^2\) exchanges the two asymptotically
Euclidean ends, both of positive order two. For a direct
curvature check set \(s=r-a/r\), whose derivative is \(u\).
Then \(g=ds^2+(s^2+4a)g_{S^3}\), \(s\in\mathbb R\), giving
completeness. Write \(f(s)=\sqrt{s^2+4a}\). The radial and
tangential sectional curvatures are \(-f''/f=-4a/f^4\) and
\((1-(f')^2)/f^2=4a/f^4\). Thus the Ricci eigenvalues are
\(-12a/f^4\) and \(4a/f^4\) with multiplicity three. With
unit sphere area \(2\pi^2\) in dimension three,
\[
 \int_X|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\,dV
   =384\pi^2a^2\int_{\mathbb R}(s^2+4a)^{-5/2}\,ds
   =32\pi^2 .
\]
This matches (6) with \(\chi(X)=0\), \(k=2\),
\(q_1=q_2=1\), and \(E_\pm^X=0\).
On compact tests the energy identity
\(6\int |d\phi|_g^2\,dV_g=6\int|d(u\phi)|_{\rm Eucl}^2\,dx\)
follows by integration by parts using harmonic \(u\), and the
\(L^4\) integrals coincide. Multiplication by \(u\) bijects the
compact tests on the punctured space. The Euclidean sharp lower
bound and the annular approximations imply \(Y_c=Y_{\rm rd}\).
The full core is a spherical annulus \(S^3\times[0,1]\), which
embeds in standard smooth \(S^4\). [Tian–Viaclovsky, remark
after Theorem 1.3][tv] · [Talenti, (1)–(3)][talenti]

At \(e=8\pi^2\) these limit-side values give equality between
the two ends of (1). More generally, if the argument is run
with \(e\le8\pi^2\) and \(y>0\) at the wall, any surviving
two-end case would need \(e=8\pi^2\), equal end orders, zero
Weyl energy on \(X\), and equality in the remaining Ricci
and Yamabe bounds. The Schwarzschild calibration only checks
the constants and the feasibility of the limit-side geometry.
It does not supply a sequence of closed unit-volume Yamabe
minimizers realizing these wall equalities.

## Quantifiers and verified evidence

One embedded full truncation suffices for the topology. To
compare the full curvature integrals or test arbitrarily far
along the ends, the embeddings and convergence are required
on each fixed compact truncation, with the source index tending
to infinity before removing the truncation. No uniform
simultaneous convergence in the source index and the end radius
is used. An arbitrary embedded piece need not carry the
topology of the full core or the relevant tests. The derived
one-end conclusion does not extract a smooth limit, prove ALE
geometry or completeness from small total Bach norm, or
exclude several distinct one-ended rescalings in one sequence.
The one remaining end group and compactification are separate
packets. [Owning register][register] · [B29][b29]
