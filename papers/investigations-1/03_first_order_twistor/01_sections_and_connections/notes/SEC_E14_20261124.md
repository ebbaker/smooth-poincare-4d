## Metric and corrected target

On \(I\times T^3\) with \(I=[-1,1]\), use the positive-definite axial
family from E13 and orient it by \(ds\wedge dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3\):

\[
 g_a=ds^2+e^{2a(s)}(dx_1^2+dx_2^2)+e^{-4a(s)}dx_3^2,\qquad
 y=e^{-2a}.
\]

The pointwise volume is \(ds\,dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3\). In the B01 transport
to the reference \(g_f\), the Weyl block \(W^+_{g_a}\) is
\(w\,\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)\), where \(4w=y''/y\). This is the
curvature-operator convention. Let \(f\) be any nonconstant even
smooth function supported in the interior of \(I\). Set \(y_0=e^{-2f}\)
and \(V_0=y_0''/y_0=4w_0\). Fix any smooth even \(p\ge0\) supported
in the interior, with \(p\not\equiv0\). The desired potential is
\(4w=\lambda V_0-\beta p\). The union of the supports of \(f,p\)
leaves a common fixed pair of open flat end collars. [B01 chart][b01] ·
[E13 Weyl and ground-form identities][e13] · [B12 source choice][b12]

## Unique correction of the Neumann ground eigenvalue

For real \(\beta\), let \(\mu(\lambda,\beta)\) denote the lowest
Neumann eigenvalue of \(P_{\lambda,\beta}=-d^2/ds^2+\lambda V_0-\beta p\).
Use its variational characterization

\[
 \mu(\lambda,\beta)=
 \inf_{\substack{u\in H^1(I)\\\|u\|_{L^2(I)}=1}}
 \int_I\left(|u'|^2+(\lambda V_0-\beta p)|u|^2\right)\,ds .
\]

The bounded potential gives an \(H^1\) bound for a minimizing sequence.
Compactness in \(L^2\) and weak lower semicontinuity of the derivative
norm give a minimizer. Taking the absolute value yields a nonnegative
minimizer. The variational equation is the eigenvalue ODE with Neumann
ends, and the smooth potential makes the solution smooth. A zero of
this nonnegative eigenfunction would have zero derivative, by the
minimum condition inside or the Neumann condition at an endpoint.
Uniqueness for the initial-value problem would make the function
identically zero. Hence it is positive. The space of solutions of the
ODE satisfying the left Neumann condition is one-dimensional at each
eigenvalue, giving simplicity.

The Rayleigh quotient gives continuity in \((\lambda,\beta)\) by the
supremum-norm change of the potential. If \(\beta_2>\beta_1\), test the
form at \(\beta_2\) on a normalized positive minimizer \(u_1\) at
\(\beta_1\). Then

\[
 \mu(\lambda,\beta_2)
 \le \mu(\lambda,\beta_1)
      -(\beta_2-\beta_1)\int_I p\,u_1^2\,ds
 <\mu(\lambda,\beta_1).
\]

A fixed nonzero test function supported where \(p>0\) shows that
\(\mu(\lambda,\beta)\) tends to \(-\infty\) as \(\beta\to+\infty\).
At \(\beta=0\), the E13 forms at \(\lambda=0,1\) are nonnegative with
null lines \(\mathbb R\) and \(\mathbb R y_0\), respectively. At
\(0<\lambda<1\) their convex combination is positive on every nonzero
function. Since the ground eigenvalue is attained, this gives
\(\mu(\lambda,0)>0\) in the interior and \(\mu(0,0)=\mu(1,0)=0\).
Thus for every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\) there is a unique real
\(\beta(\lambda)\) with ground eigenvalue zero, and

\[
 \beta(0)=\beta(1)=0,\qquad
 \beta(\lambda)>0\quad (0<\lambda<1).
\]

This proof places no smallness condition on \(f\).

## Smooth dependence and matching at both ends

At the corrected potential, reflect the positive eigenfunction by
\(s\mapsto-s\). The reflected function is another ground
eigenfunction because \(V_0,p\) are even and the Neumann ends are
symmetric. Simplicity and positivity make it identical after
\(L^2\)-normalization. The eigenfunction is even. Since the eigenvalue
and the potential both vanish on the two end collars, the eigenfunction
is affine there. The Neumann data make it constant on each, and
reflection makes both constants equal. Normalize by the common
positive constant to obtain \(y_\lambda=1\) near both ends.

To verify smooth parameter dependence, solve the initial-value problem
\(P_{\lambda,\beta}Y=0\), \(Y(-1)=1,\ Y'(-1)=0\), and set
\(G(\lambda,\beta)=Y'(1)\). Solutions depend smoothly on \(s,\lambda,\beta\).
At the desired root \(Y=y_\lambda\), let \(Z=\partial_\beta Y\).
Then \(P_{\lambda,\beta}Z=pY\) with \(Z(-1)=Z'(-1)=0\). Integration
against \(Y\) gives

\[
 \int_I pY^2\,ds
 =[ZY'-YZ']_{-1}^{1}
 =-Y(1)\,\partial_\beta G(\lambda,\beta).
\]

Since \(Y>0\), this derivative is strictly negative. The implicit
function theorem gives a smooth root locally at every parameter,
including the two endpoints. The local roots retain positive
solutions by continuity on \(I\). A positive Neumann solution at
zero eigenvalue is a ground eigenfunction, since its inner product
with the positive ground eigenfunction is nonzero. The local roots
therefore agree with the unique ground-state correction. Consequently
\((\lambda,s)\mapsto y_\lambda(s)\) is smooth up to both parameter
ends and on the spatial collar.

Take \(a_\lambda=-\tfrac12\log y_\lambda\). This produces an actual
smooth positive-definite metric path with pointwise fixed volume.
Its end collars are the fixed product metrics and have all
derivatives matching to the same exterior as in E13.
At \(\lambda=0\), \(y_\lambda=1\) everywhere and \(g_{a_\lambda}\) is
flat. At \(\lambda=1\), the normalized positive solution is \(y_0\)
and \(g_{a_\lambda}=g_f\). For interior \(\lambda\), the source is the
modified Weyl block

\[
 W^+_{g_{a_\lambda}}\ \longleftrightarrow\
 \left(\lambda w_0-\frac{\beta(\lambda)}4p\right)
             \operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)
\]

in the fixed reference frame. The correction vanishes at both
parameter endpoints. The collar energy is
\(6\,\operatorname{Vol}(T^3)\int_I(\lambda w_0-\beta(\lambda)p/4)^2ds\),
where \(\operatorname{Vol}(T^3)\) is computed with the coordinate
volume \(dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3\). Thus the energy formula includes the
correction at intermediate parameters. The construction establishes
a lift in this symmetric collar with the same flat endpoint target.
Extension to unrestricted data or a global modified-source construction
requires separate arguments. [B12 decision][b12]

## Optional small-amplitude coefficient

Keep \(p\) fixed and write \(f=\varepsilon\phi\) for a fixed nonconstant
even smooth interior bump \(\phi\). Then
\(V_0=-2\varepsilon\phi''+4\varepsilon^2(\phi')^2\) exactly.
Extend \(\beta\) and the normalized \(Y\) smoothly in
\((\lambda,\varepsilon)\) by the same shooting argument starting at
\(\varepsilon=0,\ \beta=0,\ Y=1\). There
\(\partial_\beta G=-\int_I p<0\) for every \(\lambda\in[0,1]\).
Compactness of the parameter interval and uniqueness of the positive
root yield a common \(\varepsilon\)-neighborhood. Expand
\(\beta=\varepsilon b_1+\varepsilon^2 b_2+O(|\varepsilon|^3)\) and
\(Y=1+\varepsilon u_1+\varepsilon^2u_2+O(|\varepsilon|^3)\).
At order \(\varepsilon\), the equation is
\(-u_1''-2\lambda\phi''-b_1p=0\). Integrating and using the two
Neumann conditions gives \(b_1=0\). The normalized left data give
\(u_1=-2\lambda\phi\). At order \(\varepsilon^2\), integration
again removes \(u_2''\) and yields

\[
 b_2\int_I p\,ds
  =\int_I\left(4\lambda(\phi')^2-2\lambda\phi''u_1\right)\,ds
  =4\lambda(1-\lambda)\int_I(\phi')^2\,ds .
\]

The Taylor remainder is \(O(|\varepsilon|^3)\) in
\(C^1([0,1]_\lambda)\), uniformly for these fixed \(\phi,p\), by the
joint smooth dependence. It vanishes at \(\lambda=0,1\), as does the
quadratic term. Bounding by its derivative times the distance to
the nearer endpoint sharpens the expansion to

\[
 \beta(\lambda,\varepsilon)=
 4\varepsilon^2\lambda(1-\lambda)
     \frac{\int_I(\phi')^2\,ds}{\int_I p\,ds}
 +O\!\left(|\varepsilon|^3\lambda(1-\lambda)\right)
 \qquad (0\le\lambda\le1).
\]
