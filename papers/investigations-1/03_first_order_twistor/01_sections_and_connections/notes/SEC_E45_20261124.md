## Audited statement

Take any [E27][e27] data \(M,r,O,K\). Thus \(M\) is a smooth closed
connected oriented homotopy four-sphere, \(r\) is smooth and positive
definite, and an oriented isometry identifies the coordinate ball
\(O\) with a round patch. The protected smooth closed subball
\(K\) lies compactly in \(O\). Fix \(\nu=dV_r\), and let
\[
\mathscr M_{\nu,K}=\{q\in C^\infty(S^2T^*M):q>0,\ dV_q=\nu,\ q|_K=r|_K\}.
\]
Use the relative \(C^\infty\) topology. For each \(q\) take the full
trace-free logarithmic chart \(g=q(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\),
\(h^\sharp=q^{-1}h\). Its transported positive Weyl block \(F_q(h)\)
is a section of \(B_q=S^2_0\Lambda_q^+\) with the real
curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. The transport is induced
by the positive symmetric tangent isometry from \(q\) to \(g\).
Write \(L_q=DF_q(0)\) with formal adjoint using \(q,\nu\).
Here ASD means \(W^+=0\). [E02, (5)][e02] · [E03, (1)–(4)][e03]

**The set of \(q\in\mathscr M_{\nu,K}\) with \(\ker L_q^*=0\) is
relatively open and dense.** At each such \(q\), for every integer
\(s\ge5\), a local slice in \(H^{s+2}(S^2_{0,q}T^*M)\) realizes
every sufficiently small \(f\in H^s(B_q)\) by the exact equation
\(F_q(h)=W_q^++f\). Smooth forcing gives a smooth metric at the
same low-norm threshold. At each obstructed starting reference
a smooth one-parameter reference change supported in a fixed
compact subset of \(M\setminus K\) gives regular centers for
all sufficiently small nonzero parameters. [E40][e40] · [E41][e41]

## The exterior restriction for a closed protected ball

At a fixed \(q\) write \(V_q=S^2_{0,q}T^*M\), \(L=L_q\),
\(P=LL^*\), and \(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\). The order-two Weyl symbol
on the pointwise TT fiber is \(-|\xi|_q^2/4\) times the isometric
identification of the spatial trace-free block with \(B_q\),
under the real convention \(\partial\mapsto\xi\). In normal
coordinates with \(\xi=|\xi|_q e^4\), the leading curvature-operator
entries of a TT tensor are \(-|\xi|_q^2 h_{ij}/2\) in the \(4i,4j\)
block. Projection to the normalized positive two-form frame
gives another factor \(1/2\). The scalar symbol is zero, and
the trace-free Ricci contribution is off diagonal in the Hodge
splitting. The TT orthogonal complement is the image of the
symbol of the trace-free Lie derivative \(\mathcal K_q\). It is
annihilated by \(\sigma(L)\) since the composition \(L\mathcal K_q\)
has order at most one by (2) below. Hence \(P\) has symbol
\(|\xi|_q^4 I/16\). Elliptic regularity and Sobolev Fredholmness
on the closed manifold give smooth finite-dimensional
\(\ker P=\mathcal C\) and the generalized inverse
\(G_P:H^s\to H^{s+4}\cap\mathcal C^\perp\) at all grades used
here, including \(s=0\). Self-adjointness gives
\(PG_P=I-\Pi_{\mathcal C}\). In fact a distributional range
annihilator solves \(P^*Z=0\) and is smooth, and \(\ker P=\ker L^*\)
by the adjoint pairing. [E02, §2][e02] · [E03, (3)–(4)][e03] ·
[Melrose, (1)–(2)][melrose]

If \(q=r\) near \(K\), the [E27][e27] transplant applies on that
round neighborhood. For the entire relative class, take
\(Z\in\mathcal C\) vanishing on \(M\setminus K\). It is smooth
and every jet vanishes along \(\partial K\) by continuity from
the open exterior. On \(O\) identify the \(q,r\) bundles by
their smooth symmetric isometry, which is the identity on
\(K\). Carry the resulting section supported in \(K\Subset O\)
by the original oriented round marking to \(S^4\) and extend
by zero. The round adjoint equation holds on the image of
\(\operatorname{int}K\) because \(q=r\) on that open set, and
outside the image of \(K\) because the section vanishes there.
Smoothness gives the equation on the image of \(\partial K\).
At round \(W^+=0\), so differentiating the algebraic comparison
contributes zero, and the adjoint agrees with that of the ASD
complex under the bundle identification and constant pairing
normalization. Gover and Gursky identify \(H^2_{\rm ASD}=\ker D^*\)
in §1.1, pp. 295–296, and record \(H^2_{\rm ASD}(S^4,g_c)=0\)
after Conjecture 1.4. Hence \(Z=0\). The ASD theorem is applied
to the round transplant. [E02, §5][e02] · [Gover–Gursky][gg]

This boundary argument gives injective restriction to the exterior
for every \(q\) in the marked class. If a round neighborhood
is supplied, any fixed larger protected closed subball compactly
inside it can be used in place of \(K\) at that starting metric.

## E41 rank calculation

Fix \(q\) and let \(m=\dim\mathcal C>0\). Use a real
\(L^2(q,\nu)\)-orthonormal basis \(Z_i\) at this reference.
Exterior point evaluations span \(\mathcal C^*\) by injective
restriction. Choose \(m\) independent evaluations and move any
coincident centers slightly with smooth extensions of their
polarizations. Continuity preserves the nonzero determinant.
Take disjoint relatively compact exterior balls \(U_j\) at
these centers, smooth target sections \(Y_j\) extending the
polarizations, and real \(b_j=d_j\in C_c^\infty(U_j)\) with
\(\int b_jd_j\,\nu=1\). Shrinking the balls makes the normalized
averages \(D_{ij}=\int b_jd_j\langle Z_i,Y_j\rangle\,\nu\)
arbitrarily close to the independent evaluation matrix.

Choose coordinate phases \(\phi_j\) with \(d\phi_j\ne0\).
Let \(H_j\) be the smooth TT inverse image of \(Y_j\) under
\(-\sigma_{d\phi_j}(L)\). With zero extension define
\[
X_{j,N}=N^{-1}b_j\sin(N\phi_j)\frac{\nabla\phi_j}{|d\phi_j|_q^2},
\qquad
v_N=N^{-2}\sum_jd_j\cos(N\phi_j)H_j,\qquad w_N=Lv_N .
\tag{1}
\]
The leading derivative of \(X_{j,N}\) is the symmetric rank-one
endomorphism \(b_j\cos(N\phi_j)\nabla\phi_j\otimes
d\phi_j/|d\phi_j|_q^2\). Thus \(X_{j,N}=O(N^{-1})\),
\(\nabla X_{j,N}=O(1)\), \(\operatorname{skew}\nabla X_{j,N}
=O(N^{-1})\), and \(\operatorname{div}_\nu X_{j,N}=
b_j\cos(N\phi_j)+O(N^{-1})\) in \(C^0\).
Also \(\|v_N\|_{C^p}=O(N^{p-2})\) for \(0\le p\le2\).
Two derivatives of the cosine give minus the real principal
symbol, so \(w_N=d_j\cos(N\phi_j)Y_j+O(N^{-1})\) on each
ball in \(C^0\). This fixes the sign of the \(Y_j\) term.

For each fixed \(N\) compare the operators at
\(q_a=q(e^{a v_N^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\) via the symmetric
isometry \(S_a=e^{-a v_N^\sharp/2}\) and its induced covariant
tensor maps. Denote the compared Weyl linearization, Weyl
field, target connection, infinitesimal skew pullback, and
trace-free Lie derivative by \(A_a,W_a,D^a,\Theta_X^a,\mathcal K_aX\).
The normalized pullback by the flow \(\varphi_\epsilon\) of \(X\)
multiplies \(\varphi_\epsilon^*q_a\) by
\(\rho=(\nu/\varphi_\epsilon^*\nu)^{1/2}\). The symmetric frame
subtracts the symmetric trace-free part of \(\nabla X\), and
\(\rho^{1/2}\) subtracts its scalar part. The remaining skew
part induces \(\Theta_X^a\). The Weyl tensor in covariant form
scales by \(\rho\), so its endomorphism on two-forms scales
by \(\rho^{-1}\) with infinitesimal contribution
\(+\tfrac12\operatorname{div}_\nu X\). Therefore
\[
A_a\mathcal K_aX=D_X^a W_a+[\Theta_X^a,W_a]
                   +\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}_\nu X)W_a .
\tag{2}
\]
[Fefferman–Graham, arXiv v2, §6 after (6.7)][fg] ·
[E03, (2)][e03] · [Manuscript, `lift:gauge`][ms]

Differentiate (2) at zero, where \(W'_0=w_N\). The moving
left term \(L\dot{\mathcal K}X\) pairs to zero with any
\(Z\in\mathcal C\). The connection variation \(\dot D_X W\)
is zeroth order in \(W\), with coefficient bounded by
\(C|X|(|v_N|+|\nabla v_N|)\). The compared skew term
\(\dot\Theta_X\) is bounded by
\(C(|v_N||\nabla X|+(|v_N|+|\nabla v_N|)|X|)\), as is
\(\dot{\mathcal K}X\) before applying \(L\). These bounds use
the first jet in the connection and algebraic frame and trace
comparisons. Both dotted terms on the right of (2) are
\(O(N^{-2})\) for (1). Also \([\Theta_X^0,w_N]=O(N^{-1})\).
The fixed density removes any differentiated divergence term.
Metric compatibility and integration by parts give exactly
\[
\int_M\langle Z,\nabla_X w_N\rangle\,\nu
=-\int_M(\operatorname{div}_\nu X)\langle Z,w_N\rangle\,\nu
 -\int_M\langle\nabla_XZ,w_N\rangle\,\nu .
\tag{3}
\]
The last integral is \(O_Z(N^{-1})\), and the identity avoids
differentiation of the \(C^0\) error in \(w_N\). Combining the
transport term with the \(+1/2\) divergence term and using
disjoint supports gives
\[
\langle Z_i,A'_{0,N}\mathcal K_q X_{j,N}\rangle
=-\tfrac12\int b_jd_j\cos^2(N\phi_j)\langle Z_i,Y_j\rangle\,\nu
  +O(N^{-1})\ \longrightarrow\ -D_{ij}/4 .
\tag{4}
\]
The averaging uses \(\cos^2(N\phi)=(1+\cos(2N\phi))/2\),
followed by integration by parts with \(\nabla\phi_j/|d\phi_j|_q^2\)
on the compactly supported amplitude. Separately the transport
and half-divergence limits have coefficients \(-1/2\) and
\(+1/4\) in the \(Y_j\) polarization. In terms of the unscaled
TT spatial block the integrand factor of the combined limit is
\(-|d\phi_j|_q^2/16\).

Set \(Q=L^*G_P\) and
\(k_{j,N}=\mathcal K_qX_{j,N}-QL\mathcal K_qX_{j,N}\).
At \(a=0\), (2) gives \(L\mathcal K_qX_{j,N}=
\tfrac12 b_j\cos(N\phi_j)W_q^++O(N^{-1})\) in \(L^2\).
It tends weakly to zero by the same nonstationary phase argument
against smooth tests, then uniform boundedness and density.
The map \(Q:L^2(B_q)\to H^2(V_q)\) is bounded and is compact
into \(L^2(V_q)\) by Rellich applied in a finite bundle atlas.
Hence the correction tends strongly to zero in \(L^2\).
Elliptic regularity makes it smooth. Since
\(LQ=I-\Pi_{\mathcal C}\) and \(L\mathcal K_q X_{j,N}\perp
\mathcal C\), we have \(Lk_{j,N}=0\) exactly.
[Melrose, (1)–(2)][melrose] ·
[Dyatlov, 19 November 2026, §16.2, Theorem 16.10][dyatlov]

The nonlocal correction can be tested using the structure of
the mixed derivative. In fixed local frames the coordinate
curvature expression is \(C(g)\partial^2g+
D(g)(\partial g,\partial g)\) with smooth algebraic dependence
on \(g\). The Weyl and chiral projections and the substitution
\(g=q(e^{a v_N^\sharp/2}e^{h^\sharp}e^{a v_N^\sharp/2}\cdot,\cdot)\)
are pointwise in the metric and logarithm. In the linearization
in \(h\) at zero the coefficient at input order \(\ell\) uses
at most \(2-\ell\) derivatives of the moving reference and
its algebraic comparisons. Its \(a\)-derivative at zero has
at most \(2-\ell\) derivatives of \(v_N\), linearly. Forming
the fixed formal adjoint puts at most \(\ell\) additional
derivatives on that coefficient times the fixed smooth test
and pairings. Thus \(\|(A'_{0,N})^* Z\|_{L^2}\le
C_Z\|v_N\|_{C^2}=O_Z(1)\). Pairing against the strongly
convergent correction gives \(o(1)\), even though the correction
can have global support. The operator order in \(h\) alone
would not establish this derivative bound. The matrix in (4)
with inputs \(k_{j,N}\) therefore tends to \(-D/4\).
Fix one sufficiently large finite \(N\). The single smooth
\(v=v_N\) and family \(k_j=k_{j,N}\) now give the invertible
matrix \(\mathsf M_{ij}=\langle Z_i,A'_0 k_j\rangle\).
For \(m=0\) the rank step is empty. [E41, (6)–(13)][e41]

## E40 inverse, openness and density

Fix an integer \(s\ge5\) and put \(X=H^{s+2}(V_q)\),
\(Y=H^s(B_q)\), \(U=H^{s+4}(B_q)\cap\mathcal C^\perp\),
and \(b(f)_i=\langle Z_i,f\rangle\). The comparisons are
unitary in \(L^2\) because the fiber isometries preserve
the fixed pairings and the density is fixed. The two-jet
dependence gives smoothness of \(a\mapsto A_a:X\to Y\)
in operator norm. For \(\mathcal Jc=\sum_j c_j k_j\), the
quotient \(D_a=A_a\mathcal J/a=\int_0^1 A'_{\theta a}\mathcal J\,d\theta\)
extends smoothly to zero because \(L\mathcal J=0\).
On \(U\times\mathbb R^m\) put \(T_a(u,c)=A_aL^*u+D_ac\).
At zero it is invertible with inverse
\(c=\mathsf M^{-1}b(f),\ u=G_P(f-D_0c)\), since \(bP=0\)
and \(bD_0=\mathsf M\). For \(m=0\) use \(P\) on the
whole potential space. The perturbation \(T_a-T_0=O(a)\)
in operator norm gives a uniformly bounded \(T_a^{-1}\).
For \(a\ne0\) the right inverse of \(A_a\) is
\(Q_a f=L^*u+a^{-1}\mathcal Jc\), with \((u,c)=T_a^{-1}f\).
It gains two Sobolev orders and has norm \(O(|a|^{-1})\)
for \(m>0\) and \(O(1)\) for \(m=0\).
Surjectivity in any one grade implies \(\ker L_{q_a}^*=0\)
and hence surjectivity in all grades via the normal operator
argument. [E40, (3)–(5)][e40]

The parameter power is sharp for the full operator norm
when \(m>0\). Every solution of \(A_a h=Z_1\) has
\(1=|b((A_a-L)h)|\le C|a|\|h\|_X\). This bounds every bounded
right inverse from below by a positive constant times
\(|a|^{-1}\) using the fixed norm of \(Z_1\) in \(Y\).
For a radial tangent the lower bound uses \(b(W_a)\),
and gives the matching power if \(b(W_q^+)\ne0\).
[E40, (6)][e40]

For openness at any regular \(q\), freeze
\(R=L_q^*(L_qL_q^*)^{-1}\) as its bounded right inverse.
Compare \(L_{q'}\) to the fixed bundles at \(q\) by symmetric
isometries, yielding \(A(q')\) with \(A(q)=L_q\).
The coefficient dependence gives \(A(q')\to L_q\) in
\(\mathcal L(X,Y)\) for \(q'\to q\) in \(C^{s+2}\), hence
in \(C^\infty\). Then \(A(q')R\) is invertible near \(q\),
giving the right inverse \(R(A(q')R)^{-1}\).
This proves openness in all positive smooth metrics of density
\(\nu\) and relative openness in \(\mathscr M_{\nu,K}\).
For density apply the boundary transplant, the rank calculation,
and \(T_a\) at each obstructed \(q\) in the relative class.
With \(N\) finite and fixed before \(a\to0\), the metrics \(q_a\)
converge to \(q\) in \(C^\infty\) and equal \(q\) on a
neighborhood of \(K\). This freezes the old kernel and requires
no continuous basis for moving zero modes.

## Exact nonlinear targets and smoothness

For each allowed fixed \(a\ne0\), the compared map
\(\widehat F(a,h)\) is smooth \(X\to Y\) with derivative
\(A_a\) at zero. The slice \(\mathcal S=L^*U+\mathcal J\mathbb R^m\)
is direct because \(P|_U\) and \(\mathcal J\)
are injective. It is the image of \(Q_a\) and is closed by
the projection \(Q_aA_a\). The derivative on this slice
is invertible. At other regular centers take the slice
\(L_q^*H^{s+4}(B_q)\) and its normal right inverse.
[E40, (5) and exact lift][e40]

Let \(J\ge\|Q_a\|\) and let \(\Lambda\ge1\) bound the
Lipschitz constant of the curvature derivative on a common
small ball of radius \(\rho_*\). Set
\(\rho_a=\min(\rho_*,1/(2J\Lambda))\).
For \(\|f\|_Y<\rho_a/(2J)\) the map
\[
h\longmapsto Q_a f-Q_a\big(\widehat F(a,h)-W_a-A_a h\big)
\]
is a contraction on the closed slice ball of radius \(\rho_a\),
with Lipschitz constant at most \(1/2\) and output norm at most
\(\rho_a/2+\rho_a/4\). Its fixed point gives
\(\widehat F(a,h)=W_a+f\) exactly, and the derivative on the slice
remains invertible there. The bound \(J=O(|a|^{-1})\) permits a
sufficient target radius of order \(|a|^2\) for \(m>0\).
This gives no upper bound on nonlinear target size.

For spatial regularity write \(h=L^*u+\mathcal J d\) and
use the full potential space with row \(b(u)=0\). The
derivative of the spatial row on \(U\times\mathbb R^m\)
is invertible at the solution. The complement \(\mathcal C\)
contributes through \(b|_{\mathcal C}\) and vanishes under
\(L^*\), so the enlarged derivative is invertible. Choose
smooth component pullbacks \(T_\eta\) translating on a smaller
framed chart and equal to the identity off a larger one.
Conjugate the spatial row by \(T_\eta\) and use the moment
row \(b(T_\eta^{-1}u)\). The conjugated differential expression
is jointly smooth in the same Sobolev grades by the chain rule
on the four jets of \(u\) and the smooth dependence of their
coefficients on \(\eta\). The scalar variables are unchanged.
In the moment row a change of variables puts the pullback on
the smooth coefficient functions. The translated right side
is smooth into \(H^s\) for smooth forcing. The implicit
function theorem, uniqueness and strong continuity of pullback
show \(T_\eta u\) is smooth in \(\eta\) into \(H^{s+4}\).
Differentiation there yields all local spatial weak derivatives
in \(H^{s+4}\). Sobolev embedding proves smoothness at the
same low-norm size threshold. Include \(t\) as a parameter
for forcing \(-tW_a\) to get joint smoothness along the chord.
[E27, spatial regularity][e27] · [E40, exact lift][e40]

### Moving target and the solved cap

The increment \(f=-tW_a\) gives the exact radial target
\((1-t)W_{q_a}^+\) in the \(q_a\) chart for small \(|t|\),
with \(g(0)=q_a\) and \(dV_{g(t)}=\nu\). At \(W_{q_a}^+=0\)
the constant metric solves the radial equation. For arbitrary
small smooth \(f\), the transported block on \(K\) is the
restriction of the prescribed increment. Along the radial
chord the actual \(W^+_{g(t)}\) vanishes on \(K\), including
all jets at \(\partial K\) by smoothness, and it vanishes
near \(K\) when the starting target did. The solution may
deform the metric and connection there. The equation only
prescribes the positive Weyl block. [E40, (8)][e40]

In the compared bundles \(W_a=W_q^++aLv+O(a^2)\) in \(Y\),
with \(b(W_a-W_q^+)=O(a^2)\). An order-\(|a|\) baseline
difference in the full norm can exceed the sufficient
order-\(|a|^2\) target radius. In addition, the original
chart has an orthogonal comparison depending on the unknown.
With \(\ell^\sharp=\log(e^{a v^\sharp/2}e^{h^\sharp}
e^{a v^\sharp/2})\), the composite tangent isometry is
\(e^{-a v^\sharp/2}e^{-h^\sharp/2}\) and the direct one is
\(e^{-\ell^\sharp/2}\). Their ratio is \(O=e^{\ell^\sharp/2}
e^{-a v^\sharp/2}e^{-h^\sharp/2}\). For \(h=\epsilon k\), the
\(a\epsilon\) term in \(\ell^\sharp\) vanishes by expanding the
symmetric product, and that in \(O\) is \([v^\sharp,k^\sharp]/8\).
For \(R=\Lambda^2 O^*|_{\Lambda_q^+}\) the exact comparison is
\(\widehat F(a,h)=R F_q(\ell) R^{-1}\). Thus the initial
chart's prescribed field \((1-t)W_q^+\) becomes
\((1-t)R W_q^+R^{-1}\), which depends on \(h\). E40 realizes a chord through
the moved reference with its own baseline. A differentiable
chord through the original reference with original target
still requires \(b(W_q^+)=0\), by differentiating at \(t=0\).
The local existence result gives no estimate for finite descent,
iteration or convergence to a globally zero block.
[E40, (9) and comparison][e40]
