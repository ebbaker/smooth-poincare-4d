## Setting and statement

Let \(M\) be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented homotopy
four-sphere and \(K\subset M\) a smoothly embedded closed coordinate
four-ball. Fix a smooth real positive-definite metric \(g_K\) on \(K\)
with \(W_{g_K}^{+}=0\), admitting a smooth positive-definite metric
germ across \(\partial K\). Consider smooth Riemannian metrics \(g\)
on \(M\) with \(g|_K=g_K\). This class is nonempty by extending the
germ with a cutoff and a smooth background metric. The exterior
is \(U=M\setminus K\). Test \(E_+\) against all compactly supported
smooth real \(g\)-trace-free symmetric tensors in \(U\). The norms
of the chiral Weyl blocks are their curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt norms, with ASD meaning \(W^+=0\).
[Common conventions][common]

For every such \(g\), exterior stationarity holds if and only if
the entire metric is conformally diffeomorphic to the round
four-sphere. In particular, an ASD cap with \(W^-\ne0\) somewhere
in its interior forces a strict exterior descent direction for
every smooth extension. For an ordinary round cap, stationarity
recognizes a supplied extension if it occurs. The standard sphere
with a cap taken from its round metric supplies a compatible
stationary example.

## First variation and smooth matching

For a smooth metric on all of \(M\), write
\[
 \mathcal W(g)=\int_M |W_g|_{(0,4)}^2\,dV_g,\qquad
 E_\pm(g)=\int_M |W^\pm_g|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\,dV_g.
\]
The closed oriented signature identity \(E_+-E_-=12\pi^2\tau(M)=0\)
and the factor four from the squared operator norm to the squared
four-index norm give \(\mathcal W=4(E_++E_-)=8E_+\).
In the Bach convention of Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo,
the \(L^2\) gradient of \(\mathcal W\) in the four-index norm is
\(-4B\). Here \(B_g\) is symmetric and trace-free. It follows that
for every smooth symmetric variation \(h\) on the closed manifold,
\[
 dE_+(g)[h]=-\tfrac12\int_M\langle B_g,h\rangle_g\,dV_g.
 \tag{1}
\]
This computation uses the signature of the entire \(M\) along the
metric variation. [SH-E01 signature and norms][recognition] ·
[Gover–Gursky, p. 294][gg] · [BGI+M, §1][bgim]

At an exterior-stationary metric, taking \(h=\chi B_g\) with
arbitrary nonnegative \(\chi\in C_c^\infty(U)\) in (1) gives
\(B_g=0\) on \(U\). Every allowed \(h\) is realized, for example,
by \(g_t=g(e^{t h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\), where
\(h^\sharp=g^{-1}h\). This curve is fixed on \(K\) and preserves
the volume form exactly because \(\operatorname{tr}_g h=0\).

To check the ASD cap, use auxiliary variations with compact
support in \(K^\circ\) in the closed-manifold identity (1). These
are used solely to determine the tensor \(B_g\) in the cap.
The density \(|W^+|^2_{\mathrm{op,HS}}\,dV\) has zero first
variation at each point with \(W^+=0\), since every term in its
derivative has a factor of \(W^+\). Outside the support the
metric stays fixed. Thus \(dE_+(g)[h]=0\) for every such auxiliary
variation, so \(B_g=0\) on \(K^\circ\), again by testing with
\(\chi B_g\). Smoothness on all of \(M\) makes \(B_g\) continuous
across \(\partial K\), giving global Bach-flatness. This step
uses the agreed smooth global extension and compactly supported
tests in open sets, without an interface boundary variation.

## Analytic continuation of the self-dual block

Lassas–Liimatainen use the Fefferman–Graham obstruction tensor
\(\mathcal O^{(n)}\), which in dimension four is the Bach tensor
in their convention. The zero equation is independent of the
Bach sign convention. One can check this identification
variationally using Graham–Hirachi, §1 and Theorem 1.1. Their
dimension-four formula for \(Q\) and the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet identity
give \(\int_M Q\,dV=8\pi^2\chi(M)-\mathcal W(g)/4\) on the closed
manifold. Indeed \(Q=-\Delta R/6+R^2/24-|\operatorname{Ric}_0|^2/2\)
in that convention, and the Laplacian integrates to zero. The
first variation of \(\int Q\) is a nonzero multiple of the
\(L^2\) pairing with \(\mathcal O^{(4)}\). Thus \(B_g=0\) from
(1) implies \(\mathcal O^{(4)}(g)=0\) in the analytic regularity
theorem. [Lassas–Liimatainen, §3.2, eqs. (26)–(28)][ll] ·
[Graham–Hirachi, Theorem 1.1][gh] · [BGI+M, §1][bgim]

Each point of the smooth boundaryless Riemannian manifold has
smooth conformal harmonic coordinates by Lassas–Liimatainen
Theorem 2.2. In dimension \(n=4\), Theorem 1.3(c) and its fuller
form, Theorem 3.7(c), assume \(g\in C^r\) for \(r>3\) and the
indicated regularity of
\((\det g)^{1/4}\mathcal O^{(4)}(g)\) in those coordinates.
Smoothness supplies the initial regularity, for example
\(r=9/2\), and the weighted obstruction tensor is zero and
therefore analytic. The resulting local representative
\(\widehat g=(\det g)^{-1/4}g\) is real analytic in those coordinates.
This also applies at the interface because its points are
interior points of \(M\). [Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorems 1.3(c),
2.2 and 3.7(c)][ll]

For this analytic metric, the Weyl endomorphism \(W_{\widehat g}\)
is analytic in a coordinate two-form frame. The same is true of
the oriented Hodge star since its components use the inverse
metric and the volume coefficient \(\sqrt{\det\widehat g}\).
In an oriented chart the alternating symbol is constant, and
an oppositely oriented chart changes its sign by a constant.
Consequently \(P_+W_{\widehat g}P_+\) is analytic, with
\(P_+=(1+*_{\widehat g})/2\). This endomorphism is the self-dual
block extended by zero on \(\Lambda^-\). Positive conformal
rescaling preserves the Hodge splitting and the vanishing of
each curvature block. This establishes analyticity of the
projected tensor in a local representative and transports its
zero set back to \(g\).

Let \(G\subset M\) be the set of points with a neighborhood
on which \(W_g^+=0\). This open set contains \(K^\circ\).
For a point in its closure, choose a connected conformal harmonic
coordinate neighborhood. The analytic projected tensor vanishes
on the nonempty open intersection with \(G\). Each of its
components then vanishes on the entire neighborhood by the real
analytic identity theorem. Thus \(G\) is closed, and connectedness
implies \(G=M\). This argument applies to the specified chirality
itself. Theorem 1.4 of Lassas–Liimatainen assumes vanishing of
the full Weyl tensor on an open set. The identity-theorem step here
uses the local regularity from Theorem 1.3(c). [Primary text][ll]

Now \(E_+=0\) on \(M\). Signature gives \(E_-=0\), and smoothness
implies \(W^-=0\) identically. The smooth conformal developing map
from the closed simply connected \(M\) to \(S^4\) is a covering
and hence a conformal diffeomorphism, as in SH-E01. Conversely,
any such globally conformally round extension has \(E_+=0\)
and is stationary for the admissible exterior variations.
[SH-E01 exact recognition][recognition]

## Consequences of fixing the core

* A round cap has both Weyl blocks zero. Its exterior-stationary
  extensions are exactly the globally conformally round metrics
  compatible with the cap. The input is a stationary global
  extension, and choosing round data on a coordinate ball alone
  leaves the extension problem on arbitrary \(M\) open.
* If the ASD cap has \(W^-\ne0\) somewhere in \(K^\circ\), the
  conclusion \(W^-\equiv0\) from stationarity contradicts the
  fixed data. The curvature condition concerns nonzero Weyl
  curvature, so a cap of nonzero constant sectional curvature
  does not belong to this case. The E07 Taub–NUT cap is an
  example. Its fixed opposite energy implies
  \(E_+(g)\ge 11\pi^2/2\) for every extension by signature,
  in addition to the absence of smooth exterior stationarity.
  [E07 Taub–NUT cap][e07]

At any nonstationary extension choose \(U_0\Subset U\) containing
a point where \(B_g\ne0\). A bump \(0\le\chi\le1\) supported in
\(U_0\) can equal one on a smaller coordinate ball \(V\), where
\(|B_g|\ge b>0\). Put \(h=\chi B_g\) and
\(A_g=\int_M\chi|B_g|^2\,dV_g\ge b^2\operatorname{Vol}_g(V)>0\).
Along the fixed-cap curve \(g_t=g(e^{t h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\),
(1) gives
\[
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}E_+(g_t)\right|_{t=0}=-\tfrac12 A_g,
 \qquad
 E_+(g_t)\le E_+(g)-\tfrac14 A_g t
 \quad(0<t<t_g)
\]
for some \(t_g>0\) by differentiability at zero. The coefficient
and the allowable parameter interval depend on the supplied metric
and chosen bump. In the nonflat ASD case the local decrease
excludes an attained smooth minimum. A uniform descent estimate
and the behavior of minimizing sequences remain separate questions.
