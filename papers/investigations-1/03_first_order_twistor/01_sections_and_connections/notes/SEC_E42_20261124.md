## Setting and conclusion

Use [E27][e27] on one smooth closed oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\).
Let \(r\) be a supplied smooth positive Riemannian metric, equal to the
unit round metric by an oriented identification on an embedded round
coordinate ball \(O\). Fix a smaller concentric round ball \(U\Subset O\).
Set \(\nu=dV_r\), \(V=S^2_0T^*M\), and \(B=S^2_0\Lambda^+_r\).
Use the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt pairing on \(B\), the fixed
volume chart \(g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\), \(h^\sharp=r^{-1}h\),
and the transported positive Weyl block
\[
 F(h)=I_h W^+_{g_h} I_h^{-1},\qquad W=F(0),\qquad L=DF(0).
\]
Here \(I_h\) is pullback by the positive symmetric tangent isometry
\(S_h=e^{-h^\sharp/2}:(TM,r)\to(TM,g_h)\). The reference target \(W\)
vanishes on \(O\). Write \(\mathcal K X=(\mathcal L_Xr)_0\) and use the
reference \(L^2\) pairings throughout. Fix an integer \(s\ge5\) for the
Banach calculations. [E03][e03] · [E27][e27]

The smooth kernel supported compactly in \(U\) is
\(\mathcal K C^\infty_c(U,TM)\). Every such direction is in the
radical of the projected Hessian on the *entire* smooth kernel of \(L\).
A regular solution of the ensuing signed quadratic equation on a
prescribed finite-dimensional smooth kernel family produces an exact
small radial lift, smooth in \(\sqrt t\). If the radial moment is nonzero,
compact cap directions alone cannot meet that leading-order equation.

## Classification by the closed round problem

Take \(k\in C^\infty_c(U,V)\) with \(Lk=0\). Transplant it by the
oriented round identification and extend it by zero to the standard
unit sphere \((S^4,r_0)\). There is a zero collar before the extension.
The resulting smooth tensor \(\widehat k\) satisfies \(D_0\widehat k=0\),
where \(D_0=DF_{r_0}(0)\). The equality uses the locality of the
Weyl derivative and the agreement of the underlying metric, frame
comparison and orientation on the full round chart.

Use the closed York splitting \(\widehat k=k_{\mathrm{TT}}+\mathcal K_0Y\).
Indeed, the conformal Killing operator \(\mathcal K_0\) has injective
symbol and \(\mathcal K_0^*=-2\delta_0\), with
\((\delta_0 a)_j=\nabla^i a_{ij}\). The second-order elliptic,
self-adjoint \(\mathcal K_0^*\mathcal K_0\) has kernel \(\ker\mathcal K_0\).
The right side of
\(\mathcal K_0^*\mathcal K_0Y=\mathcal K_0^*\widehat k\) is orthogonal
to that kernel, so the closed elliptic inverse on its complement gives
a smooth \(Y\). Then \(k_{\mathrm{TT}}\) is divergence-free and
trace-free. This is the four-dimensional version of York's
divergence equation and orthogonality argument, with \(2/4\) as
the trace subtraction in \(\mathcal K_0\).
[York, §3, (3.1)–(3.6)][york] ·
[Melrose, Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose]

On the round sphere \(D_0\mathcal K_0=0\) by diffeomorphism and conformal
covariance at \(W^+=0\). On round TT tensors,
\[
 D_0^*D_0=\tfrac1{16}(\nabla^*\nabla+2)(\nabla^*\nabla+4).
 \tag{1}
\]
For the normalization in (1), Gursky–Viaclovsky's four-dimensional
functional at coefficient \(-1/3\) is
\(\mathcal F_{-1/3}=\frac12\int|W^{\mathrm{full}}|_{\mathrm{tensor}}^2\,dV
-16\pi^2\chi\) by their introduction. The full tensor norm squares
count each antisymmetric index pair twice, so that on \(S^4\) the
signature formula gives \(\mathcal F_{-1/3}=4E_+-16\pi^2\chi\), where
\(E_+=\int|W^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV\). At the round background its Hessian
is \(8\langle D_0\cdot,D_0\cdot\rangle\). Their Theorem 3.10 and §7.1
with scalar curvature \(12\) and \(\Delta_L=-\nabla^*\nabla-8\) on TT
give \(\frac12(\nabla^*\nabla+2)(\nabla^*\nabla+4)\) for the Hessian
operator of \(\mathcal F_{-1/3}\). This gives (1) in the conventions
of E02. Nonnegativity of \(\nabla^*\nabla\) already makes (1) strictly
positive. Thus \(D_0k_{\mathrm{TT}}=0\) implies \(k_{\mathrm{TT}}=0\).
[Gursky–Viaclovsky, (1.1)–(1.3), Theorem 3.10, (3.41) and §7.1][gv] ·
[Gover–Gursky, §1 signature formula and §1.1, (1.2)][gg] · [E02, §5][e02]

It remains to make \(Y\) compactly supported in the patch. Choose
a smaller concentric closed ball in \(U\) containing
\(\operatorname{supp}\widehat k\) in its interior. On the connected
annulus between that ball and the edge of \(O\), \(Y\) is conformal
Killing. Stereographically project from the antipode of the center
of \(O\). The resulting coordinates are conformally Euclidean
and send the round annulus to a Euclidean annulus. A field is conformal
Killing for \(\Omega^2\delta\) exactly when it is for \(\delta\).
In flat components put \(f=\partial_iY^i/4\). Differentiation of
\(\partial_iY_j+\partial_jY_i=2f\delta_{ij}\) and permutation of
indices give
\[
 \partial_i\partial_jY_k
   =\delta_{jk}\partial_i f+\delta_{ik}\partial_j f-\delta_{ij}\partial_k f .
\]
Thus \(\Delta Y=-2\,df\). Taking its divergence gives \(\Delta f=0\).
Applying \(\Delta\) to the conformal Killing equation then gives
\(\partial_i\partial_j f=0\). Connectedness implies \(f=\lambda+2d\cdot x\)
with constant \(\lambda\in\mathbb R\), \(d\in\mathbb R^4\). The vector
field \(2(d\cdot x)x-|x|^2d\) has precisely the resulting constant
second derivatives of \(Y\). The remaining affine part obeys the
same equation with \(f=\lambda\), hence \(Y\) agrees on the full
annulus with
\[
 Y_{\mathrm{ext}}(x)=a+A x+\lambda x+2(d\cdot x)x-|x|^2d,
 \qquad A^\mathsf T=-A.
\]
The polynomial is conformal Killing on the whole Euclidean chart,
so it extends this annular field across the round ball. On \(O\)
subtract \(Y_{\mathrm{ext}}\) from \(Y\), and extend the resulting
field by zero on \(M\). The resulting smooth \(X\) is compactly
supported in \(U\) and satisfies \(k=\mathcal K X\). A compactly
supported \(\mathcal K X\) arising from \(X\in C^\infty_c(U,TM)\)
is in \(\ker L\), by the normalized action below. The same argument
applies to tensors supported in a *closed* concentric subball
\(K_0\Subset O\) by using its outer annulus. It gives \(X\)
supported in \(K_0\), even if \(k\) is supported up to its boundary.

## Pointwise-volume lift and projected Hessian

For \(X\in C^\infty_c(U,TM)\) let \(\varphi_a\) be its flow and
\(J_a=\varphi_a^*\nu/\nu\). For any \(h\) in the chart, define
\(A_a(h)\) by
\[
  g_{A_a(h)}=J_a^{-1/2}\varphi_a^*g_h.
 \tag{2}
\]
The four-dimensional metric scale power is two on volume, so (2)
has volume \(\nu\) pointwise. Its chart coordinate comes from the
real symmetric logarithm of \(r^{-1}g_{A_a(h)}\). Differentiating at
\(a=0,h=0\) gives \(\left.\partial_a A_a(0)\right|_0=\mathcal K X\).
The flow maps \(U\) onto itself and equals the identity off \(U\).
Both Weyl blocks of \(g_{A_a(0)}\) vanish on \(U\); elsewhere the
metric equals \(r\). In particular \(F(A_a(0))=W\) as a fixed section,
so the Hessian \(H=D^2F(0)\) satisfies
\[
 H[\mathcal K X,\mathcal K X]=
   -L\left.\partial_a^2 A_a(0)\right|_0 .
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus for every \(Z\in\mathcal C=\ker L^*\),
\(\langle Z,H[\mathcal K X,\mathcal K X]\rangle_{L^2(r)}=0\).

For mixed pairings, the symmetric transports in (2) give the
oriented \(r\)-isometry
\[
 O_{a,h}(x)=J_a(x)^{-1/4}\,
  S_h(\varphi_a x)^{-1}\,d\varphi_a(x)\,S_{A_a(h)}(x).
\]
On the fixed bundle take \(O_{a,h}^*\) to mean its induced pullback
on \(\Lambda^+\). Naturality and the inverse scale of a Weyl
endomorphism give
\[
 F(A_a(h))(x)=J_a(x)^{1/2}O_{a,h}(x)^*
                 F(h)(\varphi_a x)(O_{a,h}(x)^*)^{-1}.
 \tag{4}
\]
Outside \(U\) the right side is \(F(h)(x)\), including its linearization
in \(h\). Inside \(U\), \(W(\varphi_a x)=0\). Therefore the derivative
of \(O_{a,h}\) in \(h\) has zero contribution at \(h=0\) in (4).
For any smooth \(j\in\ker L\), the remaining derivative on the right
also vanishes because \(Lj=0\). Differentiating the left side once in
\(j\) and then in \(a\) at zero now yields the stronger identity
\[
 H[\mathcal K X,j]=-L\,S_X j,\qquad
 S_X j=\mathcal L_Xj-\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}_rX)j
      -\tfrac12 r\big(( (\mathcal K X)^\sharp j^\sharp+
                         j^\sharp(\mathcal K X)^\sharp)\,\cdot,\cdot\big).
 \tag{5}
\]
Here \(S_X j=\partial_a\partial_\epsilon
 A_a(\epsilon j)|_{a=\epsilon=0}\). The last term is the mixed
derivative of the exponential chart when passing from the metric
to its logarithm. The tensor is trace-free and supported where
\(X\) is supported. Consequently \(\langle Z,H[\mathcal K X,j]\rangle=0\)
for all \(Z\in\mathcal C\), in particular for any two compact cap
directions \(j=\mathcal K Y\). The mixed Hessian itself is a range
term, which need not vanish as a section. These arguments use
natural pullback and conformal covariance in the fixed comparison,
as in E03's formula (2). [E03, conformal scaling and longitudinal
directions][e03] · [Gover–Gursky, §1.1, (1.2)][gg]

More generally, if \(F(h)=(1-t)W\), equation (4) proves
\(F(A_a(h))=(1-t)W\) for every cap-supported flow within the chart.
This applies even when such a lift is only ASD inside the cap.
The cap action is an exact symmetry of this prescribed target.

## Desingularizing the E27 equation

Fix an \(L^2(r)\)-orthonormal basis \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) of \(\mathcal C\),
and take the E27 correction operator \(\mathcal W:\mathbb R^m\to H^s(B)\),
\(\mathcal Wc=\sum_i c_iwZ_i\), for its fixed weight \(w\). Write
\(b(f)_i=\langle Z_i,f\rangle_{L^2(r)}\), \(\Gamma=b\mathcal W\),
\(P=LL^*\), \(U_s=H^{s+4}(B)\cap\mathcal C^\perp\), and \(G_P\) for
the reference inverse of \(P\) on the complement. In particular
\(bL=0\) and \(\Gamma\) is invertible (with empty correction if \(m=0\)).
The full chart is smooth \(H^{s+2}(V)\to H^s(B)\). Its range split is
\(\ker L\oplus L^*U_s\) with bounded projection to the second factor
\(L^*G_PL\). [E03, normal inverse][e03] · [E27, (1)–(4)][e27]

Let \(\mathscr P\subset\mathbb R^d\) be open, and let
\(p\mapsto k(p)\) be a smooth map into a supplied fixed
finite-dimensional subspace of smooth \(\ker L\) sections. Fix an
interior parameter \(p_0\). Write \(t=\sigma\tau^2\), where
\(\sigma=1\) describes the requested contracting chord and
\(\sigma=-1\) records the reversed sign. For real \(\tau\) near zero,
apply the parameter version of the E27 implicit function theorem to
\[
 F\big(\tau k(p)+L^*u\big)-W
       +\mathcal Wc+\sigma\tau^2 W=0
 \tag{6}
\]
in \((u,c)\in U_s\times\mathbb R^m\). At
\((\tau,p,u,c)=(0,p_0,0,0)\), the \((u,c)\)-derivative is
\((v,e)\mapsto Pv+\mathcal We\). Its inverse sends \(f\) to
\[
 \left(G_P(f-\mathcal W\Gamma^{-1}b(f)),\ \Gamma^{-1}b(f)\right).
\]
Thus there is a unique small smooth \((u(\tau,p),c(\tau,p))\).
At \(\tau=0\) it is \((0,0)\) for every nearby \(p\).
Its \(\tau\)-derivative there vanishes, since the
first-order forcing is \(Lk(p)=0\). Write
\(u=\tau^2v(\tau,p)\) and \(c=\tau^2d(\tau,p)\). The quotients
are smooth through \(\tau=0\) by the integral Taylor formula
in the corresponding Banach spaces.

More explicitly, put \(N(h)=F(h)-W-Lh\), \(q=k(p)+\tau L^*v\), and
define the smooth scaled remainder by
\[
 \mathcal N(\tau,p)=
   \int_0^1(1-\lambda)D^2F(\lambda\tau q)[q,q]\,d\lambda.
\]
For \(\tau\ne0\) this is \(N(\tau q)/\tau^2\) in \(H^s(B)\).
At zero it equals \(H[k(p),k(p)]/2\), and the difference
from this value is \(\tau\) times a smooth \(H^s(B)\)-valued
function by one more integral in \(\tau\). The scaled equation is
\(Pv+\mathcal Wd+\sigma W+\mathcal N=0\). In particular
\[
 R_\sigma(\tau,p)=\sigma b(W)+b(\mathcal N(\tau,p))
     =-\Gamma d(\tau,p),\qquad
 R_\sigma(0,p)=\sigma b(W)+\mathcal Q(k(p)),
 \qquad \mathcal Q(k)=\tfrac12 b(H[k,k]).
 \tag{7}
\]
At \(\tau=0\), if \(f_0=\sigma W+H[k(p),k(p)]/2\), the scaled
solution is
\[
 (v(0,p),d(0,p))=
 \left(-G_P(f_0-\mathcal W\Gamma^{-1}b(f_0)),\
       -\Gamma^{-1}b(f_0)\right).
\]
The realized Weyl target from (6) is \((1-t)W-\mathcal W c\).
For \(\tau\ne0\) the correction vanishes exactly when \(R_\sigma=0\).

A regular signed zero is an interior \(p_0\) satisfying
\[
 \mathcal Q(k(p_0))=-\sigma b(W),\qquad
 D_pR_\sigma(0,p_0)\dot p
       =b\big(H[k(p_0),\,dk(p_0)\dot p]\big):
             \mathbb R^d\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m
 \ \text{onto}.
 \tag{8}
\]
Choose a complement of the kernel of this finite matrix and keep the
other parameters fixed. The finite-dimensional implicit function theorem
gives a smooth \(p(\tau)=p_0+O(\tau)\) with \(R_\sigma(\tau,p(\tau))=0\).
Then
\[
 h(\tau)=\tau k(p(\tau))+
             \tau^2 L^*v(\tau,p(\tau))
       =\tau k(p_0)+O_{H^{s+2}}(\tau^2),\qquad
 F(h(\tau))=(1-\sigma\tau^2)W.
\]
The chart gives positive metrics with volume \(\nu\).
For the spatial regularity, apply E27's localized-translation
argument to (6) with the additional smooth parameter \(\tau k(p)\)
inside \(F\). In the full potential space adjoin \(b(u)=0\).
After conjugating the differential row by a localized translation
\(T_\eta\), the prescribed coefficients include \(\tau T_\eta k(p)\)
and the new integral row is \(b(T_\eta^{-1}u)\). The kernel family
is finite-dimensional with smooth spatial basis, so the additional
coefficients are jointly smooth in \((\eta,\tau,p)\) at the same
grades. The derivative in \((u,c)\), with moment row adjoined,
remains invertible near the origin. Uniqueness gives the translated
solution as the smooth implicit solution in \(\eta\). Differentiating
in \(\eta\) on the smaller coordinate patches yields every spatial
derivative in the original Sobolev grades, smoothly in \((\tau,p)\).
Thus the metric family is smooth in \((x,\tau)\) on a common local
parameter interval. [E27, spatial regularity][e27]

Conversely, for a lift with \(h(\tau)/\tau\to k_\star\) in
\(H^{s+2}\) as \(\tau\to0\) along one sign, division by \(\tau\)
of the full equation gives \(Lk_\star=0\). Applying \(b\) and
dividing by \(\tau^2\) gives
\(\mathcal Q(k_\star)=-\sigma b(W)\). For a smooth leading
direction this is the first equation in (8), with surjectivity
a sufficient hypothesis for persistence. When \(b(W)\ne0\),
the same Taylor bound \(\|bN(h)\|\le C\|h\|_{H^{s+2}}^2\)
gives a positive lower bound proportional to \(\sqrt{|t|}\)
on the norm of any sufficiently small exact lift.
When \(m=0\), the condition is void and E27 already gives a
lift in the range complement. With \(m>0\) and \(b(W)=0\),
\(k=0\) obeys the leading balance but has zero \(p\)-derivative,
and this reduction gives no persistence from that zero alone.

## Which directions can contribute

Let \(\mathscr K=\ker L\cap C^\infty(M,V)\). The symmetric
bilinear form \((k,j)\mapsto b(H[k,j])\) descends to
\(\mathscr K/\mathcal K C^\infty_c(U,TM)\), by (5).
For the exterior of any fixed closed concentric \(K_0\Subset O\),
it also descends to the image of the restriction
\(\mathscr K\to C^\infty(M\setminus K_0,V)\): two extensions
with the same restriction differ by a kernel tensor supported
in \(K_0\), and the compact-support classification with its
outer annulus applies. The corresponding \(X\) is supported in
\(K_0\), and (2)–(5) apply inside a larger round subball.
This identifies how exterior restrictions determine the pairing.
The defining integral still runs over \(M\). In particular every
kernel direction with compact support anywhere in the round ball
\(O\) is a radical direction, by choosing \(K_0\) to enclose its
support. Equations (2)–(5) also apply to normalized pullbacks
supported in any open region where \(W=0\).

If \(b(W)\ne0\), every purely compact cap family fails (8):
\(\mathcal Q(k(p))=0\) identically, and \(R_\sigma(\tau,p)\)
stays nonzero near any fixed \(p_0\). A perturbation \(h\) itself
supported in the cap cannot realize a nonzero \(t\), since \(W\)
is unchanged on its exterior and has a nonzero exterior value.
Adding a compact cap kernel direction to any exterior-reaching
one leaves both the quadratic value and its pairings with all
kernel tests unchanged. More generally, locality of the two-jet
Hessian gives zero for pairs with disjoint supports. The candidates
for balance are directions supported in the exterior, or global
kernel directions spanning cap and exterior which survive the
quotient, with mixed pairings between such classes included.
Neither the existence of a signed zero on those classes nor the
rank in (8) follows from the round calculation. For a finite
family that may be chosen freely, regularity at a smooth
\(k_\star\) is equivalent to surjectivity of
\(j\mapsto b(H[k_\star,j])\) on the smooth kernel, since finitely
many preimages of a basis of \(\mathbb R^m\) would suffice to
form that family. Singular leading zeros and lifts lacking a
square-root expansion are undecided.

E33's two moments classify compact *inhomogeneous linear* cap lifts;
a lift with nonzero source is outside \(\ker L\), and at zero
source the compact-kernel classification above applies. E12 gives
a first-order radial obstruction on an Einstein product, with
no information about the sign or rank in (8) on this \(M\).
The packet concerns a small parameter interval at the fixed
reference and discharges no finite chord or exterior existence
claim. [E33][e33] · [E12][e12] · [B24][b24]
