**Disposition: pass.** The frozen E18 theorem holds with its stated
hypotheses. For one fixed smooth nonzero axial seed on the product
collar, there is a \(C^{10}\) neighborhood of the product flat metric
in which no smooth positive-definite metric agreeing exactly with the
flat metric near both ends realizes the prescribed seed-target chord
at any \(0<\lambda\le1\). The radius can depend on the fixed seed and
torus periods. The verification uses the orientation and self-dual
operator norm of [SH-B01][common] and the fixed comparison of [E18][e18].

## 1. Periodic completion with variable end widths

The orientation \(ds\wedge dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3\) and parallel
frame descend to the rectangular torus when the endpoints of
\([-1,1]\) are identified by the identity in the \(T^3\) variables.
Each individual candidate is flat on both sides of the resulting
seam. The seed \(r\) is flat there because its defining \(f\) has
compact support in the open interval. Smoothness of the periodized
metrics thus holds even for a sequence whose flat collar widths
shrink. The prescribed Weyl equation extends because both Weyl
blocks vanish near the seam for each individual candidate.

For the components \(q=g-\gamma\), the squared norm
\(\sum_{\xi}(1+|\xi|^2)^{10}|\widehat q(\xi)|^2\) is a fixed weighted
sum of squares of the \(L^2\) norms of coordinate derivatives
through order ten, by the multinomial expansion and Parseval.
Each derivative on one period agrees with the original derivative
on the collar, and its \(L^2(d\mu)\) norm is bounded by its supremum.
Here \(d\mu\) is the normalized flat measure. This verifies E18's
\(H^{10}\) bound with a constant independent of the varying flat
widths. The construction introduces no transition functions or
derivatives of cutoffs. [Frozen periodic extension][e18]

## 2. The gauge has the derivatives and the zero modes it needs

For \(X\in H^8_0\) in the parallel trivialization, smallness implies
\(\|dX\|_{C^0}<1\). The maps \(x\mapsto x+uX(x)\) are orientation-preserving
local diffeomorphisms for \(0\le u\le1\) and are homotopic to the
identity. They are degree-one coverings of the torus, hence global
\(C^1\) diffeomorphisms. The displacement chart gives
\(\phi_X\in\mathcal D^8\). Inci–Kappeler–Topalov define this space
by \(H^s\) regularity of the map inside the orientation-preserving
\(C^1\) diffeomorphisms. Their Theorem 1.2(i) assumes a closed
oriented \(n\)-manifold and integer \(s>n/2+1\). It gives \(C^r\)
composition \(H^{s+r}(M,N)\times\mathcal D^s(M)\to H^s(M,N)\)
for nonnegative integers \(r\) and smooth target \(N\). For the
metric components use \(n=4\), \(s=8\), \(r=1\) and Euclidean \(N\).
The torus paragraph of Remark 1.3 also applies after a linear
identification of the rectangular lattice with the unit lattice.
[Inci–Kappeler–Topalov, arXiv:1107.0488v2, pp. 4–5][ikt]

The component composition \(g\circ\phi_X\) is \(C^1\) into \(H^8\)
because the \(H^{10}\) input restricts continuously to \(H^9\).
Multiplying by the two \(H^7\) Jacobian factors gives a \(C^1\)
pullback into \(H^7\). Smooth matrix operations on this algebra
are valid near the positive flat matrix and divergence lands in
\(H^6\). For \(G=\gamma^{-1}\phi_X^*g\), the factor
\(\rho=(\det G)^{-1/4}\) multiplies volume by \(\rho^2\). Thus
\(dV_{\rho\phi_X^*g}=dV_\gamma\) and
\(h=\log G-(\operatorname{tr}\log G)I/4\) is trace-free.
Differentiation at \((X,g)=(0,\gamma)\) gives
\[
 d_Xh=\partial_iX_j+\partial_jX_i-\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}X)\gamma_{ij},
 \qquad
 d_X(\delta_\gamma h)=\Delta_\gamma X^\flat+\tfrac12 d\,\operatorname{div}X.
\]
Since \(\Delta_\gamma=\partial^i\partial_i\), the multipliers at
\(\xi\ne0\) are \(-|\xi|^2\) on transverse covectors and
\(-3|\xi|^2/2\) on the longitudinal line. Constant displacements are
removed by the mean-zero condition. The divergence always has zero
mean by periodicity, so the projected equation forces the actual
divergence to vanish. This gives the required isomorphism
\(H^8_0\to H^6_0\) for the gauge implicit function theorem. The local
\(C^1\) dependence and vanishing of \(X,h,\rho-1\) at \(\gamma\)
justify their linear bound in the \(H^{10}\) distance to \(\gamma\).

For smooth \(g\), the translation argument also checks. In fixed
coordinates
\(\phi_{X\circ\tau_a}=\tau_{-a}\circ\phi_X\circ\tau_a\), which gives
\(\phi_{X\circ\tau_a}^*\tau_a^*g=\tau_a^*(\phi_X^*g)\). Trace,
divergence and mean commute with translations. Local uniqueness
therefore identifies \(X(\tau_a^*g)\) with \(X(g)\circ\tau_a\) for
small \(a\). For this fixed smooth \(g\), \(a\mapsto\tau_a^*g\) is
smooth into every higher Sobolev grade. Theorem 1.2(i) then gives
each finite differentiability order for the gauge map in \((a,X)\)
with \(X\) still at grade eight. Invertibility in \(X\) persists
from the flat point in a small neighborhood. The parameter
implicit function theorem gives smoothness in \(a\) into \(H^8\).
Differentiating translations in distributions identifies every
derivative of \(X(g)\) with an \(H^8\) derivative in \(a\). Hence
\(X(g)\), and through it \(\rho\) and \(h\), are smooth. This use of
higher differentiability applies to a fixed smooth metric.

## 3. The inverse and remainder eliminate a constant-error term

Let \(h=c+v\) be the gauged logarithm with \(c=\bar h\).
Each constant matrix \(c\) gives a constant flat metric
\(\gamma(e^c\cdot,\cdot)\), so \(F_\gamma(c)=0\). Its nonzero modes
\(v\) are TT. To check the Fourier coefficient independently,
put the nonzero covector \(\xi\) in the \(e^0\) direction of an
oriented orthonormal coframe. The TT symbol tensor \(b\) has
\(b_{0i}=0\) and trace-free tangential block. The
[flat Riemann variation][e16] gives only
\(\dot R_{0a,0b}=-\partial_0^2 b_{ab}/2\), up to curvature
symmetries. Its scalar trace vanishes. Each normalized self-dual
basis element has \(e^0\wedge e^a\) coefficient \(1/\sqrt2\),
so the self-dual block is \(-\partial_0^2b_{ab}/4\).
For Fourier derivatives the resulting map is
\(|\xi|^2/4\) times the isometry of the five-dimensional TT block
onto the five-dimensional self-dual Weyl target. This verifies
the E02 symbol, including the curvature-operator norm factor.
The least nonzero lattice size \(\mu_1\) is positive and the
inverse norm between the two stated periodic grades is at most
\(4(1+\mu_1^{-2})\). The zero Fourier coefficient of \(Dv\) is
zero. [E02, eq. (5)][e02]

The curvature map is smooth \(H^7\to H^5\) by the pointwise
matrix formula and the Sobolev product bounds through two
derivatives. Its second derivative is bounded on a sufficiently
small ball by continuity at zero. Integrating \(DF_\gamma(c+uv)\)
in \(u\) gives
\(\|F_\gamma(c+v)-Dv\|_{H^5}
\le C_N(\|c\|_{H^7}+\|v\|_{H^7})\|v\|_{H^7}\), with the exact
vanishing \(F_\gamma(c)=0\). Thus the constant flat moduli can be
kept in the fixed lattice. There is no remainder that stays
nonzero when \(v\) is zero. Inverting and absorbing gives (8)
in the [frozen proof][e18], with
\(\|\bar F_\gamma(h)\|\le C_N\theta\|v\|_{H^7}\) for
\(\theta=\|c\|_{H^7}+\|v\|_{H^7}\).

## 4. Conformal weight and order of the three identifications

For positive matrices \(a,b\), \(a^{-1}b\) is \(a\)-self-adjoint.
Its positive inverse square root \(S_{a,b}\) satisfies
\(b(S_{a,b}\cdot,S_{a,b}\cdot)=a\). Use \(p=\phi^*g\) and
\(\widetilde g=\rho p\). With \(y=\phi(x)\), the map
\(O_x=\rho(x)^{1/2}d\phi_x S_{\gamma,\widetilde g,x}\)
is an oriented isometry from \(\gamma_x\) to \(g_y\).
The induced pullback on two-forms is
\(\rho S_{\gamma,\widetilde g,x}^*(d\phi_x)^*\), where these
stars denote pullbacks of two-forms. Its scalar factor cancels
in conjugation. Positive conformal rescaling preserves the
Hodge splitting on two-forms in dimension four. The covariant
Weyl tensor of \(\rho p\) is \(\rho\) times that of \(p\),
so raising the input pair to form the endomorphism gives the
additional overall weight \(\rho^{-1}\) in \(F_\gamma(h)\).
[SH-E01 conformal normalization][recognition]

Substitution of the fixed seed equation orders the remaining maps as
\[
 \Lambda^+_{\gamma,y}\xrightarrow{J_{r,\gamma}(y)}\Lambda^+_{r,y}
 \xrightarrow{J_{r,g}(y)^{-1}}\Lambda^+_{g,y}
 \xrightarrow{J_O(x)}\Lambda^+_{\gamma,x}.
\]
This gives \(Z_g=\rho^{-1}B_g(T\circ\phi)B_g^{-1}\) exactly.
At \(g=\gamma\), the two seed maps cancel and \(J_O=I\), giving
\(Z_\gamma=T\). The product is formed in the displayed order
and permits arbitrary noncommutation. In fixed two-form frames the
seed map can be computed using the positive matrix
\(r^{-1/2}g r^{-1/2}\) and its inverse square root conjugated back.
The fixed smooth \(r\) keeps its eigenvalues in a compact positive
range for \(g\) near \(\gamma\). The operations are smooth in
\(H^{10}\), and composing their components with \(\phi_X\) is
\(C^1\) into \(H^8\) by the same spare-derivative theorem. The
\(d\phi_X,\rho,\widetilde g\) factors are \(H^7\), giving a
\(C^1\) map \(g\mapsto Z_g\) into \(H^7\). A local first-derivative
bound gives (10) uniformly in the chord parameter.

## 5. Seed mean and quantitative exclusion

For the seed put \(q=f'\). The E13 conventions give sectional
curvatures \(K_{01}=K_{02}=-f''-q^2\),
\(K_{03}=2f''-4q^2\), \(K_{12}=-q^2\),
\(K_{13}=K_{23}=2q^2\). The scalar curvature is \(-6q^2\).
The self-dual operator diagonal
\((K_{0i}+K_{jk})/2-s/12\) therefore equals
\((-f''/2+q^2)(1,1,-2)\). The seed isometry to the flat
target sends the indexed orthonormal axes to the corresponding
axes without choosing eigenvectors in a repeated eigenspace.
The mean on the periodized torus is
\(\bar T=\tfrac12\int_{-1}^{1}q^2\,ds\,\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)\),
since the \(f''\) integral vanishes and \(d\mu\) divides by the
normal period two. Hence \(m_T=\sqrt6\int q^2\,ds/2>0\), as
\(f\) is nonzero and vanishes near the boundary.
[E13 curvature normalization][e13] · [E16 seed moment][e16]

Shrink the \(H^{10}\) neighborhood until the Lipschitz source
bound gives \(\|Z_g\|_{H^5}\le M_T\) and
\(|\bar Z_g|\ge3m_T/4\), as in E18. For every hypothetical
exact solution with \(\lambda>0\), first absorb the remainder
using \(C_D C_N\theta\le1/2\). This gives
\(\|v\|_{H^7}\le2C_D\lambda M_T\). Divide the curvature mean
bound by \(\lambda\) using the exact transported equation.
The result is \(|\bar Z_g|\le2C_D C_N M_T\theta\). By shrinking
the neighborhood again this is less than \(m_T/2\), a
contradiction. All smallness conditions are on \(g-\gamma\),
with constants independent of \(\lambda\) and the end widths.
This proves exclusion of individual candidates and of a branch
approaching the product flat metric in \(C^{10}\) while having
positive parameter. At \(\lambda=0\) the flat metric is a
solution. The theorem is local to the fixed flat collar chart.
Solutions farther away, exterior matching with free ends,
modified sources and global metrics on a homotopy sphere
remain outside the tested statement.
