## The full adjoint equation in the ball

On the oriented Euclidean unit ball \(B\), use the orthonormal chiral
frames of B16 and the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing for the real Weyl
endomorphism targets. Write \(A=\{2/3<|x|<1\}\). For a metric
variation \(h\) suppose \(D_+h=tf+e\) on \(B\), with \(f=\chi Z\)
the B16 seed, \(e\in L^2(B)\), \(t\in\mathbb R\), and
\(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)\). Let \(q=D_-h\). Write \(T\)
for the flat \(L^2\) chiral multiplier with symbol \(M(\xi)\)
from E21 and \(\widetilde e\) for the zero extension of \(e\).
There is no outer boundary condition on \(h\).
[B20 test][b20] · [E26 setting][e26]

The assessed flat symbols give the constant-coefficient identities
\(D_-^*D_-=D_+^*D_+\), \(D_-^*T=D_+^*\) and
\(D_-D_-^*=(\Delta^2/16)I\). For \(L^2\) Fourier fields,
the multiplier identity \(d_-^*M=d_+^*\) is sufficient in
distributions. Restricting the differentiated zero extension to
\(B\) gives the original distributional derivative there. Thus

\[
 z=q-tTf-T\widetilde e,\qquad
 D_-^*z=D_+^*(tf+e)-D_+^*(tf+e)=0
 \quad\text{in }B .
\]

In particular \(\Delta^2 z=0\) in the whole ball, so \(z\) is
smooth there by elliptic regularity. The stronger second-order
adjoint equation will be used to test its angular projection.
[E21, (3)–(6)][e21] · [E26, flat local equation][e26]

## A compact metric test for every radial probe

Let \(V\) be any constant self-dual Weyl endomorphism and put
\(Y_V(n)=M(n)V\) for \(n\in S^3\). Write \(H_4(\xi)=|\xi|^4M(\xi)\)
for the operator-valued polynomial and \(H_V(x)=H_4(x)V\).
By E23 the latter is a componentwise homogeneous harmonic
quartic, equal to \(\rho^4Y_V(n)\) for \(x=\rho n\ne0\).
The Fourier symbols give

\[
 d_-d_+^*=d_-d_-^*M=(|\xi|^4/16)M,\qquad
 D_-D_+^*=\tfrac1{16}H_4(\partial).
\]

The Fourier convention is \(e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\), so the four
derivatives contribute \(i^4=1\). For a radial function
\(\phi(|x|^2)\), the trace-free coefficient tensor of the quartic
gives \(H_V(\partial)\phi(|x|^2)=16H_V(x)\phi^{(4)}(|x|^2)\).
The two factors of 16 cancel, yielding

\[
 D_-D_+^*\big(\phi(|x|^2)V\big)
   =\rho^4\phi^{(4)}(\rho^2)Y_V(n).
\]

[E22, radial derivative identity][e22] · [E23, homogeneous harmonic quartic][e23]

For \(\eta\in C^\infty_c((2/3,1))\), set
\(\psi(s)=\eta(\sqrt{s})/s^2\) on \(s>0\), extended by zero
off its compact support in \((4/9,1)\). Define

\[
 \phi(s)=\frac{1}{6}\int_s^\infty (v-s)^3\,\psi(v)\,dv .
\]

Then \(\phi^{(4)}=\psi\). The function is zero above the support
of \(\psi\) and is a polynomial of degree at most three below
its support. Thus \(\phi(|x|^2)V\) is smooth at the origin and
compactly supported in \(B\). In particular
\(h_{\rm test}=D_+^*(\phi(|x|^2)V)\) is a compactly supported
smooth metric test and \(D_-h_{\rm test}=\eta(\rho)Y_V(n)\).
No prescribed moment conditions on \(\eta\) are needed because
the regular polynomial inside the probe support is allowed.

Pairing \(D_-^*z=0\) against this test gives

\[
 0=\int_{2/3}^1\eta(\rho)\rho^3
       \int_{S^3}\langle z(\rho n),Y_V(n)\rangle_{\rm HS}\,dS(n)\,d\rho .
\]

Arbitrariness of \(\eta\), then smoothness of \(z\), gives zero
for the inner integral at each \(2/3<\rho<1\). This argument
uses only the degree-four angular representation.

## Exact projection and the sharp annular bound

Write \(Y=Y_Z\) and let \(\mathcal Y=\{Y_V:V\in S^2_0\Lambda^+_\delta\}\).
This is a five-dimensional subspace of angular fields with
\(\int_{S^3}\langle Y_V,Y_{V'}\rangle_{\rm HS}\,dS
=2\pi^2\langle V,V'\rangle_{\rm HS}\). Let \(P_{\mathcal Y}\)
be its \(L^2(S^3,dS)\) projection on each sphere. In particular
\(\int_{S^3}|Y|_{\rm HS}^2\,dS=12\pi^2\).
E22 gives \(Tf=a(\rho)Y(n)\) on \(A\), where

\[
 a(\rho)=a_0\rho^{-4}-a_2\rho^{-6},\qquad
 a_0=\frac{6M_0}{\pi^2},\quad a_2=\frac{12M_2}{\pi^2},\qquad
 M_j=\int_{\mathbb R^4}|x|^j\chi(|x|)\,dx\quad(j=0,2).
\]

It follows from the compact probes that

\[
 P_{\mathcal Y}(q-T\widetilde e)=t a(\rho)Y(n)
 \quad\text{on }A .
\]

Equivalently, the \(Y_V\) pairing on a radius-\(\rho\) sphere
with unit-sphere angular measure is
\(2\pi^2 t a(\rho)\langle Z,V\rangle_{\rm HS}\) for
\(q-T\widetilde e\). For exact forcing \(e=0\), this fixes
the original \(Y\) projection for every such metric variation
and gives zero cross-pairing for all \(V\perp Z\).
For general \(e\) the same expression applies after subtracting
the particular field. Angular components outside \(\mathcal Y\)
are not determined by these measurements.
[E22, exact exterior field][e22] · [E23, angular isometry][e23]

Define the sharp constant

\[
 d_{\rm sharp}=\|aY\|_{L^2(A)}
 =\left[12\pi^2\big(a_0^2I_{-4}-2a_0a_2I_{-6}+a_2^2I_{-8}\big)\right]^{1/2},
 \qquad I_\nu=\int_{2/3}^1\rho^{\nu-1}\,d\rho .
\]

It is strictly positive for the stipulated seed. The angular
projection is contractive on \(L^2(A)\), and \(T\) is an isometry
on global \(L^2\). Therefore

\[
 \|q\|_{L^2(A)}+\|e\|_{L^2(B)}
 \ \ge\ \|q-T\widetilde e\|_{L^2(A)}
 \ \ge\ |t|\,d_{\rm sharp}.
\]

For \(e=0\) equality in the norm bound is precisely the
vanishing of the angular component of \(q\) orthogonal
to \(\mathcal Y\) on \(A\). The exact projection holds even
when that component is present. Equality is attained by
restriction and conformal flattening of the smooth round
TT solution for the global source \(t f_r\). E21 gives its
flattened opposite response \(tTf\), and it satisfies the
prescribed flat equation on \(B\). Hence no larger constant can
hold for all local metric responses. E26's Gram distance remains
the optimal constant for its relaxed problem with arbitrary
biharmonic residual. It is smaller here, since \(a>0\) on \(A\)
has nonzero pairing with the discarded regular power \(\rho^4\).
[E21, (2), (7)–(8)][e21] · [E26 distance][e26]

## Round and conditional nonlinear statements

For \(r=\Omega^2\delta\) on a supplied round patch,
\(u=\Omega^{-2}h\) satisfies
\(D_{\pm,\delta}u=\Omega^2D_{\pm,r}h\) in the induced
orthonormal frames. The local \(L^2\) norms agree under
this curvature scaling because \(dV_r=\Omega^4dx\).
Thus the same \(d_{\rm sharp}\) applies on the round patch
with \(f_r=\Omega^{-2}f\), and the exact angular pairing is
computed with the flattened opposite field \(\Omega^2D_{-,r}h\).

For a supplied \(C^2\)-small fixed-volume chart metric
\(g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\) with \(h\) trace-free,
let \(F_{r,\pm}(h)\) be the Weyl blocks transported into the
fixed targets. Suppose \(F_{r,+}(h)=t f_r\) on the patch.
The direct two-jet Taylor estimate of E26 gives
\(F_{r,\pm}(h)=D_{\pm,r}h+R_\pm\), with
\(\|R_\pm\|_{C^0(B,r)}\le C\|h\|_{C^2(B,r)}^2\).
Apply the linear estimate to \(h\), with error \(e_r=-R_+\),
and include \(R_-\) in the measured field. Because the chart
preserves the volume and the frame maps are isometries, this gives

\[
 \|W^-_{g_h}\|_{L^2(A,g_h)}
       \ge |t|\,d_{\rm sharp}-C'\|h\|_{C^2(B,r)}^2 .
\]

The same argument applied to the projected equation gives
\(\|P_{\mathcal Y}(\Omega^2F_{r,-}(h))-taY\|_{L^2(A,\delta)}
\le C'\|h\|_{C^2(B,r)}^2\). Both statements are conditional on
the supplied local positive-source lift. They leave existence
on an arbitrary exterior open. [E26, round patch and Taylor estimate][e26]
