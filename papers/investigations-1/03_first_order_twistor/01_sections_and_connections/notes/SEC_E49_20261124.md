## Source data and exact budgets

Let \(M\) be a fixed smooth closed connected oriented homotopy
four-sphere. Let \(g_i\) be smooth positive-definite unit-volume
Yamabe minimizers of the supplied marked conformal classes, and
write \(Y_i=Y(M,[g_i])=R_{g_i}>0\). The marking may vary or shrink.
Use the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt conventions
\(E_{i,\pm}=\int_M |W^\pm_{g_i}|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\,dV_{g_i}\),
with ASD meaning \(W^+=0\). The four-index squared norm of a chiral
block is four times its operator squared norm. Since \(\tau(M)=0\)
by the homotopy-sphere hypothesis, the signature identity gives
\(E_{i,+}=E_{i,-}=E_i\) and the full four-index Weyl integral is
\(8E_i\). Write \(T_{g_i}=\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}-R_{g_i}g_i/4\)
and \(D_i=\int_M |T_{g_i}|^2\,dV_{g_i}\). The hypotheses are
\[
 4\pi^2\le E_i\le8\pi^2-\delta,\qquad
 \frac{\int_M |B_{g_i}|_{g_i}\,dV_{g_i}}{E_i}\longrightarrow 0
 \tag{1}
\]
for fixed \(\delta>0\), with the covariant Bach tensor in the E48
convention. Its \(L^1\) norm is conformally invariant in dimension
four. Thus this condition has the same meaning if initially given
in the round-marked representative of each class. [B29][b29] ·
[SH-E01, signature and squared norms][recognition] ·
[E48, first variation][e48] · [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo,
§1, (1.1) and Bach covariance][bgim]

With \(\chi(M)=2\), Chern–Gauss–Bonnet in these norms gives the exact
unit-volume Yamabe identity
\[
 16\pi^2=2E_i-\tfrac12 D_i+\tfrac1{24}Y_i^2,\qquad
 Y_i^2=48(8\pi^2-E_i)+12D_i .
 \tag{2}
\]
The unit-volume Yamabe theorem and the round upper bound give
\(\sqrt{48\delta}\le Y_i\le 8\sqrt 6\,\pi\). The upper bound implies
\(D_i\le4E_i\). The \(D_i\) term in (2) is retained below.
The pointwise curvature decomposition in four-index norm is
\[
 |\operatorname{Rm}|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2
 =4\bigl(|W^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2+|W^-|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\bigr)
       +2|T|^2+\tfrac16 R^2 .
\]
Thus the initial integrals of \(R^2\), \(|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\)
and \(|\operatorname{Rm}|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2\) are exactly
\(Y_i^2\), \(D_i+Y_i^2/4\), and \(8E_i+2D_i+Y_i^2/6\),
respectively. The closed signature and Euler inputs use the real
oriented four-manifold, with the numerical values here coming from
the integral homology of the homotopy sphere.
[E36, (1)–(4)][e36] · [Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107, (0.4) and
Remark 2][cgy] · [Schoen, §3, Theorem 3][schoen] ·
[Lee–Parker, §2, curvature decomposition and §3, round value
and Aubin's bound][leeparker]

## Transfer to a supplied smooth limit

Suppose positive length scales \(\lambda_i\to0\), base points
\(p_i\in M\), and a smooth connected oriented positive-definite
pointed limit \((X,g_\infty,p_\infty)\) are supplied. Precisely,
on each compact part of \(X\) there are orientation-preserving
smooth embeddings from a neighborhood of that part into \(M\)
for all sufficiently large \(i\), identifying base points on
the pieces containing \(p_\infty\). The pullbacks of
\(\widetilde g_i=\lambda_i^{-2}g_i\) converge smoothly locally to
\(g_\infty\). The local analytic transfer only needs the given
embeddings and the indicated regularity. Completeness and ALE
end charts will be assumed separately for the boundary calculation.
After a further subsequence \(E_i\to e\) and \(Y_i\to y\). They
satisfy the same closed bounds and
\[
 D_i\longrightarrow D=\frac{y^2}{12}-4(8\pi^2-e),\qquad 0\le D\le4e .
 \tag{3}
\]

For real smooth \(f\) on \(M\), the initial minimizing property gives
\(Y_i\|f\|_{4,g_i}^{2}\le6\|df\|_{2,g_i}^2+Y_i\|f\|_{2,g_i}^2\).
Real test functions follow from the positive-test-function
form by approximation of \(|f|\). Under the chosen rescaling, the
squared \(L^4\) norm of \(f\) and squared \(L^2\) norm of \(df\)
each scale by \(\lambda_i^{-2}\), and the squared \(L^2\) norm of
\(f\) scales by \(\lambda_i^{-4}\). Consequently
\[
 \|f\|_{4,\widetilde g_i}^2
 \le \frac{6}{Y_i}\|df\|_{2,\widetilde g_i}^2
      +\lambda_i^2\|f\|_{2,\widetilde g_i}^2 .
 \tag{4}
\]
Transport each \(f\in C_c^\infty(X)\) to \(M\) and extend by zero
using an embedding on a neighborhood of its compact support.
Local convergence and the vanishing coefficient in (4) give
\[
 y\|f\|_{4,g_\infty}^2\le6\|df\|_{2,g_\infty}^2 .
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular the compactly supported Sobolev quotient of the
scalar-flat limit is at least \(y\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\).
The limit cannot be closed compact, by inserting the constant
one in (5) in that case. [E36, (3)][e36] ·
[Lee–Parker, §2, conformal Laplacian and Sobolev quotient][leeparker]

The scalar curvature of \(\widetilde g_i\) is \(\lambda_i^2Y_i\),
which tends to zero uniformly. It follows that \(R_{g_\infty}=0\).
For a positive constant \(c\), the two-covariant Bach tensor
obeys \(B_{cg}=c^{-1}B_g\), so \(|B_{cg}|_{cg}=c^{-2}|B_g|_g\)
and \(dV_{cg}=c^2dV_g\) in four dimensions. The total \(L^1\) norm
is therefore unchanged and tends to zero by (1) and the bound on
\(E_i\). The Bach tensor converges on the embedded compact pieces
by the assumed smooth convergence, using four metric derivatives.
On any such piece its limiting \(L^1\) integral is zero. Its smooth
limit is \(B_{g_\infty}=0\) on all of \(X\).
[Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1, (1.1)][bgim]

For the same rescaling and on the same embedded pieces, squared
\(L^2\) curvature integrals are scale invariant in four dimensions.
The trace-free Ricci tensor is unchanged as a two-covariant tensor
under constant scaling. The Weyl operator on two-forms scales
by \(c^{-1}\) under \(g\mapsto cg\), and the Hodge splitting is
unchanged. Orientation preservation prevents an interchange
of the chiralities under pullback. Smooth convergence on any
compact domain and scalar-flatness give
\[
 e_\pm^\infty:=E_\pm(g_\infty)\le e,\qquad
 D_\infty:=\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_\infty}|^2\,dV_{g_\infty}\le D,
 \qquad
 \int_X |\operatorname{Rm}_{g_\infty}|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2
 =4(e_+^\infty+e_-^\infty)+2D_\infty\le8e+2D .
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the inequalities first hold for all compact domains in
one exhaustion, then follow on \(X\) by monotone convergence.
The Ricci budget uses \(D\) from the trace-free Ricci tensor of
the original Yamabe minimizers, since in the limit \(R=0\).
The closed equality between chiral energies does not impose
equality on each domain or on \(X\). [E36, (2)–(4)][e36] ·
[SH-E01, chirality and norms][recognition]

To track losses, exhaust \(X\) by compact smooth domains \(K_j\).
For each \(j\), subtract the integral on its embedded image
from the corresponding total source integral in \(\widetilde g_i\).
Take \(i\to\infty\) first and then \(j\to\infty\). The residuals
for the two Weyl blocks, trace-free Ricci and scalar square are
\[
  \ell_+=e-e_+^\infty,\qquad \ell_-=e-e_-^\infty,\qquad
  \ell_T=D-D_\infty,\qquad \ell_R=y^2 .
 \tag{7}
\]
All are nonnegative. In particular all scalar-square mass is
outside the limiting exhaustion in this order of limits, even
though the rescaled scalar curvatures tend uniformly to zero.
The source volume on the rescaled metrics is \(\lambda_i^{-4}\).
The full four-index curvature loss is
\(4(\ell_++\ell_-)+2\ell_T+y^2/6\).
These residuals are bookkeeping on the supplied convergence.
They do not locate the residual curvature in a background,
neck or another scale, and need no energy identity across
several limits.

## When the full ALE core is supplied

Assume now that \(X\) is complete and has finitely many ends,
\(k\ge1\), and that the exterior of a connected full compact
truncation is precisely the union of their product collars.
On each end suppose there is an oriented coordinate identification
with \((\mathbb R^4\setminus B)/\Gamma_j\), where
\(\Gamma_j\subset SO(4)\) is finite and acts freely on \(S^3\).
In the lifted coordinates suppose
\(\partial^\alpha(g_{\infty,ab}-\delta_{ab})
=O(r^{-\gamma_j-|\alpha|})\) for all multi-indices with
\(\gamma_j>0\). Let \(N\) be a sufficiently large compact
truncation with all these collars deleted past their cuts, so
\(\partial N=\bigsqcup_j S^3/\Gamma_j=:Y_{\partial}\).
Require actual orientation-preserving smooth embeddings of
the full \(N\) into \(M\) from the convergence. This is automatic
for a convergence defined on a neighborhood of each compact
subset of the entire \(X\). A convergence on only a regular
subset of an orbifold does not give that premise. The collar
ends are products, so \(X\) retracts onto \(N\).
[Tian–Viaclovsky, §1, (1.3)–(1.4)][tv] · [E166 assessment][e166]

Write \(V=\overline{M\setminus N}\) after identifying \(N\) with
an embedded image. Collars give open thickenings of the two
pieces and their intersection for Mayer–Vietoris. Each boundary
component is an oriented closed rational homology three-sphere
and \(H_2(Y_{\partial};\mathbb Z)=0\), since its \(H_1\) is finite
and degree-one cohomology is its integral dual. With
\(H_2(M;\mathbb Z)=H_1(M;\mathbb Z)=0\), Mayer–Vietoris gives
\[
 H_2(N;\mathbb Z)=H_2(V;\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad
 H_1(Y_{\partial};\mathbb Z)
 \simeq H_1(N;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(V;\mathbb Z).
 \tag{8}
\]
In particular \(H_1(N;\mathbb Z)\) is finite. Also the map
\(H_0(Y_{\partial})\to H_0(N)\oplus H_0(V)\) is injective.
Any two boundary components adjoining the same component of \(V\)
would give a difference in its kernel. Every component of \(V\)
meets the boundary, by connectedness of \(M\), so the components
of \(V\) correspond to the \(k\) boundary components.

The relative rational fundamental class of \(N\) maps to the
sum of its oriented boundary classes. Since
\(H_3(N,Y_{\partial};\mathbb Q)\cong H^1(N;\mathbb Q)=0\),
the relative exact sequence gives \(b_3(N)=k-1\).
Since \(N\) is connected with nonempty boundary, \(b_4(N)=0\).
Thus
\[
 \chi(X)=\chi(N)=2-k,\qquad \tau(X)=\tau(N)=0 .
 \tag{9}
\]
Here the signature is that of the intersection form on the
image of relative in absolute degree-two cohomology of \(N\).
These homology and signature conclusions use only the integral
homology sphere hypothesis on \(M\) and the full smooth core
embedding. When \(k=1\) and the link is \(S^3\), van Kampen in the
same collar cover gives
\(\pi_1(M)\simeq\pi_1(N)*\pi_1(V)\). Now the actual simple
connectivity of the homotopy sphere implies \(\pi_1(X)=0\).
[Hatcher, Theorem 1.20, §2.2 Mayer–Vietoris, Theorem 3.43][hatcher]

The Chern–Gauss–Bonnet and APS signature formulas in the chosen
norms, on letting the cuts run to infinity, are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 8\pi^2\left(\chi(X)-\sum_j|\Gamma_j|^{-1}\right)
   &=e_+^\infty+e_-^\infty-\tfrac12 D_\infty,\\
 \tau(X)&=\frac{e_+^\infty-e_-^\infty}{12\pi^2}
            -\sum_j\eta(S^3/\Gamma_j).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{10}
\]
For the first formula the Euler boundary term on a radius \(R\)
cut tends to \(8\pi^2/|\Gamma_j|\) in the normalized identity.
Indeed the induced metric after scaling, second fundamental
form after multiplication by \(R\), and scaled ambient curvature
have errors \(O(R^{-\gamma_j})\) relative to the flat quotient.
The Euler boundary integral, formed from the second fundamental
form and curvature, consequently has error \(O(R^{-\gamma_j})\)
from its flat value. Flat Euclidean spheres contribute the
whole Euler integral for their balls, and the quotient divides
the boundary term by \(|\Gamma_j|\).

For the signature formula use the odd-signature \(\eta\) of
APS (4.14) on the outward oriented links with their round
quotient metric. To apply the product boundary version, conformally
multiply the end metrics by \(r^{-2}\) and put \(s=\log r\).
They become \(ds^2+g_{Y,\mathrm{rd}}+O(e^{-\gamma_j s})\) with
the corresponding derivatives. On a collar of fixed \(s\)-width
near each distant cut, smoothly interpolate to the exact product.
That product is locally conformally flat, and the modification
has \(O(e^{-2\gamma_j s})\) Weyl energy on the collar. Conformal
invariance of the two chiral energies then gives the same bulk
integrals in the limit. This justifies using the product
boundary theorem with no added second fundamental form term.
The round sphere has \(\eta(S^3)=0\), since an orientation-reversing
isometry conjugates the odd-signature operator to its negative.
[Atiyah–Patodi–Singer I, Theorem (4.14)][aps] ·
[Tian–Viaclovsky, §7, (7.1)–(7.2)][tv] ·
[Wright, §6.2, (5) and the round link][wright]

The source and limit Euler formulas also give the bookkeeping
identity
\[
 8\pi^2\left(2-\chi(X)+\sum_j|\Gamma_j|^{-1}\right)
     =\ell_++\ell_--\tfrac12\ell_T+\tfrac1{24}y^2 .
 \tag{11}
\]
The two chiral residuals may differ in general. By (9)–(10), the
difference on \(X\) is \(12\pi^2\sum_j\eta(S^3/\Gamma_j)\), and
the residual difference in (7) has its opposite sign.

## What the trivial link rules out

For one end and \(\Gamma_1=1\), equations (9)–(10) give
\[
 e_+^\infty=e_-^\infty=:e_X,\qquad D_\infty=4e_X .
 \tag{12}
\]
A Ricci-flat supplied model has \(D_\infty=0\) and hence both
Weyl blocks zero, also directly from the Euler equality in (10).
If either Weyl block vanishes, APS forces the other one to
vanish and the Euler equality then forces \(D_\infty=0\).
In both cases smoothness and scalar-flatness imply
\(\operatorname{Rm}=0\). By the simply connected computation
above, the complete flat metric is isometric to Euclidean
\(\mathbb R^4\). For instance a global parallel coframe on
the simply connected flat manifold integrates to a local
isometry into Euclidean space, and completeness makes it a
covering. Thus there is no nonflat example of either type with
the stated trivial end and full core. If all links are trivial
and \(k\ge2\), Ricci-flatness is already incompatible with the
negative left-hand side of the first formula in (10). For a
half-conformally flat such model, APS again kills both blocks,
so \(D_\infty=32\pi^2(k-1)\), beyond the fixed-margin budget
\(D\le32\pi^2-4\delta\). This is only the all-trivial-link case.

The supplied-core obstruction in E166 also applies when a
connected complete smooth nonflat model is both Ricci flat
and ASD, has a Wright ALE end, and its full compact truncation
embeds with orientation in an integral homology four-sphere.
For the opposite chirality reverse both orientations.
Wright's definition requires one end, a free spherical
quotient and positive differentiable ALE order. The E166
conclusion excludes that joint subclass, including with
nontrivial group. The condition of being Ricci flat alone
or half-conformally flat alone does not supply both hypotheses.
A regular region of an orbifold does not supply the full
smooth core. [E166 assessment][e166] ·
[Wright, Theorem A, §2 definition and Remark 2][wright]

## Limits of the transfer

The scalar and Bach equations do not imply the Einstein
equation in general. A complete explicit comparison is
four-dimensional Riemannian Schwarzschild,
\(g=(1+c/|x|^2)^2g_{\mathrm{Eucl}}\) on
\(\mathbb R^4\setminus\{0\}\), \(c>0\).
The positive factor is harmonic in the punctured flat space,
so the dimension-four conformal Laplacian formula gives
scalar-flatness. The metric is conformally flat and Bach flat.
Inversion identifies the small-radius end with another
asymptotically Euclidean end and gives completeness there.
The two ends have trivial links and the core is
\(S^3\times[0,1]\), with Euler characteristic zero.
The first formula of (10) gives
\(\int|\operatorname{Ric}|^2=32\pi^2>0\), so this scalar-flat
Bach-flat model is not Einstein. It lies outside (6) for every
fixed positive \(\delta\). Within the one-ended trivial-link
class, the transferred budgets and (12) allow simultaneous
nonzero \(e_X,D_\infty\) at the level of these identities.
They do not construct or exclude such a metric.
[Tian–Viaclovsky, §1 after Theorem 1.3, Schwarzschild example][tv]

The scale-invariant \(L^1\) Bach control of (1) is only an
integral control of the fourth-order residual. The source
estimates give no local bounds for its stronger norms or
derivatives and no theorem supplying smooth convergence at a
concentration scale. In particular (5)–(7) need the given
embeddings, and curvature-scale normalization is an extra
input if the limit is to be nonflat. Tian–Viaclovsky Theorem
1.1 starts with a complete exact scalar-flat Bach-flat metric
with finite curvature energy and a finite Sobolev constant.
Its finite-\(b_1\)
conclusion gives ALE of order zero. Their positive-order
Theorem 1.3 uses the additional half-flat or harmonic-curvature
hypothesis. These passages do not extract smooth limits for
the almost-Bach-flat sequence here. If an open conformally
flat mark survives in suitable charts it would be extra
geometric data. A shrinking mark supplies no such open
region. The E48 examples approach \(8\pi^2\) and therefore
cannot test the fixed positive margin in (1).
[Tian–Viaclovsky, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3][tv] · [E48][e48] ·
