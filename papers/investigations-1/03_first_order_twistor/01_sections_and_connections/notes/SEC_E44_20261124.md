## Fixed setting and hypotheses

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere.
Fix a smooth real positive-definite metric \(r\), round by an oriented
identification on a coordinate ball \(O\), and a closed coordinate
subball \(K\Subset O\). Write \(\nu=dV_r\), \(V=S^2_0T^*M\) and
\(B=S^2_0\Lambda_r^+\), with the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt
pairing on \(B\). Here ASD means \(W^+=0\). In the full trace-free chart
\(g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\) with \(h^\sharp=r^{-1}h\), set
\[
 F(h)=I_hW_{g_h}^+I_h^{-1},\qquad W=F(0),\qquad L=dF(0),
\]
where \(I_h\) is induced by the positive tangent isometry
\(e^{-h^\sharp/2}\colon(TM,r)\to(TM,g_h)\). All adjoints use \(r,\nu\).
Fix an integer \(s\ge5\); the curvature map is smooth
\(H^{s+2}(V)\to H^s(B)\) on this chart. Let \(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\)
be the finite-dimensional real space of smooth adjoint fields.
Define \(b(f)(Z)=\int_M\langle Z,f\rangle\,d\nu\) for \(Z\in\mathcal C\),
and let
\[
 \mu(x)(Z)=\langle Z(x),W(x)\rangle,\qquad x\in M\setminus K.
\]
Since \(W=0\) on \(O\), \(b(W)=\int_{M\setminus K}\mu\,d\nu\).
[E03, (1), (3)–(4)][e03] · [E27, fixed data and (1)–(4)][e27]

Assume the probe property stipulated in the [issued request][request].
For each finite collection of disjoint, arbitrarily small exterior
coordinate probes with real smooth compactly supported amplitudes
\(\beta_j\), there are smooth real \(k_{j,N}\in\ker L\) and \(\gamma>0\)
such that
\[
 b\big(d^2F(0)[k_{i,N},k_{j,N}]\big)\longrightarrow
 \begin{cases}
 \gamma\displaystyle\int\beta_j^2\,\mu\,d\nu & i=j,\\
 0 & i\ne j .
 \end{cases}                                                   \tag{1}
\]
The implication below also assumes that for **each** nonzero real
\(Z\in\mathcal C\), the exterior function \(\mu(\cdot)(Z)\) takes both
strict signs.

Under these hypotheses there exists \(\epsilon>0\) and a spatially
smooth family \(h_\tau\) for \(|\tau|<\epsilon\), jointly smooth in
\((\tau,x)\), with \(h_0=0\) and
\[
 F(h_\tau)=(1-\tau^2)W .                                        \tag{2}
\]
Thus (2) gives a one-sided uncorrected chord for \(0\le t<\epsilon^2\)
with \(t=\tau^2\) and \(\tau=\sqrt t\). The underlying manifold, chart
and prescribed comparison section \(W\) are the original ones.

## Finite positive hull and disjoint probes

First suppose \(m=\dim\mathcal C>0\). Equip \(\mathcal C\) with its
reference \(L^2\) norm and \(\mathcal C^*\) with the dual norm.
For each unit \(Z\), there is an exterior point \(x\) with
\(\mu(x)(Z)<0\). These strict inequalities define an open cover of the
unit sphere in \(\mathcal C\). Take a finite subcover, with duplicate
points removed, \(x_1,\dots,x_n\). Compactness gives a strictly positive
margin
\[
 \delta=\min_{\|Z\|=1}\max_j\{-\mu(x_j)(Z)\}>0 .
\]
Applying the same bound to \(-Z\) bounds the support function of
\(\operatorname{conv}\{\mu(x_j)\}\) from below by \(\delta\) in every
unit direction. In particular this convex hull contains the closed dual
ball of radius \(\delta\). Indeed, for a point outside the convex hull,
the direction from its nearest convex-hull point to that point separates
it strictly from the hull. A point of norm \(\le\delta\) cannot satisfy
that strict inequality. Thus the positive hull, the set of finite
nonnegative linear combinations, is all \(\mathcal C^*\). Perturbing
each vector by less than \(\delta/2\) retains a ball of radius at least
\(\delta/2\) in the convex hull.

The \(x_j\) are distinct points of the open exterior. Choose coordinate
balls about them with pairwise disjoint closures in the exterior and
of arbitrarily small radii. By continuity take
\(\|\mu(x)-\mu(x_j)\|<\delta/2\) on the \(j\)-th ball. Choose a nonzero
real smooth bump \(\beta_j\) supported there and normalize
\(\int\beta_j^2\,d\nu=1\). Its averaged vector
\(\int\beta_j^2\mu\,d\nu\) is within \(\delta/2\) of \(\mu(x_j)\).
Hence the vectors
\[
 v_j=\tfrac{\gamma}{2}\int\beta_j^2\,\mu\,d\nu
\]
have positive hull \(\mathcal C^*\) and span it. The finite configuration
therefore has both a strict perturbation margin and disjoint
localization.

## Transfer of a regular zero to finite \(N\)

Set \(z=-b(W)\). Since the positive hull is all \(\mathcal C^*\), write
\(z-\sum_jv_j=\sum_jd_jv_j\) with each \(d_j\ge0\). Then
\(z=\sum_j\lambda_jv_j\) and \(\lambda_j=1+d_j>0\), including when
\(z=0\). Set \(a_j^0=\sqrt{\lambda_j}\) and define
\[
 k_N(a)=\sum_j a_jk_{j,N},\qquad
 q_N(a)=\tfrac12 b\big(d^2F(0)[k_N(a),k_N(a)]\big),\qquad
 q_\infty(a)=\sum_j a_j^2v_j .
\]
The symmetric arrays of quadratic coefficients of \(q_N\) converge by
(1) to those of \(q_\infty\). In particular the polynomials and their
derivatives converge uniformly on each bounded amplitude set.
Choose \(m\) indices \(J\) with independent \(v_j\). Freeze the
amplitudes with indices outside \(J\) at \(a_j^0\). At \(a^0\),
\(q_\infty(a^0)=z\) and the derivative in the \(J\) variables has
columns \(2a_j^0v_j\), an isomorphism onto \(\mathcal C^*\).

Apply the finite-dimensional implicit function theorem with the
quadratic coefficients as parameters, keeping the same frozen
amplitudes and target \(z\). For every sufficiently large finite \(N\)
there is a zero \(a^N\to a^0\) of \(q_N(a)-z\), with all \(a_j^N>0\)
and the \(J\)-derivative still an isomorphism. Fix one such \(N\) and
write \(k(a)=k_N(a)\). The squared amplitudes are strictly positive at
the finite zero as well. The tensor \(k_* = k(a^N)\) is nonzero because
if it were zero, bilinearity would give \(dq_N(a^N)=0\). Only one
finite smooth family is needed in the nonlinear step. The Sobolev
smallness interval may depend on this choice of \(N\).

## Solving the exact range and moment equations

Take the E27 exterior corrections \(\mathcal Wc=\sum_i c_iwZ_i\),
where \(Z_i\) is an \(L^2\)-orthonormal real basis of \(\mathcal C\)
and \(w\) is smooth, zero on \(K\) and strictly positive on its
exterior. In these coordinates \(\Gamma=b\mathcal W\) is invertible.
E27 proves this by transplanting a field that vanishes off \(K\)
to the round sphere and extending by zero before the boundary of
the round patch. The full round adjoint has trivial kernel by the
round comparison in E02 and the round ASD unobstructed example of
Gover–Gursky. The \(L^2\) adjoint statement is used only on that round
sphere. [E27, exterior restriction and Gram matrices][e27] ·
[E02, round inverse][e02] ·
[Gover–Gursky, §1.1, (1.2) and pp. 295–296][gg]

Let \(U_s=H^{s+4}(B)\cap\mathcal C^\perp\) and \(P=LL^*\).
The symbol \(|\xi|_r^4 I_B/16\) is elliptic, and \(P:U_s\to\ker b\)
is invertible with inverse \(G_P|_{\ker b}\). This is the closed
manifold self-adjoint Fredholm alternative with the Sobolev gain for
the smooth elliptic bundle operator \(P\).
[E03, (3)–(4)][e03] · [Melrose, Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose]

For \(a\) close to \(a^N\) in the \(J\) variables, solve the E27
parametrized augmented equation
\[
 F\big(\tau k(a)+L^*u\big)-W+\mathcal Wc=-\tau^2 W,
 \qquad (u,c)\in U_s\times\mathbb R^m .                          \tag{3}
\]
Projecting by \(I-\mathcal W\Gamma^{-1}b\) gives the range equation
in \(\ker b\). The augmented derivative in \((u,c)\) at \(\tau=0\)
is \(P\,\dot u+\mathcal W\dot c\). Its inverse maps an increment \(f\)
to
\[
 \dot c=\Gamma^{-1}b(f),\qquad
 \dot u=G_P\big(f-\mathcal W\Gamma^{-1}b(f)\big).
\]
Thus (3) has a smooth small solution \((u(\tau,a),c(\tau,a))\) at
the stated Sobolev grades. For all nearby \(a\) its value at \(\tau=0\)
is zero. Its first \(\tau\)-derivative there is also zero since
\(Lk(a)=0\) and the source has no linear term. In particular
\(h(\tau,a)=\tau k(a)+L^*u(\tau,a)=\tau k(a)+O(\tau^2)\) in
\(H^{s+2}(V)\) locally uniformly in \(a\).
[E27, (1)–(4) and the parameter \(k\) discussion][e27]

To remove the correction, define
\(\widehat h(\tau,a)=\int_0^1\partial_\tau h(\rho\tau,a)\,d\rho\);
then \(h=\tau\widehat h\) and \(\widehat h(0,a)=k(a)\). Taylor's
formula with integral remainder produces the smooth divided
moment function
\[
 D(\tau,a)=b(W)+
 \int_0^1(1-\theta)\,
 b\big(d^2F(\theta h(\tau,a))[\widehat h(\tau,a),\widehat h(\tau,a)]\big)
 \,d\theta .
\]
In fact, \(bL=0\) and (3) give
\(\Gamma c(\tau,a)=-\tau^2D(\tau,a)\), including at zero.
The values at \(\tau=0\) are \(D(0,a)=b(W)+q_N(a)\). Thus the
finite-\(N\) zero and its invertible \(J\)-derivative give a smooth
\(a(\tau)\) with \(a(0)=a^N\) and \(D(\tau,a(\tau))=0\).
Consequently \(c(\tau,a(\tau))=0\) identically and
\(h_\tau=h(\tau,a(\tau))\) satisfies (2). This solves the actual
nonlinear moment equation in the fixed bundle. [Working manuscript,
labels lift:corrected, lift:balance and outlook:quadratic][ms]

If \(\mathcal C=\{0\}\), the cone is the zero space and the empty
configuration and empty moment equation suffice. Take \(k=0\) and
solve \(F(L^*u)-W=-tW\) directly with \(t\) as parameter using
invertibility of \(P\). This branch is smooth in \(t\) near zero.

## Spatial and time regularity

The bootstrap uses smooth \(r,W,w,Z_i\), the finitely many fixed
smooth probe tensors, a closed manifold and invertibility of the
augmented derivative at the small solution. Work on all of
\(H^{s+4}(B)\) and add the row \(b(u)=0\) to (3). At zero the
derivative is \((v,d)\mapsto(Pv+\mathcal Wd,b(v))\), whose inverse
adds the \(\mathcal C\) component specified by the second row to
the inverse above. It stays invertible for small \(|\tau|\) by
continuity in the fixed Sobolev grades. On a framed ball conjugate
the fourth-order differential row by local component pullbacks
\(T_\eta\) that equal translations on a smaller ball and the
identity off a larger one. With \(a=a(\tau)\), smoothness of the
finite inputs and the Sobolev chain rule give smooth dependence of
the conjugated row and source on \((\eta,\tau)\). In the moment
row \(b(T_\eta^{-1}v)\), changing variables transfers the
\(\eta\)-dependence to smooth densities and bundle pairings.

Strong continuity of the pullbacks identifies the translated solution
with the local implicit solution. Hence \(T_\eta u(\tau,a(\tau))\)
depends smoothly on \((\eta,\tau)\) in \(H^{s+4}\). On the smaller
ball, derivatives in \(\eta\) give arbitrary additional spatial
derivatives there, with smooth dependence on \(\tau\) in the
corresponding local Sobolev spaces. A finite cover and Sobolev
embedding give the asserted joint smoothness of \(h_\tau\),
\(g_{h_\tau}\) and its compatible chiral connection on the same
parameter interval. The argument with the row absent gives the
same conclusion in \(t\) when \(m=0\).
[E27, spatial regularity][e27] · [Working manuscript, §Prescribing
chiral curvature, paragraph Spatial regularity][ms]

For \(m>0\), this constructed branch has
\(h_\tau=\tau k_*+O(\tau^2)\) in each fixed finite spatial norm.
In \(t=\tau^2\) it is \(1/2\)-Hölder up to \(t=0\) and smooth for
\(t>0\). Because \(k_*\ne0\), its right derivative at \(t=0\)
does not exist in the chart norm, and the same holds for the
metric, whose derivative in the chart at zero is the identity on
\(V\). For a different branch, differentiability at zero in the
domain norm would impose \(L\partial_t h(0)=-W\) and thus
\(b(W)=0\). All metrics have volume \(\nu\) and the transported
Weyl block remains zero on \(K\). The metric on \(K\) itself can
vary. [E03, tangent obstruction][e03] · [E27, interface matching][e27]

## Scope of one-sign tests

For any nonzero \(Z\in\mathcal C\), write \(f_Z=\mu(\cdot)(Z)\).
If \(f_Z\ge0\) on the exterior, every limiting mixture evaluates
nonnegatively on \(Z\). The positive hull can then lie only in the
closed half-space evaluated by \(Z\), so the strict both-signs
criterion is also necessary for a full limiting cone. If \(f_Z\)
is positive somewhere, smoothness and the open exterior give
\(b(W)(Z)>0\); the target evaluated on \(Z\) is strictly negative.
This excludes the leading balance for every limiting mixture.
For each *fixed* finite configuration, uniform convergence on
bounded amplitude sets also excludes the finite-\(N\) balance
there once \(N\) is sufficiently large. If each selected averaged
pairing \(v_j(Z)\) is strictly positive, the limiting scalar
quadratic form is positive definite and coefficient convergence
excludes balance for all amplitudes for sufficiently large \(N\)
in that configuration. Zero averaged pairings leave small
finite-\(N\) errors potentially relevant at unbounded amplitudes.

If \(f_Z\equiv0\) on the exterior, the limiting mixtures and
target both vanish on \(Z\). The limiting equation in that row is
identically satisfied and its derivative in every amplitude
vanishes. Thus the test loses limiting full rank. It gives no
scalar incompatibility in that row, and (1) gives only a vanishing
limit there, allowing finite-\(N\) terms to affect exact rank and
balance. The case \(f_Z\le0\) is covered by replacing \(Z\) by
\(-Z\). These restrictions concern the stipulated probe mixtures.
The Hessian on other exact kernel tensors is unconstrained by
this argument.
