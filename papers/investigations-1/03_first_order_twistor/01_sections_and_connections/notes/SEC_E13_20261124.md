## Collar and fixed target

Let \(I=[-1,1]\) and orient \(I\times T^3\) by
\(ds\wedge dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3\). Take the smooth positive-definite
metrics

\[
 g_a=ds^2+\sum_{i=1}^3e^{2a_i(s)}dx_i^2,\qquad
 \sum_i a_i=0,
\]

where every \(a_i\) vanishes on open collars of both boundary components.
The reference is \(r=g_{a^0}\) for \(a^0=(f,f,-2f)\) and a smooth,
nonzero function \(f\) supported in the interior of \(I\).
All metrics have volume \(ds\,dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3\). The B01 exponential
chart perturbation relative to \(r\), in the coordinate tangent basis,
is the endomorphism with diagonal entries
\(0,2(a_1-a_1^0),2(a_2-a_2^0),2(a_3-a_3^0)\). The positive symmetric
isometry from \(r\) to \(g_a\) fixes \(\partial_s\) and sends
\(\partial_{x_i}\) to \(e^{a_i^0-a_i}\partial_{x_i}\). In the orthonormal
coframe \(\theta^0=ds,\ \theta^i=e^{a_i}dx_i\), the chiral forms

\[
 \omega_i^\pm(a)=
 \frac{\theta^0\wedge\theta^i\ \pm\ \theta^j\wedge\theta^k}{\sqrt2},
 \qquad (i,j,k)\text{ cyclic in }(1,2,3),
\]

are in their respective Hodge eigenspaces for the stated orientation.
The isometry pulls \(\omega_i^\pm(a)\) back to
\(\omega_i^\pm(a^0)\) for each \(i\). The prescribed equality therefore
compares indexed matrix entries in these fixed frames, including
where some eigenvalues coincide. [B01 chart][b01] · [B02 target][b02]

## Sectional curvatures and Weyl blocks

Use the sectional curvature sign positive on the unit round sphere.
Write \(p_i=a'_i\) and \(S=\sum_i p_i^2\). In the dual frame
\(e_0=\partial_s,\ e_i=e^{-a_i}\partial_{x_i}\), the nonzero frame
brackets are \([e_0,e_i]=-p_i e_i\) and the Levi-Civita derivatives
are \(\nabla_{e_i}e_0=p_i e_i,\ \nabla_{e_i}e_i=-p_i e_0\).
The other derivatives of the frame vanish. Substitution into
\(R(X,Y)=[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}\) gives a diagonal
curvature operator on the coordinate two-planes, with

\[
 K_{0i}=-p_i'-p_i^2,\qquad K_{jk}=-p_jp_k \quad (j\ne k).
\]

In the chiral forms each diagonal curvature block has \(i\)-th
entry \((K_{0i}+K_{jk})/2\). The off-diagonal block is diagonal with
entries \((K_{0i}-K_{jk})/2\) and does not enter either Weyl block.
The scalar curvature is
\(s_{g_a}=2\sum_i(K_{0i}+K_{jk})=-S\), since
\(\sum_i p_i'=0\) and \(\sum_{j<k}p_jp_k=-S/2\). Subtract \(s_{g_a}/12\)
from each diagonal entry. Since \(p_jp_k=p_i^2-S/2\) for cyclic
\((i,j,k)\), both Weyl blocks have the same ordered triple

\[
 (w_1,w_2,w_3),\qquad
 w_i=-\frac12p_i'-p_i^2+\frac{S}{3}.
\]

These are the curvature-operator eigenvalues in the orthonormal
Hilbert–Schmidt normalization. In particular the trace vanishes. The
matching ordered triples for \(W^+\) and \(W^-\) verify that either
chirality vanishes in this model exactly when the full Weyl tensor
vanishes. [Shared conventions][shared] · [Gover–Gursky v1, §1 curvature blocks][gg]

## Reduction using one flat end

The first two entries of the reference block agree. If
\(W^+_{g_a}=\lambda W^+_r\) in the transported frame, they must also
agree for \(g_a\). Their difference gives

\[
 (p_1-p_2)'+2(p_1+p_2)(p_1-p_2)=0,
 \qquad
 \big(e^{2(a_1+a_2)}(p_1-p_2)\big)'=0 .
\]

The difference \(p_1-p_2\) vanishes on the left flat collar and hence
on \(I\). The left collar also fixes \(a_1-a_2=0\). Set
\(a=a_1=a_2\), so \(a_3=-2a\), and put \(y=e^{-2a}\), \(y_0=e^{-2f}\).
In this family \(S=6(a')^2\), and

\[
 W^+_{g_a}
 =\frac{y''}{4y}\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2),\qquad
 \frac{y''}{4y}=-\frac12 a''+(a')^2 .
\]

The prescribed Weyl equation becomes

\[
 -y''+\lambda V y=0,\qquad V=\frac{y_0''}{y_0},\qquad
 y(-1)=y(1)=1,\qquad y'(-1)=y'(1)=0.
\]

The boundary equations follow because \(y\) is identically 1 near
both ends, and \(y_0\) has the same property. The Neumann equations
alone will suffice for the obstruction.

## Strict quadratic-form test for \(0<\lambda<1\)

For real \(u\in H^1(I)\), let
\(\mathcal Q_\lambda[u]=\int_I(|u'|^2+\lambda V|u|^2)\,ds\).
Since \(y_0>0\) and \(y_0'=0\) on both collars, writing \(u=y_0v\)
and integrating the resulting total derivative yields

\[
 \mathcal Q_1[u]=\int_I y_0^2|v'|^2\,ds
 +\left[\frac{y_0'}{y_0}|u|^2\right]_{-1}^{1}
 =\int_I y_0^2|(u/y_0)'|^2\,ds .
\]

Thus \(\mathcal Q_1\) is nonnegative with null line
\(\mathbb R y_0\). The form \(\mathcal Q_0\) is nonnegative with null
line \(\mathbb R\) of constant functions. The two lines intersect only
at zero because \(f\) is nonzero and vanishes near the ends, so
\(y_0\) is nonconstant. Consequently

\[
 \mathcal Q_\lambda[u]=(1-\lambda)\mathcal Q_0[u]
                       +\lambda\mathcal Q_1[u]>0
 \quad\text{for }0<\lambda<1\text{ and }u\ne0 .
\]

Any Neumann solution \(y\) of the prescribed equation satisfies
\(\mathcal Q_\lambda[y]=[y\,y']_{-1}^1=0\) by integration by parts.
For \(0<\lambda<1\) this implies \(y=0\), contradicting \(y=e^{-2a}>0\).
Therefore the unchanged Weyl chord admits no metric in the frozen
class for any interior \(\lambda\). At \(\lambda=0\), \(y=1\)
gives the flat collar. At \(\lambda=1\), \(y=y_0\) gives the seed.
Both match the prescribed flat metrics on open end collars, so every
normal derivative also matches. The respective null lines and the
boundary value \(y=1\) imply uniqueness within this ansatz at each
endpoint.

## Application and boundary of the result

A collar of this smooth product type embeds in an oriented chart on
any smooth four-manifold. A product of two small circles in
\(\mathbb R^2\times\mathbb R^2\) has a trivial oriented normal
two-plane bundle, using the two radial normals. The boundary of its
small normal disk bundle is \(T^2\times S^1\). A shell of positive
normal radii gives the required \(I\times T^3\) with a compatible
orientation. The flat product metrics on the end collars can be
extended unchanged to slightly larger collars. A smooth partition of
unity outside then interpolates the extension with any positive
exterior metric. The two model endpoint metrics admit the same
exterior and agree in their volume forms. This construction supplies
collar metrics on any \(M\) in the program. Their prescribed Weyl
endpoint statements concern the collar.

Any putative global lift whose restriction stays in the specified
diagonal class and preserves both flat end collars would have to solve
the excluded collar equation. The full metric domain in B02 permits
variations outside that class. Its solvability for this source remains
undecided. The result returns the fixed two-ended ansatz to B11.
[B11 decision][b11]
