## Fixed chart and analytic spaces

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere
with a supplied smooth real positive-definite Riemannian metric \(r\).
Put \(\nu=dV_r\), \(E=\Lambda_r^+\), \(V=S^2_0T^*M\) and
\(B=S^2_0E\), viewed as real trace-free symmetric endomorphisms of
\(E\) with the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. ASD means
\(W^+=0\). Write \(W=W_r^+\). For a section \(h\) of the **full**
trace-free domain \(V\) set
\[
 g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot),\qquad h^\sharp=r^{-1}h,\qquad
 S_h=e^{-h^\sharp/2}:(TM,r)\longrightarrow (TM,g_h),\qquad
 I_h=\Lambda^2 S_h^*: \Lambda_{g_h}^+\longrightarrow E.
\]
The isometry is orientation preserving. The volume is pointwise fixed
because \(dV_{g_h}=e^{\operatorname{tr}_r h/2}\nu=\nu\). Every nearby
positive metric with volume form \(\nu\) has a unique trace-free
symmetric logarithm in the chart. Define
\[
 F_r(h)=I_h W_{g_h}^+ I_h^{-1},\qquad L=L_r=dF_r(0):\Gamma(V)\to\Gamma(B).
\tag{1}
\]
Here \(F_r(0)=W\) and the prescribed source \((1-t)W\) is a fixed section
of \(B\) at each \(t\). Formal adjoints always use the reference metrics
and \(\nu\). With the chart's normalized two-forms and
\(A=\Gamma^+(g_h)\) transported via \(I_h\), the compatibility equation
holds and the trace-free symmetric curvature equation is (1).
[B01 field space][b01] · [B02 choice of source and full domain][b02] ·
[CME `rec:chiral-curvature`][cme]

Use the high Sobolev realization of the requested regularity grades.
Fix an integer \(s\ge5\) and use \(\mathcal X=H^{s+2}(V)\) and
\(\mathcal Y=H^s(B)\) on the closed four-manifold. The same argument
can start with any higher fixed \(s\). For a desired \(k,\alpha\), taking
\(s>k+\alpha+2\) embeds these spaces into the corresponding
\(C^{k+2,\alpha},C^{k,\alpha}\) spaces. In local coordinates, curvature
is linear in second metric derivatives with smooth coefficients in the
metric and inverse metric, plus terms quadratic in first derivatives.
Matrix exponentiation, inversion near \(r\), the Hodge projection,
and the frame identification in (1) are smooth algebraic or smooth
pointwise operations in the metric. Sobolev multiplication and smooth
composition since \(s>2\) give a smooth Banach map
\(F_r:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y\) near zero. [Pinned E02][e02]

## Conformal scaling and longitudinal metric directions

For this calculation allow a general positive metric \(g\) and let
\(\widetilde F_r(g)\) denote its Weyl operator expressed in the same
symmetric frame over \(r\). In dimension four the Hodge splitting on
two-forms is conformally invariant. Under \(g\mapsto\rho g\) for any
smooth positive function \(\rho\), the Weyl endomorphism scales by
\(\rho^{-1}\), and the scalar factor in the frame transport cancels
in conjugation. Thus \(\widetilde F_r(\rho g)=\rho^{-1}\widetilde F_r(g)\).
An unrestricted pure trace variation \(2fr\) would give \(-2fW\).
The reference-volume chart removes these directions pointwise. A
nonzero fixed Weyl source need not be preserved by conformal scaling.
[Shared conformal scaling and normalization][recognition]

For a smooth real vector field \(X\), let \(\varphi_\epsilon\) be its
flow and define
\[
 \rho_\epsilon=\left(\frac{\nu}{\varphi_\epsilon^*\nu}\right)^{1/2},
 \qquad g_\epsilon=\rho_\epsilon\varphi_\epsilon^*r,
 \qquad \mathcal K X=(\mathcal L_Xr)_0.
\]
Then \(dV_{g_\epsilon}=\nu\) and \(\dot g_0=\mathcal K X\), since
\(\dot\rho_0=-\operatorname{div}_r X/2\). Let \(S_\epsilon\) be the
symmetric frame map between \(r\) and \(g_\epsilon\). The map
\(O_\epsilon=\rho_\epsilon^{1/2}d\varphi_\epsilon S_\epsilon\) is an
oriented \(r\)-isometry from \(T_xM\) to \(T_{\varphi_\epsilon x}M\).
Writing \(O_\epsilon^*\) also for the induced pullback on \(\Lambda^+\),
naturality and conformal scaling give
\[
 \widetilde F_r(g_\epsilon)(x)=\rho_\epsilon^{-1}
    O_\epsilon^* W(\varphi_\epsilon x)(O_\epsilon^*)^{-1}.
\]
The covariant derivative of \(d\varphi_\epsilon\) at zero is
\(\nabla X\). Also \(\dot S_0=-\tfrac12(\mathcal K X)^\sharp\), which
cancels the symmetric trace-free part of \(\nabla X\), and the derivative
of \(\rho_\epsilon^{1/2}\) cancels its trace. The remaining covariant
derivative of \(O_\epsilon\) is \(\operatorname{skew}\nabla X\).
Let \(\Theta_X\) be its infinitesimal pullback on \(E\), obtained by
inserting \(\operatorname{skew}\nabla X\) in each argument of a two-form
and summing. Differentiation yields the exact action
\[
 L\mathcal K X=\nabla_X W+[\Theta_X,W]+\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}_r X)W.
\tag{2}
\]
It is an operator of order at most one in \(X\). At an ASD reference all
terms vanish, recovering \(D\mathcal K=0\). For a fixed source away from
ASD, the normalized pullback acts on the Weyl tensor while the specified
source in (1) is held fixed. Its infinitesimal variations can therefore
contribute to the range. Conformal Killing fields have \(\mathcal K X=0\)
and contribute zero. [Gover–Gursky, ASD case, §1.1][gg]

## Normal operator on the full domain

At any point, freeze an \(r\)-orthonormal coframe. The order-two symbol
of \(L\) is the universal linearized Weyl symbol. In the curvature
form only the terms linear in the second metric derivatives contribute.
Linearizing the algebraic frame identification contributes order zero,
even when \(W\ne0\). Thus the round computation in E02 applies to the
frozen metric values at every \(r\). In the real-symbol convention
\(\partial\mapsto\xi\), use an oriented coframe with \(\xi=|\xi|e^4\).
On the transverse-traceless symbol fiber
\(V_{\mathrm{TT}}(\xi)=\{h\in V_x:h(\xi^\sharp,\cdot)=0\}\), E02 (5)
gives \(-|\xi|^2/4\) times the isometric identification of the
trace-free \(3\)-by-\(3\) matrix of \(h|_{\xi^\perp}\) with \(B_x\).
The orthogonal complement is \(\operatorname{im}\sigma_\xi(\mathcal K)\)
because \(\mathcal K^*=-2\delta_r\), where
\((\delta_r h)_j=\nabla^i h_{ij}\), and \(\sigma_\xi(\mathcal K)\) is
injective for \(\xi\ne0\) by E02. Equation (2) shows that the order-three
composition symbol vanishes, so this complement is annihilated by
\(\sigma_\xi(L)\). Consequently
\[
 P=LL^*,\qquad \sigma_\xi(P)=\sigma_\xi(L)\sigma_\xi(L)^*
     =\frac{|\xi|_r^4}{16} I_{B_x}\quad(\xi\ne0).
\tag{3}
\]
Here \(\sigma_\xi(L)^*\) is the fiber adjoint and the even order adds
no sign in this symbol convention. Thus \(P\) is a fourth-order
elliptic formally self-adjoint operator on \(B\).
[E02, §§1–2, especially (5)][e02]

Let \(\mathcal C=\ker P\). Elliptic regularity makes its distributional
members smooth. The closed manifold adjoint identity gives
\(\langle Pu,u\rangle_{L^2(r)}=\|L^*u\|_{L^2(r)}^2\), so
\(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\). The elliptic Fredholm alternative on the closed
manifold yields finite-dimensional \(\mathcal C\) and a generalized
inverse \(G_P:H^s(B)\to H^{s+4}(B)\) satisfying
\(P G_P=I-\pi_{\mathcal C}\), where \(\pi_{\mathcal C}\) is the
reference \(L^2\) orthogonal projection. This also determines the exact
range of \(L\). Any \(Lh\) is orthogonal to \(\mathcal C\); for
\(f\perp\mathcal C\), the choice \(h=L^*G_P f\) has \(Lh=f\). Thus
\[
 \operatorname{im}(L:\mathcal X\to\mathcal Y)
 =\mathcal Y\cap\mathcal C^\perp .
\tag{4}
\]
[Kottke, §§1.3.1–1.3.3 and Corollary 1.44][kottke]

If \(\mathcal C=0\), \(P:H^{s+4}(B)\to\mathcal Y\) is invertible and
\(Q=L^*P^{-1}:\mathcal Y\to\mathcal X\) is bounded with \(LQ=I\).
The bounded projection \(QL\) has range
\(Q\mathcal Y=L^*H^{s+4}(B)\) and kernel \(\ker L\). Therefore
\(\mathcal X=\ker L\oplus Q\mathcal Y\). The construction gains four
derivatives at \(P^{-1}\) and spends two at \(L^*\), giving the two
derivatives needed in the metric domain. This is a split right
inverse for the full trace-free map.

## Local forced equation and the tangent obstruction

Under \(\mathcal C=0\), take
\[
 \Phi(z,t)=F_r(Qz)-(1-t)W,\qquad (z,t)\in\mathcal Y\times\mathbb R.
\]
It is smooth near \((0,0)\), vanishes there and has
\(\partial_z\Phi(0,0)=LQ=I\). The Banach implicit function theorem
gives a smooth \(\mathcal Y\)-valued \(z(t)\) for \(|t|\) sufficiently
small, with \(z(0)=0\). The chosen \(h(t)=Qz(t)\) satisfies the
radial equation, is unique locally in the chosen complement, and
has \(h'(0)=-QW\). Its positive metric stays in the pointwise-volume
chart. The compatible connection realizes the mixed equation by (1).

The profiles can be taken smooth in space. To see this within the
Sobolev proof, put \(u=P^{-1}z\) and write the equation as
\(\mathcal G(u)=(1-t)W\), where \(\mathcal G=F_r\circ L^*\) is a
smooth nonlinear differential operator of order at most four over
smooth background coefficients. Its derivative at zero is \(P\).
For sufficiently small \(t\), \(D\mathcal G(u(t))\) stays invertible as a
map \(H^{s+4}\to H^s\) by continuity. On any smaller coordinate ball,
choose a family of diffeomorphisms that are translations there and
are the identity outside a larger framed ball. Let \(T_a\) act on target
sections by this local component pullback, extended as the identity.
The conjugated differential operator \(T_a\mathcal G T_a^{-1}\) is
jointly smooth in \((a,v)\) from the smooth chart-dependent
coefficients and derivatives through order four of \(v\).
The conjugated right side \((1-t)T_aW\) is smooth as well. The
implicit function theorem at \((0,u(t))\) and continuity of
\(a\mapsto T_a u(t)\) in \(H^{s+4}\) show that this translated section
is smooth as an \(H^{s+4}\)-valued function of \(a\). Differentiation
of translations in the smaller ball gives all local weak derivatives
of \(u(t)\) in \(H^{s+4}\). Sobolev embedding gives spatial smoothness of
\(u(t)\), \(h(t)\), and the compatible connection. The parameter interval
here is local and depends on the supplied reference.

If \(\mathcal C\ne0\), any differentiable solution through \(h(0)=0\)
must satisfy \(Lh'(0)=-W\), hence
\[
 \int_M\langle W,U\rangle_r\,\nu=0\quad\text{for all }U\in\ker L^* .
\tag{5}
\]
By (4), (5) is also sufficient for a formal tangent in \(\mathcal X\).
Existence of such a tangent alone gives no nonlinear path. Failure of
(5) rules out a differentiable lift through this specified initial
metric and chart. It leaves open initializations at other metrics.

## Finite-dimensional longitudinal criterion

The injective symbol of \(\mathcal K\) makes \(\mathcal K^*\mathcal K\)
elliptic of order two. Its kernel is \(\ker\mathcal K\) by integration
by parts. For \(h\in\mathcal X\) solve
\(\mathcal K^*\mathcal K X=\mathcal K^*h\) with \(X\perp\ker\mathcal K\)
using the elliptic generalized inverse. The right side pairs to zero
with the kernel, and \(X\) has class \(H^{s+3}\). This gives the global
split \(h=h_T+\mathcal K X\), where \(h_T\) is TT. Write \(\Pi_T\) for
the reference \(L^2\) orthogonal projection onto TT tensors.

For completeness the TT range has a finite smooth annihilator at
every smooth \(r\). The generalized inverse of \(\mathcal K^*\mathcal K\)
is a pseudodifferential operator of order \(-2\) by the closed elliptic
theory. Thus \(\Pi_T=I-\mathcal K(\mathcal K^*\mathcal K)^\dagger\mathcal K^*\)
is order zero with symbol the orthogonal projection onto
\(V_{\mathrm{TT}}(\xi)\). The operator \(P_T=L\Pi_T L^*\) has the
same elliptic principal symbol as (3) and is formally self-adjoint.
Its kernel \(\mathcal C_T=\ker(\Pi_T L^*)\) is finite and smooth by
the identity \(\langle P_T U,U\rangle=\|\Pi_T L^*U\|^2\).
Applying the generalized inverse of \(P_T\) and then \(\Pi_T L^*\)
gives a TT solution for any source in \(\mathcal Y\cap\mathcal C_T^\perp\).
Conversely every TT image pairs to zero with \(\mathcal C_T\), so
this describes the full TT image. [Kottke, Propositions 1.21–1.28
and Corollary 1.44][kottke]

For \(\pi_T^c\) the projection of target sections onto
\(\mathcal C_T\), the York splitting gives
\[
 L(\mathcal X)= L(\mathcal X\cap\ker\delta_r)
                  + L\mathcal K (H^{s+3}(TM)),\qquad
 L(\mathcal X)=\mathcal Y\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
 \operatorname{im}(\pi_T^c L\mathcal K)=\mathcal C_T.
\tag{6}
\]
Indeed, the TT image is exactly the kernel of \(\pi_T^c\) in
\(\mathcal Y\). In the onto case, let \(e_1,\ldots,e_m\) be a smooth
basis of \(\mathcal C_T\). Solve \(Lh_j=e_j\) with
\(h_j=L^*P^{-1}e_j\), which is smooth by elliptic regularity, and split
each \(h_j=h_{T,j}+\mathcal K X_j\) using a smooth vector potential.
Then \(\pi_T^c L\mathcal K X_j=e_j\). Hence at most
\(m=\dim\mathcal C_T\) smooth conformal Lie directions suffice whenever
the criterion holds. Equivalently, the full annihilator consists of
\(U\in\mathcal C_T\) with \(\langle U,L\mathcal K X\rangle_{L^2(r)}=0\)
for all smooth \(X\). Formula (2) identifies the tested curvature
action. Whether it spans at an arbitrary supplied \(r\) is a separate
condition.
