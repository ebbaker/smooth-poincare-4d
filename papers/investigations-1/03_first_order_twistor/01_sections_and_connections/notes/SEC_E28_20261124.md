## Fixed round chart and prescribed positive block

Let \(r\) be the unit round metric on the standard smooth, closed,
oriented \(S^4\), with \(\nu=dV_r\). The metrics are real positive-definite
Riemannian, ASD means \(W^+=0\), and the Weyl blocks carry the
curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Denote by \(B_r^\pm=S^2_0\Lambda_r^\pm\)
the endomorphism targets. Use B16's oriented stereographic coordinates
\(x\) with \(\rho=|x|\), \(\Omega=2/(1+\rho^2)\), \(r=\Omega^2\delta\),
and \(S_-\) the closed southern cap \(\{\rho\ge1\}\) together with
the south pole. The fixed source \(f=f_{\mathrm{round}}\) is
\(\Omega^{-2}\chi(\rho)Z\) in the induced positive round frame and zero
at the pole, with \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)\) and
\[
 \chi(\rho)=\begin{cases}
 \exp(-1/(1-4\rho^2)),&\rho<1/2,\\
 0,&\rho\ge1/2 .
 \end{cases}
\]
The source is smooth and its support is the closed radius-\(1/2\)
coordinate ball in the northern cap. [B16 seed and interface][b16] ·
[Shared conventions][common]

For \(h\) a trace-free symmetric covariant tensor, set
\(g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\), with \(h^\sharp=r^{-1}h\).
The positive symmetric tangent isometry \(S_h=e^{-h^\sharp/2}\)
from \(r\) to \(g_h\) induces global pullback isometries
\(I_{h,\pm}=\Lambda^2 S_h^*:\Lambda_{g_h}^\pm\to\Lambda_r^\pm\).
Define the two maps on this same metric chart by
\[
 F_\pm(h)=I_{h,\pm}W_{g_h}^\pm I_{h,\pm}^{-1}.
 \tag{1}
\]
They are fields in the fixed bundles \(B_r^\pm\). The exponential
guarantees positivity and \(dV_{g_h}=\nu\) because \(\operatorname{tr}_r h=0\).
The block isometries preserve the norms exactly. All frame
identifications are over the original sphere and preserve its
orientation. [E02 chart][e02] · [E04 nonlinear chart][e04]

Set \(k=4\), \(\alpha=1/2\), take \(X=C^{k+2,\alpha}(S^2_0T^*S^4)\),
and take \(Y_\pm=C^{k,\alpha}(B_r^\pm)\). Both maps in (1) are smooth
as Banach maps by the finite-jet formulas in E04. Write
\(D_\pm=DF_\pm(0)\) and \(N_\pm(h)=F_\pm(h)-D_\pm h\), using
\(F_\pm(0)=0\). Let
\[
 Q=(D_+|_{\mathrm{TT}_r})^{-1}
   =D_+^* (D_+D_+^*)^{-1}:Y_+\to X.
\]
Its range is the fixed round TT slice, and its gain of two
derivatives holds in these Hölder grades. E02 uses the round TT
kernel estimate and the closed round Weyl cokernel result of
Gover–Gursky. E04 establishes the Hölder realization of the
inverse and the smooth differential range. [E02, §5][e02] ·
[E04, round neighborhood][e04] · [Gover–Gursky, §1.1][gg]

Choose a radius \(b>0\) on which \(DF_+\) has Lipschitz bound \(H_+\)
and \(q=\|Q\|H_+ b\le1/2\). For \(|t|\|Qf\|\le b/2\), the map
\[
 h\longmapsto t Qf-QN_+(h)
\]
is a \(q\)-contraction of the closed radius-\(b\) ball in the round
TT slice into itself. Indeed its norm is bounded by
\(|t|\|Qf\|+\|Q\|H_+ b^2/2\le3b/4\). The Lipschitz estimate for
the nonlinear remainder follows by integrating \(DF_+(h)-D_+\)
along segments in the ball. The fixed point \(h(t)\) solves
\[
 F_+(h(t))=t f,
 \qquad h(0)=0,
 \qquad \|h(t)\|_X\le\frac{|t|\|Qf\|}{1-q/2},
 \qquad h(t)=tQf+O_X(t^2).
 \tag{2}
\]
For the norm bound, use the quadratic remainder and \(\|h\|\le b\)
in the fixed-point equation. That bound and the quadratic remainder
give the stated second-order error. Set \(g(t)=g_{h(t)}\). Uniqueness
is in the chosen small ball of logarithmic variations TT with
respect to the fixed \(r\). The source is prescribed in the fixed
reference bundle, and a diffeomorphism moving that source changes
the data. [E04 contraction and split range][e04]

The Banach implicit function theorem gives smooth dependence on
\(t\) in these grades since the derivative on the fixed range is
the identity plus a norm-\(q\) perturbation. The spatial smoothness
can be checked in the E04 differential range. Put \(P=D_+D_+^*\)
and write \(h=D_+^*v\) with \(v\in C^{k+4,\alpha}(B_r^+)\). The
operator \(v\mapsto F_+(D_+^*v)\) has order four and its derivative
at zero is the elliptic \(P\) with symbol \(|\xi|^4 I/16\). At the
solution its derivative composed with \(P^{-1}\) is
\(DF_+(h)Q=I+(DF_+(h)-D_+)Q\), with invertible Neumann series in
the frozen grades. Since the reference and source are smooth,
the iterates from zero and the inverse preserve the closures of
smooth sections in the corresponding Hölder spaces. E04's local
translation argument applies in these closures, where translations
are continuous. The translated finite-jet equation and translated
smooth source depend smoothly on the translation parameter and on
\(t\). Implicit uniqueness identifies the translated potentials with
the translated solution branch. Differentiating in local translations
gives spatial derivatives of every order in the frozen grade, also
smoothly in \(t\) locally. This proves smooth metric and connection
profiles without reducing the grade in (2). [E04 spatial regularity][e04]

Let \(A(t)\) be the reference-bundle version of the chiral
Levi–Civita connection \(\Gamma^+(g(t))\), pulled to \(\Lambda_r^+\)
by \(I_{h(t),+}\). With the correspondingly transported normalized
tautological two-forms \(\Sigma_{h(t)}\), the mixed system has
\(d_{A(t)}\Sigma_{h(t)}=0\) and \(\operatorname{Sym}_0 C=tf\).
Both chiral blocks and this connection come from the same
smooth global metric. In particular the metric and connection
match along the equator as global fields. Since \(g(t)\to r\)
in \(C^2\) and \(s_r=12\), shrink the parameter interval so that
\(s_{g(t)}\ge6\). The Yamabe quotient in dimension four is
\[
 \frac{\int_{S^4}(6|du|_{g(t)}^2+s_{g(t)}u^2)\,\nu}
        {(\int_{S^4}u^4\,\nu)^{1/2}} \quad (u>0).
\]
Uniform metric equivalence and the compact round Sobolev
inequality bound this quotient uniformly below by a positive
constant. Hence \(Y(S^4,[g(t)])>0\) through this small parameter
interval. [CME `rec:chiral-curvature`][cme] ·
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107, Remark 1][cgy]

## Uniform opposite block on the closed south

Let \(U=D_-Qf\). The opposite Weyl map in (1) has a bounded second
derivative on a sufficiently small fixed chart ball. Together with
(2) this gives a uniform remainder on the whole sphere:
\[
 F_-(h(t))=tU+R(t),\qquad
 \|R(t)\|_{Y_-}\le C_R |t|^2.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed the two errors are \(D_-(h(t)-tQf)\) and \(N_-(h(t))\).
The bound in particular controls \(R(t)\) in the global pointwise
reference norm, including the two caps and the pole. The positive
frame isometries in (1) then transfer it to the moving curvature
norm of \(g(t)\).

Use the two moments of the fixed Euclidean bump
\[
 M_0=2\pi^2\int_0^{1/2}\chi(\rho)\rho^3\,d\rho,\qquad
 M_2=2\pi^2\int_0^{1/2}\chi(\rho)\rho^5\,d\rho .
\]
They satisfy \(M_0>0\) and \(0<M_2<M_0/4\) directly from the support and
strict positivity on \((0,1/2)\). E21 identifies the exact relative
multiplier with the positively signed reflection conjugation
\(M(n)Z=C_n Z C_n^{-1}\), \(C_n=(\Lambda^2R_n)|_{\Lambda^+}\),
\(n=x/\rho\), \(R_n\) reflection in \(n^\perp\). E23 proves that
\(|\xi|^4 M(\xi/|\xi|)Z\) is componentwise harmonic of degree four.
Therefore the E22 harmonic-quartic exterior formula, with E21's
round conformal weight \(\Omega^{-2}\), gives
\[
 U(x)=\Omega(x)^{-2}\frac6{\pi^2}
        \big(M_0\rho^{-4}-2M_2\rho^{-6}\big)M(n)Z
 \quad (\rho\ge1).
 \tag{4}
\]
Conjugation is orthogonal for Hilbert–Schmidt, so with
\(w=\rho^{-2}\in[0,1]\),
\[
 |U(x)|_r=\frac{3\sqrt6}{2\pi^2}
        (1+w)^2(M_0-2M_2 w) .
 \tag{5}
\]
This expression has strictly positive derivative in \(w\) on \([0,1]\),
since the derivative of the scalar product on the right, before
its positive constant, is \(2(1+w)(M_0-M_2-3M_2w)\).
The minimum occurs at the south pole \(w=0\). For the value as a
tensor there, use the oriented chart \(y=Jx/|x|^2\), with \(J=R_{e_0}\)
as in E23. The negative-frame action of \(JR_n\) conjugates
\(M(n)Z\) to \(Z\), and \(w=|y|^2\). Thus the coefficient becomes
a smooth scalar function of \(|y|^2\) times \(Z\) in a smooth frame,
with pole value \(3M_0 Z/(2\pi^2)\). This agrees with the smooth
global linear lift from E21. Consequently the exact linear minimum is
\[
 m_*:=\min_{S_-}|U|_r=\frac{3\sqrt6}{2\pi^2}M_0>0 .
 \tag{6}
\]
[E21, relative symbol and global operator][e21] ·
[E22, exterior radial formula][e22] · [E23, quartic and pole transition][e23]

Combining (3) with (6) and reducing the parameter interval gives
\[
 \inf_{p\in S_-}\frac{|W^-_{g(t)}(p)|_{g(t)}}{|t|}
   \ge m_*/2=\frac{3\sqrt6}{4\pi^2}M_0
 \quad\text{for }0<|t|\text{ sufficiently small}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Here and in (6) the pointwise norm is the curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt norm. In fact (3) gives convergence of the normalized
pointwise norm to \(|U|_r\) with error \(O(|t|)\) uniformly on the
whole sphere. For nonzero such \(t\), (2) says \(W^+_{g(t)}\) is
supported exactly in the closed coordinate ball \(\rho\le1/2\),
while (7) makes the entire closed southern cap ASD and nowhere
locally conformally flat. The vanishing of the positive block
holds on an open neighborhood of this closed cap.

## Southern and global squared Weyl energies

Define \(E_-(g;S_-)=\int_{S_-}|W_g^-|_{g,\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV_g\).
Pointwise volume preservation and the fiber isometries in (1) make
this exactly \(\int_{S_-}|F_-(h)|_{r,\mathrm{HS}}^2\,\nu\). Using (3)
on the fixed cap gives
\[
 E_-(g(t);S_-)=t^2 C_{\rm south}+O(|t|^3),\qquad
 C_{\rm south}=\int_{S_-}|U|_{r,\mathrm{HS}}^2\,\nu .
 \tag{8}
\]
The error follows by integrating the cross term between \(tU\) and
\(R(t)\) and the square of \(R(t)\) on the compact cap. In (4) the
factor \(\Omega^{-4}\) in the square cancels the \(\Omega^4\) in the
volume. Thus the angular norm \(|Z|^2=6\) and the unit three-sphere
area \(2\pi^2\) give the exact coefficient
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 C_{\rm south}
 &=\frac{432}{\pi^2}\int_1^\infty
     (M_0\rho^{-4}-2M_2\rho^{-6})^2\rho^3\,d\rho\\
 &=\frac{432}{\pi^2}\left(\frac{M_0^2}{4}
               -\frac{2M_0M_2}{3}+\frac{M_2^2}{2}\right)>0 .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{9}
\]
[E22, southern integral][e22]

For the full sphere put \(E_\pm(g)=\int_{S^4}|W_g^\pm|_{g,\mathrm{HS}}^2dV_g\).
With the stated normalization the signature identity is
\(E_+-E_-=12\pi^2\tau(S^4)=0\). The imposed positive block yields
\[
 E_-(g(t))=E_+(g(t))=t^2\|f\|_{L^2(r)}^2
                 =6t^2\int_{\mathbb R^4}\chi(|x|)^2\,dx
 \tag{10}
\]
exactly, by the Weyl weight and fixed volume. In particular the
positive energy in the northern support balances the global
opposite energy. The southern contribution in (9) is strictly
between zero and \(\|f\|_{L^2(r)}^2\). The upper inequality follows
from the E21 global isometry for \(U\) and continuity of nonzero
\(U\) at the equator into an adjacent northern neighborhood.
The signature identity is an equality across the full smooth
sphere and accommodates the locally ASD, Weyl-nonflat cap.
[SH-E01 norm and signature audit][recognition] · [E21, (7)][e21] ·
[Gover–Gursky, pp. 293–294][gg]
