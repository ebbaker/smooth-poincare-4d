## Operator and symbol at an arbitrary reference

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere and
\(r\) any smooth positive-definite Riemannian metric. Put
\(E_0=T^*M\), \(E_1=S^2_0T^*M\), \(E_2=S^2_0\Lambda^+_r\). In the B01
chart, lower the trace-free self-adjoint parameter \(h\) with \(r\), form
\(g_h=r(\exp(r^{-1}h)\,\cdot,\cdot)\), and let \(\mathcal W_r(h)\) be
\(W^+_{g_h}\) transported to \(E_2\) by the positive symmetric frame
isometry. Denote its derivative at \(h=0\) by \(L_r\). These are curvature
operators with the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product, and ASD means \(W^+=0\).
Define the divergence by \((\delta_r h)_j=\nabla^i h_{ij}\),
\(K_r(X^\flat)=(\mathcal L_Xr)_0\), and
\(\operatorname{TT}_r=\ker\delta_r\) in \(E_1\).
[B01 field choice][b01] · [Shared conventions][shb]

Off ASD, \(L_rK_r\) has differential order at most one. To see this, let
\(\varphi_t\) flow by \(X\) and use \(g_t=e^{2f_t}\varphi_t^*r\) with the
factor chosen to preserve \(dV_r\). Then \(f'_0=-\operatorname{div}_rX/4\)
and \(g'_0=K_r(X^\flat)\). The transported Weyl operator is obtained by
evaluating \(W_r^+\) at \(\varphi_t(p)\), pulling back by the fiber isometry
induced by \(e^{f_t}d\varphi_t\) composed with the chart frame isometry,
and multiplying by \(e^{-2f_t}\). Its derivative involves only \(X\) and
\(\nabla X\). At a point where \(X=0\) and \(\nabla X=B\) is skew, the
chart frame derivative and \(f'_0\) vanish there, leaving
\([W_r^+,B_+]\), with \(B_+\) the induced infinitesimal rotation on
\(\Lambda_r^+\). The map \(\mathfrak{so}(4)\to\mathfrak{so}(\Lambda^+)\)
is onto. Thus, at a point with \(W_r^+\ne0\), a choice of skew first jet
gives \(L_rK_r(X^\flat)\ne0\) there. At ASD the transported Weyl operator
vanishes along every such diffeomorphism variation, so \(L_rK_r=0\).

The real-covector symbol is exact for every \(\xi\ne0\). The symbol of
\(K_r\) is injective and, since \(\delta_r=-K_r^*/2\), its image is the
orthogonal complement of the symbol TT subspace
\(\ker\sigma_1(\delta_r)(\xi)=\{h\in E_1:h(\xi^\sharp,\cdot)=0\}\).
In an oriented orthonormal coframe with \(e^0=\xi/|\xi|\), the symbol
TT tensor is the trace-free spatial block \(h_{ab}\), \(1\le a,b\le3\).
The Riemann symbol on it has independent entries only of type
\(R'_{0a0b}\), given by a common sign times \(|\xi|^2h_{ab}/2\), and the
scalar symbol vanishes. Projecting via the orthonormal self-dual forms
\((e^0\wedge e^a+*(e^0\wedge e^a))/\sqrt2\) gives
\(\sigma_2(L_r)(\xi)h=\pm|\xi|^2(h_{ab})/4\), with common sign dependent
on the curvature and symbol convention. This is an isomorphism from the
five-dimensional symbol TT subspace onto \(E_2\). Differentiating the
frame transport or the algebraic curvature projections only changes
lower-order terms. Since \(\sigma_2(L_r)\sigma_1(K_r)=0\), these facts
establish exactness of the symbol sequence of Gover–Gursky (1.8) on
every reference metric. Their (1.9) describes cohomology on ASD
references. [Gover–Gursky v1, §1.1][gg]

## Topological index and its sign

For \(k\ge2\), take either \(H^j\) or \(C^{j,\alpha}\) with \(0<\alpha<1\)
and denote sections of order \(j\) by \(X^j(E)\). The augmented operator

\[
 A_r=(\delta_r,L_r):X^{k+2}(E_1)\to X^{k+1}(E_0)\oplus X^k(E_2)
\]

is elliptic with its indicated row orders. For example, compose the
first row with \(Q=(1+\nabla^*\nabla)^{1/2}\) on \(E_0\). This is an
isomorphism \(X^{k+1}(E_0)\to X^k(E_0)\), with principal symbol
\(|\xi|I\), and yields an ordinary order-two elliptic pseudodifferential
operator. Its parametrix gives Fredholmness in both scales, with smooth
kernel and smooth representatives for the annihilator of its range.
The Hölder assertion uses the usual noninteger Hölder mapping property
and the smoothing remainders of the parametrix.

The symbol of the formal adjoint of \((Q\delta_r,L_r)\) folds the exact
symbol sequence into an isomorphism \(E_0\oplus E_2\to E_1\) off the zero
section. The first component is proportional to \(\sigma(K_r)\) times
\(|\xi|\), and the second is \(\sigma(L_r)^*\). The row rescaling and
nonzero constant from \(K_r^*\) preserve the complex symbol class.
The folded symbol has the complex's even-to-odd index, which on an ASD
metric Gover–Gursky give as \(\dim H^0-\dim H^1+\dim H^2\) with value
\((15\chi+29\tau)/2\). The augmented operator goes from odd to even,
so its index has the opposite sign. Complexification preserves the value
of the real index. [Gover–Gursky v1, §1.1, (1.8)–(1.9) and index display][gg]

For the off-ASD application, compute that characteristic number
directly for the universal symbol. The symbol maps in an oriented
orthonormal coframe are fixed equivariant maps on the standard fibers.
Use the spin lift, which exists since \(H^2(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0\), and write
\(x,y\) for the two half-spin representations, with \(x\) belonging to
the chirality of \(\Lambda^+\). The bundles \(E_0,E_1,E_2\) after
complexification correspond respectively to \(xy\),
\(\operatorname{Sym}^2x\otimes\operatorname{Sym}^2y\), and
\(\operatorname{Sym}^4x\). Indeed the vector representation is \(xy\),
\(\Lambda^+_\mathbb C=\operatorname{Sym}^2x\), and the symmetric-square
trace removes a trivial summand in each of the last two representations.
The restriction of the universal even-to-odd symbol class to the origin
is the representation difference

\[
 [E_0]-[E_1]+[E_2]
 = (x-y)\bigl[\operatorname{Sym}^3x+
                     (\operatorname{Sym}^2x)\otimes y\bigr].
\]

Atiyah–Singer III, (2.13), (2.15)–(2.17), gives the index using the
character of this difference divided by the universal Euler class and
multiplied by \(\operatorname{Td}(TM_\mathbb C)\). Here one applies the
operator formula (2.13) to the folded symbol. The torus of
\(\operatorname{Spin}(4)\) has no fixed nonzero vector in the tangent
representation, so the Euler division takes place in the universal
rational characteristic ring before evaluation on \(M\). If the half-spin
weights are \(\pm u\) and \(\pm v\), the two oriented tangent roots are
\(u+v,u-v\). Thus \(e=u^2-v^2\), \(p_1=2(u^2+v^2)\), and through degree
four the quotient \(\operatorname{ch}(x-y)/e\) is \(1+p_1/24\). The Todd
factor is \(1-p_1/12\) through degree four, and the character of the
bracketed representation is \(10+18u^2+3v^2\). Consequently the folded
index is

\[
 \left\langle 18u^2+3v^2-\frac{10p_1}{24},[M]\right\rangle
 =\left\langle \frac{15}{2}e+\frac{29}{6}p_1,[M]\right\rangle
 =\frac{15\chi+29\tau}{2}.
\]

Only the exact universal symbol and the oriented tangent characteristic
numbers enter this calculation. In particular, at \(\chi=2,\ \tau=0\),
one obtains \(\operatorname{ind}A_r=-15\) for every smooth reference on
the given \(M\), without a hypothesis of an ASD metric or an identification
with the standard smooth sphere. [Atiyah–Singer III, §2][as] ·
[Shared characteristic-number conventions][she]

## Removing the divergence component

Write \(\mathrm{CK}_r=\ker K_r\) and \(\kappa_r=\dim\mathrm{CK}_r\).
The range of \(\delta_r\) is precisely the \(L^2(r)\)-orthogonal
complement of \(\mathrm{CK}_r\) in \(X^{k+1}(E_0)\). Orthogonality follows
from the adjoint identity. For each orthogonal \(f\), solve
\(\delta_rK_r\omega=f\) with \(\omega\perp\mathrm{CK}_r\), using the
self-adjoint elliptic operator \(-K_r^*K_r/2\). Regularity gives
\(\omega\in X^{k+3}(E_0)\) and thus the bounded right inverse
\(Rf=K_r\omega\) of \(\delta_r\). This gives the decomposition
\(X^{k+2}(E_1)=\operatorname{TT}_r\oplus R(\mathrm{CK}_r^\perp)\).
Decrease the first target to \(\mathrm{CK}_r^\perp\), raising the augmented
index by \(\kappa_r\). In the split domain and by the target operation
\((f,w)\mapsto(f,w-L_rRf)\), the augmented map becomes the direct sum
of the identity on \(\mathrm{CK}_r^\perp\) and \(L_r|_{\operatorname{TT}_r}\).
This accounts for \(L_rRf\) even when \(L_rK_r\ne0\) off ASD. It follows that

\[
 \operatorname{ind}(L_r|_{\operatorname{TT}_r})=\kappa_r-15,
 \qquad
 \dim\operatorname{coker}(L_r|_{\operatorname{TT}_r})
 =15-\kappa_r+\dim\ker(L_r|_{\operatorname{TT}_r}).
\]

## Consequences for the forced problem

If \(\kappa_r<15\), the fixed TT derivative cannot be surjective onto
the full Weyl target. In the conditional no-conformal-Killing-field
class \(\kappa_r=0\), the index is \(-15\), with cokernel dimension at
least 15, and exactly 15 if the TT kernel vanishes.

In fact \(\kappa_r\le15\). At any point a conformal Killing field is
determined by its value, the skew part of \(\nabla X\),
\(f=\frac14\operatorname{div}_rX\), and \(df\). To see uniqueness, take
the Lie derivative of the Levi-Civita connection in the equation
\(\mathcal L_Xr=2fr\). The conformal connection variation
\(\mathcal L_X\nabla\) is algebraic in \(df\), and its expression from
the flow is \(\nabla^2X\) plus a term linear in \(R_r\) and \(X\).
For the Schouten tensor \(P_r\), the conformal variation gives
\(\mathcal L_XP_r=-\nabla^2 f\). The left side is linear in \(X\) and
\(\nabla X\). These equations determine the derivatives of all the jet
data along curves and give a homogeneous linear system. The jet map
is therefore injective, with target dimension \(4+6+1+4=15\). If its
rank is 15, every skew \(\nabla X\) with \(X=0\) occurs at every point
for a conformal Killing field. Since \(K_rX^\flat=0\), the earlier
commutator calculation forces \(W_r^+\) to vanish everywhere.
Thus every off-ASD reference has a nontrivial cokernel on the B01 slice.

On the unit round standard sphere there are ten rotations and five
gradients of the restricted Cartesian coordinates. The gradients are
conformal Killing by their pure-trace Hessians. Their divergences are
\(-4\) times the independent coordinate functions, whereas rotations
have zero divergence. Hence \(\kappa_r=15\). Gover–Gursky's round
unobstructedness statement gives \(H^2_{\rm ASD}=0\). In this ASD case
\(L_rK_r=0\), so the unrestricted and restricted ranges agree. The TT
derivative is surjective and its zero index gives trivial TT kernel.
This argument uses \(H^2_{\rm ASD}=0\) for the target range. [Gover–Gursky
v1, §1.1, round statement following Conjecture 1.4][gg]

A differentiable radial solution of
\(\mathcal W_r(h(t))=(1-t)\mathcal W_r(0)\) with \(h(0)=0\) and
\(h(t)\in\operatorname{TT}_r\) must satisfy
\(L_r\dot h(0)=-\mathcal W_r(0)\). The index obstructs the all-target
surjectivity premise. The radial equation asks for membership of a
single target in the actual range, which the index leaves undecided for
an off-ASD reference. Range membership, a nonlinear solution for this
forcing, and global continuation are separate premises. Return the
failed all-target premise to B08. [B08 decision][b08]
