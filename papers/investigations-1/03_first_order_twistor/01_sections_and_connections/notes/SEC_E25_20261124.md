## Statement

Let \(M\) be a closed smooth oriented homotopy four-sphere. Fix a
smooth orientation-preserving chart parametrization
\(\psi:B_{1+d}(0)\to M\) with \(d>0\) and
\(K=\psi(\overline B_1(0))\). Specify the unit round stereographic
metric \(\Omega(x)^2\delta\) on \(K\) in these coordinates, where
\(\Omega(x)=2/(1+|x|^2)\) and \(\delta\) is Euclidean. Let
\(\mathcal G\) be all smooth positive-definite metrics on \(M\) and
\(\mathcal G_K\subset\mathcal G\) consist of metrics equal to this
model on \(K\). Let \(\mathcal G^+\) and \(\mathcal G_K^+\) impose
positive Yamabe constant \(Y(M,[g])\) in the respective classes.
In the [shared operator Hilbert–Schmidt convention][common],
\(E_+(g)=\int_M|W_g^+|_{\rm op,HS}^2\,dV_g\).

For each \(g\in\mathcal G\) and each \(\eta>0\), there exists
\(g_K\in\mathcal G_K\) with

\[
 |E_+(g_K)-E_+(g)|<\eta,\qquad
 |Y(M,[g_K])-Y(M,[g])|<\eta.
 \tag{1}
\]

The construction agrees with the marked round model on a
neighborhood of \(K\) in the extended chart. In particular,

\[
 \inf_{\mathcal G_K} E_+=\inf_{\mathcal G} E_+,\qquad
 \inf_{\mathcal G_K^+} E_+=\inf_{\mathcal G^+} E_+
    \quad\text{if }\mathcal G^+\ne\varnothing .
 \tag{2}
\]

## The local approximation in the primary source

Apply Akutagawa–Botvinnik–Kobayashi–Seshadri,
arXiv:math/0201153v3, Proposition 4.1, to the input conformal
class and the point \(o=\psi(0)\). The compact dimension-four case
gives a smooth metric \(\tilde g\) with a conformally flat germ at
\(o\) and with arbitrarily small simultaneous changes in
\(Y(M,[g])\) and the full Weyl functional \(W_{[g]}(M)\). No
Yamabe sign hypothesis appears in this proposition. In their
§1.2 the Weyl norm is the contracted full tensor norm, and in
dimension four the integrand has exponent \(n/2=2\).
[ABKS, §1.2 and Proposition 4.1, pp. 17–18][abks]

Their proof matches the metric first jet and scalar curvature at
the center. In \(g\)-normal coordinates this is possible using
\(\bar g=e^{2v}\delta\) with \(v(0)=0\), \(dv(0)=0\) and
\(-6\Delta_\delta v(0)=s_g(o)\). In a small normal ball their
interpolation \(g_\rho=g+w_\rho(\bar g-g)\) equals \(\bar g\)
on a smaller ball and \(g\) outside radius \(\rho\). The scalar
values match at \(o\), while \(H=\bar g-g\) satisfies
\(H=O(|x|^2)\), \(\partial H=O(|x|)\), and \(\partial^2H=O(1)\).
The cutoff obeys \(|r w_\rho'|+|r^2 w_\rho''|=O(\rho)\) on
the transition, where \(r=|x|\). Such cutoffs follow by making
the transition in \(\log r\) over an interval with length of
order \(\rho^{-1}\) ending at \(\log\rho\). Consequently
\(\partial^2(w_\rho H)=w_\rho\partial^2 H+O(\rho)\) uniformly.
The second-order coefficient of scalar curvature differs from
its Euclidean value by \(O(\rho^2)\), since each metric matrix
is \(I+O(\rho^2)\) and the quadratic first-derivative terms
are \(O(\rho^2)\). The Euclidean scalar contraction of
\(\partial^2H(0)\) is zero by construction. This gives
uniform \(s_{g_\rho}\to s_g\), as well as \(g_\rho\to g\) in
\(C^1\). Their compact continuity Fact 3.1 applies to these
uniform metric and scalar limits, for any starting sign.
The same second-derivative bounds give uniformly bounded
Weyl curvature in the shrinking ball, with uniformly
comparable volume elements. Its volume tends to zero and
the metrics coincide outside it, yielding convergence of the
full Weyl functional. This is the germ approximation used
here. [ABKS, Fact 3.1 and Proposition 4.1, (12)–(13)][abks]

## Marking and installing the cap on \(M\)

Choose conformally Euclidean coordinates \(y\) centered at
\(o\) for \(\tilde g\), so \(y_*\tilde g=e^{2v(y)}\delta\) near
zero for some smooth \(v\). Compose \(y\) with a Euclidean
reflection if needed to make \(y\) orientation preserving
relative to the given orientation. The transition germ
\(G=\psi^{-1}\circ y^{-1}\) fixes zero and satisfies
\(DG(0)\in\mathrm{GL}^+(4)\).

This germ extends to an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism
\(\Xi:M\to M\) in the following sense. First join \(I\) to
\(DG(0)\) by a smooth path of positive-determinant linear maps,
using polar decomposition and path connectedness of
\(\mathrm{SO}(4)\). Then join \(DG(0)\) to \(G\) by
\(x\mapsto u^{-1}G(ux)\) for \(0<u\le1\), with
\(DG(0)x\) at \(u=0\). This expression is smooth at \(u=0\)
by writing it as \(\int_0^1DG(vux)x\,dv\).
Reparametrize the two isotopies smoothly at their ends.
On a common sufficiently small ball this gives a smooth
family \(G_t\) of local orientation-preserving
diffeomorphisms. The inverses are defined on a uniform
neighborhood of zero by the inverse function theorem with
the compact time parameter. In that neighborhood multiply
the velocity field \((\partial_tG_t)\circ G_t^{-1}\) by a
fixed smooth cutoff equal to one on a smaller neighborhood
of zero. Extend it by zero in the marked chart, and then
on \(M\). There is a still smaller input ball on which all
paths \(G_t(x)\) stay where the cutoff is one, by continuity
and compactness of the time interval. Uniqueness of the
flow gives \(\Xi(\psi(x))=y^{-1}(x)\) for such \(x\). The
extension is a smooth diffeomorphism isotopic to the
identity, as the time-one map of the extended field.

Next compress the marked ball within its extended chart.
Choose \(0<d'<d\) and a nonnegative smooth radial cutoff
\(\chi\) supported in \(B_{1+d}\) and equal to one on
\(\overline B_{1+d'}\). The flow of \(-\chi(x)x\) identifies
with \(x\mapsto c x\) on \(B_{1+d'}\) at time \(-\log c\),
for \(0<c<1\), since such trajectories stay where the
cutoff equals one. Extend this field by zero to obtain an
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(D_c\) of \(M\).
Take \(c\) small enough that the compressed
\(B_{1+d'}\) lies in the ball where the germ of \(\Xi\)
was realized. For \(\phi=\Xi\circ D_c\) we then have

\[
 y(\phi(\psi(x)))=c x,\qquad
 \psi^*(\phi^*\tilde g)=c^2e^{2v(cx)}\delta
 \quad (|x|<1+d').
\]

On this neighborhood the positive smooth factor
\(u_{\rm loc}(\psi(x))=\Omega(x)/(c\,e^{v(cx)})\) gives
exactly the specified round metric. Choose a smooth cutoff
\(\zeta\) supported in \(\psi(B_{1+d'})\) and equal to one
on a neighborhood of \(K\). Extend \(\zeta\log u_{\rm loc}\)
by zero and exponentiate to get a smooth global positive
function \(u\). Then \(g_K=u^2\phi^*\tilde g\) has the desired
restriction. The entire marking step uses flows in the
given coordinate chart and a conformal factor on \(M\).
No claim about a complement of an embedded sphere or any
smooth Schoenflies theorem is used.

## Invariants, infima and the strict endpoint

Oriented pullback preserves the chirality, the integrated
operator norms and the Yamabe constant by change of variables.
For \(\widehat g=u^2g\), the Hodge splitting on two-forms
is unchanged, the two Weyl operators scale by \(u^{-2}\),
and \(dV_{\widehat g}=u^4dV_g\). Hence \(E_\pm\) are
conformally invariant, and \(Y(M,[g])\) is conformally
invariant by definition. Both invariants of \(g_K\) equal
those of \(\tilde g\).

The norm conversion and signature identity of [SH-E01][sh]
give, for every smooth metric on this oriented \(M\),

\[
 W_{[g]}(M)=4(E_+(g)+E_-(g)),\qquad
 E_+(g)-E_-(g)=12\pi^2\tau(M)=0 .
\]

Thus \(W_{[g]}(M)=8E_+(g)\). Taking the source tolerances
small gives (1). Since the marked classes are subsets, their
infima are bounded below by the corresponding unmarked infima.
Conversely (1) permits approximating the energy of each
unmarked metric within any positive error. If that metric has
\(Y(M,[g])>0\), choose the error in the sign smaller than
\(Y(M,[g])/2\). This also proves nonemptiness of the marked
positive class whenever the unmarked one is nonempty.
Taking the respective infima gives (2).

For a metric with \(Y(M,[g])>0\) and \(E_+(g)<4\pi^2\),
choose \(\eta<\min\{Y(M,[g])/2,\,4\pi^2-E_+(g)\}\) in
(1). Its marked approximation is again positive in the same
sense and lies strictly below the energy threshold. Equivalently,
the strict criterion is available in the marked class exactly
when the unmarked positive infimum is below \(4\pi^2\). The
full norm bound for [Chang–Gursky–Yang Theorem A][cgy] is
\(8E_+<32\pi^2=16\pi^2\chi(M)\). Simple connectivity
excludes the \(\mathbb{RP}^4\) outcome of that theorem.
[SH-E01 recognition][sh]

The equality of infima and the transfer of strict margins use
one marked ordinary round ball with the stated chart extension.
They concern invariant values and do not give controlled metric
convergence after the chart compression. Existence of a positive
class attaining the strict threshold on an arbitrary homotopy
sphere remains an input, and neither attainment of either
infimum nor compactness of an approximating sequence is needed.
