## Statement

Fix \(I=[-1,1]\), \(K=[-b,b]\), \(0<b<1\), \(k\geq2\), and
\(0<\alpha<1\). Let \(V=\operatorname{Sym}_0(3,\mathbb R)\) with the
Hilbert–Schmidt pairing, \(X=C_K^{k+2,\alpha}(I,V)\) and
\(Y=C_K^{k,\alpha}(I,V)\), where the support condition is taken
in the full interval \(I\). Consider

\[
 g_A=ds^2+dx^{\mathsf T}e^{2A(s)}dx,\qquad A\in X ,
\]

on the oriented collar \(I\times T^3\), with orientation
\(ds\wedge dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3\). These metrics have unit lapse,
zero shift and unit orbit volume density. The orbit matrices are
arbitrary positive symmetric determinant-one matrices of this
regularity near the identity. Fix two scalar bumps in the interior of
\(K\) with linearly independent mean and first moments, and take their
products with a basis of \(V\). Let \(\mathcal P:\mathbb R^{10}\to Y\)
be the resulting source insertion. For all sufficiently small
\(A_0\in X\), the map

\[
 (A,c)\longmapsto F_{A_0}(A)+\mathcal Pc
\]

is a smooth local diffeomorphism at \((0,0)\) into \(Y\), where
\(F_{A_0}(A)\) is the \(W^+\) curvature operator of \(g_A\), transported
to the seed frame of \(g_{A_0}\). In this neighbourhood the unaugmented
image is a smooth split codimension-ten submanifold. For the chord
\(y_\lambda=\lambda F_{A_0}(A_0)\), \(0\leq\lambda\leq1\), the inverse
supplies a path \((A(\lambda),c(\lambda))\) with both flat ends fixed,
with \(A(0)=0\), \(A(1)=A_0\), and

\[
 c(0)=c(1)=0,\qquad
 |c(\lambda)|\leq C\lambda(1-\lambda)\|A_0\|_X^2 .
\]

The solved Weyl source is \(F_{A_0}(A(\lambda))=y_\lambda-\mathcal Pc(\lambda)\).
The constant depends on the fixed interval, support, regularity and
profiles. This is the small-data fixed-end question in [B12][b12].

## Noncommuting transport and curvature

Write \(G=e^{2A}\), \(B=e^{A_0}\) and \(H=B^{-1}GB^{-1}\). The columns
of \(B^{-1}\) are the seed tangent frame on the torus. In that frame
the target metric has matrix \(H\), and the relative exponential
coordinate is \(\log H\), a symmetric trace-free matrix. Thus B01
transport is \(H^{-1/2}\) in seed coordinates. Its transported
\(g_A\)-orthonormal tangent frame has columns

\[
 Q=B^{-1}H^{-1/2},\qquad Q^{\mathsf T}GQ=I .
\]

The radial frame vector \(\partial_s\) is fixed and \(\det Q=1\).
All powers in this formula are positive symmetric matrix powers.
In particular the order of \(B^{-1}\) and \(H^{-1/2}\) is specified
even when \(A\) and \(A_0\) fail to commute. In the positively oriented
orthonormal coframe \(\vartheta^0=ds\),
\((\vartheta^1,\vartheta^2,\vartheta^3)^{\mathsf T}=Q^{-1}dx\), use the
orthonormal forms
\((\vartheta^0\wedge\vartheta^i+\vartheta^j\wedge\vartheta^k)/\sqrt{2}\)
for cyclic \((i,j,k)\). These go to their seeded counterparts under
pullback by the frame isometry. The matrix in these indexed forms is
exactly \(F_{A_0}(A)\). [B01 frame choice][b01] · [E13 diagonal check][e13]

For clarity, the principal coefficient can be read from an exact
formula. In the transported frame, let \(S\) be the shape matrix
on the torus and \(R\) be the radial curvature matrix on radial
bivectors. With curvature sign positive on the round sphere,

\[
 S=\tfrac12 Q^{\mathsf T}G'Q,\qquad
 R=-\tfrac12 Q^{\mathsf T}G''Q+
       \tfrac14 Q^{\mathsf T}G'G^{-1}G'Q .
\]

Indeed the shape endomorphism in coordinate tangent vectors is
\(T=\tfrac12G^{-1}G'\), and the normal curvature endomorphism is
\(-T'-T^2\). Here the covariant normal derivative of \(T\) equals
its coordinate derivative since the normal connection matrix in
those vectors is \(T\) itself. The torus leaves have zero intrinsic
curvature and \(T\) is parallel along each leaf, so the Gauss
equation gives tangential block \(-\bigwedge^2 S\) and the Codazzi
equation gives zero between radial and tangential bivectors. Under
the three-dimensional Hodge identification of the tangential
bivectors with the tangent frame, \(\bigwedge^2 S\) corresponds to
the cofactor matrix of the symmetric \(S\). This is the polynomial
cofactor even at singular \(S\). The self-dual block is the half-sum
of the two curvature blocks after this identification. Taking its
trace-free part gives

\[
 F_{A_0}(A)=\tfrac12\operatorname{tf}\bigl(R-\operatorname{cof} S\bigr).
\]

The removal of the trace agrees with subtracting scalar curvature
divided by twelve in the curvature-operator convention. For diagonal
\(A,A_0\) this reproduces the ordered entries of [E13][e13].
[Gover–Gursky, §1, p. 293][gg]

The formula is a smooth function of the matrix variables
\((A_0,A,A',A'')\) at each \(s\). In particular the pointwise frame
transport introduces no derivatives of \(A_0\). Matrix exponential
and the positive square root and inverse are smooth on their domains.
Pointwise composition and multiplication are smooth on these Hölder
spaces by the Hölder algebra estimate, the chain rule and Taylor
estimates for the smooth finite-dimensional functions on a
neighbourhood of the bounded ranges. The derivatives in \(s\) lose
at most two orders, giving a smooth map \(X\times X\to Y\).
On \(I\setminus K\), \(A=0\) and the metric is flat, so the support
condition is exact. In fact \(F_{A_0}(0)=0\) for each seed. At
\((A_0,A)=(0,0)\), varying \(A\) by \(a\) gives
\(\delta G''=2a''\), and all other terms in the curvature variation
vanish. Since \(a''\) is trace free,

\[
 (d_AF)_{(0,0)}[a]=-\tfrac12 a'' .
\]

## Moments and the local nonlinear image

For \(y\in Y\) set
\(\mu(y)=(\int_I y(s)\,ds,\int_I s\,y(s)\,ds)\in V\oplus V\).
If \(y=a''\) with \(a\in X\), integration by parts gives
\(\mu(y)=0\). Conversely, when both moments vanish, the formula

\[
 a(s)=\int_{-1}^s(s-t)y(t)\,dt
\]

defines an element of \(X\) with \(a''=y\). It vanishes to the left
of \(K\) by the support of \(y\) and to the right by the two moment
conditions. Its \(X\) norm is bounded by a constant times \(\|y\|_Y\).
The second derivative has zero kernel on \(X\), since an affine
function with this support is zero. Thus the operator
\(-\tfrac12\partial_s^2:X\to\ker\mu\) is an isomorphism.

For example, choose even translates of a smooth bump supported in
small intervals centered at \(\pm b/2\), rescaled to each have mean
one. The two moment columns are \((1,-b/2)\) and \((1,b/2)\).
Multiplying by any basis \(E_1,\ldots,E_5\) of \(V\) yields
\(\mathcal Pc=\sum_{\pm,i}c_{\pm i}p_\pm E_i\) with
\(\mu\mathcal P:\mathbb R^{10}\to V\oplus V\) an isomorphism. Hence
\((a,c)\mapsto-\tfrac12 a''+\mathcal Pc\) is a bounded isomorphism
onto \(Y\). Its inverse first chooses
\(c=(\mu\mathcal P)^{-1}\mu(y)\), then integrates the residual
twice with the coefficient \(-2\).

Apply the Banach inverse function theorem to
\((A_0,A,c)\mapsto(A_0,F_{A_0}(A)+\mathcal Pc)\) at the origin.
Its derivative is an isomorphism by the previous paragraph and
\(F_{A_0}(0)=0\). The smooth inverse has the form
\((A_0,y)\mapsto(A_0,\mathcal A(A_0,y),\mathcal C(A_0,y))\).
Restrict its domain to a product neighbourhood of \((0,0)\).
For each seed therein, \(y\mapsto\mathcal C(A_0,y)\) is a submersion
on this neighbourhood: its derivative is the projection to
\(\mathbb R^{10}\) composed with the inverse of the augmented
derivative at fixed seed. The zero set is exactly the local image
of \(A\mapsto F_{A_0}(A)\) with \(c=0\), proving the split
codimension-ten claim. At \(A=0\), \(H=B^{-2}\) and \(Q=I\) for
every seed. Since the flat curvature is zero, the derivative in
\(A\) there is again \(-\tfrac12\partial_s^2\). Thus the tangent
space of the local unaugmented image at \(y=0\) is
\(\ker\mu\) for each such seed. [Driver, Theorem 12.25][driver]

## Uniform chord estimate and regularity

By continuity of the inverse’s second \(y\)-derivative at the origin,
shrink the product neighbourhood to obtain a fixed bound for
\(\|D_y^2\mathcal C\|\) throughout it. Likewise the smooth forward
map and \(F_{A_0}(0)=0\) give
\(\|y_1\|_Y=\|F_{A_0}(A_0)\|_Y\leq C_0\|A_0\|_X\).
For small enough seeds this puts the whole segment in the inverse
domain and \((A_0,A_0,0)\) in the forward neighbourhood. By uniqueness
the inverse endpoints are \((0,0)\) and \((A_0,0)\). Along the chord,

\[
 c''(\lambda)=D_y^2\mathcal C(A_0,\lambda y_1)[y_1,y_1],\qquad
 \sup_{0\leq\lambda\leq1}|c''(\lambda)|\leq C_1\|A_0\|_X^2 .
\]

Integration with the two zero endpoint values gives
\(|c(\lambda)|\leq\tfrac12\lambda(1-\lambda)\sup|c''|\)
by the scalar Dirichlet Green kernel, which is nonnegative for
\(-d^2/d\lambda^2\) and has this integral. The same estimate holds
for the \(Y\)-norm of the source correction with a constant factor
\(\|\mathcal P\|\).

The exact curvature formula has the form
\(F_{A_0}(A)=\mathcal M(A_0,A)A''+\mathcal Q(A_0,A,A')\).
The coefficient \(\mathcal M\) is a smooth linear operator on
\(V\) at each point, since \(G''\) is affine in \(A''\).
At the flat origin it is \(-I/2\), so it is pointwise invertible
near the origin with smooth inverse. Solving for \(A''\) gives
a smooth ordinary differential equation in \((A,A')\), with
coefficients depending on the seed and source at \(s\).
For smooth seeds and sources this bootstraps the Hölder solution
to a smooth function on all of \(I\). In particular the collar
remains exactly flat on the open end neighbourhoods, with all jets
matching there for smooth data.

The result concerns one prescribed collar and its small-data
invariant family. Control for larger data, lapse or shift
variation, torus-dependent perturbations, and global interface
matching remains outside this calculation. The solved source is
the corrected chord and the proof supplies no exact radial energy
law for that chord.
