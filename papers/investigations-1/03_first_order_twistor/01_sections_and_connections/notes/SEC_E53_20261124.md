## Fixed setting and conclusion

Let \(M\) be one smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere.
Assume it admits a smooth positive-definite Riemannian conformal class
\([g]\) with \(B_g=0\), \(Y=Y(M,[g])>0\), and
\[
 4\pi^2<E=E_+(g)<8\pi^2-\delta,\qquad \delta>0.
\]
Here \(E_\pm=\int_M |W^\pm|_{\rm op,HS}^2\,dV\) are the two chiral
curvature-operator energies. The orientation determines the chiralities
and the signature gives \(E_-=E_+\). Let
\(\mathfrak B(r)=\int_M |B_r|_r\,dV_r\) for the covariant Bach tensor
in the convention of E24 and E30. Fix any point \(p\) and one smooth
representative \(g\) of the supplied class. The construction yields
smooth metrics \(r_\varepsilon\) with an exactly unit-round germ on a
ball of background radius comparable to \(\varepsilon\) at \(p\), and
\[
 |E_+(r_\varepsilon)-E|=O(\varepsilon^4),\qquad
 \mathfrak B(r_\varepsilon)=O(\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 |Y(M,[r_\varepsilon])-Y|=O(\varepsilon^2).                 \tag{1}
\]
For small \(\varepsilon\) the metrics have positive Yamabe constant and
stay strictly within the fixed band. The constants can depend on
the supplied metric and the cutoff. The round core can be presented
on a fixed marked ball, or kept at shrinking physical scale in a
unit-volume Yamabe representative where it is conformally round.
[B29][b29] · [E36][e36] · [SH-E01][sh]

## Bounded-curvature splice and invariant costs

Choose oriented normal coordinates \(x\) for \(g\) at \(p\) on a
fixed ball. Put \(s_{ij}(x)=(1+|x|^2/4)^{-2}\delta_{ij}\), the
sectional-curvature-one round metric in rescaled stereographic
coordinates. Both \(g\) and \(s\) have value \(\delta_{ij}\) and
first derivative zero at \(x=0\). Take a smooth radial function
\(0\le\chi\le1\), zero on \(B_1\) and one outside \(B_2\), and set
\[
 r_\varepsilon=s+\chi(x/\varepsilon)(g-s)
\]
in the chart, using \(g\) elsewhere. The metric agrees with \(s\)
for \(|x|<\varepsilon\) and with \(g\) for \(|x|\ge2\varepsilon\).
A closed mark of radius \(\varepsilon/2\) has an open round collar.
The convex interpolation is positive definite, with the metric,
inverse metric and volume density uniformly comparable to the
Euclidean ones in the chart. The product rule and the matched
first jets give
\[
 |D^j(r_\varepsilon-g)|\le C\varepsilon^{2-j}
 \quad(0\le j\le4,\ |x|\le2\varepsilon).
                                                        \tag{2}
\]
Terms with at least two derivatives on \(s-g\) use its bounded
derivatives on the fixed chart. Terms with zero or one derivative
on that difference use respectively its quadratic or linear
vanishing. Fixed smooth coordinate changes preserve the
resulting \(C^1\) convergence and bounded second derivatives on a
fixed atlas. No estimate on derivatives of an
\(\varepsilon\)-dependent marking map is needed for the invariant
costs.

The curvature formula and (2) give \(|{\rm Rm}|\le C\) on the
splice, \(|\nabla{\rm Rm}|\le C\varepsilon^{-1}\) and
\(|\nabla^2{\rm Rm}|\le C\varepsilon^{-2}\) there. The Bach formula
has two derivatives of the Schouten tensor and quadratic curvature
terms. It gives \(|B|\le C\varepsilon^{-2}\) on the splice and
\(B=0\) both in the round core and outside the splice by the input
Bach-flatness. The affected volume is \(O(\varepsilon^4)\).
Integration of the uniformly bounded Weyl densities and the Bach
bound proves the first two estimates in (1). The pointwise
\(W^\pm\)-energy densities are also uniformly bounded relative
to the background volume, including on the splice, so this family
has no Weyl-energy concentration in these coordinates.
[E30, local derivative bounds and cost][e30] ·
[Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1, (1.1)][bgim]

## Uniform positive Yamabe sign

Write the conformal-Laplacian quadratic form as
\[
 Q_r(v)=\int_M(6|dv|_r^2+R_rv^2)\,dV_r ,
 \qquad Y(M,[r])=\inf_{v\ne0}\frac{Q_r(v)}{\|v\|_{L^4(r)}^2}.
\]
The infimum can equivalently use \(H^1\) functions by density
and approximation of the absolute value. Since \(Y>0\), the
fixed form satisfies \(Q_g(v)\ge Y\|v\|_{L^4(g)}^2\), which
controls \(\|v\|_{L^2(g)}^2\) by the fixed volume. The gradient
term then gives \(Q_g(v)\ge c\|v\|_{H^1(g)}^2\), by moving
the negative part of \(R_g\) to the other side. In (2), the
gradients in the form only use the metric coefficients and
volume, which differ from those of \(g\) by \(O(\varepsilon^2)\).
Scalar curvature is uniformly bounded and differs from \(R_g\)
only on a set of volume \(O(\varepsilon^4)\). With
\(J_\varepsilon=dV_{r_\varepsilon}/dV_g\),
\[
 \|R_{r_\varepsilon}J_\varepsilon-R_g\|_{L^2(g)}
   \le C\varepsilon^2,\qquad
 |Q_{r_\varepsilon}(v)-Q_g(v)|
   \le C\varepsilon^2\|v\|^2_{H^1(g)} .
\]
The last inequality uses Hölder for the potential term and the fixed
Sobolev bound \(H^1(g)\subset L^4(g)\). Thus the two positive forms
are within multiplicative factors \(1\pm C\varepsilon^2\) uniformly
over all test functions. The \(L^4\) denominator and the \(L^2\)
denominator have the same multiplicative comparison. Taking infima
proves the third estimate in (1) and gives a strictly positive
uniform lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of
\(6\Delta_{r_\varepsilon}+R_{r_\varepsilon}\), with
\(\Delta=-\operatorname{div}\nabla\). The scalar curvature itself
need not converge uniformly for this splice. Once (1) gives the fixed
energy margin and positivity, E36 also gives the band lower bound
\(Y(M,[r_\varepsilon])\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\).
[Lee–Parker, §2, (2.6)–(2.7), Sobolev Theorem 2.2][lp] ·
[E36, (2)–(3)][e36]

## Fixed marking and physical radius

For a prescribed orientation-preserving extended chart
\(\psi:B_{1+d}\to M\) centered at \(p\), fix
\(K=\psi(\overline B_1)\). The oriented transition from the marked
coordinates to the normal coordinates can be realized near zero by
a diffeomorphism \(\Xi\) supported in the extended chart. The
construction joins the linear part of the transition to the
identity in \({\rm GL}^+(4)\), joins the germ to its derivative
by rescaling its argument, and integrates the resulting velocity
field with a cutoff. Use the smooth radial compression in E25
to obtain a diffeomorphism \(D_c\) that acts as \(x\mapsto cx\)
on \(B_{1+d'}\) in the marked coordinates for some \(0<d'<d\).
Then \(\phi_\varepsilon=\Xi\circ D_c\) satisfies
\(x(\phi_\varepsilon(\psi(z)))=cz\) on this larger marked ball.
Take \(c\) a fixed sufficiently small multiple of \(\varepsilon\)
so its image lies in the round core. On the larger marked ball
the pullback has metric \(c^2(1+c^2|z|^2/4)^{-2}\delta\).
Multiply by the square of a positive factor \(v_\varepsilon\)
that near \(K\) equals
\[
 \frac{2(1+c^2|z|^2/4)}{c(1+|z|^2)}.
\]
Use a cutoff of its logarithm supported where the displayed
pullback formula holds. The metric
\(\widehat r_\varepsilon=v_\varepsilon^2\phi_\varepsilon^*
r_\varepsilon\) has the prescribed unit round stereographic
metric on a neighborhood of the same \(K\). [E25, marking construction][e25]

The Weyl operator has conformal weight \(-2\) under a change
by \(v^2\), and the covariant Bach tensor scales by \(v^{-2}\).
In the new metric the Bach norm scales by \(v^{-4}\),
so its product with the volume density is unchanged. The chiral
Weyl-energy densities, the Yamabe constant and total Bach norm
are invariant under the preceding positive rescaling and
orientation-preserving pullback. Moreover, the representative
\(\widehat r_\varepsilon\) has uniformly bounded total volume.
The support of the conformal factor is in a region where
\(dV_{\phi_\varepsilon^*r_\varepsilon}=O(c^4)\,dz\) and
\(v_\varepsilon=O(c^{-1})\), and on the complement the volume
is at most the raw total volume. Thus even in this fixed
unit-round marking the first eigenvalue is bounded below by
\(Y(M,[\widehat r_\varepsilon])/\operatorname{Vol}(\widehat
r_\varepsilon)^{1/2}>c_0>0\). This bound uses the controlled
volume of this choice of representatives.
[Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1, Bach weight after (1.1)][bgim]
· [SH-E01][sh]

In the raw representatives the marked core has diameter of order
\(\varepsilon\) and volume of order \(\varepsilon^4\). A fixed
set within a fixed larger coordinate neighborhood can be used
as its label by pullback alone, in which case the metric on
that set still has shrinking scale. In \(\widehat r_\varepsilon\)
the literal unit-round cap has fixed positive intrinsic diameter
and volume, and its image before pullback and dilation is the
shrinking cap. A cap with a prescribed fixed unit-round metric
on a fixed ball cannot have shrinking physical volume in that
same representative.

## Unit-volume Yamabe representatives

For the physical-scale claim in the Yamabe gauge, let
\(g_\varepsilon^{Y}=u_\varepsilon^2 r_\varepsilon\) be any
unit-volume Yamabe minimizer, with \(u_\varepsilon>0\).
The following estimates also apply to the regularized raw
metrics below. The form estimates show that \(u_\varepsilon\)
is a bounded \(H^1(g)\) minimizing sequence for the fixed
quotient, normalized in \(L^4(g)\) up to \(o(1)\). Since \(E>0\),
\([g]\) is not conformally diffeomorphic to the round sphere,
so \(0<Y<Y_S=Y(S^4,[g_{\rm rd}])\) by the strict Yamabe
theorem. [Schoen, Theorems 2–3, pp. 493–495][schoen]

Along a subsequence write \(u_\varepsilon\rightharpoonup u\)
in \(H^1(g)\) and strongly in \(L^2(g)\). Set
\(a=\|u\|_{L^4(g)}^4\). Splitting the fourth power gives
\(\lim\|u_\varepsilon-u\|_{L^4(g)}^4=1-a\).
The mixed terms vanish by first approximating \(u\) by bounded
functions and using strong \(L^q\) convergence for \(q<4\)
and the uniform \(L^4\) bound. Splitting the energy gives
\(Q_g(u_\varepsilon)=Q_g(u)+6\|d(u_\varepsilon-u)\|_2^2+o(1)\).
Aubin's almost-sharp Sobolev estimate on this fixed smooth
compact background, followed by its arbitrarily small
coefficient loss tending to zero, gives
\[
 Y\ \ge\ Y\sqrt a+Y_S\sqrt{1-a}.
\]
Since \(\sqrt a+\sqrt{1-a}\ge1\) and \(Y<Y_S\), one has \(a=1\).
Thus the convergence is strong in \(L^4(g)\) and \(H^1(g)\).
[Lee–Parker, Theorems 2.2–2.3 and model constant (3.5)][lp]

For a uniform physical radius bound it suffices to control
\(u_\varepsilon\) above and below in \(C^0\). On a fixed coordinate
ball apply the minimizing equation
\(6\Delta_{r_\varepsilon}u_\varepsilon+
R_{r_\varepsilon}u_\varepsilon=Y_\varepsilon u_\varepsilon^3\)
to the test \(\eta^2 u_\varepsilon^3\), where \(\eta\) is a
cutoff on that ball. Integration by parts, Cauchy–Schwarz for
the cutoff cross term, the bounded scalar curvature and the
uniform Sobolev inequality give
\[
 \|\eta u_\varepsilon^2\|_4^2\le
 C\int (1+|d\eta|^2)u_\varepsilon^4\,dV_{r_\varepsilon}
 + C\|u_\varepsilon\|_{L^4(\operatorname{supp}\eta)}^2
          \|\eta u_\varepsilon^2\|_4^2 .
\]
Norms here use \(r_\varepsilon\); the constant in front of the
last term is independent of the cutoff and of \(\varepsilon\).
By strong \(L^4\) convergence choose a fixed finite cover
whose enlarged balls make the last coefficient less than \(1/2\)
for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) on this subsequence.
This gives a uniform \(L^8\) bound there. The right side of
the Laplacian equation is then bounded in \(L^{8/3}\).
Use the fixed \(g\) global \(W^{2,8/3}\) estimate and absorb
the operator difference
\(\Delta_g-\Delta_{r_\varepsilon}\), whose
\(W^{2,8/3}\to L^{8/3}\) norm is \(o(1)\) by the \(C^1\)
metric convergence. This gives a subsequence converging
uniformly to \(u\). The limit solves the fixed minimizing
equation in the weak sense by the coefficient and scalar
form estimates. Elliptic bootstrapping gives smoothness and
the strong minimum principle gives strict positivity, since
\(\|u\|_4=1\) and \(M\) is connected. These steps apply to a
subsequence of any sequence of minimizers as \(\varepsilon\)
tends to zero. A contradiction subsequence therefore gives
uniform bounds \(0<c_1\le u_\varepsilon\le C_1\).
[Lee–Parker, §2, Theorems 2.2, 2.5–2.6][lp]

The raw and Yamabe gauges are consequently uniformly comparable.
The conformally round cap in \(g_\varepsilon^{Y}\) has diameter
of order \(\varepsilon\) and volume of order \(\varepsilon^4\).
Its physical scale remains shrinking if its label is fixed by
pullback. No assertion of constant sectional curvature of
\(g_\varepsilon^{Y}\) on the cap follows from this argument.
Smooth convergence in a Yamabe gauge would require further
derivative control.

## Regular centers and the actual radial tangent

Regularity here means surjectivity of the full trace-free fixed-volume
chiral Weyl derivative \(L_r\) at a smooth reference in the
Sobolev grades of E46. If desired, apply E46 separately to each
raw \(r_\varepsilon\) with the smaller closed cap. It produces regular
centers \(\bar r_\varepsilon\) of the same density and exactly the
same energy with the exact round metric on a neighborhood of
the marked cap. They can be chosen arbitrarily close in \(C^4\)
in a fixed atlas at each \(\varepsilon\). Choose the perturbation
of size at most \(\varepsilon^4\) in \(C^4\) and, by continuity
of the total Bach norm at each smooth \(r_\varepsilon\), with
\(|\mathfrak B(\bar r_\varepsilon)-\mathfrak B(r_\varepsilon)|
\le\varepsilon^2\). There is no uniform choice of the E46 path
parameter required. The global \(C^1\) estimate, uniform scalar
bound and \(O(\varepsilon^2)\) scalar \(L^2\) form bound persist:
the small \(C^2\) perturbation changes scalar curvature by
\(O(\varepsilon^4)\) uniformly because the raw metrics have
uniformly bounded second jets and uniformly positive matrices.
Thus all conclusions of (1), including the form argument and
the Yamabe gauge conclusion, hold at the regular centers.
[E46, reference construction and Yamabe sign][e46]

Regularity also passes to smooth positive conformal changes and
oriented pullbacks. For \(r'=u^2r\), identify trace-free metric
increments as \(h'=u^2h\), so their logarithmic endomorphisms
agree. The positive symmetric tangent frame comparison is the
same linear map on the underlying tangent spaces. In the common
self-dual splitting the transported Weyl endomorphism scales
by \(u^{-2}\), and hence \(L_{r'}(u^2h)=u^{-2}L_rh\) under the
corresponding bundle identifications. The multipliers on source
and target are invertible at each fixed smooth \(u\). An
orientation-preserving pullback intertwines the operators by
naturality. This is a pointwise-in-reference claim with no
uniform Sobolev inverse estimate for the marking changes.

For \(E>0\) the positive-energy round-marked metrics cannot be
Bach-flat by E24. At any regular center, every differentiable
fixed-chart radial lift of \(W^+\) starting there has tangent
\(h\) obeying \(L_rh=-W_r^+\). E48 then gives
\[
 \|h\|_{L^\infty(r)}\,\mathfrak B(r)\ge 4E_+(r).
\]
By (1) all such tangents cost at least \(c\varepsilon^{-2}\);
E46 supplies only a reference-dependent positive local time
interval. The supplied class itself is stationary for \(E_+\),
so \(E>0\) forbids a differentiable radial tangent there.
These facts distinguish a conditional family with small total
Bach norm and expensive radial motion from existence of a
nonround closed Bach-flat class. They do not extract such a
class or a pointed ALE limit from general sequences.
[E24, (1) and exterior stationarity][e24] · [E46][e46] · [E48][e48]

## Band endpoints

The strict inequalities at the input give the margins used to
put all sufficiently small splices inside the original fixed
band. At \(E=4\pi^2\) a positive-Yamabe closed Bach-flat class
on a homotopy sphere is excluded by the Bach-flat equality
classification. If \(M\) is diffeomorphic to standard \(S^4\),
apply Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1 with \(\int|W|_{(0,4)}^2=8E\).
Their full tensor normalization is verified by the quarter
factor in (2.5). If \(M\) is not diffeomorphic to \(S^4\),
it is also not diffeomorphic to \(\mathbb{RP}^4\), and
Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem C applies at
\(\int|W|_{(0,4)}^2=16\pi^2\chi(M)\). The resulting
\(\mathbb{CP}^2\) and the manifold covered by
\(S^3\times S^1\) are excluded by the homology and fundamental
group of \(M\), respectively. This invokes exact Bach-flatness
of the input, not small Bach norm of splice metrics.
[Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1 and (2.5), pp. 385–386][cc] ·
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, Remark 2 and Theorem C, pp. 107, 109][cgy]

At \(E=8\pi^2-\delta\) with \(\delta>0\), (1) still gives
convergence of the invariants and uniform Yamabe positivity,
but cannot in general keep this construction within the same
upper bound. In fact choose \(p\) with \(W_g^+(p)\ne0\).
Let \(d_\varepsilon=r_\varepsilon-g\) and represent the Weyl
block of \(g+d_\varepsilon\) in the positive symmetric
bundle identification at \(g\), multiplied by the square root
of its volume-density ratio to \(g\). Denote this map by
\(\mathcal F(d)\), so \(E_+(g+d)=\|\mathcal F(d)\|^2_{L^2(g)}\).
In coordinates curvature is affine in the second metric
derivatives with smooth coefficients in the lower jets.
With \(\mathcal D=d\mathcal F(0)\), (2) gives
\[
 \mathcal F(d_\varepsilon)=W_g^+ +\mathcal D d_\varepsilon
                              +O(\varepsilon^2)
\]
uniformly, with the error supported in the splice. Indeed,
the coefficient variation multiplying \(D^2d_\varepsilon\)
is \(O(\varepsilon^2)\), since the second-derivative coefficient
depends only on the metric values. The lower-order squared
first-jet terms and the algebraic comparisons also have
remainders \(O(\varepsilon^2)\). In the integral expansion the
linear term \(2\langle W_g^+,\mathcal D d_\varepsilon\rangle_{L^2}\)
is zero by stationarity. Since \(\mathcal D d_\varepsilon=O(1)\)
and the affected volume is \(O(\varepsilon^4)\),
\[
 E_+(r_\varepsilon)-E
    =\|\mathcal D d_\varepsilon\|_2^2+O(\varepsilon^6).
\]
On the core \(\mathcal F(d_\varepsilon)=0\), so
\(\mathcal D d_\varepsilon=-W_g^+ +O(\varepsilon^2)\), and
the square integral is at least \(c\varepsilon^4\) at this choice
of \(p\). Thus this splice overshoots the original upper endpoint
there. A weaker positive upper margin suffices, for example
\(\delta/2\). E46 preserves the splice energy and cannot remove
this overshoot. At the wall \(E=8\pi^2\), no fixed positive
energy margin follows from convergence to that energy.
[E24, first variation][e24] · [E46, exact energy preservation][e46]
