## Source and component hypotheses

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere.
Take smooth real positive-definite Riemannian unit-volume Yamabe
minimizers \(g_i\) on \(M\), with positive class constants \(Y_i\).
Use Hilbert–Schmidt norms of the Weyl endomorphisms on \(\Lambda^\pm\),
and write \(T_g=\operatorname{Ric}_g-R_g g/4\). The signature vanishes,
so assume the common block energies satisfy
\[
 E_i=E_+(g_i)=E_-(g_i)\in[4\pi^2,8\pi^2-\delta],\qquad
 \delta>0,\qquad E_i\longrightarrow E_* .
 \tag{1}
\]
The limit can be taken on a common subsequence. The almost-Bach
premise \(\int_M|B_{g_i}|\,dV_{g_i}/E_i\to0\) implies
\(\int_M|B_{g_i}|\,dV_{g_i}\to0\). The source identity in this scale
is
\[
 R_{g_i}=Y_i,\qquad
 Y_i^2=384\pi^2-48E_i+12\|T_{g_i}\|^2_{L^2(M,g_i)},\qquad
 \sqrt{48\delta}\le Y_i\le Y_{\rm rd}=8\sqrt6\,\pi .
 \tag{2}
\]
[B29][b29] · [E36, (2)][e36] · [CGY, (0.4)][cgy]

The base is a supplied smooth metric \(g_0\) on
\(U_0=\widehat M_0\setminus P_0\), with \(P_0\) a finite set and
\(\widehat M_0\) a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy sphere.
Assume a smooth positive Riemannian metric \(\widehat g_0\) on
\(\widehat M_0\) is conformal on \(U_0\) to \(g_0\). Additional
components \(X_a\) are supplied complete smooth oriented ALE limits.
Every nonflat component used in the accounting is assumed to have
one end with trivial group, positive differentiable AE order
(decay through two derivatives suffices here), and a smooth conformal
one-point compactification \((\widehat X_a,\widehat g_a)\) with
homotopy-sphere topology. Flat components contribute zero Weyl
energy. The singleton-link and smooth compactification requirements
can be imported only when their own hypotheses hold. This argument
takes them as premises and uses the [E54 closed and open estimates][e54].

Specify the convergence as follows. For a fixed compact smooth
truncation of \(U_0\) or a piece, there are orientation-preserving
embeddings into \(M\) of neighborhoods of the truncation for all
sufficiently large \(i\) along the common subsequence. The base
metric converges smoothly on compacts after pullback, allowing a
smooth positive conformal factor on each pullback. For the ALE
pieces the factors are constants \(\lambda_{i,a}^{-2}\) at the
metric level, with \(\lambda_{i,a}\to0\). These constant scales
are needed to transfer the trace-free Ricci bound. To allow a
specified nesting, one may use smooth convergence on compacts in
\(U_a=X_a\setminus P_a\) for a finite set \(P_a\) of already smooth
points of \(X_a\). In the full-core case \(P_a=\varnothing\).
For every fixed finite subcollection of matched truncations assume
their source images, including the base image, have disjoint
interiors for all sufficiently large \(i\). These are conditions on
simultaneous embeddings along the sequence.

## Yamabe sign, Bach equation and missing points

For any nonzero real smooth test \(f\) compactly supported in a
convergence domain \(U_a\), put the support in a truncation interior.
Suppose the pulled-back conformal representative is
\(v_i^2\Phi_i^*g_i\). The dimension-four Yamabe quotient of \(f\)
in this metric equals the quotient of \(v_i f\) in \(\Phi_i^*g_i\).
Push that function to \(M\) and extend it by zero. It is a smooth
test and its quotient is bounded below by \(Y_i\). Passing to the
limit for this fixed test gives \(Y_c(U_a,g_a)\ge\limsup_iY_i\),
where \(Y_c\) is the infimum over nonzero \(C_c^\infty\) functions.
[Lee–Parker, §1 (1.3)–(1.5) and §3 after Theorem 3.2][lp]

For the base and for each nonflat piece with the stated smooth
compactification, conformal transformation of the test identifies
this infimum with the infimum on the smooth closed manifold with
finitely many points deleted. At each deleted point, choose a
coordinate cutoff that is zero in a radius-\(\epsilon\) ball and
one outside radius \(2\epsilon\), with squared \(L^2\) gradient
norm \(O(\epsilon^2)\). Multiplying a smooth closed test by the
finite product of cutoffs gives convergence in \(H^1\) and \(L^4\).
The Yamabe quotients converge by smoothness of the compact metric.
Therefore the punctured quotient infimum equals the closed constant,
and the supplied compactifications satisfy
\[
 \widehat Y_0,\ \widehat Y_a\ \ge\ \limsup_iY_i
 \ \ge\ \sqrt{48\delta}>0 .
 \tag{3}
\]
This obtains the sign from convergence and test transfer across
points with zero capacity. For a smooth removable extension supplied
without these convergence data, the positive sign remains a
separate premise.

The four-dimensional densities \(|B_g|_g\,dV_g\) and
\(\|W^\pm_g\|^2_{\rm HS}\,dV_g\) are invariant under positive
conformal scaling. Smooth local convergence and the vanishing
source Bach integral imply that \(B_{g_a}\) vanishes on each
convergence domain, by testing on every fixed compact truncation.
It extends as zero across each supplied smooth point, and hence
the compactifications are Bach-flat. On each piece the scale
and (2) also give \(R_{g_a}=0\) by smooth convergence and continuity.
The Bach inference uses convergence through four derivatives.
[Chang–Chen, §2, pp. 384–385][cc] · [SH-E01, conformal Weyl density][sh]

## Topology when actual cuts are supplied

Suppose the base is topologically obtained by actually cutting the
source along finitely many smooth embedded disjoint \(S^3\)'s and
capping each boundary sphere of the base component by a standard
smooth \(B^4\) via a supplied boundary identification. For one
such sphere \(S\), the normal line is orientable and admits a
collar. The exact sequence of \((M,M\setminus S)\), together with
duality \(H_1(M,M\setminus S;\mathbb Z)\simeq H^3(S;\mathbb Z)
\simeq\mathbb Z\), gives two complementary components using
\(H_1(M)=0\). Let \(A_1,A_2\) be their closures after cutting.
The collars give a cover for van Kampen and integral
Mayer–Vietoris, with overlap homotopy equivalent to \(S^3\).
The free product \(\pi_1(A_1)*\pi_1(A_2)=\pi_1(M)=0\) forces
both groups to vanish. The same cover gives
\(H_2(A_1;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_2(A_2;\mathbb Z)\simeq H_2(M;\mathbb Z)=0\).

Capping a side preserves its \(\pi_1\) and \(H_2\), since \(S^3\)
and \(B^4\) have the relevant vanishing groups. The capped closed
manifold is therefore simply connected with \(H_2=0\) integrally.
Poincaré duality gives the remaining homology of \(S^4\).
Hurewicz gives a representative \(S^4\to\widehat A_j\) of the
fundamental class and homological Whitehead gives a homotopy
equivalence. Repeat on each capped piece as further disjoint
spheres are cut. The same argument applies at each stage of a
supplied recursive cutting and capping in the capped parent.
[Hatcher, Theorem 1.20][hat1] · [§2.2 Mayer–Vietoris][hat2] ·
[Theorems 3.30 and 3.44][hat3] · [Theorem 4.32 and Corollary 4.33][hat4]

Removing finitely many smooth point neighborhoods replaces them
by boundary \(S^3\)'s and leaves \(\pi_1,H_2\) unchanged. Thus
when a parent is obtained by the cuts above and subsequently
punctured at finitely many smooth points, filling those punctures
by smooth caps also yields a homotopy sphere. In particular, a
single embedding into \(M\) of a full compact base truncation
obtained by deleting smooth point neighborhoods suffices for the
topology calculation. The embedded boundary spheres are the cut
surfaces, and the core interior is a connected component of their
complement, since it is open there and closed there by compactness
of the core. Capping this side gives the topology of the supplied
compactification. If the base is unpunctured and the full closed
base embeds, that embedding identifies it diffeomorphically
with \(M\) by compactness and local invertibility.
The cut argument needs only the capped topology and actual full
core embeddings. It supplies no metric convergence or disjoint
convergence maps, and it makes no assertion about either side of
an embedded sphere being a smooth ball.

## Disjoint measures and the residual

For each sign, let \(\mu_i^\pm=\|W^\pm_{g_i}\|^2_{\rm HS}dV_{g_i}\).
These are finite nonnegative Borel measures. An image of a compact
smooth truncation is closed in \(M\), and an embedded smooth boundary
hypersurface has zero \(\mu_i^\pm\) measure. Thus disjoint interiors
suffice to add the integrals on any fixed matching family. In the
limit, smooth local convergence and conformal invariance identify the
integral on each fixed image with the limiting chirality integral
on the truncation. Exhaust all the regions after taking \(i\to\infty\).
Smooth deleted points and the smooth compactification points have
zero limiting Weyl measure. The result for the matched family is
\[
 e_{0,\pm}+\sum_a e_{a,\pm}\le E_*,
 \qquad e_{0,\pm}=E_\pm(\widehat M_0,\widehat g_0),\quad
 e_{a,\pm}=E_\pm(X_a,g_a).
 \tag{4}
\]
For a countable supplied family, (4) follows by taking the supremum
over finite subfamilies. The two signs agree on the base by its
compactification and signature zero, and likewise on every
compactified piece. Flat pieces also have equal, zero signs.
Use \(e_0,e_a\) for the resulting common values. Exact no-loss
accounting is the additional assertion of equality in (4).
[SH-E01, signature and normalization][sh] · [E54, (6)][e54]

In a finite family, the difference between the right and left sides
of (4) is precisely the iterated remaining mass outside the matched
images. For compact exhaustions \(K_{a,m}\subset U_a\), the number
\[
 \lim_{m\to\infty}\lim_{i\to\infty}
 \mu_i^\pm\!\left(M\setminus\bigcup_{a\ge0}\Phi_{i,a}(K_{a,m})\right)
 =E_* - e_0-\sum_a e_a
 \tag{5}
\]
uses convergence at fixed \(m\), followed by monotone convergence
in the limiting domains. Thus an equality in (4) is a claim about
the complement. A nesting of rescalings alone does not imply
either the disjointness premise or (5). Removing specified smooth
points allows an exhaustion if the resulting disjoint embeddings
are supplied. It does not itself produce them, nor recover
smoothness at any missing singular point.

By [E54, (4) and (10)][e54], each nonflat piece in the supplied
class costs \(e_a>4\pi^2\) and leaves
\[
 E_*-e_a\ \ge\ \frac{Y_{\rm rd}^2-\widehat Y_a^2}{48}>0 .
 \tag{6}
\]
For convergence punctured at finitely many smooth points the
same estimate follows as in E54. The constant-scale convergence
on the punctured compact truncations gives
\(\liminf_i\|T_{g_i}\|_{L^2}^2\ge\|T_{g_a}\|_{L^2}^2=4e_a\)
by exhausting and ignoring the smooth volume-zero points. The
AE boundary term and scalar-flat Chern–Gauss–Bonnet give the
last identity. Equation (3) transfers the Yamabe bound through
all those points. Thus (2) gives
\(\widehat Y_a^2\ge Y_{\rm rd}^2-48(E_*-e_a)\).
The equality case in the sharp compact Yamabe bound would make
the nonflat piece's compactification conformally round and
its Weyl energy zero. [Tian–Viaclovsky, §7 (7.2)][tv] ·
[Schoen, (0.2) and Theorem 2][schoen]

If a nonflat piece is present, (1), (4) and its strict cost allow
at most one. For this piece, denoted by \(X\),
\[
 0\le e_0\le E_*-e_X<4\pi^2-\delta .
 \tag{7}
\]
The base is smooth, Bach-flat, positive Yamabe and a homotopy
sphere, so the closed Bach-flat gap forces \(e_0=0\). Exact
recognition then makes the base compactification conformally
round. All other piece energies vanish. Therefore the energy
missing from this representation under the inequality in (4) is,
in each sign,
\[
 E_*-e_0-\sum_a e_a=E_*-e_X
 \ge\frac{384\pi^2-\widehat Y_X^2}{48}>0 .
 \tag{8}
\]
This is a positive bound for the supplied nonflat piece. It
contradicts the asserted exact no-loss identities. Equations
(5) and (8) do not locate the remainder in specific annuli or
give a further concentration theorem. [E54, closed gap][e54] ·
[Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1][cc] · [CGY, Theorems A–B][cgy] ·
[SH-E01, exact recognition][sh]

## Zero nonflat pieces

With no nonflat pieces, the exact identity reduces to \(e_0=E_*\).
Since \(E_*\ge4\pi^2>0\), the smooth positive-Yamabe Bach-flat base
must be nonround. The closed gap then forces
\(4\pi^2<E_*\le8\pi^2-\delta\), excluding the lower endpoint.
Within that strict band the accounting condition is that the
base carry exactly the full limiting energy. Existence of such
a base is not established by the accounting. With the identity
relaxed, a round base leaves all \(E_*\) unrepresented, and a
nonround base must have \(4\pi^2<e_0\le E_*\). In this latter
case the comparison gives no positive missing-mass lower bound.

The result is conditional on a supplied smooth compactified
base and the stated convergence and disjointness data. It does
not transfer an exact Bach-flat compactness theorem to an
almost-Bach sequence or infer smooth convergence on the original
\(M\) from an energy identity alone.
