## Relative symbol and radial constants

Work in the E21 orthonormal chiral frames on oriented Euclidean
\(\mathbb R^4\) and with the five-dimensional trace-free symmetric
Weyl-block targets carrying the real curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. Replacing the derivatives by real
\(\xi=\ell e^0\), the TT symbol fiber has only the trace-free
\((h_{ij})_{1\le i,j\le3}\). Its only potentially nonzero
curvature-operator entries before projection are
\(\mathcal R'_{0i,0j}=-\ell^2h_{ij}/2\).
The respective \((e^0\wedge e^i\pm e^j\wedge e^k)/\sqrt2\)
compressions both give \(-\ell^2 h_{ij}/4\). The sign change
to Fourier differentiation affects both simultaneously.
Reflection normal to \(e^0\) sends the positive indexed basis
to minus the negative indexed basis, so the minus signs cancel
in endomorphism conjugation. Naturality and the common
longitudinal kernel therefore give
\(d_-=M d_+\) with positive relative sign on the full
trace-free symbol domain. This yields
\(d_-^*M=d_+^*\), \(d_-^*d_-=d_+^*d_+\), and
\(d_\pm d_\pm^*=|\xi|^4 I/16\), with \(\xi\ne0\).
The orthogonal reflection conjugation \(M\) is even and
norm-preserving. [E21, (3)–(6)][e21] · [Gover–Gursky, chiral
curvature splitting, p. 293][gg]

For the angular numerator, the E23 quaternionic matrix
\(L(x)u=\bar xux\) on the imaginary quaternions satisfies
\(\Delta L=0\), and \(P_V=L V L^{\mathsf T}\) is the quartic
\(H_4(x)V\) with \(H_4(x)=|x|^4M(x/|x|)\) off zero.
At \(x=1\), the derivatives are \(2I,-2J_i\), where
\(J_i u=e_i\times u\). The contraction
\(\sum_iJ_i VJ_i^{\mathsf T}=(\operatorname{tr}V)I-V^{\mathsf T}\)
gives \(\Delta P_V(1)=8(V+(\operatorname{tr}V)I-V^{\mathsf T})=0\).
Right-unit-quaternion equivariance propagates this to the unit
sphere, and homogeneity gives \(\Delta P_V=0\) everywhere.
[E23, quaternionic angular action and homogeneous quartic][e23]

With \(\Delta=\sum_i\partial_i^2\), spherical flux and
\(|S^3|=2\pi^2\) give
\(\Delta\log\rho=2\rho^{-2}\) and
\(\Delta\rho^{-2}=-4\pi^2\delta_0\), where \(\rho=|x|\).
Consequently \(G=-\log\rho/(8\pi^2)\) satisfies
\(\Delta^2G=\delta_0\). The \(e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\) convention
makes the fourth-order harmonic numerator positive:
\(H_4(\partial)\) has symbol \(i^4H_4(\xi)=H_4(\xi)\).
The transform of \(H_4(\partial)G\) is homogeneous of degree
zero, so its agreement off \(\xi=0\) with \(M(\xi)\) is exact.
A discrepancy supported at a point would be a sum of delta
derivatives with homogeneity degrees \(-4-k\).
On the physical side the associated kernel is the spherical
principal value of \(6P_V(x)/(\pi^2\rho^8)\). The quartic angular
mean is zero, so the principal value exists and tests to zero
against radial functions. The same holds for \(P_V(\partial)G\)
by the radial derivative identity below. Their difference,
if supported at zero and of degree \(-4\), could only be a
delta term, which radial testing excludes. [E22, kernel normalization][e22] ·
[Dyatlov, Theorem 4.19][dyatlov]

For \(t<\rho\), the spherical average of \(\log|x-t\omega|\)
is \(\log\rho+t^2/(4\rho^2)\): its radial Laplacian in \(t\)
is \(2/\rho^2\) by the harmonic mean of \(2|x-\cdot|^{-2}\).
Outside the radial source,
\(G*\chi=-(m_0\log\rho+m_2/(4\rho^2))/(8\pi^2)\).
For any harmonic quartic \(P_V\) the trace-free coefficient
tensor gives
\(P_V(\partial)\varphi(\rho^2)=16P_V(x)\varphi^{(4)}(\rho^2)\).
The fourth derivatives of \(\tfrac12\log s\) and \(s^{-1}\)
are \(-3s^{-4}\) and \(24s^{-5}\), respectively.
Thus the exterior response of \(T\), the flat relative multiplier,
to \(f=\chi Z\) is

\[
 Tf=a(\rho)M(n)Z,\qquad
 a(\rho)=\frac6{\pi^2}
              (m_0\rho^{-4}-2m_2\rho^{-6}),\qquad n=x/\rho.
\]

Here \(m_j=\int_{\mathbb R^4}|x|^j\chi\,dx\), without normalized
angular measure. This verifies the Riesz normalization and the
relative minus sign between the two radial moments. At the
support boundary the equality persists by smoothness of the
response to the smooth compact input. [E22, exterior moments][e22]

## Compact probes, zero extension and sharpness

On \(B=\{\rho<1\}\) take \(D_+h=tf+e\) and \(q=D_-h\)
for the same smooth variation, with \(e\in L^2(B)\).
The distribution computation also applies when the outputs
have the indicated \(L^2\) regularity for the measured norms.
Extension by zero is an \(L^2\) isometry. Polynomial symbol
identities act on Fourier \(L^2\) fields as tempered
distribution identities, and any derivative of the zero
extension agrees in \(B\) with the distributional derivative
of the original \(e\), by testing at compact support in \(B\).
Thus

\[
 z=q-T\widetilde e-tTf,\qquad
 D_-^*z= D_+^*D_+h-D_+^*(tf+e)=0\quad\text{on }B .
\]

There is no boundary term in this restriction.
Applying \(D_-\) gives \(\Delta^2z=0\), hence \(z\) is smooth
in the whole open ball. In particular the subtracted field
\(q-T\widetilde e=z+tTf\) has a smooth representative there.
When using separate \(L^2\) representatives their common
projection identity is an almost-everywhere identity.
[E26, zero extension][e26] · [E32, adjoint equation][e32] ·
[Melrose, Lecture 22, local elliptic regularity (1)][melrose]

Let \(A=\{2/3<\rho<1\}\), \(Y_V(n)=M(n)V\), and
\(\eta\in C^\infty_c((2/3,1))\). Set
\(\psi(s)=\eta(\sqrt{s})/s^2\) on its positive support and
extend smoothly by zero outside. For

\[
 \varphi(s)=\tfrac16\int_s^\infty(v-s)^3\psi(v)\,dv
\]

four differentiations give \(\varphi^{(4)}=\psi\), since
the signs of the four derivatives multiply to \(+1\).
Below the support \(\varphi\) is a polynomial of degree at
most three, so \(\varphi(|x|^2)\) is smooth at the origin.
It vanishes on a neighborhood of \(\rho=1\) and is smooth
across the outer support endpoint by smoothness of \(\psi\).
The two factors of \(16\) in
\(D_-D_+^*=H_4(\partial)/16\) and the radial derivative
formula cancel. Hence

\[
 D_-D_+^*(\varphi(|x|^2)V)=\eta(\rho)Y_V(n).
\]

The metric test \(D_+^*(\varphi(|x|^2)V)\) is smooth and
compactly supported in \(B\), including when it is nonzero
at the origin. The construction uses no conditions on
the radial moments of \(\eta\). Pairing against \(D_-^*z=0\)
eliminates the \(Y_V\) coefficient for all \(V\) on each
sphere in \(A\) by arbitrary radial tests and smoothness.
[E32, compact radial probes][e32]

Pointwise orthogonality of \(M(n)\) gives
\(\langle Y_V,Y_{V'}\rangle_{L^2(S^3)}
=2\pi^2\langle V,V'\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}\).
Thus the angular space \(\mathcal Y=\{Y_V\}\) has dimension five
and the norm squared for \(V=Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)\)
is \(12\pi^2\). The resulting projection is
\(P_{\mathcal Y}(q-T\widetilde e)=t a Y_Z\).
The projection is contractive, and \(T\) is an \(L^2\) isometry
by Plancherel. The sharp norm constant is

\[
 d_{\rm sharp}=\bigl[12\pi^2(a_0^2I_{-4}
         -2a_0a_2 I_{-6}+a_2^2 I_{-8})\bigr]^{1/2},
 \quad a_0=6m_0/\pi^2,\quad a_2=12m_2/\pi^2,\quad
 I_\nu=\int_{2/3}^1\rho^{\nu-1}\,d\rho .
\]

The three exponents follow from multiplying \(a^2\) by
the radial measure \(\rho^3\,d\rho\).
They give \(\|q\|_{L^2(A)}+\|e\|_{L^2(B)}
\ge |t|d_{\rm sharp}\). For \(e=0\), equality requires
and follows from vanishing of the component orthogonal
to \(\mathcal Y\) on \(A\). This happens for the restriction
and flattening of the global round **linear** TT lift of
\(tf_{\rm round}\), for which \(q=tTf\). It is an actual
smooth metric variation and proves optimality for the stated
local metric-variation class. Its flat curvature fields lie
in global \(L^2\), so the symbol relation off frequency zero
leaves no undetermined distributional component in their
\(L^2\) relation. [E21, (7)][e21] · [E32, norm estimate and attainment][e32]

E26's weaker estimate discards \(D_-^*z=0\) and keeps
\(\Delta^2z=0\). The fourth angular degree has biharmonic
radial powers \(4,6,-4,-6\), with the last two excluded at the
center. Projection gives the relaxed distance to the span
of \(\rho^4,\rho^6\) in measure \(\rho^3\,d\rho\).
The E26 Gram powers \(I_{12},I_{14},I_{16}\) and its
pairing vector follow directly by multiplying these two
regular powers against \(a\). This distance is strictly
smaller than \(d_{\rm sharp}\) because the stipulated seed
has \(a>0\) on \(A\), so its pairing with \(\rho^4\) is
nonzero. The positivity uses the B16 bump estimate
\(m_2/m_0<1/8\) from E22. Indeed \(v=4\rho^2\) converts
this ratio to one quarter of the mean of \(v\) for density
\(v\exp(-1/(1-v))\,dv\) on \((0,1)\). The density at \(v>1/2\)
is smaller than at \(1-v\), since the log of their ratio is
\(\log b-b+1/b<0\) for \(b=v/(1-v)>1\). This verifies the
strict estimate used here. The relaxed optimizer does not meet the
exact angular test. [E22, bump moment bound][e22] · [E26, Gram distance][e26]

## Compact lifts and the global adjoint pairing

The compact criterion concerns smooth signed radial
\(f=\chi Z\) with support in the inner ball and \(Z\ne0\) fixed.
For its operator-norm normalization, the pairing of the
linearized Riemann tensor with the four-index tensor \(T^z\)
of a pure positive Weyl block includes a factor \(1/4\)
from the two skew index pairs. In the real curvature
convention of E21, the four second-derivative terms in
\(\mathcal R'\) reduce, under this pairing, to four copies
of \(-T^z_{ij,pq}\partial_i\partial_p h_{jq}/2\)
before the \(1/4\) factor. Integration twice by parts gives

\[
 (D_+^*z)_{jq}=-\tfrac12\partial_i\partial_p T^z_{ij,pq}.
\]

Hence \(Z\) is an adjoint test, and for \(z=|x|^2 Z\) the
adjoint is a Ricci contraction of \(T^Z\), which vanishes.
Testing any compact metric lift gives both \(m_0=0\) and
\(m_2=0\). For sufficiency, \(p=G*\chi\) is smooth because
derivatives can be moved to the smooth compact factor.
The exterior potential formula proves \(p=0\) outside
the source radius when both moments vanish. Then
\(16D_+^*(pZ)\) is smooth, trace-free, supported no farther
out than that radius, and solves the equation by
\(D_+D_+^*=\Delta^2 I/16\). The result is linear and uses
the full trace-free domain. [E33, (2)–(5)][e33] ·
[Gover–Gursky, p. 295, double-divergence adjoint][gg]

In the supplied round patch \(r=\Omega^2\delta\), a
covariant metric variation \(h\) flattens to \(\Omega^{-2}h\)
and the Weyl outputs flatten by \(\Omega^2\) in the
induced orthonormal chiral frames, for both signs.
This follows from the \(\Omega^{-2}\) scaling of the
Weyl operator from flat to round. Differentiating the
positive symmetric frame map contributes zero at either
background because its Weyl block vanishes.
The curvature weight is an \(L^2\) isometry on each
region, using \(dV_r=\Omega^4\,dx\).
For the pairing with a global adjoint field, let \(a\)
denote its components in the round frame and put \(z=\Omega^2a\).
The weight follows by pairing
\(D_{+,r}h=\Omega^{-2}D_{+,\delta}(\Omega^{-2}h)\) with
\(a\) against \(\Omega^4dx\). Since \(\Delta^2 z=0\),
the angular average is \(z(0)+\rho^2\Delta z(0)/8\)
by the harmonic mean property for \(\Delta z\) and
\(\Delta|x|^2=8\). The global pairing of the radial
source is therefore
\(\langle Z,m_0 z(0)+(m_2/8)\Delta z(0)\rangle\).
This formula uses the actual adjoint center-data image
and leaves the rank of that global image unspecified.
[E21, conformal identity (2)][e21] · [E33, (6)–(8)][e33] ·
[Fefferman–Graham, discussion after (6.7)][fg]

## Nonlinear scope and the round southern calibration

On the supplied round patch the nonlinear estimate requires
the same fixed-volume chart
\(g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\), an \(r\)-trace-free
\(C^2\)-small \(h\), and the exact transported source
\(F_{r,+}(h)=t f_{\rm round}\). The linear source error is
\(-N_+(h)\) in round frames and the measured negative
block differs from its derivative by \(N_-(h)\).
The two-jet Taylor estimates bound both errors by
\(C\|h\|_{C^2}^2\). The positive symmetric frame
isometries preserve the pointwise curvature norms,
and the fixed volume gives the asserted bound in \(g_h\)
with the same \(d_{\rm sharp}\). A strictly positive
conclusion from this lower bound requires
\(C'\|h\|_{C^2}^2<|t|d_{\rm sharp}\). The local estimate
is an \(L^2\) statement on the annulus. [E26, Taylor estimate][e26] ·
[E32, refined nonlinear estimate][e32]

E28 uses the known *standard smooth* \(S^4\) with its
unit round metric and global round inverse. The
closed-cap result follows there from the uniform
first-order response and the \(O(t^2)\) curvature
remainder. The oriented pole chart
\(y=Jx/\rho^2\), \(J=R_{e_0}\), has
orthonormal frame transition \(JR_n\), of determinant
\(+1\). Its negative-block action composed with
the positive-to-negative reflection is the fixed
\(-I\), so conjugation cancels the angular term.
Writing \(w=|y|^2=\rho^{-2}\), the amplitude in the
new frame is
\((3/(2\pi^2))(1+w)^2(m_0-2m_2w)\) times \(Z\).
The equatorial and pole norms in the synthesis follow
by evaluating at \(w=1,0\). For \(0\le w\le1\)
the support bound \(m_2<m_0/4\) gives an increasing
norm and positive minimum at the pole.
The square of the angular norm contributes \(6\);
the sphere area contributes \(2\pi^2\); the \(\Omega\)
factors cancel in the squared \(L^2\) integral. This gives
the southern coefficient
\[
 \frac{432}{\pi^2}\left(\frac{m_0^2}{4}
         -\frac{2m_0m_2}{3}+\frac{m_2^2}{2}\right).
\]
The \(O(t^2)\) pointwise field remainder contributes
\(O(|t|^3)\) to this squared energy. The global example
has no arbitrary-exterior conclusion. [E23, pole transition][e23] ·
[E28, (2)–(9)][e28]
