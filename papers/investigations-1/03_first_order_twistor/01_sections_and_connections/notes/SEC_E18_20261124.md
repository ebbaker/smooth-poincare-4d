## Fixed collar and theorem

Use the product \(C=[-1,1]\times T^3\) with rectangular torus and
orientation \(ds\wedge dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3\). Write
\(\gamma=ds^2+\sum_i dx_i^2\) and fix the smooth positive seed
\(r=ds^2+e^{2f}(dx_1^2+dx_2^2)+e^{-4f}dx_3^2\), where \(f\) is nonzero
and smooth with support in the interior of \([-1,1]\). Metrics are
real Riemannian. ASD means \(W^+=0\) and Weyl blocks are curvature
endomorphisms with the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing.
[Fixed axial seed][e13] · [Shared conventions][common]

For two positive metrics \(a,b\) at a point, write
\(S_{a,b}=(a^{-1}b)^{-1/2}:(T,a)\to(T,b)\) for the positive symmetric
isometry and \(J_{a,b}=\Lambda^2 S_{a,b}^*:\Lambda_b^+\to\Lambda_a^+\)
for its induced pullback. For a general candidate \(g\), the fixed
seed-target equation is
\[
 J_{r,g}W_g^+J_{r,g}^{-1}=\lambda W_r^+ .
 \tag{1}
\]
For this fixed \(r\) and the fixed periods there is an
\(\epsilon>0\) such that no smooth positive metric \(g\) with
\(\|g-\gamma\|_{C^{10}(C)}<\epsilon\) and \(g=\gamma\) on open collars
of both ends solves (1) for any \(0<\lambda\le1\). The norm uses the
parallel product frame and coordinate derivatives through order ten.
The metric is unrestricted in the interior and may have arbitrary
normal–tangential components and dependence on all four coordinates.
The theorem applies in particular when \(dV_g=dV_\gamma\) is imposed
on the original collar. [B14 decision][b14]

## Periodic completion and the fixed target mean

Identify the two boundary tori by the identity on the \(x_i\) and
use \(s\) modulo two. This forms the auxiliary rectangular
\(\mathbb T^4\). The metrics \(g,r,\gamma\) extend periodically and
smoothly since each is exactly flat near both sides of the seam.
Both Weyl blocks in (1) are zero in a neighborhood of the seam, so
the equation also extends globally. Denote normalized flat measure
by \(d\mu=dV_\gamma/\operatorname{Vol}_\gamma(\mathbb T^4)\) and the
mean in parallel frames by a bar. Sobolev norms use the Fourier
weights \((1+|\xi|^2)^j\) in squared \(H^j\) norm, with \(|\xi|\)
computed for \(\gamma\) and coefficients normalized by \(d\mu\).
The periodic extension has
\(\|g-\gamma\|_{H^{10}}\le C_{\mathrm{ext}}\|g-\gamma\|_{C^{10}(C)}\)
with a constant depending on the fixed periods and the norm
conventions. The extension width does not enter this bound.

Use the parallel orthonormal basis of \(\Lambda_\gamma^+\) consisting
of \((ds\wedge dx_i+dx_j\wedge dx_k)/\sqrt2\) with cyclic \((i,j,k)\).
In this basis the transported seed is
\[
 T=J_{r,\gamma}^{-1}W_r^+J_{r,\gamma}
   =\Big(-\tfrac12 f''+(f')^2\Big) A,
 \qquad A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2).
\]
Indeed \(J_{r,\gamma}\) sends the flat basis to the specified seed
orthonormal basis. This identity uses indexed frames even at a
repeated seed eigenvalue. By periodicity,
\[
 \bar T=\tfrac12\left(\int_{-1}^1(f')^2\,ds\right) A,
 \qquad m_T:=|\bar T|_{\mathrm{HS}}>0 .
 \tag{2}
\]
The factor \(1/2\) comes from the normal period. No size assumption
on \(f\) is made. [E13 curvature calculation][e13] · [E16, (3)][e16]

## Volume and divergence gauge at a fixed finite grade

Use the global affine coordinates of the flat torus. Let
\(X\in H^8_0(T\mathbb T^4)\) be a mean-zero displacement vector,
and put \(\phi_X(x)=x+X(x)\) modulo the fixed lattice. For small
\(X\), Sobolev embedding gives \(\|dX\|_{C^0}<1\). The maps
\(x\mapsto x+uX(x)\) are local diffeomorphisms for \(0\le u\le1\),
preserve orientation, and have degree one by the resulting homotopy.
They are global diffeomorphisms on the torus. This uses only the
affine parallelization of the auxiliary torus.

Set \(p=\phi_X^*g\), \(G=\gamma^{-1}p\), and define
\[
 \rho=(\det G)^{-1/4},\qquad
 \widetilde g=\rho p,\qquad
 h=\log(\gamma^{-1}\widetilde g)
    =\log G-\tfrac14(\operatorname{tr}\log G)I .
 \tag{3}
\]
Thus \(dV_{\widetilde g}=dV_\gamma\) and \(h\) is \(\gamma\)-symmetric
and trace-free. Endomorphisms and covariant two-tensors here are
identified by \(\gamma\). Let \(\Pi_0\) subtract the mean, and define
the gauge map
\[\mathfrak D(X,g)=\Pi_0\delta_\gamma h\in H^6_0(T^*\mathbb T^4),
 \qquad (\delta_\gamma h)_j=\partial^i h_{ij}.\]
For \(g-\gamma\in H^{10}\) and \(X\in H^8_0\), this is a \(C^1\)
Banach map near \((0,\gamma)\). In components, composition of the
metric with \(\phi_X\) is a \(C^1\) map to \(H^8\) by the Sobolev
composition theorem with one spare derivative, applied from
\(H^9\times\mathcal D^8\) on the closed four-manifold. The factors
\(d\phi_X\) in the pullback are \(H^7\). Products, determinant,
logarithm and positive scalar power are smooth in the resulting
\(H^7\) algebra near \(\gamma\), and divergence maps \(H^7\) to
\(H^6\). Thus the inverse function argument does not require
differentiating a same-grade pullback. [Inci–Kappeler–Topalov v2,
Theorem 1.2(i), p. 5][ikt]

The derivative in the displacement at \((0,\gamma)\) is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_X\mathfrak D(0,\gamma)X
 &=\delta_\gamma\bigl(\mathcal L_X\gamma
                  -\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}_\gamma X)\gamma\bigr)\\
 &=\Delta_\gamma X^\flat+\tfrac12 d(\operatorname{div}_\gamma X).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{4}
\]
Here \(\Delta_\gamma=\partial^i\partial_i\) in the flat frame.
The mean projection in (4) is redundant because every periodic
divergence integrates to zero. At a nonzero lattice covector
\(\xi\), the eigenvalues in the transverse and longitudinal spaces
are \(-|\xi|^2\) and \(-3|\xi|^2/2\). Fourier inversion gives a
bounded isomorphism \(H^8_0\to H^6_0\). Constant vector fields are
excluded by the chosen displacement domain. The Banach implicit
function theorem gives a unique small \(X=X(g)\) there, \(C^1\) as
a function of \(g\), with \(\mathfrak D=0\). The actual divergence
already has zero mean. Consequently \(h\) is TT and, on a sufficiently
small \(H^{10}\) neighborhood, for a fixed constant \(C_G\) one has
\[
 \|X\|_{H^8}+\|h\|_{H^7}+\|\rho-1\|_{H^7}
       \le C_G\|g-\gamma\|_{H^{10}} .
 \tag{5}
\]
This follows from the local \(C^1\) maps and their values at \(\gamma\).

For smooth \(g\) the gauge and conformal factor are smooth as well.
For torus translation \(\tau_a\), translating the components of
\(X(g)\) by \(a\) preserves the mean-zero normalization and gives
the small solution for \(\tau_a^*g\). Indeed
\(\phi_{X(g)\circ\tau_a}=\tau_{-a}\circ\phi_{X(g)}\circ\tau_a\),
and the normalization and divergence commute with translations.
For fixed smooth \(g\), the map \((a,X)\mapsto\mathfrak D(X,\tau_a^*g)\)
is smooth in the same grades by the composition theorem for each
finite number of derivatives, since the translating metric has all
spare derivatives. The derivative in \(X\) at the gauged solution is
invertible by continuity from (4), after shrinking the neighborhood.
The implicit function theorem in \(a\) makes the translated
displacement a smooth \(H^8\)-valued function. Differentiating its
translations gives every spatial derivative of \(X\) in \(H^8\),
which implies smoothness. Equation (3) then gives smooth
\(\rho,\widetilde g,h\). The gauge is used on the closed auxiliary
torus and need not fix the original end collars.

## Flat constant modes and control of the Weyl average

Define the flat-chart Weyl map
\[
 F_\gamma(h)=J_{\gamma,\gamma(e^h\cdot,\cdot)}\,
           W_{\gamma(e^h\cdot,\cdot)}^+\,
           J_{\gamma,\gamma(e^h\cdot,\cdot)}^{-1},
 \qquad D=DF_\gamma(0).
\]
It is a smooth map of a small \(H^7\) neighborhood of zero in
trace-free symmetric tensors to \(H^5\) Weyl matrices. In the parallel
coordinates this follows from the exponential, curvature formula
through two derivatives, smooth matrix operations, and the Sobolev
algebra since \(5>4/2\). Write \(h=c+v\), \(c=\bar h\). The constant
trace-free symmetric \(c\) is retained. The metric
\(\gamma(e^c\cdot,\cdot)\) is flat and has fixed volume on the same
torus, so \(F_\gamma(c)=0\). It need not be identified with \(\gamma\)
by a periodic coordinate change. The remaining \(v\) is mean-zero TT.

The flat linear operator \(D\) is constant-coefficient of order two
with zero action on the constant Fourier mode. At each real
\(\xi\ne0\), choose an oriented orthonormal coframe with \(\xi\)
in its fourth direction. As in E02 (5), restriction to the TT
symbol fiber gives a scalar of magnitude \(|\xi|^2/4\) times the
isometric identification of the trace-free tangential block with
\(S^2_0\Lambda_\gamma^+\). The sign is immaterial after inserting
\(i\xi\) for Fourier derivatives. The coefficient only uses the
metric value and the second spatial jet of the variation, and
the flat background has no lower-order curvature terms.
Fourier inversion at each \(\xi\ne0\) defines an isomorphism from
mean-zero TT \(H^7\) to mean-zero Weyl \(H^5\). Its inverse has bound
\[
 \|v\|_{H^7}\le C_D\|Dv\|_{H^5},\qquad
 C_D=4(1+\mu_1^{-2}),\quad \mu_1=\min_{\xi\ne0}|\xi|.
 \tag{6}
\]
Indeed the quotient of the gain-two Sobolev weights per mode
is \((1+|\xi|^2)/|\xi|^2\). The real inverse follows by conjugation
symmetry of the Fourier multipliers. There is no inversion on
constant Weyl targets. [E02, §2 and (5)][e02] · [E16, (1)][e16]

Continuity of \(D^2F_\gamma\) gives a Lipschitz bound for the first
derivative on a sufficiently small \(H^7\) ball. Using
\(F_\gamma(c)=0\) and integrating along \(c+uv\), \(0\le u\le1\),
gives a fixed constant \(C_N\) such that
\[
 \|F_\gamma(c+v)-Dv\|_{H^5}
 \le C_N (\|c\|_{H^7}+\|v\|_{H^7})\|v\|_{H^7} .
 \tag{7}
\]
Set \(\theta=\|c\|_{H^7}+\|v\|_{H^7}\le2\|h\|_{H^7}\).
When \(C_D C_N\theta\le1/2\), (6)–(7) imply
\[
 \|v\|_{H^7}\le2C_D\|F_\gamma(h)\|_{H^5},\qquad
 |\overline{F_\gamma(h)}|_{\mathrm{HS}}
       \le C_N\theta\|v\|_{H^7} .
 \tag{8}
\]
The mean inequality uses \(\overline{Dv}=0\) and that averaging
has norm at most one for the normalized Sobolev norms. A term
independent of \(v\) in (7) would not give (8) after dividing by a
small chord parameter. Retaining the constant flat moduli is thus
part of the estimate.

## The moved target and the noncommuting frame maps

Let \(\phi=\phi_{X(g)}\), \(y=\phi(x)\) and use (3). Define
\[
 O_x=\rho(x)^{1/2}d\phi_x S_{\gamma,\widetilde g,x}
       :(T_x,\gamma_x)\longrightarrow(T_y,g_y).
\]
This is an orientation-preserving isometry because
\(\phi^*g=\rho^{-1}\widetilde g\). Denote its pullback on self-dual
two-forms by \(J_O(x)\). In dimension four the Hodge splitting
on two-forms is invariant under positive conformal scaling. The
Weyl curvature endomorphism scales by \(\rho^{-1}\) under the
metric change \(p\mapsto\rho p\). Naturality under pullback then gives
\[
 F_\gamma(h)(x)=\rho(x)^{-1}J_O(x)W_g^+(y)J_O(x)^{-1} .
\]
The scalar factor in the induced two-form pullback defining
\(J_O\) cancels in the conjugation. This is Weyl conformal
covariance and does not make the original fixed source invariant.
[Shared normalization][recognition]

Insert (1) in this identity and express the result in the flat
parallel frames at \(x,y\). Define
\[
 B_g(x)=J_O(x) J_{r,g}(y)^{-1}J_{r,\gamma}(y):
       \Lambda_{\gamma,y}^+\longrightarrow\Lambda_{\gamma,x}^+.
\]
It is an oriented fiber isometry. Its three factors have the
stated order, with no composition law assumed for symmetric
isometries of successive metric pairs. For a solution of (1) we have
the exact identity
\[
 \lambda^{-1}F_\gamma(h)=Z_g,
 \qquad Z_g(x)=\rho(x)^{-1} B_g(x)T(\phi(x)) B_g(x)^{-1} .
 \tag{9}
\]
Here \(Z_g\) on the right is defined for all small \(g\) independently
of \(\lambda\). In particular at \(g=\gamma\), \(X=0\), \(\rho=1\),
and the two seed transport factors cancel, giving \(B_\gamma=I\)
and \(Z_\gamma=T\).

The transport is \(C^1\) into \(H^7\) for \(g\) near \(\gamma\)
in \(H^{10}\). To check this in fixed bundles, form the matrices of
the tangent maps in the global coordinate frames and multiply their
induced six-by-six pullback matrices on all two-forms before
restriction to the parallel self-dual frames. The matrix functions
\(S_{r,g}\) are smooth pointwise in positive \(g\) with \(r\) fixed.
For example one can use the ordinary positive square root of
\(r^{-1/2}g r^{-1/2}\) and conjugate back. With \(r\) smooth these
matrix entries depend smoothly in \(H^{10}\) near the flat metric.
They are pulled back by \(\phi\) with one spare derivative as in the
gauge step. The other factors involve \(\widetilde g,\rho\) and
\(d\phi\) in \(H^7\), while \(T\circ\phi\) has the same Sobolev
composition control because \(T\) is fixed and smooth. Matrix
products and inversion are smooth in \(H^7\) on the positive or
invertible matrices under consideration. Thus after shrinking
the neighborhood there is a finite \(C_Z\), depending on the
fixed seed and torus, with
\[
 \|Z_g-T\|_{H^7}\le C_Z\|g-\gamma\|_{H^{10}} .
 \tag{10}
\]
In particular its \(H^5\) norm stays bounded and its mean converges to
\(\bar T\) along any sequence of such metrics approaching \(\gamma\).
[Inci–Kappeler–Topalov v2, Theorem 1.2(i)][ikt]

## Contradiction and scope

Choose the \(H^{10}\) neighborhood sufficiently small that the
gauge and estimates above hold and \(\|Z_g-T\|_{H^7}\le\min(1,m_T/4)\).
Then \(\|Z_g\|_{H^5}\le M_T:=\|T\|_{H^5}+1\) and
\(|\bar Z_g|_{\mathrm{HS}}\ge3m_T/4\). If (1) holds with \(\lambda>0\),
(8) and (9) give
\[
 \|v\|_{H^7}\le2C_D\lambda M_T,
 \qquad |\bar Z_g|_{\mathrm{HS}}
 \le 2C_DC_N M_T\theta .
 \tag{11}
\]
By (5), \(\theta\le2C_G\|g-\gamma\|_{H^{10}}\), so one further choice
of neighborhood makes the right side of (11) less than \(m_T/2\).
This contradicts the lower bound. Taking \(\epsilon\) in the theorem
small enough using \(C_{\mathrm{ext}}\) proves the \(C^{10}\)-open
exclusion uniformly over the specified chord parameters. The choices
use the fixed seed and the periods, and the argument concerns each
nearby metric individually without a path or parameter regularity
hypothesis. Periodic completion here requires the fixed flat ends.
This proof leaves unrestricted metrics farther from the flat chart,
free end data, and different prescribed sources as separate cases.
