## Reference and statement

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere
and \(r\) a supplied smooth positive-definite Riemannian metric.
Assume that \(K\) is a closed coordinate subball in an open round
patch \(O\) of \(r\), with orientation preserved by the round
identification. Work at fixed density \(\nu=dV_r\) in the full
trace-free metric chart \(g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\,\cdot,\cdot)\), where
\(h^\sharp=r^{-1}h\). The fixed bundles are \(V=S^2_{0,r}T^*M\) and
\(B=S^2_0\Lambda^+_r\). The Weyl target is the self-dual block with
its real curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Write
\(W=F_r(0)=W^+_r\), \(L=DF_r(0)\), \(E_0=E(r)=\|W\|_{L^2}^2\) and
\(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\) with respect to \(r,\nu\). Fix the E27
integer grade \(s\ge5\), \(X=H^{s+2}(V)\), \(Y=H^s(B)\).
Let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) be a real \(L^2\)-orthonormal basis of the
smooth kernel \(\mathcal C\), and let
\(b_{\mathrm{old}}(f)_i=\langle Z_i,f\rangle_{L^2}\).
[E03, chart (1), normal operator (3) and range][e03] · [E27][e27] ·
[Manuscript, fixed target and local lift][ms]

If \(E_0>0\) and \(m>0\), there is a smooth path of reference metrics
\(r_a\) through \(r\), with \(r_a=r\) on a common neighborhood of \(K\),
\(dV_{r_a}=\nu\), and \(E(r_a)=E_0\). For sufficiently small \(a\ne0\)
their compared full linearizations are surjective. For each such
\(a\), a smooth radial step in the chart at \(r_a\) attains energy
\((1-t)^2E_0\) for \(0\le t\le c|a|\) if \(b_{\mathrm{old}}(W)=0\),
and for \(0\le t\le c a^2\) if \(b_{\mathrm{old}}(W)\ne0\).
Here \(c>0\) and the allowable \(a\)-interval depend on the fixed
reference and chosen smooth path. The powers are optimal for
solutions in a ball of radius \(O(|a|)\) in \(X\).
[B26 decision][b26] · [E40][e40] · [E41][e41]

## Tangent to the energy level with invertible mixed matrix

In the fixed-volume chart and symmetric Weyl comparison,
\(E(g_h)=\|F_r(h)\|_{L^2(r,\nu)}^2\). Therefore for a smooth
trace-free covariant variation \(q\),
\[
 dE_r[q]=2\langle W,Lq\rangle_{L^2}
        =2\langle L^*W,q\rangle_{L^2}.
 \tag{1}
\]
If \(E_0>0\), there is a smooth \(u\in\Gamma(V)\) compactly supported
in \(M\setminus K\) with \(dE_r[u]\ne0\). Indeed E24 proves that
vanishing against all such variations makes the smooth cap extension
globally conformally round and hence zero-energy. In the E24
convention \(\int_M |W_g|_{(0,4)}^2dV_g=8E(g)\), by the signature
formula and the factor of four between the squared norms. The first
variation is \(dE_r[q]=-\frac12\langle B_r,q\rangle_{L^2}\), where
\(B_r\) denotes the Bach tensor in the cited convention. One may
choose a bump times \(B_r\) in the exterior where it is nonzero.
The E24 rigidity uses the smooth Bach-flat equation on the closed
manifold and the analytic local conformal representative in
Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorem 1.3(c), with conformal harmonic
coordinates from their Theorem 2.2. It applies to the present round
cap without any assumption on the smooth exterior.
[E24, (1) and exterior stationarity][e24] ·
[Gover–Gursky, p. 294][gg] · [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo,
§1 and (1.1)][bgim] · [Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorems 1.3(c), 2.2][ll]

Suppose \(m>0\). Regard the compared derivative \(A\) as a function
of a logarithmic reference displacement \(p\) in the chart over \(r\),
using the positive symmetric identifications of E40 for tangent,
trace-free metric and Weyl bundles. Write \(A'_0[q]\) for its
directional derivative at \(p=0\) in \(q\). The fixed-bundle
two-jet formula of E40 is jointly smooth in \(p,h\) at the chosen
Sobolev grades, so \(A'_0[q]\) is linear in \(q\). For fixed smooth \(q\)
it is a differential operator in the metric input of order at most
two with smooth coefficients.

Use the E41 oscillatory fields \(v_N\) and the smooth exact old
kernel fields \(k_{j,N}\). They satisfy
\(\|v_N\|_{C^0}=O(N^{-2})\) and \(\|k_{j,N}\|_{L^2}=O(1)\).
For the second bound, E41 (4) bounds the first derivatives of the
longitudinal vector fields and the projection correction in
E41 (11) tends to zero in \(L^2\). Their original matrix
\(\langle Z_i,A'_0[v_N]k_{j,N}\rangle\) tends to the nonsingular
matrix \(-D/4\) of E41 (2), (9) and (13). By (1),
\(|dE_r[v_N]|\le 2\|L^*W\|_{L^1}\|v_N\|_{C^0}=O(N^{-2})\).
Define
\[
 v_N^\parallel=v_N-\frac{dE_r[v_N]}{dE_r[u]}\,u .
 \tag{2}
\]
Then \(dE_r[v_N^\parallel]=0\). The change to any matrix entry has
absolute value at most
\[
 \left|\frac{dE_r[v_N]}{dE_r[u]}\right|
      \|(A'_0[u])^*Z_i\|_{L^2}\,\|k_{j,N}\|_{L^2}
   =O(N^{-2}).
\]
The adjoint is for the fixed \(r,\nu\). Its value on the smooth \(Z_i\)
is fixed, regardless of the high Sobolev norms of the \(k_{j,N}\).
Thus the limiting matrix remains nonsingular. Fix one sufficiently
large finite \(N\), let \(v=v_N^\parallel\), and write \(k_j=k_{j,N}\).
Their matrix \(\mathsf M_{ij}=\langle Z_i,A'_0[v]k_j\rangle\) is
invertible. The support of \(v\) lies in the compact union of the
exterior supports of \(v_N\) and \(u\). This support conclusion
concerns the reference variations \(u,v\). The \(k_j\) may be global.
[E41, (2)–(4), (9), (11)–(13)][e41] · [E40, compared derivative][e40]

Define \(r(a,b)=r(e^{(av+bu)^\sharp}\,\cdot,\cdot)\) and
\(\mathcal E(a,b)=E(r(a,b))-E_0\). This scalar function is smooth.
At \((0,0)\), \(\mathcal E=0\), \(\partial_a\mathcal E=0\),
and \(\partial_b\mathcal E=dE_r[u]\ne0\). The implicit function
theorem gives a smooth \(b=b(a)\) near zero with \(b(0)=b'(0)=0\)
and \(\mathcal E(a,b(a))=0\). In particular \(b(a)=O(a^2)\), and
\[
 r_a=r\bigl(e^{(a v+b(a)u)^\sharp}\,\cdot,\cdot\bigr),\qquad E(r_a)=E_0 .
 \tag{3}
\]
The exponential is positive and has unit determinant as an
endomorphism relative to \(r\). The support of the logarithm is
in a fixed compact subset of \(M\setminus K\), so a common
neighborhood of \(K\) inside \(O\) retains the round metric exactly
along (3). Its logarithmic tangent at zero is \(v\). By the chain
rule, the compared family \(A_a\) of E40 along (3) is smooth in
operator norm and has \(\partial_a A_a|_0=A'_0[v]\).
The \(O(a^2)\) logarithmic correction therefore preserves the mixed
matrix \(\mathsf M\). [E40, finite-jet comparison and (3)–(5)][e40]

## The radial direction in the desingularized inverse

Let \(U=H^{s+4}(B)\cap\mathcal C^\perp\), \(\mathcal Jc=\sum_j c_jk_j\),
and \(P=LL^*\). Along the path (3), write \(\widehat F(a,h)\) for
the curvature map in the symmetric chart at \(r_a\) expressed in
the fixed bundles over \(r\), with \(W_a=\widehat F(a,0)\). In the
notation of E40, the desingularized column operator is
\[
 T_a(\psi,c)=A_a L^*\psi+\frac{A_a\mathcal J}{a}\,c .
 \tag{4}
\]
It extends smoothly to \(a=0\), since \(L\mathcal J=0\) and
\(a^{-1}A_a\mathcal J=\int_0^1(\partial_a A)_{\theta a}\mathcal J\,d\theta\).
Its limiting matrix under \(b_{\mathrm{old}}\) is \(\mathsf M\).
The restricted inverse of \(P:U\to\mathcal C^\perp\cap Y\) and
\(\mathsf M^{-1}\) give the bounded inverse at zero. Hence \(T_a^{-1}\)
exists and is smooth across zero for small \(|a|\), by bounded-operator
inversion. For \(a\ne0\) use precisely the E40 right inverse
\[
 Q_a f=L^*\psi+a^{-1}\mathcal J c,\qquad (\psi,c)=T_a^{-1}f .
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular \(\|Q_a\|_{Y\to X}\le C_Q/|a|\), and the power
\(|a|^{-1}\) is necessary for every bounded right inverse on all
of \(Y\), since \(b_{\mathrm{old}}A_a=b_{\mathrm{old}}(A_a-L)=O(|a|)\).
The maps retain the gain of two Sobolev orders.
[E03, elliptic inverse][e03] · [E40, (3)–(6)][e40]

Set \((\psi_a,c_a)=T_a^{-1}W_a\). Both are smooth through zero and
\[
 c_0=\mathsf M^{-1}b_{\mathrm{old}}(W).
 \tag{6}
\]
If \(b_{\mathrm{old}}(W)=0\), division of \(c_a\) by \(a\) has a
smooth extension, obtained by integrating \(c'_{\theta a}\)
over \(\theta\in[0,1]\). Hence \(Q_a W_a\) has a limit in \(X\)
and is bounded there. It is also bounded away from zero when
\(E_0>0\), since \(A_aQ_a W_a=W_a\), \(\sup\|A_a\|<\infty\)
and \(W_a\to W\ne0\).

If \(b_{\mathrm{old}}(W)\ne0\), then \(\mathcal J c_0\ne0\) by
matrix invertibility and
\[
 aQ_a W_a\longrightarrow \mathcal J c_0\quad\text{in }X .
 \tag{7}
\]
Thus \(\|Q_a W_a\|_X\) has sharp two-sided order \(|a|^{-1}\).
Moreover any solution \(A_a h=W_a\) satisfies
\(|b_{\mathrm{old}}(W_a)|\le C|a|\|h\|_X\), which gives the same
pole lower bound for any right inverse evaluated on this direction.
For the nonlinear slice below, the initial radial tangent is
\(-Q_a W_a\).

## Duration in a shrinking chart

For the fixed path (3), the joint two-jet smoothness gives a bound
\(H\ge1\) for \(D_h^2\widehat F\) on a common small chart ball for
all small \(a\). In particular
\[
 N_a(h)=\widehat F(a,h)-W_a-A_a h,\qquad
 \|N_a(h)\|_Y\le (H/2)\|h\|_X^2
\]
and \(N_a\) has Lipschitz constant at most \(H\rho\) on the radius
\(\rho\) ball. As in E40, \(\mathcal S=L^*U\oplus\mathcal J\mathbb R^m\)
is the closed image of \(Q_a\) for \(a\ne0\). Choose
\(\rho_a=\gamma|a|\) with fixed \(\gamma>0\) small so that
\(\|Q_a\|H\rho_a\le1/2\), and \(\rho_a\) lies within the common
chart ball. Put \(\eta_a=\|Q_a W_a\|_X\).
For \(t\eta_a\le\rho_a/2\), the map
\[
 h\longmapsto -t Q_a W_a-Q_a N_a(h)
\]
is a contraction of the closed radius-\(\rho_a\) ball in
\(\mathcal S\) into itself. The quadratic remainder contributes
at most \(\rho_a/4\). The fixed point is unique in this ball
within the slice, starts at \(h=0\) and solves
\[
 \widehat F(a,h_a(t))=(1-t)W_a . \tag{8}
\]
Using the bound \(\eta_a\le C\) from the first case of (6), or the
bound \(\eta_a\le C/|a|\) from (7), proves the respective sufficient
durations \(c|a|\) and \(c a^2\), with \(c>0\) independent of \(a\)
on the fixed path. In each case shrink \(|a|\) so those intervals
lie in \([0,1)\). The differential on the slice is invertible
throughout the ball by the same contraction bound. The E40
translation argument, with the row \(b_{\mathrm{old}}(\psi)=0\)
on the potential \(\psi\in H^{s+4}(B)\), gives smoothness in space
and \(t\) for each fixed \(a\ne0\). The source \(tW_a\) is smooth
and all background fields, including the finite columns \(k_j\),
are smooth. [E04, (1)–(4)][e04] ·
[E40, exact lift and spatial regularity][e40]

For optimality on this metric scale, fix \(C_*>0\) and consider
any solution of (8) with \(\|h\|_X\le C_*|a|\), allowing the full
chart, and \(0\le t\le1\). If \(b_{\mathrm{old}}(W)\ne0\), then
\[
 t |b_{\mathrm{old}}(W_a)|
 \le C\bigl(|a|\|h\|_X+\|h\|_X^2\bigr) = O(a^2).
 \tag{9}
\]
This follows by applying \(b_{\mathrm{old}}\) to (8),
using \(b_{\mathrm{old}} L=0\) and the same remainder estimate.
The left coefficient tends to a positive number. If
\(b_{\mathrm{old}}(W)=0\) and \(E_0>0\), the corresponding
unprojected bound is \(t\|W_a\|_Y\le C\|h\|_X=O(|a|)\), and
\(\|W_a\|_Y\) is bounded away from zero. Thus the duration powers
are necessary on the specified shrinking metric scale, beyond
the particular inverse used to construct a solution. These bounds
give no time restriction for solutions allowed to leave that scale.

If \(E_0>0\) and \(m=0\), choose \(r_a=r\) and work directly at
\(r\). The inverse \(Q_0=L^*(LL^*)^{-1}\) has finite norm and one
can take a fixed positive chart radius and a fixed positive
radial duration from the same contraction. The old moment rows
are empty. If \(E_0=0\), smoothness gives \(W^+=0\) and the
signature gives \(W^-=0\). The exact conformally flat recognition
on the closed simply connected \(M\) then identifies its smooth
type with \(S^4\). For the prescription the constant metric \(r\)
is a solution for all \(t\). [E03, normal inverse][e03] ·
[SH-E01, exact recognition][recognition]

## Energy, Yamabe sign and geometry of the cap

Equation (8) holds in the new reference's own chart. In particular
it preserves the fixed pointwise density \(\nu\). The chiral
isometries preserve the Hilbert–Schmidt norm, so along the radial
step on the original \(M\),
\[
 E(g_{a,t})=(1-t)^2 E(r_a)=(1-t)^2 E_0 .
 \tag{10}
\]
For \(E_0>0\) this is a strict decrease at every supplied
\(0<t<1\), following an exactly level move (3).
The locality and the moving Weyl target in (8) are those of E40.
The constructed metric equals the round input near \(K\) at
\(t=0\). Throughout the radial step the marking, density and
\(W^+=0\) survive on the common neighborhood because the
prescribed target vanishes there. The metric can deform there
and the construction gives no control of \(W^-\) there. In
particular the resulting ASD neighborhood need no longer be round.
[E40, fixed-target comparison and scope][e40]

For any supplied time the sign of the conformal-class Yamabe constant
\(Y_{a,t}\) equals that of the bottom eigenvalue of
\(6\Delta_{g_{a,t}}+s_{g_{a,t}}\), with \(\Delta=-\mathrm{div}\nabla\).
This follows from the conformal Laplacian and a positive Yamabe
minimizer. The eigenvalue is continuous in the \(C^2\) metric
topology by Rayleigh quotient comparison. In the \(m>0\) construction,
(3), the \(O(|a|)\) bound on the chart perturbation, and Sobolev
embedding give \(g_{a,t}\to r\) in \(C^2\) uniformly over the
supplied intervals. In particular if \(Y(M,[r])>0\), that sign
is retained for small enough \(|a|\) over both parts of the move.
[Lee–Parker, (2.6)–(2.7)][leeparker] ·
[Schoen, §3, Theorem 3 and proof][schoen] · [E10, sign argument][e10]

For the unit-volume Yamabe representative \(\widehat g\) of any
supplied slice, Chern–Gauss–Bonnet and signature give
\[
 Y_{a,t}^{\,2}
    =48(8\pi^2-E(g_{a,t}))+12\|\operatorname{Ric}_0(\widehat g)\|_{L^2}^2.
 \tag{11}
\]
Thus the sign is constant on the connected part of the radial
interval where \(E(g_{a,t})<8\pi^2\), and one positive checkpoint
there determines positivity throughout that part. If the initial
marked reference (including \(r_a\)) has energy exactly \(8\pi^2\),
vanishing Yamabe constant would require a Ricci-flat conformal
representative by (11). Its conformal class would be Bach-flat,
contradicting E24 with the round neighborhood and nonzero energy.
The equality case for a general radial output is outside that
round-reference argument. [E10, (2)–(3)][e10] ·
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107, (0.4)][cgy] ·
[E24][e24] · [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1][bgim]

At a supplied time with \(Y_{a,t}>0\) and \(E(g_{a,t})<4\pi^2\),
Chang–Gursky–Yang Theorem A gives \(S^4\) or \(\mathbb{RP}^4\)
up to diffeomorphism. Simple connectivity excludes the latter.
Indeed their threshold uses the full four-index energy \(8E\)
and \(\chi(M)=2\). For \(E_0>0\), the energy test is
\(t>1-2\pi/\sqrt{E_0}\) within the constructed interval.
The local durations neither guarantee passage through that
threshold nor supply a positive checkpoint from the energy
alone. There is no uniform step efficiency or repeated-step
estimate in this result. The constants use one fixed finite
frequency and adjusted path. Further, the round-patch hypothesis
for E41 is a property of the reference used here, and is not
guaranteed by the ASD patch after the radial solve.
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107, Theorem A and Remarks 1–2][cgy] · [E10][e10]
