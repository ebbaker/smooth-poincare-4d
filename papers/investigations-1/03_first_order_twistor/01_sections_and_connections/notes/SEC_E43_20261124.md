## Statement

Use the smooth closed oriented Riemannian homotopy four-sphere, smooth
reference metric \(r\) and protected closed round subball \(K\) of
[E27][e27]. Set \(\nu=dV_r\), \(E=\Lambda_r^+\), \(V=S^2_0T^*M\) and
\(B=S^2_0E\), with the real trace-free curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt
pairing on \(B\). All adjoints and Sobolev norms use \(r,\nu\). For
\(h\in\Gamma(V)\), put \(a=h^\sharp=r^{-1}h\),
\(g_h=r(e^a\cdot,\cdot)\), and \(I_h=\Lambda^2(e^{-a/2})^*\)
restricted to \(\Lambda^+_{g_h}\). Write
\[
 F_r(h)=I_h W_{g_h}^+ I_h^{-1},\qquad W=F_r(0),\qquad L=dF_r(0),
 \qquad \mathcal C=\ker L^*,\qquad P=LL^*,\quad Q=L^*G_P .
\]
Here \(G_P\) is the orthogonal generalized inverse. The chart is
pointwise fixed-volume. This proof uses the full trace-free metric
domain. [E03, (1)–(4)][e03] · [Manuscript, `lift:chart` and `lift:normal`][manuscript]

Let \(\phi\) be a smooth Gaussian distance coordinate on an open patch
\(U\subset M\setminus K\), with \(n=\nabla_r\phi\) of unit norm, and
let \(b\in C_c^\infty(U;\mathbb R)\). For \(N\to+\infty\) define
\[
 X_N=N^{-1}b\sin(N\phi)n,\quad e_N=(\mathcal L_{X_N}r)_0,\quad
 p_N=QLe_N,\quad k_N=e_N-p_N ,
\]
extending \(X_N\) smoothly by zero. Each \(k_N\) is a smooth global
member of \(\ker L\). For every \(Z\in\mathcal C\), with the subscript
\(2\) denoting the real \(L^2(r,\nu)\) pairing,
\[
 \lim_{N\to\infty}\langle Z,F''_r(0)[k_N,k_N]\rangle_2
     =\frac18\int_M b^2\langle Z,W\rangle_r\,\nu .
 \tag{1}
\]
For a finite family of such cutoffs with disjoint compact supports,
the corresponding projected mixed Hessians tend to zero for different
supports, including independent frequencies tending to infinity. The
Hessian in (1) is the second derivative. The quadratic Taylor coefficient
has half its value.

## Exact action in the symmetric chart

Fix a smooth real vector field \(X\) for the differentiation. Let
\(\varphi_t\) be its flow, \(J_t=\varphi_t^*\nu/\nu\),
\(\rho_t=J_t^{-1/2}\), \(D_t=d\varphi_t|_x\) and
\(y=\varphi_t(x)\). The metric \(\rho_t\varphi_t^*g_h\) has volume
\(\nu\) at every base \(h\). Its chart coordinate will be denoted
\(H(t,h)\), with \(H(0,h)=h\). Define
\[
 A(t,h)=\rho_t^{1/2}e^{a(y)/2}D_t,\qquad
 e^{H(t,h)^\sharp}=A(t,h)^\dagger A(t,h),\qquad
 O(t,h)=A(t,h)(A(t,h)^\dagger A(t,h))^{-1/2},
 \tag{2}
\]
where \(\dagger\) is the reference metric adjoint. The polar isometry
\(O(t,h)\) goes from \((T_xM,r_x)\) to \((T_yM,r_y)\) and preserves
orientation. It factors as
\(e^{a(y)/2}\rho_t^{1/2}D_t e^{-H(t,h)^\sharp/2}\), the composition of
the two chart comparisons and the normalized pullback isometry. Hence
with \(O^*\) on self-dual two-forms,
\[
 F_r(H(t,h))(x)=J_t(x)^{1/2}
       O(t,h)^* F_r(h)(y)(O(t,h)^*)^{-1}.
 \tag{3}
\]
The scalar follows from the conformal transformation of the covariant
Weyl tensor by \(\rho_t\), then raising the curvature-operator pair with
the inverse metric twice. The Hodge star on two-forms is unchanged by
the scaling in dimension four. [Fefferman–Graham, §6, discussion after (6.7)][fg]

Define \(\Omega_X(h)=\partial_t O(0,h)\) in a frame parallel along
\(\varphi_t(x)\), and let \(\iota\) be the infinitesimal pullback on
\(E\) induced by a skew tangent endomorphism \(S\). Thus
\((\iota(S)\alpha)(u,v)=\alpha(Su,v)+\alpha(u,Sv)\). Write
\(d=\operatorname{div}_r X\), \(M_X=\nabla X\) as a tangent
endomorphism, and \(A_X=M_X-dI/4\). In the parallel frames,
\(\partial_t D_0=M_X\) and \(\partial_t(\rho_t^{1/2})_0=-d/4\).
At \(t=0\), the positive polar factor of \(A(0,h)\) is \(e^{a/2}\)
and \(O(0,h)=I\). Differentiating the polar product gives the exact
Sylvester equation
\[
 \Omega_X(h)e^{a/2}+e^{a/2}\Omega_X(h)
       =\partial_t A(0,h)-\partial_t A(0,h)^\dagger,\qquad
 \partial_t A(0,h)=\nabla_X(e^{a/2})+e^{a/2}A_X .
 \tag{4}
\]
The first term of \(\partial_t A(0,h)\) is self-adjoint and cancels
on the right. Put \(S_X=\operatorname{sym} A_X\) and
\(\Omega_0=\operatorname{skew} A_X=\operatorname{skew}\nabla X\).
For \(e=(\mathcal L_Xr)_0\), one has \(e^\sharp=2S_X\). At
\(h=sv\), differentiate (4) in \(s\) at zero. The constant term gives
\(\Omega_X(0)=\Omega_0\), and the derivative \(\Omega'_v\) obeys
\[
 2\Omega'_v+\tfrac12(\Omega_0v^\sharp+v^\sharp\Omega_0)
   =\tfrac12(v^\sharp A_X-A_X^\dagger v^\sharp),
 \qquad
 \Omega'_v=\tfrac14[v^\sharp,S_X]=\tfrac18[v^\sharp,e^\sharp].
 \tag{5}
\]
This rotation term is algebraic in \(v\).

Let \(B_X(h)=\partial_t H(0,h)\), so \(B_X(0)=e\) by (2).
Differentiating (3) in \(t\) at zero gives
\[
 dF_r(h)B_X(h)=
 \nabla_X F_r(h)+\tfrac{d}{2}F_r(h)+[\iota(\Omega_X(h)),F_r(h)] .
 \tag{6}
\]
On target sections define \(T_X=\nabla_X+d/2+[\iota(\Omega_0),\cdot]\).
Thus \(Le=T_XW\), agreeing with the manuscript's `lift:gauge`.
Differentiate (6) in the base direction \(v\) at \(h=0\). The extra
term on the left is \(L(D B_X(0)[v])\), which pairs to zero with
every \(Z\in\mathcal C\) for each individual \(X,v\). Consequently
\[
 \langle Z,F''_r(0)[e,v]\rangle_2
   =\langle Z,T_X Lv\rangle_2
     +\left\langle Z,\big[\iota([v^\sharp,e^\sharp]/8),W\big]\right\rangle_2.
 \tag{7}
\]
The pure normalized pullback is included by taking \(v=e\). Its
mixed base rotation in (7) vanishes exactly since
\([e^\sharp,e^\sharp]=0\). With \(v=p_N\), the rotation contributes
at most \(C_{r,W,Z}\|e_N\|_{L^\infty}\|p_N\|_{L^2}\).
Equation (7) uses a flow derivative and a base derivative in the
fixed chart, keeping the base dependence of the isometry in (3).

## Projection and limit

Suppress \(N\) on \(X,e,p,k\) in the calculation below.
Differentiation on the patch gives
\[
 \nabla X=b\cos(N\phi)\,n\otimes n^\flat
  +N^{-1}\sin(N\phi)(n\otimes db+b\nabla n).
 \tag{8}
\]
Thus \(X=O(N^{-1})\), \(\Omega_0=O(N^{-1})\),
\(d=b\cos(N\phi)+O(N^{-1})\) and \(e=O(1)\) in \(L^\infty\),
with constants for fixed \(r,b,\phi\). In particular, for every fixed
smooth target section \(Y\),
\[
 T_XY=\tfrac12 b\cos(N\phi)Y+O_Y(N^{-1})\quad\text{in }L^\infty .
 \tag{9}
\]
All terms from \(db\) and \(\nabla n\) in the divergence and skew action
are in the displayed remainder. For a smooth compactly supported
amplitude in the patch, the integrals against \(\cos(N\phi)\) and
\(\cos(2N\phi)\) are \(O(N^{-1})\) by integration by parts along
\(n\), where \(n(\phi)=1\). By (9) and density, \(Le=T_XW\) is bounded
and weakly converges to zero in \(L^2\).

The symbol of \(P\) in E03 is \(|\xi|_r^4 I_B/16\).
Elliptic regularity and the closed-manifold Fredholm theorem give the
bounded inverse \(G_P:L^2\cap\mathcal C^\perp\to
H^4\cap\mathcal C^\perp\), extended by zero on \(\mathcal C\).
Thus \(Q:L^2\to H^2\) is bounded. Since \(Le\perp\mathcal C\),
\(Lp=PG_P Le=Le\) and \(Lk=0\). Elliptic regularity implies
smoothness of \(p\) and \(k\). The bounded linear map \(Q\) gives
\(p\rightharpoonup 0\) in \(H^2\), hence \(p\to 0\) in \(H^1\) by
compact inclusion. [E03, (3)–(4)][e03] · [Melrose, Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose] ·
[Kottke, Proposition 1.27 and Theorem 1.43][kottke]

For any fixed smooth \(Z\), the paired Hessian satisfies
\[
 |\langle Z,F''_r(0)[u,v]\rangle_2|
 \le C_{Z,r}\|u\|_{H^1}\|v\|_{H^1}\qquad (u,v\text{ smooth}).
 \tag{10}
\]
Indeed, the curvature expression is affine in the second metric
derivatives and polynomial of degree at most two in the first, with
smooth coefficients in the metric and inverse. Substitution of the
pointwise exponential chart and algebraic Weyl projection and
transport preserves that structure for the two-jet of \(h\).
The bilinear second derivative at zero has smooth coefficients
multiplying contractions of \(u\nabla^2v\), \(v\nabla^2u\),
\(\nabla u\nabla v\), and terms of total derivative order at most one.
After contracting with \(Z\), integrate the second derivative terms
by parts once on the closed manifold, including derivatives of the
coefficients and \(Z\). Cauchy–Schwarz gives (10). It follows that
the Hessian pairing with \((p,p)\) tends to zero.

The formal adjoint of \(\nabla_X\) is \(-\nabla_X-d\).
Commutation by the skew endomorphism \(\iota(\Omega_0)\) is
skew-adjoint for the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing, so \(T_X^*=-T_X\).
This gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \langle Z,T_XLe\rangle_2
   &=-\langle T_XZ,T_XW\rangle_2\\
   &=-\tfrac14\int_M b^2\cos^2(N\phi)\langle Z,W\rangle_r\,\nu
     +O(N^{-1})\\
   &\longrightarrow -\tfrac18\int_M b^2\langle Z,W\rangle_r\,\nu .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{11}
\]
Moving \(T_X\) to the fixed test field controls the derivative of the
oscillatory correction and the skew remainder at frequency \(N\).
Equation (7) with \(v=e\) gives the pure limit (11). With \(v=p\),
use \(Lp=Le\) and the rotation estimate following (7) to get the same
mixed limit. By symmetry of the Hessian and (10), the pairing with
\((k,k)\) equals the pure pairing minus twice the mixed pairing plus
an \(o(1)\) correction. This gives the positive coefficient in (1).

## Disjoint supports and scope

For different indices \(i,j\), the uncorrected sections \(e_i,e_j\)
have disjoint compact supports, and locality gives
\(F''_r(0)[e_i,e_j]=0\). The transport term for \((e_i,p_j)\) in
(7) vanishes because \(Lp_j=Le_j\) has support in the other compact
set, and \(X_i\) and its first derivative vanish off the \(i\)-th
support. The rotation term is bounded by
\(C\|e_i\|_{L^\infty}\|p_j\|_{L^2}\) and tends to zero.
Exchange \(i,j\) for the other mixed term and apply (10) to
\((p_i,p_j)\). These estimates apply to a fixed finite family, with
frequencies tending to infinity independently. Since \(\mathcal C\)
is finite dimensional and smooth, (1) and the mixed limits also hold
for the projected vectors in \(\mathcal C\).

The result calculates the moments of genuine kernel tensors in the
original chart. The cutoffs are exterior, while the corrections and
kernel tensors are global. Exterior sign and the hypotheses for a
nonlinear radial chord enter the separate E44 test.
