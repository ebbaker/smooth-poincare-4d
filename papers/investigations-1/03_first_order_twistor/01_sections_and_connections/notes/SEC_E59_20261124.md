## Setting and statements

Let \(A=\{r\le|x|\le R\}\) and \(A^*=\{r/2<|x|<2R\}\) in \(\mathbb R^4\), with \(R\ge2r\). Let \(g=\delta+v\) be a smooth Riemannian metric on \(A^*\) and put
\[
 \varepsilon=\|v\|_{L^\infty(A^*)},\qquad K=\|D^2v\|_{L^2(A^*)},\qquad
 \mathfrak B=\int_{A^*}|B_g|_g\,dV_g .
\]
Norms and derivatives of \(v\) are Euclidean, \(|v|\) is the Frobenius norm of the matrix \((v_{ij})\), and unlabelled \(L^p\) norms use \(dx\). The Bach tensor is the one of the manuscript, equation `var:bach`, so that the gradient of the full Weyl functional is \(-4B_g\). The Weyl norm is the full tensor norm \(|W|^2=W_{ijkl}W^{ijkl}\), which is \(4(|W^+|^2+|W^-|^2)\) in the Hilbert–Schmidt norms of the manuscript. A constant is called universal when it depends only on the dimension four and on the two cutoff profiles fixed below. The three data are unchanged by a dilation of the chart, and the length \(\log(R/r)\) does not enter any constant.

**Theorem 1 (one chart).** There are universal constants \(\varepsilon_0\in(0,\tfrac14]\) and \(C\) such that \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\) implies
\[
 \int_A|W_g|^2\,dV_g\ \le\ C\,\varepsilon\,\bigl[\mathfrak B+(K+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon K)\bigr]. \tag{1}
\]
For the cutoff \(\eta\) of the proof, \(\|\eta\,Dv\|_{L^4}^2\le C\varepsilon(K+\varepsilon)\).

Nothing is assumed about \(\|Dv\|_{L^4}\), about the size of \(\mathfrak B\), or about the derivative energy of \(v\) on the two end shells. Under the hypotheses of the handoff, \(\varepsilon_i\to0\) with \(K_i\) bounded, the Weyl energy of \(A_i\) tends to zero as soon as \(\mathfrak B_i\) stays bounded.

**Corollary 2 (divergence-free trace-free chart).** If in addition \(\partial_iv_{ij}=0\) and \(v_{ii}=0\) on \(A^*\), then
\[
 \int_A|W_g|^2\,dV_g\le C\,(\varepsilon\,\mathfrak B+\varepsilon^2),\qquad
 \|D^2v\|_{L^2(A)}\le C\,(\varepsilon\,\mathfrak B+\varepsilon^2)^{1/2}, \tag{2}
\]
with no hypothesis on \(K\).

**Proposition 3 (comparison with a second metric).** Let \(g\) and \(\bar g\) be smooth metrics on \(A^*\) with \(|g-\delta|\le\tfrac14\) and \(|\bar g-\delta|\le\tfrac14\). Put \(k=g-\bar g\), \(\varepsilon=\|k\|_{L^\infty(A^*)}\) and \(M=\|D^2g\|_{L^2}+\|D^2\bar g\|_{L^2}+\|Dg\|_{L^4}+\|D\bar g\|_{L^4}\) on \(A^*\). Then, for the cutoff \(\eta\) of the proof,
\[
 \int\eta^4|W_g|^2\,dV_g\ \le\ \int\eta^4|W_{\bar g}|^2\,dV_{\bar g}
 +4\,\bigl\||k|_g\bigr\|_{L^\infty}\,\mathfrak B(g)+C\,(1+M)^4\,\varepsilon^{1/2}. \tag{3}
\]

**Sharpness.** The product \(\varepsilon\mathfrak B\) is attained up to universal factors by a high-frequency plane wave on a fixed annulus and by cones of every length. The term \(\varepsilon^2\) in (2) is attained by a metric with constant Weyl tensor to first order. Details are in the section on examples.

**Not proved here.** The chart is a hypothesis. The last section records what this leaves open.

## Conventions and three facts

*Cutoff.* Fix smooth \(\zeta_1,\zeta_2:[0,\infty)\to[0,1]\) with \(\zeta_1=0\) on \([0,\tfrac58]\), \(\zeta_1=1\) on \([\tfrac78,\infty)\), \(\zeta_2=1\) on \([0,\tfrac98]\), \(\zeta_2=0\) on \([\tfrac{15}8,\infty)\), and put
\[
 \eta(x)=\zeta_1(|x|/r)\,\zeta_2(|x|/R).
\]
Then \(\eta=1\) on \(\{\tfrac78r\le|x|\le\tfrac98R\}\supset A\), and \(\operatorname{supp}\eta\subset\{\tfrac58r\le|x|\le\tfrac{15}8R\}\subset A^*\). Since \(R\ge2r\), the inner transition shell \(\{\tfrac58r\le|x|\le\tfrac78r\}\) lies where \(\zeta_2(|x|/R)=1\) and the outer one \(\{\tfrac98R\le|x|\le\tfrac{15}8R\}\) lies where \(\zeta_1(|x|/r)=1\). On each shell \(\eta\) is one fixed profile at one scale, and \(\int|D\eta|^4dx\) and \(\int|D^2\eta|^2dx\) are unchanged by dilation. Hence
\[
 c_\eta:=\|D\eta\|_{L^4}+\|D^2\eta\|_{L^2} \tag{4}
\]
is a universal number. This is the only place where the length could have entered. A cutoff that changed slowly along the whole annulus would not be needed and is not used.

*Equivalent norms.* If \(|v|\le\tfrac14\) then \(\tfrac34\delta\le g\le\tfrac54\delta\). The \(g\)-norms of tensors and the Euclidean norms of their components are comparable with universal factors, and so are \(dV_g\) and \(dx\).

*Fact A, structure.* In the chart,
\[
 W_g=\mathfrak A(g)[D^2g]+\mathfrak Q(g)[Dg,Dg], \tag{5}
\]
where \(\mathfrak A(g)\) is linear in its argument, \(\mathfrak Q(g)\) is bilinear and symmetric, and both have coefficients that are rational functions of the components of \(g\), smooth on \(\{|g-\delta|\le\tfrac14\}\). Indeed \(R_{ijkl}=\tfrac12(\partial_j\partial_kg_{il}+\partial_i\partial_lg_{jk}-\partial_i\partial_kg_{jl}-\partial_j\partial_lg_{ik})+g_{pq}(\Gamma^p_{jk}\Gamma^q_{il}-\Gamma^p_{jl}\Gamma^q_{ik})\), and the Weyl tensor is the image of \(R_{ijkl}\) under the algebraic projection determined by \(g\). Write \(W'_g[h]\) for the derivative of \(s\mapsto W_{g+sh}\) at \(s=0\), as a tensor with four lower indices, and \(\mathfrak A'(g;h)\), \(\mathfrak Q'(g;h)\) for the derivatives of the coefficients in the direction \(h\). These contain no derivative of \(h\) and are bounded by \(C|h|\). Then
\[
 W'_g[h]=\mathfrak A(g)[D^2h]+\mathfrak A'(g;h)[D^2g]+2\mathfrak Q(g)[Dg,Dh]+\mathfrak Q'(g;h)[Dg,Dg]. \tag{6}
\]
Two consequences are used. First, for \(g=\delta+v\) one has \(Dg=Dv\) and \(D^2g=D^2v\), and subtracting (5) from (6) with \(h=v\) gives
\[
 \bigl|W'_g[v]-W_g\bigr|\le C\bigl(|v|\,|D^2v|+|Dv|^2\bigr). \tag{7}
\]
The top-order terms cancel exactly. This is the algebraic reason the test tensor \(v\) sees the whole Weyl tensor. Second, for a scalar function \(\chi\),
\[
 \bigl|W'_g[\chi h]-\chi W'_g[h]\bigr|\le C\bigl(|D\chi|\,|Dh|+|D^2\chi|\,|h|+|D\chi|\,|h|\,|Dg|\bigr), \tag{8}
\]
because only \(D^2(\chi h)\) and \(D(\chi h)\) produce derivatives of \(\chi\).

*Fact B, first variation.* For every smooth symmetric \(h\) with compact support in \(A^*\),
\[
 \int\Bigl(2\langle W_g,W'_g[h]\rangle_g+\mathcal N_g(h)\Bigr)dV_g=-4\int\langle B_g,h\rangle_g\,dV_g,\qquad |\mathcal N_g(h)|\le C|h|\,|W_g|^2. \tag{9}
\]
The left side is the derivative of \(\int|W_{g+sh}|^2dV_{g+sh}\) over a fixed compact set containing the support. The term \(\mathcal N_g(h)\) comes from the four inverse metrics and the volume form. In dimension four it equals \(-\tfrac12(\operatorname{tr}_gh)|W_g|^2\) by the identity \(W_{ijkl}W_a{}^{jkl}=\tfrac14|W|^2g_{ia}\), which is not needed below. The right side is the first variation of [E24](SEC_E24_20261124.md) and of the manuscript. It holds for compactly supported variations on an open set because the two integrands differ by a divergence. [E61](SEC_E61_20261124.md) writes that divergence explicitly and checks the identity pointwise.

*Fact C, interpolation.* For smooth \(u\) on \(A^*\) with values in a Euclidean space, put \(X=\|\eta^2D^2u\|_{L^2}\) and \(Y=\|\eta\,Du\|_{L^4}\). Then
\[
 Y\le4c_\eta\|u\|_\infty+2\bigl(\|u\|_\infty X\bigr)^{1/2},\qquad
 Y^2\le 32c_\eta^2\|u\|_\infty^2+8\|u\|_\infty X. \tag{10}
\]
Proof. Integrating by parts,
\[
 Y^4=\int\eta^4|Du|^2\,\partial_au\cdot\partial_au\,dx=-\int u\cdot\partial_a\bigl(\eta^4|Du|^2\partial_au\bigr)dx .
\]
The derivative is bounded by \(4\eta^3|D\eta||Du|^3+4\eta^4|Du|^2|D^2u|\), using \(|\Delta u|\le2|D^2u|\) in four dimensions. Hölder's inequality gives \(Y^4\le\|u\|_\infty(4c_\eta Y^3+4Y^2X)\), so \(Y^2\le4c_\eta\|u\|_\infty Y+4\|u\|_\infty X\), and \(Y^2\le aY+b\) implies \(Y\le a+\sqrt b\). \(\square\)

The constant in (10) does not see the length because the integration by parts has no boundary term and the cutoff enters only through (4).

## Proof of Theorem 1

Let \(\varepsilon\le\tfrac14\) and put
\[
 E=\int\eta^4|W_g|_g^2\,dV_g,\qquad X=\|\eta^2D^2v\|_{L^2}\le K,\qquad Y=\|\eta\,Dv\|_{L^4}.
\]
Apply (9) with \(h=\eta^4v\). By (8) with \(\chi=\eta^4\) and \(h=v\), and then (7),
\[
 W'_g[\eta^4v]=\eta^4W_g+\eta^4R_2+R_1,
\]
\[
 |R_2|\le C(|v||D^2v|+|Dv|^2),\qquad
 |R_1|\le C\bigl(\eta^3|D\eta|\,|Dv|+(\eta^3|D^2\eta|+\eta^2|D\eta|^2)|v|\bigr),
\]
where \(D\eta^4=4\eta^3D\eta\), \(D^2\eta^4=4\eta^3D^2\eta+12\eta^2D\eta\otimes D\eta\) and \(|v|\le\tfrac14\) were used. Therefore
\[
 2E=-4\int\eta^4\langle B_g,v\rangle_g\,dV_g-\int\mathcal N_g(\eta^4v)\,dV_g-2\int\langle W_g,\eta^4R_2+R_1\rangle_g\,dV_g. \tag{11}
\]
The four terms are estimated as follows.

1. \(\bigl|4\int\eta^4\langle B_g,v\rangle_g\,dV_g\bigr|\le C\varepsilon\,\mathfrak B\).
2. \(\bigl|\int\mathcal N_g(\eta^4v)\,dV_g\bigr|\le C_1\varepsilon E\).
3. \(\bigl|2\int\eta^4\langle W_g,R_2\rangle_g\,dV_g\bigr|\le CE^{1/2}\bigl(\varepsilon X+Y^2\bigr)\), by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality with the weight \(\eta^4=\eta^2\cdot\eta^2\).
4. \(\bigl|2\int\langle W_g,R_1\rangle_g\,dV_g\bigr|\le CE^{1/2}\bigl(c_\eta Y+\varepsilon(c_\eta+c_\eta^2)\bigr)\), since each term of \(R_1\) carries at least the factor \(\eta^2\).

Hence
\[
 2E\le C_1\varepsilon E+C\varepsilon\mathfrak B+CE^{1/2}\,T,\qquad T=\varepsilon X+Y^2+Y+\varepsilon. \tag{12}
\]
Choose \(\varepsilon_0=\min(\tfrac14,1/C_1)\). For \(\varepsilon\le\varepsilon_0\), (12) and Young's inequality give \(E\le C\varepsilon\mathfrak B+CT^2\). By (10), \(Y^2\le C\varepsilon(X+\varepsilon)\) and \(Y\le C(\varepsilon+(\varepsilon X)^{1/2})\), so \(T\le C(\varepsilon X+\varepsilon+(\varepsilon X)^{1/2})\) and
\[
 T^2\le C\varepsilon\bigl(X+\varepsilon+\varepsilon X^2\bigr)\le C\varepsilon(X+\varepsilon)(1+\varepsilon X).
\]
Since \(\eta=1\) on \(A\) and \(X\le K\), this is (1). The bound for \(\|\eta Dv\|_{L^4}^2\) is the second inequality in (10). \(\square\)

Where the hypotheses of the handoff went. The supremum bound is used in every term. The bound for \(D^2v\) is used only through \(X\). A bound for \(\|Dv\|_{L^4}\) is a conclusion, by (10). The two end shells contribute the terms \(c_\eta Y\) and \(\varepsilon(c_\eta+c_\eta^2)\) of item 4. They are of order \(\varepsilon^{1/2}(K+\varepsilon)^{1/2}\) and \(\varepsilon\), so no vanishing of the shell energies has to be assumed. The total Bach norm enters once, multiplied by \(\varepsilon\).

## Proof of Corollary 2

Two facts about the flat linearization are needed. They are proved by the Fourier transform. Let \(h\) be smooth, symmetric and compactly supported in \(\mathbb R^4\). At each \(\xi\ne0\) write \(\hat\xi=\xi/|\xi|\) and
\[
 \hat h=\hat h^{TT}+\hat\xi\otimes w+w\otimes\hat\xi+\tfrac13\varphi(\delta-\hat\xi\otimes\hat\xi),\qquad
 w=\hat h\hat\xi-\tfrac12(\hat\xi\cdot\hat h\hat\xi)\hat\xi,\quad \varphi=\operatorname{tr}\hat h-\hat\xi\cdot\hat h\hat\xi,
\]
so that \(\hat h^{TT}\hat\xi=0\) and \(\operatorname{tr}\hat h^{TT}=0\). The flat linearization \(W'_\delta\) annihilates Lie derivatives of \(\delta\) and multiples of \(\delta\), so its symbol sees only \(\hat h^{TT}\). For a plane wave \(\sin(\xi\cdot x)H\) with \(H\xi=0\) and \(\operatorname{tr}H=0\), formula (5) gives \(R'_{ijkl}=-\tfrac12\sin(\xi\cdot x)(\xi_j\xi_kH_{il}+\xi_i\xi_lH_{jk}-\xi_i\xi_kH_{jl}-\xi_j\xi_lH_{ik})\), \(\operatorname{Ric}'=\tfrac12|\xi|^2\sin(\xi\cdot x)H\), zero scalar curvature, and \(|W'|^2=|R'|^2-2|\operatorname{Ric}'|^2=\tfrac12|\xi|^4|H|^2\sin^2(\xi\cdot x)\). Plancherel's theorem therefore gives
\[
 \|D^2h\|_{L^2}\le\sqrt2\,\|W'_\delta[h]\|_{L^2}+C\bigl(\|D\operatorname{div}h\|_{L^2}+\|D^2\operatorname{tr}h\|_{L^2}\bigr), \tag{13}
\]
because \(|\xi|^2|\hat h-\hat h^{TT}|\le C(|\xi||\hat h\xi|+|\xi|^2|\operatorname{tr}\hat h|)\).

Apply (13) to \(h=\eta^2v\) with \(\partial_iv_{ij}=0\) and \(v_{ii}=0\). Then \(\operatorname{tr}h=0\) and \(\operatorname{div}h=2\eta\,v(D\eta,\cdot)\), so \(\|D\operatorname{div}h\|_{L^2}\le C(Y+\varepsilon)\). Expanding \(D^2(\eta^2v)\) gives \(X\le\|D^2(\eta^2v)\|_{L^2}+C(Y+\varepsilon)\). By (8) at \(g=\delta\), \(\|W'_\delta[\eta^2v]\|_{L^2}\le\|\eta^2W'_\delta[v]\|_{L^2}+C(Y+\varepsilon)\). By (5), \(W'_\delta[v]=\mathfrak A(\delta)[D^2v]\) and \(|W'_\delta[v]-W_g|\le C(|v||D^2v|+|Dv|^2)\). Together,
\[
 X\le C\bigl(E^{1/2}+\varepsilon X+Y^2+Y+\varepsilon\bigr).
\]
The number \(X\) is finite because \(v\) is smooth on a neighborhood of the support of \(\eta\). With (10) and \((\varepsilon X)^{1/2}\le\theta X+\varepsilon/(4\theta)\), and after decreasing the universal \(\varepsilon_0\),
\[
 X\le C\bigl(E^{1/2}+\varepsilon\bigr). \tag{14}
\]
Insert (14) in \(T\le C(\varepsilon X+\varepsilon+(\varepsilon X)^{1/2})\) and in \(E\le C\varepsilon\mathfrak B+CE^{1/2}T\):
\[
 E\le C\varepsilon\mathfrak B+C\varepsilon E+C\varepsilon E^{1/2}+C\varepsilon^{1/2}E^{3/4}.
\]
Young's inequality with exponents \((2,2)\) and \((\tfrac43,4)\) bounds the last two terms by \(\tfrac14E+C\varepsilon^2\). This gives the first part of (2), and (14) gives the second. \(\square\)

The gauge condition replaces the hypothesis on \(K\) by a conclusion. This is the linear ellipticity of the Weyl operator on divergence-free trace-free tensors, applied with the cutoff.

## Proof of Proposition 3

Put \(g_s=\bar g+sk\) for \(0\le s\le1\). Then \(|g_s-\delta|\le\tfrac14\), and the norms defining \(M\) bound \(\|D^2g_s\|_{L^2}\), \(\|Dg_s\|_{L^4}\), \(\|D^2k\|_{L^2}\) and \(\|Dk\|_{L^4}\). Let \(F(s)=\int\eta^4|W_{g_s}|^2dV_{g_s}\). By (5), \(F(s)^{1/2}\le C(M+M^2)\). Taylor's formula at the endpoint \(s=1\) reads
\[
 F(1)=F(0)+F'(1)-\int_0^1sF''(s)\,ds . \tag{15}
\]
*The first derivative at \(g\).* \(F'(1)=\int\eta^4(2\langle W_g,W'_g[k]\rangle_g+\mathcal N_g(k))dV_g\). Comparing with (9) for \(h=\eta^4k\) and using (8),
\[
 F'(1)=-4\int\eta^4\langle B_g,k\rangle_g\,dV_g-2\int\langle W_g,R_1\rangle_g\,dV_g,\qquad
 |R_1|\le C\bigl(|D\eta^4||Dk|+|D^2\eta^4||k|+|D\eta^4||k||Dg|\bigr).
\]
The first term is at most \(4\||k|_g\|_\infty\mathfrak B(g)\). By (10) applied to \(k\), \(\|\eta Dk\|_{L^4}^2\le C\varepsilon(M+\varepsilon)\), and the second term is at most \(CF(1)^{1/2}(\varepsilon^{1/2}(M+\varepsilon)^{1/2}+\varepsilon+\varepsilon M)\le C(1+M)^3\varepsilon^{1/2}\).

*The second derivative.* Differentiating once more,
\[
 F''(s)=\int\eta^4\Bigl(2|W'_{g_s}[k]|^2+2\langle W_{g_s},W''_{g_s}[k,k]\rangle+\mathcal R_s\Bigr)dV_{g_s},
\]
where \(\mathcal R_s\) collects the derivatives of the inverse metrics and of the volume form and satisfies \(|\mathcal R_s|\le C(|k||W_{g_s}||W'_{g_s}[k]|+|k|^2|W_{g_s}|^2)\). The first term is nonnegative and absorbs the cross term of \(\mathcal R_s\). By (5),
\[
 |W''_{g_s}[k,k]|\le C\bigl(|k||D^2k|+|Dk|^2+|k||Dk||Dg_s|+|k|^2(|D^2g_s|+|Dg_s|^2)\bigr),
\]
so \(\|\eta^2W''_{g_s}[k,k]\|_{L^2}\le C\varepsilon(1+M)^2\), again by (10) for \(k\). Therefore
\[
 F''(s)\ \ge\ -C\varepsilon^2F(s)-CF(s)^{1/2}\varepsilon(1+M)^2\ \ge\ -C\varepsilon(1+M)^4 .
\]
Inserting both parts in (15) gives (3). \(\square\)

Three remarks.

1. With \(\bar g=\delta\) the right side of (3) has no energy term, and (3) is a qualitative form of Theorem 1. The theorem is sharper because it absorbs the energy on the left.
2. The inequality says that a metric with small total Bach norm almost minimizes the Weyl energy among metrics that are uniformly close to it and have bounded second-derivative energy. The reason is the identity \(4\mathfrak B(g)=\sup\{D\mathcal W_g[h]:\ |h|_g\le1\}\) together with the nonnegative term \(2|W'[k]|^2\) of the second derivative. The total Bach norm is the slope of the Weyl functional for the supremum distance between metrics.
3. Let \(g_i\to\bar g\) uniformly on \(A^*\) with \(M\) bounded and \(\varepsilon_i\mathfrak B(g_i)\to0\). Then (10) gives \(Dg_i\to D\bar g\) in \(L^4\) on \(\{\eta=1\}\), the Weyl tensors converge weakly in \(L^2\) there, and (3) with weak lower semicontinuity gives strong \(L^2\) convergence. This is the local form of the mechanism of [E57](SEC_E57_20261124.md). It uses second derivatives in \(L^2\) and a bounded total Bach norm. The limit in E57 is a \(W^{2,q}\) metric, and the extension of (3) to a nonsmooth \(\bar g\) has not been written.

## Examples

*A plane wave.* Fix \(A\) with \(r=1\), \(R=2\). Let \(H\) be symmetric, trace free, with \(He_1=0\) and \(H\ne0\), and let \(v=a\,k^{-2}\sin(kx_1)H\) with \(a\) fixed and \(k\to\infty\). Then \(v\) is divergence free and trace free, \(\varepsilon=a|H|k^{-2}\to0\), and \(K\) stays bounded. By the plane-wave formula in the proof of Corollary 2, \(|W'_\delta[v]|^2=\tfrac12a^2|H|^2\sin^2(kx_1)\), and the nonlinear corrections are \(O(\varepsilon a+a^2k^{-2})\). So
\[
 \int_A|W_g|^2dV_g\to\tfrac14a^2|H|^2\operatorname{vol}(A)>0 .
\]
The linearized Bach tensor on divergence-free trace-free tensors is \(-\tfrac14\Delta^2\) ([E60](SEC_E60_20261124.md)), which gives \(-\tfrac14a\,k^2\sin(kx_1)H\), and the nonlinear corrections are \(O(a^2)\). So \(\mathfrak B=(2\pi)^{-1}a\,k^2|H|\operatorname{vol}(A^*)(1+o(1))\), and \(\varepsilon\mathfrak B\to(2\pi)^{-1}a^2|H|^2\operatorname{vol}(A^*)\). The energy does not tend to zero although \(\varepsilon\to0\) with \(K\) bounded. The Bach term in (1) and (2) cannot be dropped, and \(\int_A|W|^2\) is comparable to \(\varepsilon\mathfrak B\).

*Cones.* Let \(s\) be a smooth symmetric tensor field on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\) with Cartesian components homogeneous of degree zero, and \(g=\delta+c\,s\) with \(c\) small. The dilations satisfy \(D_\lambda^*g=\lambda^2g\), so \(g\) is a cone metric and the densities \(|W_g|^2dV_g\) and \(|B_g|_gdV_g\) are dilation invariant. With \(L=\log(R/r)\),
\[
 \int_A|W_g|^2dV_g=e(c)\,L,\qquad \mathfrak B=b(c)\,(L+\log4),\qquad K^2=c^2k_2\,(L+\log4),\qquad \varepsilon=c\,\|s\|_\infty .
\]
All terms of (1) of highest order in \(L\) are linear in \(L\), and dividing by \(L\) gives \(e(c)\le C\varepsilon\,(b(c)+\varepsilon c^2k_2)\). For small \(c\) the two densities are \(e(c)=c^2e_2+O(c^3)\) and \(b(c)=c\,b_1+O(c^2)\), so the theorem reads \(e_2\le C\|s\|_\infty b_1\) at leading order: a cone whose linearized Bach density vanishes has vanishing linearized Weyl density. The energy is proportional to the length at fixed \(\varepsilon\), and it is comparable to \(\varepsilon\mathfrak B\) for every \(L\) whenever \(e_2>0\). E60 computes \(e_2\) and \(b_1\) for an explicit family. Neither the factor \(\varepsilon\) nor the factor \(\mathfrak B\) in (1) can be replaced by a higher power.

*Constant Weyl tensor.* Let \(\mathsf W\) be a constant tensor with the symmetries of a Weyl tensor and \(q_{ij}=-\tfrac13\mathsf W_{ikjl}x_kx_l\). Then \(q\) is divergence free and trace free, and \(R'_{ijkl}[q]=\mathsf W_{ijkl}\) exactly. Take \(v=\lambda q\) on \(A^*\). Then \(\varepsilon\le C\lambda|\mathsf W|R^2\), the energy of \(A\) is comparable to \(\lambda^2|\mathsf W|^2R^4\), hence to \(\varepsilon^2\) when \(R\ge2r\), and the linearized Bach tensor \(\partial_k\partial_lW'_{ikjl}\) vanishes, so \(\mathfrak B\le C\varepsilon^2\). The term \(\varepsilon^2\) in (2) cannot be dropped. It is an effect of the two ends: this field is the growing quadrupole mode of E60.

## What the estimate does not give

The theorem assumes one chart on the whole annulus with small supremum of \(v\). It does not produce the chart.

Suppose the curvature is small in \(L^2\) on every dyadic annulus \(A_j=\{2^j\le|x|\le2^{j+1}\}\), with scale-invariant sizes \(e_j\). A chart on each \(A_j\) in which the metric is within \(Ce_j\) of a flat one is the standard expectation, and it is not proved here. What a single chart then needs is control of the composition of the transition maps along the neck. The obvious bound for the accumulated deviation is the sum \(\sum_je_j\), and the energy controls only \(\sum_je_j^2\). The norm that would produce the chart is therefore the \(\ell^1\) sum of the dyadic \(L^2\) norms of the curvature, which is the scale of the Lorentz space \(L^{2,1}\), or any scale-invariant norm that controls it. With that norm (1) would read \(\sum_je_j^2\le C(\sum_je_j)\,\mathfrak B\) up to end terms.

The scalar function \(u=c\log|x|\) marks the borderline. It is biharmonic away from the origin, \(|D^2u|^2=4c^2|x|^{-4}\), its Hessian energy on \(A\) is \(8\pi^2c^2L\), and its oscillation is \(cL\). With \(c=L^{-1/2}\) the energy is bounded and the oscillation diverges. It is not a counterexample for metrics. The conformal metric \(e^{2u}\delta=|x|^{2c}\delta\) is a flat cone, equal to \(d\sigma^2+(1+c)^2\sigma^2g_{S^3}\) in the radial variable \(\sigma=|x|^{1+c}/(1+c)\), and in that chart its deviation from a flat metric is of size \(c\) with no factor \(L\). The logarithm was produced by the chart. Whether a deviation that is not produced by the chart can accumulate along an almost Bach-flat neck is the question left for the next decision. E60 answers it for the linearized equations.

The estimate is also silent about necks whose cross-section is a quotient of the three-sphere, and about the relation between the chart and the fixed-margin hypotheses. Both were excluded by the request.
