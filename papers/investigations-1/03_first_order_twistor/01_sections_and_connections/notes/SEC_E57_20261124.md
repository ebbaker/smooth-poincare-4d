## Setting and conclusions

Let \(M\) be a fixed smooth closed connected oriented homotopy
four-sphere. Suppose smooth positive-definite Riemannian metrics
\(\bar g_i\) admit smooth gauges \(g_i=u_i^2\phi_i^*\bar g_i\), with
\(u_i>0\) and \(\phi_i:M\to M\) diffeomorphisms, such that
\[
 c r\le g_i\le C r,\qquad \|g_i\|_{W^{2,q}(r)}\le C_q,\qquad
 \mathfrak B_i=\int_M |B_{g_i}|_{g_i}\,dV_{g_i}\longrightarrow0
 \tag{1}
\]
for a fixed smooth reference \(r\), constants \(0<c\le C\), and
some finite \(q>2\). A bound at \(q=\infty\) permits a finite
choice. The Bach tensor is the covariant two-tensor in E24.
The integral in (1) is unchanged by both gauges, since
\(B_{u^2g}=u^{-2}B_g\), \(|B_{u^2g}|_{u^2g}=u^{-4}|B_g|_g\), and
\(dV_{u^2g}=u^4dV_g\). For the fixed-margin application retain
the independent premises of B29
\[
 Y(M,[\bar g_i])>0,\qquad
 4\pi^2\le E_i=E_+(\bar g_i)=E_-(\bar g_i)\le8\pi^2-\delta .
 \tag{2}
\]
Here \(E_\pm\) use the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norms
of the two Weyl blocks and \(W^+=0\) means ASD. In (2) the
ratio condition of B29 implies (1)'s total Bach condition.
The compact metric conclusion below uses (1) alone.
[E24, first variation and norms][e24] · [E48, \(\mathfrak B\)][e48] ·
[B29][b29] · [B32][b32]

After a subsequence \(g_i\rightharpoonup g\) in \(W^{2,q}\),
\(g_i\to g\) in \(C^0\cap W^{1,4}\), the Weyl tensors converge
strongly in \(L^2\), and \(g\) is a critical \(W^{2,q}\) metric
for the full Weyl functional. Its Yamabe constant is the
limit of those of \(g_i\). The regularity preprint used below
provides a smooth Bach-flat representative on this same
smooth \(M\), preserving this limiting Yamabe constant and
the limiting Weyl energies. If sets on which \(W_{g_i}=0\)
retain volume bounded below in these gauges along the
subsequence, the smooth representative is conformally round.
In (2) such survival contradicts the positive lower Weyl
energy bound. [Avalos–Cogo–Royo Abrego, Theorem 1.1][acr]

## Curvature at the weak metric limit

Use the Levi-Civita derivative \(\nabla^r\), the reference fiber
norms and \(d\mu=dV_r\) for all fixed-space pairings. By reflexivity,
Morrey and Rellich compactness on a finite atlas, pass to a
subsequence with
\[
 g_i\rightharpoonup g\text{ in }W^{2,q},\qquad
 g_i\longrightarrow g\text{ in }C^0\cap W^{1,2q}.
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed \(W^{1,q}\) of first derivatives embeds compactly in
\(L^{2q}\) in dimension four because \(q>2\), including
\(q\ge4\) with any finite target exponent. Thus (3) gives the
stated \(W^{1,4}\) convergence. Uniform ellipticity passes to
the continuous representative of \(g\).

Use the full \((0,4)\) Weyl tensor \(W_g\) in the fixed
covariant tensor bundle. In expressions in a finite atlas write
\(D\) for a fixed coordinate or reference derivative.
The curvature formula for the connection difference
\(\Gamma(g)-\Gamma(r)\) is linear in \(D^2g\) and quadratic in
\(Dg\) with smooth coefficients in \(g\) and \(g^{-1}\),
plus smooth background terms. Weyl projection and lowering
indices use only algebraic metric operations. Thus write
\[
 W_g=A(g) D^2 g+J(g,Dg),
 \tag{4}
\]
where \(J\) is polynomial of degree at most two in \(Dg\)
with smoothly metric-dependent coefficients and background
coefficients in the chosen chart. Analogous expressions
hold for \(R_g\) and \(\operatorname{Rm}_g\).
Uniform ellipticity, (3), and the \(L^{2q}\) bounds on \(Dg_i\)
give \(W_{g_i}\rightharpoonup W_g\) and \(R_{g_i}\rightharpoonup R_g\)
in \(L^q\): the principal coefficient converges uniformly,
the second derivative converges weakly, and the remaining
terms converge strongly. The same calculus gives a \(C^1\)
Weyl-tensor map from the open cone of positive \(W^{2,q}\)
metrics to \(L^q\), since inversion and multiplication of
the zeroth-order coefficients are smooth in \(C^0\) and the
two first-derivative factors lie in \(L^{2q}\).
[Avalos–Cogo–Royo Abrego, Lemma 2.1 and §5.1][acr]

Write \(h_i=g_i-g\), \(W_i=W_{g_i}\), and use \(D_g W(g_i)[h]\)
for the derivative of the Weyl-tensor map in the metric
direction \(h\). In the leading second-jet term the exact
cancellation is
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D_g(A(g_i)D^2g_i)[h_i]
        -\big(A(g_i)D^2g_i-A(g)D^2 g\big)
   &= A'(g_i)[h_i]D^2g_i-(A(g_i)-A(g))D^2g .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]
Both terms tend strongly to zero in \(L^2\), because the
coefficient differences are \(O(\|h_i\|_\infty)\) and the
second derivatives are bounded in \(L^2\). In the lower terms
of (4), \(J(g_i,Dg_i)\to J(g,Dg)\) strongly in \(L^2\).
Its derivative in \((h_i,Dh_i)\) is bounded there by a
constant times
\[
 \|h_i\|_\infty(1+\|Dg_i\|_4^2)
      +\|Dh_i\|_4(1+\|Dg_i\|_4),
\]
which tends to zero. These estimates on finitely many smaller
chart domains establish the requisite relation
\[
 D_g W(g_i)[h_i]-(W_i-W_g)\longrightarrow0
                       \quad\text{in }L^2(d\mu).
 \tag{6}
\]
There is no strong second-derivative premise.

## The varying first-variation test

Let \(\mathcal W(g)=\int_M |W_g|_{g,4\mathrm{ind}}^2\,dV_g\).
Absorb the four inverse metrics and density ratio in a positive
symmetric reference fiber map \(H(g)\) so that
\(\mathcal W(g)=\int_M\langle H(g)W_g,W_g\rangle_r\,d\mu\).
For positive \(W^{2,q}\) metrics it is \(C^1\), and
\[
 D\mathcal W_g[h]=
 2\int_M \langle H(g)W_g,D_g W(g)[h]\rangle_r\,d\mu
 +\int_M \langle H'(g)[h]W_g,W_g\rangle_r\,d\mu .
 \tag{7}
\]
In particular the second integral incorporates the variation
of all norm contractions and the volume. In the smooth
convention of E24 the derivative is
\(-4\int_M\langle B_g,h\rangle_g\,dV_g\).
The difference tensors \(h_i\in W^{2,q}\) are admissible
directions in the open cone, with no cap restriction.
For each fixed \(i\), approximate \(h_i\) by smooth tensors
in \(W^{2,q}\) and thus uniformly. Equation (7) is continuous
in these directions at \(g_i\), and the smooth Bach pairing
converges by uniform convergence. This proves
\[
 |D\mathcal W_{g_i}[h_i]|
   \le C'\|h_i\|_{\infty,r}\mathfrak B_i\longrightarrow0 .
 \tag{8}
\]
The constant compares the norms using (1). No approximate
criticality claim on a restricted set of variations would
suffice for this test. [E24, (1)][e24] ·
[Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1][bgim]

The second integral of (7) with \(h_i\) tends to zero
by the bound \(C'\|h_i\|_\infty\|W_i\|_2^2\).
Then (6)–(8) give
\(\int\langle H(g_i)W_i,W_i-W_g\rangle_r\,d\mu=o(1)\).
Set \(Z_i=W_i-W_g\), so \(Z_i\rightharpoonup0\) in \(L^2\).
Since \(H(g_i)\to H(g)\) uniformly,
\(\int\langle H(g_i)W_g,Z_i\rangle_r\,d\mu\to0\).
It follows that \(\int\langle H(g_i)Z_i,Z_i\rangle_r\,d\mu\to0\).
Uniform positive definiteness of \(H(g_i)\) proves
\[
       W_i\longrightarrow W_g\quad \text{strongly in }L^2(d\mu).
 \tag{9}
\]
In particular \(\mathcal W(g_i)\to\mathcal W(g)\).
The Hodge projections and norms of each block are zeroth-order
smooth tensor operations in \(g\), so each chiral energy also
converges in the fixed orientation. On this \(M\),
\(\mathcal W(g_i)=8E_+(g_i)=8E_-(g_i)\) by signature.
The same identity carries to \(g\) by (9).
[SH-E01, signature and norms][recognition]

For each fixed smooth symmetric tensor \(h\),
\(D_gW(g_i)[h]\rightharpoonup D_gW(g)[h]\) in \(L^2\).
Indeed the only term involving \(D^2g_i\) has coefficient
\(A'(g_i)[h]\), which converges uniformly. The \(D^2h\) term
and the differentiated lower products converge strongly.
In (7) this weak factor pairs against the strongly converging
factor \(H(g_i)W_i\), and the metric/density contribution
converges by (9) and (3). Thus
\[
 D\mathcal W_g[h]=\lim_i D\mathcal W_{g_i}[h]=0 .
 \tag{10}
\]
The last limit is from (1) and the full tensor Bach pairing
on fixed smooth tests. Smooth density and continuity of (7)
give (10) for all \(W^{2,q}\) directions. This is criticality
for the exact Weyl functional of the regularity preprint
regardless of the numerical convention for its Bach gradient.
[Avalos–Cogo–Royo Abrego, Theorem 1.1 and §5.1][acr]

## Yamabe testing in the common gauge

For a positive \(W^{2,q}\) metric \(G\) define its Yamabe
constant by the infimum over nonzero real \(H^1(M,r)\) of
\[
 \frac{Q_G(f)}{\|f\|_{4,G}^2},\qquad
 Q_G(f)=\int_M(6|df|_G^2+R_G f^2)\,dV_G .
 \tag{11}
\]
This agrees with smooth tests by density, because \(R_G\in L^q\)
and \(H^1\hookrightarrow L^4\). The density and ellipticity
allow the same \(H^1\) domain for all the metrics. For smooth
\(G\), (11) is its usual class Yamabe constant. For fixed smooth
\(f\), the kinetic term and denominator converge by (3), and
the scalar term converges by weak \(L^q\) scalar curvature
and uniform convergence of densities. Taking the infimum
of the resulting inequality for all such \(f\) proves
\[
 Y(g)\ge\limsup_i Y(M,[g_i]).
 \tag{12}
\]
The Yamabe constant is unchanged by the gauges of the smooth
metrics. Under (2), E36 and positive sign give the independent
bound \(Y(M,[g_i])\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\), so (12) yields this
positive lower bound at \(g\). This premise, or an independent
positive uniform lower bound, is used for positivity.
[E36, (2)–(3)][e36] · [Lee–Parker, §2, (2.6)–(2.7)][leeparker]

In fact \(Y(M,[g_i])\to Y(g)\). For the reverse inequality,
use a subsequence realizing the liminf and choose real smooth
almost-minimizers \(f_i\) with
\(\|f_i\|_{4,g_i}=1\), \(Q_{g_i}(f_i)\le Y(M,[g_i])+1/i\).
Their energies have a uniform upper bound from the constant
test in (11), and the scalar term has uniformly bounded
absolute value by the \(L^q\) scalar and normalized \(L^4\)
bounds. Equation (1) now gives a uniform \(H^1(r)\) bound.
For \(q'=q/(q-1)\) one has \(2q'<4\), so after another
subsequence \(f_i\to f\) in \(L^{2q'}(r)\). Thus
\(R_{g_i}\,dV_{g_i}/d\mu\rightharpoonup R_g\,dV_g/d\mu\)
in \(L^q\) can be tested against \(f_i^2\to f^2\) in \(L^{q'}\).
The kinetic forms on \(f_i\) differ by \(o(1)\) using the
uniform convergence of metric-density coefficients and the
bounded \(L^2\) gradient. Hence \(Q_{g_i}(f_i)-Q_g(f_i)\to0\).
Uniform convergence of densities gives
\(\|f_i\|_{4,g}^2=1+o(1)\), even without compactness in \(L^4\).
Inserting \(f_i\) in (11) at \(g\) proves the reverse inequality.

## Smooth representative and covariance at the needed regularity

Theorem 1.1 of the 26 October 2026
Avalos–Cogo–Royo Abrego preprint arXiv:2610.35257v1 applies to a
critical \(W^{2,q}\) Riemannian metric, \(q>2\), on a smooth
compact four-manifold. It supplies a positive \(u\in W^{2,q}\)
and a global \(W^{3,q}\) diffeomorphism \(\Phi:M\to M\) such that
\(\gamma=\Phi^*(u^2g)\) is smooth on the original smooth \(M\).
Their §2.3 defines such a diffeomorphism to be a \(C^1\)
diffeomorphism with both maps of the indicated Sobolev class.
On compact \(M\) it is bi-Lipschitz and takes null sets to
null sets in each direction. The continuous positive \(u\)
has a positive minimum. [Avalos–Cogo–Royo Abrego, Theorem 1.1,
§2.3, Lemma 2.4 and §5.1][acr]

Here are the covariance checks used with the theorem.
The map \(h\mapsto\Phi^*(u^2h)\) is a bounded linear isomorphism
in \(W^{2,q}\) by the multiplication property and the pullback
in Lemma 2.4. Smooth approximations of the positive \(g,u\)
converge strongly in \(W^{2,q}\), hence uniformly with
positive lower bounds. Approximate \(\Phi\) strongly in
\(W^{3,q}\) by smooth maps using smoothing in an embedding
of \(M\) and a smooth tubular projection. The approximations
are \(C^1\)-close by Morrey, so they are local diffeomorphisms
of the same degree as \(\Phi\) by the nearby-map homotopy.
Compactness and degree \(\pm1\) make them diffeomorphisms.
Their Jacobians and inverse Jacobians are uniformly bounded.
In finite coordinate charts the pullback on \(W^{2,q}\)
is uniformly bounded for these maps: the chain rule uses
the \(C^0\) bound on first derivatives of the maps, the
\(L^{2q}\) bound on second derivatives, the \(L^q\) bound on
third derivatives, and the Jacobian change of variables.
The pulled-back metric converges strongly in \(W^{2,q}\)
by checking first on smooth tensors then using this bound
and density. For scalar or covariant tensor fields in \(L^q\),
the same argument uses only the Jacobian and first-derivative
bounds. Passing the smooth covariance formulas to the limit
therefore gives
\[
 R_{\Phi^*G}=R_G\circ\Phi,\qquad
 W_{\Phi^*G}=\Phi^*W_G,\qquad W_{u^2 g}=u^2 W_g
              \quad \text{for the }(0,4)\text{ tensor}.
 \tag{13}
\]
The curvature maps used in the passage are strongly continuous
at \(W^{2,q}\) by (4). Hence \(\mathcal W\) is invariant under
the entire change to \(\gamma\). Since this fixed change is a
linear isomorphism of metric directions, (10) gives
\(D\mathcal W_\gamma=0\), so the smooth \(\gamma\) is Bach-flat.
The individual chiral energies are also unchanged, up to
possible exchange by an orientation-reversing \(\Phi\).
On this \(M\) the two equal energies make the exchange immaterial.
[Avalos–Cogo–Royo Abrego, Lemmas 2.1 and 2.4][acr] ·
[E24, gradient and conformal invariance][e24]

For (11), the dimension-four conformal scalar formula for smooth
metrics gives \(Q_{u^2g}(f)=Q_g(uf)\) and
\(\|f\|_{4,u^2g}=\|uf\|_{4,g}\). The same identities hold here
by strong \(W^{2,q}\) approximation for smooth tests and then
\(H^1\) density. Multiplication by \(u\) and by \(u^{-1}\)
is bounded on \(H^1\) since \(\nabla u,\nabla(u^{-1})\in L^4\).
The \(C^1\) diffeomorphism gives an \(H^1\) composition
isomorphism and preserves the quotient under change of
variables, using (13) for the scalar term. Thus \(Y(M,[\gamma])\)
is exactly the constant in (11) at \(g\), in particular positive
under (2). [Lee–Parker, §2, (2.6)–(2.7)][leeparker]

## Positive measure of the Weyl zero set and the cap alternative

Suppose measurable sets \(K_i\) have \(W_{g_i}=0\) almost
everywhere on \(K_i\) and \(\operatorname{Vol}_{g_i}(K_i)\ge v>0\)
on the convergent tail. When the original cap is round,
its preimage by \(\phi_i\) is such a set after the smooth gauge.
The assumed lower bound is in the gauged volume. By (1) there
is \(v_0>0\) with \(\mu(K_i)\ge v_0\).
For any \(\varepsilon>0\), (9) gives
\[
 \mu\big(K_i\cap\{|W_g|_r>\varepsilon\}\big)
 \le \varepsilon^{-2}\|W_i-W_g\|_{2,r}^2\longrightarrow0 .
 \tag{14}
\]
Therefore \(\mu(\{|W_g|_r\le\varepsilon\})\ge v_0\).
Continuity of finite measure on decreasing sets as
\(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) shows that the (almost-everywhere)
Weyl zero set of \(g\) has measure at least \(v_0\).
No common open subset or convergence of the markings is
used. By (13) and bi-Lipschitz change of variables the
zero set of the smooth \(\gamma\) has positive measure as well.

A smooth Bach-flat metric in dimension four is obstruction-flat
in the convention of Lassas–Liimatainen, by the E24 comparison.
Their Theorems 2.2 and 3.7(c) give a local real-analytic
determinant-normalized conformal representative in smooth
conformal harmonic coordinates. The \(C^r\), \(r>3\), and
analytic obstruction hypotheses hold for the smooth
\(\gamma\) with obstruction zero. Choose a connected such
chart meeting the zero set in positive coordinate measure,
which exists by a countable cover and the smooth positive
Jacobians. The Weyl tensor of the analytic representative has
analytic components, and its vanishing set agrees with that
of \(\gamma\). A non-identically-zero real analytic function
on a connected domain has a zero set of measure zero.
For example each zero of a nontrivial analytic function lies
in the regular zero set of one of its derivatives, by taking
a minimal nonvanishing jet. These countably many
hypersurface pieces have measure zero by the implicit
function theorem. Consequently all Weyl components are
identically zero in the chosen chart. Now Lassas–Liimatainen
Theorem 1.4, with its open-set hypothesis thus met and
connectedness of \(M\), propagates full Weyl vanishing globally.
[E24 and E31, obstruction comparison and analytic continuation][e24]
· [E31][e31] · [Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorems 1.4, 2.2, 3.7(c)][ll]
· [Mityagin, Proposition 0][mityagin]

The smooth metric \(\gamma\) is then conformally diffeomorphic
to the round \(S^4\) by SH-E01, and \(\mathcal W(g_i)\to0\)
along this subsequence. This recognizes the original smooth
\(M\) because the preprint's smooth metric is on that manifold.
In the fixed-margin band (2), positive retained volume along
any subsequence contradicts \(E_i\ge4\pi^2\). Thus on any sequence
with the supplied gauges and marked Weyl-flat sets in (2),
their gauged volumes must tend to zero. If the markings
shrink, the compact convergence still produces a smooth
Bach-flat metric on that same \(M\), with
\(Y(M,[\gamma])\ge\sqrt{48\delta}\) and
\(E_\pm(\gamma)=\lim_i E_i\in[4\pi^2,8\pi^2-\delta]\) along a
convergent subsequence. This last conclusion uses (9)
and differs from losing curvature in a pointed rescaling.
The proof supplies no construction of the common gauges
or of surviving marked volume from the bare fixed-margin
energy and Bach assumptions. [SH-E01, exact recognition][recognition]
