## The closed gap in the branch conventions

Let \(H\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere
and \(h\) a smooth positive-definite Riemannian metric on \(H\).
Write \(W^\pm_h\) as endomorphisms of the two Hodge eigenspaces and
\(E_\pm(h)=\int_H\|W^\pm_h\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV_h\), where
\(\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{HS}}\) is the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt
norm. In the orthonormal basis \(e^i\wedge e^j\), \(i<j\), the
entries of \(W\) are \(W_{ijkl}\) with \(i<j\), \(k<l\). Summation
over the two ordered pairs in the full covariant tensor norm counts
each entry four times. Hence
\[
 |W|_{4{\rm ind}}^2
 =4\|W\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
 =4\bigl(\|W^+\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2+\|W^-\|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\bigr).
 \tag{1}
\]
The same factor relates the two squared norms of each block.
The ordinary signature formula in this convention is
\(E_+-E_-=12\pi^2\tau(H)=0\), so
\[
 \int_H |W|_{4{\rm ind}}^2\,dV_h = 8E_+(h).
 \tag{2}
\]
Chang–Chen use the full tensor square in Theorem 2.1, as fixed by
their p. 382 Remark 2 and the \(1/4\) coefficient in (1.4).
Their p. 387–388 switch to the operator square in the proof is
consistent with (1). The explanatory norm line on p. 388 omits
the square on the operator side, which is present in the preceding
display. CGY record the two conventions explicitly in Remark 2
and (4.13). [Chang–Chen, pp. 382–385 and 387–388][cc] ·
[CGY, pp. 107 and 130][cgy] · [SH-E01, norm and signature][sh]

Suppose now that \(h\) is Bach-flat and \(Y(H,[h])>0\), with \(Y\)
the Yamabe constant of the supplied conformal class. Chang–Chen
Theorem 2.1, stated on the standard smooth \(S^4\), gives \(W=0\)
if the full tensor integral is at most \(32\pi^2\). To apply it
below that threshold on \(H\), first use CGY Theorem A. Its strict
bound \(16\pi^2\chi(H)=32\pi^2\) and the positive class Yamabe
constant imply a diffeomorphism to \(S^4\) or \(\mathbb{RP}^4\).
Simple connectivity excludes the latter. Moving \(h\) by the
resulting diffeomorphism then supplies the smooth domain needed
by Chang–Chen. [CGY, Theorem A (0.3), p. 107][cgy] ·
[Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1 (2.1), p. 385][cc]

At the exact threshold on a smooth homotopy sphere, use CGY
Theorem B if \(H\) is outside its two excluded smooth types.
That theorem classifies this equality case and would give either
\(\mathbb{CP}^2\), which has \(b_2=1\), or a manifold isometrically
covered after a conformal change by \(S^3\times S^1\). The latter
cover is impossible for a simply connected base with connected
covering space \(S^3\times S^1\). Thus the equality hypotheses
also force the standard smooth type before applying Chang–Chen.
Its conclusion \(W=0\) at equality contradicts the positive
value \(32\pi^2\). [CGY, Theorem B (0.6), p. 108][cgy] ·
[Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1, p. 385][cc]

### Equality proof and regularity

The proof at equality also works directly with the signature of \(H\).
This checks the endpoint without treating the smooth normal form
asserted in Chang–Chen Proposition 2.5 as an immediate consequence
of CGY Theorem 1.1. That existence theorem assumes a strict
inequality. At equality with positive Weyl integral, pass to
\(\alpha<1\) as in CGY §4. With \(A=\operatorname{Ric}-Rh/6\) in
their convention, the equality says
\(\int\sigma_2(A)=\frac14\int|W|_{4{\rm ind}}^2\), so the hypothesis
with the Weyl term multiplied by \(\alpha\) is strict. Theorem 1.1
gives positive-scalar metrics \(h_\alpha=e^{2u_\alpha}h\)
obeying
\(\sigma_2(A_{h_\alpha})-\alpha|W_{h_\alpha}|_{4{\rm ind}}^2/4
=\lambda_\alpha>0\). In unit volume,
\(\lambda_\alpha=(1-\alpha)\int |W_h|_{4{\rm ind}}^2/4\to0\).
The a priori bounds used in CGY (4.4)–(4.5) apply because the
positive Weyl integral rules out a conformally round class,
independently of the smooth type. They give a \(C^{1,1}\) limit
with smooth local convergence on \(\{W_h\ne0\}\). In (3.25),
the integrand on \(\{W_h=0\}\) is nonnegative by (4.6)–(4.7).
The uniform second-derivative bound and smooth convergence elsewhere
give the integral inequality (4.8) on the nonzero set, hence (4.9).
Proposition 4.1 now gives smoothness, Einstein geometry and the
global vanishing of one Weyl block. The signature identity forces
the other block to vanish as well, contradicting the assumed
threshold. These analytic steps precede any identification of a
smooth sphere. [CGY, Theorem 1.1, Proposition 1.7, (3.25),
(4.1)–(4.9) and Proposition 4.1, pp. 111, 118 and 127–130][cgy] ·
[Chang–Chen, Propositions 2.5–2.6 and final equality step, pp. 386–389][cc]

Thus the closed gap says \(W_h=0\) or \(E_+(h)=E_-(h)>4\pi^2\)
under the Bach-flat and positive Yamabe hypotheses. Separately,
when \(W_h=0\), the conformal developing argument on the simply
connected closed \(H\) identifies its conformal class with the
round sphere. [SH-E01, exact recognition][sh]

## One supplied smooth compactifiable component

Let \((X,g)\) be a smooth connected complete oriented real
Riemannian four-manifold, scalar-flat and Bach-flat, with one
asymptotically Euclidean end and no other end. Here the end has
trivial group and an exterior coordinate chart in which
\(\partial^j(g_{ab}-\delta_{ab})=O(r^{-\eta-j})\), for
\(0\le j\le2\) and some \(\eta>0\). Suppose a smooth positive
Riemannian metric \(\widehat g\) on a smooth homotopy sphere
\(\widehat X=X\cup\{\infty\}\) restricts on \(X\) to \(u^2g\)
for a smooth positive function \(u\) there. Orient the cap
compatibly and write \(\widehat Y=Y(\widehat X,[\widehat g])>0\).
Smoothness extends Bach-flatness over the added point since it
holds in the punctured conformal metric. In four dimensions the
Hodge star on two-forms is unchanged by the rescaling and
\(\|W^\pm\|^2\,dV\) is conformally invariant. The added point
has zero contribution. Hence \(E_\pm(X,g)=E_\pm(\widehat X,\widehat g)\),
and both equal a single number \(e_X\). [Chang–Chen, §2 conformal
invariance, pp. 384–385][cc] · [SH-E01][sh]

Let \(T=\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/4\). Removing a small closed
coordinate ball about the compactification point gives a
compact core of Euler characteristic
\(\chi(\widehat X)-\chi(B^4)+\chi(S^3)=1\), and \(X\) retracts
onto such a core. The Chern–Gauss–Bonnet end contribution
for a single Euclidean link is \(1\) in Euler characteristic
units. Indeed, for a large coordinate sphere of radius \(r\),
the mixed ambient curvature and second fundamental form
boundary terms are \(O(r^{-3-\eta})\), and the cubic term differs
from the Euclidean boundary density by \(O(r^{-3-\eta})\)
in an orthonormal frame. The area densities are relatively
\(1+O(r^{-\eta})\). Therefore the integrated boundary contribution
converges to that of the Euclidean ball, with interior curvature
squares integrable by the same derivative falloff. In the branch
normalization this yields
\[
 8\pi^2(\chi(X)-1)
 =E_+(X,g)+E_-(X,g)-\tfrac12\|T_g\|_{L^2(X,g)}^2
 =0,\qquad
 \|T_g\|_{L^2(X,g)}^2=4e_X .
 \tag{3}
\]
[Tian–Viaclovsky, §7 (7.2)][tv] · [CGY, (0.4)][cgy]

If \(e_X=0\), both Weyl blocks and \(T_g\) vanish by (3) and
smoothness. Scalar flatness then gives \(\mathrm{Rm}_g=0\).
Thus nonflatness of this supplied component implies
\[
 e_X=E_+(X,g)=E_-(X,g)>4\pi^2 .
 \tag{4}
\]
This is the strict threshold supplied by the closed gap.

## Additivity for disjoint truncations

Let the sources be smooth unit-volume Yamabe minimizers \(g_i\)
on a fixed smooth closed oriented homotopy sphere \(M\), with
positive class Yamabe constants \(Y_i\) and
\[
 E_i=E_+(g_i)=E_-(g_i),\qquad
 4\pi^2\le E_i\le 8\pi^2-\delta,\qquad \delta>0.
 \tag{5}
\]
Let \(E_i\to E_*\) along the subsequence under consideration.
Assume smooth oriented convergence on full compact truncations of each
supplied complete limit. This means that for any fixed compact
truncation there are orientation-preserving embeddings into the source
for all sufficiently large indices of the common subsequence, with
smooth convergence of the pulled-back metrics after multiplying
by the inverse squared length scale of that component. The length
scales tend to zero. Scalar flatness is inherited because
\(R_{g_i}=Y_i\) is bounded. Each counted component also meets the
Bach-flatness and smooth compactification hypotheses above.
[B29][b29] · [E36, (2)][e36]

For a finite family of components \(X_a\), assume that at each
fixed common truncation the embeddings have pairwise disjoint
images for all sufficiently large indices. The length scales may
vary between components. The integrals of each Weyl square are
invariant under constant rescaling and converge on each fixed
truncation. Nonnegativity and disjointness give a sum bounded by
\(E_i\) at finite \(i\). First let \(i\to\infty\) at fixed
truncation, then exhaust the components to obtain
\[
 \sum_a E_\pm(X_a,g_a)\le E_* .
 \tag{6}
\]
Two nonflat components in this class would contribute strictly
more than \(8\pi^2\) in each chirality by (4). Thus there is at
most one in any such disjoint family. The count depends on
disjointness at matching truncations, so it does not count
overlapping descriptions or nested rescalings as separate
energy contributions.

## Strict residual for a nonflat component

For one supplied component define \(Y_c(X,g)\) by the
dimension-four Yamabe quotient over nonzero \(C_c^\infty(X)\):
\[
 Q_g(f)=
 \frac{\int_X(6|df|_g^2+R_gf^2)\,dV_g}
      {(\int_X |f|^4\,dV_g)^{1/2}}.
 \tag{7}
\]
Given \(f\), place its compact support in a truncation interior.
Transport the test to \(M\) and extend by zero. Conformal
invariance under the constant rescaling and smooth convergence
on that compact set give \(Q_g(f)\ge\limsup_i Y_i\).
Therefore \(Y_c(X,g)\ge\limsup_iY_i\).

The conformal covariance of (7) identifies \(Q_g(f)\) with
\(Q_{\widehat g}(u^{-1}f)\) on the punctured compactification.
A cutoff \(\eta_\epsilon\) equal to zero near \(\infty\) and one
outside a coordinate ball of radius \(2\epsilon\) has
\(\int |d\eta_\epsilon|^2\,dV_{\widehat g}=O(\epsilon^2)\).
Multiplying a smooth compact test by it gives approximation in
\(H^1\) and \(L^4\), by the derivative bound and the shrinking
volume. Hence the punctured smooth tests and the smooth closed
tests have the same infimum, so \(Y_c(X,g)=\widehat Y\).
In particular \(\widehat Y\ge\limsup_i Y_i>0\) under (5) and
the positive margin in (8) below.
[Lee–Parker, §1 (1.3)–(1.5) and §3 Theorem 3.3][lp]

Write \(Y_0=Y(S^4,[g_{\rm rd}])=8\sqrt6\,\pi\). Equation (0.4)
of CGY applied to the volume-one Yamabe representatives gives
the source identity
\[
 Y_i^2=Y_0^2-48E_i+12\|T_{g_i}\|_{L^2(M,g_i)}^2,
 \qquad \sqrt{48\delta}\le Y_i\le Y_0 .
 \tag{8}
\]
The \(L^2\) integral of the trace-free Ricci norm is invariant
under constant rescaling, so truncation convergence and
nonnegativity give
\(\liminf_i\|T_{g_i}\|_{L^2(M,g_i)}^2\ge\|T_g\|_{L^2(X,g)}^2=4e_X\).
Using (8), (3) and the transferred Yamabe inequality yields
\[
 \widehat Y^2
 \ge \limsup_i Y_i^2
 \ge \liminf_i Y_i^2
 \ge Y_0^2-48(E_*-e_X).
 \tag{9}
\]
[CGY, (0.4)][cgy] · [E36, (2)][e36]

The sharp compact Yamabe inequality has equality only for a
conformal diffeomorphism to the standard round sphere. For a
nonflat component, (4) implies positive compact Weyl energy,
which excludes that equality. Thus
\[
 0<\frac{Y_0^2-\widehat Y^2}{48}
 \ \le\ E_*-e_X\ <\ 4\pi^2-\delta .
 \tag{10}
\]
In particular exhaustion of the Weyl energy by the nonflat
component is impossible. If one assumes exhaustion, (9) gives
\(\widehat Y=Y_0\), whence round conformal compactification and
\(e_X=0\), with full flatness following by (3).
[Schoen, p. 480 (0.2) and p. 493 Theorem 2][schoen] ·
[Lee–Parker, §3 Theorems 3.2–3.3][lp]

The remainder in (10) is the difference of total source and
component energies. The comparison uses additivity for disjoint
cores and lower bounds from convergence. It supplies neither a
no-neck assertion nor a decomposition of the remaining energy.
Smooth convergence, the asymptotics and smooth compactification
are premises of this packet.
