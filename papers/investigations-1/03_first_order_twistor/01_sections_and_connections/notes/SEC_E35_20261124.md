## Result and normalization

On the fixed oriented standard smooth \(S^4\) with the two
[E29][e29] marked Taub–NUT cores \(r\le R\), there are smooth
positive-definite metrics \(g_R^-\), \(R\ge1\), with exact original
cores and a negative Yamabe constant. For constants \(C\ge c>0\)
independent of \(R\), they satisfy

\[
 -C R^{-1/2}\le Y(S^4,[g_R^-])\le -cR^{-1/2},\qquad
 E_+(g_R^-)=E_-(g_R^-)=8\pi^2+O(R^{-1}),\qquad
 \int_{S^4}|B_{g_R^-}|_{g_R^-}\,dV_{g_R^-}=O(R^{-1}).
 \tag{1}
\]

A version with an exact preassigned round ball in a separate common
inner core satisfies the same bounds. The indicated energies use
the [operator Hilbert–Schmidt convention][common]. In the rest of
the proof write \(\widehat g_R^-=R^{-2}g_R^-\) for the scaled
metric and let \(\Delta=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{grad}\),
which has nonpositive spectrum. Use the sectional curvature sign
positive on the unit sphere. The four-dimensional Yamabe quotient is

\[
 Q_g(U)=
 \frac{\int_{S^4}(6|dU|_g^2+s_g U^2)\,dV_g}
      {(\int_{S^4}|U|^4\,dV_g)^{1/2}}
 \quad (U\not\equiv 0).
\]

Its infimum over smooth functions is the Yamabe constant of the
class. Nonnegative tests with zeroes are admissible by this
definition and give the same bound if strictly positive tests
are used, by adding a positive constant and taking a limit.
[Akutagawa–Botvinnik–Kobayashi–Seshadri, Definition 3.1][abks]

## A fixed base metric and separated test

Let \(h_0\) be the standard unit round metric on the oriented
two-sphere, \(s_{h_0}=2\), and take \(u=2+x_3\) with \(x_3\) the
height function. The function \(u\) is positive and nonconstant.
Set

\[
 D=\int_{S^2}(\Delta_0(u^2))^2\,dA_0>0,\qquad
 v=k\Delta_0(u^2),\qquad h=e^{2v}h_0 .
\]

Strict positivity of \(D\) follows because a harmonic function on
the closed connected sphere is constant and \(u^2\) is not.
In two dimensions \(s_h=e^{-2v}(2-2\Delta_0v)\) and the two
dimensional Dirichlet integral is conformally invariant.
Self-adjointness of \(\Delta_0\) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 I_h&=\int_{S^2}(6|du|_h^2+s_hu^2)\,dA_h\\
    &=6\int_{S^2}|du|_0^2\,dA_0+
      2\int_{S^2}u^2\,dA_0-2kD .
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]

Fix \(k>0\) large enough that \(I_h=-a<0\).
All subsequent constants may depend on this fixed \(h,u,a\).
Write \(J=\int_{S^2}u^2\,dA_h>0\).

On a fixed central segment put the actual scaled metric
\(dt^2+\pi^*h+R^{-2}\eta^2\), using E29's Hopf projection,
connection \(\eta\), and circle period \(2\pi\).
Write \(d\eta=\pi^*F\). In the three-dimensional slice,
let \(X_1,X_2\) be horizontal lifts of an oriented orthonormal
pair for \(h\) and \(V\) the unit vertical vector. The fiber
length is constant, so the fibers are totally geodesic.
The horizontal bracket has vertical part
\(-R^{-1}F(X_1,X_2)V\), with \(F(X_1,X_2)\) evaluated on the
base. The horizontal sectional curvature is
\(K_h-\frac{3}{4R^2}F(X_1,X_2)^2\), and each of the two mixed
sectional curvatures is \(\frac{1}{4R^2}F(X_1,X_2)^2\).
These are the horizontal and mixed formulas for a constant
fiber coefficient in the proof of Shankar–Tapp–Tuschmann
Lemma 3.2. The product \(t\)-direction adds zero sectional
curvatures. Twice the sum gives the exact scalar curvature and
volume density

\[
 s_{\widehat g_R^-}=s_h-\frac1{2R^2}|F|_{\Lambda^2h}^2,\qquad
 dV_{\widehat g_R^-}=R^{-1}dt\,|\pi^*dA_h\wedge\eta|.
 \tag{3}
\]

The exterior norm counts each unordered pair once, so in two
dimensions \(|F|_{\Lambda^2h}^2=F(X_1,X_2)^2\).
[Shankar–Tapp–Tuschmann, §3, proof of Lemma 3.2][stt]

Fix nonzero \(\chi\in C_c^\infty((-1,1))\) with \(\chi\ge0\)
and put \(w(t)=\chi(t/L)\) for a fixed \(L\ge1\).
Choose \(L\) large enough that

\[
 6J \int_{\mathbb R}|w'|^2\,dt
 \le \tfrac{a}{2}\int_{\mathbb R}w^2\,dt .
\]

This is possible since the two integrals scale as \(L^{-1}\)
and \(L\), respectively. Reserve the exact central product on
\([-L,L]\times S^3\). The function \(U(t,z)=w(t)u(\pi z)\),
extended by zero, is smooth and supported in the product.
Fiber integration in (3) gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{S^4}(6|dU|^2+s_{\widehat g_R^-}U^2)\,dV_{\widehat g_R^-}
 &=\frac{2\pi}{R}\left[
 6J\int |w'|^2\,dt
 +\left(I_h-\frac{1}{2R^2}\int_{S^2}
          |F|_{\Lambda^2h}^2u^2\,dA_h\right)\int w^2\,dt
 \right]\\
 &\le -\frac{2\pi}{R}\frac{a}{2}\int w^2\,dt .
 \end{aligned}
\]

The denominator of \(Q_{\widehat g_R^-}(U)\) is exactly
\((2\pi R^{-1}\int |w|^4\,dt\int_{S^2}u^4\,dA_h)^{1/2}\).
This proves the asserted strictly negative upper bound in (1).

## Seam matching, uniform geometry and the lower Yamabe bound

Use E29's identity \(S^3\) marking at both ends of the double.
The two Taub–NUT regions are standard coordinate four-balls
with boundary direction \(z/\sqrt{2R}\). Gluing them through
the identity-marked product cylinder gives the standard smooth
\(S^4\), as seen using two ordinary latitude caps and their
product complement. The opposite outward normals at the two
attachments give opposite Taub–NUT orientations relative to the
fixed orientation of the double. For all \(R\) use E29's fixed
smooth identification that is the identity on each inner
\(r\le1/2\) core. Before the final constant rescaling the metric
on both balls is \(R^{-2}g_{\rm TN}\). [E29 core and marking][e29]

Take the neck parameter interval \([-T,T]\) with \(T=L+2\).
Write \(\rho=r/R\), \(V_R(\rho)=1+(2R\rho)^{-1}\), and in
small fixed end collars set
\(\rho_-(t)=1+t+T\), \(\rho_+(t)=1+T-t\).
The exact scaled seam metrics there are

\[
 S_{R,\pm}
 =V_R(\rho_\pm)(dt^2+\rho_\pm^2\pi^*h_0)
             +R^{-2}V_R(\rho_\pm)^{-1}\eta^2 .
\]

Choose fixed smooth cutoffs \(0\le\beta_\pm\le1\) with disjoint
small end supports, each equal to one near its own end, and put
\(\beta_0=1-\beta_--\beta_+\). On the neck set
\(\widehat g_R^-=\beta_-S_{R,-}+\beta_+S_{R,+}
+\beta_0(dt^2+\pi^*h+R^{-2}\eta^2)\).
The seam formulas are used only where their cutoffs are supported.
This is a convex combination of smooth positive forms and has the
exact central product. Near each end it is the Taub–NUT
continuation in the matching \(\rho\) chart, including every
boundary jet.

On a fixed finite cover of the base by relatively compact gauge
charts, write \(\eta=d\phi+\alpha\), unwrap the fiber locally,
and put \(\zeta=\phi/R\). In these lifts \(R^{-1}\eta=d\zeta+
R^{-1}\alpha\). The seam and central metric matrices are uniformly
elliptic, with uniformly bounded coefficients and derivatives
through order four in \((t,x^1,x^2,\zeta)\). This follows from the
fixed base metrics, fixed smooth gauge forms and cutoffs, the
bounded \(\rho_\pm\) ranges near one, and the bounds on \(V_R\)
and \(V_R^{-1}\) with these derivatives. A convex combination
preserves uniform ellipticity and \(C^4\) bounds. Differentiating
the inverse and using the coordinate connection formula gives
uniform intrinsic bounds on \(\mathrm{Rm},\nabla\mathrm{Rm}\)
and \(\nabla^2\mathrm{Rm}\) throughout the scaled neck. The
lift is a local isometry and does not assert noncollapse on \(S^3\).

The original fiber coordinate has period \(2\pi\), so the
period in \(\zeta\) is \(2\pi/R\). The volume determinant on
the lifts is uniformly bounded above and below. Equivalently,
the triangular coframe \(dt,dx^1,dx^2,d\zeta+R^{-1}\alpha\)
gives a volume density uniformly comparable to
\(R^{-1}dt\,|\pi^*dA_0\wedge\eta|\) on the quotient.
Thus \(c_0R^{-1}\le \operatorname{Vol}(N,\widehat g_R^-)\le C_0R^{-1}\).
The cores are scalar flat and the scaled neck has bounded
scalar curvature. If \(s_-=\max\{-s,0\}\), Hölder gives for every
test \(Q_{\widehat g_R^-}(U)\ge-\|s_-\|_{L^2(\widehat g_R^-)}\),
and this is at least \(-C R^{-1/2}\). Constant homothety
preserves the Yamabe constant, proving both bounds in (1)
for \(g_R^-=R^2\widehat g_R^-\).

The Bach tensor vanishes on the exact Ricci-flat cores and
on the scaled neck is bounded by
\(C(|\nabla^2\mathrm{Rm}|+|\mathrm{Rm}|^2)\).
The chiral Weyl densities on the scaled neck are bounded by
curvature. The associated integrals on the neck are \(O(R^{-1})\)
and are invariant under the constant rescaling back to the
original Taub–NUT cores. On the core of the nonzero chirality,
the [E07 and E29][e29] cutoff energy is
\(8\pi^2(1-4(1+2R)^{-3}+3(1+2R)^{-4})\).
Thus \(E_\pm\to 8\pi^2\) with the stated error and are equal
by the closed signature identity. The Bach estimate uses the
same covariant tensor convention and scale as [E30][e30].
[SH-E01][sh] · [BGI+M, §1, (1.1)][bgim]

## Common neck parameter and compatible marking

A finite neck with the E29 marking can be put on the
same fixed cylinder by a smooth increasing reparametrization
that equals the required seam coordinate map near each end.
For E29's original interval \([-1,1]\), the map from
\([-T,T]\) has derivative one near both ends and can be made
uniformly positive on the interior. A profile specified in
unit scaled Taub–NUT radial distance uses instead

\[
 D_R(\rho)=\int_1^\rho\sqrt{V_R(a)}\,da .
\]

Near the left end impose the new coordinate as the left
endpoint value plus \(D_R(\rho_-(t))\), and near the right
end as the right endpoint value minus \(D_R(\rho_+(t))\).
In either case its derivative there is
\(\sqrt{V_R(\rho_\pm(t))}\), positive and uniformly bounded
with derivatives through order four. This restores the same
entire metric germ \(S_{R,\pm}\) in the common parameter.

For a target parameter interval of length \(\ell\) bounded
above and below by positive constants, choose small fixed
cutoffs \(b_-,b_+\) in the end collars, one near the
respective ends, with disjoint supports. Denote the prescribed
end derivatives by \(d_-,d_+\) and set the derivative
of the map to
\(b_-d_-+b_+d_++\gamma(1-b_--b_+)\).
Take \(\gamma=(\ell-\int(b_-d_-+b_+d_+))/
\int(1-b_--b_+)\).
The collar supports can be fixed small enough that the
subtracted integral is less than half the target length for
all \(R\). The derivative and its reciprocal are uniformly
bounded, and the map and inverse have bounded derivatives
through order five. Integrating from the left end gives the
right endpoint value by construction and the prescribed
germs at both ends.

Consequently any pair of endpoints that have these seam germs
and uniform lifted \(C^4\) bounds can be compared in the common
cylinder without changing those bounds. Convex interpolation
there is of the full positive metric forms, including the
complete tangential quadratic form with all base–fiber mixed
coefficients. In the lifted charts the positive lower
matrix bound and \(C^4\) estimates persist along the interpolation.
The seam metric is identical on a fixed collar throughout,
so all boundary jets stay matched. The quotient fiber period
and the upper and lower volume orders persist. This compatibility
argument uses only the stated coordinate and metric hypotheses.

For an exact marked round cap choose an extended coordinate ball
in one common inner core, disjoint from the entire neck. In the
unscaled \(g_R^-\) this is one fixed Taub–NUT germ. Apply E30's
local flat replacement and E25's marking with a sufficiently
small \(\varepsilon_R=\varepsilon_*/R\) there. Up to and including
the replacement the separated test has unchanged support,
numerator and denominator. The unscaled replacement adds at most
\(O(\varepsilon_R^2)\) to the negative scalar \(L^2\) norm,
because the local scalar bound is \(O(1)\) in the fixed germ
and the region has volume \(O(\varepsilon_R^4)\).
The scalar \(L^2\) norm before replacement equals its norm in
the scaled metric by constant homothety. Hölder therefore
retains the same lower order bound after replacement. The
E25 marking acts by diffeomorphism and smooth positive conformal
factor and preserves the signed Yamabe constant exactly. The
Weyl energy cost is \(O(\varepsilon_R^4)\), and the total Bach
cost is \(O(\varepsilon_R^2)\), by [E30][e30]. After marking
the other core and the neck may be kept fixed by
choosing the marking operations supported in the first core's
extended chart. This preserves the topology and (1). The marked
round region itself is then the prescribed metric. [E25][e25]

This packet supplies the negative endpoint and its uniform neck
compatibility. The positive endpoint and any zero of the Yamabe
constant along a path are separate questions.
