## Hypotheses and result

Let \((X,h)\) be a supplied connected, complete, smooth, oriented,
positive-definite four-manifold with \(R_h=0\) and \(B_h=0\).
Assume one asymptotically Euclidean end, namely an end chart with
trivial group and, for some \(\tau>0\),
\[
 \partial^j(h_{ab}-\delta_{ab})=O(r^{-\tau-j}),
 \qquad r=|x|,\qquad 0\le j\le5 .
 \tag{1}
\]
The chart is smooth on the end and all bounds are uniform in angle.
The usual all-derivative differentiable AE assumption implies (1).
When an all-derivative estimate is stated below, the all-derivative
input gives that version. Suppose a full compact truncation of
\(X\) embeds smoothly, with its boundary and orientation, as a
codimension-zero submanifold of a smooth closed oriented homotopy
four-sphere \(M\).

There is a smooth one-point compactification \((\widehat X,\bar h)\)
such that \(\bar h=a^2h\) on \(X\) for a smooth positive \(a\),
scalar-flat near the added point \(p\), and Bach-flat everywhere.
The compactification, equipped with its extending smooth atlas, is a
homotopy four-sphere. Its conformal-class Yamabe constant equals the
compactly supported Yamabe infimum of \((X,h)\) and is positive.
The extending atlas may change the chart at the added point. The
construction of the extending metric uses only the analytic end
hypotheses. The full-core embedding supplies the topology.

For the application to the supplied B29 concentration test, suppose in
addition that \(h\) is the smooth pointed limit of rescalings with
diverging length-upscaling factors of smooth unit-volume Yamabe
metrics \(g_i\) on \(M\). Let \(Y_i=Y(M,[g_i])\to y>0\).
Use the operator Hilbert–Schmidt normalization for the Weyl blocks
with ASD meaning \(W^+=0\), and take a subsequence with
\(E_+(g_i)=E_-(g_i)\to e_{\rm source}\). Set
\(Y_{\rm rd}=8\sqrt6\,\pi\) and \(e_X=E_+(h)=E_-(h)\). Then
\[
 e_{\rm source}-e_X\ \ge\
       \frac{Y_{\rm rd}^{\,2}-y^2}{48}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Equality \(e_X=e_{\rm source}\) forces \(y=Y_{\rm rd}\), conformal
roundness of \(\widehat X\), and Euclidean \(h\). In the B29 band
\(e_{\rm source}\ge4\pi^2\), one has \(e_X<e_{\rm source}\).
[B29][b29] · [E36, (1)–(4)][e36] · [SH-E01, normalization][recognition]

## Construct a scalar-flat conformal gauge at infinity

Use \(\mathcal D_h=\operatorname{div}_h\nabla\) for this step.
The Euclidean identity \(\mathcal D_\delta r^{-p}=p(p-2)r^{-p-2}\)
and (1) give
\(\mathcal D_hr^{-2}=O(r^{-4-\tau})\). For
\(0<\epsilon<\min(1,\tau)\), they give
\[
 \mathcal D_hr^{-2-\epsilon}
     =\epsilon(2+\epsilon)r^{-4-\epsilon}
                 +O(r^{-4-\epsilon-\tau}).
\]
For \(R_0\) sufficiently large, the functions
\(\tfrac12(r^{-2}+r^{-2-\epsilon})\) and
\(2(r^{-2}-r^{-2-\epsilon})\) are respectively positive sub- and
supersolutions on \(r\ge R_0\) that bound \(r^{-2}\) from below
and above. On each bounded annulus \(R_0<r<T\), solve the smooth
Dirichlet problem with the value \(r^{-2}\) on both boundaries.
The maximum principle gives the same two barriers for all \(T\).
Local elliptic estimates and diagonal extraction give a positive
harmonic \(u\) on the end with \(c_1r^{-2}\le u\le c_2r^{-2}\).
There is no interior condition on \(u\) beyond its end domain.

Uniform interior estimates on fixed-size rescaled annuli give
\(\partial^j u=O(r^{-2-j})\) through order five. To see the grades,
in rescaled nondivergence form the leading coefficients are the
rescaled inverse metric, and the first-order coefficients use one
metric derivative. The bounds through order five in (1) give
uniform \(C^{3,\alpha}\) coefficient bounds, \(0<\alpha<1\), and
uniform ellipticity on expanded annuli. Apply interior Schauder
estimates to \(r^2u(r\,\cdot)\) on smaller annuli. The same reasoning
applies at any greater finite grade when the decay derivatives are
available.

Now cut \(u\) off to a smooth function \(U\) on \(\mathbb R^4\)
equal to \(u\) near infinity. The equation and (1) give
\(F=\mathcal D_\delta U=O(r^{-4-\tau})\). This is integrable with
finite absolute \(\epsilon\)-moment. Write
\(V=\Phi*F\), where \(\Phi\) is the Euclidean fundamental solution
of \(\mathcal D_\delta\) in dimension four, a constant times
\(|x|^{-2}\). For \(r=|x|\to\infty\),
\[
 V(x)=\Phi(x)\int_{\mathbb R^4}F+O(r^{-2-\epsilon}).
 \tag{3}
\]
Indeed for \(|z|\le r/2\),
\(\big||x-z|^{-2}-r^{-2}\big|
\le C r^{-2-\epsilon}|z|^\epsilon\). On the tail \(|z|>r/2\)
and \(|x-z|\ge r/4\), the kernel times the \(L^1\) tail of \(F\)
is \(O(r^{-2-\tau})\). On \(|x-z|<r/4\), \(F=O(r^{-4-\tau})\)
and the singular kernel has integral \(O(r^2)\). The term from the
tail in the subtracted mass has the same bound. Thus \(V\to0\),
and \(U-V\) is entire harmonic and tends to zero. It vanishes.
The lower barrier makes the coefficient \(c\) of \(r^{-2}\) in
\(u\) strictly positive.

The error \(u-cr^{-2}\) solves
\(\mathcal D_h(u-cr^{-2})=-c\mathcal D_hr^{-2}\). Its right side
has weighted bounds through the orders required by the same
rescaled estimates, since it depends on the metric and its first
derivatives. By (3) this gives
\(\partial^j(u-cr^{-2})=O(r^{-2-\epsilon-j})\) for \(0\le j\le5\).
Divide by \(c\), and extend the resulting function smoothly and
positively across the compact core by interpolating to a positive
constant. The function \(a\) satisfies
\[
 \mathcal D_h a=0\ \text{near infinity},\qquad
 \partial^j(a-r^{-2})=O(r^{-2-\epsilon-j}),\qquad 0\le j\le5.
 \tag{4}
\]

The conformal scalar formula \(R_{a^2h}=a^{-3}(6\Delta_h+R_h)a\)
uses \(\Delta_h=-\mathcal D_h\). It makes \(a^2h\) scalar-flat
near infinity. Bach-flatness is preserved by the positive conformal
change on all of \(X\). In the inverted coordinate \(z=x/|x|^2\),
with \(s=|z|=r^{-1}\), the derivative of inversion is \(s^{-2}\)
times an orthogonal reflection depending on \(z/s\). The factors
\(a^2\) and \(s^{-4}\) cancel at leading order by (4). Derivatives
of the orthogonal factors cost one power \(s^{-1}\) each. Combining
with (1) and (4) gives, for the inverted metric coefficients,
\[
 |\partial_z^j(\bar h_{ab}-\delta_{ab})|
           =O(s^{\epsilon-j}),\qquad 0\le j\le5 .
 \tag{5}
\]
One can compose inversion with a fixed reflection for an oriented
comparison. The orientation on \(X\) extends across the new point.
[Lee–Parker, (2.6)–(2.7)][lp] ·
[Ache–Viaclovsky, §1.1, obstruction tensor in dimension four][av]

## Exact removable-singularity input

Ache–Viaclovsky Definition 1.6 requires \(g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+o(1)\)
and \(\partial^j g_{ij}=o(s^{-j})\) as \(s\downarrow0\) on the
punctured cone cover. Their Theorem 1.8 requires obstruction-flatness
and constant scalar curvature there. The conclusion is a smooth
extension in extending orbifold coordinates after diffeomorphism.
Remark 7.2 permits derivative order \(2(k+1)+1\) in place of all
orders in that proof. In dimension four obstruction-flatness is
Bach-flatness, \(k=1\), and (5) supplies the required five derivative
bounds. The harmonic gauge above supplies constant scalar curvature.
Thus Theorem 1.8 applies with trivial local group. The new point
has an ordinary smooth ball chart. Both \(R_{\bar h}=0\) near
the point and \(B_{\bar h}=0\) hold across it by smoothness.
[Ache–Viaclovsky, Definition 1.6, Theorem 1.8, §7 and Remark 7.2][av]

For comparison, naive inversion with factor \(r^{-2}\) gives a
continuous metric with positive differentiable decay on the
punctured ball. A harmonic equation for that particular factor and
hence constant scalar curvature in that gauge do not follow from
AE geometry. Ache–Viaclovsky Theorem 1.2 upgrades differentiable
order zero to order two in the four-dimensional scalar-flat
Bach-flat case, with its own derivative hypotheses. That upgrade
alone is an end estimate. Tian–Viaclovsky Theorem 1.1 gives
differentiable order zero under the finite curvature, Sobolev and
Betti hypotheses, and Theorem 1.3 gives positive orders below two
in its indicated half-conformally-flat or harmonic-curvature
classes. Neither is used as a compactification theorem here.
Chen–Weber Proposition 5.2 is a regularity theorem in the
extremal Kähler setting, a structure absent from the present input.
[Ache–Viaclovsky §§1.1–1.2][av] ·
[Tian–Viaclovsky, Theorems 1.1 and 1.3][tv] · [Chen–Weber][cw]

Laurain–Martino Theorem 1.7 and §3.1 Theorem 3.1 use finite
volume, small \(L^2\) total curvature on the punctured ball,
\(L^p\) Bach control, the \(L^4\) Sobolev estimate, and
\(\|df\|_4\le C\|\Delta f\|_2\) for smooth test functions compactly
supported there. The naive inverted metric is uniformly comparable
with \(\delta\). Its curvature is \(O(s^{\tau-2})\) by (1),
which is in \(L^{2+\eta}\) for sufficiently small \(\eta>0\) and
has vanishing \(L^2\) norm on shrinking balls. Bach vanishes there.
The first functional estimate follows by metric comparison.
For the second, the local first-order coefficients are
\(O(s^{\tau-1})\) with \(L^4\) norm \(O(\rho^\tau)\) on radius
\(\rho\). The second-order perturbation of the Euclidean Laplacian
is \(O(\rho^\tau)\) in coefficient \(L^\infty\). In the flat
estimate \(\|D^2f\|_2+\|df\|_4\le C\|\Delta_\delta f\|_2\),
these two errors can be absorbed by taking \(\rho\) small.
The displayed estimates use strong \(L^4\) on the first-order
coefficients and need no endpoint Lorentz assumption. Theorem 1.7
and Corollary 1.8 give a conformal representative and a punctured
diffeomorphism with \(W^{2,2p}\), hence Hölder, regularity at the
point. Smoothness in this packet follows instead from the harmonic
gauge and Ache–Viaclovsky's scalar-curvature elliptic bootstrap.
[Laurain–Martino, Theorem 1.7, Corollary 1.8, §3.1 and §4][lm]

## Topology, capacity and the Green factor

Let \(C\) be the full compact truncation with \(\partial C=S^3\)
and \(D=\overline{M\setminus C^\circ}\) using the supplied embedding.
Both are connected. A component of \(D\) disjoint from its connected
boundary would be open and closed in \(M\). Applying van Kampen with
collars gives \(\pi_1(M)=\pi_1(C)*\pi_1(D)\), so both factors are
trivial. Mayer–Vietoris with integer coefficients gives vanishing
\(H_1,H_2\) for both. Its degree-four connecting map from
\(H_4(M)=\mathbb Z\) to \(H_3(S^3)=\mathbb Z\) sends the oriented
fundamental class to the boundary orientation class, up to sign.
It is an isomorphism and \(H_3(C)=H_3(D)=0\). There is no top
absolute homology for either compact manifold with nonempty boundary.
Thus \(C\) is an acyclic simply connected finite CW type and
contractible by the homology form of Whitehead's theorem.
The end collar gives \(X\simeq C\), so \(\chi(X)=1\).
The one-point addition to the open end fills its \(S^3\) by a
topological ball. Equivalently the compact space is obtained from a
truncation by attaching the cone on its boundary. Van Kampen and
Mayer–Vietoris now give a simply connected integral homology
four-sphere. The smooth compactification is a homotopy sphere by
Hurewicz and Whitehead. This argument uses \(M\) simply connected,
and does not identify the diffeomorphism type of the smooth cap
gluing. [E166, supplied full-core distinction][e166]

For any smooth conformal one-point compactification the four-dimensional
Yamabe quotient \(Q\) obeys \(Q_{\bar h}(\varphi)=Q_h(a\varphi)\) for
\(\varphi\in C_c^\infty(\widehat X\setminus\{p\})\setminus\{0\}\).
The same correspondence preserves the \(L^4\) denominator by
conformal scaling. The point has zero \(H^1\) capacity. In a smooth
chart take \(\eta_\rho=0\) on radius \(\rho\), \(\eta_\rho=1\)
outside \(2\rho\), and \(|d\eta_\rho|\le C/\rho\).
Its Dirichlet integral is \(O(\rho^2)\), so
\(\eta_\rho\varphi\to\varphi\) in \(H^1\) and \(L^4\) for each fixed
smooth global \(\varphi\). The scalar term is continuous for this
approximation. Taking the infimum in both directions gives
\[
 Y(\widehat X,[\bar h])
 = Y_c(X,h)
 :=\inf_{0\ne u\in C_c^\infty(X)}
        \frac{6\int_X |du|_h^2\,dV_h}{(\int_X |u|^4\,dV_h)^{1/2}} .
 \tag{6}
\]
Both chiral Weyl integrals agree across this conformal compactification
because their densities are conformally invariant and the added point
has measure zero. Signature zero on the closed \(\widehat X\) gives
\(E_+(h)=E_-(h)=e_X\). [Lee–Parker, (1.4)–(1.5) and (2.7)][lp]
· [SH-E01, signature and norm conventions][recognition]

For the normalization (4), let \(G=a^{-1}\). It solves
\((6\Delta_{\bar h}+R_{\bar h})G=0\) off \(p\) and is locally
integrable since \(GdV_{\bar h}=a^3dV_h\). On large coordinate
spheres bounding the compact truncation, the outward normals and
measures obey
\(\nu_{\bar h}=a^{-1}\nu_h\), \(dS_{\bar h}=a^3dS_h\), hence
\(\partial_{\nu_{\bar h}}G\,dS_{\bar h}
=-\partial_{\nu_h}a\,dS_h\). The flux tends to
\(2|S^3|=4\pi^2\). The other boundary term in Green's formula has
\(G=O(r^2)\), \(dS_{\bar h}\) of total area \(O(r^{-3})\), and
bounded \(|d\varphi|_{\bar h}\) for a smooth compactification test
\(\varphi\). Its integral tends to zero. Thus in the nonnegative
Laplacian convention \( (6\Delta_{\bar h}+R_{\bar h})G
=24\pi^2\delta_p\).
Testing with a positive first eigenfunction \(f\) of the compact
operator gives \(\lambda_1\int G f\,dV_{\bar h}=24\pi^2 f(p)>0\).
This proves positivity independently of an inherited positive
Yamabe bound. The equivalence of positive first eigenvalue and
positive Yamabe constant follows from the compact \(H^1\) Sobolev
inequality and coercivity of \(Q\)'s numerator, as in E48.
The Green function with the displayed normalization is unique by
invertibility. [Lee–Parker, §6, Green equation][lp] · [E48][e48]

## Strict loss of Weyl energy

On a compact support in the supplied pointed limit, transport a
test function in the opposite direction through the convergence
embeddings. The unit-volume Yamabe inequality, written in the
rescaled metric, has scalar coefficient tending to zero and
Yamabe constant \(Y_i\) unchanged. Passing to the smooth limit
gives \(y\le Y_c(X,h)\). For the trace-free Ricci curvature the
dimension-four integral is invariant under constant rescaling,
and it converges on each fixed compact truncation by smoothness.
Monotone exhaustion and \(R_h=0\) therefore give
\[
 \int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_h|^2\,dV_h
     \le\liminf_i\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}^{\,0}|^2\,dV_{g_i}.
 \tag{7}
\]
The end curvature decays \(O(r^{-2-\tau})\) by (1), so the relevant
integrals are finite. With operator Weyl norm, Chern–Gauss–Bonnet
on the scalar-flat AE space yields
\[
 8\pi^2(\chi(X)-1)
   =2e_X-\tfrac12\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_h|^2\,dV_h.
 \tag{8}
\]
The end contribution is one in the Euler characteristic formula,
as can also be seen by rescaling the boundary annulus to Euclidean
size one and using convergence of the boundary Gauss–Bonnet form.
Here \(\chi(X)=1\), so \(\int|\operatorname{Ric}_h|^2=4e_X\).
[Tian–Viaclovsky, §7, (7.2) at \(R=0\)][tv]

For the closed unit-volume Yamabe metrics \(R_{g_i}=Y_i\), and
Chern–Gauss–Bonnet gives
\[
 16\pi^2=2E_+(g_i)
       -\tfrac12\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}^{\,0}|^2\,dV_{g_i}
       +Y_i^2/24 .
 \tag{9}
\]
Applying (7) to the limit of (9) gives (2), since
\(Y_{\rm rd}^2=384\pi^2\). The sphere upper bound \(y\le Y_{\rm rd}\)
holds for the source Yamabe constants. If \(e_X=e_{\rm source}\),
then \(y=Y_{\rm rd}\) by (2). The inherited bound, (6) and the
same universal sphere upper bound on the compactification give
\(Y(\widehat X,[\bar h])=Y_{\rm rd}\). The strict Yamabe inequality
of Schoen for a smooth closed four-manifold outside the conformally
round case forces its conformal class to be round. The Weyl
energies of \(h\) vanish by conformal invariance. Equation (8)
also forces its Ricci tensor to vanish. The scalar-flat curvature
decomposition then gives zero Riemann tensor, and completeness and
simple connectivity yield Euclidean space. In the B29 band equality
would imply \(e_{\rm source}=0\), contrary to the lower bound.
[E36, (1)–(2)][e36] · [Lee–Parker, §3 and introductory Theorem C][lp]
· [Schoen, Theorem 2, p. 493][schoen]

## Converse and evidence limits

Given any supplied smooth closed connected oriented positive-Yamabe
Bach-flat four-manifold \((N,\gamma)\), choose a point \(p\). Use the conformal
normal representative of \([\gamma]\) there and its positive
conformal-Laplacian Green function with leading \(s^{-2}\) term.
We continue to denote this representative by \(\gamma\).
Conformal covariance changes the corresponding Green metric for
another smooth representative only by an overall positive constant
when the Dirac normalization is fixed.
The Green metric \(G_p^2\gamma\) on \(N\setminus\{p\}\) is
scalar-flat and Bach-flat, is complete towards \(p\), and has
one differentiably AE end of order two. Indeed in the conformal
normal gauge \(\gamma_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+O(s^2)\) and
\(G_p=s^{-2}+A+O(s)\) by the Green expansion. Higher derivative
bounds follow by scaling the equation on annuli for the bounded
error \(G_p-s^{-2}\), since its conformal-Laplacian forcing is
\(O(s^{-2})\) with the corresponding derivative bounds.
Inversion gives (1) with \(\tau=2\). Equation (6) and the Weyl
identity apply in reverse. Full compact truncations naturally
embed in \(N\). If \(N\) is a homotopy sphere with a nonround
class, this gives a nonflat AE metric of the conditional type
examined here. The converse starts with such a class as input.
[Lee–Parker, §6, Lemma 6.4(a), Theorem 6.5 and (2.7)][lp]

The compactification depends on the smooth differentiable end
hypotheses or on a supplied scalar-flat conformal gauge with the
derivative bounds of Ache–Viaclovsky. The five weighted derivatives
in (1) suffice. The packet supplies no extraction of a smooth
pointed limit or of its end and full-core hypotheses from small
total Bach norm alone. The strict energy loss for a nonflat
supplied model leaves possible residual energy in the sequence.
The compactification need not carry the source's round cap.
