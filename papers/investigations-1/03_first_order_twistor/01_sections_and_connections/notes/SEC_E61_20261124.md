## Results

Let \(g\) be a smooth Riemannian metric on a four-manifold. \(W\) is the Weyl tensor with four lower indices, \(|W|^2=W_{ijkl}W^{ijkl}\), and \(B\) is the Bach tensor of E24 and of the manuscript, \(B_{ij}=\nabla^k\nabla^lW_{ikjl}+\tfrac12R^{kl}W_{ikjl}\), so that the gradient of \(\int|W|^2dV\) is \(-4B\).

1. **Divergence term.** For every symmetric \(h\),
\[
 D\bigl(|W|^2dV\bigr)_g[h]=\bigl(-4\langle B,h\rangle+\operatorname{div}\mathfrak b(W;h)\bigr)dV,\qquad
 \mathfrak b(W;h)^m=4W^{imkl}\nabla_kh_{il}-4(\nabla_jW^{ijml})h_{il},
\]
and \(\mathfrak b(W;fg)=0\) for every function \(f\) (Lemma 1).
2. **Conservation law.** For every vector field \(X\) the vector field
\[
 P_X=|W|^2X+8B(X,\cdot)^\sharp-\mathfrak b(W;L_Xg)
\]
is divergence free, and its flux through every compact two-sided hypersurface is zero (Theorems 2 and 3):
\[
 \oint_S|W|^2g(X,\nu)\,dS=-8\oint_SB(X,\nu)\,dS+\oint_Sg\bigl(\mathfrak b(W;L_Xg),\nu\bigr)dS .
\]
No term at a far boundary appears, for any metric and any \(X\).
3. **Conformal Killing fields.** If \(X\) is conformal Killing near \(S\), the last term vanishes. If in addition \(g\) is Bach-flat near \(S\) and \(X\) is transverse to \(S\), then \(W=0\) near \(S\). A Bach-flat metric that is a cone over a closed three-manifold, in the wide sense of admitting a homothetic vector field transverse to a compact hypersurface, is locally conformally flat. A product \(\mathbb R\times N^3\) with \(N\) closed is Bach-flat only if \(N\) has constant sectional curvature (Corollary 4).
4. **Neck estimate.** If a neck carries a function \(\tau\) and a vector field \(X\) with \(X(\tau)=1\), then for a cutoff \(\varphi(\tau)\)
\[
 \int\varphi(\tau)|W|^2dV\le8\Lambda\int_{\operatorname{supp}\varphi(\tau)}|B|\,dV+C\,\|W\|_{L^2(\operatorname{supp}\varphi(\tau))}\,\mathfrak d ,
\]
where \(\Lambda=\sup|X||\nabla\tau|\) and \(\mathfrak d\) measures the trace-free part of \(L_Xg\) and vanishes when \(X\) is conformal Killing (Proposition 5 and Corollary 6). The length of the neck does not enter. The statement is intrinsic, so it applies to necks with any cross-section, including quotients of the three-sphere.

## 1. The divergence term

**Lemma 1.** The formula of item 1 holds for every smooth metric and every smooth symmetric \(h\).

Proof. In normal coordinates at a point, \(R_{ijkl}=\tfrac12(\partial_j\partial_kg_{il}+\partial_i\partial_lg_{jk}-\partial_i\partial_kg_{jl}-\partial_j\partial_lg_{ik})\) up to terms quadratic in the Christoffel symbols. Hence the derivative of the curvature tensor in the direction \(h\) is
\[
 \tfrac12(\nabla_j\nabla_kh_{il}+\nabla_i\nabla_lh_{jk}-\nabla_i\nabla_kh_{jl}-\nabla_j\nabla_lh_{ik})+\mathcal Z_1(h),
\]
with \(\mathcal Z_1\) of order zero in \(h\). The Weyl tensor is the curvature tensor minus \(A\owedge g\). The derivative of the second term is \(A'\owedge g+A\owedge h\). The first part is a sum of products with \(g\), so its contraction with \(W\) vanishes; the second is of order zero in \(h\). By the symmetries of \(W\) the four second-derivative terms give the same contraction, so
\[
 \langle W,W'_g[h]\rangle=2W^{ijkl}\nabla_j\nabla_kh_{il}+\mathcal Z_2(h).
\]
The derivatives of the inverse metrics and of the volume form are of order zero in \(h\). Therefore \(D(|W|^2dV)[h]=(4W^{ijkl}\nabla_j\nabla_kh_{il}+\mathcal Z_3(h))dV\), and
\[
 4W^{ijkl}\nabla_j\nabla_kh_{il}=\nabla_j\bigl(4W^{ijkl}\nabla_kh_{il}\bigr)-\nabla_k\bigl(4(\nabla_jW^{ijkl})h_{il}\bigr)+4(\nabla_k\nabla_jW^{ijkl})h_{il}.
\]
The first two terms are \(\operatorname{div}\mathfrak b(W;h)\). What remains is \(\langle Q,h\rangle\) for a symmetric tensor \(Q\) that depends on \(g\) alone. Integrating over compactly supported \(h\) and comparing with the first variation, \(Q=-4B\). Finally \(\mathfrak b(W;fg)^m=4W^{imk}{}_i\nabla_kf-4f\nabla_jW^{ijm}{}_i=0\) because \(W\) is trace free. \(\square\)

The proof uses the first variation only to identify \(Q\). The identity is checked pointwise at random metrics and directions: the two sides agree to between \(2\times10^{-12}\) and \(5\times10^{-10}\), limited by the difference quotient on the left, whose error decreases like the sixth power of the step (check 3).

## 2. The conservation law

**Theorem 2.** For every smooth metric \(g\) and vector field \(X\), \(\operatorname{div}P_X=0\).

Proof. The density is natural: \(D(|W|^2dV)_g[L_Xg]=L_X(|W|^2dV)=\operatorname{div}(|W|^2X)\,dV\). By Lemma 1 the left side is \((-4\langle B,L_Xg\rangle+\operatorname{div}\mathfrak b(W;L_Xg))dV\). Since \(\langle B,L_Xg\rangle=2B^{ij}\nabla_iX_j=2\operatorname{div}(B(X,\cdot)^\sharp)-2(\operatorname{div}B)(X)\), it remains to recall that the Bach tensor is divergence free. That follows from the same two facts: for compactly supported \(X\), \(0=\int D(|W|^2dV)[L_Xg]=-4\int\langle B,L_Xg\rangle dV=8\int(\operatorname{div}B)(X)\,dV\). \(\square\)

**Theorem 3.** Let \(S\) be a compact hypersurface with a unit normal field \(\nu\), and let \(X\) be a vector field defined near \(S\). Then \(\oint_Sg(P_X,\nu)\,dS=0\).

Proof. \(P_X\) at a point depends on the two-jet of \(X\) at that point. Take a collar \(S\times(-a,a)\) and a function \(\chi\) on it with \(\chi=1\) near \(S\times[0,a)\) and \(\chi=0\) near \(S\times\{-a/2\}\). Then \(P_{\chi X}\) is divergence free on the collar, equals \(P_X\) near \(S\times\{0\}\) and vanishes near \(S\times\{-a/2\}\). The divergence theorem on \(S\times[-a/2,0]\) gives the claim. \(\square\)

This is Noether's second theorem for the group of diffeomorphisms: \(P_X\) is the divergence of an antisymmetric tensor built from \(X\) and the metric. The cutoff argument avoids writing that tensor. The consequence used below is that the identity has no remainder at the other end of a neck, in contrast with a Pohozaev identity for an equation that is invariant only under a finite-dimensional group. The identity is an expression of invariance and holds for every metric; it constrains a metric only once \(X\) is chosen so that the last term is small or zero. Divergence of \(\sqrt g\,P_X\) is verified at random metrics and random vector fields to \(10^{-14}\) relative to the three terms (check 4), and the flux identity is verified by quadrature on the unit three-sphere for a metric that is not a cone and a vector field that is not conformal (check 6).

## 3. Conformal Killing fields, cones and products

**Corollary 4.** Let \(L_Xg=fg\) near a compact two-sided hypersurface \(S\). Then
\[
 \oint_S|W|^2g(X,\nu)\,dS=-8\oint_SB(X,\nu)\,dS .
\]
If moreover \(B=0\) near \(S\) and \(g(X,\nu)>0\) on \(S\), then \(W=0\) on a neighborhood of \(S\).

Proof. \(\mathfrak b(W;fg)=0\) by Lemma 1. For the second statement the left side vanishes with a positive weight, so \(W=0\) on \(S\). The flow of \(X\) moves \(S\) through compact hypersurfaces to which the same argument applies, and these fill a neighborhood. \(\square\)

*Cones.* Call \(g\) a cone in the wide sense if it has a vector field \(X\) with \(L_Xg=2g\) that is transverse to a compact hypersurface \(S\) meeting every orbit. This includes metric cones \(d\rho^2+\rho^2\gamma\) over a closed three-manifold \((N,\gamma)\), with \(X=\rho\partial_\rho\), and metrics on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus0\) whose Cartesian components are homogeneous of degree zero, with \(X=x^k\partial_k\); the latter need not be metric cones. By the corollary a Bach-flat cone in the wide sense has \(W=0\) everywhere. For a cone in the wide sense the Weyl energy per unit of \(\log\rho\) equals \(-8\oint_SB(X,\nu)dS\) on any cross-section, with no assumption of smallness. E59 obtained the conclusion for cones \(\delta+cs\) with \(c\) small and to leading order only.

*Products.* For \(g=dt^2+\gamma\) on \(\mathbb R\times N\) take \(X=\partial_t\), which is Killing, and \(S=\{t=0\}\):
\[
 \int_N|W|^2dV_\gamma=-8\int_NB(\partial_t,\partial_t)\,dV_\gamma .
\]
This can be checked by hand. The components \(W_{tktl}\) form the tensor \(E=-\tfrac12\operatorname{Ric}_0^\gamma\), where \(\operatorname{Ric}_0^\gamma\) is the trace-free Ricci tensor of \(\gamma\); the components with exactly one index \(t\) vanish; and \(|W|^2=8|E|^2=2|\operatorname{Ric}_0^\gamma|^2\). Since \(\partial_t\) is parallel, \(B(\partial_t,\partial_t)=\operatorname{div}_\gamma\operatorname{div}_\gamma E+\tfrac12\langle\operatorname{Ric}^\gamma,E\rangle=-\tfrac1{12}\Delta_\gamma R^\gamma-\tfrac14|\operatorname{Ric}_0^\gamma|^2\), using \(\operatorname{div}_\gamma\operatorname{Ric}_0^\gamma=\tfrac16dR^\gamma\). Integration over \(N\) gives the identity. So a product with closed \(N\) is Bach-flat only if \(\operatorname{Ric}_0^\gamma=0\), which in dimension three means constant sectional curvature. A metric cone is conformal to the product by \(t=\log\rho\), and its Weyl energy per unit of \(\log\rho\) is \(2\int_N|\operatorname{Ric}_0^\gamma|^2dV_\gamma\). On \(\mathbb R^2\times S^2\) the values are \(|W|^2=\tfrac43\) and \(B(\partial_t,\partial_t)=-\tfrac16\) (check 1).

The statement for products is elementary and is probably known. It is recorded as the consistency check requested, and no priority is claimed for it or for the statement about cones.

## 4. A neck with a nearly homothetic field

**Proposition 5.** Let \(X\) be a vector field on an open set \(U\) and \(F\) a smooth function on \(U\) whose differential has compact support in \(U\). Then, with \(k=L_Xg-fg\) for any smooth function \(f\),
\[
 \int_UX(F)\,|W|^2dV=-8\int_UB(\nabla F,X)\,dV+4\int_UW^{ijkl}\bigl[(\nabla_j\nabla_kF)\,k_{il}+\nabla_jF\,\nabla_kk_{il}+\nabla_kF\,\nabla_jk_{il}\bigr]dV .
\]

Proof. Choose a compact domain \(\Omega\subset U\) with smooth boundary whose interior contains the support of \(dF\). Then \(F\) is constant on each component of \(\partial\Omega\), and by Theorems 2 and 3
\[
 \int_\Omega g(\nabla F,P_X)\,dV=\int_\Omega\operatorname{div}(FP_X)\,dV=\sum_{\text{components}}F\oint g(P_X,\nu)\,dS=0 .
\]
Expand \(P_X\) and integrate the second term of \(\mathfrak b\) by parts; there is no boundary term because \(\nabla F\) has compact support:
\[
 \int g(\mathfrak b(W;h),\nabla F)\,dV=4\int W^{ijkl}\bigl[(\nabla_j\nabla_kF)h_{il}+\nabla_jF\,\nabla_kh_{il}+\nabla_kF\,\nabla_jh_{il}\bigr]dV,\qquad h=L_Xg .
\]
Replacing \(h\) by \(h-fg\) changes nothing, since every term then contains a trace of \(W\). \(\square\)

The natural choice is \(f=\tfrac12\operatorname{div}X\), for which \(k\) is the trace-free part of \(L_Xg\), the defect of \(X\) from a conformal Killing field.

**Corollary 6 (neck estimate).** Let \(\tau\) be a smooth function on \(U\) and \(X\) a vector field with \(X(\tau)=1\). Let \(\varphi\ge0\) be a smooth function of one variable such that \(\varphi\circ\tau\) has compact support in \(U\), and write \(U_\varphi\) for that support. Then
\[
 \int\varphi(\tau)|W|^2dV=-8\int\varphi(\tau)B(X,\nabla\tau)\,dV+4\int W^{ijkl}\bigl[(\varphi'\tau_j\tau_k+\varphi\,\nabla_j\nabla_k\tau)k_{il}+\varphi(\tau_j\nabla_kk_{il}+\tau_k\nabla_jk_{il})\bigr]dV,
\]
and consequently
\[
 \int\varphi(\tau)|W|^2dV\le8\Lambda\int_{U_\varphi}|B|\,dV+4\,\|W\|_{L^2(U_\varphi)}\,\mathfrak d,
\]
\[
 \Lambda=\sup_{U_\varphi}|X|\,|\nabla\tau|,\qquad
 \mathfrak d=\bigl\|\bigl(|\varphi'||\nabla\tau|^2+\varphi|\nabla^2\tau|\bigr)|k|\bigr\|_{L^2}+2\bigl\|\varphi|\nabla\tau||\nabla k|\bigr\|_{L^2}.
\]

Proof. Apply Proposition 5 to \(F=\Phi\circ\tau\) with \(\Phi'=\varphi\). Then \(X(F)=\varphi(\tau)\), \(\nabla F=\varphi\nabla\tau\) and \(\nabla_j\nabla_kF=\varphi'\tau_j\tau_k+\varphi\nabla_j\nabla_k\tau\). The estimate is the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality. \(\square\)

*In a chart.* Let \(g=\delta+v\) on \(A^*=\{r/2<|x|<2R\}\) with \(|v|\le\tfrac14\), \(X=x^k\partial_k\), \(\tau=\log|x|\), and \(\varphi\) equal to one on \([\log r,\log R]\), supported in \([\log\tfrac58r,\log\tfrac{15}8R]\), with a fixed profile on each end. Then \(L_Xg=\dot g+2g\) with \(\dot g_{ij}=x^k\partial_kv_{ij}\), so \(k=\dot g\) is admissible, \(\Lambda\le(5/3)^{1/2}\), and
\[
 \int_A|W|^2dV\le8\Lambda\,\mathfrak B+C\,\|W\|_{L^2(A^*)}\Bigl(\|\rho^{-2}\dot g\|_{L^2(A^*)}+\|\rho^{-1}D\dot g\|_{L^2(A^*)}+\|\rho^{-1}|Dv||\dot g|\|_{L^2(A^*)}\Bigr),
\]
with a universal \(C\). The three norms are dilation invariant and vanish exactly when the components of \(g\) are homogeneous of degree zero. No smallness of \(v\) is used beyond \(|v|\le\tfrac14\), and \(\log(R/r)\) does not enter.

## 5. Comparison with E59 and limits

- E59 assumes a chart in which the metric is uniformly close to flat and gives \(\int_A|W|^2\le C\varepsilon\mathfrak B\) plus end terms. Corollary 6 assumes a vector field that is nearly conformal Killing along the neck and gives \(\int_A|W|^2\le8\Lambda\mathfrak B\) plus a term proportional to the defect. It has no factor \(\varepsilon\) on the Bach norm, and it does not need the neck to be close to flat or its cross-section to be a sphere.
- On the cones \(\delta+c\,s_H\) both apply. E59 gives the correct order \(c^2\) for the energy density. Corollary 6 gives a bound of order \(c\), which is not sharp: the identity is \(e=-8\oint B(X,\nu)\), and the part of \(B(X,\nu)\) that is linear in \(c\) has zero mean, \(\oint b_H(\nu,\nu)d\sigma=0\) ([E60](SEC_E60_20261124.md)). The estimate discards that cancellation.
- For a sequence with total Bach norm tending to zero, either hypothesis forces the energy of the neck to zero: E59 if a chart with bounded \(\varepsilon\) and \(K\) exists, Corollary 6 if \(\Lambda\) is bounded and \(\mathfrak d\to0\).
- Neither the chart nor the vector field is produced from the fixed-margin hypotheses. With the Bach tensor controlled only in \(L^1\), there is no pointwise regularity theory that would supply them, and this is the open step.
- Other choices of \(X\) were not explored, apart from a generic quadratic field in the numerical check.

## Dependencies

Lemma 1 uses the first variation of E24 and of the manuscript to identify the gradient. Everything else is proved above, and no external source is used in a proof.

Related work. For a divergence-free symmetric tensor \(T\) and a vector field \(X\), \(\operatorname{div}(T(X,\cdot)^\sharp)=\tfrac12\langle T,L_Xg\rangle\); integrated over a domain with a conformal Killing \(X\), this is the Pohozaev–Schoen identity in the general form of Gover and Ørsted, who derive it for tensors that are gradients of invariant functionals and relate it to Noether's conservation laws (arXiv:1010.4614v2, abstract and introduction read; [source record](../../../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_SEC_ANNULAR_20261124.md), R117). For the Bach tensor that identity says that the flux of \(B(X,\cdot)\) through homologous hypersurfaces is the same when \(X\) is conformal Killing. Theorem 3 adds the value of that flux, \(-\tfrac18\oint|W|^2g(X,\nu)\) up to the explicit boundary form, and holds for every \(X\). Whether this form is in the literature was not checked, and no claim of novelty is made. The same model wrote the request and this note in one session.
