## Fourier normalization and radial potential

Work on Euclidean \(\mathbb R^4\), with forward Fourier phase
\(e^{-ix\cdot\xi}\) and inverse measure \((2\pi)^{-4}d\xi\).
Write \(\Delta=\sum_j\partial_j^2\). Let \(H\) be a homogeneous
harmonic real quartic, scalar or componentwise matrix-valued. Define
\(T\) by the bounded multiplier \(H(\xi)/|\xi|^4\) for \(\xi\ne0\).
This calculation concerns that angular degree alone. [B16 data][b16]

For \(\rho=|x|\), the locally integrable distribution
\(G(x)=-\log\rho/(8\pi^2)\) obeys \(\Delta^2G=\delta_0\).
Indeed \(\Delta\log\rho=2\rho^{-2}\) distributionally. The flux
on the radius-\(\varepsilon\) sphere tends to zero for the first
Laplacian. The flux of \(\nabla\rho^{-2}\) is
\(-2|S^3|=-4\pi^2\), giving \(\Delta\rho^{-2}=-4\pi^2\delta_0\).
Consequently \(\widehat G=|\xi|^{-4}\) off the origin.
With the stated Fourier convention, \(H(\partial)\) has multiplier
\(i^4 H(\xi)=H(\xi)\). Also \(H(\partial)G\) is exactly homogeneous
of degree \(-4\) as a distribution, because the additive constant
from dilating \(\log\rho\) is annihilated by the four derivatives.
Its Fourier transform is homogeneous of degree zero. It agrees off
the origin with the bounded degree-zero multiplier above. A
distribution supported at \(\xi=0\) is a linear combination of
derivatives of \(\delta_0\), of degrees \(-4,-5,\ldots\), so the
agreement is exact distributionally. In particular
\(T\chi=H(\partial)(G*\chi)\) for smooth compactly supported \(\chi\).

Let \(\chi(x)=\chi(|x|)\) be supported in \(\rho\le R\), where
\(R=1/2\) for the stipulated seed. Its two moments in this
normalization are

\[
 M_0=\int_{\mathbb R^4}\chi(x)\,dx
     =2\pi^2\int_0^R t^3\chi(t)\,dt,\qquad
 M_2=\int_{\mathbb R^4}|x|^2\chi(x)\,dx
     =2\pi^2\int_0^R t^5\chi(t)\,dt .
\]

For \(t<\rho\), take the normalized sphere average
\(A(t)=\dashint_{S^3}\log|x-t\omega|\,d\omega\). As a function of
the source displacement, the Laplacian of \(\log|x-\cdot|\) is
\(2|x-\cdot|^{-2}\), harmonic in the origin-centered ball of
radius \(t\). Its normalized sphere average is \(2/\rho^2\).
By the polar-coordinate Laplacian, the average \(A\) solves
\(A''+3A'/t=2/\rho^2\). The initial data
\(A(0)=\log\rho,\ A'(0)=0\) give

\[
 A(t)=\log\rho+\frac{t^2}{4\rho^2},\qquad
 (G*\chi)(x)=-\frac{1}{8\pi^2}
           \left(M_0\log\rho+\frac{M_2}{4\rho^2}\right)
 \quad(\rho>R).
\]

For a smooth radial function \(F(|x|^2)\) away from \(0\),
\(H(\partial)F(|x|^2)=16H(x)F^{(4)}(|x|^2)\). To see this, write
\(H\) as the contraction of a symmetric fourth-order coefficient
tensor with \(x^{\otimes4}\). Its traces vanish by harmonicity.
Each term in the four derivatives of \(F(|x|^2)\) that contracts
two derivative indices therefore drops out. The remaining term
differentiates the radial argument once in each index. Applying the
formula to \(F(v)=\tfrac12\log v\) and \(F(v)=v^{-1}\) gives

\[
 H(\partial)\log\rho=-48\frac{H(x)}{\rho^8},\qquad
 H(\partial)\rho^{-2}=384\frac{H(x)}{\rho^{10}} .
\]

For completeness, the corresponding homogeneous kernel in the
physical variable is the spherical principal value of
\((6/\pi^2)H(x)/|x|^8\). The angular mean of \(H\) vanishes, so this
principal value exists and is homogeneous of degree \(-4\).
Its difference from \(H(\partial)G\) is supported at \(x=0\);
homogeneity would allow only a multiple of \(\delta_0\).
Both distributions vanish on every radial test function, by the
harmonic angular mean and, for \(H(\partial)G\), the same radial
derivative formula applied to the test function. This removes the
possible point mass.

Combining the two radial terms and using homogeneity of \(H\)
gives the exterior field

\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 (T\chi)(x)=\frac{6}{\pi^2}
      \big(M_0\rho^{-4}-2 M_2\rho^{-6}\big)H(x/\rho)
      \quad(\rho\ge R).}
\]

The formula extends to the boundary \(\rho=R>0\) by continuity.
The bounded multiplier times the rapidly decreasing
\(\widehat\chi\) is integrable even after multiplication by any
polynomial in \(\xi\), giving a smooth field \(T\chi\) by Fourier
inversion. This also justifies the exterior limit at \(\rho=R\).

## When the exterior field vanishes

If \(H\not\equiv0\), the exterior field vanishes identically exactly
when \(M_0=M_2=0\), since the two powers of \(\rho\) are independent.
A signed nonzero radial bump can have both moments zero. For
example, take \(\chi=\Delta^2\psi\) with a nonzero smooth compactly
supported radial \(\psi\) in \(\rho<R\). This is nonzero by integration
against \(\psi\), and its two moments vanish by integration by parts.
If \(M_0=0\) and \(M_2\ne0\), the field has only the
\(\rho^{-6}\) tail and a nonzero radial factor at every finite
exterior radius. If \(M_0\ne0\), the field has a
\(\rho^{-4}\) leading term, with a vanishing sphere only if
\(\rho^2=2M_2/M_0\) falls in the exterior. At other radii
the angular zeros are exactly those of \(H|_{S^3}\).
For \(H\equiv0\), the field vanishes for every seed.

For the B16 bump, \(\chi(t)=\exp(-1/(1-4t^2))\) on \(0\le t<1/2\)
and zero outside, \(M_0,M_2>0\). The radial factor is positive
throughout \(\rho\ge1/2\). In fact let \(v=4t^2\) and
\(q(v)=v\exp(-1/(1-v))\). Then \(M_2/M_0\) is one quarter of
the mean of \(v\) for density \(q(v)\,dv\) on \((0,1)\).
For \(v>1/2\), with \(b=v/(1-v)>1\), the logarithm of
\(q(v)/q(1-v)\) is \(\log b-b+1/b\), strictly negative by
its derivative and its value at \(b=1\). Thus the mean is
less than \(1/2\), giving \(2M_2/M_0<1/4\).

## Conditional round norms for an isometric angular matrix

Suppose \(H(\omega)\) acts isometrically on the Euclidean seed
space for every \(\omega\in S^3\). Apply the multiplier to
\(\chi Z\) where \(Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)\) has
Hilbert–Schmidt norm \(\sqrt6\). Let
\(U=\Omega^{-2}(T(\chi Z))\) in the stipulated conformal scaling
with \(\Omega=2/(1+\rho^2)\). The equator is \(\rho=1\), and
the southern pole corresponds to \(\rho\to\infty\). Thus

\[
 |U|_{\rho=1}=\frac{6\sqrt6}{\pi^2}(M_0-2M_2),\qquad
 \lim_{\rho\to\infty}|U|=\frac{3\sqrt6}{2\pi^2}M_0 .
\]

The pole statement is a norm limit. Smooth bundle extension there
would require a geometric identification. In the southern
integral, \(dV_{\rm round}=\Omega^4dx\) cancels the squared norm
weight. Therefore the squared southern \(L^2\) norm is

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{\rm south}|U|^2\,dV_{\rm round}
 &=2\pi^2\cdot6\left(\frac{6}{\pi^2}\right)^2
   \int_1^\infty\big(M_0\rho^{-4}-2M_2\rho^{-6}\big)^2
                  \rho^3\,d\rho\\
 &=\frac{432}{\pi^2}
   \left(\frac{M_0^2}{4}-\frac{2M_0M_2}{3}
              +\frac{M_2^2}{2}\right).
 \end{aligned}
\]

The round words here refer to B16 coordinates and the specified
weight. Application to the opposite-chiral curvature derivative
requires the separate geometric operator identification and proof
of the angular isometry. [B16 data][b16] · [E02 chiral conventions][e02]
