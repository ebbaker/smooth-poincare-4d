## Statement and conventions

Let \(M\) be any smooth closed oriented four-manifold and \(g\) a supplied
smooth real Riemannian metric. Use \(\Lambda_g^+\) and call \(W_g^+=0\)
ASD. Write \(E_+(g)=\int_M |W_g^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV_g\), with the
Hilbert–Schmidt norm of the curvature operator on \(\Lambda_g^+\).
For a smooth positive orthogonal almost complex structure \(J\), let
\(\Pi=(1+iJ)/2\), \(Q=1-\Pi\) and \(F(X,Y)=Q[\Pi X,\Pi Y]\).
Extend tensors complex linearly and use the induced Hermitian norms on
full tensors, summing over ordered input slots. The real unit axis of
\(J\) is \(g(J\cdot,\cdot)/\sqrt2\). For a patch triple define

\[
L_g(J_1,J_2,J_3)=\sum_{i=1}^3
  \left(|\nabla^g F_i|_g^2+|\nabla^g J_i|_g^2|F_i|_g^2\right).
\]

As fixed by [SEC-B07][b07], \(I(g)\) is the infimum of
\(\int_M\sum_a\chi_a L_g(J_{a1},J_{a2},J_{a3})\,dV_g\) over finite
open covers with smooth positive triples having orthonormal axes
and smooth nonnegative subordinate partitions of unity \((\chi_a)\).
With these norms, \(I(g)\le 8E_+(g)\). For the separate infimum over
the same class with \(\sum_i |F_i|_g^4\) as the patch density, the value
is zero for every such \(g\).

## Conformal normalization at a center

Fix \(p\in M\). Write \(P_g=\frac12(\operatorname{Ric}_g-\frac{s_g}{6}g)\)
for the Schouten tensor. In \(g\)-geodesic normal coordinates \(y\)
take \(f(y)=\frac12(P_g)_{ab}(p)y^ay^b\). Thus \(f(p)=df(p)=0\) and
\(\nabla_g^2 f(p)=P_g(p)\). The auxiliary metric \(h=e^{2f}g\) is smooth
and positive definite on the coordinate neighborhood. The Schouten law
\(P_h=P_g-\nabla_g^2 f+df\otimes df-\frac12|df|_g^2g\) gives \(P_h(p)=0\).
Tracing \(P_h\) gives \(s_h/6\) in dimension four, hence
\(\operatorname{Ric}_h(p)=0\). The metrics agree to first order at \(p\).
This uses the second-order normalization in the proof of Fefferman–Graham,
Proposition 8.4, and their Schouten transformation. [Fefferman–Graham][fg]

The Hodge star on two-forms is conformally invariant in dimension four.
As a covariant four-tensor \(W_h=e^{2f}W_g\), so \(W_h^+=e^{-2f}W_g^+\)
as operators on the common bundle \(\Lambda^+\). Their Hilbert–Schmidt
norms agree at \(p\). AHS §1, p. 427, gives the Hodge and curvature-operator
conventions, and Fefferman–Graham gives the four-tensor scaling just
after (6.7). [AHS][ahs] · [Fefferman–Graham][fg]

## The radial chiral connection jet

In \(h\)-geodesic normal coordinates \(x\) at \(p\), radially transport
one oriented \(h\)-orthonormal frame \((\omega_i)\) of \(\Lambda_h^+\)
from \(p\). The Levi–Civita connection preserves this oriented Euclidean
bundle. Smooth dependence on the initial velocity of the radial geodesic
gives a smooth frame including \(p\). Define \(J_i\) by
\(h(J_iX,Y)=\sqrt2\,\omega_i(X,Y)\). In the oriented Euclidean model
\(\mathbb H\) with basis \(1,\mathbf i,\mathbf j,\mathbf k\), left
multiplications by the three imaginary units give positive orthogonal
complex structures. Their Kähler forms divided by \(\sqrt2\) give a
basis of \(\Lambda^+\). Every unit linear combination squares to \(-1\)
and has positive orientation by continuity on the sphere of unit
combinations. This verifies the correspondence for any orthonormal
\((\omega_i)\). The \(g\)-Kähler forms of the \(J_i\) divided by
\(\sqrt2\) are \(e^{-2f}\omega_i\), which are orthonormal for \(g\)
on the whole patch.

Write \(\nabla^h_{\partial_b}\omega_i=\sum_j(A_b)_{ji}\omega_j\) and
\(\Omega=dA+A\wedge A\) for the connection matrix and curvature. Radial
transport gives \(x^bA_b(x)=0\), with summation in \(b\). Its first and
second coordinate derivatives at \(0\) give \(A_b(p)=0\) and
\(\partial_a A_b(p)+\partial_b A_a(p)=0\), respectively. Consequently

\[
  \partial_a A_b(p)=\tfrac12\Omega_{ab}(p).
\]

The \(h\)-parallel identification \(\sqrt2\,\omega_i\leftrightarrow J_i\)
gives \(\nabla^h_{\partial_b}J_i=\sum_j(A_b)_{ji}J_j\). Since \(A(p)=0\),
\(\nabla^hJ_i(p)=0\) and the full covariant second derivative satisfies
\(((\nabla^h)^2 J_i)(e_a,e_b)(p)=\sum_j(\partial_a A_b)_{ji}(p) J_j(p)\),
where \(e_a=\partial_a|_p\). The \(J_j\) have squared Hilbert–Schmidt
norm \(4\) and are mutually orthogonal as endomorphisms. The directions
\(e_a\) are orthonormal at \(p\), hence

\[
  \sum_i |(\nabla^h)^2 J_i|_h^2(p)
  =4\sum_{a,b}|\partial_a A_b(p)|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
  =2\sum_{a<b}|\Omega_{ab}(p)|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2.
\]

To identify \(\Omega\), send a two-form \(\alpha\) to the skew
endomorphism \(K_\alpha\) defined by \(h(K_\alpha X,Y)=\alpha(X,Y)\).
The induced action on \(\Lambda^+\) corresponds under this identification
to \(K_\beta\mapsto[K_\alpha,K_\beta]\). The negative summand acts
trivially because the two summands of \(\mathfrak{so}(4)\) are the
commuting left and right quaternion multiplication algebras, with left
multiplication giving \(\Lambda^+\) under the displayed orientation.
For the normalized left generators \(L_{\mathbf i}/\sqrt2\),
\(L_{\mathbf j}/\sqrt2\), \(L_{\mathbf k}/\sqrt2\), the adjoint action
of each one has two entries of magnitude \(\sqrt2\) in the associated
orthonormal basis of two-forms. These three matrices are mutually
orthogonal. The Hilbert–Schmidt norm squared of the action of \(\alpha\)
is therefore \(4|\pi_+\alpha|_h^2\), where \(\pi_+\) projects to \(\Lambda_h^+\).

For \(a<b\), the two-form corresponding to \(R_h(e_a,e_b)\) is
\(\mathcal R_h(e^a\wedge e^b)\) up to the overall curvature sign.
The curvature operator at this point is \(W_h^+\oplus W_h^-\)
because \(\operatorname{Ric}_h(p)=0\). Hence
\(\pi_+\mathcal R_h=W_h^+\pi_+\) at \(p\). Since the \(e^a\wedge e^b\)
with \(a<b\) form an orthonormal basis,

\[
  \sum_{a<b}|\Omega_{ab}|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2(p)
    =4|W_h^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2(p),
  \qquad
  \sum_i |(\nabla^h)^2 J_i|_h^2(p)=8|W_g^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2(p).
\]

The pointwise decomposition and the two commuting ideals are the
algebra in AHS §1, pp. 427–428, including (1.1). The chiral row also
agrees with the companion manuscript's `rec:chiral-curvature` in its
stated sign and frame normalization. [AHS][ahs] · [CME manuscript][cme]

## Frobenius tensor and transfer to the fixed metric

For any torsion-free connection \(\nabla\), expansion of the bracket and
\(Q\Pi=0\) give the full tensor formula

\[
F(X,Y)=Q(\nabla_{\Pi X}\Pi)Y-Q(\nabla_{\Pi Y}\Pi)X.
\]

For \(\nabla=\nabla^h\) and the constructed sections, \(\nabla^h\Pi(p)=0\)
and hence \(F(p)=0\). Differentiating the formula at \(p\) gives

\[
(\nabla^h_Z F)(X,Y)=Q((\nabla^h)^2\Pi)(Z,\Pi X)Y
                    -Q((\nabla^h)^2\Pi)(Z,\Pi Y)X.
\]

Terms differentiating the explicit projectors multiply \(\nabla^h\Pi(p)=0\).
The \(h\)-Hermitian orthogonal projections \(\Pi,Q\) have operator norm
one. Each term above has full tensor norm at most
\(|(\nabla^h)^2\Pi|_h=\frac12|(\nabla^h)^2J|_h\). Thus
\(|\nabla^h F|_h(p)\le |(\nabla^h)^2 J|_h(p)\).

The projectors depend on \(J\) as a tangent endomorphism, so the two
metrics give exactly the same \(F=Q[\Pi\,\cdot,\Pi\,\cdot]\) on the
patch. The norm of this type-\((1,2)\) tensor scales by \(e^{-f}\) from
\(g\) to \(h\). For a tangent connection difference
\(S_Z=\nabla^h_Z-\nabla^g_Z\), the difference on \(F\) is

\[
((\nabla^h_Z-\nabla^g_Z)F)(X,Y)
 = S_Z(F(X,Y))-F(S_ZX,Y)-F(X,S_ZY).
\]

The conformal connection formula is
\(S_ZY=df(Z)Y+df(Y)Z-g(Z,Y)\operatorname{grad}_g f\), hence \(S(p)=0\).
The difference on \(J\) is \([S_Z,J]\), which also vanishes at \(p\).
The two metrics induce identical tensor norms at \(p\) because \(f(p)=0\).
In particular the transfers are \(\nabla^gF(p)=\nabla^hF(p)\) and
\(\nabla^gJ_i(p)=\nabla^hJ_i(p)=0\). The calculation of \(\nabla F(p)\)
uses the second derivative of \(J\) in the auxiliary metric and the
first derivative of the common tensor \(F\). Combining the estimates yields

\[
  L_g(J_1,J_2,J_3)(p)=\sum_i |\nabla^g F_i|_g^2(p)
  \le 8|W_g^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2(p).
\]

The patch tensor and norm convention come from the preliminary
assessment, §4.2, before (1) and in (4). The opening of the proof of
GHN Theorem 5.10 invokes (74) for the related expression for a component
of \(W^+\) in terms of Frobenius coefficients. The estimate here follows
from the constructed jets. [Preliminary assessment][prelim] ·
[Gover–Hill–Nurowski][ghn]

## Smooth localization and infima

Fix \(\varepsilon>0\) and put \(\eta=\varepsilon/\operatorname{Vol}_g(M)\)
for nonempty \(M\). At each \(p\) the center inequality gives a continuous
density difference \(L_g-8|W_g^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\) which is nonpositive at
the center. Shrink the constructed domain to an open \(U_p\) on which
the difference is less than \(\eta\). On further shrinking one can also
ensure \(\sum_i|F_i|_g^4<\eta\), since all \(F_i(p)=0\). Compactness
provides a finite subcover \((U_a)\). Take a smooth partition of unity
\((\chi_a)\) with \(\operatorname{supp}\chi_a\subset U_a\). The weighted
densities extend smoothly by zero where their patch expressions are
undefined. Pointwise nonnegativity of the weights and \(\sum_a\chi_a=1\)
give

\[
 \int_M \sum_a\chi_a L_g\,dV_g
 \le 8E_+(g)+\varepsilon,
 \qquad
 \int_M \sum_a\chi_a \sum_i |F_{ai}|_g^4\,dV_g\le\varepsilon.
\]

The proof differentiates the section tensors on their own patches before
applying the weights. Taking the infima and then \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\)
proves the two assertions. The radii may depend on \(p,g,\eta\).
The result concerns infima for a supplied smooth metric with freely
refinable covers and independent local triples. Attainment, a lower
bound for \(I(g)\), interpatch compatibility, weak extension and
construction of a recognizing metric on an arbitrary homotopy
four-sphere are outside the tested scope.
