## Quantitative statement

Let \(M\) be an oriented smooth four-manifold with a smooth positive-definite
Riemannian metric \(g\). On \(U\subset M\), let \(J_1,J_2,J_3\) be smooth
\(g\)-orthogonal almost complex structures inducing the given orientation.
Set \(n_i=\omega_{J_i}/\sqrt{2}\in S(\Lambda_g^+)\), where
\(\omega_J(X,Y)=g(JX,Y)\), and suppose for some \(0<\varepsilon\leq 1\)
that \(\sqrt{1-\langle n_i,n_j\rangle_g^2}\geq\varepsilon\) at every
point of \(U\) for \(i\ne j\). Put \(P_i=(I+\mathrm iJ_i)/2\),
\(Q_i=I-P_i\) and \(\mathcal F_i(X,Y)=Q_i[P_iX,P_iY]\). This is the
full projected Frobenius tensor of [PRE §§4.1–4.4][pre].

Use the Hermitian extensions of the metric for tensor norms, with each
unordered exterior pair counted once. In the [shared convention][common],
\(W_g^+\) is a real symmetric trace-free operator on \(\Lambda_g^+\).
There is a constant \(C_\varepsilon\) depending only on \(\varepsilon\)
such that on \(U\)

\[
 |W_g^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2
 \le C_\varepsilon\sum_{i=1}^3
   \bigl(|\nabla\mathcal F_i|^2+|\nabla J_i|^2|\mathcal F_i|^2\bigr).
 \tag{1}
\]

Here \(\nabla\) is the Levi–Civita connection of \(g\). One admissible
choice is \(C_\varepsilon=9/(4m_\varepsilon)\), where \(m_\varepsilon\)
is the positive minimum in (7). The proof uses at most two classical
derivatives of \(g,J_i\), so the pointwise calculation also holds for
\(C^2\) data. The recognition implications below use smooth data.

## Null Koszul and the adapted connection

For one \(J\), write \(g_{\mathbb C}\) for the complex-bilinear metric and
\(h(U,V)=g_{\mathbb C}(U,\overline V)\) for its Hermitian counterpart.
The \(h\)-orthogonal subbundles \(\mathcal D=T^{0,1}_J\) and
\(\overline{\mathcal D}\) are individually totally null for \(g_{\mathbb C}\),
which pairs the two bundles nondegenerately. Bracket terms from
differentiating scalar factors lie in \(\mathcal D\) and are killed by
\(Q\). Hence \(\mathcal F=Q[P\cdot,P\cdot]\) is tensorial, takes values in
\(\overline{\mathcal D}\), and has input projection \(P\wedge P\).
Its vanishing is equivalent to involutivity of \(\mathcal D\). Torsion
freeness of \(\nabla\) gives the first-order expression

\[
 \mathcal F(X,Y)=Q\bigl((\nabla_{PX}P)Y-(\nabla_{PY}P)X\bigr).
\]

For \(X,Y\in\mathcal D\) let \(B(X,Y)=Q\nabla_XY\).
For \(X,Y,Z\in\mathcal D\) the metric-derivative terms in Koszul vanish
because the plane is totally null, leaving

\[
 2g_{\mathbb C}(B(X,Y),Z)
  =g_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(X,Y),Z)
   -g_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(Y,Z),X)
   +g_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(Z,X),Y).
 \tag{2}
\]

Define \(\nabla^0_Xs=P\nabla_X(Ps)+Q\nabla_X(Qs)\). This connection
preserves \(P,Q\) and \(h\), and commutes with conjugation, so it also
preserves \(g_{\mathbb C}\). Direct expansion gives
\(K_X=\nabla_X-\nabla^0_X=(\nabla_XP)(P-Q)\). Differentiating \(P^2=P\)
shows that \(K_X\) interchanges the subbundles. Since \(P-Q=\mathrm iJ\)
is an \(h\)-isometry and \(\nabla_XP=\mathrm i(\nabla_XJ)/2\),
\(|K|=|\nabla J|/2\). For \(X,Y\in\mathcal D\) the adapted torsion is

\[
 T^0(X,Y)=\nabla^0_XY-\nabla^0_YX-[X,Y]
          =P[X,Y]-[X,Y]=-\mathcal F(X,Y).
\]

With \(R(X,Y)=[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}\), expansion gives
\(R=R^0+d^{\nabla^0}K+K\wedge K\), where
\((K\wedge K)(X,Y)=[K_X,K_Y]\) and

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 (d^{\nabla^0}K)(X,Y)
 &=\nabla^{0,\mathrm{End}}_X(K_Y)
   -\nabla^{0,\mathrm{End}}_Y(K_X)-K_{[X,Y]}\\
 &=(\nabla^0_XK)_Y-(\nabla^0_YK)_X+K_{T^0(X,Y)}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3}
\]

Thus the torsion term has a plus sign with \(T^0\) and a minus sign
with \(\mathcal F\). Both \(R^0(X,Y)\) and \([K_X,K_Y]\) preserve
\(\mathcal D\). For \(X,Y,Z,T\in\mathcal D\) their pairings on \(Z,T\)
vanish. Since \(K_YZ=B(Y,Z)\) for \(Y,Z\in\mathcal D\) and
\(\nabla^0\) preserves the input and output bundles, what remains is

\[
 g_{\mathbb C}(R(X,Y)Z,T)=
 g_{\mathbb C}\bigl((\nabla^0_XB)(Y,Z)-(\nabla^0_YB)(X,Z)
                 -K_{\mathcal F(X,Y)}Z,T\bigr).
 \tag{4}
\]

For \(U,V\in\mathcal D\) and any derivative vector \(L\), the tensor
difference is
\[
 (\nabla^0_L\mathcal F)(U,V)
 =(\nabla_L\mathcal F)(U,V)-K_L\mathcal F(U,V)
   +\mathcal F(K_LU,V)+\mathcal F(U,K_LV).
\]
The last two terms vanish by the projected input types, and
\(K_L\mathcal F(U,V)\in\mathcal D\). The adapted derivative therefore
satisfies \((\nabla^0_L\mathcal F)(U,V)=Q(\nabla_L\mathcal F)(U,V)\).
This is an exact projection of the full \(\nabla\mathcal F\) in (1).

Choose \(h\)-orthonormal \(X,Y\in\mathcal D\). Use \(g_{\mathbb C}\)
to lower the indices of the Hermitian-unit bivector \(\xi=X\wedge Y\).
In a positive orthonormal frame with \(e_2=Je_1,\ e_4=Je_3\) take
\(X=(e_1+\mathrm i e_2)/\sqrt2,\ Y=(e_3+\mathrm i e_4)/\sqrt2\).
Then \(\xi=(e^{13}-e^{24}+\mathrm i(e^{14}+e^{23}))/2\) is self-dual
and bilinear-null, where \(e^{ij}=e^i\wedge e^j\). Use the curvature
operator convention
\(\langle\mathcal R(X\wedge Y),U\wedge V\rangle_{\mathbb C}
=g_{\mathbb C}(R(X,Y)V,U)\), which gives positive sectional curvature
on the round sphere. The Schouten part pairs to zero on \((\xi,\xi)\)
because every term contains a bilinear metric pairing of two of
\(X,Y\). Thus the bilinear evaluation
\(q=\langle W_g^+\xi,\xi\rangle_{\mathbb C}\) equals
\(g_{\mathbb C}(R(X,Y)Y,X)\).

Take \(Z=Y,\ T=X\) in (4). The two contractions of (2) needed here
are \(g_{\mathbb C}(B(Y,Y),X)=g_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(X,Y),Y)\)
and \(g_{\mathbb C}(B(X,Y),X)=g_{\mathbb C}(\mathcal F(X,Y),X)\).
The tensor identities can be differentiated using \(\nabla^0\)
because it preserves the metric and the subbundles. Using the
projection identity in the resulting null pairings gives

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 q={}&g_{\mathbb C}\bigl((\nabla_X\mathcal F)(X,Y),Y\bigr)
   -g_{\mathbb C}\bigl((\nabla_Y\mathcal F)(X,Y),X\bigr)\\
    &-g_{\mathbb C}\bigl(K_{\mathcal F(X,Y)}Y,X\bigr).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{5}
\]

For bounds, \(g_{\mathbb C}(A,X)=h(A,\bar X)\) allows Hermitian
Cauchy–Schwarz. The two derivative terms have orthonormal inputs
\(X\otimes(X\wedge Y)\), \(Y\otimes(X\wedge Y)\) and their sum in
absolute value is at most \(\sqrt2|\nabla\mathcal F|\). The last term
is bounded by \(|K||\mathcal F|=\tfrac12|\nabla J||\mathcal F|\).
One more Cauchy–Schwarz inequality gives

\[
 |q|^2\leq\tfrac94\bigl(|\nabla\mathcal F|^2+
                          |\nabla J|^2|\mathcal F|^2\bigr).
 \tag{6}
\]

This is an intrinsic form of the derivative and product terms in
[Gover–Hill–Nurowski][ghn], equation (13), Proposition 5.1 and
Appendix (74). The opening of their proof of Theorem 5.10 observes
that the one null component vanishes when the Frobenius coefficients
vanish, independently of Ricci. Equation (5) derives that component
directly for the real Riemannian metric.

## Quartic reconstruction and orthogonal calibration

The decomposable null line \(\Lambda^2\mathcal D\subset\Lambda^+_{\mathbb C}\)
recovers \(\mathcal D\) and hence \(J\). Replacing \(J\) by \(-J\)
conjugates the line, and reality of \(W=W^+\) conjugates \(q\) when
the representatives are conjugate. An \(h\)-unit phase multiplies
\(q\) by its square and leaves \(|q|\) unchanged.

In a real orthonormal basis \(u_1,u_2,u_3\), the conic of projective
null lines has the parametrization
\(\zeta(s,t)=(s^2-t^2)u_1+\mathrm i(s^2+t^2)u_2+2st\,u_3\).
For conic coordinates \([a:b:c]\), the inverse is
\([s:t]=[a-\mathrm ib:c]\) when \(a-\mathrm ib\ne0\), with the
remaining point obtained at \(s=0\).
Thus \(\langle W\zeta,\zeta\rangle_{\mathbb C}\) is a homogeneous
quartic on \(\mathbb{CP}^1\), as in [GHN, Proposition 5.3,
equations (19)–(20)][ghn]. If the three evaluations \(q_i\) vanish,
so do those of the conjugate lines. All six roots are distinct by
separation and the injectivity of the null-plane correspondence.
The quartic then vanishes identically. Evaluation at
\((u_i+\mathrm i u_j)/\sqrt2\) for all \(i<j\) shows that the real
symmetric \(W\) has equal diagonals and zero off-diagonals.
Trace-freeness gives \(W=0\).

Identify \(\Lambda_g^+\) with one oriented Euclidean three-space at
a point. The squared evaluations are continuous on the sphere of
directions because they can be calculated in local unit frames and
are phase invariant. The admissible triples form a nonempty compact
set for \(0<\varepsilon\le1\). The Hilbert–Schmidt unit sphere of
real symmetric trace-free operators is also compact. Set

\[
 m_\varepsilon=
   \min_{\substack{|W|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}=1,\;
        W\in\operatorname{Sym}_0(\mathbb R^3)\\
        n_1,n_2,n_3\in S^2,\;
        \sqrt{1-\langle n_i,n_j\rangle^2}\ge\varepsilon\ (i\ne j)}}
   \sum_{i=1}^3|\langle W\xi_i,\xi_i\rangle_{\mathbb C}|^2 .
 \tag{7}
\]

The six-root argument gives \(m_\varepsilon>0\). Homogeneity gives
\(|W|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\le m_\varepsilon^{-1}\sum_i|q_i|^2\).
Combining with (6) proves (1).

For an orthogonal triple take \(n_i=u_i\), and use the cyclic
unit null representatives \(\xi_i=(u_j+\mathrm i u_k)/\sqrt2\)
for \((i,j,k)=(1,2,3),(2,3,1),(3,1,2)\). Conjugating a representative
leaves the resulting absolute value unchanged. Write the diagonal of
\(W\) as \((a,b,c)\) with \(a+b+c=0\), and its \((12),(13),(23)\)
entries as \((x,y,z)\). The evaluations are \((b-c)/2+\mathrm iz\),
\((c-a)/2+\mathrm iy\), \((a-b)/2+\mathrm ix\). Hence

\[
 \sum_i|q_i|^2=\tfrac34(a^2+b^2+c^2)+x^2+y^2+z^2,\qquad
 |W|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2=a^2+b^2+c^2+2(x^2+y^2+z^2).
\]

This verifies PRE's lower coefficient \(1/2\), attained by pure
off-diagonal blocks, and upper coefficient \(3/4\), attained by
diagonal blocks. Thus \(m_1=1/2\) and \(C_1=9/2\) is admissible
for (1). The calibration is pointwise algebra.

All objects in (1) and (5) are intrinsic to \(g,J\). A unitary
frame change in \(\mathcal D\) multiplies \(\xi\) by the determinant
of that change, of modulus one. The absolute value in (6) and the
Hermitian tensor norms are frame invariant. Oriented orthonormal
frame changes in \(\Lambda^+\) preserve the angles and the
operator norm in (7).

## Finite-cover integration and recognition

On a smooth closed \(M\), assume the hypotheses of (1) on each
member \(U_a\) of a finite cover, with the common metric \(g\) and
uniform \(\varepsilon\). For a smooth nonnegative partition of unity
\(\rho_a\) subordinate to \(U_a\), multiply (1) by \(\rho_a\), sum
and integrate to obtain

\[
 E_+(g)\le C_\varepsilon\sum_{a,i}
    \int_{U_a}\rho_a
       \bigl(|\nabla\mathcal F_{ai}|^2+
                   |\nabla J_{ai}|^2|\mathcal F_{ai}|^2\bigr)\,dV_g .
 \tag{8}
\]

The weights enter only by multiplication. The local section triples
can be selected independently on overlaps. Hölder bounds each
product integral by
\(\|\nabla J_{ai}\|_{L^4(U_a,\rho_a\,dV_g)}^2
\|\mathcal F_{ai}\|_{L^4(U_a,\rho_a\,dV_g)}^2\).
For a constant rescaling \(g\mapsto\lambda^2g\), the connection and
\(P,Q,\mathcal F\) are unchanged. The norms of \(\nabla J,\mathcal F\)
scale by \(\lambda^{-1}\), the norm of \(\nabla\mathcal F\) by
\(\lambda^{-2}\), and the volume by \(\lambda^4\). The integrals in
(8) and the weighted fourth-power integrals are therefore
invariant under constant scaling.

For PRE §4.3 take
\(g=dx_1^2+dx_2^2+(1+a x_1^2)dx_3^2+dx_4^2\), \(a\ne0\), near the
origin. The first metric derivatives vanish at the origin and the
only curved plane is \((1,3)\), with curvature
\(k=\partial_1\Gamma^1_{33}(0)=-a\). The positive block of the
curvature operator is \(\operatorname{diag}(0,k/2,0)\) in the basis
\((e^{12}+e^{34},e^{13}-e^{24},e^{14}+e^{23})/\sqrt2\).
Subtracting \(sI/12=(k/6)I\) gives
\(\operatorname{diag}(-k/6,k/3,-k/6)\), with squared norm \(k^2/6\).
In the orthonormal frame obtained by rescaling the third coordinate
vector, the connection vanishes at the origin. Choosing three constant
orthogonal positive \(J_i\) matrices in that frame gives
\(\nabla J_i(0)=\mathcal F_i(0)=0\). Hence an undifferentiated pointwise
bound whose right side vanishes when all \(\mathcal F_i\) vanish fails,
even for an orthogonal triple.

On a smooth homotopy four-sphere with a supplied smooth metric, a zero
right side of (8) gives \(W^+=0\) and exact recognition by
[SH-E01][recognition]. A right side strictly below \(4\pi^2\),
together with \(Y(M,[g])>0\) for the same class, meets the strict
approximate criterion. Indeed the signature identity gives
\(E_+=E_-\), and the full four-index Weyl integral is \(8E_+\),
strictly below \(16\pi^2\chi(M)=32\pi^2\). These are the two
hypotheses of [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A, equation (0.3) and
Remarks 1–2, p. 107][cgy]. Construction and defect reduction for a
common metric on every homotopy sphere and Yamabe positivity for the
strict approximate endpoint remain open premises.
