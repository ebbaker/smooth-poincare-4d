## Data and compared linearization

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere.
Let \(r\) be the smooth positive-definite Riemannian reference of E27,
round by an oriented isometry on the coordinate ball \(O\), and let
\(K\Subset O\) be the closed marked subball. Fix \(\nu=dV_r\). Set
\(V=S^2_{0,r}T^*M\), \(E=\Lambda_r^+\), and \(B=S^2_0E\) with its real
curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. Here ASD means \(W^+=0\).
Fix the E27 integer grade \(s\ge5\), with
\(X=H^{s+2}(V)\), \(Y=H^s(B)\). Write \(h^\sharp=r^{-1}h\) and use
\[
 g_{r,h}=r(e^{h^\sharp}\,\cdot,\cdot),\qquad
 F_r(h)=I_{r,h}W^+_{g_{r,h}}I_{r,h}^{-1},\qquad
 I_{r,h}=\Lambda^2(e^{-h^\sharp/2})^*|_{\Lambda^+_{g_{r,h}}}.
\]
All logarithmic variations are in the full trace-free domain. Write
\(W=F_r(0)\), \(L=DF_r(0)\), and let \(L^*\) be the fixed \(L^2(r,\nu)\)
formal adjoint. [E03, chart (1) and normal operator (3)][e03] ·
[E27, fixed data][e27] · [Manuscript, §Prescribing chiral curvature][ms]

Let \(v\) be a smooth \(r\)-self-adjoint trace-free endomorphism supported
away from \(K\). Put
\(r_a=r(e^{av}\,\cdot,\cdot)\), with \(a\in\mathbb R\). It has density
\(\nu\) and equals \(r\) on a neighborhood of \(K\). The positive symmetric
oriented isometry \(S_a=e^{-av/2}\colon (TM,r)\to (TM,r_a)\) induces
\(J_a h=(S_a^{-1})^*h\) on metric tensors and \(I_a=\Lambda^2 S_a^*|_{\Lambda^+_{r_a}}\)
on positive two-forms. Thus \((J_a h)^{\sharp_{r_a}}=S_a h^\sharp S_a^{-1}\).
Define
\[
 \widehat F(a,h)=I_a F_{r_a}(J_a h)I_a^{-1},\qquad
 W_a=\widehat F(a,0),\qquad A_a=D_h\widehat F(a,0).
 \tag{1}
\]
In particular \(A_0=L\). The isometries and fixed density make these
comparisons unitary in \(L^2\). The fixed \(X,Y\) grades also agree
topologically with the transported Sobolev grades for each \(a\).
The metric in (1) is
\[
 g(a,h)=r\bigl(e^{av/2}e^{h^\sharp}e^{av/2}\,\cdot,\cdot\bigr).
 \tag{2}
\]
The positive symmetric matrix in (2) has determinant one.

In a fixed smooth atlas, the coordinate Riemann tensor is linear in
second derivatives of \(g\) with smooth lower-jet coefficients, plus
quadratic terms in the first derivatives. The inverse metric, Weyl
projection, chiral projection and identifications are smooth
pointwise operations on positive metrics and the logarithmic
variables. Since \(s>4/2\), multiplication and composition in these
Sobolev spaces show that \(\widehat F\) is jointly smooth as a map
\((a,h)\mapsto Y\) near \((0,0)\). More explicitly, differentiating in \(h\)
at zero gives a second-order operator
\(\sum_{|\gamma|\le2} C_\gamma(a,x)\partial^\gamma h\) in fixed
trivializations. Each coefficient is smooth in \((a,x)\), including
all \(a\)-derivatives with values in \(C^s\) on compact chart pieces.
By the Leibniz bound, multiplying \(\partial^\gamma h\) by a \(C^s\)
coefficient is bounded into \(H^s\), with norm at most a fixed multiple
of the coefficient's \(C^s\) norm. Applying this bound to each
coefficient difference quotient proves smoothness of
\(a\mapsto A_a\) in \(\mathcal L(X,Y)\), including the operator-norm
Taylor remainders used below. The same two-jet calculus bounds
\(D_h^2\widehat F\) on a common sufficiently small neighborhood of
\((0,0)\). [E03, fixed chart][e03] · [Manuscript, equation (lift:chart)][ms]

## Column inverse and its sharp parameter loss

Write \(P=LL^*\), whose fourth-order principal symbol at \(\xi\ne0\)
is \(|\xi|_r^4 I_B/16\) in the E03 normalization. It is formally
self-adjoint on the closed manifold and its smooth kernel equals
\(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\) by the \(L^2\) pairing. Put \(m=\dim\mathcal C\),
let \(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) be a smooth \(L^2\)-orthonormal real basis, and
write
\[
 b(f)_i=\langle Z_i,f\rangle_{L^2},\qquad
 \Pi f=\sum_{i=1}^m b(f)_i Z_i,\qquad
 U=H^{s+4}(B)\cap\mathcal C^\perp,\qquad Y_0=(I-\Pi)Y .
\]
Elliptic regularity for smooth systems and Sobolev Fredholmness give
the bounded inverse \(G:Y_0\to U\) of \(P|_U\). The cokernel claim
follows by testing any distributional annihilator against smooth
inputs and applying elliptic regularity to \(P^*=P\).
Thus \(L(X)=Y_0\) and \(L^*:U\to X\) is bounded.
[Melrose, MIT 18.155 Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose] ·
[E03, normal inverse][e03]

Assume there are smooth \(k_j\in\ker L\) for \(j=1,\ldots,m\) with
\(\mathsf M_{ij}=\langle Z_i,A'_0 k_j\rangle_{L^2}\) invertible.
Let \(\mathcal J c=\sum_j c_j k_j\). Define \(D_0=A'_0\mathcal J\) and
\[
 D_a=\frac{A_a\mathcal J}{a}
     =\int_0^1 A'_{\theta a}\mathcal J\,d\theta\quad(a\ne0),\qquad
 T_a(u,c)=A_aL^*u+D_a c.
 \tag{3}
\]
The integral gives a smooth extension at zero since \(L\mathcal J=0\).
At zero \(T_0\) is a bounded isomorphism \(U\times\mathbb R^m\to Y\).
For output \(f\), its inverse is
\[
 c=\mathsf M^{-1}b(f),\qquad
 u=G(f-D_0 c).
 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(bD_0=\mathsf M\), so the argument of \(G\) belongs to \(Y_0\).
When \(m=0\) the finite rows and columns are empty and \(T_0=P|_U\).

In the sum norm on \(U\times\mathbb R^m\), an explicit upper bound for
\(\|T_0^{-1}\|\) is
\[
 C_0=\|G\|\bigl(1+\|D_0\|\,\|\mathsf M^{-1}\|\,\|b\|\bigr)
               +\|\mathsf M^{-1}\|\,\|b\|.
\]
The finite terms are read as zero for \(m=0\).
On a fixed compact interval around zero let \(B_1=\sup\|A'_a\|_{X\to Y}\)
and \(B_2=\sup\|A''_a\|_{X\to Y}\).
Then
\[
 \|T_a-T_0\|\le |a|C_1,\qquad
 C_1=B_1\|L^*:U\to X\|+\tfrac12 B_2\|\mathcal J\|.
\]
For \(a\) in this interval with \(|a|C_0 C_1\le 1/2\), the Neumann
series gives \(\|T_a^{-1}\|\le2C_0\). For such \(a\ne0\), a right
inverse for \(A_a\) is
\[
 Q_a f=L^*u+a^{-1}\mathcal J c,\qquad (u,c)=T_a^{-1}f,\qquad
 \|Q_a\|\le 2C_0\bigl(\|L^*\|+|a|^{-1}\|\mathcal J\|\bigr).
 \tag{5}
\]
In particular \(A_a\) is surjective and the formal adjoint kernel at \(r_a\)
is trivial. The norm estimate has the usual gain of two Sobolev orders.
The coefficients \(a^{-1}c\) are the source of the parameter loss.
Moreover \(b(f)=b((A_a-L)h)\) for every solution \(A_a h=f\), so
\[
 |b(f)|\le \|b\| B_1 |a|\,\|h\|_X.
 \tag{6}
\]
For \(m>0\), test (6) on \(f=Z_1\). Every bounded right inverse then has
norm at least \((\|b\|B_1\|Z_1\|_Y)^{-1}|a|^{-1}\).
Under the matrix hypothesis \(B_1>0\) in this case. Thus the power
one in the parameter loss is sharp over the full target space.
For \(m=0\) (5) is uniform and the right inverse extends to \(a=0\).

## Observable finite rows

The map \(b\) is the exact obstruction observable for the old
linearized range. It gives nonzero tests on smooth sources supported
away from \(K\) when \(m>0\). Choose a closed round subball
\(K'\Subset O\) with \(K\subset\operatorname{int}K'\) and a smooth
\(w\) zero on \(K'\) and strictly positive outside \(K'\).
If \(Z\in\mathcal C\) vanishes outside \(K'\), its round transplant has
a smooth zero extension to the standard round \(S^4\) solving the
round adjoint equation. The round kernel is zero. The local
identification of the adjoint follows from the common volume form
and fiber metrics in the round region. Gover–Gursky §1.1, pp. 295–296,
identifies \(H^2_{\rm ASD}\) with the adjoint kernel and records round
unobstructedness after Conjecture 1.4. That passage concerns an ASD
metric, so it applies to the transplanted round problem.
It follows that the matrix \(\Gamma_{ij}=b(wZ_j)_i\) is positive
definite, by integrating \(w|\sum_j c_j Z_j|^2\). The \(wZ_j\) thus
realize all old obstruction coordinates as sources. This establishes
a finite observable without asserting \(b(W)\ne0\) for a supplied
reference. [E27, exterior restriction][e27] · [Gover–Gursky][gg]

There is also an exact test of the missing rows of the moved
normal slice. For small \(|a|\), \(\mathcal D_a=(I-\Pi)A_aL^*:U\to Y_0\)
is invertible as a perturbation of \(P|_U\). The map
\[
 b_a^{\rm slice}(f)=b(f)-b A_aL^*\mathcal D_a^{-1}(I-\Pi)f
 \tag{7}
\]
has kernel \(A_aL^*U\): the second term removes the contribution of
the unique \(u\) accounting for \((I-\Pi)f\). Since \(bP=0\) on \(U\),
we have \(b A_a L^*=O(a)\) and \(A_a\mathcal J=aD_0+O(a^2)\).
Consequently \(b_a^{\rm slice} A_a\mathcal J=a\mathsf M+O(a^2)\).
These rows concern the moving slice, while the full linearization
at the new reference is onto under (3)–(5).

## Exact local nonlinear lift on the supplied manifold

For each fixed allowed \(a\ne0\), the slice
\(\mathcal S=L^*U+\mathcal J\mathbb R^m\) equals \(\operatorname{im}Q_a\)
since \(T_a\) is bijective. The sum is direct: if \(L^*u=\mathcal J c\),
then \(Pu=0\) and \(u=0\). The matrix hypothesis also makes \(\mathcal J\)
injective. Also \(Q_a A_a\) is a bounded projection onto \(\mathcal S\),
so \(\mathcal S\) is closed. Restrict \(\widehat F(a,\cdot)-W_a\) to this
slice. Its derivative is invertible at zero with inverse \(Q_a\).

A sufficient size for this local inverse follows from the common
Lipschitz bound \(\Lambda\ge1\) on \(D_h\widehat F\) on
\(\|h\|_X\le\rho_*\), obtained by shrinking near \((0,0)\).
Write \(\beta=1\) for \(m>0\) and \(\beta=0\) for \(m=0\).
Take \(J\ge1\) with \(\|Q_a\|\le J|a|^{-\beta}\) from (5) and
restrict \(|a|\le1\). Set
\[
 \rho_a=\min\bigl(\rho_*,\,|a|^\beta/(2J\Lambda)\bigr),\qquad
 \delta_a=\rho_a |a|^\beta/(2J).
\]
If \(\|f\|_Y<\delta_a\), the map
\(h\mapsto Q_a f-Q_a(\widehat F(a,h)-W_a-A_a h)\) is a contraction
on the closed radius-\(\rho_a\) ball in \(\mathcal S\).
The derivative remainder there has Lipschitz constant
at most \(\Lambda\rho_a\), and the quadratic remainder has norm at most
\(\Lambda\rho_a^2/2\). Hence the contraction constant is at most \(1/2\)
and the map takes the ball into itself. Its unique solution in this
ball satisfies \(\widehat F(a,h)=W_a+f\) and
\(\|h\|_X\le2\|Q_a\|\|f\|_Y\). The derivative on the slice remains
invertible throughout the ball by the same bound.
This yields smooth dependence in the fixed Sobolev grade. The size
\(\delta_a\) is a sufficient radius of order \(a^2\) for \(m>0\), with
no optimality statement about nonlinear radii.
[Manuscript, quantitative lift, Proposition (lift:radial)][ms]

For smooth \(f\), the solution is spatially smooth at this same low
norm threshold. Write \(h=L^*u+\mathcal J d\) and augment the nonlinear
row on \(H^{s+4}(B)\times\mathbb R^m\) by the row \(b(u)=0\).
The differential restricted to \(U\times\mathbb R^m\) is invertible
by the slice estimate and its complement is the isomorphism
\(b:\mathcal C\to\mathbb R^m\), with \(L^*\mathcal C=0\).
Conjugate the spatial row by local smooth component pullbacks that
act as translations on smaller framed balls, leaving \(d\) fixed.
The conjugated differential expression has order at most four in
\(u\) and depends smoothly on the translation parameter as a map at
the same grades. For the moment row, a change of variables transfers
the pullback to its smooth coefficients. The translated right-hand
side is smooth in the translation parameter in \(H^s\).
The implicit function theorem and strong continuity of pullback
therefore identify the translated solution as a smooth function of
that parameter with values in \(H^{s+4}\times\mathbb R^m\).
Taking its parameter derivatives yields every spatial derivative
of \(u\) locally in \(H^{s+4}\), which gives smoothness by Sobolev
embedding. With \(f=-tW_a\) the same argument uses both translation
and \(t\) as parameters, giving joint smoothness. [E27, spatial
regularity with finite rows][e27]

In particular, for each fixed such \(a\) and
\(|t|\,\|W_a\|_Y<\delta_a\) there is a smooth fixed-volume metric
\(g(a,h_a(t))\) on the original smooth \(M\), with \(h_a(0)=0\).
In the \(r_a\) chart it satisfies the exact equation
\[
 F_{r_a}(J_a h_a(t))=(1-t)W^+_{r_a}.
 \tag{8}
\]
If \(W_a=0\), the constant solution at \(r_a\) works for all \(t\).
The metric is positive by (2) and its compatible chiral connection is
the transported Levi–Civita chiral connection. Its positive Weyl block
vanishes near \(K\), since \(r_a=r\) there and the target in (8) vanishes
there. The solved metric may deform \(K\). The proof takes place on
the supplied \(M\) with its given transitions and imposes no
diffeomorphism type on its actual exterior.

## Comparison with the originally fixed prescription

The same single symmetric comparison from \(r\) to \(r_a\) gives
\(W_a=F_r(a v^\flat)\), where \(v^\flat=r(v\,\cdot,\cdot)\).
In \(Y\),
\[
 W_a-W=aLv^\flat+O(a^2),\qquad b(W_a-W)=O(a^2).
 \tag{9}
\]
The difference is supported away from \(K\). Even in the fixed vector
space \(Y\) the radial target in (8) is \((1-t)W_a\).
To apply the moving-chart inverse to \((1-t)W\) in that comparison
would require a size check on \(f=(1-t)W-W_a\).
Its constant discrepancy is generally allowed by (9) to be order
\(|a|\), whereas the sufficient full-ball radius for \(m>0\) is
order \(a^2\). If \(b(W)\ne0\), (6) also forces the \(X\)-norm of the
radial tangent at the moved reference to be at least a positive
multiple of \(|a|^{-1}\), since \(b(W_a)\to b(W)\).

For the originally fixed \(F_r\) prescription there is a further
target change from composing the isometries. Put
\(\ell^\sharp=\log(e^{av/2}e^{h^\sharp}e^{av/2})\).
The composite tangent map from \(r\) through \(r_a\) to \(g(a,h)\)
is \(e^{-av/2}e^{-h^\sharp/2}\), whereas the direct symmetric map
is \(e^{-\ell^\sharp/2}\). Their ratio on the \(r\) tangent space is
the oriented orthogonal map
\[
 O(a,h)=e^{\ell^\sharp/2}e^{-av/2}e^{-h^\sharp/2},\qquad
 R(a,h)=\Lambda^2 O(a,h)^*|_E .
\]
Consequently \(\widehat F(a,h)=R(a,h)F_r(\ell)R(a,h)^{-1}\).
The fixed equation \(F_r(\ell)=(1-t)W\) would demand the moving target
\((1-t)R(a,h)W R(a,h)^{-1}\), which depends on the unknown \(h\).
The direct and composite maps agree on either parameter axis.
For \(h=\epsilon k\), the mixed \(a\epsilon\) coefficient of
\(\ell^\sharp\) is zero, by multiplying the three exponentials and
taking the logarithm to second order. The mixed coefficient of
\(O(a,\epsilon k)\) is \([v,k^\sharp]/8\).
Differentiating the target comparison therefore gives
\[
 A_a k= DF_r(a v^\flat)\,D_h\ell(a,0)k+[\Theta_a(k),W_a],
 \qquad
 \Theta_a(k)=D_hR(a,0)k,\qquad D_h\ell(a,0)=I+O(a^2).
\]
Thus the first-order matrix for the original fixed prescription at
\(a v^\flat\) has entries
\(\mathsf M_{ij}-b([\partial_a\Theta_a|_0(k_j),W])_i\).
The two target conventions coincide at a zero Weyl block. At the
original reference, a differentiable lift of \((1-t)W\) through \(r\)
still requires \(b(W)=0\), by \(Lh'(0)=-W\). The changed chart
establishes (8) only on its local interval. A fixed-target lift,
a continuation estimate and realization of the matrix premise
on a general supplied \(M,r\) require independent arguments.
