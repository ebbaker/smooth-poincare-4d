## Result

Use the standard smooth \(S^4\), the boundary markings and the two
oppositely oriented \(\ell=1\) Taub–NUT cores of [E29][e29]. There
is a new joining metric \(g_R^+\), \(R\geq1\), which is smooth,
agrees with both exact unscaled Taub–NUT metrics on neighbourhoods
of their \(r\leq R\) balls and satisfies

\[
 \operatorname{Scal}_{g_R^+}\geq 0,\qquad
 0<Y(S^4,[g_R^+])\leq C R^{-1/2},\qquad
 E_\pm(g_R^+)=8\pi^2+O(R^{-1}),\qquad
 \int_{S^4}|B_{g_R^+}|_{g_R^+}\,dV_{g_R^+}=O(R^{-1}).
\]

Scalar curvature is strictly positive on a middle subcylinder.
The estimates use the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt convention
and \(W^+=0\) for the hyperkähler orientation. Each \(R\) also admits
the optional E30 round marking with positive Yamabe sign and
arbitrarily small prescribed energy and Bach costs.
[B21][b21] · [E07][e07] · [E29][e29]

## Scaled coordinates and a concave join

Put \(u=r/R\) and \(V_R(u)=1+1/(2Ru)\). In a shell about the
boundary, the scaled core \(R^{-2}g_{\rm TN}\) is \(ds^2+\gamma_s\),
where outward unit radial distance \(s=s_R(u)\) and its slice
coefficients are given by

\[
 \begin{gathered}
   s_R(u)=\int_1^u\sqrt{V_R(v)}\,dv,\qquad
   \frac{du}{ds}=V_R(u)^{-1/2},\\
   \gamma_s=a_R^2\,\pi^*g_{S^2}+c_R^2\,\eta^2,\qquad
   a_R^2=u^2 V_R(u),\qquad c_R^2=R^{-2}V_R(u)^{-1}.
 \end{gathered}
\]

The sphere \(S^2\) has unit radius. In this normalization
\(d\eta=-\tfrac12\pi^*dA_{S^2}\) with the chosen base orientation,
by \(d\eta=*dV\) for the harmonic function \(V=1+1/(2r)\).
The circle coordinate has period \(2\pi\).
[Cherkis–Hurtubise, §2.1, (1) and footnote 1][ch] · [E07][e07]

Take the same cylinder \(N=[-1,1]\times S^3\) as in E29. Fix a
smooth odd nondecreasing function \(q(t)\) equal to zero on
\(|t|\leq 1/4\), equal to \(1\) for \(t\geq 3/4\) and equal to
\(-1\) for \(t\leq-3/4\). Such a function is obtained by integrating
a normalized nonnegative smooth bump on \((1/4,3/4)\) on the positive
side and extending oddly. Set

\[
 \sigma(t)=-\int_{-1}^{t}q(v)\,dv,\qquad
 \widehat g_R^+|_N=dt^2+\gamma_{\sigma(t)}.
\]

The profile has \(\sigma(-1)=\sigma(1)=0\), \(|\sigma'|\leq1\),
\(\sigma''\leq0\), and \(0\leq\sigma\leq1\). The last bounds
follow from the sign of \(-q\) on the two halves and the unit
distance from either seam to the middle. Near the left seam
\(\sigma=t+1\), and near the right seam \(\sigma=1-t\).
Consequently \(dt^2=ds^2\) in the two matching neighbourhoods
and the slice functions are those of the exact Taub–NUT
continuations. This gives smooth matching of all jets at both
seams. In the middle \(\sigma\) is constant and the longitudinal
coefficient is one. On the whole join the shell coordinate satisfies
\(1\leq u_R(\sigma(t))\leq2\) since \(ds_R/du\geq1\).

On the cores take \(R^{-2}g_{\rm TN}\). This defines
\(\widehat g_R^+\) using the arc-distance collar smooth structure.
To put it on the E29 double, use the identity on both balls and
on the angular marking. If \(t_{\rm old}\) is the E29 cylinder
coordinate, send it to \(-1+s_R(t_{\rm old}+2)\) near the left
end and to \(1-s_R(2-t_{\rm old})\) near the right end. These
have positive derivative and the prescribed endpoint values.
They extend to an increasing smooth cylinder diffeomorphism:
use the indicated derivatives on small end collars and interpolate
them by cutoffs to a positive middle constant chosen to give
total derivative integral \(2\). The endpoint formulas then hold
on both small collars, and the map is the identity in \(u\)
across each seam. Pull back by this identification, preserving
the markings, opposite core orientations and exact metrics on
the cores. Finally \(g_R^+=R^2\widehat g_R^+\) restores the
unscaled cores exactly. The \(t\) coordinate used for intrinsic
neck estimates is the new arc parameter.

## Scalar curvature of the slices and the join

Fix a slice and abbreviate \(a=a_R\), \(c=c_R\). Its projection to
\((S^2,a^2g_{S^2})\) is a Riemannian submersion with geodesic circle
fibres. For an orthonormal horizontal pair \(E_1,E_2\) and the unit
vertical vector \(U\), the vertical component of
\([E_1,E_2]\) is \(vU\) with \(v=-c\,d\eta(E_1,E_2)\) and
\(|v|=c/(2a^2)\). To check the curvature factors, use horizontal
lifts of an oriented base orthonormal frame normal at the point.
They commute with \(U\). The Koszul formula gives the base
horizontal Levi-Civita terms together with
\((\nabla_{E_1}E_2)^{\rm vert}=(v/2)U\),
\((\nabla_{E_2}E_1)^{\rm vert}=-(v/2)U\),
\(\nabla_{E_1}U=\nabla_U E_1=-(v/2)E_2\),
\(\nabla_{E_2}U=\nabla_U E_2=(v/2)E_1\), and
\(\nabla_U U=0\). Thus the horizontal sectional curvature is
\(a^{-2}-3v^2/4\) and each mixed sectional curvature is
\(v^2/4\). Summing them gives

\[
 \operatorname{Scal}(\gamma_s)=\frac{2}{a^2}-\frac{c^2}{8a^4}.
\]

On the used range \(1\leq u\leq2\), \(R\geq1\),
\(1\leq a^2\leq6\) and \(c^2\leq1\), so this scalar curvature
is bounded below by \(5/16\) and above by \(2\).
The outward mean curvature, with the trace convention, is

\[
 H_s=\frac{d}{ds}\log(a_R^2 c_R)
 =V_R(u)^{-1/2}
      \left(\frac2u-\frac{1}{4Ru^2V_R(u)}\right).
\]

Thus \(H_s\geq(3/4)(3/2)^{-1/2}>0\) and \(H_s\leq2\) on
the same range.

Let \(A_s\) denote the second fundamental form of the
original shells, with the \(+\partial_s\) normal. The Gauss
formula for the horizontal scalar trace and the normal
Riccati trace give

\[
 \operatorname{Scal}(ds^2+\gamma_s)
  =\operatorname{Scal}(\gamma_s)-|A_s|^2-H_s^2-2\partial_sH_s .
\]

Indeed the tangential part of the ambient scalar is
\(\operatorname{Scal}(\gamma_s)-H_s^2+|A_s|^2\) and the
normal Ricci term is \(-\partial_s H_s-|A_s|^2\), counted twice.
This agrees with the general cylinder formula of Ebert–Frenck
after rewriting in terms of the trace \(H_s\).
For the join, the second fundamental form in \(+\partial_t\)
is \(\sigma' A_{\sigma(t)}\), its trace is
\(\sigma'H_{\sigma(t)}\), and the scalar of the original
Taub–NUT is zero. Substitution yields exactly

\[
 \operatorname{Scal}(\widehat g_R^+|_N)
 =(1-\sigma'^2)\operatorname{Scal}(\gamma_{\sigma(t)})
     -2\sigma''H_{\sigma(t)} .
\]

Both terms are nonnegative on the neck, and the first is
strictly positive on the middle plateau. The cores are
scalar-flat and the sign is preserved by constant homothety.
[Ebert–Frenck, Lemma 2.4][ef]

## Collapsed geometry, Bach norm and the Yamabe constant

The fixed shell formulas for \(a_R^2\) and \(R^2c_R^2\) and
their inverse coefficients are uniformly bounded through four
derivatives on a fixed \(u\)-interval containing \([1,2]\) in
\((0,\infty)\). The relation \(du/ds=V_R^{-1/2}\) and the
fixed smooth \(\sigma\) preserve these bounds in \(t\).
In a fixed finite base cover, write \(\eta=d\phi+\alpha\)
and unwrap as in E29 with \(\psi=\phi/R\). The scaled neck
then reads

\[
 dt^2+a_R^2 g_{S^2}+V_R^{-1}(d\psi+R^{-1}\alpha)^2 .
\]

It has uniformly positive metric eigenvalues and bounded
coefficients and inverse through \(C^4\) in the lifted charts,
so \(|\operatorname{Rm}|\), \(|\nabla\operatorname{Rm}|\) and
\(|\nabla^2\operatorname{Rm}|\) are bounded on the scaled neck.
The shell continuation covers the seam points. Integrating the
circle and base factors gives the axial volume measure
\(8\pi^2 a_R^2 c_R\,dt=(8\pi^2/R)u^2\sqrt{V_R}\,dt\).
Its density is bounded above and below by positive constants
times \(R^{-1}\). In particular the neck volume is \(O(R^{-1})\).

The Bach tensor vanishes on each Ricci-flat core and is
uniformly bounded on the scaled neck by the Schouten formula
and the curvature bounds. Similarly the neck contribution to
each \(E_\pm\) is \(O(R^{-1})\). Each chirality receives one
exact core contribution
\(8\pi^2(1-4/(1+2R)^3+3/(1+2R)^4)\), with \(W^-=0\) on the
orientation-reversed core and \(W^+=0\) on the other.
The four-dimensional integrals of \(|B|\) and \(|W^\pm|^2\)
are invariant under the restoring homothety, as in E29.
This proves the claimed chiral limits and Bach bound. The exact
inner balls give \(E_\pm\geq11\pi^2/2\). [E07][e07] · [E29][e29] ·
[Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1, (1.1)][bgim]

For \(h=\widehat g_R^+\) or \(g_R^+\), the Yamabe quotient in
dimension four is \(Q_h[f]/D_h[f]\), where
\(Q_h[f]=\int(6|\nabla f|_h^2+\operatorname{Scal}_h f^2)dV_h\)
and \(D_h[f]=(\int|f|^4dV_h)^{1/2}\).
For fixed \(R\), the first \(L^2\) eigenvalue of the conformal
Laplacian is strictly positive. A normalized zero-eigenvalue
minimizer would have zero Dirichlet integral and would be
constant by connectedness. Its scalar term could not then
vanish because the scalar curvature is positive on the
middle open set. This eigenvalue and the compact Sobolev
inequality bound \(D_h[f]\) by a fixed-\(R\) multiple of
\(Q_h[f]\). Thus \(Y(S^4,[h])>0\).

In the rescaled metric take \(f(t)\) a fixed smooth nonzero
nonnegative function supported in the middle open plateau and
extend it by zero. It is admissible in computing an upper
bound on \(Y\) by approximation with positive functions.
Its gradient there has norm \(|f'|\), scalar curvature is
uniformly bounded, and the axial volume density just found is
comparable to \(R^{-1}\). Hence \(Q_h[f]=O(R^{-1})\) and
\(D_h[f]\geq c R^{-1/2}\). The quotient gives the stated
upper bound, which is invariant under constant homothety.
[Lee–Parker, §1, (1.4)–(1.5)][lp]

## Optional exact round marking

For each fixed \(R\), apply the normal-coordinate cutoff of E30
at a chosen marked point in a common core, with the extended
marked chart away from the neck. Denote the metric before the
marking diffeomorphism and conformal factor by \(h_\varepsilon\)
and the original metric by \(h=g_R^+\). E30's estimate (3) gives
\(C^0\) metric error \(O_h(\varepsilon^2)\) and bounded
scalar curvature through the shrinking cutoff region, of
volume \(O_h(\varepsilon^4)\). Since the metrics agree
outside this region, the scalar curvature error is
\(O_h(\varepsilon^2)\) in \(L^2(dV_h)\). Therefore, uniformly
over smooth \(f\), the volume and inverse metric comparisons
and Hölder's inequality give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 |D_{h_\varepsilon}[f]-D_h[f]|&\leq C_h\varepsilon^2 D_h[f],\\
 |Q_{h_\varepsilon}[f]-Q_h[f]|&\leq C_h\varepsilon^2
                                  (Q_h[f]+D_h[f]).
 \end{aligned}
\]

The Dirichlet term here is controlled by \(Q_h\) because
\(\operatorname{Scal}_h\geq0\). The scalar comparison uses the
same \(L^2\) bound after incorporating the volume-density ratio.
Taking the infimum yields \(Y(S^4,[h_\varepsilon])\to
Y(S^4,[h])>0\): the lower comparison follows by division
using the denominator bounds for small \(\varepsilon\), and
the upper comparison follows by testing a fixed approximate
minimizer of \(Q_h/D_h\). Alternatively the same axial test
is unchanged and supplies the uniform upper bound on \(Y\).

E30's orientation-preserving marking pullback and positive
conformal factor preserve the Yamabe constant and both
curvature integrals. Thus for any prescribed positive cost
tolerance for each \(R\) one can choose \(\varepsilon(R)>0\)
small enough to keep \(Y>0\) and meet that tolerance for
both \(E_\pm\) and \(\int|B|\), using E30's \(O_h(\varepsilon^4)\)
and \(O_h(\varepsilon^2)\) cost bounds. The second \(E_-\) bound
also follows by signature. On choosing a tolerance tending to
zero, the marked family retains the limiting energies and
Bach conclusion. Exact Taub–NUT data away from the marking
chart remain available, and a chart disjoint from the inner
balls preserves both of their exact lower bounds. Positivity
of the Yamabe class survives the marking even though this
proof of nonnegative pointwise scalar curvature concerns
the unmarked join. [E30, (2)–(6) and marking][e30]
