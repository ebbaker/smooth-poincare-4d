## Chart and contraction theorem

Let \(r\) be a smooth real positive-definite Riemannian metric on a
smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere. Set \(\nu=dV_r\),
\(V_r=S^2_0 T^*M\), \(B_r=S^2_0\Lambda_r^+\), and \(W=W_r^+\). The
Weyl target is the real curvature-operator block with its Hilbert–Schmidt
pairing, and ASD means \(W^+=0\). Take \(k\ge2\), \(0<\alpha<1\),
\(X_r=C^{k+2,\alpha}(V_r)\), and \(Y_r=C^{k,\alpha}(B_r)\). In the
full trace-free chart the metric and map are
\[
 g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot),\qquad
 F_r(h)=I_h W_{g_h}^+ I_h^{-1},\qquad L_r= DF_r(0),
\]
where \(h^\sharp=r^{-1}h\) and \(I_h\) is pullback on self-dual forms by
the positive symmetric frame isometry \(e^{-h^\sharp/2}:(TM,r)\to
(TM,g_h)\). All norms at fixed \(r\) are fixed reference Hölder norms.
Curvature is smooth in these grades by its local expression through
the second metric jet and the Hölder Banach algebra operations.
The frame identification also uses smooth pointwise operations.
[B01 field-space decision][b01] · [E03 normal operator][e03]

Suppose there is a bounded right inverse \(Q:Y_r\to X_r\) for
\(L=L_r\). Then \(QL\) is a bounded projection onto \(S=\operatorname{im}Q\),
so \(S\) is closed. Assume on the closed radius \(\rho>0\) chart ball
that \(DF_r\) has Lipschitz constant at most \(H\). Put
\(\eta=\|QW\|_{X_r}\) and \(q=\|Q\|H\rho\). The B03 criterion is
\[
 \eta+\tfrac12 q\rho\le\rho,\qquad q<1 .
 \tag{1}
\]
Let \(N(h)=F_r(h)-F_r(0)-Lh\). Integrating the first derivative along
segments in the ball gives
\[
 \|N(h)\|_{Y_r}\le\tfrac12 H\|h\|_{X_r}^2,\qquad
 \|N(h)-N(\widetilde h)\|_{Y_r}\le H\rho
                      \|h-\widetilde h\|_{X_r} .
 \tag{2}
\]
For every \(t\in[0,1]\), define \(T_t(h)=-tQW-QN(h)\) on the closed
radius \(\rho\) ball in \(S\). Its norm is bounded by
\(t\eta+\|Q\|H\rho^2/2\le\rho\) and its Lipschitz constant is at most
\(q\). Thus there is a unique fixed point \(h(t)\) in this complete ball.
Applying \(L\) to the fixed-point identity and using \(LQ=I\) gives
\[
 F_r(h(t))=(1-t)W .
 \tag{3}
\]
Conversely, (3) for \(h\in S\) in the ball gives that fixed-point
identity because \(QLh=h\). In the compatible mixed equations take
\(A=\Gamma^+(g_{h(t)})\) in the chosen frame, so that
\(d_A\Sigma=0\) and \(\operatorname{Sym}_0 C=(1-t)W\) in the corresponding
reference frame. The curvature normalization here is the one in the
companion's `rec:chiral-curvature`. [B03 criterion][b03] · [CME][cme]

Subtracting the fixed-point equations gives the parameter bound
\[
 \|h(t)-h(u)\|_{X_r}\le\frac{\eta}{1-q}|t-u|,
 \qquad
 \|h(t)\|_{X_r}\le\frac{t\eta}{1-q/2} .
 \tag{4}
\]
For the second inequality use (2) and \(\|h(t)\|\le\rho\) in the fixed
point equation. The derivative with respect to \(h\) of
\(h+tQW+QN(h)\) on \(S\) is the identity plus an operator of norm at
most \(q\), hence invertible. The Banach implicit function theorem and
continuity from (4) give smooth parameter dependence in \(X_r\), also
locally across the two endpoints in the open chart. In particular,
\(h(0)=0\), \(h'(0)=-QW\), and \(\|h'(t)\|_{X_r}\le\eta/(1-q)\).
The construction thus gives the entire interval in one fixed chart.

The chart ball controls geometry at the specified finite order. If
\(c_e\) is the norm bound for \(h\mapsto h^\sharp\) into pointwise
operator norm, then throughout the ball
\[
 e^{-c_e\rho}r\le g_h\le e^{c_e\rho}r,
 \qquad dV_{g_h}=\exp(\operatorname{tr}_r h/2)\nu=\nu .
 \tag{5}
\]
The products and matrix exponentials for \(g_h\) and \(g_h^{-1}\) give
uniform \(C^{k+2,\alpha}\) bounds in a finite fixed atlas from the
radius and the reference coefficients. The coordinate curvature and
its covariant derivatives through order \(k\) have the corresponding
bounds. A uniform positive injectivity-radius bound follows directly
in this setting. Choose finitely many coordinate balls with smaller
interior balls covering \(M\). There are uniform bounds for the
Christoffel coefficients and their first derivatives, and uniform
coordinate metric equivalences. For tangent vectors of uniformly small
coordinate norm, the geodesic stays in the larger coordinate ball up
to time one. The geodesic variation equation gives derivative of the
exponential map in its initial velocity equal to \(I+O(|v|)\), with
uniform constants from those coefficient bounds. On a sufficiently
small convex velocity ball this derivative differs from \(I\) by less
than \(1/2\), so the coordinate exponential is injective and locally
invertible there. Coordinate metric equivalence gives a common radius
in metric norm. These finite-order bounds are uniform in \(t\).

## Round neighborhood in common spaces

Take the standard oriented smooth \(S^4\) with round metric \(r_0\)
of sectional curvature one and use fixed atlas norms from \(r_0\).
For \(r\) close in \(C^{k+2,\alpha}\), the positive symmetric isometry
\(S_r=(r_0^{-1}r)^{-1/2}:(TM,r_0)\to(TM,r)\) gives two smooth
identifications. Let \(J_r:V_r\to V_0\) be pullback of symmetric
two-tensors by \(S_r\). Let \(U_r:B_r\to B_0\) be conjugation by its
pullback on self-dual two-forms. These maps preserve the respective
fiber metrics and types. Define on fixed spaces \(X_0,Y_0\)
\[
 \widetilde F_r(x)=U_r F_r(J_r^{-1}x),\qquad
 \widetilde L_r=D_x\widetilde F_r(0),\qquad
 \widetilde W_r=\widetilde F_r(0).
 \tag{6}
\]
All inverse norms and constants in the neighborhood can be taken with
these common spaces. Equivalently the reference norms are pulled
back via \(J_r,U_r\). They are uniformly equivalent to norms induced
from the geometric references \(r\) in this neighborhood.

Let \(D_0=\widetilde L_{r_0}\), \(R=D_0^*\), and \(P_0=D_0D_0^*\),
with the adjoint at \(r_0\). The E02 round computation gives fourth-order
symbol \(|\xi|_{r_0}^4 I/16\), \(\ker D_0^*=0\), and trivial kernel of
\(D_0\) on global \(r_0\)-TT tensors. The Weyl cokernel input is
Gover–Gursky §1.1 for the standard round ASD sphere. The closed elliptic
generalized inverse of \(P_0\) is then its true inverse of order \(-4\).
On noninteger Hölder grades its boundedness
\(P_0^{-1}:Y_0\to C^{k+4,\alpha}(B_0)\) follows by localizing the
pseudodifferential mapping formula in Taylor (A.1.6)–(A.1.7).
It applies to the full Hölder space, with a smoothing remainder
in the parametrix. The right inverse \(Q_0=RP_0^{-1}\) takes values
in the \(r_0\)-TT image because \(D_0\mathcal K_{r_0}=0\), and it is
the round TT inverse by kernel triviality. [E02, §§2 and 5][e02] ·
[Kottke, Propositions 1.21–1.28][kottke] · [Taylor, §§0.4, 0.12 and
Appendix A.1][taylor] · [Gover–Gursky, §1.1, pp. 295–296][gg]

The map \((r,x)\mapsto\widetilde F_r(x)\) is jointly smooth from a
neighborhood of \((r_0,0)\) in the \(C^{k+2,\alpha}\) metric space
and \(X_0\) to \(Y_0\). Indeed, local coefficients in (6) are smooth
functions of \(r,x\) and their first two derivatives, with the
fiberwise matrix functions smooth near the positive definite
background. Pointwise matrix composition and products act smoothly
on these Hölder Banach spaces. Continuity of its derivatives at
\((r_0,0)\) gives a product neighborhood with bounds on
\(D_x^2\widetilde F\), \(D_rD_x\widetilde F\) and \(D_r\widetilde F\).
Taking smaller closed radii inside the neighborhood and integrating
on segments proves a uniform Lipschitz bound \(H\) for
\(D_x\widetilde F_r\) on the chosen ball, and constants such that
\[
 \|\widetilde L_r-D_0\|_{\mathcal L(X_0,Y_0)}
    +\|\widetilde W_r\|_{Y_0}
 \le C\|r-r_0\|_{C^{k+2,\alpha}} .
 \tag{7}
\]
Here \(W_{r_0}^+=0\) was used. The bound uses one fixed regularity
grade throughout.

Let \(M_0=\|Q_0\|\). Shrink the reference neighborhood so that
\(\| (\widetilde L_r-D_0)Q_0\|<1/2\). Then define
\[
 A_r=\widetilde L_r Q_0=I+(\widetilde L_r-D_0)Q_0,
 \qquad \widetilde Q_r=Q_0 A_r^{-1}.
 \tag{8}
\]
The Neumann series gives \(\widetilde L_r\widetilde Q_r=I\),
\(\|\widetilde Q_r\|\le2M_0\), and smooth dependence of this bounded
operator on \(r\) in the fixed grade. Its image is always
\(\operatorname{im}Q_0\) in the common domain. In particular the
construction keeps the round complement even when the reference
conformal Killing fields change. In the original chart the inverse
is \(J_r^{-1}\widetilde Q_r U_r\).

Choose \(\rho>0\) within the product neighborhood so that
\(2M_0 H\rho\le 1/2\) and shrink the reference neighborhood again so
that \(\eta_r=\|\widetilde Q_r\widetilde W_r\|\le\rho/2\), using
(7). The left side of the self-mapping bound in (1) is at most
\(3\rho/4\) for every such \(r\). Thus the path and the geometry
bounds hold uniformly on this neighborhood. The joint implicit
function theorem for \(h+tQW+QN(h)\) in the common range also yields
joint smooth dependence in \((r,t)\) in the fixed Banach grade,
using the uniform contraction and the strict ball margin.

The comparison is made at the unit round scale. Every solution for
a reference \(r\) has the prescribed volume form \(dV_r\), which may
vary with the reference. If a fixed total reference volume is desired,
replace \(r\) before the comparison by
\(c_r r\) with \(c_r=(\operatorname{Vol}(r_0)/\operatorname{Vol}(r))^{1/2}\),
and impose the same neighborhood test on this rescaled metric.
For any fixed constant \(c>0\), the chart identity is
\(g^{cr}_{ch}=c g^r_h\), and under the common self-dual bundle
identification \(F_{cr}(ch)=c^{-1}F_r(h)\). The source scales by the
same factor. This uses the conformal weight of the Weyl endomorphism
and distinguishes the reference-volume choice from the prescribed
pointwise metric volume along a path. [E03 scaling][e03]

For an explicit nonzero-forcing family, choose a nonzero smooth
\(f\in\Gamma(B_0)\) and \(p=Q_0 f\), which is smooth by elliptic
regularity. Let \(r_\epsilon=r_0(e^{\epsilon p^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\)
for sufficiently small real \(\epsilon\). It has pointwise volume
\(dV_{r_0}\), lies in the neighborhood, and
\(\widetilde W_{r_\epsilon}=\epsilon f+O(\epsilon^2)\) by the derivative
at round and (6). Its forcing is nonzero for all sufficiently small
\(\epsilon\ne0\). Thus this neighborhood test includes references
that require a correction.

## Spatial regularity and the range used for solving

An abstract bounded right inverse in (1) gives a solution in the
specified Banach grade. Spatial smoothness requires a regular choice
of range. In the round neighborhood the range in (8) is
\(R C^{k+4,\alpha}(B_0)\) for the fixed smooth differential operator
\(R=D_0^*\). In potential coordinates \(x=Ru\) the equation is
\(\mathcal G_r(u)=(1-t)\widetilde W_r\) with
\(\mathcal G_r=\widetilde F_r\circ R\), a smooth nonlinear
fourth-order differential operator. At round its principal
linearization is \(|\xi|_{r_0}^4I/16\). Near round the coefficients of
the highest derivatives in \(D\mathcal G_r(u)\) vary continuously
with \((r,x)\). By reducing the chosen radii, the symmetric part of
the symbol is bounded below by \(|\xi|_{r_0}^4I/32\) on the entire
ball. This provides a fixed smooth differential range and an
elliptic potential equation.

The following bootstrap verifies spatial smoothness in that range.
Write \(c^{j,\alpha}\) for the closure of smooth sections in
\(C^{j,\alpha}\). For smooth \(r\), each factor in (8) preserves
the appropriate closures. The classical elliptic inverse \(P_0^{-1}\)
maps smooth sections to smooth sections and acts boundedly, and the
Neumann series preserves the closed subspaces. Also \(\widetilde F_r\)
preserves these closures by approximation with smooth sections and
continuity. Iteration of \(T_t\) from zero therefore yields \(x(t)\)
in \(c^{k+2,\alpha}\) and a potential \(u(t)\in c^{k+4,\alpha}\).
At this solution,
\[
 D\mathcal G_r(u) P_0^{-1}
     =D\widetilde F_r(x)\,\widetilde Q_r A_r .
 \tag{9}
\]
The first factor \(D\widetilde F_r(x)\widetilde Q_r\) is the identity
plus an operator of norm at most \(q\), and \(A_r\) is invertible by
(8). The same Neumann series give invertibility on the little Hölder
spaces since the operators preserve them. Hence \(D\mathcal G_r(u)\)
is invertible between the indicated little Hölder grades.

On a smaller coordinate ball, take a smooth family of local
translations extended to diffeomorphisms supported in a larger
framed ball. Let \(T_a\) act as pullback on local components of the
potential and target in that frame, extended by the identity.
The local expression through jets makes
\(T_a\mathcal G_r T_a^{-1}\) a jointly smooth map of \(a\) and its
argument between the little Hölder grades. Its coefficients come
from the smooth reference and smooth translations, and only the
finite-order derivatives of the argument occur. With the translated
smooth source, the Banach implicit function theorem and (9) give a
smooth branch near \(a=0\). For fixed \(u\in c^{k+4,\alpha}\) the map
\(a\mapsto T_a u\) is continuous in that grade, by approximation with
smooth sections and the locally uniform pullback bounds. It solves
the translated equation, so uniqueness identifies it with the smooth
branch. Differentiation at zero gives all local spatial derivatives
of \(u\) in the fixed grade. Covering the manifold yields smooth
spatial \(u,x,g_h\) and compatible connection. The closure argument
is relevant because arbitrary sections of the full Hölder space
need not have continuous translations in their original norm.
[E03, Sobolev translation argument][e03]

At a general smooth reference, the same argument is available if the
specific \(Q=L_r^*(L_rL_r^*)^{-1}\) of E03 is defined and satisfies (1)
in the Hölder grades. Its inverse has the same closed elliptic Hölder
mapping property and one replaces \(R,P_0\) by \(L_r^*,L_rL_r^*\) in
the potential argument. In that case \(DF_r(h)Q\) itself is the
identity plus a norm-\(q\) perturbation. The contraction for an
arbitrary abstract \(Q\) establishes the Banach-grade result, and
the compatible smooth differential range and regularity argument
are additional information for smooth profiles.
