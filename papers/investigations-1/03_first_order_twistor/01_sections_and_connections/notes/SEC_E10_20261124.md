## Radial energy and the two entry parameters

Let \(M\) be a smooth, closed, connected, oriented homotopy four-sphere.
Fix a smooth positive-definite Riemannian reference metric \(r\). Let
\(g_t\) be smooth positive-definite metrics on \(M\), continuous in
\(C^2\) on a supplied initial interval \(I\subset[0,1]\) containing zero.
Assume
\[
 dV_{g_t}=dV_r,\qquad
 U_t W^+_{g_t}U_t^{-1}=(1-t)W_r^+,\qquad
 U_t:\Lambda^+_{g_t}\longrightarrow\Lambda^+_r
 \ \text{a fiberwise isometry}.
\]
Here \(W^+=0\) is ASD and the Weyl block norm is the curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Set \(E(t)=E_+(g_t)=\int_M
|W^+_{g_t}|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2dV_{g_t}\) and \(E_0=E_+(r)=E(0)\).
The isometry preserves the operator norm convention and the volume is
fixed pointwise, so
\[
 E(t)=(1-t)^2E_0 . \tag{1}
\]
The signature identity on the smooth closed oriented \(M\) gives
\(E_+-E_-=12\pi^2\tau(M)=0\). The squared full four-index Weyl norm
integrates to \(4(E_++E_-)=8E(t)\). In Chang–Gursky–Yang's Theorem A,
the strict inequality \(\int|W|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2<16\pi^2\chi(M)\)
therefore means \(E(t)<4\pi^2\), using \(\chi(M)=2\). The theorem also
requires \(Y(M,[g_t])>0\) for that conformal class.
[SH-E01 normalization][recognition] · [Gover–Gursky, p. 294][gg] ·
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107][cgy]

For \(E_0>0\), define
\[
 t_{\mathrm{CGY}}=1-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{E_0}},\qquad
 t_{\mathrm{sf}}=1-\frac{2\sqrt{2}\pi}{\sqrt{E_0}} .
\]
Then the strict spherical energy interval is \(I\cap\{t>t_{\mathrm{CGY}}\}\).
The low-energy interval is \(J=I\cap\{t>t_{\mathrm{sf}}\}\), equivalently
\(\{t\in I:E(t)<8\pi^2\}\) by (1). Both are relative to \(I\) and
may be empty. For either threshold, initial energy below its energy
level gives a negative entry parameter and includes zero. Initial
energy equal to its energy level makes the entry parameter zero and
includes only positive times. Higher initial energy makes the entry
parameter positive, with the equality slice excluded from the
corresponding strict interval. The case \(E_0=0\) has \(E(t)=0\) for
every supplied time, including zero, and uses the exact recognition
argument below.

## Gauss–Bonnet and the scalar-flat boundary

At each supplied time write \(Y_t=Y(M,[g_t])\). The Yamabe theorem on
the closed smooth Riemannian four-manifold gives a smooth positive
factor \(u_t\) and a minimizing metric \(\widehat g_t=u_t^2g_t\).
Choose \(\int_M u_t^4dV_r=1\), so its constant scalar curvature is
\(s_{\widehat g_t}=Y_t\). This normalization is imposed individually
on the minimizing metrics. [Schoen, §3, Theorem 3, pp. 493–495][schoen]

In dimension four the Hodge splitting on two-forms is conformally
invariant. Under \(\widehat g_t=u_t^2g_t\) the Weyl endomorphism scales
by \(u_t^{-2}\) and the volume by \(u_t^4\), so the energies of both
Weyl blocks are preserved. Write
\(B(t)=\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}_0(\widehat g_t)|^2\,dV_{\widehat g_t}\)
for the squared \(L^2\) norm of its trace-free Ricci tensor. The
Chern–Gauss–Bonnet identity at \(\widehat g_t\) gives
\[
 16\pi^2=2E(t)-\frac12 B(t)+\frac{Y_t^2}{24},
 \qquad
 Y_t^2=48(8\pi^2-E(t))+12B(t). \tag{2}
\]
The coefficient \(2E(t)\) uses one quarter of the full four-index
energy in the Euler density. [Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107, eq. (0.4)
and Remark 2][cgy]

Thus \(Y_t=0\) implies \(E(t)=8\pi^2+B(t)/4\ge 8\pi^2\). A scalar-flat
representative has Yamabe constant zero, since the nonnegative
gradient term alone remains in its Yamabe quotient and constants
attain zero. On \(J\), (2) gives the quantitative gap
\[
 |Y_t|\ge \sqrt{48(8\pi^2-(1-t)^2E_0)}>0 . \tag{3}
\]
For \(E(t)=8\pi^2\), the identity becomes \(Y_t^2=12B(t)\), so a zero
Yamabe constant at that equality requires a Ricci-flat minimizing
metric. Conversely a Ricci-flat representative is scalar-flat and
has \(Y_t=0\). For \(E(t)=4\pi^2\), (3) gives
\(|Y_t|\ge 8\sqrt{3}\pi\). The energy hypothesis of Theorem A is
satisfied only at supplied times with \(E(t)<4\pi^2\).

## Continuity and sign of the first eigenvalue

Take \(\Delta_g=-\operatorname{div}_g\nabla\) and \(L_t=6\Delta_{g_t}+s_{g_t}\).
For real \(v\in H^1(M,r)\), put
\[
 Q_t(v)=\int_M(6|dv|_{g_t}^2+s_{g_t}v^2)\,dV_r,\qquad
 \lambda_1(t)=\inf_{v\ne0}\frac{Q_t(v)}{\|v\|_{L^2(dV_r)}^2}.
\]
The conformal scalar-curvature equation for the minimizer is
\(L_tu_t=Y_tu_t^3\). Inserting \(v=u_tf\), integrating the cross term
by parts and using this equation gives
\[
 Q_t(v)=6\int_M u_t^2|d(v/u_t)|_{g_t}^2\,dV_r
           +Y_t\int_Mu_t^2v^2\,dV_r .
\]
For \(Y_t>0\), the compactness of \(M\) and strict positivity of \(u_t\)
give \(\lambda_1(t)\ge Y_t\min_M u_t^2>0\). For \(Y_t=0\), the form is
nonnegative and \(v=u_t\) attains zero. For \(Y_t<0\), the same test
has \(Q_t(u_t)=Y_t<0\). This proves
\(\operatorname{sgn}\lambda_1(t)=\operatorname{sgn}Y_t\).
[Lee–Parker, §2, eqs. (2.6)–(2.7), p. 43][leeparker]

For continuity, fix \(t_0\in I\) and \(K=\|s_{g_{t_0}}\|_\infty+1\).
The \(C^2\) continuity gives uniform convergence of \(g_t^{-1}\) and
\(s_{g_t}\) on \(M\) as \(t\to t_0\). In particular
\[
 |Q_t(v)-Q_{t_0}(v)|
 \le \varepsilon(t)\left(Q_{t_0}(v)+K\|v\|_{L^2(dV_r)}^2\right),
 \qquad \varepsilon(t)\longrightarrow0 ,
\]
because the shifted form controls \(\int_M (6|dv|_{g_{t_0}}^2+v^2)dV_r\).
Divide the resulting two-sided comparison of the shifted forms by
\(\|v\|_{L^2(dV_r)}^2\) and take infima. This bounds
\(\lambda_1(t)+K\) between \((1-\varepsilon(t))(\lambda_1(t_0)+K)\)
and \((1+\varepsilon(t))(\lambda_1(t_0)+K)\), proving continuity.
One uses only continuity of the inverse metric and scalar potential,
with the common quadratic-form and measure domains.

By (1) the supplied set \(J\) is a connected interval if nonempty.
By (2) the continuous \(\lambda_1(t)\) has no zero there, so its sign
and the sign of \(Y_t\) are constant throughout \(J\). A negative
checkpoint forces the negative sign throughout \(J\), including
any portion with \(E(t)<4\pi^2\). With unknown sign, the positive-energy
gap gives \(Y_t\ne0\) on \(J\). Choosing the sign requires an anchor.
The sign-propagation argument applies within \(J\).
The earlier Bach-flow note derives the scalar-flat obstruction under
the same normalization for a different supplied path.
[Bach-flow sign argument][bach]

## Conditional finite-time certificate

Assume one independently certified \(Y_{t_*}>0\) at a supplied
\(t_*\in J\). For example, an independent proof of \(\lambda_1(t_*)>0\)
serves as that certificate. If the supplied path contains a time
\(t_{\mathrm{rec}}<1\) with \(E(t_{\mathrm{rec}})<4\pi^2\), then
\(t_{\mathrm{rec}}\in J\) and sign propagation gives
\(Y_{t_{\mathrm{rec}}}>0\). Apply Theorem A to that slice. Its
\(\mathbb{RP}^4\) alternative is excluded by simple connectivity, so
\(M\) is diffeomorphic to \(S^4\). When \(E_0>0\), the energy premise
is exactly \(t_{\mathrm{rec}}>t_{\mathrm{CGY}}\).
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A, p. 107][cgy]

Any supplied zero-energy slice invokes the exact recognition of
SH-E01. Smoothness makes \(W^+=0\) everywhere, and the signature
and developing map give conformal equivalence to the round sphere.
That conformal class is Yamabe-positive, as seen from the positive
round scalar curvature and the sign identity for \(\lambda_1\).
This handles \(E_0=0\) immediately. If a smooth \(t=1\) slice is
already supplied, it has zero energy and likewise fixes the sign in
any connected low-energy interval containing it.
[SH-E01 exact recognition][recognition]
