## Result and setting

On every smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere \(M\),
there is a smooth metric family \(r_L\), \(L\geq1\), with a flat
\(T^3\times[-L,L]\) collar, fixed caps and constant \(E_+\).
For the full fixed-volume Weyl derivative \(D_L\) at \(r_L\), write
\(P_L=D_LD_L^*\). Number its eigenvalues with multiplicity,
starting at \(j=1\), and write \(\lambda_j^+\) for the separate
enumeration starting at the first strictly positive eigenvalue. For
each fixed \(j\),

\[
 0\leq \lambda_j(P_L)\leq \lambda_j^+(P_L)\leq C_jL^{-4},\qquad
 \operatorname{Vol}(M,r_L)\,\lambda_j^+(P_L)=O_j(L^{-3}).
\]

In particular the bottom and first positive eigenvalue both tend to
zero on normalizing to unit volume. The positive bound uses test
tensors in \(\operatorname{im}D_L\), so it holds for any dimension of
the adjoint kernel in the family.

## Embedded product and fixed caps

In an oriented coordinate chart with Euclidean coordinates
\(\mathbb R^4\simeq\mathbb C^2\), take the product of two circles
with positive radii \(a,b\). The two radial unit normals
\((e^{i\theta},0)\) and \((0,e^{i\varphi})\) give its explicit
normal framing. The framed tubular map is

\[
 (\theta,\varphi,u,v)\longmapsto
 ((a+u)e^{i\theta},(b+v)e^{i\varphi}).
\]

It is a smooth embedding on the normal disk bundle of radius
\(\varepsilon+2\delta<\min(a,b)\), with \(0<2\delta<\varepsilon\).
All radii can be chosen small enough to fit in the chart. The subbundle
\(u^2+v^2\leq\varepsilon^2\) embeds \(T^2\times D^2\). On a two-sided
collar of its boundary set
\((u,v)=(\varepsilon+\delta t)(\cos\psi,\sin\psi)\). This embeds
\(T^3\times[-1,1]\), with each level separating the inner tube from
its exterior. The exterior is connected because the hypersurface is
connected and its outer collar meets every exterior component. In
detail, a component must meet the boundary in its closure by the
connectedness of \(M\), and the smooth collar places it in the component
of the connected outer side. The two caps are the closures of the
complementary components of the removed open collar.

Fix the flat torus metric \(k=d\theta^2+d\varphi^2+d\psi^2\).
On each cap, start with a smooth metric and interpolate by a smooth
cutoff to \(k+dt^2\) in the seam collar. Convexity of the cone of
positive quadratic forms gives a smooth positive cap metric. Keep
both caps fixed. For any \(L\geq1\), take
\(s_L(t)=t+(L-1)f(t)\), where \(f\) is smooth nondecreasing and
equals \(-1\) and \(1\) near \(-1\) and \(1\), respectively.
Then \(s_L'\geq1\), and \(s_L\) is a diffeomorphism onto \([-L,L]\)
with derivative one near the endpoints. The pullback of \(k+ds^2\)
by \(s=s_L(t)\) matches both cap metrics on the same smooth \(M\).
With \(A=\operatorname{Vol}(T^3,k)\) and \(V_{\rm caps}\) fixed,

\[
  V_L=\operatorname{Vol}(M,r_L)=V_{\rm caps}+2LA,\qquad
  E_+(r_L)=\int_{\rm caps}|W^+_{r_L}|_{\rm HS}^2\,dV_{r_L}.
\]

Only the caps contribute to this energy. The construction requires an
oriented chart and uses no identification of \(M\) with the standard
sphere.

## The Weyl derivative and the flat test fields

For a smooth reference \(r\), let \(F_r(h)\) be the \(W^+\)
operator of \(r(e^h\,\cdot,\cdot)\), transported by the positive
symmetric frame identification into \(S_0^2(\Lambda^+_r)\).
The variable \(h\) is any \(r\)-symmetric trace-free endomorphism.
This chart fixes \(dV_r\) pointwise. Define \(D_r=dF_r(0)\) and
take the adjoint using \(dV_r\), with the endomorphism Hilbert–Schmidt
pairings on both bundles. These are the [B02 choices][b02].

For a unit covector \(e^4\), let \(H\) be a trace-free symmetric
variation on its tangential three-plane, extended by zero on the
radial line. The derivative of the curvature operator under a flat
variation \(h=\phi(s)H\) has radial–radial bivector entries
\(-\tfrac12\phi'' H_{ij}\) and all other entries zero. The self-dual
orthonormal bivectors have radial projection of length \(1/\sqrt2\),
so the trace-free self-dual block is \(-\tfrac14\phi'' U H\), where
\(U\) is the resulting isometry from tangential trace-free matrices to
the Weyl target. The trace-free symmetrized-gradient symbol is the
orthogonal complement of these tangential matrices and is killed by
the flat Weyl symbol by diffeomorphism and conformal invariance at a
flat metric. Thus the flat calculation reproduces the symbol and norm
in [E02, §2, (5)][e02]. Away from flat geometry the principal symbol
is the same in a metric-orthonormal frame, since connection and
curvature terms and the variation of frame contribute only lower
orders. This gives \(\sigma_\xi(P_r)=|\xi|_r^4 I/16\) everywhere,
including on the caps. The ASD complex of Gover–Gursky gives a
comparison at ASD backgrounds. [Gover–Gursky §1.1, (1.2)][gg]

The nonnegative formally self-adjoint elliptic operator \(P_r\) on
the closed manifold has a discrete spectrum and a complete orthonormal
basis of smooth eigensections. Its form is
\(\langle P_rZ,Z\rangle=\|D_r^*Z\|_2^2\), so its finite-dimensional
kernel equals \(\ker D_r^*\). This uses the compact elliptic spectral
theorem for the complexified bundle, where the real and complex
multiplicities agree since the operator is real. [Ebert Theorem 3.8.1][ebert]

In the parallel product frame on the collar, \(D_L\) has only
constant-coefficient second derivatives. This follows by differentiating
curvature at a flat constant metric, where first derivative products
and moving-frame curvature terms vanish. The parallel \(H_0\) and
\(Z_0=UH_0\) can be chosen globally on the flat product with unit norm.
The flat symbol calculation and its transpose give, for functions of
\(s\) with compact support in the collar,

\[
  D_L(\phi H_0)=-\tfrac14\phi''Z_0,\qquad
  D_L^*(\phi Z_0)=-\tfrac14\phi''H_0.
\]

For any nonzero smooth \(\chi\) compactly supported in \((-1,1)\), set
\(Z_L=\chi(s/L)Z_0\) on the collar and extend by zero to \(M\).
Then

\[
  \frac{\|D_L^*Z_L\|_2^2}{\|Z_L\|_2^2}
   =\frac{1}{16L^4}
      \frac{\int_{-1}^1 |\chi''(x)|^2\,dx}
           {\int_{-1}^1 |\chi(x)|^2\,dx}.
\]

In particular, \(j\) profiles with disjoint supports give a
\(j\)-dimensional space on which the supremum of the quotient is the
largest individual quotient. The ordinary min–max bound counts zero
eigenvalues as well. To handle the positive spectrum, choose nonzero
\(\beta_i\in C_c^\infty(I_i)\) in pairwise disjoint intervals
\(I_i\subset(-1,1)\), \(1\leq i\leq j\), and use \(\chi_i=\beta_i''\).
Each is nonzero, and its test field has an exact compactly supported
preimage,

\[
 \chi_i(s/L)Z_0=D_L\bigl(-4L^2\beta_i(s/L)H_0\bigr).
\]

Thus the test space lies in \((\ker D_L^*)^\perp\). Apply min–max
on this subspace to get the same \(L^{-4}\) bound for every fixed
positive eigenvalue, independently of the kernel dimension. The
min–max inequality here follows by intersecting each \(j\)-dimensional
test space with the orthogonal complement of the first \(j-1\)
eigenvectors in the relevant enumeration and using the spectral basis.

## Constant metric rescaling and the inverse conclusion

For \(c>0\), identify the endomorphism domains by the identity and
the target bundles by the isometry induced by
\(\omega\mapsto c\omega\) on covariant self-dual two-forms. Under this
identification Weyl operators scale by \(c^{-1}\). The endomorphism
chart obeys \((cr)(e^h\,\cdot,\cdot)=c r(e^h\,\cdot,\cdot)\), and
the transporting isometry commutes with constant rescaling. Hence
\(F_{cr}=c^{-1}F_r\) and \(D_{cr}=c^{-1}D_r\). Both \(L^2\) pairings
acquire the same factor \(c^2\) in four dimensions, so
\(D_{cr}^*=c^{-1}D_r^*\) and \(P_{cr}=c^{-2}P_r\). For
\(\widehat r_L=V_L^{-1/2}r_L\) the volume is one and the eigenvalues
are multiplied by \(V_L\). Moreover
\(|W^+_{\widehat r_L}|^2\,dV_{\widehat r_L}
=|W^+_{r_L}|^2\,dV_{r_L}\), so the Weyl energy remains fixed.

For a unit target eigensection at a strictly positive eigenvalue
\(\lambda\), every solution of \(D_rH=Z\) obeys
\(1=\langle H,D_r^*Z\rangle\leq\|H\|_2\sqrt{\lambda}\).
Such a solution exists, for example \(H=\lambda^{-1}D_r^*Z\).
Thus the unit-volume family rules out a positive uniform lower bound
for the positive normal spectrum and a uniform all-target right
inverse bound, even on the range. The test fields themselves pair to
zero with the prescribed Weyl direction, which vanishes on their flat
supports. The solvability or norm of a radial lift in that prescribed
direction remains open here. The protocol required no Yamabe assumption.
