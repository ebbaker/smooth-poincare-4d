## Statement

Let \((X,g)\) be a supplied complete connected smooth oriented
Riemannian four-manifold with one ALE end modeled smoothly on
\((\mathbb R^4\setminus\overline{B})/\Gamma\), for a finite
\(\Gamma<SO(4)\) acting freely on \(S^3\). Require positive
differentiable order through two derivatives, in the sense
\[
 \left|\partial^\alpha(g_{ij}-\delta_{ij})\right|
        =O(r^{-a-|\alpha|}),\qquad a>0,\quad |\alpha|\le2
\]
on the Euclidean cover in end coordinates. Write \(K_R\) for a sufficiently
large full compact truncation of \(X\), with oriented boundary link
\(Y=S^3/\Gamma\). Suppose \(K_R\) smoothly embeds as a full core into
a closed smooth oriented integral homology four-sphere \(\Sigma\).
Use the core orientation induced by \(X\). Assume \(\tau(K_R)=0\),
which also follows from the embedding. Assume both chiral energies
in curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norms satisfy
\[
 E_\pm=\int_X|W^\pm_g|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV_g\le 8\pi^2-\delta
 \qquad(\delta>0).
 \tag{1}
\]
Then \(\Gamma=1\), \(K_R\) is an integral homology ball
and \(E_+=E_-\). The proof uses the smooth embedding, the
positive-order end and the separate energy bounds. It requires
no Ricci, scalar curvature or Bach equation. [B29][b29] · [SH-E01,
operator normalization][sh]

## The actual ambient space and the finite list

**Primary theorem.** J. A. Hillman's author-hosted *Locally flat
embeddings of 3-manifolds in \(S^4\)*, Theorem 5.2(1), printed p. 54,
explicitly takes an embedding into a **homology four-sphere** as
its ambient hypothesis. Its case of finite fundamental group gives
the possibilities \(1,Q(8),I^*=\mathrm{SL}(2,5)\), where the \(I^*\)
space form is the Poincaré homology sphere. Section 2.2, printed p. 13,
sets TOP locally flat embeddings as the default category and
separately defines Poincaré embeddings. Here restriction of the
given smooth full-core embedding to its boundary is an actual
locally flat embedding, as seen in a collar. The ambient
\(\Sigma\) satisfies the homology and orientation hypotheses,
regardless of its fundamental group or smooth type. The input
\(\pi_1(Y)=\Gamma\) is finite. [Hillman, §2.2 and Theorem 5.2(1)
and its finite-case proof][hillman]

The finite-case proof uses the direct-double property of the
link's abelianization to reduce free orthogonal \(S^3\) groups
to \(1,I^*,Q(8k)\) with \(k\ge1\). Although the exposition there
uses \(S^4\) while discussing the examples, the direct-double
necessity holds in the supplied ambient by the argument below.
The same proof identifies \(S^3/Q(8k)\) as an orientable circle
bundle over \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) of Euler number \(2k\), up to sign.
Its referenced Corollary 4.4.2, printed p. 47, explicitly
tests embeddability in a homology four-sphere. For a circle
bundle over \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) it permits only Euler numbers
\(\pm2\), so \(k=1\). This verifies the ambient in both steps of
the finite branch. In particular the direct-double conclusion
by itself would leave higher generalized quaternion groups.
The group \(I^*\) is the only remaining nontrivial perfect case.
[Hillman, finite case of Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 4.4.2][hillman]

The 1998 Crisp–Hillman article is the antecedent. Its accessible
publisher abstract is framed in \(S^4\). The theorem text used
here is the coauthor's later statement and proof with the
homology-four-sphere hypothesis. Chang–Ge's Lemma 4.14 states
the same necessary reduction and attributes it to Crisp–Hillman
Theorem 2.2. Their subsequent proposition has a Ricci-flat limit and
uses the Euler formula as well. Our exclusion below uses only the
signature identity and the inherited two chiral bounds in the
quaternionic case. [Crisp–Hillman, publication record][ch] ·
[Hillman, Preface and Theorem 5.2][hillman] ·
[Chang–Ge, Lemma 4.14 and Proposition 4.15][chg]

### What follows directly from the core and what uses smoothness

Write \(C\) for the closure of the other side in \(\Sigma\).
Both \(K_R\) and \(C\) are connected, since the full core and its
boundary are connected and any other component of the complement
would have empty boundary in connected \(\Sigma\). Give each
side its induced ambient orientation for the homological argument.
The spherical link has finite \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\) and
\(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\) by duality. Mayer–Vietoris across the collar
therefore gives
\[
 H_2(K_R;\mathbb Z)=H_2(C;\mathbb Z)=0,\qquad
 H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(K_R;\mathbb Z)\oplus H_1(C;\mathbb Z).
 \tag{2}
\]
The homology four-sphere fundamental class maps to the boundary
orientation class in \(H_3(Y;\mathbb Z)\) under the connecting map.
This follows by restricting to \(H_4(\Sigma,C)\cong H_4(K_R,Y)\)
and taking the relative boundary. It follows also that
\(H_3(K_R;\mathbb Z)=H_3(C;\mathbb Z)=0\).
The top absolute homology of each connected side vanishes since
the boundary is nonempty. Thus both sides are rational homology
balls, and the core signature is zero. By excision, the pair
sequence, relative duality and the universal coefficient theorem,
\[
 H_1(C;\mathbb Z)\cong H_2(\Sigma,C;\mathbb Z)
       \cong H_2(K_R,Y;\mathbb Z)
       \cong H^2(K_R;\mathbb Z)
       \cong \operatorname{Ext}(H_1(K_R;\mathbb Z),\mathbb Z).
\]
For the finite group in (2) the Ext group is abstractly isomorphic
to the original group. Hence \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong G\oplus G\).
This also rederives the ambient-independent part of the earlier
core audit. [E166 assessment][e166] · [Hillman, §2.3][hillman]

If \(Y\) is an integral homology sphere, (2) makes both sides
integral homology balls. Puncturing either side in its smooth
interior gives an integral homology cobordism with \(S^3\).
This is an implication of the given embedding; it need not
identify the complementary smooth structures or fundamental
groups. In the quaternionic case \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)\cong
(\mathbb Z/2)^2\) instead, and each side has first homology
\(\mathbb Z/2\); the rational homology conclusion remains.
[Hillman, §2.5 for homology cobordism and ambient change][hillman]

For the \(I^*\) candidate, Hillman's finite list identifies \(Y\)
topologically as the Poincaré homology sphere. The smooth
three-dimensional structure is the same up to diffeomorphism
and orientation, by the three-dimensional uniqueness of
compatible smoothing. It is the boundary, up to orientation,
of the smooth negative \(E_8\) plumbing of eight Euler number
\(-2\) disk bundles over \(S^2\), of signature \(-8\).
The spheres of that plumbing represent a basis of its second
homology, and each has even self-intersection. The plumbing is
simply connected, so these even intersections make it spin.
Equivalently one can evaluate \(w_2\) on those generators.
The core is spin because \(H^2(K_R;\mathbb Z/2)=0\), and the
shared integral homology-sphere boundary has a unique spin
structure. Gluing the core with the oppositely oriented spin
filling, choosing signs to match the boundary, gives a smooth
closed oriented spin four-manifold of signature \(\pm8\);
additivity also follows here from the orthogonal direct sum
on second homology since \(Y\) is an integral homology sphere.
Rokhlin divisibility by \(16\) for the closed smooth spin
manifold is a contradiction. This eliminates the perfect
candidate by the smooth category alone. The topologically
locally flat implication in Hillman's theorem leaves it
available, consistent with the topological contractible
fillings described in his Preface and §7.3.
[Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton, Theorem 3.1, Moise's theorem][moise] ·
[Kirby–Scharlemann, Descriptions (1) and (4) and their
identifications][ks] · [Rokhlin, differential-category
convention and §1.2][rokhlin] · [Hillman, Preface and §7.3][hillman]

Thus at the smooth spherical embedding stage the candidates
are \(S^3\) and \(S^3/Q_8\). These two do occur as smooth
hypersurfaces, for instance in the standard \(S^4\), in the
second case as the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of
an embedded \(\mathbb{RP}^2\). This example is only an
existence observation about the link. [Hillman, proof of
Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 4.4.2][hillman]

## APS normalization and passage to a positive-order ALE end

Let \(\eta_{\mathrm{sig}}(Y,\gamma)\) denote the value at zero
of the eta function of the odd signature operator acting on
**even differential forms** on the outward oriented boundary.
Zero eigenvalues are omitted. The compact APS signature theorem
(4.14) states, for a compact oriented Riemannian four-manifold
with metric exactly a product near its boundary,
\[
 \tau(K)=\int_K L_1-\eta_{\mathrm{sig}}(\partial K,\gamma).
 \tag{3}
\]
This is the topological signature of the image of relative
middle-degree cohomology in absolute cohomology. Its boundary
term is the unhalved even-form eta value with no kernel
dimension added. The all-form tangential operator convention
has twice this eta value and correspondingly uses a factor \(1/2\)
in (3). It is essential to use the same convention in the
curvature and round-link calculations.
[Atiyah–Patodi–Singer I, (1.7), (4.14) and following
even/odd comparison][aps1] · [Dai–Wei, §2 for the all-form
boundary formula][dai]

In the curvature-operator convention of (1) the pointwise
density is
\[
 L_1=\frac{1}{12\pi^2}
   \big(|W^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2-|W^-|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\big)dV .
 \tag{4}
\]
Indeed \(L_1=p_1/3=-(24\pi^2)^{-1}\operatorname{Tr}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)\)
for the oriented orthonormal connection curvature matrix
\(\Omega\). In a two-form frame the wedge pairing has signs
\((+I,-I)\) on the Hodge summands. The off-diagonal Ricci
terms cancel from \(\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal R^\dagger\star\mathcal R)\)
by equality of the two squared block norms, with \(\star\) the
Hodge endomorphism. The equal scalar blocks cancel as well and
each Weyl block is trace-free. Counting both orders of the
antisymmetric connection indices gives
\(\operatorname{Tr}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)
=-2(|W^+|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}-|W^-|^2_{\mathrm{HS}})dV\).
This yields (4) for all smooth positive metrics.
[Dai–Wei, §2, \(4\)-dimensional signature density][dai] ·
[SH-E01, norm conversion][sh]

For application to the actual end, first conformally rescale \(g\)
by a smooth positive factor equal to \(r^{-2}\) on the end.
This preserves each chiral Weyl energy density in dimension
four. In \(t=\log r\) coordinates the conformally rescaled end
metric \(g'\) is \(C^2\)-close to the cylinder
\(g_{\mathrm{cyl}}=dt^2+\gamma\), where \(\gamma\) is the unit round
quotient metric. More precisely the error in fixed product charts
on \([T-1,T]\times Y\) is \(O(e^{-aT})\) with its first two
derivatives. The powers follow by writing Euclidean coordinate
directions on a fixed sphere as \(r\) times smooth angular and
radial-log directions and applying the assumed derivative bounds.

On \(K_{e^T}\) keep \(g'\) up to \(t=T-1\), then use a fixed-width
smooth cutoff to interpolate from \(g'\) to \(g_{\mathrm{cyl}}\).
The interpolating metric is smooth, positive, and is exactly
the round product near its \(t=T\) boundary. The new collar
is uniformly \(C^2\)-close to the conformally flat cylinder
with error \(O(e^{-aT})\). Its Weyl energy tends to zero
on the fixed-width bounded-volume collar, with bound
\(O(e^{-2aT})\). Thus the chiral integrals in (4) for these
compact product-collar metrics tend to the original \(E_\pm\).
Their boundary metric is precisely \(\gamma\) for every large \(T\).
The signature is stable under adding the end collar.
Applying (3) and passing to the limit gives
\[
 \tau(K_R)=\frac{E_+-E_-}{12\pi^2}
                  -\eta_{\mathrm{sig}}(Y,\gamma).
 \tag{5}
\]
This construction supplies the product boundary condition directly,
with no spectral continuity or Ricci-flatness requirement for
the ALE passage. [Atiyah–Patodi–Singer I, (4.14)][aps1] ·
[Fefferman–Graham, §6 just after (6.7), Weyl weight][fg]

## Round quaternionic link and its energy defect

For a free orthogonal \(\rho:Q_8\hookrightarrow SO(4)\),
the central element \(-1\) acts by \(-I\): an orthogonal
involution has eigenvalues \(\pm1\), and freeness excludes \(+1\).
Let \(I=\rho(i)\) and \(J=\rho(j)\) for quaternion generators.
They are skew-orthogonal complex structures with
\(I^2=J^2=-I_{\mathbb R^4}\) and \(IJ=-JI\).
For any unit vector \(v\), the four vectors
\((v,Iv,Jv,IJv)\) are orthonormal. The orthogonality follows
from skew-adjointness of \(I,J,IJ\) and the quaternion
relations. In that basis \(I,J\) act as left multiplication
by \(i,j\) on \(\mathbb H\). This conjugates the action by
an isometry of \(\mathbb R^4\); the choice of sign of its
orientation changes the corresponding link orientation.
It proves the round isometry class up to the orientation
sign, rather than using a topological identification to
deduce the eta invariant.

For the quaternion orientation \((1,i,j,k)\), a generator of any
of the three order-four subgroups acts by oriented plane angles
\((\pi/2,\pi/2)\), up to simultaneous sign or oriented isometric
conjugacy. The common order-two subgroup acts by antipodal
angles \((\pi,\pi)\). APS II, Proposition (2.12),
for a free cyclic linear action on \(S^3\) gives the same
even-form eta convention as (3), with the finite sum
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{sig}}(S^3/C_m)
    =-\frac1m\sum_{\ell=1}^{m-1}
        \cot(\ell\theta_1/2)\cot(\ell\theta_2/2).
\]
For \(C_4\) this is \(-\tfrac14(1+0+1)=-\tfrac12\);
for the antipodal \(C_2\) it is zero. To assemble the
noncyclic quotient, for each eigenspace on the round
cover use the trace of its invariant projection
\(|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G}g\). The eta function of \(S^3/G\)
is thereby the group average of the equivariant eta
functions, first for large real part and then at zero
by continuation. The three order-four subgroups cover
\(Q_8\), and each contains the same order-two group.
The sum over \(Q_8\) is the sum over the three order-four
subgroups less twice the sum over their common order-two
subgroup. It follows that
\[
 \eta_{\mathrm{sig}}(S^3/Q_8)
     =\tfrac18\big(3\cdot4(-\tfrac12)-2\cdot2(0)\big)
     =-\tfrac34 .
\]
An orientation reversal negates the even-form
signature operator and its eta value, giving the
orientation-independent magnitude \(3/4\).
[Atiyah–Patodi–Singer II, §2, (2.1), Proposition (2.12)
and (2.13)–(2.14)][aps2]

Since the core signature is zero, equation (5) requires
\[
 |E_+-E_-|=12\pi^2\cdot\tfrac34=9\pi^2
 \qquad\text{if }\Gamma=Q_8 .
\]
Both energies are nonnegative, so (1) implies instead
\(|E_+-E_-|\le\max(E_+,E_-)\le8\pi^2-\delta\).
This excludes the quaternionic case and completes the
claimed nontrivial-link exclusion. In fact the same step would
work with any common energy ceiling strictly less than \(9\pi^2\).
For the surviving trivial link, the round \(S^3\) has eta zero
by an orientation-reversing isometry. Equations (2) and (5)
then give an integral homology ball core and equal chiral
energies. The calculation supplies no flatness, compactification
or extraction result for that survivor.
