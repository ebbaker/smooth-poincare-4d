## Conditional endpoint

Let \(M\) be a fixed smooth closed connected oriented homotopy
four-sphere, with smooth Riemannian reference metric \(r\). Let \(g_j\)
be smooth positive-definite metrics with \(dV_{g_j}=dV_r\) and
\(E_+(g_j)\to0\). We use the Hilbert–Schmidt norm on the curvature
operator block \(W^+\), and ASD means \(W^+=0\). Assume a uniform
\(C^{3,\alpha}\) coefficient bound in a fixed finite smooth atlas,
with \(0<\alpha<1\). For the pullback option, take
\(\widetilde g_j=\phi_j^*g_j\) with smooth orientation-preserving
diffeomorphisms \(\phi_j:M\to M\), and impose the bound together
with the pointwise volume hypothesis in the atlas for \(\widetilde
g_j\). The identity maps give the fixed-atlas option. The proof
uses only a uniform pointwise lower bound
\(dV_{\widetilde g_j}\ge c\,dV_r\), \(c>0\), with equality to
\(dV_r\) as the requested normalization. A total volume bound alone
would not provide this premise after arbitrary pullbacks.

There is a subsequence on the original smooth \(M\) converging in
\(C^{3,\beta}\) for every \(0<\beta<\alpha\) to a positive metric
\(g_\infty\) with both Weyl blocks zero. The limiting conformal
metric admits a \(C^{3,\beta}\) conformal diffeomorphism to the unit
round sphere. The same \(M\) admits a smooth diffeomorphism to \(S^4\).
The finite-regularity limit has positive first conformal eigenvalue
and positive Yamabe quotient infimum, and the smooth metrics are
Yamabe-positive for all sufficiently large \(j\).

## Compactness and the signature step

The coefficient bound and the fixed atlas bound the eigenvalues of
\(r^{-1}\widetilde g_j\) above by a uniform \(K\). Indeed, one can
work over smaller coordinate domains whose closures lie inside
the atlas charts, with all comparisons to \(r\) uniformly bounded.
The product of those eigenvalues is
\((dV_{\widetilde g_j}/dV_r)^2\ge c^2\). Each is therefore at least
\(c^2/K^3\). Arzelà–Ascoli on finitely many such domains yields
uniform convergence of coefficients and their derivatives through
order three along a subsequence. Interpolation between this
uniform convergence and the uniform \(\alpha\)-Hölder seminorm
gives convergence of the derivatives of order three in each
\(\beta\)-Hölder norm, \(\beta<\alpha\). The fixed smooth tensor
transitions preserve agreement on overlaps, giving a tensor
\(g_\infty\) on the same \(M\). The two-sided metric comparison
passes to the limit, as does the exact volume form under the
fixed-volume normalization.

Orientation-preserving pullbacks preserve both Weyl energies.
Before taking a limit, the smooth signature theorem applies to
every \(\widetilde g_j\). Since \(\tau(M)=0\), the operator-normalized
formula is

\[
 E_+(\widetilde g_j)-E_-(\widetilde g_j)=12\pi^2\tau(M)=0 .
\]

Consequently both energies tend to zero. Metric inverses, curvature,
the Hodge splitting and the respective tensor norms depend
continuously on the metric through the second jet with the uniform
positive lower bound. The volume measures also converge. Thus each
limiting Weyl block has zero squared integral and vanishes everywhere
by continuity. This invokes signature on the smooth sequence only.
[Norm and signature derivation in SH-E01][recognition] ·
[Gover–Gursky, §1, pp. 293–294][gg]

## The flat real tractor connection at the limit

Write \(g=g_\infty\) in the following construction. Adopt
\(R(X,Y)=[\nabla_X,\nabla_Y]-\nabla_{[X,Y]}\) and
\(R_{ab cd}=g(R(e_a,e_b)e_d,e_c)\). Define

\[
 P_{ab}=\tfrac12(\operatorname{Ric}_{ab}-s\,g_{ab}/6),\qquad
 C_{ab c}=\nabla_aP_{bc}-\nabla_bP_{ac}.
\]

The contracted differential Bianchi identity and
\(R=W+g\owedge P\) give
\(\nabla^aW_{ab cd}=(n-3)C_{cd b}\) in this index order.
In fact \(\nabla^aR_{ab cd}=\nabla_c\operatorname{Ric}_{db}
-\nabla_d\operatorname{Ric}_{cb}\); inserting the Schouten decomposition
and using \(\nabla^aP_{ab}=\nabla_b\operatorname{tr}_gP\) gives the
displayed identity. These computations involve at most three
derivatives of \(g\) and hold classically. Thus \(C=0\) in dimension
four when \(W=0\). [Lee–Parker, §2, eqs. (2.3)–(2.4) and the Weyl
decomposition, pp. 42–43][leeparker]

In the scale \(g\), represent the real standard tractor bundle by
\(\mathcal T=\mathbb R\oplus T^*M\oplus\mathbb R\) with its two
density slots in this scale. For \(z=(\sigma,\mu_b,\rho)\), use
the tractor metric \(H(z,z)=2\sigma\rho+g^{ab}\mu_a\mu_b\) and the
connection

\[
 D_a
 \begin{pmatrix}\sigma\\ \mu_b\\ \rho\end{pmatrix}
 =
 \begin{pmatrix}
 \partial_a\sigma-\mu_a\\
 \nabla_a\mu_b+P_{ab}\sigma+g_{ab}\rho\\
 \partial_a\rho-P_a{}^b\mu_b
 \end{pmatrix}.
 \tag{1}
\]

This preserves \(H\), which has signature \((5,1)\). The
Levi–Civita part preserves the middle pairing, the two terms
coupling \(\mu\) and \(\rho\) cancel in the metric derivative,
and the Schouten terms cancel as well.
In a smooth frame of the displayed bundle the connection
coefficients are \(C^{1,\beta}\), since \(P\) is \(C^{1,\beta}\).
The curvature is therefore defined classically. In coordinates,
expanding (1) gives zero top slot, middle slot
\(W_{ab c d}\mu^d+C_{ab c}\sigma\) and bottom slot
\(-C_{ab d}\mu^d\). The first-order terms from the off-diagonal
pieces cancel, the algebraic Schouten terms subtract the Schouten
part of the covector curvature, and differentiating \(P\) leaves
the Cotton terms. The connection is flat. These are the standard
tractor formulas, used here as classical formulas on the fixed
bundle. [Gover–Šilhan, §3.1, eqs. (10), (13)][gs]

On a small coordinate box, a \(C^{1,\beta}\) flat connection admits a
parallel frame. To see this at the given regularity, solve the
linear ODE along the first coordinate from a chosen frame at the
center, then extend successively in each next coordinate by
solving its parallel ODE with the preceding face as initial data.
The coefficients give \(C^1\) dependence on parameters.
Differentiating the ODE in an earlier coordinate shows that the
covariant derivative of the extending frame in that earlier
direction obeys the same homogeneous parallel ODE because the
curvature is zero. Its initial value vanishes, so the final frame
is parallel in every coordinate direction.
The equations \(\partial_a B=-\Gamma_a B\) for its frame matrix
\(B\) improve it to \(C^{2,\beta}\). Parallel frames differ by
constant matrices on each connected overlap component.
Transport defined with these frames depends only on path homotopy
relative to endpoints: subdivide a homotopy into pieces contained
in frame neighborhoods and cancel the constant transitions along
shared sides. Since \(M\) is simply connected, a single choice of
orthonormal frame at one point gives a global isometric parallel
trivialization \(T_x:\mathcal T_x\to V=\mathbb R^{5,1}\) of class
\(C^{2,\beta}\).

## Developing map and the original smooth manifold

In the chosen scale the canonical null ray has the nonzero lift
\(e=(0,0,1)\). In the parallel trivialization let \(v(x)=T_x e(x)\).
This is a nowhere-zero null vector at every point. From (1),

\[
 dv(X)=T_x(0,g(X,\cdot),0),\qquad
 H(dv(X),dv(Y))=g(X,Y),\qquad H(v,dv(X))=0 .
\]

The first formula also gives \(dv\) of class \(C^{2,\beta}\) in
smooth charts, hence \(v\in C^{3,\beta}\). Choose a constant unit
timelike \(t\in V\) with sign so that \(a(x)=-H(v(x),t)>0\).
Such a sign is global because a timelike vector has no nonzero
null vector in its orthogonal complement and \(M\) is connected.
Realize \(S^4\) as the unit sphere in the Euclidean space \(t^\perp\)
and define

\[
 \operatorname{dev}(x)=\frac{v(x)}{a(x)}-t\in S^4 .
\]

This is the canonical ray written in an affine section of the light
cone. Its derivative has first term \(a^{-1}dv\) and second term
\(-a^{-2} da\,v\). Since \(v\) is null and orthogonal to \(dv\),

\[
 \operatorname{dev}^*g_{\rm rd}=a^{-2}g .
 \tag{2}
\]

The derivative is injective by positive definiteness of \(g\), so
\(\operatorname{dev}\) is a \(C^{3,\beta}\) local diffeomorphism.
Compactness of \(M\) makes it proper and its image closed, while a
local diffeomorphism has open image. The image is all of \(S^4\).
A proper local diffeomorphism is a covering: fibers are compact
and discrete, hence finite, and disjoint local inverses around
their points yield evenly covered neighborhoods after shrinking
by properness. Connectedness of \(M\) and simple connectivity of
\(S^4\) give one sheet. The map therefore has a \(C^{3,\beta}\)
inverse on the original smooth \(M\).

For smooth recognition, regard \(\operatorname{dev}\) as a map
into \(\mathbb R^5\). Approximate its component functions in
\(C^1\) by smooth functions in the original smooth atlas, using
mollification and a smooth partition of unity. A sufficiently
close smooth approximation takes values away from the origin.
Radial normalization then gives a smooth map \(F:M\to S^4\) still
\(C^1\)-close to \(\operatorname{dev}\). Since the derivative of
\(\operatorname{dev}\) has a uniformly positive smallest singular
value relative to \(r\) on compact \(M\), \(dF\) has rank four
everywhere for a sufficiently close approximation. The same
proper-covering argument makes \(F\) a smooth diffeomorphism.
This constructs a smooth diffeomorphism independently of the
differentiability of the coefficients of \(g\) beyond \(C^{3,\beta}\).

## Yamabe sign and the negative radial tail

For a positive metric \(k\) of the indicated regularity, take
\(\Delta_k=-\operatorname{div}_k\nabla\), \(L_k=6\Delta_k+s_k\), and
the scalar quadratic form

\[
 Q_k(f)=\int_M(6|df|_k^2+s_k f^2)\,dV_k .
\]

Use real \(H^1(M,r)\) as common form domain, and define the first
eigenvalue by \(\inf_{f\ne0} Q_k(f)/\|f\|_{L^2(k)}^2\).
The Yamabe quotient infimum is
\(\inf_{f\ne0}Q_k(f)/\|f\|_{L^4(k)}^2\). On smooth metrics this
is the ordinary conformal Yamabe constant. Restriction to smooth
positive \(f\) gives the same infimum by density and approximation
of the absolute value of a smooth function by positive smooth
functions. For finite \(k\) the quotient gives the meaning of
the limit's Yamabe sign.

With \(u=a^{-1}>0\), (2) reads \(u^2g=\operatorname{dev}^*g_{\rm rd}\).
The scalar curvature of \(u^2g\) is \(12\) by local pullback of the
unit round metric. The \(C^{3,\beta}\) map and factor suffice for
the classical scalar-curvature change and naturality. Thus
\(L_g u=12u^3\). Substituting \(f=u(f/u)\) and integrating the
cross term by parts yields

\[
 Q_g(f)=6\int_M u^2 |d(f/u)|_g^2\,dV_g
             +12\int_M u^2 f^2\,dV_g .
 \tag{3}
\]

This computation holds for smooth functions and extends by
density to \(H^1(M,r)\). In particular the first eigenvalue is at
least \(12\min_M u^2>0\). Also \(Q_g\) controls the full \(H^1\)
norm: \(6\|df\|_{L^2(g)}^2\le Q_g(f)+\|s_g\|_\infty
\|f\|_{L^2(g)}^2\), and the \(L^2\) term is controlled by (3).
The Sobolev inequality on the compact manifold gives a strictly
positive Yamabe quotient infimum. [Lee–Parker, §2, pp. 42–43,
eqs. (2.6)–(2.7)][leeparker]

Along the convergent subsequence, \(C^2\) convergence and uniform
positive definiteness give
\(\left|Q_{\widetilde g_j}(f)-Q_g(f)\right|
\le o(1)\|f\|_{H^1(g)}^2\) uniformly in \(f\) by comparing
inverse metric, scalar potential and volume density. Thus
\(Q_{\widetilde g_j}(f)\ge c_0\|f\|_{H^1(g)}^2\) for a fixed
\(c_0>0\) and sufficiently large \(j\) in the subsequence.
This yields positive first eigenvalues and Yamabe constants
by comparability of the \(L^2\) and \(L^4\) norms. The same
argument applies to every \(C^2\)-nearby smooth metric. Both signs
are invariant under the pullbacks by change of variables. If
infinitely many metrics in the full sequence had nonpositive
Yamabe sign, applying the compactness argument to that subsequence
would give a further subsequence with positive sign. This proves
eventual positivity for the full sequence under the uniform
premises.

In the supplied radial-path setting of [SEC-E10][e10],
\(E_+(g_t)=(1-t)^2 E_+(r)\). A negative checkpoint on its connected
low-energy interval gives \(Y(M,[g_t])<0\) throughout that interval.
If this interval contains times tending to \(1\), no sequence
of such times can have both the fixed-atlas \(C^{3,\alpha}\)
coefficient bound and the required pointwise volume lower bound
in the allowed gauges. In the original fixed-volume atlas this
excludes a bounded \(C^{3,\alpha}\) subsequence along the negative
tail. The sign conclusion comes from the round conformal limit
and does not assume a positive initial scalar curvature.
