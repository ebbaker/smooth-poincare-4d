## Product curvature and topology

Let \(M=S^2\times S^2\) with its product orientation and smooth
Riemannian product \(r=g_1\oplus g_2\) of the unit sectional
curvature metrics. In an oriented orthonormal coframe \(e^1,e^2\)
on the first factor, \(e^3,e^4\) on the second, the curvature operator
is the orthogonal projection onto
\(\operatorname{span}(e^{12},e^{34})\) in \(\Lambda^2_r\),
where \(e^{ij}=e^i\wedge e^j\). The Ricci tensor is \(r\), so
\(s_r=4>0\). Use the two Hodge bases

\[
 \frac{e^{12}\pm e^{34}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \frac{e^{13}\mp e^{24}}{\sqrt2},\qquad
 \frac{e^{14}\pm e^{23}}{\sqrt2}.
\]

The diagonal curvature blocks have entries \((1,0,0)\).
Subtracting \((s_r/12)I=\tfrac13 I\) gives the spectrum of each
\(W^\pm_r\), namely \((2/3,-1/3,-1/3)\). This is the block convention
in [SH-B01][common] and the opening curvature decomposition in
[Gover–Gursky, p. 293][gg]. In operator Hilbert–Schmidt norms,

\[
 |W^+_r|^2_{\mathrm{op,HS}}=|W^-_r|^2_{\mathrm{op,HS}}=\frac23,
 \qquad \operatorname{Vol}(M,r)=(4\pi)^2=16\pi^2,\qquad
 E_+(r)=E_-(r)=\frac{32\pi^2}{3}.
 \tag{1}
\]

The product is closed and simply connected. Its second integral
cohomology has rank two. The pullbacks of the two unit integral
factor classes have square zero and product evaluating to one in
the product orientation, so the intersection matrix is
\(\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\1&0\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\) and
\(\tau(M)=0\). The product has \(\chi(M)=4\). It is spin because
each oriented tangent plane bundle of \(S^2\) has even Euler class,
hence vanishing second Stiefel–Whitney class, and the tangent bundle
of the product is the direct sum of their pullbacks.

## Stationarity in the full fixed-volume chart

Use the chart of [SEC-B01][b01] and [SEC-B02][b02].
For a smooth \(r\)-symmetric endomorphism \(h\) with
\(\operatorname{tr}h=0\) pointwise put \(g_h=r(e^h\cdot,\cdot)\).
The reference-to-metric tangent isometry \(A_h=e^{-h/2}\) preserves
orientation and gives the pullback \(I_h=A_h^*:\Lambda^+_{g_h}\to
E=\Lambda_r^+\). The volume is \(dV_{g_h}=dV_r\) since
\(\det(e^h)=1\). Define the transported Weyl operator and its
derivative by

\[
 F_r(h)=I_h W^+_{g_h} I_h^{-1}\in\Gamma(S^2_0 E),
 \qquad L=dF_r(0).
\]

Here \(S^2_0E\) is identified with symmetric trace-free endomorphisms.
The target pairing is \(\operatorname{tr}(AB)\) for symmetric \(A,B\)
in the reference metric, integrated against \(dV_r\). The domain
pairing is the reference Hilbert–Schmidt pairing of trace-free
symmetric endomorphisms, also integrated against \(dV_r\). These
define the formal \(L^2\) adjoint \(L^*\). No divergence condition
is imposed on \(h\). Because \(I_h\) is an isometry at every point,

\[
 E_+(g_h)=\|F_r(h)\|_{L^2(r)}^2,\qquad
 \left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_0 E_+(g_{th})
 =2\langle W^+_r,Lh\rangle_{L^2(r)}.
 \tag{2}
\]

On any closed oriented Riemannian four-manifold, the Chern–Gauss–Bonnet
formula and signature formula in these norms give

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 8\pi^2\chi(M)
 &=E_+(g)+E_-(g)
   -\frac12\int_M|\operatorname{Ric}_{0,g}|_g^2\,dV_g
   +\frac1{24}\int_M s_g^2\,dV_g,\\
 12\pi^2\tau(M)&=E_+(g)-E_-(g).
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{3}
\]

The first identity is [Chang–Gursky–Yang, p. 107, (0.4)][cgy],
using Remark 2's factor of four from the squared full four-index
Weyl norm to the squared operator norm. Gover–Gursky's signature
display uses the full tensor convention and accordingly has
\(48\pi^2\tau\) on its left. Its operator conversion is derived in
[SH-E01][recognition]. [Gover–Gursky, p. 294][gg]

Set \(k=\left.\tfrac{d}{dt}\right|_0g_{th}=r(h\cdot,\cdot)\).
All derivatives below are at \(r\), with \(\nabla\) its
Levi–Civita connection. Variation of the Christoffel symbols gives

\[
 \dot\Gamma^a_{ij}=\tfrac12r^{ab}
   (\nabla_i k_{jb}+\nabla_j k_{ib}-\nabla_b k_{ij}),\qquad
 \dot{\operatorname{Ric}}_{ij}
   =\nabla_a\dot\Gamma^a_{ij}-\nabla_j\dot\Gamma^a_{ia}.
\]

Contracting and varying the inverse metric therefore yields

\[
 \dot s=\nabla^i\nabla^j k_{ij}
        -\Delta_r(\operatorname{tr}_r k)
        -\langle \operatorname{Ric}_r,k\rangle_r .
 \tag{4}
\]

At the Einstein product both trace terms are zero, and the
remaining divergence integrates to zero on \(M\). The same
argument holds at any closed Einstein reference with constant
scalar curvature. Since the volume form stays fixed,
\(\tfrac{d}{dt}\int s^2\,dV=2s_r\int\dot s\,dV_r=0\) at the
reference. The trace-free Ricci tensor vanishes there, so the
first variation of its squared integral also vanishes. Differentiating
(3) now gives \(E_+'+E_-'=0\) and \(E_+'-E_-'=0\), hence
\(E_+'=0\). Equation (2) holds for every smooth trace-free \(h\),
and nondegeneracy of the domain pairing yields

\[
 L^*W_r^+=0 .
 \tag{5}
\]

For an explicit variation in the full chart, let \(h\) act
as the identity on the first factor and minus the identity on
the second. Then \(g_{th}=e^t g_1\oplus e^{-t}g_2\), with
factor curvatures \(e^{-t},e^t\). In the transported Hodge bases the
diagonal curvature blocks have first entry \((e^{-t}+e^t)/2\)
and other entries zero. Subtracting the scalar contribution
\(2(e^{-t}+e^t)I/12\) gives
\(F_r(th)=\cosh(t)\,W^+_r\). This variation has \(Lh=0\) and
\(E_+(g_{th})=E_+(r)\cosh^2(t)\).

## Obstruction to the prescribed initial tangent

Consider a path \(h(t)\) with \(h(0)=0\) in the full chart with
one-sided derivative at \(t=0\) in \(C^2\) or stronger, and
\(F_r(h(t))=(1-t)W^+_r\) for \(t\ge0\) near zero. The curvature
map and the pointwise isometric transport are differentiable from
these chart variables into \(C^0\). The required tangent satisfies
\(L\dot h(0)=-W^+_r\). But (5) and (1) give

\[
 0=\langle L^*W^+_r,\dot h(0)\rangle_{L^2(r)}
  =\langle W^+_r,L\dot h(0)\rangle_{L^2(r)}
  =-\frac{32\pi^2}{3}.
\]

The adjoint equality for a \(C^2\) tangent also follows directly
from the first variation above, or by smooth approximation in
\(C^2\). The radial path from the Einstein product therefore
cannot have the prescribed differentiable start even on the full
domain. The example tests the combination of Einstein,
positive scalar curvature, simple connectivity, spin and zero
signature. The product has \(b_2=2\) and \(\chi=4\). The radial
range on homotopy four-spheres remains a separate question.
This test settles the first variation at the specified product.
