## Flat local equation and subtraction of the particular response

Let \(B=\{|x|<1\}\subset\mathbb R^4\) be oriented as in B16 and
\(A=\{2/3<|x|<1\}\). Use the real chiral Weyl endomorphism spaces
\(S^2_0\Lambda^\pm_\delta\) in the fixed orthonormal frames, with the
operator Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. Write \(D_\pm=D_{\pm,\delta}\)
for the Euclidean Weyl derivatives, and suppose a smooth symmetric
metric variation \(u\) on \(B\) satisfies

\[
 D_+u=tf+e,\qquad t\in\mathbb R,\quad e\in L^2(B),\quad
 f=\chi(|x|)Z,\quad Z=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2).
\]

The prescribed \(\chi\) is the B16 smooth bump supported in
\(|x|\le1/2\). Put \(q=D_-u\). There is no boundary condition on
\(u\). The same proof applies to a distributional variation with
these equations and \(q\in L^2(A)\). If the latter norm for a smooth
variation is infinite, the desired bound holds trivially.
[B16 data][b16] · [B18 buffer decision][b18]

For \(\xi\ne0\), the assessed E21 symbols satisfy \(d_-=M d_+\)
with \(M(\xi)\) an isometry. On the TT symbol fiber each \(d_\pm\)
is an isometry times \(|\xi|^2/4\), and both annihilate the
orthogonal longitudinal and trace directions. It follows as
constant-coefficient operator identities that

\[
 D_-^*D_-=D_+^*D_+,\qquad D_-D_-^*=\frac{\Delta^2}{16}I.
\]

In particular the distributional equation on \(B\) is

\[
 \frac{\Delta^2}{16}\,q=D_-D_+^*(tf+e).
\]

Let \(T\) be the global flat \(L^2\) multiplier with symbol \(M\)
and let \(\widetilde e\) be the zero extension of \(e\).
For an \(L^2\) input on the whole Euclidean space the identities
\(d_-^*M=d_+^*\) and \((|\xi|^4/16)M=d_-d_+^*\) give

\[
 D_-^*T=D_+^*,\qquad (\Delta^2/16)T=D_-D_+^*
\]

in distributions. Polynomial multiplication of an \(L^2\) Fourier
field gives the corresponding tempered derivative, so the
identities away from \(\xi=0\) suffice. In particular the choice
of the multiplier at \(\xi=0\) has no effect. Derivatives of the
zero extension give the original distributional derivatives on
\(B\), since tests there have support disjoint from the boundary.
Thus

\[
 w=q-tTf-T\widetilde e\quad\text{on }B
\]

satisfies \(\Delta^2w=0\) on all of \(B\), including the seed
support and the center. In fact \(D_-^*w=0\) by the first
identity for \(T\) and \(D_-^*D_-=D_+^*D_+\).
Constant-coefficient elliptic regularity for \(\Delta^2\) makes
\(w\) smooth on \(B\). Plancherel gives
\(\|T\widetilde e\|_{L^2(\mathbb R^4)}=\|e\|_{L^2(B)}\).
[E21, (3)–(6) and flat \(L^2\) identification][e21]

## The degree-four projection and the explicit positive distance

Write \(n=x/|x|\) and \(Y(n)=M(n)Z\). E23 identifies
\(\rho^4Y(n)\) as a componentwise harmonic quartic, so the
sphere Laplacian satisfies \(\Delta_{S^3}Y=-24Y\).
The norm \(|Y(n)|_{\rm HS}^2=|Z|_{\rm HS}^2=6\) is constant.
With the ordinary sphere volume \(dS\), put

\[
 N=\int_{S^3}|Y|_{\rm HS}^2\,dS
   =6|S^3|=12\pi^2 .
\]

The angular orthogonal projection of \(w(\rho n)\) onto the
single direction \(Y\) is \(c(\rho)Y(n)\), with
\(c(\rho)=N^{-1}\int_{S^3}\langle w(\rho n),Y(n)\rangle_{\rm HS}\,dS\).
The flat Laplacian under this projection becomes
\(\mathcal L_4=\partial_\rho^2+3\rho^{-1}\partial_\rho-24\rho^{-2}\)
on the coefficient. Indeed the sphere Laplacian is self-adjoint
and \(Y\) is its eigenfunction. Hence \(\mathcal L_4^2c=0\)
on \((0,1)\). Its Euler equation on \(\rho^\gamma\) has roots
\(4,-6,6,-4\), from
\((\gamma(\gamma+2)-24)((\gamma-2)\gamma-24)=0\).
They are distinct. Smoothness of \(w\) at the center makes \(c\)
bounded there, excluding both negative powers. Therefore
\(c(\rho)=c_4\rho^4+c_6\rho^6\).
[E23, homogeneous harmonic quartic][e23]

By E22, on \(A\) the response \(Tf\) is \(\phi(\rho)Y(n)\), where

\[
 \phi(\rho)=a_0\rho^{-4}-a_2\rho^{-6},\qquad
 a_0=\frac{6M_0}{\pi^2},\qquad a_2=\frac{12M_2}{\pi^2},\qquad
 M_j=\int_{\mathbb R^4}|x|^j\chi(|x|)\,dx\quad(j=0,2).
\]

Put \(b=2/3\) and \(\mathcal R=\operatorname{span}\{\rho^4,\rho^6\}\).
The norm of the projected field in \(L^2(A)\) is \(\sqrt N\)
times the coefficient norm in \(L^2((b,1),\rho^3d\rho)\).
Consequently, with this radial inner product,

\[
 d=\sqrt{12\pi^2}\operatorname{dist}(\phi,\mathcal R),\qquad
 \|q-T\widetilde e\|_{L^2(A)}
   =\|tTf+w\|_{L^2(A)}\ \ge\ |t|d .
\]

Here is the Gram formula with no numerical evaluation. Let
\(I_\nu=\int_b^1\rho^{\nu-1}d\rho=(1-b^\nu)/\nu\) for
\(\nu\ne0\), with \(I_0=\log(1/b)\). Then

\[
 G=\begin{pmatrix} I_{12}&I_{14}\\ I_{14}&I_{16}\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 v=\begin{pmatrix}a_0I_4-a_2I_2\\ a_0I_6-a_2I_4\end{pmatrix},
 \qquad
 d^2=12\pi^2
 \big(a_0^2 I_{-4}-2a_0a_2 I_{-6}+a_2^2 I_{-8}-v^{\mathsf T}G^{-1}v\big).
\]

The Gram matrix is positive definite by independence of the two
regular powers. The distance is strictly positive because
\(a_0>0\) for the prescribed seed and the distinct powers
\(\rho^{-4},\rho^{-6},\rho^4,\rho^6\) are linearly independent
on the annulus. The triangle inequality and the \(L^2\) isometry
of \(T\) now give the desired exterior-independent estimate

\[
 \boxed{\quad \|q\|_{L^2(A)}+\|e\|_{L^2(B)}\ \ge\ |t|d .\quad}
\]

The distance \(d\) is attained for the relaxed problem of adding
an arbitrary smooth biharmonic field \(w\) to \(Tf\). Choose
\(w=-\Pi_{\mathcal R}\phi\,Y\), which is smooth and biharmonic on
\(B\) because both \(\rho^4Y\) and \(\rho^6Y\) have those
properties. The metric-response residual satisfies \(D_-^*w=0\)
as well. The calculation does not establish that the relaxed
optimizer arises from a metric variation, or that the robust
bound is sharp for such variations. [E22, exterior field][e22]

## Round patch and conditional nonlinear consequence

On a supplied round coordinate patch with \(r=\Omega^2\delta\),
\(\Omega=2/(1+\rho^2)\), use \(u=\Omega^{-2}h\) for a covariant
variation \(h\) of \(r\). For each chirality in the induced
orthonormal frames one has
\(D_{\pm,\delta}u=\Omega^2D_{\pm,r}h\). This holds independently
of the round or flat divergence gauge. Use
\(f_r=\Omega^{-2}f\) and suppose \(D_{+,r}h=tf_r+e_r\).
Then \(e=\Omega^2e_r\), and multiplication of a curvature
endomorphism by \(\Omega^2\) in these frames is an \(L^2\) isometry
on each region, since \(dV_r=\Omega^4dx\). The same \(d\) therefore
gives

\[
 \|D_{-,r}h\|_{L^2(A,r)}+\|e_r\|_{L^2(B,r)}\ge |t|d .
\]

No geometry outside this patch is used. [E21, conformal identity (2)][e21]

For the fixed-volume chart put \(g_h=r(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\)
for \(r\)-trace-free covariant \(h\). Let \(F_{r,\pm}(h)\) be
\(W^\pm_{g_h}\) in the fixed target under the positive-symmetric
frame isometry. On the patch \(F_{r,\pm}(0)=0\). In its fixed local
coordinates the Weyl operator and this transport are smooth
functions of the two-jet of \(h\), with uniformly bounded
second jet derivatives near the zero section over the chart
closure. This follows from differentiating the smooth matrix
exponential, inverse metric and the usual first- and
second-derivative expressions for curvature. Taylor expansion gives

\[
 F_{r,\pm}(h)=D_{\pm,r}h+R_\pm(h),\qquad
 \|R_\pm(h)\|_{C^0(B,r)}\le C\|h\|_{C^2(B,r)}^2
\]

for \(C^2\)-small \(h\), with \(C\) depending only on the fixed
local baseline and norms. This expansion requires no solution
operator. If \(F_{r,+}(h)=tf_r\) exactly on the patch, take
\(e_r=-R_+(h)\) in the linear estimate and absorb both residuals
using the fixed chart volumes. Since the frame transport is
isometric and \(dV_{g_h}=dV_r\), this yields

\[
 \|W^-_{g_h}\|_{L^2(A,g_h)}
 \ge |t|d-C'\|h\|_{C^2(B,r)}^2.
\]

The positive block vanishes on \(A\) for this prescribed source.
For a supplied family with \(\|h\|_{C^2}^2=o(|t|)\), the
opposite block is nonzero there in \(L^2\) for small nonzero
\(t\). Existence of such a family on an arbitrary exterior is a
separate premise. [B16 marking and target][b16] · [B18 scope][b18]
