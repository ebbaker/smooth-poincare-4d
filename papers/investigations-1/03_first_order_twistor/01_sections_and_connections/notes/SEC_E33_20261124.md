## Fixed data and local adjoint tests

Work in the unit Euclidean ball \(B\subset\mathbb R^4\), oriented as
in B16, with background \(\delta\). Write
\(B_\delta^+=S^2_0\Lambda_\delta^+\) for the positive Weyl target,
using the real curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm and ASD
convention \(W^+=0\). Write \(D_+=D_{+,\delta}\) for the flat Weyl
linearization on symmetric trace-free covariant metric tensors and
\(D_+^*\) for its Euclidean formal adjoint. Fix a nonzero real
constant \(Z\in B_\delta^+\) in the normalized self-dual frame, and a
smooth signed radial scalar function supported in \(B_{1/2}\), written
\(x\mapsto\chi(|x|)\). Let \(f=\chi Z\). The unnormalized Euclidean
moments are
\[
 m_0=\int_{\mathbb R^4}\chi(|x|)\,dx,\qquad
 m_2=\int_{\mathbb R^4}|x|^2\chi(|x|)\,dx .
 \tag{1}
\]
No sign or size condition is imposed on either moment.
[B20 compact-lift test][b20] · [B16 orientation and frames][b16]

There is a compactly supported smooth trace-free metric lift
\(u\in C_c^\infty(B;S^2_0T^*)\) with \(D_+u=f\) exactly when
\(m_0=m_2=0\). For the necessity, the two local adjoint fields are
\(Z\) and \(|x|^2Z\). To check the quadratic field, associate to
\(Z\) the algebraic Weyl tensor \(T^Z_{ij,pq}\) obtained by extending
the self-dual block by zero on \(\Lambda_\delta^-\). Its Ricci
contractions vanish, and it has the algebraic curvature symmetries.
For example the total alternation is proportional to the contraction
of the matrix of \(Z\) with the wedge pairing of the normalized
self-dual two-forms, hence to \(\operatorname{tr}Z=0\). The normalized
two-form matrices have quaternionic anticommutators, so the Ricci
contraction of their symmetric coefficient combination is proportional
to \((\operatorname{tr}Z)\delta\). This is the pure self-dual Weyl block
in the curvature decomposition. [Gover–Gursky, p. 293][gg]

For a variable target \(z\), let \(T^z\) be the same four-index
realization. Using \(R_{ij,pq}=\langle R(e_i,e_j)e_q,e_p\rangle\),
the flat curvature derivative is
\[
 R'_{ij,pq}=\tfrac12(\partial_i\partial_q u_{jp}
         +\partial_j\partial_p u_{iq}
         -\partial_i\partial_p u_{jq}
         -\partial_j\partial_q u_{ip}).
\]
In the pairing with a Weyl tensor the Ricci and scalar subtractions
vanish. The two antisymmetric index pairs are counted once in the
operator norm, so pairing \(R'\) with \(T^z\) in all ordered indices
uses a factor \(1/4\). Interchanging dummy indices and using the
two skew symmetries gives the pointwise pairing
\(-\tfrac12\sum_{i,j,p,q}T^z_{ij,pq}\partial_i\partial_p u_{jq}\).
Twice integrating by parts on compact metric tests gives
\[
 (D_+^*z)_{jq}=-\tfrac12\sum_{i,p}\partial_i\partial_p T^z_{ij,pq}.
 \tag{2}
\]
Pair symmetry and vanishing contractions make this tensor symmetric
and trace-free. The derivative of a constant target is zero. For
\(z=|x|^2 Z\), the expression is
\(-\sum_i T^Z_{ij,iq}=0\). This uses the same local double-divergence
adjoint as in E21, with the operator-pairing normalization fixed here.
[E21, compact correction test and flat curvature symbol][e21] ·
[Gover–Gursky, p. 295, formal adjoint][gg]

Pair \(D_+u=f\) against those two adjoint fields on \(B\). The two
left hand sides vanish by compact support of \(u\). The right hand
sides are \(|Z|_{\rm HS}^2m_0\) and \(|Z|_{\rm HS}^2m_2\), respectively.
This proves necessity for the unrestricted trace-free metric domain.

## Construction when both moments vanish

The flat operators have only homogeneous second-order terms. E21
gives a scalar of absolute value \(|\xi|^2/4\) times an isometry
from the TT symbol fiber onto the positive Weyl target at \(\xi\ne0\).
Its kernel is the orthogonal complementary longitudinal symbol
space in trace-free tensors. Thus the Fourier symbol of
\(D_+D_+^*\) is \(|\xi|^4 I/16\). These polynomial symbols agree
also at \(\xi=0\), so with \(\Delta=\sum_i\partial_i^2\),
\[
 D_+D_+^*=\tfrac1{16}\Delta^2 I_{B_\delta^+} .
 \tag{3}
\]
The product is obtained from the full trace-free domain through
the symbol kernel and needs no boundary TT hypothesis.
[E21, (3)–(5)][e21]

Take \(G(x)=-\log|x|/(8\pi^2)\), so \(\Delta^2G=\delta_0\) as a
distribution with the E22 Fourier convention. The convolution
\(p=G*\chi\) is a smooth scalar potential because the compact
smooth factor can absorb all derivatives. It solves \(\Delta^2p=\chi\).
If \(R\le1/2\) contains the support radius, the exact E22
exterior potential is
\[
 p(x)=-\frac{1}{8\pi^2}
            \left(m_0\log|x|+\frac{m_2}{4|x|^2}\right)
     \quad (|x|>R) .
 \tag{4}
\]
For vanishing moments this potential vanishes for \(|x|>R\).
Define the smooth trace-free tensor
\[
 u=16D_+^*(p Z).
 \tag{5}
\]
It is compactly supported within \(B\) by locality, and (3)
gives \(D_+u=\chi Z\). This proves sufficiency and yields a lift
supported no farther out than the support radius of the source.
[E22, biharmonic fundamental solution and exterior potential][e22]

On the round ball \(r=\Omega^2\delta\), \(\Omega=2/(1+|x|^2)\),
the flat lift \(u\) represents the round covariant variation
\(h=\Omega^2u\). On the induced orthonormal Weyl frames,
\(D_{+,\delta}(\Omega^{-2}h)=\Omega^2D_{+,r}h\). Thus the round
source is \(f_{\mathrm{round}}=\Omega^{-2}\chi Z\), and the same
compact support classification holds. The covariant tensor
weight preserves trace-freeness and support. There is no boundary
condition besides compact support in this statement.
[E21, linearized conformal weight (2)][e21]

## Center moments of the global cokernel on a round patch

Let \(g\) be a supplied smooth real Riemannian background on a
closed oriented smooth four-manifold, with a marked coordinate
ball \(B\) where \(g\) equals the round model \(r\). Outside it the
background may vary arbitrarily. Use E03's derivative \(L_g\)
of the positive Weyl operator in its fixed-frame pointwise-volume
chart, with the full trace-free symmetric metric domain. Its
cokernel is represented by the finite smooth space
\(\mathcal C_g=\ker L_g^*\), and its range is the reference
\(L^2\) orthogonal complement of that space. Regard
\(f_{\mathrm{round}}\) as a global target by extending it by zero.
The round local derivative equals \(L_g\) on the chart because
their backgrounds agree there. [E03, normal operator and range (4)][e03]

For \(A\in\mathcal C_g\), let \(a\) be its components in the
induced orthonormal positive round frame on \(B\), and set
\(z=\Omega^2 a\) in the corresponding constant flat frame.
For a compact trace-free \(h\) in the round ball,
\[
 0=\int_B\langle L_g h,A\rangle_g\,dV_g
   =\int_B\langle D_{+,\delta}(\Omega^{-2}h),z\rangle_\delta\,dx .
 \tag{6}
\]
One factor \(\Omega^{-2}\) in the round output and the volume
factor \(\Omega^4\) explain the weight \(\Omega^2\) on the
adjoint field. All compact trace-free flat tests occur in (6).
Consequently \(D_+^*z=0\) on \(B\), and (3) gives \(\Delta^2z=0\)
there. In particular the same conformal weight in the source
pairing gives
\[
 \langle f_{\mathrm{round}},A\rangle_{L^2(g)}
   =\int_B\chi(|x|)\langle Z,z(x)\rangle_{\rm HS}\,dx .
 \tag{7}
\]
[E21, conformal comparison and local round adjoint test][e21]

The componentwise spherical average \(b(\rho)=\dashint_{S^3}
z(\rho n)\,dS(n)\) satisfies
\(b''+3b'/\rho=(\Delta z)(0)\). Indeed \(\Delta z\) is harmonic
on \(B\), so its average is its center value. The regular
initial conditions \(b(0)=z(0)\) and \(b'(0)=0\) give
\(b(\rho)=z(0)+\rho^2(\Delta z)(0)/8\). Substitution into (7)
therefore yields the exact moment map
\[
 \boxed{\displaystyle
 \langle f_{\mathrm{round}},A\rangle_{L^2(g)}
 =\left\langle Z,\ m_0 z(0)+\frac{m_2}{8}(\Delta z)(0)
                \right\rangle_{\rm HS}.}
 \tag{8}
\]
The center Laplacian here is the flat componentwise Laplacian of the
weighted adjoint representative \(z=\Omega^2a\).

On all radial polarizations, including their finite sums, (8)
uses the image of the linear center-data map
\(A\mapsto (z(0),(\Delta z)(0)/8)\) in the ten-dimensional
space \(B_\delta^+\oplus B_\delta^+\), five real entries per
factor. For a fixed polarization it uses at most two scalar
pairings. The two radial moment functionals are independent:
choose nonnegative smooth radial bumps supported in two disjoint
annuli with separated squared-radius ranges, so their ratios
\(m_2/m_0\) differ. The local adjoint equation admits every
center-data pair through \(z(x)=Z_0+|x|^2 Z_2\), with arbitrary
constant Weyl matrices \(Z_0,Z_2\). Thus ten is the available
local dimension, while the global image may have smaller rank.
By E03 the prescribed global source has a smooth full-domain
linear lift precisely when \((m_0 Z,m_2 Z)\) is orthogonal to
that image. The explicit compact construction already gives
a global lift at zero moments. [E03, range and smoothness][e03]

## Exterior distinction and finite nonlinear scope

The flat relative multiplier of E21 has harmonic quartic
numerator by E23. For the present data the flat opposite field
on the region beyond the support radius is therefore
\((6/\pi^2)(m_0\rho^{-4}-2m_2\rho^{-6})M(x/\rho)Z\), where
\(M\) is an isometry from the positive to the negative Weyl
target. Its exterior field is identically zero exactly when
both moments vanish. A nonzero exterior signal excludes a
compact metric lift by the local test. A global lift can still
exist. On the closed unit round sphere the positive cokernel
is trivial, while a positive radial bump compactly supported
in \(B_{1/2}\) has \(m_0,m_2>0\) and a nonzero opposite response
on the southern exterior by E21–E23. For other backgrounds
the actual obstruction is the image test in (8).
[E21, global round lift][e21] · [E22, radial response][e22] ·
[E23, angular isometry][e23]

For a zero-moment compact lift \(h\) on the round chart, extended
by zero, the volume-preserving metric \(g(e^{t h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\)
has transported positive Weyl increment
\(F_g(th)-F_g(0)=t f_{\mathrm{round}}+O(t^2)\) in each fixed finite
smooth grade. This follows from local finite-jet Taylor expansion
in the fixed frame. The remainder is supported in \(\operatorname{supp}h\)
since the metric and its jets agree with the background on the complement.
This optional observation realizes the source with a compact
correction of order \(t^2\). The compact classification and the
global moment map above are linear results. [E03 chart][e03]
