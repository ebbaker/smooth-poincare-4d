## Statement and normalization

Let \(M\) be a smooth closed connected oriented homotopy four-sphere
and \(K\) a closed coordinate four-ball with the smooth oriented unit
round marking of E37. Suppose \(r\) is a smooth positive Riemannian
metric realizing that marking with \(Y(M,[r])=0\). In every \(C^4\)
neighborhood of \(r\) there is a smooth marked reference \(g\) with
\(dV_g=dV_r\) and \(Y(M,[g])=0\) whose full trace-free linearization
\(L_g\) is surjective at each fixed Sobolev grade \(s\ge5\). The
reference change can be supported in the open exterior \(M\setminus K\)
with positive separation from \(K\).

Use the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt pairing on the positive
Weyl block and write
\[
  E_+(g)=\int_M |W_g^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\,dV_g,\qquad
  \mathfrak B(g)=\int_M |B_g|_g\,dV_g .
\]
The Bach tensor \(B_g\) is the covariant tensor in the E24 convention.
In the full \(g\)-trace-free logarithmic chart
\(g_h=g(e^{h^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\), the density is \(dV_g\),
and symmetric isometries identify the moving positive Weyl block
with the fixed bundle. Denote the resulting map by \(F_g(h)\)
and its linearization at zero by \(L_g\). The statement concerns
the moved references and the radial source in each reference's
own chart. [E24][e24] · [E40][e40] · [E41][e41]

## Radial strain from the first variation

For trace-free \(h\), isometric comparison and the fixed density give
\[
 dE_+(g)[h]=2\int_M\langle W_g^+,L_gh\rangle\,dV_g .
 \tag{1}
\]
On a homotopy four-sphere the signature identity gives \(E_+=E_-\).
The squared covariant four-index norm is four times the squared
full operator norm, so the total four-index Weyl integral is
\(8E_+\). The first variation in the E24 convention is therefore
\(dE_+(g)[h]=-\frac12\int_M\langle B_g,h\rangle_g\,dV_g\).
[E24, (1)][e24] · [Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, §1][bgim]

If \(L_g h=-W_g^+\), (1) is \(-2E_+(g)\). Consequently
\[
   4E_+(g)=\int_M\langle B_g,h\rangle_g\,dV_g
       \le \mathfrak B(g)\,\|h\|_{L^\infty(g)} .
   \tag{2}
\]
The formula applies to smooth \(h\), and to \(H^{s+2}\) solutions
for \(s\ge5\) by smooth approximation, the boundedness of \(L_g\)
and the Sobolev embedding into \(C^0\). For a differentiable
radial lift \(F_g(h(t))=(1-t)W_g^+\) through \(h(0)=0\),
its initial strain \(h'(0)\) satisfies the same equation and bound.
Surjectivity and the fixed-reference normal slice supply an exact
local lift for each fixed smooth regular reference. [E40, (7)–(8)][e40]

## The exact eigenvalue level

Take the nonnegative Laplacian \(\Delta_r=-\operatorname{div}_r\nabla\)
and put \(H_r=6\Delta_r+R_r\). Its first eigenvalue is the infimum
of the quotient \(Q_r(f)/\|f\|_{L^2(r)}^2\), with
\[
 Q_r(f)=\int_M (6|df|_r^2+R_rf^2)\,dV_r,\qquad
 Y(M,[r])=\inf_{f\ne0} Q_r(f)/\|f\|_{L^4(r)}^2 .
\]
The sign equivalence used here follows directly from these forms.
If \(\lambda_1>0\), then \(Q_r(f)\ge\lambda_1\|f\|_2^2\) and
\(6\|df\|_2^2\le Q_r(f)+\|R_r^-\|_\infty\|f\|_2^2\).
The compact-manifold Sobolev inequality gives \(Y>0\).
If \(\lambda_1=0\), the form is nonnegative and a first
eigenfunction has Yamabe quotient zero. If \(\lambda_1<0\),
the first eigenfunction gives a negative Yamabe quotient.
For existence, a normalized minimizing sequence for \(\lambda_1\)
is bounded in \(H^1\) by the scalar lower bound, has a strongly
convergent subsequence in \(L^2\), and attains the infimum by weak
lower semicontinuity. Its absolute value is also a minimizer.
Elliptic regularity and the strong minimum principle give a
smooth strictly positive first eigenfunction \(u\). For the latter
apply the principle to \((\Delta_r+C)u\ge0\) with
\(C\ge \max\{0,\sup(R_r-\lambda_1)/6\}\).
For smooth \(f\) integration by parts using \(H_ru=\lambda_1 u\) gives
\[
 Q_r(uf)-\lambda_1\|uf\|_2^2
       =6\int_M u^2|df|_r^2\,dV_r .
 \tag{3}
\]
Connectedness implies simplicity. The same identity shows that any
positive eigenfunction realizes the first eigenvalue. [Lee–Parker,
§1 and §2, strong maximum principle][lp] · [E37, sign comparison][e37]

For a fixed smooth finite-parameter family \(\xi\mapsto r_\xi\)
preserving \(dV_r=\nu\), take a fixed \(s\ge5\), normalize
\(\int u^2\nu=1\), and apply the Banach implicit function theorem to
\[
 (\xi,f,\ell)\longmapsto
       (H_{r_\xi} f-\ell f,\ \int_M f^2\nu-1)
 \quad\text{in }H^s\times\mathbb R,\qquad f\in H^{s+2}.
 \tag{4}
\]
The coefficients depend smoothly on \(\xi\) in fixed smooth charts.
At \(r\) with \(\lambda_1=0\), the derivative in \((f,\ell)\) is
\((\dot f,\dot\ell)\mapsto (H_r\dot f-\dot\ell u,2\langle u,\dot f\rangle_2)\).
The component along \(u\) determines \(\dot\ell\), and the remaining
forcing determines \(\dot f\) in \(u^\perp\) by the bounded inverse of
\(H_r:u^\perp\cap H^{s+2}\to u^\perp\cap H^s\). This inverse follows
from self-adjoint elliptic Fredholmness and the kernel description
in (3). The scalar second output determines the component of
\(\dot f\) along \(u\). The resulting local eigenpair is smooth in the
parameters and smooth in space by elliptic regularity. Its eigenfunction
stays positive by Sobolev embedding into \(C^0\), so its eigenvalue
is exactly the first one by (3). [Melrose, Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose]

At the zero level set \(k=u^2r\). The conformal scalar formula in
dimension four is \(R_{u^2r}=u^{-3}H_r u\), so \(k\) is scalar-flat.
Differentiating the eigenpair at \(\lambda_1=0\) and testing with \(u\)
annihilates the eigenfunction derivative by self-adjointness. Thus
\(d\lambda_1(r)[h]\) equals the derivative of \(Q_r(u)\) with \(u\)
fixed and \(\|u\|_{L^2(r)}=1\). In fact, for a fixed \(u\) the conformal
scalar identity gives
\(Q_{r_\xi}(u)=\int_M R_{u^2r_\xi}\,dV_{u^2r_\xi}\).
For \(q=\dot k=u^2h\), variation of the connection gives
\(\dot\Gamma^i_{j\ell}=\frac12k^{ip}(\nabla_jq_{\ell p}
+\nabla_\ell q_{jp}-\nabla_p q_{j\ell})\). Contracting its Ricci
variation and including the inverse-metric variation gives
\(\dot R_k=-\langle\operatorname{Ric}_k,q\rangle_k
+\nabla_i\nabla_jq^{ij}-\nabla^i\nabla_i\operatorname{tr}_k q\).
The two divergence terms integrate to zero and the volume variation
is multiplied by \(R_k=0\). Hence
\[
 d\lambda_1(r)[h]
    =-\int_M\langle\operatorname{Ric}_k,u^2h\rangle_k\,dV_k .
 \tag{5}
\]
Without \(L^2(r)\)-normalization there is the additional denominator
\(\int_M u^2\,dV_r\). [Lee–Parker, (2.6)–(2.7)][lp]

There is a point in \(M\setminus K\) with \(\operatorname{Ric}_k\ne0\).
Indeed, if \(\operatorname{Ric}_k=0\) throughout the open exterior,
the Schouten formula for \(B\) makes \(k\) Bach-flat there. On the
interior of the cap \(k\) is conformally flat by its conformal
relation to the round \(r\), so it is Bach-flat there as well.
Smoothness gives global Bach-flatness. In dimension four this is the
obstruction-flat equation. Lassas–Liimatainen Theorem 1.4 applies to
the connected smooth positive Riemannian metric on the entire
boundaryless manifold with full Weyl vanishing on the open cap, so
\(W_k=0\) globally. The closed simply connected conformally flat
manifold has a global conformal developing map into the round
sphere, which is a covering by compactness and a diffeomorphism
because the sphere is simply connected. Its conformal class then
has positive Yamabe constant, contradicting the zero level.
[E24, recognition][e24] · [SH-E01, developing map][recognition]
· [Bach formula (1.1)][bgim] ·
[Lassas–Liimatainen, §3.2 and Theorem 1.4][ll]

Choose a nonnegative smooth bump \(\chi\) with compact support in an
exterior ball about a point of nonzero Ricci tensor and with
\(\chi>0\) there. Put \(w=\chi u^{-2}\operatorname{Ric}_k\), a
smooth \(r\)-trace-free tensor since \(R_k=0\). Then (5) gives
\(d\lambda_1(r)[w]=-\int\chi|\operatorname{Ric}_k|_k^2dV_k<0\).

## Full rank while retaining the zero level and the marking

The restriction of the old \(\mathcal C=\ker L_r^*\) to \(M\setminus K\)
is injective even if the round marking is assumed only on the
closed cap. A smooth element supported in \(K\) has all jets zero
at its boundary, by continuity from the open exterior. Transplant
by the oriented round marking and extend by zero to the
standard round sphere. It is smooth, solves the round adjoint
equation on the interior and exterior and therefore at the
boundary, and vanishes by round adjoint injectivity. Thus the
exterior probes and rank calculation of E41 still apply. All
perturbing supports are compact in the open exterior. The
column argument of E40 uses the normal inverse and the old
pairing matrix with this injectivity as needed for the probes.
[E41, exterior restriction and (9)–(13)][e41] · [E40, (3)–(5)][e40]
· [Gover–Gursky, §1.1, round unobstructedness][gg]

Write \(\mathscr A(j)\) for the compared linearization at
\(r(e^{j^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\) in that metric's own symmetric
fixed-density chart, transported to the \(r\)-bundles. Its derivative
\(\mathscr A'(0)[j]\) is linear in \(j\), by the smooth coefficient
calculus of E40. For \(m=\dim\mathcal C>0\), take the E41 perturbation
\(v_N\), exact old kernel columns \(k_{j,N}\), and a basis
\(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) of \(\mathcal C\). The matrix
\(\langle Z_i,\mathscr A'(0)[v_N]k_{j,N}\rangle_{L^2(r)}\)
tends to the invertible \(-D/4\) of E41. In that construction
\(\|v_N\|_\infty=O(N^{-2})\) and \(\|k_{j,N}\|_{L^2(r)}=O(1)\).
The latter bound follows from the bounded first derivative of the
probe vector fields and the strongly vanishing \(L^2\) projection
correction. Formula (5) gives \(d\lambda_1(r)[v_N]=O_r(N^{-2})\).
Set
\[
 \beta_N=-\frac{d\lambda_1(r)[v_N]}{d\lambda_1(r)[w]}=O_r(N^{-2}).
\]
For fixed \(w,Z_i\), the formal adjoint
\((\mathscr A'(0)[w])^*Z_i\) is a smooth tensor, independent of \(N\).
Thus the change in each mixed matrix entry on replacing \(v_N\)
by \(v_N+\beta_Nw\) has absolute value at most
\[
 |\beta_N|\,\|(\mathscr A'(0)[w])^*Z_i\|_{L^2(r)}
         \|k_{j,N}\|_{L^2(r)}=O_r(N^{-2}) .
 \tag{6}
\]
The matrix remains invertible for sufficiently large finite \(N\).
Fix one such \(N\) before constructing the metric curve.

On the two-parameter family
\(r_{a,b}=r(e^{(av_N+bw)^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\), (4) gives a smooth
\(\lambda_1(a,b)\) and the derivative in \(b\) is nonzero at
\((0,0)\). The scalar implicit function theorem supplies \(b=b(a)\)
with \(b(0)=0\), \(b'(0)=\beta_N\) and \(\lambda_1(a,b(a))=0\)
identically near zero. The first derivative of the compared
linearization along the curve is exactly the one used in (6).
The desingularized columns \(A_a k_{j,N}/a\) therefore extend
smoothly to zero and give the E40 surjectivity for each sufficiently
small \(a\ne0\). Their complements use \(A_a L_r^*\) as in E40.
All these metrics preserve \(dV_r\) and equal \(r\) near \(K\).
Surjectivity at one grade makes the smooth adjoint kernel zero.
The normal elliptic inverse then gives surjectivity at all the
stated grades for the same metric.
For \(m=0\) the reference itself is surjective and satisfies the
constraints. The choices and implicit neighborhoods are per
reference. [E40, (3)–(5)][e40] · [E41, (1), (11)–(13)][e41]

## Sequences with actual radial strain

For the E37/E38 marked sequence \(r_R=g_R\) on the standard sphere,
\(Y([r_R])=0\), \(E_+(r_R)=8\pi^2+O(R^{-1})\) and
\(\mathfrak B(r_R)=O(R^{-1})\). At each \(R\) choose the above regular
reference \(g_R^{(0)}\) sufficiently close in \(C^4\) to make each
error in \(E_+\) and \(\mathfrak B\) at most \(1/R\).
These functionals are continuous in \(C^4\) near each fixed smooth
positive metric by the curvature and Bach jet formulas, continuity
of the fiber norms, and compactness of \(M\). The choice retains
the exact zero sign, volume form of \(r_R\), and round marking.
There are constants \(C_B>0\) and \(R_0\) with
\(\mathfrak B(g_R^{(0)})\le C_B/R\) and \(E_+(g_R^{(0)})\ge4\pi^2\)
for \(R\ge R_0\). Surjectivity supplies \(h\) for
\(L_{g_R^{(0)}}h=-W_{g_R^{(0)}}^+\), and (2) forces
\(\mathfrak B(g_R^{(0)})>0\) and
\[
 \inf_{\,L_{g_R^{(0)}} h=-W_{g_R^{(0)}}^+}
           \|h\|_{L^\infty(g_R^{(0)})}
 \ \ge\ \frac{16\pi^2}{C_B}\,R .
 \tag{7}
\]
The estimate is on preimages of the actual radial target.
[E37, (6) and exact sign][e37] · [E38 audit][e38]

For a positive analogue use the marked E34 positive joins, for
example the common marked positive endpoints of E37. The E34
plateau test is supported on the neck, away from the local flat
replacement. Pullback and conformal covariance transfer its
quotient bound to the marked class. Thus the starting points have
\(0<Y\le C R^{-1/2}\), \(E_+=8\pi^2+O(R^{-1})\) and
\(\mathfrak B=O(R^{-1})\). The smallest eigenvalue is continuous
in \(C^2\) at each fixed metric, by comparing the relatively close
shifted coercive Rayleigh forms and their denominators as in E37.
The positive sign is open. Apply E41/E40 without an eigenvalue
constraint, choosing the regular reference close enough in \(C^4\)
to maintain positivity, the same two curvature tolerances, and a
change of at most \(1/R\) in the quotient of the transferred test
for this \(R\). The resulting marked \(g_R^{(+)}\) have
\(0<Y([g_R^{(+)}])=O(R^{-1/2})\) and a bound as in (7), with a
possibly different constant. [E34, plateau estimate and marking][e34]
· [E37, shifted forms and common marking][e37]

Both sequences approach the energy \(8\pi^2\). A subcritical region
\(E_+\le8\pi^2-\delta\) with fixed \(\delta>0\) lies outside these
estimates, as do uniform eigenvalue gaps, finite radial steps and
motions without a differentiable initial tangent. The regular
reference perturbation enforces the sign and marking constraints.
The radial tangent bound allows all full trace-free preimages at
each reference. The rank argument varies a single scalar constraint,
\(\lambda_1\).
