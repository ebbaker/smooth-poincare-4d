## Reflection and the chiral frames

Orient the Euclidean \(\mathbb R^4=\mathbb H\) by the ordered orthonormal
frame \((1,\mathbf i,\mathbf j,\mathbf k)=(e_0,e_1,e_2,e_3)\).
For cyclic \((i,j,k)\) in \((1,2,3)\), use the orthonormal frames

\[
 \beta_i^\pm=\frac{e^0\wedge e^i\ \pm\ e^j\wedge e^k}{\sqrt2}
 \quad\text{of }\Lambda^\pm.
\]

These are the \(\pm1\) Hodge eigenspaces for the stated orientation.
For unit \(n\) let \(R_n=I-2n\otimes n\). Its orthogonal action on
two-forms exchanges the Hodge eigenspaces. Write
\(Q_n=\Lambda^2 R_n|_{\Lambda^+}\) as a map \(\Lambda^+\to\Lambda^-\)
in the two indicated frames. For a symmetric trace-free
endomorphism \(Z\) of \(\Lambda^+\), use \(M_nZ=Q_nZQ_n^{-1}\).

In Euclidean signature identify a skew endomorphism \(B\) with
the two-form \((v,w)\mapsto\langle Bv,w\rangle\).
For \(u\in\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H\), left multiplication by
\(u\) corresponds to \(\sqrt2\,\beta_u^+\) and right multiplication
by \(u\) to \(\sqrt2\,\beta_u^-\), where the subscript denotes
linear extension in the coordinates of \(u\). For example,
left and right multiplication by \(\mathbf i\) both take \(1\)
to \(\mathbf i\), and they take \(\mathbf j\) to \(\mathbf k\)
and \(-\mathbf k\), respectively. The other cases follow by cyclic
permutation.

On quaternions \(R_n(q)=-n\bar q n\), obtained by conjugating the
real-axis normal reflection \(R_{e_0}(q)=-\bar q\) by left multiplication
by \(n\). Since \(\bar u=-u\), conjugating left multiplication by \(u\)
by \(R_n\) gives \(q\mapsto-q\bar n u n\). Thus, if
\(O(n):u\mapsto\bar n u n\) is the rotation of the imaginary
quaternions, the frame matrix of the chiral map is

\[
 Q_n=-O(n),\qquad M_nZ=O(n)Z\,O(n)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

The rotation \(O(n)\) preserves the Euclidean product and the quaternion
cross product on the imaginary space. In particular \(Q_n\) is
orthogonal with determinant \(-1\) between these indexed frames.
Conjugation preserves symmetry, trace and the Hilbert–Schmidt pairing.
Hence \(M_n\) is an isometry of the two five-dimensional trace-free
Weyl spaces.

At \(n=e_0=1\), \(Q_{e_0}=-I\) and \(M_{e_0}Z=Z\). At
\(n=e_1=\mathbf i\), \(Q_{e_1}=\operatorname{diag}(-1,1,1)\)
and \(M_{e_1}Z=DZD\) for \(D=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,-1)\).
In particular the entries \(Z_{12}\) and \(Z_{13}\) change sign,
while the diagonal and \(Z_{23}\) are preserved. The checks use the
same forms \(\beta^\pm\) at both axes. If the positively oriented
coframe is ordered as \((e^1,e^2,e^3,\widehat e^4)\) in [E02 §2][e02],
the identification \(e^0=-\widehat e^4\) makes
\(e^0\wedge e^i=e^i\wedge\widehat e^4\) and preserves the
orientation and chiral labels. The minus sign of \(Q_{e_0}\)
is a frame sign and cancels in the endomorphism action.

## Homogeneous harmonic quartic

For any \(x=x_0+b\in\mathbb H\), with \(b\) imaginary, define the
real quadratic matrix \(L(x)\) by \(L(x)u=\bar xux\). For the matrix
\([b]_\times u=b\times u\), multiplication gives

\[
 L(x)=(x_0^2-|b|^2)I+2bb^{\mathsf T}-2x_0[b]_\times .
\]

For \(x\ne0\), \(L(x)=|x|^2O(x/|x|)\). The matrix-valued polynomial

\[
 P_Z(x)=L(x)ZL(x)^{\mathsf T}
\]

therefore extends \(|x|^4 M_{x/|x|}Z\) homogeneously to the origin.
For the Euclidean Laplacian \(\Delta=\sum_{\mu=0}^3\partial_\mu^2\),
the terms of \(L\) give \(\Delta L=(-4+4)I=0\). Consequently

\[
 \Delta P_Z
 =2\sum_{\mu=0}^3(\partial_\mu L)\,Z\,(\partial_\mu L)^{\mathsf T}.
\]

At \(e_0\), \(\partial_0L=2I\) and \(\partial_iL=-2J_i\) with
\(J_i u=e_i\times u\). Contracting the two alternating symbols gives
\(\sum_iJ_iZJ_i^{\mathsf T}=(\operatorname{tr}Z)I-Z^{\mathsf T}\),
since \(\sum_i\epsilon_{aic}\epsilon_{bid}
=\delta_{ab}\delta_{cd}-\delta_{ad}\delta_{bc}\).
Thus \(\Delta P_Z(e_0)=8(Z+(\operatorname{tr}Z)I-Z^{\mathsf T})=0\).
For a unit quaternion \(a\), \(L(xa)=O(a)L(x)\), so
\(P_Z(xa)=O(a)P_Z(x)O(a)^{\mathsf T}\).
Right multiplication by \(a\) in \(\mathbb R^4\) is orthogonal,
so the Laplacian has the same equivariance. Hence \(\Delta P_Z\)
vanishes at every unit quaternion. This homogeneous quadratic
polynomial consequently vanishes everywhere.

For each choice of \(Z\) all entries of \(P_Z\) are homogeneous harmonic
quartics, including the possibility of zero. Taking \(Z\) in any
basis proves the statement for the entries of \(M\) as an operator
on the trace-free symmetric space. In the homogeneous decomposition
\(H_4+|x|^2H_2+|x|^4H_0\), with \(H_j\) harmonic of degree \(j\),
the Laplacian of the last two terms is \(16H_2+24|x|^2H_0\).
A second Laplacian gives \(192H_0\). Thus harmonicity removes
both the degree-zero and degree-two angular pieces.

## Oriented transition at the opposite pole

Use the \(x\) chart of [B16][b16] and take an opposite-pole chart
\(y=Jx/|x|^2\), with \(J=R_{e_0}\) the same fixed reflection in
the coordinate model. With \(n=x/|x|\),
\(dy=|x|^{-2}JR_n\,dx\) and
\(\Omega(y)=|x|^2\Omega(x)\) for \(\Omega(z)=2/(1+|z|^2)\).
The change of round orthonormal coframes, written from \(x\) to
\(y\) components, is therefore \(JR_n\). It has determinant \(+1\)
and preserves chirality. Raw inversion has derivative proportional
to \(R_n\), with determinant \(-1\) in the orthogonal factor, and
exchanges chirality. The extra fixed reflection ensures that both pole
frames respect the specified manifold orientation.

Let \(B_-(n)=\Lambda^2(JR_n)|_{\Lambda^-}\) denote the resulting
change of anti-self-dual components. Since \(R_n^2=I\),

\[
 B_-(n)Q_n=\Lambda^2J|_{\Lambda^+}=Q_{e_0}=-I,\qquad
 B_-(n)(M_nZ)B_-(n)^{-1}=Z .
\]

In the last expression \(Z\) is the same numerical matrix, now in
the anti-self-dual \(y\) frame. This is the algebraic cancellation of
the angular action at the pole. Radial amplitude, actual metric
response and its regularity belong to separate packets.
