## 1. Mixed equations and differential orders

Let \(S(h)=\dot\Sigma\) in the symmetric frame. Its components are
\[
 S_i(h)(X,Y)=\tfrac12\{\Sigma_i(h^\sharp X,Y)+\Sigma_i(X,h^\sharp Y)\}.
\]
The derivative of the wedge normalization gives a skew matrix of
self-dual coefficients. The induced endomorphism on two-forms from
\(h^\sharp\) is self-adjoint, so that matrix is also symmetric and hence
zero. Thus \(S_i(h)\in\Lambda_r^-\). The map
\(S:S^2_0T^*\to E\otimes\Lambda_r^-\) is an isomorphism. Indeed, for
diagonal \(h\) in an oriented orthonormal frame with eigenvalues
\(\lambda_j\), the \(e^i\wedge e^4\) coefficient of \(S_i\) is
\((\lambda_i+\lambda_4)/2\). If all these coefficients vanish,
trace freeness forces every \(\lambda_j=0\), and the two spaces both
have dimension nine.

Define the zeroth-order operators
\[
 \mathcal B a=[a,\Sigma],\qquad
 \mathcal P u=\operatorname{Sym}_0
    \left(\frac{u^i\wedge\Sigma_j}{2\nu}\right)_{ij}.
\]
Here \(\mathcal B:\Omega^1(E)\to\Omega^3(E)\) is invertible pointwise.
With \(\mathscr C=*\mathcal B\) the same frame algebra as in
`rec:compatibility` gives \(\mathscr C^2+\mathscr C-2I=0\), so
\(\mathcal B^{-1}=(\mathscr C+I)*/2\) with the star on three-forms.
This calculation uses a normalized triple. [CME compatibility][cme]

The definition \(C_{ij}\nu=-F_A^i\wedge\Sigma_j/2\) gives
\(\dot C_{ij}\nu=-((d_\Gamma a)^i\wedge\Sigma_j+F_\Gamma^i\wedge S_j)/2\).
The second wedge term vanishes at round curvature because \(F_\Gamma^i\)
is self-dual and \(S_j\) is anti-self-dual. The linearized map with
\(a=\dot A\) is therefore
\[
 L_a(h,a)=\big(d_\Gamma S(h)+\mathcal B a,\,-\mathcal P d_\Gamma a\big).
\tag{1}
\]
The compatible connection variation \(\mathcal H h=\dot\Gamma^+(g_t)\)
satisfies
\[
 \mathcal H h=-\mathcal B^{-1}d_\Gamma S(h),\qquad
 (\mathcal H h)_{ib}=\tfrac12\eta_{i,cd}\nabla_ch_{bd},\qquad
 D=-\mathcal P d_\Gamma\mathcal H .
\tag{2}
\]
The first identity differentiates \(d_{\Gamma^+(g_t)}\Sigma_t=0\).
The frame formula is `metric:H-definition`. The last identity follows
from `rec:chiral-curvature`, since the scalar term in the compatible
\(C=W^++(s/12)I\) projects to zero. [First-order manuscript,
`eq:chiral-first-order`][fot] · [CME frame and curvature identities][cme]

For \(A=\Gamma^+(g_t)+b\), let \(\beta=\dot b=a-\mathcal H h\).
Equation (1) becomes
\[
 L_b(h,\beta)=\big(\mathcal B\beta,\,Dh-\mathcal P d_\Gamma\beta\big).
\tag{3}
\]
With compatibility source \(u\in\Omega^3(E)\) and curvature source
\(f\in\Gamma(S^2_0 E)\), pointwise elimination gives
\[
 \beta=\mathcal B^{-1}u,\qquad
 Dh=f+\mathcal P d_\Gamma\mathcal B^{-1}u,\qquad
 a=\mathcal H h+\mathcal B^{-1}u.
\tag{4}
\]
In \(a\)-variables the equations contain at most one derivative of each
independent field, with an essential algebraic term in the compatibility
row. In \(b\)-variables the first row is algebraic and the second has
order two in \(h\) and one in \(\beta\). The natural mixed source orders
are \(u\in H^{s+1}\) and \(f\in H^s\) for \(h\in H^{s+2}\) and
\(a,\beta\in H^{s+1}\).

## 2. Mixed symbol and the Weyl symbol

For \(\xi=t e^4\ne0\) in an oriented \(r\)-orthonormal coframe, the
TT symbol slice consists of the trace-free symmetric three-by-three
matrix \(h_{ij}\) on \(\xi^\perp\). Take
\(\Sigma_i=e^i\wedge e^4+\tfrac12\epsilon_{ijk}e^j\wedge e^k\).
Then \(\eta_{i,4j}=-\delta_{ij}\). Equation (2) gives
\(\sigma_\xi(\mathcal H)h=-\frac{t}{2}\sum_j h_{ji}e^j\) in row \(i\).
The self-dual component of \(e^j\wedge e^4\) is \(\Sigma_j/2\). Thus
\[
 \sigma_\xi(D)h=-\frac{|\xi|^2}{4}(h_{ij})
 \quad\text{on the TT symbol slice}.
\tag{5}
\]
The matrix is in the orthonormal Weyl target frame obtained by dividing
the two-forms \(\Sigma_i\) by \(\sqrt{2}\). In particular its squared
singular value is \(|\xi|^4/16\).

Writing \(\mathcal KX=(\mathcal L_Xr)_0\), its symbol has
\(\iota_{\xi^\sharp}\sigma_\xi(\mathcal K)X
=|\xi|^2X^\flat+\tfrac12\xi(X)\xi\). This splits the trace-free
symbol domain as the TT slice plus the image of
\(\sigma_\xi(\mathcal K)\). Since \(D\mathcal K=0\) at the ASD metric,
(5) shows that the four-dimensional latter space is exactly
\(\ker\sigma_\xi(D)\), and \(\sigma_\xi(D)\) is onto the
five-dimensional Weyl target. [Gover–Gursky, §1.1][gg]

At the orders in (4), the mixed symbol in \(a\)-variables is
\[
 (h,a)\longmapsto
 \big(\xi\wedge S(h)+\mathcal B a,\,-\mathcal P(\xi\wedge a)\big).
\tag{6}
\]
In \(b\)-variables it takes the equivalent form
\((h,\beta)\mapsto(\mathcal B\beta,\sigma_\xi(D)h-
\mathcal P(\xi\wedge\beta))\), with input change
\(\beta=a-\sigma_\xi(\mathcal H)h\).
Eliminating \(a\) in (6) gives exactly (5) with the differentiated
compatibility source. Hence (6) is an isomorphism on the TT symbol slice,
with real domain and target dimension 17. Before the TT condition the
kernel is the four-dimensional graph of \(\sigma_\xi(\mathcal H)\)
over \(\operatorname{im}\sigma_\xi(\mathcal K)\). For comparison,
the equal-order first-order symbol of (1) drops \(\mathcal B a\).
Then \(\xi\wedge S(h)=0\) already forces \(h=0\) because each
\(S_i(h)\) is anti-self-dual. Its connection kernel has dimension
seven, consisting of the three components \(a^i_4\) and the skew and
trace parts of \((a^i_j)_{i,j\le3}\). These modes disappear in the
TT mixed-order symbol (6).

## 3. Local projective variations and Frobenius symbol

On the stereographic patch write \(r=\Omega^2\delta\), with \(\delta\)
Euclidean. Choose three constant positive \(\delta\)-orthogonal
complex structures with directions distinct modulo sign, for example a
quaternionic triple. For each \(J\), use its linear complex coordinates
\(z_1,z_2\) with Euclidean metric \(\sum_j|dz_j|^2\). Write
\(U_j=\partial_{\bar z_j}\), \(Z_j=\partial_{z_j}\), and
\(\widehat h=\Omega^{-2}h\). At first order the null plane is the
graph spanned by \(U_a+t\sum_b B_a{}^b Z_b+O(t^2)\). The
positive symmetric transport of the plane contributes
\(H_{ab}=-\widehat h(U_a,U_b)\), because
\(\delta(U_a,Z_b)=\delta_{ab}/2\) and the transport velocity is
\(-h^\sharp/2\). At fixed metric a graph is null to first order if its
graph matrix is skew. Thus the free vertical projective variation has
entries \(B_1{}^2-H_{12}=v\), \(B_2{}^1-H_{21}=-v\), with diagonal
entries fixed by \(H\). The complex coordinate \(v\) identifies the
two real vertical tangent directions. Reality determines the variation
of the conjugate null plane.

The coordinate fields commute at the baseline. The linearized
Frobenius component on the ordered pair \((U_1,U_2)\), identified with
a coefficient pair in the \(Z_b\), is
\[
 \mathcal F_J(h,v)=M_J(h)-(U_1v,U_2v),\qquad
 M_J(h)=(U_1H_{21}-U_2H_{11},\,U_1H_{22}-U_2H_{12}).
\tag{7}
\]
This is an exact first-order graph formula on the chart with the stated
\(\widehat h\). A local change of projective coordinate amounts to a
change of coordinates on the same vertical tangent. [First-order
manuscript, `eq:projective-frobenius`][fot]

For a real \(\xi\ne0\), set \(p_j=\xi(U_j)\). The pair \(p\) is
nonzero because the conjugates of \(U_1,U_2\) supply the other half
of the complexified tangent space. The vertical symbol \(-v p\) is
injective over the reals, with complex line image. Identify its complex
one-dimensional quotient by \(\ell_p(c)=p_1c_2-p_2c_1\).
Projecting the metric symbol gives
\[
 \ell_p\,\sigma_\xi(M_J)h
 =p_1^2H_{22}-2p_1p_2H_{12}+p_2^2H_{11}.
\tag{8}
\]
The quotient symbol has order two through this chosen quotient map.
It equals twice the symbol of the flat Weyl contraction on
\(U_1\wedge U_2\), evaluated on \(\widehat h\), by (9) below.
The conversion from the round to the flat Weyl endomorphism is a
nonzero conformal rescaling with the same Hodge splitting. Hence (8)
is the corresponding null quartic evaluation of \(\sigma_\xi(D)h\)
up to that scale and the chosen bivector normalization.

For a real trace-free symmetric endomorphism of \(\Lambda^+\), the
quadratic contraction restricts to a quartic on its complex projective
null conic. The Plücker bivectors of a positive \(J\) and \(-J\)
are conjugate null lines. Three directions distinct modulo sign
therefore give six distinct zeros if all evaluations vanish. The
quartic is then identically zero. Vanishing on the conic forces this
endomorphism to be scalar, as can also be checked by evaluating on
\(e_j+i e_k\) for an orthonormal real basis and \(j\ne k\). Trace
freeness makes it zero. Thus the three evaluations embed the
five-real-dimensional Weyl target into \(\mathbb C^3\). For a
quaternionic triple, choosing bivectors proportional to
\(e_2+i e_3,e_3+i e_1,e_1+i e_2\) or their appropriate conjugates
exhibits the relation as the sum of the three normalized real parts.
[Gover–Hill–Nurowski v3, Remark 5.2, Proposition 5.3 and (19)][ghn]

Consequently projection to \(h\) of the homogeneous three-section
symbol kernel yields exactly \(\ker\sigma_\xi(D)\), and the three
vertical symbols then determine a unique lift. On the TT symbol
slice the full section symbol is injective of real rank 11 into
dimension 12. The symbol has one real range relation. Before the TT
condition the kernel has dimension four. In particular, a full
right inverse to arbitrary independent section sources would need
to address a restriction absent from the round mixed symbol.

## 4. Exact local elimination and the vertical kernel

The symbol restriction has a differential counterpart at this baseline.
Via the \(d\bar z_j\) basis, the pair \(M_J(h)\) is a \((0,1)\)-form.
For any smooth real \(h\), the coefficient of
\(d\bar z_1\wedge d\bar z_2\) satisfies
\[
 U_1\mathcal F_J^2-U_2\mathcal F_J^1
 =U_1^2H_{22}-2U_1U_2H_{12}+U_2^2H_{11}
 =2\big\langle (W^+)'_\delta[\widehat h]\,m,m\big\rangle_\delta,
 \qquad m=U_1\wedge U_2 .
\tag{9}
\]
The pairing is complex bilinear on bivectors using the flat metric.
To check the sign and factor, use the curvature convention for which
\(\langle R(X,Y)Y,X\rangle\) is positive on orthonormal round
vectors. Linearizing this contraction at \(\delta\) in constant
commuting directions \(X,Y\) gives
\(XY\widehat h(X,Y)-\tfrac12(X^2\widehat h(Y,Y)+Y^2\widehat h(X,X))\).
For \(X=U_1,Y=U_2\) the Weyl trace terms vanish since all metric
pairings in the plane vanish and the flat background curvature is
zero. Its bivector is self-dual in the chosen orientation. This
gives (9) after substituting \(H=-\widehat h|_{T^{0,1}\times T^{0,1}}\).
The fixed scaling \(g_t\mapsto\Omega^{-2}g_t\) identifies the
vanishing of the two Weyl variations on the patch. For example the
flat Weyl endomorphism variation is \(\Omega^2\) times the round
one in the common Hodge identification.

Where a supplied smooth \(h\) satisfies \(Dh=0\), (9) makes each
\(M_J(h)\) \(\bar\partial_J\)-closed. On a sufficiently small
\(J\)-polydisk the \(\bar\partial\) Poincaré lemma therefore supplies
a smooth \(v\) with \(\bar\partial_Jv=M_J(h)\). One can first solve
the \(d\bar z_1\) component using the one-variable Cauchy–Green
operator with a cutoff in \(z_1\), equal to one on a smaller disk.
The remaining \(d\bar z_2\) coefficient is holomorphic in \(z_1\)
by closedness. A second Cauchy–Green operation with a cutoff in
\(z_2\) solves it and preserves that holomorphic dependence. Restrict
the three solutions to a common smaller neighborhood. Conversely,
three smooth local solutions of \(\mathcal F_{J_i}(h,v_i)=0\)
give \(Dh=0\) there by (9) and the quartic argument. This proves an
equivalence for smooth homogeneous linearized germs. Each lift is
determined up to a \(J_i\)-holomorphic function, an infinite-dimensional
vertical kernel even though the real-covector vertical symbol is
injective. Prescribing the value at one point leaves holomorphic
functions vanishing at that point available.

More generally, for prescribed smooth graph sources \(f_i\), a
given \(h\) admits germ lifts exactly when the coefficients of
\(\bar\partial_{J_i} f_i\) equal the three right sides of (9).
In particular these coefficients, with their null-bivector
normalizations, must lie pointwise in the real rank-five evaluation
image. This condition concerns the forced section equations. The
calculation on germs leaves boundary values, support restrictions,
overlap data and the choice of bounded vertical normalization open.

## 5. Closed round sphere calibration

The companion's `metric:D-factorization` uses the curvature-operator
normalization adopted here. On global TT tensors it gives
\[
 D^*D=\tfrac1{16}(L+2)(L+4),\qquad L=\nabla^*\nabla .
\]
Its leading symbol agrees with (5), and its positivity proves
\(\ker(D|_{\mathrm{TT}})=0\). The inherited TT gap is \(L\ge8\)
on this slice (`metric:TT-gap`). This is a closed sphere kernel estimate.
[CME metric Hessian and TT gap][cme]

Gover–Gursky §1.1 gives \(H^2_{\mathrm{ASD}}=\ker D^*\) and
unobstructedness of the standard round sphere, hence \(\ker D^*=0\).
The tensor realization in their adjoint formula and the operator
pairing here differ by fixed bundle identifications and a constant
norm factor. Surjectivity in (5) makes \(DD^*\) elliptic, while
\(D\mathcal K=0\) and \(\mathcal K^*=-2\delta_r\) give TT values
for \(D^*\). The self-adjoint elliptic inverse on the closed sphere
therefore gives
\[
 R_D=D^*(DD^*)^{-1}:H^s(S^2_0 E)\longrightarrow H^{s+2}_{\mathrm{TT}}
\]
for \(s\ge0\), with smooth output for smooth sources. It is a right
inverse to \(D\) and the inverse of \(D|_{\mathrm{TT}}\) by the TT
kernel result. Inserting it into (4) solves the closed linearized
mixed map at the stated mixed orders, uniquely in the fixed TT
chart for arbitrary sources in those spaces.
[Gover–Gursky, §1.1, published pp. 295–296, (1.2)–(1.3) and
discussion after Conjecture 1.4][gg]

The remaining conformal stabilizer is \(H^0_{\mathrm{ASD}}=\ker\mathcal K\).
On the round sphere it has dimension 15 by the §1.1 index formula
and the vanishing of \(H^1\) and \(H^2\). Vector potentials can be
chosen orthogonal to it as in B01. This stabilizer concerns the
choice of conformal gauge and is distinct from the local holomorphic
vertical freedom in (7). Gover–Gursky Proposition 1.2 assumes
\(H^0=H^2=0\) for its smooth moduli statement. The inverse above
uses the separate cokernel result and the TT kernel estimate.
[Gover–Gursky, §1.1 and Proposition 1.2][gg]
