## Collar, forcing and frame

Let \(X=[-1,1]\times T^3\) with coordinates \(s,x_1,x_2,x_3\), the
product orientation \(ds\wedge dx_1\wedge dx_2\wedge dx_3\), and
the flat metric \(\delta=ds^2+\sum_i dx_i^2\). Write
\(\nu=ds\,dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3\) and \(V_T=\int_{T^3}dx_1\,dx_2\,dx_3\).
The [E13 seed][e13] is

\[
 r=ds^2+\sum_{i=1}^3 e^{2a_i^0(s)} dx_i^2,\qquad
 (a_1^0,a_2^0,a_3^0)=(f,f,-2f),
\]

where \(f\) is smooth, nonzero and supported in the interior of
\([-1,1]\). Thus \(dV_r=\nu=dV_\delta\). For the unrestricted
metric write \(g_h=\delta(e^h\cdot,\cdot)\), with \(h\) an arbitrary
smooth \(\delta\)-symmetric trace-free endomorphism subject to
\(g_h=\delta\) on neighborhoods of both boundary components.
This chart represents all smooth positive-definite metrics of
volume \(\nu\) satisfying the fixed ends. It allows normal–tangential
cross terms and arbitrary dependence on the torus variables.

Use the seed-target transport in [B01][b01] as specified in
[B13][b13]. The seed-to-metric tangent isometry is
\(A_g=(r^{-1}g)^{-1/2}\), where \(r^{-1}g\) is a positive
\(r\)-self-adjoint endomorphism. It pulls two-forms back by
\(I_g=A_g^*:\Lambda_g^+\to\Lambda_r^+\). Set \(\theta^0=ds\),
\(\theta^i=e^{a_i^0}dx_i\) and use the normalized chiral axes

\[
 \omega_i^r=(\theta^0\wedge\theta^i+\theta^j\wedge\theta^k)/\sqrt2,
 \qquad (i,j,k)\ \text{cyclic in }(1,2,3).
\]

The flat axes \(\omega_i^0\) are defined by the same expression
with \(ds,dx_i\) as coframe. At \(g=\delta\), \(A_\delta\) fixes
\(\partial_s\) and sends \(\partial_{x_i}\) to
\(e^{a_i^0}\partial_{x_i}\). Hence \(I_\delta\omega_i^0=\omega_i^r\)
for every \(i\), including where seed eigenvalues agree.

Bring the seed target into the flat bundle by the fixed
identification \(I_\delta\). Define

\[
 \Phi(h)=I_\delta^{-1}I_{g_h}W^+_{g_h}I_{g_h}^{-1}I_\delta,\qquad
 Z=I_\delta^{-1}W_r^+I_\delta .
\]

The unchanged chord equation is \(\Phi(h)=\lambda Z\) for
\(0\le\lambda\le1\). In the flat axes, \(Z=\kappa_f A\) with
\(A=\operatorname{diag}(1,1,-2)\) parallel and
\(\kappa_f=-f''/2+(f')^2\), from the E13 diagonal curvature
calculation. Norms and pairings here are those of curvature
operators, with a normalized exterior basis and
\(\langle B,C\rangle_{\rm HS}=\operatorname{tr}(BC)\) for
symmetric matrices. [SH-B01][common] · [E13][e13]

For the derivative, the flat-to-metric isometry \(e^{-h/2}\)
defines the pullback \(J_h=(e^{-h/2})^*:\Lambda^+_{g_h}\to
\Lambda_\delta^+\). Thus \(\Phi(h)=U_h(J_hW^+_{g_h}J_h^{-1})U_h^{-1}\)
where \(U_h=I_\delta^{-1}I_{g_h}J_h^{-1}\) and \(U_0=I\). These
fiber isometries depend smoothly on \(h\). Every derivative of
the transport contributing to the first variation multiplies
the zero flat Weyl operator. Consequently \(L=d\Phi(0)\) is
the flat Weyl derivative in the fixed \(\omega_i^0\) frame.

## Linearized boundary flux

Use index \(0\) for \(s\). Lower the indices of \(h\) with
\(\delta\), so \(h_{ab}\) is the first metric variation. In the
positive-sectional-curvature convention
\(\mathcal R_{ab,cd}=g(R(e_a,e_b)e_d,e_c)\), the flat variation is

\[
 \dot{\mathcal R}_{ab,cd}
 =\tfrac12\bigl(
 \partial_a\partial_d h_{bc}-\partial_a\partial_c h_{bd}
 -\partial_b\partial_d h_{ac}+\partial_b\partial_c h_{ad}\bigr).
 \tag{1}
\]

Indeed the Christoffel variation is
\(\dot\Gamma^c_{bd}=(\partial_b h_{dc}+\partial_d h_{bc}
-\partial_c h_{bd})/2\). Differentiating it in the two skew
directions gives (1). The zero background curvature eliminates
the variations of the metric and frame in the contraction.
The \(i\)-th diagonal entry of \(Lh\) is

\[
 \tfrac12(\dot{\mathcal R}_{0i,0i}
          +2\dot{\mathcal R}_{0i,jk}
          +\dot{\mathcal R}_{jk,jk})-\tfrac1{12}\dot s_{\rm scal},
 \qquad (i,j,k)\ \text{cyclic}.
\]

Here \(\dot s_{\rm scal}\) is the variation of scalar curvature.
The scalar term cancels in pairing against the trace-free \(A\)
by the chiral curvature decomposition. [Gover–Gursky, p. 293][gg]
Both the mixed term \(\dot{\mathcal R}_{0i,jk}\) and
\(\dot{\mathcal R}_{jk,jk}\) contain at least one tangential
derivative in every summand, so their torus averages vanish.
The only surviving term from
\[
 \dot{\mathcal R}_{0i,0i}
   =\partial_s\partial_i h_{0i}
       -\tfrac12\partial_s^2h_{ii}-\tfrac12\partial_i^2h_{00}
\]
is the average of \(-\partial_s^2h_{ii}/2\). It follows for
every smooth \(h\), with arbitrary boundary values, that

\[
 \begin{aligned}
 \int_{T^3}\langle Lh,A\rangle_{\rm HS}\,dx
  &=-\tfrac14\partial_s^2
        \int_{T^3}(h_{11}+h_{22}-2h_{33})\,dx,\\
 \int_X\langle Lh,A\rangle_{\rm HS}\,\nu
  &=-\tfrac14\left[
        \int_{T^3}\partial_s(h_{11}+h_{22}-2h_{33})\,dx
      \right]_{s=-1}^{s=1}.
 \end{aligned}
 \tag{2}
\]

This agrees with the normal-direction Weyl principal symbol
on tangential TT data in [E02, equation (5)][e02]. The flux
calculation allows every trace-free variation and does not impose
a divergence condition. In particular it vanishes for perturbations
supported away from the ends or with the first jets matching the
flat background at the boundary.

The seed moment is strictly positive. Since
\(\langle A,A\rangle_{\rm HS}=6\) and \(f'\) vanishes near both ends,

\[
 B_f:=\int_X\langle Z,A\rangle_{\rm HS}\,\nu
    =6V_T\int_{-1}^1\bigl(-\tfrac12 f''+(f')^2\bigr)\,ds
    =6V_T\int_{-1}^1(f')^2\,ds>0.
 \tag{3}
\]

Strictness follows because \(f\) is nonzero and vanishes near
both ends. Equations (2)–(3) show \(Lh=Z\) is impossible with the
fixed ends, even allowing arbitrary tangential dependence.

## Quadratic remainder and parameter scope

Use a \(C^2\) norm of \(h\) given by the suprema of its derivatives
in the flat product frame through order two. In these coordinates
\(\Phi(h)(x)\) is a smooth function of the finite jet
\((h,\partial h,\partial^2h)(x)\). Curvature is a smooth expression
in the metric, its inverse and its first two derivatives.
The matrix exponential and the positive symmetric transport are
smooth at each point. Taylor's formula in the jet variables and
compactness of \(X\) give \(\eta>0\) and \(C_0>0\) such that

\[
 \|\Phi(h)-Lh\|_{C^0}\le C_0\|h\|_{C^2}^2
              \quad \text{if }\|h\|_{C^2}\le\eta .
 \tag{4}
\]

The estimate is uniform over support locations. When the
first boundary jets match, (2) still cancels the linear term.
Thus every such sufficiently small solution with \(\lambda>0\)
satisfies

\[
 \lambda B_f
   \le 2 V_T\sqrt6\,C_0\|h\|_{C^2}^2,\qquad
 \|h\|_{C^2}\ge c_f\sqrt\lambda,\qquad
 c_f=\left(\frac{B_f}{2V_T\sqrt6\,C_0}\right)^{1/2}>0 .
 \tag{5}
\]

A one-sided path with \(h(0)=0\) that is differentiable in \(C^2\)
with respect to \(\lambda\) at the flat end would have
\(\|h(\lambda)\|_{C^2}=O(\lambda)\), contradicting (5). More
generally, differentiating \(\Phi(h(t))=\lambda(t)Z\) at a
parameter where \(h=0\), the fixed-end condition and (2) force
\(\dot\lambda=0\) whenever both derivatives exist in the
stated spaces. This includes traversing the original radial
parameter \(t=1-\lambda\) toward its flat end at \(t=1\).
Any branch approaching flat with nonzero chord parameter must
obey (5); existence at that scale, branches farther from flat,
and paths with zero chord speed remain undecided. The calculation
tests the specified collar with fixed end jets. Free exterior
matching could contribute the explicit flux in (2).
