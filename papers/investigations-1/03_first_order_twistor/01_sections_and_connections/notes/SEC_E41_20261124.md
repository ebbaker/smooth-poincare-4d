## Statement and comparison

Let \(M,r,K,O\) be the E27 data. Thus \(M\) is a smooth closed oriented
homotopy four-sphere, \(r\) is a smooth positive-definite Riemannian
metric, and \(K\Subset O\) is a closed coordinate subball of an
orientation-preservingly round patch \(O\). Take \(\nu=dV_r\),
\(V=S^2_0T^*M\), and \(B=S^2_0\Lambda_r^+\) with the curvature-operator
Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. Put \(L=dF_r(0)\) in the E03 full trace-free
fixed-volume chart and \(\mathcal C=\ker L^*\). For a smooth real
\(v\in\Gamma(V)\), set \(r_a=r(e^{a v^\sharp}\cdot,\cdot)\), where
\(v^\sharp=r^{-1}v\). Each \(r_a\) has volume form \(\nu\).

Write \(S_a=e^{-av^\sharp/2}\) for the oriented tangent isometry
\((TM,r)\to(TM,r_a)\). Let \(J_a=S_a^*\) on symmetric covariant tensors
and let \(I_a=\Lambda^2 S_a^*\) on chiral forms. Denote by \(T_a\) the
isometric comparison \(U\mapsto I_a U I_a^{-1}\) on Weyl blocks. The
compared full trace-free operator is \(A_a=T_a L_{r_a}J_a^{-1}\), with
\(L_{r_a}=dF_{r_a}(0)\) defined in the symmetric fixed-volume chart
centered at \(r_a\). In particular \(A_0=L\). Set
\(m=\dim\mathcal C\). There is a smooth \(v\) compactly supported in
\(M\setminus K\) and smooth \(k_1,\ldots,k_m\in\ker L\) such that
\[
  \mathcal C\longrightarrow\mathbb R^m,\qquad
  Z\longmapsto
  \big(\langle Z,A'_0 k_j\rangle_{L^2(r)}\big)_{j=1}^m
  \tag{1}
\]
is an isomorphism. When \(m=0\) the assertion is empty and we use
\(v=0\). The support requirement concerns the reference variation
\(v\). The projected old kernel directions in (1) can have global
support. [E03][e03] · [E27][e27] · [Working manuscript][manuscript]

## Exterior probes and symbol polarization

By E03, \(P=LL^*\) is elliptic of order four with symbol
\(|\xi|_r^4\operatorname{Id}_B/16\). The kernel \(\mathcal C\) is
finite dimensional and smooth. By E27 its restriction to the open
exterior \(M\setminus K\) is injective. In fact, a member vanishing
there can be transplanted from \(O\) to the round sphere and extended
by zero across the collar where it vanishes. The extension solves the
round adjoint equation, whose kernel is zero. The round linearization
is the one in the ASD deformation complex because the symmetric
frame variation multiplies the zero Weyl block. The real chiral
curvature-operator identification is the one in E02. [E03, (3)–(4)][e03]
· [E27, exterior restriction][e27] · [E02, round inverse][e02]
· [Gover–Gursky, §1.1, (1.2) and the example following Conjecture 1.4][gg]

The functionals \(Z\mapsto\langle Z(p),u\rangle_r\) for
\(p\in M\setminus K\), \(u\in B_p\), span \(\mathcal C^*\), since their
common annihilator vanishes on the exterior. Select a basis of \(m\)
such functionals. If any selected centers coincide, smoothly extend
their polarizations in a local trivialization and move the centers
slightly to distinct points. The evaluation matrix on a fixed real basis
\(Z_1,\ldots,Z_m\) is continuous in those choices and its determinant
stays nonzero. Use disjoint relatively compact exterior coordinate
balls \(U_j\) about the resulting centers \(p_j\), and smooth local
target sections \(Y_j\) with \(Y_j(p_j)=u_j\). Take smooth real coordinate
phases \(\phi_j\) with \(d\phi_j\ne0\) on each ball. In the real symbol
convention \(\partial\mapsto\xi\), the map \(-\sigma_{d\phi_j}(L)\)
from pointwise TT symbol tensors to \(B\) is an isomorphism with
factor \(|d\phi_j|_r^2/4\) in the orientations and orthonormal
frames of E02/E03. Choose its smooth TT inverse image \(H_j\) of
\(Y_j\). In particular the \(H_j\) are trace-free. [E02, (5)][e02]
· [E03, normal operator][e03]

Choose real \(b_j,d_j\in C_c^\infty(U_j)\) with \(b_j=d_j\) and
\(\int b_jd_j\,\nu=1\). The balls may be chosen so small that
\[
  D_{ij}=\int_{U_j} b_j d_j \langle Z_i,Y_j\rangle_r\,\nu
  \tag{2}
\]
is invertible. Indeed, the difference between each entry and
\(\langle Z_i(p_j),u_j\rangle_r\) is bounded by the supremum of the
corresponding scalar difference on the ball, using the nonnegative
normalized weight. Fix the balls and cutoffs before taking frequencies.
Their supports have positive separation from \(K\). The transplant
argument also works with a slightly larger closed subball within \(O\)
when a given round buffer is required.

For positive integers \(N\), extend the following smooth fields by zero
from their patches and put
\[
 \begin{split}
 X_{j,N}&=N^{-1}b_j\sin(N\phi_j)\frac{\operatorname{grad}_r\phi_j}
                                      {|d\phi_j|_r^2},\\
 v_N&=N^{-2}\sum_j d_j\cos(N\phi_j)H_j,\qquad w_N=Lv_N .
 \end{split}
 \tag{3}
\]
The leading derivative of \(X_{j,N}\) is the self-adjoint longitudinal
endomorphism \(b_j\cos(N\phi_j)\operatorname{grad}_r\phi_j\otimes
d\phi_j/|d\phi_j|_r^2\). Thus, with all estimates over the fixed
patches and extended by zero where appropriate,
\[
 \begin{split}
 \|X_{j,N}\|_{C^0}&=O(N^{-1}),\quad \|\nabla X_{j,N}\|_{C^0}=O(1),\quad
 \operatorname{div}_\nu X_{j,N}=b_j\cos(N\phi_j)+O(N^{-1}),\quad
 \operatorname{skew}\nabla X_{j,N}=O(N^{-1}),\\
 \|v_N\|_{C^q}&=O(N^{q-2})\quad (q=0,1,2),\qquad
 w_N\big|_{U_j}=d_j\cos(N\phi_j)Y_j+O(N^{-1}).
 \end{split}
 \tag{4}
\]
The errors here are uniform \(C^0\) errors for the tensors displayed.
The last line uses the second derivative of \(\cos(N\phi_j)\), which
contributes \(-N^2\cos(N\phi_j)d\phi_j\otimes d\phi_j\) at principal
order. This gives \(-\sigma_{d\phi_j}(L)H_j=Y_j\) with the stated sign.

## Differentiate the compared gauge formula

For any fixed \(N\) take \(v=v_N\) in the comparison, and denote the
resulting derivative by \(A'_{0,N}\). For a fixed vector field \(X\),
write \(\mathcal K_a X=J_a(\mathcal L_X r_a)_0\) and
\(\mathcal K=\mathcal K_0\). Write \(W_a=T_a W_{r_a}^+\)
and \(W=W_0\), so \(W'_0=w_N\). Write \(D^a\) for the compared
connection induced by the Levi–Civita connection of \(r_a\) on the
fixed target \(B\), and \(\Theta_X^a\) for the compared infinitesimal
pullback of \(\operatorname{skew}_{r_a}\nabla^{r_a} X\) on the chiral
two-form bundle. The manuscript identity `lift:gauge` at \(r_a\)
gives
\[
 A_a \mathcal K_a X=D^a_X W_a+[\Theta_X^a,W_a]
                 +\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}_\nu X)W_a .
 \tag{5}
\]
Here the divergence uses the common density. In the normalized pullback
formula the conformal factor is \(\rho=(\nu/\varphi^*\nu)^{1/2}\).
Its inverse multiplies the Weyl endomorphism and has derivative
\(+\tfrac12\operatorname{div}_\nu X\) at the identity. The transported
orthogonal factor supplies the skew term by cancellation of the symmetric
and trace parts. The Weyl weight follows by raising two slots in the
covariant Weyl scaling law for \(\rho g\), using conformal invariance
of the Hodge splitting on two-forms in dimension four.
[Working manuscript, `lift:gauge`][manuscript] · [E03, (2)][e03]
· [Fefferman–Graham, §6 after (6.7), arXiv v2][fg]

Use a dot at \(a=0\) for frame, connection and gauge variations. The
derivative of (5) is
\[
 \begin{split}
 A'_{0,N}\mathcal KX+ L\dot{\mathcal K}X
   ={}& \nabla_X w_N+[\Theta_X^0,w_N]
   +\tfrac12(\operatorname{div}_\nu X)w_N\\
   &+\dot D_X W+[\dot\Theta_X,W].
 \end{split}
 \tag{6}
\]
The moving \(\mathcal K_a\) term pairs to zero exactly with \(Z\in\mathcal C\)
since \(L^*Z=0\). In fixed smooth frames the compared connections
depend on the first jet of \(r_a\) and on the first derivatives of
the algebraic frame isometries. Thus \(\dot D_X\) is a zeroth-order
operator on the target, pointwise bounded by
\(C|X|(|v_N|+|\nabla v_N|)\). The variation of the skew endomorphism
obeys
\[
 |\dot\Theta_X|\le C\bigl(|v_N||\nabla X|
                  +( |v_N|+|\nabla v_N| )|X|\bigr).
 \tag{7}
\]
This follows by varying the algebraic skew projection and two-form
comparison on the term linear in \(\nabla X\), and the connection in the
term linear in \(X\). The same bound holds for \(\dot{\mathcal K}X\)
by differentiating \(\mathcal L_Xr_a\), the trace projection and \(J_a\).
For \(X=X_{j,N}\), (4) bounds \(\dot{\mathcal K}X\) before applying
\(L\), as well as both lower terms on the right of (6), by \(O(N^{-2})\)
in \(C^0\). The remaining commutator
\([\Theta_X^0,w_N]\) is \(O(N^{-1})\), since its order-one
longitudinal skew symbol vanishes and \(w_N\) is uniformly bounded.

The transport term can be paired without differentiating the uniform
error in \(w_N\). Metric compatibility and integration by parts on
the closed manifold yield
\[
 \langle Z,\nabla_X w_N\rangle_{L^2(r)}
 =-\int_M (\operatorname{div}_\nu X)\langle Z,w_N\rangle_r\,\nu
  -\int_M\langle \nabla_XZ,w_N\rangle_r\,\nu .
 \tag{8}
\]
The second term is \(O_Z(N^{-1})\). The supports of the vector fields
are disjoint, so the sole leading Weyl perturbation seen by
\(X_{j,N}\) is its own. Combining (4), (6) and (8) now gives
\[
 \begin{split}
 \langle Z,A'_{0,N}\mathcal KX_{j,N}\rangle_{L^2(r)}
   &=-\tfrac12\int_{U_j} b_jd_j\cos^2(N\phi_j)
               \langle Z,Y_j\rangle_r\,\nu+O_Z(N^{-1})\\
   &\longrightarrow-\tfrac14\int_{U_j} b_jd_j
                       \langle Z,Y_j\rangle_r\,\nu .
 \end{split}
 \tag{9}
\]
In the final step \(\cos^2(N\phi)=(1+\cos(2N\phi))/2\), and the
oscillatory integral tends to zero by integration by parts with
\(\operatorname{grad}_r\phi/|d\phi|_r^2\) on the compactly supported
amplitude. Separately, the limiting coefficients of transport and
half-divergence are \(-1/2\) and \(+1/4\) in the \(Y_j\) polarization.
If \(H_j\) is expressed by its orthonormal TT spatial block, the
unscaled block has factor \(-|d\phi_j|_r^2/16\) in (9).

## Projection to the exact kernel and the finite matrix

Let \(G_P\) be the inverse of \(P\) on \(\mathcal C^\perp\), extended
as zero on \(\mathcal C\), and let \(Q=L^*G_P\). Elliptic regularity
and the Fredholm theorem at grade zero give \(G_P:L^2(B)\to H^4(B)\)
boundedly. In particular \(Q:L^2(B)\to H^2(V)\) is bounded and
\(Q:L^2(B)\to L^2(V)\) is compact by Rellich, applied componentwise
in a finite bundle atlas. These are estimates on one fixed smooth
closed manifold and fixed bundles. [E03, (3)–(4)][e03]
· [Melrose, Lecture 22, (1)–(2)][melrose]
· [Dyatlov, Theorem 15.10][dyatlov]

Use the exact gauge formula at \(r\) and (4) to obtain
\[
   L\mathcal K X_{j,N}=\tfrac12 b_j\cos(N\phi_j)W + O(N^{-1})
   \quad\text{in }L^2(B).
 \tag{10}
\]
The leading term tends weakly to zero by the same nonstationary
phase argument against smooth sections, then by their density and
uniform boundedness in \(L^2\). Compactness therefore gives
\(\|QL\mathcal K X_{j,N}\|_{L^2(V)}\to0\). Set
\[
  k_{j,N}=\mathcal KX_{j,N}-QL\mathcal K X_{j,N} .
 \tag{11}
\]
These are smooth by elliptic regularity. The adjoint identity gives
\(L\mathcal K X_{j,N}\perp\mathcal C\) and \(LQ=I-\pi_{\mathcal C}\).
Hence \(Lk_{j,N}=0\) exactly.

Uniform control of the mixed formal adjoint is needed to use the
strong \(L^2\) convergence of the correction in (11). In fixed local
frames, the coefficient of each order-\(\ell\) derivative of the
metric input in \(A_a\) depends smoothly on the jet of \(r_a\) through
order \(2-\ell\), for \(\ell=0,1,2\), and on smooth background data.
Indeed the curvature formula is linear in second metric derivatives
with algebraic coefficients and is otherwise quadratic in first
derivatives. Chirality, the trace removal, and the symmetric domain
and target comparisons are pointwise algebraic or smooth pointwise
operations in the metrics. Differentiating the two-jet expression
for \(A_a\) at zero thus gives a coefficient linear in derivatives of
\(v_N\) of order at most \(2-\ell\) at input order \(\ell\).
Taking the formal adjoint puts at most \(\ell\) further derivatives
on that coefficient, including derivatives of the smooth fixed
pairings or density. A finite atlas and (4) give
\[
 \| (A'_{0,N})^*Z\|_{L^2(V)}
     \le C_Z \|v_N\|_{C^2}\le C'_Z .
 \tag{12}
\]
The coefficient structure is essential for this estimate. Merely using
the order two of the mixed differential operator would allow higher
derivatives of \(v_N\) when forming its adjoint. By (12),
\[
 \left|\langle Z,A'_{0,N} QL\mathcal K X_{j,N}\rangle_{L^2(r)}\right|
 \le C'_Z\|QL\mathcal K X_{j,N}\|_{L^2(V)}=o(1).
 \tag{13}
\]
Consequently the matrix with entries
\(\langle Z_i,A'_{0,N}k_{j,N}\rangle_{L^2(r)}\) converges to
\(-D/4\), which is invertible by (2). It is invertible for all
sufficiently large finite \(N\). Choose one such \(N\) and take
\(v=v_N\), \(k_j=k_{j,N}\) to prove (1).
