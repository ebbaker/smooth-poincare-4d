## Conditional statement

Let \(M\) be a fixed closed, connected, oriented smooth homotopy
four-sphere and \(K\) the marked ordinary round coordinate ball of
[E25][e25]. Use smooth real positive-definite Riemannian metrics and
the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt energy \(E_+\), with ASD meaning
\(W^+=0\). Write \(\mathcal G_K\) for smooth metrics equal to the given
round metric on \(K\), and \(\mathcal G\) for all smooth positive
metrics on \(M\).

Suppose a minimizing sequence \(g_j\in\mathcal G_K\) admits gauges
\(G_j=u_j^2\phi_j^*g_j\), where \(\phi_j:M\to M\) are smooth
diffeomorphisms and \(u_j>0\) are smooth conformal factors. In a fixed
smooth atlas on \(M\), suppose \(G_j\ge c r\) for a fixed smooth
reference metric \(r\) and \(c>0\), and \(G_j\to G\) strongly in
\(C^4\). A uniform \(C^{4,\alpha}\) bound with \(0<\alpha<1\) gives this
convergence after passing to a subsequence by Arzelà–Ascoli on finitely
many precompact chart domains, with positivity inherited. Assume that
one nonempty open set \(U\) of this same \(M\) lies in
\(\phi_j^{-1}(K^\circ)\) throughout the convergent tail. Then \(M\)
is smoothly diffeomorphic to \(S^4\), and the marked and global
infima of \(E_+\) are zero.

The common open set only needs to supply Weyl vanishing in the limit.
The gauged metric on \(U\) is conformally flat, as the round cap is.
The argument allows the gauges to change the cap's marking and its
round representative. If positive factors are initially specified at
\(C^4\) grade, one may approximate each factor by a positive smooth
factor and choose errors tending to zero in the resulting metric's
\(C^4\) norm. The common preimage condition and conformal flatness
on \(U\) persist in that smooth approximation.

## The limit is Bach-flat without a prescribed infimum value

By E25, \(I:=\inf_{\mathcal G_K}E_+=\inf_{\mathcal G}E_+\).
For \(a>0\), the Hodge splitting of two-forms is invariant under
\(g\mapsto a^2g\), the Weyl endomorphisms have weight \(a^{-2}\),
and the volume weight is \(a^4\). Thus the integrated squared
operator norms are conformally invariant. Orientation-preserving
pullbacks preserve each energy, and orientation-reversing pullbacks
exchange the energies. The smooth closed signature identity
\(E_+(g)-E_-(g)=12\pi^2\tau(M)=0\) makes this exchange immaterial
here. Hence \(E_+(G_j)=E_+(g_j)\to I\).
[E25 marking and invariance][e25] · [SH-E01 signature and norms][sh]

Fix any smooth real symmetric covariant two-tensor \(h\) on \(M\).
Uniform positivity gives \(G_j+s h>0\) for both signs of \(s\) and
all sufficiently small \(|s|\), with the interval independent of
\(j\). For each fixed \(s\) in this interval, \(G_j+s h\) is a
smooth competitor for the global infimum. Smoothness and the
two-jet expression of the \(E_+\) density give
\[
 I\le E_+(G_j+s h)\longrightarrow E_+(G+s h),
 \qquad E_+(G)=I.
 \tag{1}
\]
Equation (1) allows arbitrary perturbations after gauging because
the marked infimum equals the global infimum.
The finite-regularity energy at \(G\) is defined by its usual
classical curvature density.

In the convention of Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, the derivative
of the full four-index Weyl functional at a smooth metric is the
\(L^2\) pairing with \(-4B\), where \(B\) is the covariant Bach
tensor. The full functional is \(8E_+\) for smooth metrics on this
\(M\), by signature and the factor four between squared tensor
and operator norms. With uniform positive lower bounds, the
density is a smooth function of the metric two-jet, so the directional
derivative against fixed smooth \(h\) is continuous under strong
\(C^2\) convergence. The Bach tensor is continuous under strong
\(C^4\) convergence by its Schouten and Weyl formula. Passing to
the limit in the smooth first variations therefore gives
\[
 0=dE_+(G)[h]
   =-\tfrac12\int_M\langle B_G,h\rangle_G\,dV_G .
 \tag{2}
\]
The first equality comes from the two-sided minimum (1).
All smooth \(h\) are available, so the continuous tensor \(B_G\)
vanishes everywhere by the fundamental lemma.
[BGI+M, §1, eq. (1.1) and Weyl first variation][bgim] ·
[E24 normalization][e24]

## The finite-regularity analytic continuation

Each \(G_j\) has zero Weyl curvature on \(U\), by conformal and
diffeomorphism covariance of this condition. Strong \(C^2\)
convergence yields \(W_G^+=0\) on \(U\). The E24 sign-convention
comparison gives \(\mathcal O^{(4)}=0\) when \(B=0\), where
\(\mathcal O^{(4)}\) is the obstruction tensor in the
Lassas–Liimatainen analytic theorem. The comparison is between
fixed nonzero multiples of the two tensors. For smooth metrics
it follows by comparing the first variations of
\(\int Q\,dV=8\pi^2\chi-\mathcal W/4\); for the limit it also
holds by their continuity in the original smooth \(C^4\) atlas.
[E24 obstruction comparison][e24] ·
[Graham–Hirachi, §1 and Theorem 1.1][gh]

Fix \(0<\beta<1\). The limit \(G\) is \(C^{3,\beta}\) in the
original atlas, and Theorem 2.2 of Lassas–Liimatainen gives local
conformal harmonic coordinates of class \(C^{4,\beta}\). In these
coordinates the metric is \(C^{3,\beta}\), sufficient to interpret
the obstruction equation distributionally. To check that the zero
equation transfers, let \(\psi\) be the inverse coordinate map
on a smaller coordinate ball and approximate it in \(C^4\) by
smooth local diffeomorphisms \(\psi_\ell\) into the original smooth
coordinate chart. The metrics \(\psi_\ell^*G_\ell\) converge to
\(\psi^*G\) in local \(C^3\), with locally uniform positivity.
By smooth tensor naturality,
\(\mathcal O^{(4)}(\psi_\ell^*G_\ell)
=\psi_\ell^*\mathcal O^{(4)}(G_\ell)\) converges to zero in \(C^0\),
since \(\mathcal O^{(4)}(G_\ell)\to0\) in the original atlas.
For positive \(C^3\) metrics the formula for the Bach tensor is
continuous into distributions. The highest terms have two
derivatives of the Schouten tensor. Integrating once by parts
against a fixed compact test function leaves only first
derivatives of Schouten and first derivatives of metric
coefficients multiplying the highest derivatives. The
remaining terms are continuous expressions of at most three
metric derivatives. Therefore
\(\mathcal O^{(4)}(\psi^*G)=0\) distributionally in the
conformal harmonic chart. This argument uses the \(C^4\)
chart solely for the \(C^3\) pullback convergence and never
requires fifth derivatives of a coordinate change.
[Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorem 2.2 and §3.2, eqs. (26)–(28)][ll]

The initial \(C^{3,\beta}\) regularity is strictly above the
threshold \(r>3\) in Theorem 1.3(c) and Theorem 3.7(c). The weighted
obstruction being zero satisfies the analytic regularity hypothesis.
The determinant-normalized representative is therefore real
analytic in the conformal harmonic chart. Its Weyl operator and
Hodge star are analytic in the coordinate two-form frame. In
particular \(P_+WP_+\) is analytic, with \(P_+=(1+*)/2\) using the
fixed orientation. Its zero set agrees with that of \(W_G^+\)
under the positive determinant normalization. As in E24, the
set of points with a neighborhood of chiral vanishing is open,
contains \(U\), and is closed by applying the analytic identity
theorem in a connected coordinate ball at any point of its
closure. It is all of \(M\). The local analytic argument concerns
the projected block, using Theorem 1.3(c). Theorem 1.4 of
Lassas–Liimatainen concerns the full Weyl tensor.
[Lassas–Liimatainen, Theorems 1.3(c) and 3.7(c)][ll] ·
[E24 one-chirality continuation][e24]

## Recognition on the original smooth manifold

With \(W_G^+=0\), the energies \(E_+(G_j)\) tend to zero by \(C^2\)
continuity. Apply signature to the smooth \(G_j\) to find
\(E_-(G_j)=E_+(G_j)\to0\). Again using continuity, \(E_-(G)=0\)
and hence \(W_G^-=0\). This proves \(I=0\) in the minimizing case.
The positive metric \(G\) is \(C^{3,\beta}\) and Weyl-flat, so the
finite-regularity part of E05 applies from precisely that point
in its proof.

For clarity, the Bianchi identity gives vanishing Cotton tensor
at \(C^{3,\beta}\). The normal real tractor connection on
\(\mathbb R\oplus T^*M\oplus\mathbb R\), split by \(G\), is then
flat with \(C^{1,\beta}\) coefficients. Its curvature formula
has only Weyl and Cotton terms. Local parallel frames from
ordinary differential equations are \(C^{2,\beta}\), and simple
connectivity gives a global parallel trivialization. The canonical
null ray yields the \(C^{3,\beta}\) developing map into the
projective light cone \(S^4\). If \(v\) denotes its null lift and
\(a=-\langle v,t\rangle>0\) for a chosen unit timelike \(t\), the
map's pullback of the unit round metric is \(a^{-2}G\). Thus it
is a local diffeomorphism. Compactness makes it a covering, and
simple connectivity of \(S^4\) makes it a global diffeomorphism
in this regularity. Approximate the map in \(C^1\) by a smooth
map with values in \(S^4\), using the embedding in \(\mathbb R^5\)
and radial projection. The derivative remains invertible by the
positive lower bound on its smallest singular value on compact
\(M\). The smooth approximation is again a covering and hence
a smooth diffeomorphism. This last step concerns the original
smooth structure on \(M\).
[E05 finite-regularity development and smoothing][e05] ·
[Gover–Šilhan, §3.1, eqs. (10) and (13)][gs] ·
[SH-E01 exact recognition][sh]

## Conditional variant with small total Bach norm

Consider any smooth sequence with the stated same-manifold gauges,
uniform positive lower bound, and strong \(C^4\) precompactness
as above. Suppose a convergent subsequence keeps a common open
cap and the original sequence satisfies
\(\int_M |B_{g_j}|_{g_j}\,dV_{g_j}\to0\). This variant dispenses
with minimization. Diffeomorphisms preserve the integral. Under
\(G=a^2g\), the covariant two-tensor obeys \(B_G=a^{-2}B_g\).
The two inverse metric factors in the squared norm give
\(|B_G|_G=a^{-4}|B_g|_g\), so the volume weight \(a^4\) cancels.
The total Bach norm is therefore unchanged by both gauges.
Strong \(C^4\) convergence yields uniform convergence of the
Bach tensors and the norm densities in the fixed atlas. The
limiting Bach tensor has integral of its norm zero and is
continuous, so it vanishes. The common open cap, analytic
continuation and finite-regularity development above finish
the same smooth recognition. In this case the energy of the
chosen convergent subsequence tends to zero as a consequence.
[BGI+M, §1 Bach conformal weight][bgim]

## Scope and verified inputs

The argument uses the common open cap to start propagation.
Without this common-open-set condition or an independent open set
of Weyl vanishing in the limit, unmarked compactness gives the
Bach-flat conclusion but supplies no starting set for the analytic
identity theorem. A collapsed or degenerate limit fails the
positive-metric hypothesis in the curvature and development steps.
Bounded critical Weyl energy supplies no strong \(C^4\) control
in the proof. Existence of the required same-manifold compact
sequence with the surviving open cap remains a separate premise.
