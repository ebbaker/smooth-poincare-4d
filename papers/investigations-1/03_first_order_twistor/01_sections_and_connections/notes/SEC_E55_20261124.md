## Statement

Fix \(\delta>0\). Consider smooth closed connected oriented homotopy
four-spheres with smooth positive-definite real Riemannian metrics
that are exactly Bach-flat, have positive conformal-class Yamabe
constant \(Y\), and have chiral energy
\(E=E_+=E_-\in[4\pi^2,8\pi^2-\delta]\). Here \(W^\pm\) act on the two
Hodge eigenspaces and \(E_\pm=\int |W^\pm|_{\rm op,HS}^2\,dV\).
Then there is a uniform \(d_\delta>0\) such that
\[
Y\le Y_{\rm rd}-d_\delta,\qquad Y_{\rm rd}=Y(S^4,[g_{\rm rd}])
                                            =8\sqrt6\,\pi .
\tag{1}
\]
The assertion is vacuous if the subclass is empty. The proof allows
the smooth homotopy sphere to vary. It also yields a uniform positive
Weyl energy remainder outside any nonflat complete smooth ALE
rescaling supplied in the sense of B29. Extraction for the almost-Bach
sequence is a separate premise. [B29][b29] · [B31][b31]

## Verify exact compactness inputs

Suppose \(Y_i\to Y_{\rm rd}\) in the exact subclass. Take smooth
unit-volume Yamabe minimizers \(g_i\), with \(R_i=Y_i\).
Bach-flatness is conformally invariant and the Weyl energies
are unchanged. By the closed signature and Chern–Gauss–Bonnet
formulas in E36, for \(T_i=\operatorname{Ric}_{g_i}-R_i g_i/4\),
\[
 \frac{Y_i^2}{48}=(8\pi^2-E_i)+\frac14\int|T_i|^2\,dV_{g_i},
 \qquad
 \int|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_i}|_{4{\rm ind}}^2\,dV_{g_i}
 \le 16E_i+64\pi^2 .
\tag{2}
\]
In particular \(0<\sqrt{48\delta}\le Y_i\le Y_{\rm rd}\), the total
curvature is bounded, and \(b_1(M_i)=0\). The square root of the
Yamabe inequality bounds the Sobolev constant in the strong form
of Tian–Viaclovsky Proposition 3.1, with coefficient one on the
term \(\operatorname{Vol}(g_i)^{-1/4}\|f\|_2\). This persists under
constant scaling. [E36, (1)–(4)][e36] ·
[Tian–Viaclovsky, §2.1 and Proposition 3.1][tvmod]

Chang–Qing–Yang's first-bubble construction, §4 of
arXiv:math/0508621v2, starts from precisely the exact Bach-flat
condition, uniform positive lower \(Y\), zero \(b_1\), and uniform
\(L^2\) curvature in (4.1)–(4.4), with Yamabe representatives as in
§2. The minimum concentration radius in (4.5)–(4.6) uses a
fixed positive curvature-energy threshold and on a concentrating
subsequence tends to zero. The first rescaling is asserted to be
a complete scalar-flat Bach-flat smooth leaf with one ALE end in
the paragraph around Definition 4.1 and (4.7)–(4.8). Their §5,
Theorem 5.1, recites the compact orbifold convergence for normalized
Yamabe volumes \(8\pi^2/3\), obtained from volume one by a common
constant scaling. A smooth underlying orbifold with trivial
isotropy would still require ruling out concentration to conclude
global smooth convergence. [Chang–Qing–Yang, §§2, 4 and 5][cqy]

The following curvature-maximum version makes the smooth
compact comparison maps and the dichotomy explicit. For the
unit-volume source, write \(V_x(r)=\operatorname{Vol}_{g_i} B(x,r)\).
The Yamabe inequality applied to a Lipschitz test supported on
\(B(x,r)\), equal to one on \(B(x,r/2)\), gives
\(V_x(r)\ge c r^2 V_x(r/2)^{1/2}\) for \(0<r\le1\). Here \(c>0\)
uses the uniform lower \(Y_i\), upper \(R_i\), and cutoff
gradient \(O(r^{-1})\). Apply this to \(V_x(r)/r^4\) and
iterate dyadically. The small-radius Euclidean limit at each
center yields \(V_x(r)\ge c_0 r^4\) for all \(r\le1\), with
uniform \(c_0>0\). This argument uses a fixed small-radius
bound on the closed metrics. Large-radius volume lower bounds
of this form would be incompatible with their finite volume.
By packing disjoint radius-\(1/3\) balls along a minimizing
geodesic, unit volume then bounds the source diameters from
above. [Chang–Qing–Yang, (2.1) and Lemma 2.2][cqy]

Pass to a subsequence on which either
\(K_i=\max_{M_i}|\operatorname{Rm}_{g_i}|\) is bounded or
\(K_i\to\infty\).

- In the bounded case all sectional curvatures are uniformly
  bounded. The diameter is also bounded away from zero by
  Bishop volume comparison and \(\operatorname{Vol}=1\).
  On small fixed balls, Bishop volume comparison from the
  Ricci lower bound makes the \(L^2\) curvature small enough
  for the exact Bach-flat \(\epsilon\)-regularity theorem.
  It gives the bounds for all covariant derivatives of curvature.
  The volume lower bound and sectional curvature bound give
  a uniform injectivity radius by Cheeger–Gromov–Taylor.
  Smooth bounded-geometry compactness with the diameter upper
  bound gives a compact smooth limit with convergence by global
  diffeomorphisms. The limit has volume one. If one starts with
  the pointed embedding formulation of smooth compactness,
  bounded diameter gives a compact limit, and once its whole
  domain is embedded the image is open and closed in the
  connected target. [Tian–Viaclovsky, Theorem 4.1][tvmod] ·
  [Cheeger–Gromov–Taylor, §4, Theorem 4.3][cgt] ·
  [Static smooth compactness, Andrews–Hopper Theorem 8.8][ah]
- In the unbounded case set \(\lambda_i=K_i^{-1/2}\) and
  \(\tilde g_i=\lambda_i^{-2}g_i\), based at a maximum point.
  Then \(|\operatorname{Rm}|\le1\) globally and equals \(1\)
  at the base point. The rescaled volumes are \(\lambda_i^{-4}\to
  \infty\), so the scaled diameters tend to infinity by Bishop
  volume comparison using \(|\operatorname{Rm}|\le1\).
  The rescaled volume lower bounds hold at any fixed radius
  for large \(i\) by the corresponding original bound up to
  radius one. Thus the same injectivity and exact \(\epsilon\)-
  regularity arguments give an everywhere smooth complete
  pointed limit with comparison embeddings on neighborhoods
  of all fixed compact sets. The limit is nonflat by its base
  curvature and noncompact by the scaled diameters. It is
  scalar-flat since \(\lambda_i^2 Y_i\to0\), and remains
  Bach-flat by smooth convergence. Its ALE end assertion
  follows independently of the first-bubble construction from
  Tian–Viaclovsky Theorem 1.1 and the Bach-flat remark after
  Theorem 1.3. The hypotheses of that pointed result hold for
  the rescaled sequence with the same Sobolev, \(L^2\) curvature
  and \(b_1\) bounds and a uniform positive volume lower bound.
  Its general Bach-flat conclusion only gives ALE order zero,
  in the differentiable sense given before Theorem 1.1.
  The pointed limit here is smooth everywhere by the stronger
  bounds just obtained. Smooth convergence identifies it with
  the pointed metric limit of their theorem, hence it has
  finitely many order-zero ALE ends. [Tian–Viaclovsky,
  (1.5)–(1.11), Theorems 1.1 and 4.1, and remark after
  Theorem 1.3][tvmod]

In this argument each use of local \(\epsilon\)-regularity is on
balls of radii smaller than half the diameter, using the
uniform upper volume bound on those balls that follows from the
curvature bound in the relevant branch. It uses the exact
constant-scalar Bach equation in the form of Tian–Viaclovsky
(2.4)–(2.5). The original almost-Bach sequence supplies no such
equation. [Tian–Viaclovsky, §§2.1 and 4][tvmod]

## Exclude the two limiting cases

For the compact case, smooth convergence and volume one give
\(R_\infty=Y_{\rm rd}\). By passing the source Yamabe inequality
for each smooth test, the limit conformal class has constant
at least \(Y_{\rm rd}\), and testing with \(1\) shows equality.
Schoen's strict inequality in Theorem 2 on a smooth compact
manifold outside the round conformal class forces this limit
class to be round. In particular \(W_{g_\infty}=0\). Smooth
convergence on the entire manifold then gives \(E_i\to0\),
contradicting \(E_i\ge4\pi^2\).
[Schoen, Theorems 2–3, p. 493][schoen]

For the nonflat pointed limit \((X,g)\), the order-zero ALE
result by itself supplies no positive power of curvature
decay. Ache–Viaclovsky Theorem 1.2 applies in dimension four
with \(k=1\), since obstruction-flatness is Bach-flatness
there and \(R_g=0\). It gives ALE order \(4-2=2\) after
changing the exterior coordinates. The full compact
truncations embed by the smooth convergence. Thus the
end-count argument of E50 applies with the source margin
and shows that the limit has one end. For this step it is
enough to use any further subsequence with convergent
source Weyl energies. [Ache–Viaclovsky, Definition 1.1 and
Theorem 1.2][av] · [E50, (1) and (7)][e50]

For each compactly supported test on \(X\), push it forward
to the rescaled sources by the comparison maps and extend by
zero outside. Passing \(i\to\infty\) removes the rescaled scalar
term. Thus the compact-support quotient infimum \(Y_c(X,g)\)
satisfies \(Y_c(X,g)\ge Y_{\rm rd}\). If \(q\) is the order of
the end group, the escaping annular tests on its Euclidean cover
give \(Y_c(X,g)\le Y_{\rm rd}/\sqrt q\). The factor comes from
the quotient dividing each integral by \(q\), with a square
root in the denominator. It follows \(q=1\) and
\(Y_c(X,g)=Y_{\rm rd}\). This use of the cone tests at the
round Yamabe value needs no link classification.
[E50, (4)–(5)][e50]

### Smooth compactification and the endpoint

Here is a scalar-flat conformal gauge at the order-two end.
In inverted coordinates \(y=x/|x|^2\), set \(\tilde g\) to be
the expression of \(|x|^{-4}g\). Inversion has conformal
Jacobian with orthogonal part depending on angle, so the ALE
bounds give \(\partial^\beta(\tilde g-\delta)=O(|y|^{2-|\beta|})\).
It follows that \(\tilde g\) is \(C^{1,1}\) on a small ball
including \(0\), with scalar curvature bounded there in \(L^\infty\).
For example the first derivatives tend to zero and are
Lipschitz by the bounded second derivatives, using line segments
and continuity across the origin. Smoothness holds off the
origin.

Use the nonnegative Laplacian and solve
\((6\Delta_{\tilde g}+R_{\tilde g})v=0\) with \(v=1\) on a small
sphere, taking the equation in \(W^{2,p}\) on its ball for
fixed \(p>4\). To justify solvability and positivity, rescale a
radius-\(\rho\) ball to \(B_1\). The rescaled metric components
are \(\tilde g(\rho\,\cdot)\), with leading coefficients
uniformly \(O(\rho^2)\) from Euclidean, first-order
coefficients \(O(\rho^2)\), and scalar potential \(O(\rho^2)\)
in \(L^\infty\). The operator is an \(O(\rho^2)\) perturbation
of \(6\Delta_{\rm Eucl}\) from \(W^{2,p}\cap W^{1,p}_0\)
to \(L^p\). The Dirichlet Laplacian inverse and a Neumann
series solve for \(v-1\) and bound its \(W^{2,p}\) norm on the
unit ball by \(O(\rho^2)\). Sobolev embedding makes \(v\)
strictly positive for small \(\rho\). Elliptic regularity
makes it smooth off \(0\). By scalar conformal covariance,
\(v^2\tilde g\) is scalar-flat off the point. Equivalently
\(|x|^{-2}v(x/|x|^2)\) is a positive harmonic factor for
the original scalar-flat metric on its end.
[Gilbarg–Trudinger, Dirichlet \(W^{2,p}\) theorem 9.15][gt] ·
[Lee–Parker, (2.6)–(2.7)][lp]

The hypotheses of Ache–Viaclovsky Theorem 1.8 include more
than continuous extension. To check Definition 1.6, fix a
small annulus dilation \(\varepsilon\) in the inverted chart.
In the dilated annular equation for \(v(\varepsilon\,\cdot)-v(0)\),
the coefficients converge smoothly to those of the flat
Laplacian and the forcing from the scalar potential is
\(O(\varepsilon^2)\) in every fixed differentiability grade on
annuli. The sup norm of the unknown tends to zero by
continuity of \(v\). Interior Schauder estimates on nested fixed
annuli therefore give convergence to zero in each \(C^j\)
there. After returning to the original chart this says
\(\partial^\beta v=o(|y|^{-|\beta|})\) for \(|\beta|\ge1\).
Together with the corresponding estimates for \(\tilde g\),
and a fixed linear dilation so the limiting constant metric
is Euclidean, these are (1.11)–(1.12) of Ache–Viaclovsky.
The end group is trivial, \(v^2\tilde g\) is scalar-flat
and Bach-flat away from the point, and the theorem gives
a smooth extension after a change of coordinates. The
smooth coordinate structure may change at the added point.
Extend its conformal factor positively on the rest of \(X\)
by interpolation to obtain a smooth conformal compactification
\((\overline X,\bar g)\). The vanishing of Bach extends by
smoothness. [Ache–Viaclovsky, Definition 1.6 and Theorem
1.8][av] · [Gilbarg–Trudinger, interior Schauder estimates][gt]

For conformally related metrics the quotient of a compact
test transforms by multiplication by the positive conformal
factor, bijectively on compact tests. On the smooth compactification
a puncture has zero \(H^1\) capacity. A cutoff of a fixed smooth
test, vanishing within distance \(\varepsilon\) of the point
and equal to one outside \(2\varepsilon\), changes its squared
Dirichlet norm by a vanishing amount, since the squared cutoff
gradient costs \(O(\varepsilon^2)\). The \(L^4\) and scalar
terms converge as well. Thus \(Y(\overline X,[\bar g])=Y_c(X,g)\).
At the round value Schoen's equality implication makes
\(\bar g\) conformally round, so \(W_g=0\).
The full embedded core has \(\chi(X)=1\) by E50, and the
end contributes \(1\) to the Euler characteristic formula.
In scalar-flat ALE Chern–Gauss–Bonnet the left side
\(8\pi^2(\chi(X)-1)\) is therefore zero, and its right side
is now \(-\tfrac12\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\,dV_g\).
Thus \(g\) is Ricci-flat and conformally flat, hence flat.
This contradicts its nonzero base curvature. The contradiction
in both branches proves (1).
[Schoen, Theorem 2][schoen] · [Tian–Viaclovsky, (7.2)][tv]
· [E50, topological and Euler identities][e50]

## Uniform remainder for a supplied almost-Bach limit

Take the B29 source sequence and a **supplied** nonflat complete
smooth ALE rescaling with the stated positive differentiable order
and full core embeddings. The compactification step here uses
positive-order bounds through at least five derivatives as in E52.
The supplied smooth convergence and small total Bach norm make
the limit Bach-flat, and \(\lambda_i^2 Y_i\to0\) gives
scalar-flatness. Write the source
limits as \(e,y\) as in E50. Apply E50 and E51 to obtain a
single trivial-group end. The smooth Bach-flat compactification
of E52 has positive Yamabe constant \(Y_{\overline X}=Y_c(X,g)\)
and is a homotopy four-sphere. The topology can also be seen
directly by van Kampen splitting over the spherical link and
Mayer–Vietoris over \(\mathbb Z\), followed by adding a ball.
Its chiral energies agree and equal \(e_X\) by the closed
signature theorem and conformal invariance. [E50][e50] ·
[E51][e51] · [E52][e52]

The nonflat hypothesis forces the compactification to be
nonconformally flat, using the same open Euler identity as
above. The closed Bach-flat threshold gives \(e_X>4\pi^2\) by
E54. For transfer to the subclass in (1) it suffices that
\(e_X\ge4\pi^2\): if the strict reverse inequality held,
Chang–Gursky–Yang Theorem A would identify the smooth core
compactification with standard \(S^4\) and Chang–Chen Theorem
2.1 would make its Bach-flat conformal class flat in Weyl.
The strict recognition step uses positive Yamabe constant,
simple connectivity, and total four-index energy \(8e_X\).
The original chiral energy budgets give \(e_X\le e\le8\pi^2-\delta\).
Thus (1) applies to the compactification. [E54][e54] ·
[Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A][cgy] ·
[Chang–Chen, Theorem 2.1, p. 385][cc]

In the one-ended trivial-link case (6) of E50 gives
\(\tfrac14\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}|^2\,dV_g=e_X\).
Equations (2)–(5) of E50 and (1) here give
\[
e-e_X
\ \ge\ \frac{Y_{\rm rd}^2-y^2}{48}
\ \ge\ \frac{Y_{\rm rd}^2-Y_{\overline X}^2}{48}
\ \ge\ \frac{Y_{\rm rd}^2-(Y_{\rm rd}-d_\delta)^2}{48}
\ >\ 0 .
\tag{3}
\]
The last constant is uniform and unspecified. If the exact
subclass is empty, the preceding reduction already excludes such
a nonflat supplied limit. The estimates do not identify the
geometry carrying the remainder or supply full embeddings for
any other concentration scales.
