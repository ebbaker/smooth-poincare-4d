## Statement and convention

Let \(g\) be a smooth positive-definite metric on a smooth closed
oriented four-manifold \(M\). Fix a smoothly parameterized
orientation-preserving extended coordinate ball
\(\psi:B_{1+d}(0)\to M\), \(d>0\), with marked
\(K=\psi(\overline B_1(0))\) and center \(p=\psi(0)\).
Write

\[
 E_+(g)=\int_M |W_g^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\,dV_g,\qquad
 \mathfrak B(g)=\int_M |B_g|_g\,dV_g.
\]

The norm of \(W^+\) is the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm
in [SH-B01][common], and \(B_g\) is the covariant two-tensor in the
Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo convention. For sufficiently small
\(\varepsilon>0\) there is a smooth metric \(\widehat g_\varepsilon\)
with the unit round stereographic metric on the prescribed marked ball
and

\[
 |E_+(\widehat g_\varepsilon)-E_+(g)|\le C_E\varepsilon^4,\qquad
 |\mathfrak B(\widehat g_\varepsilon)-\mathfrak B(g)|
       \le C_B\varepsilon^2.
 \tag{1}
\]

The small parameter is measured in normal coordinates at \(p\) before
marking. The constants depend on a fixed metric germ and the fixed
cutoff. This statement makes no scalar curvature or Yamabe sign
assumption. It applies in particular to the homotopy four-spheres
of [B19][b19].

## The small flat replacement and derivatives

Choose oriented \(g\)-normal coordinates \(x\) about \(p\) on a ball
whose closure is in the extended marked chart. There
\(g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}+q_{ij}\), where \(q(0)=0\), \(Dq(0)=0\).
On a fixed smaller coordinate neighborhood,
\(|D^j q(x)|\le C|x|^{2-j}\) for \(j=0,1\), and
\(D^j q\) is bounded for \(2\le j\le4\). Use a fixed smooth cutoff
\(\chi\) on \(\mathbb R^4\) with \(0\le\chi\le1\), equal to zero on
\(\overline B_1\) and one on \(\mathbb R^4\setminus B_2\). Set

\[
 (g_\varepsilon)_{ij}(x)=\delta_{ij}+\chi(x/\varepsilon)q_{ij}(x).
 \tag{2}
\]

It is a convex combination of positive metric matrices, exactly flat
on \(|x|\le\varepsilon\) and equal to \(g\) on \(|x|\ge2\varepsilon\).
Extend \(g_\varepsilon\) by \(g\) elsewhere. The interpolation is
smooth across both cutoff interfaces. On \(B_{2\varepsilon}\) the
metric and its inverse are uniformly comparable to the Euclidean ones,
and \(dV_{g_\varepsilon}\le C\,dx\) there.

For \(0<\varepsilon\le1\), a product-rule term of order \(j\)
with \(a\) cutoff derivatives and \(b=j-a\) derivatives of \(q\)
is \(O(\varepsilon^{-a}\varepsilon^{2-b})\). For \(b=0,1\)
this uses vanishing of the first jet and the shrinking ball.
For \(b\ge2\) it follows from the fixed derivative bound and
\(\varepsilon^{2-b}\ge1\). Thus throughout \(B_{2\varepsilon}\)
the coordinate estimates are

\[
 \begin{array}{c|ccccc}
 j&0&1&2&3&4\\ \hline
 |D^j(g_\varepsilon-\delta)|
 &O(\varepsilon^2)&O(\varepsilon)&O(1)
 &O(\varepsilon^{-1})&O(\varepsilon^{-2}).
 \end{array}
 \tag{3}
\]

Differentiating the inverse matrix gives \(Dg_\varepsilon^{-1}
=O(\varepsilon)\), \(D^2g_\varepsilon^{-1}=O(1)\), and
\(D^3g_\varepsilon^{-1}=O(\varepsilon^{-1})\). The Christoffel
symbols are linear in \(Dg_\varepsilon\) with coefficients from
the inverse. Consequently \(\Gamma=O(\varepsilon)\),
\(D\Gamma=O(1)\), \(D^2\Gamma=O(\varepsilon^{-1})\), and
\(D^3\Gamma=O(\varepsilon^{-2})\).
The formula for curvature as first derivatives of \(\Gamma\)
plus quadratic products of \(\Gamma\) gives the coordinate
curvature bounds. To pass to \(\nabla\mathrm{Rm}\) and
\(\nabla^2\mathrm{Rm}\), the additional terms involve respectively
\(\Gamma\,\mathrm{Rm}\) and
\(D\Gamma\,\mathrm{Rm}\), \(\Gamma\,D\mathrm{Rm}\),
and \(\Gamma^2\mathrm{Rm}\), with fixed sums of contractions.
All are within the stated orders. With the intrinsic norms,
on the transition and indeed on \(B_{2\varepsilon}\),

\[
 |\mathrm{Rm}_{g_\varepsilon}|\le C,\qquad
 |\nabla\mathrm{Rm}_{g_\varepsilon}|\le C\varepsilon^{-1},\qquad
 |\nabla^2\mathrm{Rm}_{g_\varepsilon}|\le C\varepsilon^{-2}.
 \tag{4}
\]

## Bach sign, local cost and conformal weight

With \(P=\frac12(\mathrm{Ric}-\frac16s\,g)\), use the sign of
Bahuaud–Guenther–Isenberg–Mazzeo, equation (1.1). In their
covariant derivative and curvature index convention,

\[
 B_{ij}=P_{ij,k}{}^k-P_{ik,j}{}^k+P^{kl}W_{kijl}.
 \tag{5}
\]

In particular, their Weyl functional
\(\mathcal W(g)=\int_M |W_g|_{(0,4)}^2\,dV_g\) has first
variation \(-4\int_M\langle B_g,h\rangle_g\,dV_g\) for a smooth
covariant metric variation \(h\) on the closed manifold.
On a homotopy four-sphere, \(\mathcal W=8E_+\) by signature
and the norm conversion, so the corresponding coefficient for
\(dE_+[h]\) is \(-1/2\). [BGI+M, §1 and equation (1.1)][bgim]
· [SH-E01][sh] · [E24, equation (1)][e24]

Parallelism of \(g_\varepsilon\) lets the two covariant
derivatives in (5) commute with the contractions defining \(P\)
from \(\mathrm{Rm}\). Thus (4) bounds \(B_{g_\varepsilon}\) by a
constant times \(|\nabla^2\mathrm{Rm}_{g_\varepsilon}|
+|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_\varepsilon}|^2\), hence by \(C\varepsilon^{-2}\).
The chiral Weyl projection and scalar subtraction give
\(|W_{g_\varepsilon}^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}\le C\).
The original \(B_g,W^+_g\) are bounded on the fixed normal
neighborhood. Both original and replaced densities agree
outside \(B_{2\varepsilon}\), and the replaced densities
vanish in the flat core. Integrating the sum of the original
and replaced absolute densities over \(B_{2\varepsilon}\)
therefore yields

\[
 |E_+(g_\varepsilon)-E_+(g)|\le C_E\varepsilon^4,\qquad
 |\mathfrak B(g_\varepsilon)-\mathfrak B(g)|
       \le C_B\varepsilon^2.
 \tag{6}
\]

For an arbitrary smooth positive factor \(u\), the
dimension-four covariance in the same convention is
\(B_{u^2g}=u^{-2} B_g\) as a covariant two-tensor.
This weight can be checked from the signed variation above.
For the fixed \(u\), use \(\mathcal W(u^2(g+t h))=\mathcal W(g+t h)\)
and differentiate. Contracting the left derivative with \(u^2 h\)
uses two inverse metrics of weight \(-2\) and a volume of
weight \(+4\). Equality of the variations for all \(h\) yields
\(u^2 B_{u^2g}=B_g\). This is also stated directly in the
primary source after (1.1). [BGI+M, §1, pp. 1–2][bgim]

Taking the norm of a covariant two-tensor with \(u^2g\)
contributes a further factor \(u^{-2}\).
Therefore \(|B_{u^2g}|_{u^2g}=u^{-4}|B_g|_g\), and
\(|B_{u^2g}|_{u^2g}\,dV_{u^2g}=|B_g|_g\,dV_g\) pointwise.
This proves invariance of the integral of the norm itself
under arbitrary smooth positive scales. Both \(\mathfrak B\)
and \(E_+\) are invariant under orientation-preserving
diffeomorphisms by tensoriality and change of variables.
Conformal invariance of \(E_+\) follows from the unchanged
Hodge splitting, Weyl-operator weight \(-2\), and volume
weight \(+4\). [SH-E01][sh] · [E25][e25]

## Marking and scope of uniformity

The flat core of (2) is an oriented conformally Euclidean
germ at the center of the marked ball. Apply the marking
construction of [E25][e25] to this germ. Its oriented
transition germ extends by a localized flow in the
extended marked chart. A radial compression sends a
neighborhood of \(K\) to the small germ. A smooth positive
factor extended using a cutoff logarithm gives the
prescribed unit round stereographic metric on a neighborhood
of \(K\). Denote the resulting metric by
\(\widehat g_\varepsilon=u_\varepsilon^2\phi_\varepsilon^*
g_\varepsilon\). Here \(\phi_\varepsilon\) is an
orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of the same \(M\).
The marking step needs only the small germ and does not
invoke the separate approximation theorem used in E25.
As the two integrals are exactly invariant in this step,
(6) gives (1).

Before marking the replacement is supported in the
normal-coordinate \(\overline B_{2\varepsilon}\).
After marking the corresponding difference from
\(u_\varepsilon^2\phi_\varepsilon^*g\) is supported in
\(\phi_\varepsilon^{-1}(\overline B_{2\varepsilon})\),
with the normal chart implicit in this notation. In
comparison with the original \(g\), the diffeomorphism and
conformal factor can both be supported in the extended
marked chart. The final metric thus equals \(g\) outside
that chart. This construction makes no shrinking support
or uniform derivative claim about the final metric in the
original marking.

If a sequence of metrics has the same normal-coordinate
representative on a fixed neighborhood of \(p\), then all
constants and an admissible starting radius can be chosen
there once for the sequence. If their germs agree only on
possibly shrinking neighborhoods, the constants from the
common germ still apply when each radius is chosen within
its own neighborhood. A uniform admissible radius would
require a common domain or equivalent uniform local
control. The estimates use the smooth germ through order
four and metric comparability. They give no uniform
constant for a sequence without such local control, and
they do not imply global compactness.
