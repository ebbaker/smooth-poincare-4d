## Statement and norms

Let \((M^4,g)\) be a smooth oriented real Riemannian spin manifold.
Use the untwisted half-spin bundles \(S^\pm\), with self-dual two-forms
acting on \(S^+\), and the Levi–Civita spin connection \(\nabla\).
The Clifford action satisfies \(c(X)^2=-|X|^2\) for real \(X\).
For the Dirac operator \(D=\sum_a c(e_a)\nabla_{e_a}\) in a real
orthonormal frame, put

\[
 (P_{\rm tw}\pi)_X=\nabla_X\pi+\tfrac14c(X)D\pi .
\]

Here \(D\) takes positive spinors to negative spinors. The complex
extension in \(X\) carries the Hermitian tensor norm induced by \(g\)
and the spinor metrics, so \(|P_{\rm tw}\pi|^2=\sum_a|(P_{\rm tw}\pi)_{e_a}|^2\).
This is the convention of [SH-B01][common] and [DIR-E01, §4][dir],
using Friedrich's twistor equation (1.1), p. 59. [Friedrich][friedrich]

Let \(j\) be a parallel antiunitary quaternionic structure on \(S^+\).
For a smooth positive spinor \(\psi\), set \(U=\{\psi\ne0\}\) and use
the following spinors on \(U\):

\[
 \pi_1=\psi,\qquad
 \pi_2=(\psi+j\psi)/\sqrt2,\qquad
 \pi_3=(\psi+\mathrm i\,j\psi)/\sqrt2.
\]

For each projective line \([\pi_i]\), let \(J_i\) be the positive
orthogonal almost complex structure with
\(\mathcal D_i=T^{0,1}_{J_i}=S^-\otimes\mathbb C\pi_i\). Put
\(P_i=(I+\mathrm i J_i)/2\), \(Q_i=I-P_i\), and
\(F_i(X,Y)=Q_i[P_iX,P_iY]\). The unit axes
\(g(J_i\,\cdot,\cdot)/\sqrt2\) are orthonormal in \(\Lambda^+\), and
pointwise on \(U\)

\[
 \sum_{i=1}^3 |F_i|^2
 \le 3\,\frac{|P_{\rm tw}\psi|^2}{|\psi|^2}.
 \tag{1}
\]

The \(F_i\) use Hermitian norms as elements of
\(\Lambda^2(T_{\mathbb C}^*M)\otimes T_{\mathbb C}M\), with each
unordered exterior pair counted once as in [SEC-E01][sec1]. The
constant is \(6\) when both ordered tensor slots are summed. These
constants are sufficient and their optimality is outside the packet.

## Reality and orthogonality of the directions

Use a parallel equivariant isometry \(T_{\mathbb C}M\simeq
S^-\otimes S^+\) with the product Hermitian metric. The underlying
\(\operatorname{Spin}(4)=\operatorname{SU}(2)_-\times
\operatorname{SU}(2)_+\) model for the complexified vector
representation is the tensor of the two defining representations.
Its invariant Hermitian metric is unique up to scale, so normalize
the identification to be isometric. Real conjugation on
\(T_{\mathbb C}M\) corresponds up to a constant unit phase to
\(j_-\otimes j\), where \(j_-\) is the negative quaternionic structure.
Indeed, both are equivariant antiunitary involutions and their composite
is an equivariant linear unitary map on an irreducible representation.

For a general nonzero \(\pi\) on an open set, put \(r=|\pi|\),
\(u=\pi/r\), and let \(p=u u^*\), \(q=I-p\) be the two spinor
projections. Antiunitarity and \(j^2=-I\) give
\(\langle v,jv\rangle=0\) for every \(v\in S^+\).
Thus \(\overline{\mathcal D_\pi}=S^-\otimes\mathbb C(ju)\) is its
Hermitian orthogonal complement. These complementary planes are
individually null for the complex-bilinear metric. The tangent
projectors are \(P=I_{S^-}\otimes p\), \(Q=I_{S^-}\otimes q\), and
\(J=-\mathrm i(P-Q)\) commutes with conjugation and squares to \(-I\).
It is orthogonal, and its skew endomorphism lies in the positive
factor since \(-\mathrm i(2p-I)\in\mathfrak{su}(S^+)\) acts on the
positive tensor factor. Its Kähler two-form is therefore self-dual
and the induced orientation is positive. This uses the two chiral
factors, compatible projective directions and Clifford action in
AHS §1, pp. 428–429, and the null-plane correspondence of GHN
Proposition 3.1. [AHS][ahs] · [Gover–Hill–Nurowski][ghn] ·
[First-order manuscript, `eq:projective-frobenius`][fot]

For two such directions put \(H_i=2p_i-I\), where \(p_i=u_i u_i^*\),
and \(n_i=g(J_i\,\cdot,\cdot)/\sqrt2\). Since the norm squared of the
two-form of a skew endomorphism is half its Hilbert–Schmidt norm
squared, and complexification preserves the trace of a real
endomorphism, one gets

\[
 \langle n_i,n_k\rangle
 =\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}(J_i^*J_k)
 =\tfrac12\operatorname{tr}(H_iH_k)
 =2|\langle u_i,u_k\rangle|^2-1 .
\]

The second trace is on \(S^+\) and the factor two in passing from it
to the tangent trace is \(\dim_{\mathbb C}S^-=2\).
In the unitary frame \((\psi,j\psi)/|\psi|\), each of \(\pi_1,\pi_2,\pi_3\)
has length \(|\psi|\), and their unit representatives have coordinates
\((1,0)\), \((1,1)/\sqrt2\), and \((1,\mathrm i)/\sqrt2\). The three
pairwise squared inner products equal \(1/2\). This proves the asserted
orthogonality of the real axes.

## The derivative of one line and the Clifford trace

For real \(X\), differentiation of \(p=uu^*\) gives

\[
 \nabla_X p=v_Xu^*+uv_X^*,\qquad
 v_X=q\nabla_Xu=\frac{q\nabla_X\pi}{r}.
 \tag{2}
\]

The vertical part is the derivative into the perpendicular line.
The component of \(\nabla_Xu\) in \(\mathbb C u\) is purely imaginary
and cancels between the terms in \(\nabla_X p\). For complex \(X\), the
identity \((\nabla_Xp)u=v_X\) extends complex linearly from the real
directions. This gives the derivative of the tangent plane projector
\(P=I_{S^-}\otimes p\) in the parallel tensor product model.

The Clifford action of a decomposable complex vector \(a\otimes u\)
on a negative spinor \(\chi\) is

\[
 c(a\otimes u)\chi=\kappa\,\varepsilon_-(a,\chi)\,u ,
 \tag{3}
\]

where \(\varepsilon_-\) is the invariant alternating complex bilinear
form on \(S^-\) and \(\kappa\) is a fixed nonzero normalization scalar.
In fact the action is equivariant and complex linear on this tensor
product. The two \(S^-\) slots contract by the invariant alternating
form of the defining \(\operatorname{SU}(2)_-\) representation, and
the positive factor is a scalar multiple of the identity by
equivariance. This establishes (3) with the sign and phase absorbed
in \(\kappa\). For \(X\in\mathcal D_\pi\) it follows that

\[
 q\,c(X)D\pi=0,\qquad
 v_X=\frac{q(P_{\rm tw}\pi)_X}{r}.
 \tag{4}
\]

For \(X,Y\in\mathcal D_\pi\) write \(X=a\otimes u\), \(Y=b\otimes u\).
For any smooth extensions of this type, the product connection gives
\(Q\nabla_XY=b\otimes v_X\). The derivative of \(b\) contributes only
to the projected-out \(\mathbb C u\) factor. Torsion freeness now
gives the exact formula

\[
 F(X,Y)=b\otimes v_X-a\otimes v_Y
 \qquad(X,Y\in\mathcal D_\pi).
 \tag{5}
\]

The projected bracket is tensorial because derivatives of scalar
multipliers lie in the \(P\)-plane and are killed by \(Q\). Thus \(F\)
is determined by (5) and has input projection \(P\wedge P\).
Choose two orthonormal vectors \(a_1,a_2\) in \(S^-\). The resulting
\(X_\ell=a_\ell\otimes u\) are a unitary basis of \(\mathcal D_\pi\)
and extend to one of \(T_{\mathbb C}M\) by adding a basis of the
complement. Only \(F(X_1,X_2)\) contributes to the exterior-pair norm.
The two terms in (5) have orthogonal negative spinor factors, giving

\[
 |F|^2=\frac{1}{r^2}\sum_{\ell=1}^2
               |q(P_{\rm tw}\pi)_{X_\ell}|^2
 \le \frac{|P_{\rm tw}\pi|^2}{|\pi|^2}.
 \tag{6}
\]

The same computation sums two equal contributions for the ordered
norm. The real-frame definition of \(|P_{\rm tw}\pi|\) agrees with the
norm computed in any unitary basis of the complexified tangent space.

## The three residuals and their transitions

For real \(X,Y\), the even Clifford product on \(S^+\) is
\(c(X)c(Y)=-g(X,Y)I+\frac12[c(X),c(Y)]\).
The commutator acts by the spin Lie algebra and commutes with \(j\).
Indeed, in an oriented unitary spin frame the defining
\(\operatorname{SU}(2)\) action preserves the quaternionic structure.
Any antiunitary quaternionic structure on this complex two-dimensional
space is a unit-phase multiple of that structure, as follows from
the unitary basis \((u,ju)\). Thus \(j\) commutes with the real
even Clifford action on \(S^+\). Since \(j\) is parallel and
\((P_{\rm tw}\pi)_X=\nabla_X\pi+\frac14\sum_a
c(X)c(e_a)\nabla_{e_a}\pi\) for real \(X\), it follows that

\[
 (P_{\rm tw}(j\psi))_X=j((P_{\rm tw}\psi)_X).
\]

For any such \(X\) write \(v=(P_{\rm tw}\psi)_X\).
The corresponding components for \(\pi_2,\pi_3\) are
\((v+jv)/\sqrt2\) and \((v+\mathrm i jv)/\sqrt2\), both of length
\(|v|\). Summing over a real orthonormal frame gives
\(|P_{\rm tw}\pi_i|=|P_{\rm tw}\psi|\) for all \(i\).
Combining this equality and \(|\pi_i|=|\psi|\) with (6) proves (1).

Under a spin frame transition, each \(\pi_i\) transforms by the same
positive factor as \(\psi\) because \(j\) is equivariant. The tangent
planes, projectors, bracket tensors and their norms are intrinsic.
Under multiplication of an individual representative \(\pi\) by a
smooth nonvanishing complex function \(\lambda\), the line is fixed.
In (2), \(u\) and \(v_X\) acquire the unit phase \(\lambda/|\lambda|\)
because the derivatives of \(\lambda\) are killed by \(q\).
The coefficients \(a,b\) then acquire the inverse phase in (5), so
the expression for \(F\) agrees across projective rescalings. Equation
(1) uses the three specific lines made from the supplied spinor.
Changing the phase of that spinor as a field can change the two
auxiliary lines even when its own line is fixed.

Where \(P_{\rm tw}\psi\) vanishes on an open subset of \(U\), the
three plane fields are involutive there, equivalently the three
structures are integrable. This is a pointwise first-jet bridge
with the explicit divisor \(|\psi|^2\). Differentiated estimates,
control at zeros and any use requiring existence of a qualifying
spinor on a prescribed metric lie outside the result.
