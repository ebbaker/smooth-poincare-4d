## Setting and normalization

Let \(M\) be any smooth, closed, connected, oriented four-manifold homotopy
equivalent to \(S^4\), and let \(g\) be a smooth positive-definite Riemannian
metric on \(M\). Then \(\pi_1(M)=0\), \(H^2(M;\mathbb R)=0\), \(\tau(M)=0\)
and \(\chi(M)=2\). Here \(\tau\) is the signature of the oriented real
intersection form. Define \(W_g^\pm\) on the \(\pm1\) Hodge eigenspaces
\(\Lambda_g^\pm\). The ASD condition is \(W_g^+=0\), and
\(E_\pm(g)=\int_M|W_g^\pm|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\,dV_g\). These are the
[shared conventions][common] and the conventions of the [first-order
manuscript][fot].

In an orthonormal coframe, the entries of the Weyl operator in the
orthonormal basis \(e^i\wedge e^j\), \(i<j\), are \(W_{ijkl}\) with
\(i<j\), \(k<l\). The operator acts by
\((W\omega)_{ij}=\tfrac12 W_{ij}{}^{kl}\omega_{kl}\) with summation over all
ordered pairs. Summing the squares over all four indices counts each
unordered pair twice in each slot. The two Hodge blocks are orthogonal,
so the same conversion applies to each block and gives

\[
 |W_g^\pm|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2=4|W_g^\pm|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2,
 \qquad
 |W_g|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2=4\bigl(|W_g^+|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2+
                                |W_g^-|_{\mathrm{op,HS}}^2\bigr).
\]

This agrees with the operator identification and norms in [Chang–Gursky–Yang,
Remark 2, p. 107][cgy].

The ordinary signature theorem gives
\(3\tau(M)=\langle p_1(TM),[M]\rangle\) for a compact oriented differentiable
four-manifold without boundary. [Hirzebruch, §4, p. 11, eq. (16)][hirz].
For the normalization, use the real Levi-Civita curvature matrix \(\Omega\).
The Chern–Weil representative of \(p_1(TM)\) is
\(-\operatorname{tr}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)/(8\pi^2)\). By skew symmetry
this is \(\sum_{i<j}\Omega_{ij}\wedge\Omega_{ij}/(4\pi^2)\).
The curvature symmetries identify its rows with those of the self-adjoint
curvature operator \(\mathcal R\), up to the common curvature sign.
Using \(\alpha\wedge\alpha=\langle\alpha,*\alpha\rangle\,dV_g\),
the density is \(\operatorname{tr}(*\mathcal R^2)/(4\pi^2)\) times
\(dV_g\). The off-diagonal Ricci blocks have equal Hilbert–Schmidt
norms and cancel. The diagonal blocks are \(W^\pm+(s/12)I\). Their
scalar terms cancel in the difference of their squared norms because
\(\operatorname{tr}W^\pm=0\) and both blocks have rank three. Thus

\[
 12\pi^2\tau(M)=E_+(g)-E_-(g).
\]

The four-index formula is \(48\pi^2\tau(M)\) equal to the difference
of the four-index block integrals, as in [Gover–Gursky, pp. 293–294
and 308][gg]. In particular \(E_-=E_+\) and the four-index integral
of the full Weyl tensor on \(M\) is exactly \(8E_+\).

## Supplied ASD metric

If \(W_g^+=0\), then \(E_+=0\) and hence \(E_-=0\). The smooth
nonnegative density of \(E_-\) vanishes everywhere, so \(W_g=0\).
In real dimension four the Weyl-flatness criterion gives locally
conformally flat charts for the smooth Riemannian metric. Regard
them as charts into the round \(S^4\). By Liouville's theorem, on each
connected overlap component the transition extends to a Möbius
transformation. An extension is unique by its values on an open set.
This uniqueness gives the cocycle on triple overlap components, and
analytic continuation across chart chains depends only on the path
homotopy class relative to its endpoints. In particular, subdividing
a homotopy into chart-contained pieces changes chart chains by these
same transition identities. Simple connectivity of \(M\) makes the
result a single smooth conformal local diffeomorphism
\(\operatorname{dev}:M\to S^4\). The Weyl-flatness and development
inputs are [Schoen–Yau, p. 47 and §1, pp. 49–50][sy].

The image is open by local invertibility and closed by compactness of
\(M\). Connectedness of \(S^4\) gives surjectivity. Each fiber is finite
because it is compact and discrete. For each fiber, take disjoint smooth
inverse-chart neighborhoods of its points. Properness permits shrinking
the common target neighborhood to exclude other inverse images, so it
is evenly covered. The connected domain and simply connected target give
one sheet. The smooth inverse charts therefore give a global smooth
inverse, and local conformal factors patch to the smooth positive
function \(\tfrac14\operatorname{tr}_g(\operatorname{dev}^*g_{\mathrm{round}})\)
with \(\operatorname{dev}^*g_{\mathrm{round}}\) equal to that function
times \(g\). This is precisely the conformal diffeomorphism for each
supplied ASD metric in the [CME theorem `thm:ctx-ASD-sphere`][cme].

Conversely, pulling back the round metric along a diffeomorphism gives
a locally conformally flat metric, which is ASD. Thus the three
existence statements in the CME theorem are equivalent. The forward
recognition from a supplied ASD metric works for every scalar-curvature
sign and uses only the smooth Riemannian, topological and Weyl hypotheses
specified above.

## Supplied metric satisfying strict Weyl and Yamabe bounds

Suppose \(Y(M,[g])>0\) and \(E_+(g)<4\pi^2\). Here \(Y(M,[g])\) is the
infimum of \(\operatorname{Vol}(\widehat g)^{-1/2}\int_M s_{\widehat g}\,dV_{\widehat g}\)
over smooth metrics \(\widehat g\) conformal to \(g\). This is the constant
of the supplied conformal class in [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Remark 1][cgy].
The Weyl integral uses the same class. Indeed, a positive rescaling
\(\widehat g=u^2g\) leaves the Hodge splitting on two-forms unchanged,
rescales each Weyl endomorphism by \(u^{-2}\) and multiplies the volume
form by \(u^4\). The resulting quadratic curvature densities are
conformally invariant.

The full tensor energy satisfies

\[
 \int_M |W_g|_{4\mathrm{ind}}^2\,dV_g
 =4(E_++E_-)=8E_+<32\pi^2=16\pi^2\chi(M).
\]

This is exactly the strict hypothesis in [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem A,
eq. (0.3) and Remark 2][cgy]. Theorem A gives a diffeomorphism to
\(S^4\) or \(\mathbb{RP}^4\). The latter has fundamental group
\(\mathbb Z/2\), whereas \(M\) is simply connected. The conclusion of
this application concerns the smooth type of \(M\). Its hypotheses
include both the strict inequality and Yamabe positivity.
