## The two physical native cuts

Put \(Y_j=\#_j(S^1\times S^2)\). The forward link is GST Figure 1, \(L_{n,1}=Q\cup V_n\) in \(S^3\), \(n\ge 3\), with both framings zero and joint outlet \(Y_2\). The upper body \(H=\natural_2(S^1\times D^3)\) is specified by the two dotted handles in Figure 9. Use the carried marking \(\alpha:\partial H\to Y_2\) and the forward closing \(\gamma=\alpha^{-1}\). For the outgoing tube chart \(o_i\) of a forward handle, its reverse chart is \(\gamma o_i\tau\) with inverse \(\tau^{-1}o_i^{-1}\gamma^{-1}\) on its image, where \(\tau\) exchanges the disk and circle factors. Figures 9–13 carry the two ordinary tubes through the bracketed ambient moves. The ordinary tubes never slide over each other in that comparison. The Figure 9 attaching words, up to separate conjugation and inversion, are \(r=yxy(xyx)^{-1}\) and \(s=x^{n+1}y^{-n}\). GST Lemma 2.1 exchanges the moving and stationary roles on the dual link. Scharlemann’s Lemma 7.1 identifies the one-handle reverse surgery trace for \(V_n^*\) with braid group \(\langle a,b\mid aba=bab\rangle\). This group has central quotient \(C_2*C_3\), whereas the central quotient of \(F(x,y)/\langle\!\langle s\rangle\!\rangle\) is \(C_{n+1}*C_n\). Their abelianizations have different orders for \(n\ge 3\). For each torus group its common power is central and its image under the quotient is trivial, and the free product has trivial center. This identifies \(r\) with \(V_n^*\) and \(s\) with \(Q^*\) in the literal diagram. The cut has the opposite boundary orientation on the upper side. Above each cut in the forward order the one remaining 2-handle is followed by the closing 3- and 4-handles. Attaching these to the cut collar makes \(\pi_1(Z_K)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(W_K)\), since their indices are at least two. Equivalently, \(\partial H\to H\) gives the free-group identification and attaching the indicated single reverse handle quotients by its relator. The lower ordinary trace \(B^4\cup h_K\) is simply connected. The one-relator group therefore belongs to the upper side. In the reverse description the surjection runs from the cut to the attached side. It is a map of groups induced by inclusion, and no degree of a map from the cut into the four-dimensional side is involved.

For a positive-genus fibered knot, longitude filling caps its Seifert fibers to give a closed surface bundle over \(S^1\). Both \(Q\) and \(V_n=T_{n,n+1}\#\overline{T}_{n,n+1}\) are fibered, with genera two and \(n(n-1)\), respectively. Their closed bundle cuts are aspherical, since the pullback over \(\mathbb R\to S^1\) is the product of the fiber with \(\mathbb R\), and the fiber has contractible universal cover. Irreducibility also follows directly from Gabai's Corollary 8.3. The zero surgery is prime and its least genus of an oriented nonseparating surface is the knot genus. A separating essential sphere would violate primeness, and positive genus rules out a nonseparating sphere. This argument proves irreducibility for the zero surgery on any nontrivial knot in \(S^3\), whether fibered or otherwise. Both native cuts have \(H_1(-;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\). The two irreducible rank-two cuts of the separate \(Y_1\to Y_3\) fixture in 05 are a different result. ## What domination would imply

For any intermediate cut of a genuine one-sided two-middle presentation with incoming \(S^3\) and full outlet \(Y_2\), the forward two-component linking matrix is zero. Its cokernel has rank two, forcing the whole matrix, including both framings, to vanish. This applies after physical reverse slides whose transported inverse diagram is read in the marked \(S^3\). Let \(Z=S^3_0(K)\) be the first-forward cut and \(V=B^4\cup h_K\) the lower trace. The degree condition is a continuous map \(f:P^3\to Z\) of any nonzero integer degree from a closed oriented PSC three-manifold. If the source is disconnected, a component of nonzero degree suffices.

The 05 zero-matrix lemma (`lem:zero`, case \(j=1\)) follows through as written. The intersection form of \(V\) is zero, \(\pi_1V=1\), and \(H_2(V;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z\). In the pair sequence the terms bracketing \(H_2(Z)\to H_2(V)\) give an injective and surjective map, since \(H_3(V,Z)\cong H^1(V)=0\) and \(H_2(V)\to H_2(V,Z)\) is the zero intersection map. Proposition 2.2 of Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee makes the dominated target a connected sum of spherical space forms and product factors. The first homology forces precisely one product factor and torsion-free summands, so the rest is an integral homology sphere \(\Sigma\) with PSC. Its nontrivial prime factors are signed Poincaré spheres by their Corollary 2.3. Compression along the product sphere uses one auxiliary 3-handle whose relative boundary map is an integral unit. The compressed filling of \(\Sigma\) is smooth, simply connected and acyclic, hence contractible. Their Proposition 4.2 forces \(\Sigma\cong S^3\). Conversely, a product metric and an oriented identification with \(S^1\times S^2\) supply a degree \(+1\) witness. Thus PSC domination here is equivalent to \(Z\cong S^1\times S^2\). The auxiliary compression is used to identify the boundary. Two certificate paths follow.

- If a specified \(\rho:F(x,y)\to SU(2)\) kills the actual reverse relator \(w\) for the upper side and has nonabelian image, it descends to \(G_w\). The cut representation obtained by precomposing with \(\pi_1Z\twoheadrightarrow G_w\) has the same image. Domination would give \(\pi_1Z\cong\mathbb Z\), impossible. This works for the native relators and for an actual slid relator only after verifying the equality \(\rho(w)=1\). Independently, each native cut is irreducible with positive first Betti number, so the decomposition in Proposition 2.2 alone excludes domination on either native cut. A nonabelian representation by itself is not a general obstruction on PSC three-manifolds.
- If the actual reverse word \(w\) is known to be nonprimitive, apply the 05 penultimate-level theorem (`thm:last`) to the ordinary simply connected forward prefix. Domination forces the last forward 2-handle to cancel with an upper 3-handle after an extending re-marking. In detail, \(Z\cong Y_1\) and the last nonmeridional surgery is \(Y_1\to Y_2\). The rank increase of one in Meier–Schirmer–Zupan Theorem 5.1 identifies a disk-framed local unknot at that last attachment. Its dual meets the new sphere once, which gives primitivity in the rank-two upper group. The lower first knot is also the zero-framed unknot by Property R, making the lower side \(S^2\times D^2\). The upper cancellation identifies the upper side with \(S^1\times D^3\), with transported marking. These standard-piece conclusions require the geometric recognition step. The irreducible-cut argument only gives a PSC obstruction. ## The actual doubled disk exterior and meridian

Take the marked 4-ball \(B'\) on the upper side of the forward inlet \(S^3\), obtained by completing the Figure 9 reverse handles before the final reverse cap. GST §8, p. 2334 identifies this ball and its two proper slice disks as the original forward 2-handle cores. For \(K=Q\) or \(V_n\), let \(D_K\) be the corresponding disk. The full 2-handle \(D^2\times D^2\) gives its normal product neighborhood with the prescribed incoming zero framing. Removing its interior, with corners rounded, gives exactly \(W_K\). Its boundary is \(Z_K=E_K\cup(D^2\times S^1)\), where \(E_K=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu K\) is the original knot exterior in the marked inlet. The inclusions give
\[
\pi_1(E_K)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(Z_K)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(W_K).
\]
The first map is the quotient by the zero longitude from Dehn filling, since the fundamental group of the added solid torus is generated by its boundary image. The second is the relative handle argument above. The disk meridian at the outgoing face is \(\{0\}\times S^1\subset D^2\times S^1\). It is the first-forward belt core. It is carried through the remaining forward attachment in the common marked exterior, then represented in \(\partial H\) by the other reverse attaching core via \(\gamma o_i\tau\). This proves the last column of the table up to conjugacy and inversion. The ball identification also follows from the closed recognition above. The closed trace is simply connected with Euler characteristic two, hence a homotopy sphere. After the Meier–Zupan diffeomorphism to \(S^4\), Palais's Theorem B carries the embedded forward bottom ball to a standard hemisphere by an ambient diffeomorphism, so its complement \(B'\) is a smooth 4-ball. The theorem compares two parametrized embedded balls in the same manifold. [Palais, *Extending diffeomorphisms*, Proc. Amer. Math. Soc. 11 (1960), Theorem B](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/238867175_Extending_diffeomorphisms)

Double \((B',D_K)\) using the identity of the marked boundary, reversing the orientation of the second copy. The two balls give \(S^4\) and the doubled disk is a smooth two-sphere after collar rounding. Doubling its normal product gives \(S^2\times D^2\). Its actual exterior is
\[
X_K=W_K\cup_{E_K}\overline{W_K},\qquad
\partial X_K=S^2\times S^1.
\]
The pushout of the two identical surjections \(\pi_1(E_K)\twoheadrightarrow G_w\) is \(G_w\). Indeed, the fold map \(X_K\to W_K\) is identity on one half and reflects the other onto it. Surjectivity of the common inclusion identifies every group generator of the second half with its first-half copy. Thus either half inclusion gives an isomorphism on \(\pi_1\) with the fold as inverse. This is a marked group map from the actual exterior and it identifies its peripheral meridian with that listed in the table. The ordinary \(S^2\times D^2\) gluing restores the double of \(B'\). No isotopy to a chosen spun or ribbon model, and no GST slide to a split lower zero, enters this construction.

At \(n=3\) the three stored representations with \(s\) killed extend by the fold to \(X_V\), with meridian represented by \(r\). The two with \(r\) killed extend to \(X_Q\), with meridian represented by \(s\). In each of the three former pairs, \(x=(1-i)/\sqrt2\) and the \(j\)-coefficient of \(y\) is nonzero, giving a nonzero \(k\)-coefficient of \(xy-yx\). For either of the two latter pairs write \(x=a+bi+ck\), \(y=a+bi-ck\), with \(bc\ne0\). Then \(xy-yx=4bcj\). All five remain nonabelian under \(\operatorname{Ad}:SU(2)\to SO(3)\). If the projected generators commuted, \(xy=\pm yx\) would hold. The negative sign would make \(y\) conjugate to \(-y\), contradicting the nonzero real part of \(y\), and the positive sign has just been excluded. These give flat principal \(SO(3)\) connections via the quotient of the product over the universal cover. Such an image rules out a circle of rotations commuting with the entire holonomy image, since the centralizer of a rotation circle in \(SO(3)\) is the circle itself. This restricts that fiberwise symmetry of the flat bundle. It does not decide whether the exterior or an ambient metric admits a circle action. No Fuchsian realization, metric holonomy, energy bound or subcritical entry is supplied by the group map. Its cyclic group maps to the peripheral meridian subgroup of \(\pi_1X_K\). This inclusion does not surject onto the nonabelian exterior group. The meridian normally generates the exterior group, by van Kampen for its gluing to \(S^2\times D^2\) in \(S^4\), so its image is nonidentity for each of the five adjoint representations. The native physical cut has a surjective group map into the disk exterior and fails domination, while the double has a PSC seam and permits nonabelian flat holonomy on its exterior.
