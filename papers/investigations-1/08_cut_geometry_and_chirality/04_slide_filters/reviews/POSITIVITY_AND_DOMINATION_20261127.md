## Lemma 2.1

Let \(M\) be a closed connected oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere and \([g_0]\) a smooth Riemannian conformal class of positive Yamabe constant. With the manuscript’s Hilbert–Schmidt endomorphism norms on the oriented bundles \(\Lambda^\pm\), let \(E_\pm=\int_M |W^\pm|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV\). These integrals are conformally invariant. Signature zero implies \(E_+=E_-=e\). With \(\chi(M)=2\) and \(A=(\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/6)/2\), Chern–Gauss–Bonnet gives

\[
\int_M\sigma_2(A)\,dV=4\pi^2-\frac e2>0
\qquad\text{if } e<8\pi^2.
\]

Chang–Gursky–Yang use \(A_{\mathrm{CGY}}=2A\) in dimension four. Their \(\sigma_2\) integral therefore equals four times this integral. Their equation (0.2) puts a factor \(1/4\) on the full covariant Weyl tensor norm squared, which converts it to the manuscript’s curvature-operator norm squared. The factor counts the two antisymmetric index pairs. Theorem A applies to the compact four-manifold with \(Y([g_0])>0\) and \(\int_M\sigma_2(A_{\mathrm{CGY}})>0\), and gives a smooth conformal representative with \(\sigma_2(A)>0\). Corollary B gives \(\operatorname{Ric}>0\) and \((R/2)g-\operatorname{Ric}>0\) for the resulting representative. Equivalently, Lemma 1.1 and the identity for \(\sigma_2\) force scalar curvature to have constant sign, and the positive Yamabe constant selects the positive sign. The positive sign follows by evaluating the four-dimensional Yamabe quotient on the constant function in that representative. [Chang–Gursky–Yang, *Ann. of Math.* 155 (2002), pp. 709–787, arXiv:math/0409583v1, eq. (0.2), Theorem A and Corollaries B–C, pp. 710–713, and Lemmas 1.1–1.2, p. 715](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583v1).

For the manuscript’s trace-free Ricci tensor \(Z=\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/4\), direct contraction gives

\[
\sigma_2(A)=\frac{R^2}{96}-\frac{|Z|^2}{8}.
\]

In particular \(|Z|^2<R^2/12\) in the resulting gauge. For a unit \(\nu\), trace-freeness and Cauchy–Schwarz give \(|Z(\nu,\nu)|\leq |Z|\,|\nu^\flat\otimes\nu^\flat-g/4|=(\sqrt3/2)|Z|<R/4\). Hence \(0<\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)<R/2\) and \(P(\nu)=R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)>0\). The calculation uses the strict energy bound and the Yamabe constant of the supplied class. It applies directly to smooth metrics on the closed manifold.

## Theorem 4.1

Work in an entering gauge \(g\) as above. For the induced metric \(h\) of a smooth hypersurface and \(B(X,Y)=g(\nabla_X\nu,Y)\), write \(H=\operatorname{tr}_h B\) and \(B^\circ=B-(H/3)h\). In an orthonormal tangent frame the ambient tangential scalar contraction is \(R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\). The two quadratic terms in the Gauss equation contract to \(H^2-|B|_h^2\). Thus

\[
R_h=P(\nu)+\frac23H^2-|B^\circ|_h^2=P(\nu)+2\sigma_2(B).
\]

All terms are unchanged by reversing the unit normal. Pointwise \(\sigma_2(B)\geq0\) implies \(R_h>0\). For \(\widehat g=e^{2u}g\), the Levi-Civita change and \(\widehat\nu=e^{-u}\nu\) give \(\widehat B=e^u(B+(\nu u)h)\) and \(\widehat B^\circ=e^uB^\circ\). Consequently a hypersurface umbilic in the supplied class is umbilic in the entering gauge, and the induced metric from that gauge has positive scalar curvature (PSC). Its induced conformal class is the one inherited from \([g_0]\).

For the third assertion take a closed \(\Sigma\), \(\Delta_h=\operatorname{div}_h\nabla_h\), and smooth nonzero \(u\). Let \(Q_0\) be the displayed infimum in Theorem 4.1 using \(P+(2/3)H^2\). Hölder, with exponents \(3/2\) and \(3\), gives

\[
\frac{\int_\Sigma (8|\nabla u|^2+R_hu^2)\,dV_h}{\|u\|_{6}^2}
\ \geq\ Q_0-\|B^\circ\|_{3}^2.
\]

Under the asserted strict inequality the right side is a positive number uniform in \(u\). This proves positivity of the Yamabe constant by its variational definition. It also gives a positive first eigenvalue for \(-8\Delta_h+R_h\) on a connected component, by \(\|u\|_2^2\leq\operatorname{Vol}(h)^{2/3}\|u\|_6^2\). For a positive first eigenfunction \(v\), the scalar curvature of \(v^4h\) is \(\lambda_1 v^{-4}>0\).

The adjacent Remark 4.2 needs strict \(\sigma_2(B)>0\) in its last sentence for strict two-convexity. Case (1) also allows principal curvatures \((H,0,0)\), \(H>0\), whose two smallest entries sum to zero. The scalar curvature conclusion of case (1) holds at equality because \(P(\nu)>0\).

## Corollary 4.3 and the degree premise in 05

A closed connected embedded cut in \(M\) separates and is two-sided. A transverse loop crossing just once would have nonzero mod-two intersection with the cut, contrary to \(H_1(M;\mathbb Z/2)=0\). Choosing a side gives the cut its boundary orientation. Perelman’s PSC conclusion in §6.1 applies to a closed oriented Riemannian three-manifold with \(R\geq a>0\), where compactness supplies \(a\) for the induced metric. The resulting diffeomorphism type is \(S^3\) or a connected sum of spherical space forms and \(S^1\times S^2\) factors. Conversely, the factors have positive round or product metrics, and Gromov–Lawson’s codimension-at-least-three surgery theorem gives PSC on their connected sums in dimension three. [Perelman, *Ricci flow with surgery on three-manifolds*, arXiv:math/0303109v1, §6.1, pp. 12–13](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0303109v1) · [Gromov–Lawson, *Ann. of Math.* 111 (1980), Theorem A and the connected-sum conclusion, p. 423](https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/827.pdf).

For a tube around a smooth embedded closed connected surface \(F\), the boundary is the unit circle bundle of its real rank-two normal bundle. Its total space is oriented by the ambient orientation and outward normal. When \(F\) is nonorientable, the circle bundle may reverse the fiber orientation. If \(\chi(F)\leq0\), the universal cover of \(F\) is \(\mathbb R^2\). Over that plane the pulled-back circle bundle is trivial, so the boundary has universal cover \(\mathbb R^3\) and infinite fundamental group. For irreducibility, a lift of any embedded two-sphere bounds a ball by three-dimensional smooth Schoenflies. Distinct deck translates of its boundary are disjoint, and no two translated balls can strictly nest since deck transformations preserve the volume of the lifted metric. The ball therefore projects to an embedded ball. An irreducible orientable closed three-manifold on the PSC list has finite fundamental group, since a product summand exhibits a nonseparating sphere and a nontrivial connected sum exhibits an essential separating sphere. This leaves only \(S^2\) and \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) as possible cores when the tube boundary is an umbilic cut. The argument gives a necessary restriction on the core.

The 05 definition of PSC domination takes a continuous map from a closed oriented PSC three-manifold to the closed connected oriented cut \(Y\), of degree \(d\in\mathbb Z\setminus\{0\}\). If the total degree of a disconnected source is nonzero, some component has nonzero degree. For such a map Proposition 2.2 of Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee gives exactly the three-dimensional PSC decomposition above. Gromov–Lawson then supplies an actual PSC metric on \(Y\). In the other direction an actual PSC metric on \(Y\) supplies the identity map of degree \(+1\). For an umbilic \(\Sigma\) diffeomorphic to the marked \(Y\), the degree witness is the diffeomorphism from \(\Sigma\) with the induced entering-gauge metric. Its degree is \(+1\) for an orientation-preserving identification and \(-1\) for an orientation-reversing one. The mapping statement for arbitrary nonzero degree is the input that permits use of a source other than the cut in the 05 premise. [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, *Complete Riemannian 4-manifolds with uniformly positive scalar curvature*, arXiv:2407.05574v1, Proposition 2.2, pp. 4–5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1) · [Gromov–Lawson, Theorem A](https://www.ihes.fr/~gromov/wp-content/uploads/2018/08/827.pdf).

In the product-band argument of 05’s `prop:band`, the oriented separator is homologous in \(Y\times[t_-,t_+]\) to the incoming slice, using the boundary of the lower region and the slice orientation on \(Y\). Applying the projection to the homology equality gives a **total** degree of \(+1\). If the separator is PSC, the component selected by this sum need only have nonzero degree for Proposition 2.2 to apply.

## Corollary 4.4 and the mixed recognition input

The reduced mixed presentation has one 0-handle, one lower 1-handle, two middle 2-handles, one upper 3-handle and one 4-handle, with actual attachments and upper boundary marking. Normalize the oriented winding row to \((1,0)\) by framed slides. At the actual first cut \(Z_a\) in the winding-one-first order, the lower trace \(X_a\) has the homotopy type of a 0/1/2 CW complex. Its one relation kills the generator of the lower \(\mathbb Z\) and its cellular boundary \(C_2\to C_1\) is multiplication by a unit. Thus \(X_a\) is smooth, simply connected and acyclic, hence contractible. Its boundary is a closed connected oriented integral homology sphere by Poincaré–Lefschetz duality and the pair sequence. This first-cut argument still applies in the presence of additional pending tubes.

If \(Z_a\) is PSC-dominated, Proposition 2.2 and the connected-sum construction give it a PSC metric. Corollary 2.3 of Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee applies to this PSC integral homology sphere and identifies it as \(S^3\) or a sum of signed Poincaré spheres. Their Proposition 4.2 uses the **smooth compact contractible** filling to force the sum empty. The topological conclusion about the filling from Proposition 4.2 is a homeomorphism to the ball. Its proof uses the Floer correction term to equate the counts of opposite signs, and the periodic-end obstruction to eliminate the remaining paired sum. There is no interior curvature premise for Proposition 4.2. The extra smooth handle argument is what upgrades the conclusion in this marked situation. [Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee, Corollary 2.3, p. 5, and Lemma 4.1 and Proposition 4.2, pp. 9–10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1).

In 05’s `prop:peripheral`, the given attachment is integral and has dual surgery of distance one from the meridian on its surgery core in \(Z_a\). Once \(Z_a\cong S^3\), that inverse surgery has target \(S^1\times S^2\). Homology forces its slope to be zero in the preferred longitude framing of the dual knot, and Property R forces that knot to be the unknot. The original attaching core is therefore the product core in the surgery coordinates for \(S^1\times S^2\). The boundary re-marking extends over the lower one-handlebody, and its product belt sphere meets the attaching core geometrically once. Milnor’s first cancellation theorem then cancels the chosen lower 1/2 pair and transports the remaining framed tube and outgoing map through the comparison. In the two-middle mixed case the remaining ordinary trace starts on \(D^4\) and its marked outgoing boundary is \(S^1\times S^2\). Another application of homology and Property R makes this the zero-framed unknot trace \(S^2\times D^2\). Laudenbach–Poénaru’s Theorem A for rank one gives \(S^4\) for the given gluing to the upper \(S^1\times D^3\). [Gabai, *J. Differential Geom.* 26 (1987), Corollary 8.3 and Remark 8.5, pp. 525–526](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214441488), checked in a [scan with OCR](https://www.scribd.com/document/1026013102/2-Gabai) · [Milnor, *Lectures on the h-cobordism theorem* (1965), §5, Theorem 5.4, p. 47](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~hekmati/Books/Milnor2.pdf) · [Laudenbach–Poénaru, *Bull. Soc. Math. France* 100 (1972), Theorem A and its proof, pp. 337 and 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf).

The companion winding-zero statement in 05’s `thm:mixed` uses a different filling. With the dot changed to an ordinary zero-framed component for boundary surgery, the determinant of the full three-component linking matrix is minus the framing of the winding-zero component. The full outgoing boundary is \(Y_1=S^1\times S^2\), so that framing vanishes. The first cut \(Z_b\) of the reverse order has an auxiliary ordinary two-handle filling with zero linking matrix. Its inclusion \(H_2(Z_b;\mathbb Z)\to H_2\) of the filling is an isomorphism by the pair sequence and zero intersection pairing. Under PSC domination the boundary has two product factors and a spherical homology-sphere sum, because \(H_1(Z_b;\mathbb Z)=\mathbb Z^2\). Compressing the two product factors by auxiliary 3-handles kills an integral basis of the filling’s \(H_2\) and keeps its fundamental group trivial. The resulting contractible filling of the spherical sum forces it to be empty by Proposition 4.2. Hence \(Z_b\cong Y_2=\#_2(S^1\times S^2)\). The separate marked recognition on \(Y_2\) is the theorem at `thm:mixed` of the 04 marked-height manuscript. Its split model for each side uses the proof of Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson’s Proposition 3.2 for a two-component ordinary surgery diagram with one component an unknot and outlet \(Y_2\). That result uses the isotopy of the other component into a ball in the actual intermediate \(S^1\times S^2\), carrying the framing. The subsequent interior surgery and inverse surgery on the generator of \(\pi_1(S^1\times S^3)\) use the original assembly and the rank-one gluing theorem. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, *Geom. Topol.* 14 (2010), Proposition 3.2 and proof, pp. 2313–2314](https://msp.org/gt/2010/14-4/gt-v14-n4-p13-s.pdf).

## Implication for the table

For each actual closed connected oriented cut \(Y\) in a smooth homotopy four-sphere, an umbilic embedding of its diffeomorphism type in any smooth class with positive Yamabe constant and \(E_+,E_-<8\pi^2\) gives a PSC source of degree \(\pm1\). Thus an obstruction to PSC domination on that actual \(Y\) excludes such umbilic embeddings for every class in this range. The conclusion concerns the specified cut type, even if another cut in the same presentation supplies recognition. The chain uses the topology of the cut or a certified factor map from its group. A nonabelian image alone has no such consequence for a general cut. The Poincaré sphere, for example, has a nonabelian finite fundamental group and a round quotient metric. A certificate that uses a standard-piece implication needs the exact recognition hypotheses and a representation on the matched piece group that contradicts that conclusion. Outside the two-middle mixed case, the winding-one argument above gives the scoped 1/2 cancellation. Conclusions for other terminal cuts require their own input.
