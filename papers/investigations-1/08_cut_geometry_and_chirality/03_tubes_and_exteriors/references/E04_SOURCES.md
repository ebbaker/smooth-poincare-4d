## Primary topology passages

| Source and version | Inspected passage and use |
| --- | --- |
| E. C. Zeeman, *Twisting spun knots*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 115 (1965), 471–495. DOI [10.1090/S0002-9947-1965-0195085-8](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1965-0195085-8). [Published article scan](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/zeemantwist.pdf). | Checked §6 Main Theorem, p. 486, parts 1–3, and §7's corollaries to Lemmas 4–5 and Lemma 6 on pp. 489–490 and 492. For a smooth codimension-two sphere knot and nonzero twist number, the complement is a bundle over the circle whose fibre is the punctured cyclic branched cover. Zeeman identifies the cyclic generator with the canonical covering map and gives a smooth bounded puncture and smooth spun knot. His equivariant description in part 3 gives one rotation of the knot itself under the circle action. E04 uses the theorem with the trefoil, twist number two, and the independently constructed equivariant round cover. |
| C. McA. Gordon, *Knots in the 4-sphere*, Comment. Math. Helv. 51 (1976), 585–596. [Published article scan](https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=com-001%3A1976%3A51%3A%3A41). | Checked §3, Theorem 3.1 and remark (2), pp. 587–588. The Gluck reconstruction on any integral twist-spin of a classical knot has standard total space. The paper begins in the PL category for convenience, and the remark explicitly states that the proofs in Propositions 3.2 and 3.3 are valid in the smooth category and give a diffeomorphism to the standard sphere. This supplies the smooth conclusion for the second trefoil framing. |
| Mizuki Fukuda, *Gluck twists along 2-knots with periodic monodromy*, [arXiv:1811.05109v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1811.05109v1), 13 November 2018. | Checked §2.1, Definition 2.1 and §2.2 on pp. 2–4, then §3 Theorems 3.1 and 3.2 on pp. 7–9 and the proof of Corollary 3.3 and Remark 3.4 on p. 10. Theorem 3.1, attributed to Pao and reproduced there, gives a diffeomorphism to \(S^4\) after a Gluck twist along the branched twist spin \(K^{m,n}\) for coprime \((m,n)\) with \(n>0\). Theorem 3.2 describes the effect on the *other* core, then the proof of Corollary 3.3 identifies the new image of the twisted core. The odd-\(m\) assumption in the corollary is used for inequivalence, which is unnecessary for the trefoil row with \(m=2\). The local weights in E04 use a signed axial angle, and Fukuda indexes his oriented cores by signed \(m\) and positive \(n\). |
| Ronald Fintushel, *Locally smooth circle actions on homotopy 4-spheres*, Duke Math. J. 43 (1976), 63–70. DOI [10.1215/S0012-7094-76-04306-4](https://doi.org/10.1215/S0012-7094-76-04306-4). Also Fintushel, *Circle actions on simply connected 4-manifolds*, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 230 (1977), 147–171. DOI [10.1090/S0002-9947-1977-0458456-6](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1977-0458456-6). | Verified bibliographic identities. Project Euclid returned an access challenge for the 1976 full text and PDF. The AMS 1977 PDF request was denied. The 1976 Theorem 1 on orbit data for locally smooth actions on homotopy four-spheres is identified in a secondary account. It remains an original-source dependency for the historical proof chain. The joint 1983 primary paper by Fintushel and Pao, *Circle actions on homotopy spheres with codimension 4 fixed point set*, Pacific J. Math. 109, 349–362, was inspected at [pp. 352–353 and 361](https://msp.org/pjm/1983/109-2/pjm-v109-n2-p09-p.pdf). Its orbit-space propositions concern smooth actions with codimension-four fixed points, and its final discussion points back to the earlier papers for \(n=4\). It does not supply the general four-dimensional recognition statement on its own. |
| Peter Sie Pao, *Non-linear circle actions on the 4-sphere and twisting spun knots*, Topology 17 (1978), 291–296. DOI [10.1016/0040-9383(78)90033-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-9383(78)90033-2). | Checked the [publisher abstract](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0040938378900332). The accessible abstract describes the conditional orbit-space classification and identification of homotopy four-spheres. Publisher PDF and alternate full-text links did not return full text. The exact theorem number for general standardness is therefore unverified from Pao's paper. Fukuda's checked Theorem 3.1 provides his branched-twist-spin conclusion with proof, and the next row supplies a checked synthesis for the general smooth action. |
| Fernando Galaz-García and Catherine Searle, *Nonnegatively curved 5-manifolds with almost maximal symmetry rank*, Geom. Topol. 18 (2014), 1397–1435. [Published paper](https://msp.org/gt/2014/18-3/gt-v18-n3-p05-s.pdf). | Checked §3.2, p. 1404, Theorem 3.2 and its attribution to Fintushel, Pao and Perelman. This theorem applies to any closed simply connected smooth four-manifold with smooth circle action, independently of the later curvature hypotheses in Theorem 3.3. It gives a diffeomorphism to a connected sum of standard four-spheres, signed complex projective planes and sphere products. The additivity of \(b_2\) on connected sums then gives \(S^4\) for the smooth homotopy-sphere case. This is a published restatement rather than a reading of the original classification proofs. |
| Grisha Perelman, *Finite extinction time for the solutions to the Ricci flow on certain three-manifolds*, [arXiv:math/0307245v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0307245), 17 July 2003. John Morgan and Gang Tian, *Ricci Flow and the Poincaré Conjecture*, Clay Mathematics Monographs 3 (2007). [Book PDF](https://www.claymath.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/03/Ricci-pdf.pdf). | Checked Perelman's Theorem 1.1 for closed oriented three-manifolds with no aspherical prime factors and the Morgan–Tian explanation of Theorem 0.1 in Chapter 18, pp. 414–415. The latter explicitly gives \(S^3\) up to diffeomorphism in the simply connected closed case. The ball version used in the historical circle-action argument additionally uses the three-dimensional Schoenflies theorem after capping a spherical boundary. This is a three-dimensional input. |

The explicit \(\mathbb Z_3\) quotient and deck-action calculation for the trefoil in [E04 §6](../reviews/E04_PARENT_AND_BOUND.md#6-smooth-action-and-topology) is a derivation within the packet. Zeeman's theorem identifies its mapping-torus exterior. Gordon or Fukuda supplies smooth standardness after the alternate framing.

## Gursky and the doubling inequality

- Matthew J. Gursky, *The Weyl functional, de Rham cohomology and Kähler–Einstein metrics*, Ann. of Math. (2) 148 (1998), 315–337, DOI [10.2307/120996](https://doi.org/10.2307/120996). The [publisher](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/12993) returned metadata and the JSTOR text endpoint returned no article body. This packet uses the checked statement in Fu's reproductions, as the [earlier source receipt](../../../../../references/claude/SOURCE_NOTES_REFLECTIVE_SEAMS_20261126.md) did.
- Hai-Ping Fu, *Four-manifolds with positive Yamabe constant*, Pacific J. Math. 296 (2018), 79–101, DOI [10.2140/pjm.2018.296.79](https://doi.org/10.2140/pjm.2018.296.79). Checked Eq. (1-1) and Theorems A and C on pp. 80–81 of the [published paper](https://msp.org/pjm/2018/296-1/pjm-v296-n1-p05-s.pdf). The hypotheses are positive Yamabe type on a closed oriented Riemannian four-manifold and \(b_2^+>0\) or \(b_1>0\), respectively. Fu's integral is in four-index norm, seen from the signature coefficient \(48\pi^2\). Division by four gives the Hilbert–Schmidt chiral bounds \(E_+\ge (4\pi^2/3)(2\chi+3\tau)\) and \(E_+\ge 2\pi^2(2\chi+3\tau)\). The doubles in Section 5 are closed and oriented, with \(\tau=0\), \(E_+=2e_V\), and \(\chi=2\chi(V)\). The [E04 review](../reviews/E04_PARENT_AND_BOUND.md#5-seam-atlas-and-the-scope-of-its-one-tube-use) checks smoothing and the sign hypothesis algebraically.
