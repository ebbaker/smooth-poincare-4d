## GN — Local conformal geometry

A. Rod Gover and Paweł Nurowski, *Obstructions to conformally Einstein metrics in n dimensions*, J. Geom. Phys. **56** (2006), no. 3, 450–484. Checked passages in [arXiv:math/0405304v3, 9 June 2004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0405304v3), and publisher metadata at [DOI 10.1016/j.geomphys.2005.03.001](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.geomphys.2005.03.001). Equations (3.1)–(3.4) give the scale, tractor pairing and connection. Equation (3.7) expresses curvature in terms of Weyl and Cotton. Section 2.1 gives the Schouten transformation and contracted Bianchi identity. The packet specializes these formulas to positive-definite dimension four and solves the flat connection on a half-ball using smooth coefficients up to the boundary. The paper's weak genericity hypotheses occur in its separate conformally Einstein obstruction results. The formulas used here hold without weak genericity. The packet supplies the local Liouville argument and reflected atlas explicitly.

## BC — Pinching equality and the product

Vincent Bour and Gilles Carron, *Optimal integral pinching results*, Ann. Sci. Éc. Norm. Supér. (4) **48** (2015), no. 1, 41–70, [DOI 10.24033/asens.2238](https://doi.org/10.24033/asens.2238). Checked passages in the [published PDF at Numdam](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.2238.pdf). Theorem 1.1(i), p. 42, states the four-dimensional positive-Yamabe, closed oriented form of Gursky's trace-free Ricci pinching theorem. If \(b_1>0\) and \(\int|Z|^2\le Y([g])^2/12\), equality holds and the given conformal class contains a quotient of the round cylinder. The first part of the theorem assumes the displayed bound involving the class Yamabe constant, without assuming that the given metric is a minimizer. They identify the given metric at equality as a Yamabe minimizer and distinguish it from the product quotient representative in Remark 7.1(i). Corollary 1.3(iii), pp. 44–45, uses the scale-invariant inequality (1.8) with the Weyl and scalar-curvature integrals instead. Their operator norm \(|W|^2=\tfrac14 W_{ijkl}W^{ijkl}\) is the sum of the parent's two chiral Hilbert–Schmidt squares. Checked the display in the [authors’ preprint](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1203.0384) as well because the published PDF text extraction interleaves the integrals. Equation (1.8) compares \(\int|W|^2+\tfrac12\int|Z|^2\) with \(\tfrac1{24}\int R^2\). On E03's double, \(\chi=0\) and \(W=0\) yield equality in (1.8), and \(b_1=1,b_2=0\) select case (iii).

Their own proof is in §§5, 6.2 and 7.2. Checked the equality passage in §7.2, p. 61, including its use of the warped product from Proposition 5.1 and the vanishing of the three-dimensional trace-free Ricci tensor after removal of the warping. The stated conclusion is a **conformal** equivalence to a product quotient. E03 uses the equivalence with that product class and proves the action of the original reflection independently. E03 uses only the product representative in the class. Its translation length is unrestricted.

Sun-Yung A. Chang, Matthew J. Gursky and Paul C. Yang, *An equation of Monge-Ampère type in conformal geometry, and four-manifolds of positive Ricci curvature*, Ann. of Math. (2) **155** (2002), no. 3, 709–787, [DOI 10.2307/3062131](https://doi.org/10.2307/3062131). Checked equation (0.2) and the introduction in [arXiv:math/0409583v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583v1), which reproduces the journal pagination. Their Schouten tensor is twice the packet's, and their squared Weyl norm in (0.2) is four times the packet's sum. This corroborates the closed Chern–Gauss–Bonnet identity used in E03. Their introduction attributes the implication from a positive Yamabe class and positive \(\int\sigma_2\) to \(b_1=0\) to Gursky. It does not supply the equality statement there.

## DG — Smooth boundary markings

Fan Ding and Hansjörg Geiges, *The diffeotopy group of \(S^1\times S^2\) via contact topology*, Compos. Math. **146** (2010), 1096–1112, [DOI 10.1112/S0010437X09004606](https://doi.org/10.1112/S0010437X09004606). Checked §2 of [arXiv:0903.1488v2, 9 April 2009](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0903.1488v2), including the displayed maps \(s,a,r\), Lemma 1, Proposition 2 and the following group calculation. The paper establishes that every smooth self-diffeomorphism of \(S^1\times S^2\), including orientation-reversing maps, is smoothly isotopic to a product of circle reversal, spherical antipodal map and the Gluck twist. Each displayed representative extends over the disk exterior by the same formula. The extensions are on the exterior side of the gluing. It applies to any choice of marking after §4 identifies the smooth pair. The two framed fillings alone follow from their explicit representatives and extensions.
