## Sharp global conformal Korn constant and the schedule

Write the deck action on \(S^3\subset\mathbb H\) as \(q\mapsto u q z^{-1}\) for \(u\in Q_8,\ z\in C_{17}\). The four ambient components of an equivariant tangent field are odd because left multiplication by \(-1\) is a deck transformation. Its degree-one harmonic part is \(Pq\) for a real linear map \(P\) commuting with the deck transformations. Commuting with all left multiplications by \(i,j,k\) means \(P\) is right multiplication by \(P(1)\). Commutation with the noncentral right \(C_{17}\) puts \(P(1)\) in \(\langle1,i\rangle_{\mathbb R}\). The trace of \(P\) is zero since its degree-one projection pairing with \(q\mapsto q\) equals the pairing of the original tangent field with \(q\). Thus this degree-one part is exactly the \(L^2\) projection onto the lift of \(q\mapsto q i\).

The other component harmonics have odd degree at least three, with scalar eigenvalues \(m(m+2)\ge15\). This follows by separating radial powers in the Euclidean Laplacian, using harmonic decomposition of polynomials and density on \(S^3\). For \(W\) orthogonal to \(q i\), the ambient gradient square exceeds the intrinsic gradient square by \(|W|^2\): the normal component of \(D_{e_a}W\) is \(-\langle e_a,W\rangle q\). Dividing the integral on the cover by its degree gives \(\int|\nabla W|^2\ge14\int|W|^2\) on the quotient. Integration by parts using \(\operatorname{Ric}=2g\) gives
\[
 \int|(\operatorname{sym}\nabla W)^\circ|^2
       =\tfrac12\int|\nabla W|^2+\tfrac16\int(\operatorname{div}W)^2
                            -\int|W|^2 \ \ge 6\int|W|^2 .
\]
The projected field is Killing. This proves the full conformal Killing kernel is the single real direction \(q i\), which also gives the entire Killing kernel.

For sharpness, take \(n(q)=qiq^{-1}\) and a nonzero traceless diagonal real matrix \(D\) on \(\operatorname{Im}\mathbb H\), for example \(\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0)\). The tangent field \(W(q)=(D n(q))q\) is equivariant under left \(Q_8\) and right \(C_{17}\), because \(D\) commutes with the left adjoint sign changes and \(n\) is invariant under the right action. Expanding \(W=\sum_i D_{ii} n_i e_i q\), each \(e_i q\) is Killing and annihilates its own coefficient \(n_i\), since its adjoint motion is \([e_i,n]\). Thus \(\operatorname{div}W=0\). Its pairing with \(q i\) integrates to zero by spherical averaging of \(n_i^2\) and \(\operatorname{tr}D=0\). Before restriction to the sphere its components are the cubic polynomial \((D(qi\bar q))q\); with degree one absent they are pure degree three. Hence equality holds in both spectral steps.

For the moving germ, translate its lift to \(1\) by left multiplication, preserving the right \(i\) field. With \(q=c+s(y_i i+y_j j+y_k k)\), \(c=\cos r,\ s=\sin r\), the velocity field is \(-c j+s y_j\). Its pairing with \(q i\) is \(-s^2y_i y_j-sc y_k\), of angular mean zero, and its norm squared is at least \(c^2\). On the embedded ball this gives exactly the E34 lower bound \(24\pi\int_0^{r_0}\cos^2r\sin^2r\,dr>5\pi r_0^3\) for the squared trace-free strain of any global extension when \(r_0<1/10\). The strain itself vanishes inside the rigid ball. No outer boundary flux or restriction on the global projection uses the ball.

For a fixed smooth family \(V_\ell\), pulled back along its flow, the electric tensor on the round region is \(-v'k_\ell^\circ+v^2Q_\ell\) with the stated \(Q_\ell=(-\partial_\ell k_\ell-\mathcal L_{V_\ell}k_\ell+(\operatorname{tr}k_\ell)k_\ell)^\circ\). The \(\partial_\ell k_\ell\) term is required because the co-moving shape changes. There is no mixed-term cancellation here. On E34's first ramp with plateau speed \(v_*=d/(1-\delta)\), the reverse triangle bound is \(\kappa v_*/\sqrt\delta-M_*v_*^2\sqrt\delta\); it is at least \(\kappa v_* /(2\sqrt\delta)\) if \(\delta\le \min(1/4,\kappa/(4dM_*))\). Here \(\kappa^2=5\pi r_0^3\) and \(M_*=\sup_\ell\|Q_\ell\|_2\). The two ramp integrals total \(\delta v_*\), so the stated plateau and ramps cover exactly displacement \(d\) and the constant zero end germs give smooth time matching. With the static estimate on the specified positive-scalar radial bend, minimization of \(AL+C/L^3\) gives the E34 fourth-power ratio \(15/(16384\pi^4 r_0^6)>1\).

The explicit derivative bounds also have slack. For \(F=\sum n_i^4\) the ambient operator bounds \(24,168,1008\) follow using first and second derivative bounds two on \(n_i\). Restricting to the sphere gives third derivative operator bound \(1008+3(168)+24\); the Hilbert–Schmidt factor is less than six. At \(r_0=.01,\ R=.02\), the spatial cutoff has \(|\nabla\chi|\le1200,\ |\nabla^2\chi|\le2160000\), by the bump normalization and \(|\nabla^2r|<2/r_0\). Product differentiation with the checked bounds on \(Z,W_0\) gives \(|V_\ell|\le24,\ |\nabla V_\ell|<31000,\ |\nabla^2V_\ell|<56000000,\ |\nabla\partial_\ell V_\ell|<55000000\). Consequently
\[
  |Q_\ell|\le 55000000+24(56000000)+4(31000)^2<10^{10}.
\]
Since the quotient volume is less than one and \(\kappa>1/300\), the chosen \(\delta=10^{-14}\) is inside the ramp bound. The spectral inequality holds for arbitrary instantaneous global fields. The electric conclusion requires the specified short acceleration, support separation and static positive radial bend. General smooth schedules do not inherit that conclusion from the spectral gap alone.

## Suspension, stabilizer and orbital filling slope

Let \(n=(i+j+k)/\sqrt3,\ b=e^{n\pi/3}\), and \(J(q)=bqj^{-1}\). On the closed prism the induced \(h\) has order six. In the central product notation, the group generated with the deck group is \(T^*\times_{\{\pm1\}}Q_{68}\) of order \((24\cdot68)/2=816\). This follows because the actual \(J\) generates a quotient of order six over the order-136 deck group, and its factors lie in the claimed central product. Its parity-even subgroup consists of the \(T^*\times C_{17}\) action and is free: equality of the quaternion conjugacy classes needed for a fixed point is impossible for nonidentity elements of coprime factor orders. Any point stabilizer injects into both the index-two quotient by that free subgroup and the cyclic quotient of order six by the original free deck group. A nontrivial stabilizer therefore has order two and projects to \(h^3\). The suspension action has orbits of base degree six or three, whereas the actual section has degree one. Degree is preserved up to sign by isotopy of the section circle.

For \(p_0jp_0^{-1}=n\), use the great circle \(C=p_0e^{j\mathbb R}\). If a central-product element \((A,B)\) sends \(C\) to a circle meeting \(C\), preserving inner products under conjugation at an intersection point gives \(j\cdot\operatorname{Ad}_B j=n\cdot\operatorname{Ad}_A n\). For \(B=z^r j^c\) the left value is \(\cos(4\pi r/17)\). The tetrahedral action gives right values \(1,-1/3\). The latter would make \(2\cos(4\pi r/17)=-2/3\), impossible for an algebraic integer. The former forces \(A\in\langle b\rangle,\ B\in\langle j\rangle\). These elements do preserve \(C\), with shift \(a\pi/3-c\pi/2\). Their group of order twelve is exactly the cyclic group generated by \(J\) on \(C\), acting freely. Thus the 68 translates are disjoint and the projected loop lies in the free stratum. In \((t+1,q)\sim(t,h(q))\) the section \([t,p_0 e^{j\pi t/6}]\) projects once around this embedded loop.

In coordinates \(q=p_0u\), \(J\) acts as \(e^{j\pi/3}u e^{-j\pi/2}\). Its tangential angle is \(-\pi/6\) on \(\langle1,j\rangle\). On the normal plane \(\langle i,k\rangle\) the right factor becomes left \(e^{j\pi/2}\), so the normal angle is \(5\pi/6\) in the orientation of left \(j\)-rotation. The positive longitude travels by \(+\pi/6\) and has normal holonomy \(-5\pi/6\), using \(J^{-1}\). Therefore \(a+s\mu\) lifts after twelve traversals as \(\lambda_C+(12s-5)\mu_C\), where \(\lambda_C\) is the constant-frame longitude. Other orientations exchange signs according to the chosen marking.

The preimage torus over this regular loop is principal and has the actual section. A principal circle bundle over its solid-torus neighborhood is trivial, and taking the section as the constant fibre choice gives product coordinates. A single positive slope twist twists the two disk directions once and fixes the fibre direction, giving the nonzero element in \(\pi_1(SO(3))\). In this product the first surgery compresses the fibre torus to a sphere with product normal neighbourhood. To see the second surgery locally, write the neighbourhood as \(S^1_a\times Y_0\), \(Y_0=D^2_\mu\times S^1_f\). Remove \(S^1_a\times B\) around the section and insert \(D^2_a\times\partial B\). The compressed sphere is the product of \(S^1_a\) with the remaining core arc of \(Y_0\), capped by two fibre discs. Its complement in the modified neighbourhood is \(S^1_\mu\) times a punctured solid torus with meridian \(a\), from attaching a three-dimensional two-handle along \(a\). The sphere surgery fills that puncture and yields \(S^1_\mu\times D^2_a\times S^1_f\). Rotation of \(S^1_f\) extends the action on this second manifold, whose orbit solid torus has attaching meridian \(a+s\mu\) in the marked original coordinates. This independently checks the local operation of the order-seven preprint in the present setting. It gives an effective action after the second surgery, with the initial homotopy sphere obtained by reversing that surgery along its new core circle. The required filled-orbifold group for the reverse-surgery recognition remains a separate computation. [Jabłonowski, §8.1, Lemma 8.3 and Proposition 8.4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29839)

The unfilled orbifold has \(\Lambda^{\rm ab}=C_3\times C_2\): the two factor abelianizations are \(C_3,C_4\), and identifying their central involutions kills the order-two element of \(C_4\). Thus the parity-even group above is its unique index-two subgroup. Its free quotient has first homology \(C_3\times C_{17}\), of order 51. This constrains an unfilled model independently of attaching choices. The homotopy type of the unfilled group is insufficient to compute the quotient by the marked slope on the *punctured* orbifold.

## The two branched total spaces and their framings

Use the quotient of \((Q_8\times C_{17})\rtimes\mathbb Z\) to \(C_{17}\rtimes_{\rm inv}C_2\) which kills \(Q_8\) and sends the actual section generator to a reflection. The degree-17 cover from a reflection subgroup has sheet permutation \(r\mapsto-r\) along the section, so the preimage contains one degree-one and eight degree-two circles. The normal subgroup kernel gives degree 34 and seventeen degree-two circles. In the first cover the fibre is \(Q_8\backslash S^3\) over the original base, and in the second it is the same fibre over the double base. Pulling back the original normal framing on a degree-\(d\) component gives the boundary covering \((u,y)\mapsto(u^d,y)\). Completing in cap coordinates by \((w,y)\mapsto(w^d,y)\) gives a smooth branched map, with compatible radius collars. The degree-two components are genuinely branched. The regular deck action extends on the caps by permuting the caps and by normal rotations on the local disc cover.

To see the selected lifted surgery directly, take the lift beginning at \(p_0\). Its base period \(m\) is one in the degree-17 cover and two in the degree-34 cover. In base time \(t\in[0,m]\) the lift is \(p_0 e^{j\pi t/6}\), since \(J^{-1}\) adds \(+\pi/6\) along \(C\). On the one-sided quaternionic quotient the global isometry \(F_t(q)=q e^{j\pi t/6}\) identifies that section with \(p_0\), with exact matched end jets since \(J\) commutes with right multiplication by \(e^{j\theta}\). The pointed isometry in the moving frame is
\[
               J^m F_m(q)=b^m q e^{-jm\pi/3}.
\]
It fixes \(p_0\), has order three and preserves a round ball about \(p_0\). Its induced automorphism of \(Q_8/[Q_8,Q_8]\) permutes the three nonzero classes. Killing the selected lifted section kills the abelianization of the resulting quotient of the solvable \(Q_8\), so that quotient is trivial. Both framed closings are smooth homotopy four-spheres. The pointed periodic representative gives an effective suspension circle action on the punctured mapping torus, extending over each framing by the corresponding disc and sphere weights. The classification of smooth circle actions on simply connected closed four-manifolds identifies each selected lifted surgery as standard \(S^4\). The hypotheses hold for these lifted surgeries, where the actual centre is fixed by an isometry. This argument uses no such action downstairs. [Galaz-García–Searle, Theorem 3.2 and attribution to Fintushel and Pao](https://msp.org/gt/2014/18-3/gt-v18-n3-p05-s.pdf) · [Periodic extension formulas](../notes/E05_TWISTED_EXTERIORS.md)

The fibre spin torsor over \(H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)=\mathbb F_2^2\) has a unique monodromy-fixed element because the order-three linear part has no fixed nonzero vector. Gluing this spin structure gives spin structures on the original mapping torus differing by the base class. The spin induced on a framed circle must be bounding for extension across \(D^2\times S^2\); this follows by restricting the product spin cap to a meridian disc. Conversely a bounding circle spin gives the cap extension, since the boundary spin structures on \(S^1\times S^2\) differ only by the circle class. On a degree-two lift of either framing, the circle spin holonomy squares and hence becomes the nonbounding spin structure.

For degree 17 choose the downstairs spin structure bounding on the selected degree-one lift, by using its base class if needed. After the selected surgery this is the unique spin on \(S^4\), leaving all eight remaining lifted circles nonbounding. In degree 34 all seventeen degree-two lifts initially have nonbounding spin. Use the new base class, which evaluates once on every lifted circle, to make all of them bounding before the selected surgery. Its extending structure is the unique one on the resulting \(S^4\), with the remaining sixteen circles bounding.

The remaining links in each \(S^4\) are unlinks up to smooth ambient isotopy. Since \(S^4\) is simply connected, take simultaneous homotopies to a link of disjoint small round circles. For generic homotopies relative to the ends, the equal-time pairwise coincidence condition has domain dimension three and diagonal codimension four. The immersion condition is also generic in a one-parameter family, so this is an isotopy of the disjoint union, extending to an ambient isotopy. The framings transport along it. Surgery on an unknot replaces \(S^1\times D^3\) by one \(D^2\times S^2\) on the other such piece. The two clutching choices yield the two oriented \(S^2\)-bundles over \(S^2\). The extending spin framing gives the product, which is spin; the other gives the odd bundle, since the link complement has the unique spin inherited from \(S^4\). Disjoint local surgeries give connected sums. This yields \(\#_8(S^2\widetilde\times S^2)\) in degree 17 and \(\#_{16}(S^2\times S^2)\) in degree 34, for either original framing. Recognizing these smooth total spaces leaves the branch spheres and the regular deck action essential for recognizing the smooth quotient.
