## Integer slopes, connected lifts and group decisions

Retain the underlying orientation used by E54. Since \((a,\mu)\) orients the exterior boundary, \((\mu_0,\lambda_0)=(a,-3a-\mu)\) is the positive meridian–longitude basis of the dual unknot in the \(s=0\) filling. The regular-core slope \(a+s\mu\) equals \(-(3s-1)\mu_0-s\lambda_0\). Thus the orientation-sensitive rational surgery coefficient is \((3s-1)/s\) for \(s\ne0\), with \(s=0\) the meridian. The dual unknot exterior \(V\) is a solid torus with infinite cyclic group generated by \(\mu_0\). Its slope \(\lambda_0\) bounds there.

The old singular locus is the one knot \(S\) of E54. Its homology class has winding magnitude three in \(V\). An independent check uses the oriented fixed circle \(F=\{q:qj q^{-1}=i\}\) above it. Its setwise stabilizer has order eight, with two-element point stabilizer. The right factor cannot send \(j\) to \(-j\), since the rotation by \(4\pi r/17\) never sends \(j\) to its negative, and the commuting left and right factors have order four each with a shared central element. Hence \(F\to S\) has degree four. The linking formula in E48 gives signed row sums \(7\) twice and \(-5\) twice for \(F\) against the 68 surgery circles. In the positive tetrahedral rows the negative links have cyclic distances at most two, using \(\cos(4\pi/17)>1/\sqrt3>\cos(6\pi/17)\). In the negative rows the positive links have distances six through eight, using \(\cos(10\pi/17)>-1/\sqrt3>\cos(12\pi/17)\). The last strict comparison also follows from \(\cos(5\pi/17)>\sin(\pi/5)>1/\sqrt3\). The total signed linking sum is four and each oriented meridian upstairs pushes to \(\mu\). Dividing by the degree four in ordinary exterior homology gives \([S]=[\mu]=-3[a]\).

Write \(p=|3s-1|\). The connected cyclic cover of \(V\) of degree \(p\) has peripheral map \(\widetilde\mu_0\mapsto p\mu_0,\ \widetilde\lambda_0\mapsto\lambda_0\). Its filling along \(\operatorname{sgn}(3s-1)\widetilde\mu_0+s\widetilde\lambda_0\) extends the degree-\(p\) ordinary cover since this is a primitive lift of the downstairs meridian, with local meridian degree one. The filled cover has underlying \(S^3\), as its unknot surgery numerator is \(\pm1\). As \(\gcd(p,3)=1\), the action on sheets along \(S\) is transitive. Its lift \(K_s\) is one knot and still has isotropy label two. In particular \(s=1\) gives the degree-two ambient lift and the \(+1\) filling. This construction concerns the core with trivial isotropy. The degree-twelve branched completion of E48 is a different filling.

Let \(G_s=\pi_1^{\rm orb}(O(a+s\mu))\), \(B_s=\pi_1^{\rm orb}(S^3,K_s;2)\), and \(H_s=\pi_1(\Sigma_2(K_s))\). The ambient lens cover and the meridian-parity branched cover yield exact sequences
\[
  1\to B_s\to G_s\to C_p\to1,\qquad
  1\to H_s\to B_s\to C_2\to1 .
\]
In particular \(H_s\) is normal in \(G_s\) here: \(B_s\) is normal and its meridian parity is characteristic, since its abelianization is \(C_2\) by the ordinary knot meridian presentation. If \(H_s=1\), the kernel \(B_s=C_2\) is central in \(G_s\) and the quotient is cyclic. Then \(G_s\) is abelian, hence is cyclic \(C_{2p}\) by E48's exact abelianization. Conversely if \(G_s\) is cyclic, that abelianization forces it to have order \(2p\) and \(B_s\) to have order two. This gives \(H_s=1\).

For \(s=0\) and \(H_0=C_m\) nontrivial and odd, the local meridian of order two splits \(G_0=C_m\rtimes C_2\). If its conjugation acts by \(b\in(\mathbb Z/m)^\times\), then \(b^2=1\) and \(\gcd(b-1,m)=1\) by \(G_0^{\rm ab}=C_2\). Thus \(b=-1\bmod m\) and the group is odd-dihedral. Conversely an odd-dihedral group has this cyclic kernel for the unique meridian-parity epimorphism. An order or determinant of first homology of the double cover alone gives neither group criterion.

## An exact mirror chart and the turnbacks at the corner

Use the Hopf projection \(q\mapsto qi q^{-1}\). The all-positive octant with its cyclic edge identification is a base chart. The generic right fibre angle \(\phi\) has period \(P=\pi/17\), combining right \(C_{17}\) with the shared antipodal action. Along the edge from \(i\) to \(j\) take the section \(q_0(t,0)=e^{kt/2}\), \(0\le t\le\pi/2\). On a strip around its open edge set
\[
 m(t,y)=\cos y(\cos t\,i+\sin t\,j)+\sin y\,k,\qquad
 q_0(t,y)=e^{kt/2}e^{-jy/2}.
\]
Then \(\operatorname{Ad}_{q_0(t,y)}i=m(t,y)\). In this gauge the edge reflection \((k,j)\) acts on \(q=q_0(t,y)e^{i\phi}\) as
\[
      (t,y,\phi)\longmapsto(t,-y,\pi/2-\phi)\pmod P.
\]
Indeed \(k e^{-jy/2}=e^{jy/2}k\), \(k e^{i\phi}=e^{-i\phi}k\), and \(k j^{-1}=i\). Thus the two old singular strands on this open edge are the constant phases \(P/4,3P/4\), equivalently \(\pi/4,\pi/4+P/2\) modulo \(P\).

At the \(i\)-corner there is also the reflection \((j,j)\) across the other face. In the fibre based at \(q_0=1\) it acts by \(\phi\mapsto-\phi\), with fixed phases \(0,P/2\). The two residual reflections commute modulo the already-quotiented fibre translations and their product acts by a half-period on this fibre. Each reflection exchanges the two fixed points of the other. The local continuation therefore joins each face's two singular branches in its own turnback at the corner. The two turnbacks are distinct. The terminal \(j\)-corner is identified to the initial \(i\)-corner by left multiplication by \(b^{-1}\) and cyclic relabelling, with \(b=(1+i+j+k)/2\). The transition in the chosen section is
\[
            b^{-1}q_0(\pi/2,0)=b^{-1}e^{k\pi/4}=e^{-i\pi/4}.
\]
It sends the terminal pair to \(0,P/2\) in the initial corner gauge, on the face distinct from the incoming pair at \(P/4,3P/4\). In particular the edge representatives are two explicit strands capped at both ends. Treating the corner as a continuation that swaps the distinct cap pairs changes the singular locus.

## The actual contact and its relative branch twist

Let the positive direction of \(\ell\) come from increasing great-circle parameter on \(q=p_0e^{js}\), where \(p_0jp_0^{-1}=n=(i+j+k)/\sqrt3\). Before folding to the positive edge take the contact lift \(qi q^{-1}=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2\). Then \(qjq^{-1}=n\), and orthogonality gives
\[
 q^{-1}kq=j/\sqrt3+\sqrt{2/3}\,k .
\]
Fold this contact by \((j,j)\), so \(q'=j q j^{-1}\) projects to \((1,1,0)/\sqrt2\). One obtains \(q'^{-1}kq'=-j/\sqrt3+\sqrt{2/3}\,k\). It follows in the above edge trivialization \(q'=q_0(\pi/4,0)e^{i\phi_\ell}\) that
\[
            \phi_\ell=\tfrac12\arctan\sqrt2-\pi/4\pmod P.
\]
Indeed conjugating \(k\) by \(e^{-i\phi_\ell}\) gives \(k\cos(2\phi_\ell)+j\sin(2\phi_\ell)\). The phase modulo \(P\) lies strictly in \((P/4,P/2)\). To check this exactly, \(\beta=\arctan\sqrt2\) lies between \(3\pi/10\) and \(5\pi/16\): the cosine of twice the lower angle is \(-(\sqrt5-1)/4>-1/3\), and that of twice the upper is \(-\sin(\pi/8)<-1/3\). These inequalities imply \(5\pi/17<\beta<11\pi/34\) and give the stated phase.

Put \(\tau=2\pi(\phi-\pi/4)/P\). An explicit underlying disk coordinate is
\[
                  W=\cosh(y+i\tau).
\]
Equivalently \(W=(\zeta+\zeta^{-1})/2\) with \(\zeta=e^{y+i\tau}\). On a strip of bounded \(|y|\) this realizes the annular reflection quotient as an ellipse disk. The mirror fibre maps to the interval \([-1,1]\), and its two singular points are the marked branch endpoints \(+1,-1\). The contact of \(\ell\) is at \(0<W<1\) on the real interval, since \(0<\tau<\pi/2\). Its oriented Hopf tangent before the fold is proportional to \((1,1,-2)\). After the fold the edge-tangent component is still nonzero and the \(y\)-component is negative. Thus the arc is a graph in the longitudinal coordinate and, in the positive great-circle direction, its \(W\)-path crosses the slit from upper to lower half-plane.

In the punctured disk \(D\setminus\{-1,+1\}\), a path that avoids the whole mirror interval and has the same endpoints as the contact arc has the opposite sheet transition in the annular double cover. The initial path crosses the slit once. Concatenating it with the reverse of an interior detour has odd intersection modulo two with the segment between the two punctures, hence odd branch monodromy. It cannot be relatively homotopic to the constant path in the punctured disk. This proves that the local smoothing of \(\ell\) as an underlying knot cannot be performed inside this chart with both old singular strands fixed, even if the isotopy is allowed to leave the class of graphs.

For minimal detours this gives a signed local instruction in the complex \(W\)-orientation. The original descending crossing followed by the reverse of the right-end detour winds \(+1\) about \(+1\) and zero about \(-1\). With the left-end detour it winds \(-1\) about \(-1\) and zero about \(+1\). This is the planar meridian convention for the two specified detours, up to basing. Both computations use a detour that winds only around its named endpoint. Extra wraps are additional relative homotopy data. Consequently a marked reconstruction must carry a strand motion or insert this relative meridian when replacing the crossing by an interior graph. It cannot simply erase the crossing or replace the two mirror points by an isotropy label on the new surgery core.

The edge phases, corner transition, and local correction determine this part of the marked tangle. They do not yet identify the full knot \(S\subset V\) after a global ambient isotopy, or \(K_s\) after its marked filling. A global move tracking the two old singular strands through the complementary solid-torus identification remains necessary before the cyclic or odd-dihedral tests above can be used.
