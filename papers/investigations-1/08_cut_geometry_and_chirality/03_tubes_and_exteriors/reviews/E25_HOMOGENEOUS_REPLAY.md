**Verdict.** Let \(Y=\Gamma\backslash SU(2)\) be a homogeneous spherical three-manifold, with a finite group acting on the left of the unit round model. Delete the interior of an actual smooth ball to obtain \(P\). Every orientation-preserving smooth monodromy of \(P\) that preserves its boundary setwise is isotopic through such maps to a periodic map. For the mapping torus of each such monodromy, both fillings by \(D^2\times S^2\) admit smooth effective circle actions. Whenever a filling is a homotopy four-sphere it is the standard smooth \(S^4\). The comparison carries the two smooth boundary framings as an unordered pair.

## 1. The puncture and its smooth blow-up

Let \(\psi:P\to P\) be the given monodromy and \(b=\psi|_{\partial P}\). Fix a smooth product collar of the actual boundary. After an isotopy relative to the boundary, \(\psi\) is \((x,s)\mapsto(b(x),s)\) on a thinner collar. To see the collar correction, compare the fixed collar with its image by \(\psi\), parametrizing both by the same boundary points using \(b^{-1}\). In fixed collar coordinates the image has tangential part equal to \(x\) at \(s=0\), and positive first normal derivative. Interpolate the small tangential displacement in an exponential chart and the positive normal derivatives on a short collar. The resulting path of collars is an isotopy rel boundary and extends into \(P\) by using its time-dependent vector field with a cutoff.

Cap \(P\) with the actual ball \(B\) and choose its centre \(p\) in a smooth parametrization of \(B\). The orientation-preserving diffeomorphism \(b\) of \(S^2\) is smoothly isotopic to the identity. Extend \(\psi\) across \(B\) by an angular interpolation from \(b\) near its outer boundary to the identity on a small inner ball. This yields a smooth orientation-preserving \(\bar\psi:Y\to Y\) fixing \(p\). The only surface-group input here is path connectivity of \(\operatorname{Diff}^+(S^2)\), from Smale's deformation retract onto \(SO(3)\). The AMS PDF route was denied. The statement of Theorem A was checked in its reproduced first-page excerpt. [Smale, *Diffeomorphisms of the 2-sphere*, Proc. AMS 10 (1959), 621–626](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1959-0112149-8) · [Theorem A excerpt](https://math.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/seminars/download.php?fid=2693).

A smooth diffeomorphism \(F\) fixing \(p\) lifts to a diffeomorphism of the spherical real blow-up \(Y[p]\). In a smooth coordinate chart at \(p\), write \(F(ru)=rA(r,u)\), with \(A\) given by integrating \(DF\) along the radial segment. At \(r=0\) it is \(DF_pu\ne0\). Thus the lifted collar map is
\[
(r,u)\longmapsto\left(r|A(r,u)|,\frac{A(r,u)}{|A(r,u)|}\right).
\]
The inverse and every smooth pointed isotopy lift by the same formula. In particular the new boundary sphere records the normalized derivative, and the lift may move this sphere pointwise.

The lift of \(\bar\psi\) represents the actual punctured monodromy. Indeed, using the ball parametrization, the blow-up is \(P\) with an extra spherical collar. On that added collar the lift is \((s,u)\mapsto(s,b_s(u))\), with \(b_s=b\) near its join \(s=0\). Replacing \(b_s\) by \(b_{(1-v)s}\) for \(0\le v\le1\) is a smooth isotopy through boundary-setwise diffeomorphisms, ending with the constant extension of \(b\). Stretch a product collar of the original \(P\) to include the added collar. The stretch carries the product representative of \(\psi\) exactly to this constant extension. This shows why the result concerns the chosen smooth puncture and does not follow from an unpointed identification of the capped bundle alone.

## 2. Pointed realization and point pushes

Let \(I^+(Y)\) and \(D^+(Y)\) be the orientation-preserving isometry and smooth diffeomorphism groups. Bamler–Kleiner's Theorem 1.3 in the 2019 paper states that the inclusion of the isometry group into the smooth diffeomorphism group is a homotopy equivalence for an isometric quotient of the round sphere. Its hypotheses include \(S^3\) and \(\mathbb{RP}^3\). Their earlier Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.2 give the expected inclusion for the nonexceptional spherical space forms and expressly exclude those two from that argument. Orientation sign is constant along paths, so the relevant versions give the corresponding orientation-preserving component comparison. [Bamler–Kleiner 2019, Theorem 1.3, p. 2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08710v1) · [Earlier author text, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.06197) · [Earlier journal version, JAMS 36 (2023), 563–589](https://doi.org/10.1090/jams/1003).

Right translations of \(SU(2)\) are isometries of the left quotient and are transitive. Choose \(p=[1]\) by a fixed isometry. Evaluation at \(p\) on either group is locally trivial using local sections supplied by the right translations. Comparing the two evaluation fibrations over the same \(Y\) and using the unpointed homotopy equivalence gives a bijection on the path components of their fibres \(I^+(Y,p)\) and \(D^+(Y,p)\). Here the comparison applies to their full fibres, including components associated with loops in \(Y\).

There is an explicit surjectivity argument. For \(F\in D^+(Y,p)\) choose a smooth isotopy \(F_t\) from \(F\) to an isometry. The component information initially supplies a continuous path in the smooth topology. Subdividing and interpolating successive small diffeomorphisms in exponential coordinates gives a smooth isotopy, which can be made constant near its endpoints. Lift \(F_t(p)\) to \(a_t\in SU(2)\) starting at \(1\), and let \(R_{a_t}\) denote right translation. Then \(R_{a_t}^{-1}F_t\) fixes \(p\) for all \(t\). Its endpoint is an isometry fixing \(p\).

For a point push, take a diffeomorphism isotopy from the identity to the push with evaluation the based loop in question. The right-translation path follows that same loop and ends at right translation by some \(\gamma\in\Gamma\). The correction \(R_{a_t}^{-1}F_t\) is a pointed isotopy from the identity to \(R_\gamma^{-1}F_1\). Thus that point push is represented by \(R_\gamma\) in the pointed component group. The order divides the finite order of \(\gamma\). Some loops yield the same pointed component, which is compatible with this argument. The evaluation correction accounts for every point-pushing class. A separate framing of \(T_pY\) is allowed to rotate in the isotopy, and the consequence for the exterior is a boundary-setwise isotopy.

The compact Lie group \(I^+(Y,p)\) contains a finite-order element in every component. For \(g\) in this group, the closure \(C\) of its powers is compact abelian. Its identity component is a torus, and some \(g^d\) lies in it. Choose \(a\in C^0\) with \(a^d=g^{-d}\), using surjectivity of the power map on the torus. Then \(ga\) has finite order and is in the same component as \(g\). Applying this to the pointed isometry representative and then lifting the pointed isotopy gives a periodic monodromy representing the original \(\psi\) up to boundary-setwise isotopy. Neither an unpointed realization nor a boundary-pointwise realization has been assumed.

## 3. The two framings and effective smooth actions

Let \(J\) be the finite-order pointed orientation-preserving isometry, of exact order \(d\). In geodesic normal coordinates its action is determined by \(DJ_p\), acting orthogonally on directions. The derivative has order \(d\): an isometry fixing a point with derivative the identity is the identity, first by the exponential map and then by continuation on the connected manifold. In particular on the blown-up boundary \(DJ_p=A_{2\pi k/d}\) is a rotation about a chosen axis, with \(\gcd(k,d)=1\) for \(d>1\), and \(k=0\) when \(d=1\).

Isotopic monodromies through boundary-setwise diffeomorphisms give diffeomorphic smooth mapping tori with boundary. For the convention \((1,x)\sim(0,\psi_0(x))\), if the target monodromy is \(\psi_1\), use a path \(h_t\) from the identity to \(\psi_1^{-1}\psi_0\), stationary near the endpoints, and send \((t,x)\) to \((t,h_t(x))\). This gives a diffeomorphism over the base circle. It induces a smooth map on the entire boundary, with possible rotation parity in its \(S^2\)-coordinates over the circle.

Hatcher's December 2003 revision, p. 1, identifies the homotopy type of \(\operatorname{Diff}(S^1\times S^2)\) by inclusion of the explicit subgroup with factors \(O(2)\), \(O(3)\) and \(\Omega SO(3)\). The first two factors extend over \(D^2\times S^2\). The remaining parity is represented by the identity or one full rotation of \(S^2\) going around \(S^1\). An odd rotation cannot extend over this cap. Gluing two caps by it produces the nontrivial \(S^2\)-bundle over \(S^2\): the section through a fixed pole has normal clutching rotation of degree one, hence odd self-intersection. The identity double \(S^2\times S^2\) has even intersection form. An extension would identify the doubles, a contradiction. Thus exactly two rotation framings occur after quotienting the boundary isotopy classes by cap extensions. Under a diffeomorphism of exteriors the two classes are transported as an unordered pair. Equality of the two resulting closed diffeomorphism types on a specific exterior is compatible with distinct cap-side framings. [Hatcher, *On the Diffeomorphism Group of \(S^1\times S^2\)*, author revision](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/Papers/newDiffS1xS2.pdf).

The parity can occur even for a monodromy that is the identity on the boundary. On a collar with inward coordinate \(s\), choose \(\chi(s)=1\) near the boundary and zero near the inner end. The maps \(h_t(x,s)=(A_{2\pi t\chi(s)}x,s)\), extended by the identity elsewhere, give a boundary-setwise isotopy from the identity to a collar twist \(\psi=h_1\) fixing the boundary pointwise. For the comparison from its mapping torus to the identity mapping torus, the induced boundary map from the formula above runs through \(A_{2\pi t}\). It has odd rotation parity. This exhibits why the terminal recognition can carry both framings without assigning either of the original labels individually.

For the periodic representative use the convention \((t+1,u)\sim(t,A_{2\pi k/d}u)\) on the boundary. The product coordinates
\[
\zeta=e^{2\pi it},\qquad y=A_{2\pi kt/d}u
\]
are global there. Translation by \(d\lambda/(2\pi)\) defines a circle action with \(\lambda\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z)\). In these coordinates it sends \((\zeta,y)\) to \((e^{id\lambda}\zeta,A_{k\lambda}y)\). The action is effective on the exterior because the period of the actual monodromy is exactly \(d\). It extends in either framing to
\[
(z,y')\longmapsto(e^{id\lambda}z,A_{(k+jd)\lambda}y'),\qquad
z\in D^2,\quad j\in\{0,1\},
\]
where the odd case uses \(y'=A_{j\arg\zeta}y\) on the boundary. The formula is smooth at the disc zero section and the two collar actions agree. This verifies smoothness across the attaching map; it does not depend on the cap metric. Applying the boundary isotopy for general representatives gives the action on their diffeomorphic fillings.

## 4. Smooth recognition and limits

Galaz-García and Searle, Theorem 3.2, p. 1404, state the curvature-free smooth classification for a closed simply connected smooth four-manifold with a circle action. The smooth connected-sum factors are \(S^4\), signed \(\mathbb{CP}^2\), and \(S^2\times S^2\). This is their published synthesis of Fintushel, Pao and three-dimensional Poincaré. The differently scoped curvature theorem immediately following is Theorem 3.3 and is unnecessary here. For a homotopy sphere \(b_2=0\), so only the \(S^4\) factor can occur. Both cap framings kill the same boundary generator up to sign. Thus if one filling is a homotopy sphere, both are simply connected by van Kampen. Each has Euler characteristic two by additivity over the mapping torus and the cap, so orientable duality and Hurewicz–Whitehead give the homotopy-sphere condition for both. The collar construction above supplies the smooth effective action on the actual filled manifold up to diffeomorphism. The historical Fintushel and Pao theorem passages were inaccessible in the E04 audit. This packet uses the checked published smooth statement in Theorem 3.2 and does not upgrade the original-source inspection level.
