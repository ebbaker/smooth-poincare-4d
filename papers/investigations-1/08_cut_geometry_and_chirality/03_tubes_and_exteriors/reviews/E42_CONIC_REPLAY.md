## Orbit shapes and marking

For the Fubini–Study metric of holomorphic sectional curvature four, consider the line generated by the unit vector \(Z=(\cos t,\mathrm i\sin t,0)\) in \(\mathbb C^3\). Its derivative is horizontal for \(S^5\to\mathbb{CP}^2\), has norm one, and is real-orthogonal to the orbit directions. The squared norm of a projected vector \(\xi\) is \(|\xi|^2-|\langle Z,\xi\rangle|^2\). The elementary rotations about the three real coordinate axes have projected lengths \(\sin t,\cos t,\cos(2t)\) on \(0\le t\le\pi/4\). For the third one, the lift has norm one and its vertical component has length \(\sin(2t)\). The quadratic form is diagonal on these generators. A unit quaternion generator rotates physical coordinates at twice its speed. Take the order \(i,j,k\) to correspond to the third, first, and second physical axes, and put \(y=\pi/4-t\), distance outward from the conic. With \(z=\sin(2y)\) and \(C=\sqrt{1-z^2}=\cos(2y)>0\) on the open conic chamber, this gives the length triple
\[
 (a_1,a_2,a_3)=(2z,\sqrt{2(1-z)},\sqrt{2(1+z)}).
\]
The published rotation normalization and shapes agree with this direct projection. [Chi Li, *Numerical Solutions of Kähler–Einstein Metrics on \(\mathbb P^2\) with Conical Singularities along a Smooth Quadric Curve*, J. Geom. Anal. 25 (2015), 1773–1797, Example 3.1](https://sites.math.rutgers.edu/~cl1412/papers/numcone.pdf) · [Ziller, *On the Geometry of Cohomogeneity One Manifolds with Positive Curvature*, §2, pp. 5–6](https://www2.math.upenn.edu/~wziller/papers/GeometryofCohOneExamples.pdf)

The principal \(SO(3)\) stabilizer is the half turn around the third physical axis since the unequal lengths of the real and imaginary parts require the preserving complex phase to be \(\pm1\). Its lift is \(\langle i\rangle\) of order four. Complex conjugation freely acts on the conic-side closed segment away from the real locus, and on this ray is represented by the half turn about the first physical axis. It enlarges the regular stabilizer downstairs to \(Q_8\), without changing the collapsing circle and its angular period \(\pi/2\). Hence \(a_1=4y+O(y^3)\) at the conic and smooth physical radial arclength \(r\) requires \(y_r(0)=1\). The prescribed terminal \(y=\pi/12\) gives \(z=1/2\), lengths \((1,1,\sqrt3)\), and the first axis is short. Here the logarithmic derivatives with respect to \(y\) are unequal, so the actual face is umbilic precisely when \(y_r=0\). This condition is preserved by a smooth conformal rescaling. ## Two chiral blocks and scalar curvature

Put \(v=y_r\), \(u=v^2\), \(q=v_r/C\), and \(p_i=\partial_y\log a_i\) on any regular shape. Let \(K_i\) be the intrinsic sectional curvature in the other two axis directions and \(M_i\) the mixed entry of the paired two-form block. From the diagonal Cartan calculation, with \(d\sigma_i=2\sigma_j\wedge\sigma_k\) cyclically, the radial entry is \(-a_{i,rr}/a_i\), the tangential entry is \(K_i-v^2p_jp_k\), and the mixed entry is
\[
 M_i=v\left(-\mu_i p_i+N_kp_j+N_jp_k\right),\quad
 \mu_i=\frac{2a_i}{a_j a_k},\quad
 N_i=\frac{\mu_j+\mu_k-\mu_i}{2}.
\]
Substitution gives
\[
\begin{aligned}
 p&=(2C/z,-C/(1-z),C/(1+z)),\\
 \mu&=(2z/C,(1-z)/(zC),(1+z)/(zC)),\\
 N&=(C/z,(1+z)/C,(z-1)/C),\\
 K_i&=N_i\mu_i-N_jN_k,\qquad K=(3,2/z-1,-2/z-1),\\
 M&=v(-2,1,1).
\end{aligned}
\]
The radial shape contribution is \(u(4,1,1)-v_r p\). Since \(\sum K_i=1\), \(\sum p_jp_k=-5\), and \(\sum p_i=2(1-2z^2)/(zC)\), the scalar curvature and Weyl blocks reduce to
\[
\begin{aligned}
 R&=2+22u-\frac{4(1-2z^2)}{z}\,q,\\
 V_\pm:=\lambda_1^\pm
   &=\frac23(u+2)\mp2v-\frac{2-z^2}{3z}\,q,\\
 D:=\lambda_2^\pm-\lambda_3^\pm
   &=\frac{2(1-u)}{z}+q .
\end{aligned}
\]
In particular at \(v=1\) and \(q=0\) this gives \(R=24\), a zero plus block, and a minus block with eigenvalues \((4,-2,-2)\). The chirality sign is therefore fixed without using an ambient complex-orientation convention. The regular formulas in \(r\) apply even at a stop with \(0<z<1\). On any strictly increasing branch \(z_r=2Cv>0\) and \(q=u_z\). Downstairs the orbit volume is \((2\pi^2/8)a_1a_2a_3=\pi^2 zC\). The squared norm of a trace-free block of that axial eigenvalue and transverse difference is \((3V_\pm^2+D^2)/2\). Thus the energy in unnormalized arclength and the normalized energy on an increasing branch are
\[
 \frac{dE_\pm}{\pi^2}=\frac{zC}{2}(3V_\pm^2+D^2)\,dr,
 \qquad
 \frac{dE_\pm}{\pi^2}=\frac{z}{4v}(3V_\pm^2+D^2)\,dz .
\]
The second expression is only needed where \(v>0\). Using it as an integral with positive \(dz\) through an interval of negative speed would lose the orientation of that integration. [E20, Cartan and quotient normalization](E20_SHORT_AXIS_READBACK.md#fixed-profile-and-independent-curvature-replay)

There is also a direct check of the signed defect, valid even through stops and reversals in \(r\). Write \(V_\pm=B\mp2v\), where \(B=\frac23(u+2)-\frac{2-z^2}{3z}q\). The two squared densities differ by
\[
 \frac{d(E_+-E_-)}{\pi^2}
   =-12zCvB\,dr=d\bigl(2(2-z^2)v^2-4z^2\bigr).
\]
This endpoint expression starts at \(4\) for \((z,v)=(0,1)\) and finishes at \(-1\) for \((z,v)=(1/2,0)\). Therefore the normalized signed defect is exactly \(-5\), in agreement with the APS calculation and without any speed quantifier beyond smoothness and the endpoint data.

## Weighted bound and the role of the shape range

Take the first zero of \(v\) and the last point with \(v=1\) preceding that zero, using the bolt if necessary. On the intervening open interval \(0<v<1\) and \(z\) increases strictly. The speed can still vary nonmonotonically there. This selection allows earlier values \(v>1\) and permits later reversals. It is needed because the undifferentiated term \(\frac23(v-1)(v-2)\) has negative sign on \(1<v<2\).

On compact truncations of this branch let \(h(z)=3z/((2-z^2)\sqrt v)\). The nonnegative undifferentiated term in \(V_+\) gives
\[
 \int h(z)V_+\,dz \ \ge\ -\int u^{-1/4}u_z\,dz
  =\tfrac43\bigl(v_{\rm in}^{3/2}-v_{\rm out}^{3/2}\bigr).
\]
The primitive requires no sign for \(u_z\). Squaring the Cauchy–Schwarz bound for the left side uses the axial energy weight \(3z/(4v)\), so the squared dual weight is \(h^2\,4v/(3z)=12z/(2-z^2)^2\). To take the endpoint limits, select truncations with \(v_{\rm in}>v_{\rm out}>0\). Their right sides tend to \(4/3\), even if \(h\) itself is unbounded at the stop. If \(b\) is the value of \(z\) at the first stop, the full energy therefore satisfies
\[
 \frac{E_+}{\pi^2}\ge
 \frac{(4/3)^2}{\int_0^b12z/(2-z^2)^2\,dz}
 =\frac{16(2-b^2)}{27b^2}.
\]
The denominator was enlarged by including shapes from zero through the start of the selected branch. For the prescribed shape range \(0\le y\le\pi/12\), \(b\le1/2\) and the lower bound is at least \(112/27\). If one permits the shapes themselves to continue beyond \(y=\pi/12\) while remaining in the regular chamber, the conclusion of this calculation is the \(b\)-dependent bound. It still excludes the closed wall when \(b^2\le32/97\), through \(E_-\ge E_++5\pi^2\). The fixed numerical bound \(112/27\) uses \(b\le1/2\).

On the stated class the defect gives \(E_-/\pi^2\ge247/27>8\) and \(e_W/\pi^2\ge359/54\). Any smooth homotopy-sphere closing has equal signed energies by signature zero and adds nonnegative energy of each sign to this cap. Thus every closing retaining it exceeds the strict closed wall. This argument uses neither positive scalar curvature nor the existence of a particular closing.

## Positive Neumann sign and the explicit test

If the speed is nonnegative and nonincreasing in \(r\), then \(q=v_r/C\le0\), and \(1-2z^2\ge1/2\) on the prescribed range. The physical arclength formula gives \(R\ge2\) away from the bolt and by continuity at any smooth bolt and seam. The coefficient keeps the requisite sign through \(0<y\le\pi/8\) in the regular chamber, and changes sign after that. This scalar estimate is distinct from the speed-unrestricted energy bound.

The exact trial \(v^2+4z^2=1\) follows the smooth first-order system \(z_r=2Cv\), \(v_r=-8zC\) with \((z,v)=(0,1)\) initially. The first point \((z,v)=(1/2,0)\) is reached in finite radial length, as seen by \(z=\tfrac12\sin\theta\), \(v=\cos\theta\), \(\theta_r=4\sqrt{1-\sin^2\theta/4}\). The system's reversal about the bolt gives \(z\) odd and \(v\) even, and reversal about the terminal slice gives \(z\) even and \(v\) odd. Smooth bolt regularity follows from the slope-four collapsing profile and the horizontal sum of squares being constant with its trace-free difference proportional to \(z\). In the marked slice the trace-free horizontal tensor rotates with the same weight four and reflection character as the Cartesian normal coordinates. Since \(z\) is odd, its coefficient gives a smooth equivariant tensor on that normal disk. The terminal orbit coefficients are even, so the reflection is smooth. [E20, marked bolt regularity](E20_SHORT_AXIS_READBACK.md#fixed-profile-and-independent-curvature-replay)

Here \(q=-8z\), \(D=0\), and \(R=56-152z^2\ge18\). This directly gives strict positivity of the Neumann form on the cap and positive scalar curvature on its smooth reflective double. The two energies reduce separately to
\[
 \frac{E_\pm}{\pi^2}
 =\frac1{12}\int_0^1(2v^2\mp3v+9)^2\,dv
 =\begin{cases}
  167/30&(+),\\
  317/30&(-).
 \end{cases}
\]
Thus the positive exact profile satisfies the marking and misses the band. The exclusion covers speed changes on this prescribed curve of conic orbit shapes. It gives no lower bound for arbitrary deformation of that curve.
