## Actual geometric hypotheses and reverse circle

Let \(N\) be the period-six capped mapping torus, and call its actual section \(\gamma\) and its projection \(\ell\). Its suspension circle acts effectively, with no fixed points. The \(Q_8\times C_{17}\) deck extensions in E39 show that \(\ell\) lies in the regular stratum and that \(\gamma\) projects diffeomorphically to \(\ell\). The old order-two singular circle is disjoint from this regular tube. The principal circle bundle over a solid torus around \(\ell\) is trivial, since its base has \(H^2=0\). Multiplying the trivialization by the radially extended inverse fibre coordinate of \(\gamma\) puts the actual section at constant fibre coordinate. Use coordinates
\[
 P=S^1_a\times D^2_\mu\times S^1_f,\qquad
 T=S^1_a\times\{0\}\times S^1_f,\qquad
 \gamma=S^1_a\times\{0\}\times\{1\}.
\]
For the slope \(a+s\mu\) the disk chart differs by \(s\) turns along \(S^1_a\). The new three-frame rotates its disk two-plane by \(2\pi s\) while fixing the fibre direction. Its class in \(\pi_1 SO(3)=C_2\) is \(s\bmod2\). Thus \(s=-1\) gives the same initial framed surgery as \(s=1\), although the intermediate orbit filling retains the integer slope. In either parity, the original circle surgery kills the actual stable generator of \(\pi_1N\). The \(Q_8\) cycle and \(C_{17}\) inversion then trivialize the other generators, and the closed result has Euler characteristic two. Consequently each result \(\Sigma_s\) is a smooth homotopy sphere, with either orientation.

Here is the relative local model of the second surgery. Let \(C_f=\{0\}\times S^1_f\) be the fibre core in \(D^2_\mu\times S^1_f\). Take a small ball \(B\) about \((0,1)\) meeting \(C_f\) in a diameter, and perform the first surgery in \(S^1_a\times B\) by attaching \(D^2_a\times\partial B\). The remaining core arc \(c=C_f\setminus\operatorname{int}B\) produces the compressed sphere \(S_T\) from \(S^1_a\times c\) and the two disks \(D^2_a\) at its ends. Its normal disk bundle is product, using the normal meridian of \(C_f\) and the two polar disks on \(\partial B\) to match the annulus and cap normal charts. Remove a small product neighbourhood of \(S_T\). Locally the complement is \(S^1_\mu\) times a punctured solid torus: the second factor is obtained by attaching a three-dimensional two-handle \(D^2_a\times I\) to \(S^1_a\times S^1_f\times[0,1]\) along \(S^1_a\times I\) at the inner end. The compression of the inner torus is a sphere, and the uncompressed torus has meridian \(a\). Filling that puncture by \(B^3\) times \(S^1_\mu\) is precisely sphere surgery. Thus the new local piece is
\[
    S^1_\mu\times D^2_a\times S^1_f .
\]
All local labels are in the rotating chart for \(a+s\mu\). Fibre rotation on \(S^1_f\) matches the original action on its boundary. The resulting smooth effective action on \(W_s\) is fixed-point-free and free on the replacement, so the orbit space is the actual \(Q_s=O(a+s\mu)\) with a regular new dual core and the old exceptional locus retained. The reverse-surgery circle \(\delta\) is the \(S^1_\mu\) core of the ball product inserted by sphere surgery, with the framing supplied by the product identification. Reverse framed surgery recovers the original \(\Sigma_s\), not a sphere obtained by filling a different orbifold. ## Total-space group and the degree of a principal orbit

The common exterior \(V=\Sigma_s\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu S_T=W_s\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu\delta\) has \(H_1(V)=\mathbb Z\), generated by a normal meridian to \(S_T\). Indeed excision and the Thom isomorphism give \(H_2(\Sigma_s,V)=\mathbb Z\) with boundary this meridian, and \(H_2(\Sigma_s)=H_1(\Sigma_s)=0\). The \(S^1\times D^3\) reattachment induces the identity on this \(H_1\) via its common boundary. Hence \(H_1(W_s)=\mathbb Z\), with \([\delta]\) primitive. This argument requires no topological identification of the homotopy sphere. Reverse surgery implies further that \([\delta]\) normally generates \(\Pi_s=\pi_1(W_s)\).

Use degree for the abelianization \(\Pi_s\to\mathbb Z\), oriented so \(\deg\delta=1\). A principal orbit determines an element \(h\) which is central: moving its basepoint along a loop sweeps a cylinder that conjugates the two orbit classes. The Borel classifying space of the locally free action has the orbit orbifold's fundamental group. One can take the homotopy sequence of the principal bundle \(S^1\to ES^1\times W_s\to ES^1\times_{S^1}W_s\); over each slice this model has the Borel classifying space of the finite slice group. Thus
\[
   \Pi_s/\langle h\rangle=\pi_1^{\mathrm{orb}}(Q_s)=G_s .
\]
In the local slice \(S^1\times_{C_b}D^3\), the principal orbit runs \(b\) times around the exceptional core orbit \(e\) after retraction to that core. Hence \(e^b=h^{\pm1}\) up to basepoint paths. In the two cases of interest \(|\deg h|=|G_s^{\mathrm{ab}}|\) by the quotient identity, because \(H_1(W_s)=\mathbb Z\). In particular \(h\) has infinite order and the kernel is genuinely infinite cyclic. The order-two orbit retained in \(W_s\) is the one over the old singular knot from E39, which the regular tube replacement never meets. ## Odd dihedral recognition for \(s=0\)

Suppose the actual \(G_0=S_3\). The degree of the principal orbit has magnitude two, and the retained \(C_2\)-orbit has degree of magnitude one. The commutator subgroup \(C=[\Pi_0,\Pi_0]\) maps onto \([S_3,S_3]\cong C_3\) and injects because its intersection with \(\langle h\rangle\) has degree zero. It follows that \(\Pi_0=C_3\rtimes\langle t\rangle\), where \(t\) is any degree-one lift. Its image is a reflection in \(S_3\), so \(t\) acts on \(C_3\) by inversion. Every degree-one element has form \(yt\), and \(x t x^{-1}=x^2t\) for \(x\in C_3\). Since squaring permutes \(C_3\), all degree-one elements lie in one conjugacy class. Orient the exceptional orbit to have degree one. Its class is freely homotopic to \(\delta\). A generic smooth one-parameter homotopy between loops, fixed in collars of the endpoint times, misses simultaneous double points in a four-manifold by transversality, since the source of such pairs at equal times has dimension three. It also misses vanishing tangent vectors. Thus the free homotopy becomes an isotopy of embedded loops, and isotopy extension carries the actual inverse-surgery framing to the exceptional orbit.

The smooth slice action in an oriented normal framing is \(u\cdot(z,y)=(u^b z,R_{a\arg u}y)\) for \(z\in S^1,y\in D^3\), where \(b=2\), \(R\) is rotation about a fixed axis, and \(a\) lifts the faithful slice weight modulo \(b\). It extends over the surgery cap \(S^2\times D^2\) by \((x,w)\mapsto(R_{a\arg u}x,u^b w)\). The other oriented normal framing is obtained by rotating \(y\) once along \(z\). This changes \(a\) by \(b\) and gives the same cap construction. Both actions are smooth and agree with the old action on an open complement. Any kernel element would fix that open set pointwise and, as an isometry for an averaged metric on the original connected intermediate, would be the identity. The resulting homotopy sphere is therefore standard by the curvature-free smooth circle-action classification. Galaz-García and Searle's published Theorem 3.2 applies to a closed simply connected smooth four-manifold with a smooth circle action, and its only listed summand with no second homology is \(S^4\). This is the published Fintushel–Pao–Perelman synthesis. The separate curvature theorem 3.3 is unnecessary. [Galaz-García–Searle, §3.2, Theorem 3.2, printed p. 1404](https://msp.org/gt/2014/18-3/gt-v18-n3-p05-s.pdf#page=8)

## Cyclic recognition, conditional comparison at \(s=-1\)

Suppose the actual \(G_{-1}=C_8\). A group with a central subgroup and cyclic quotient is abelian, so \(\Pi_{-1}=\mathbb Z\). Orient \(h\) and the generator \(t\) consistently so \(h=t^8\). The circle action lifts on the universal cover \(\widetilde W_{-1}\) to a commuting \(\mathbb R\)-action whose time-one translation is \(h\). It is free, since if a nonzero time closes upstairs, it represents a nonzero power of a principal or exceptional orbit downstairs. A power of that loop is a nonzero power of \(h\), which cannot lift to a loop. The action is proper as well: for compact \(A\), the set \([0,1]\cdot A\) is compact, and an intersection of \(A\) with its translate at time \(n+r\), \(n\in\mathbb Z,0\le r<1\), forces \(h^n([0,1]\cdot A)\) to meet \([0,1]\cdot A\). Only finitely many \(n\) are allowed by proper discontinuity of the deck action. Hence the transverse quotient \(\widetilde Q\) of the universal cover is a smooth three-manifold, simply connected by the principal real-line bundle sequence. The residual finite group \(C_8\) has quotient the compact underlying orbit space \(|Q_{-1}|\). It follows that \(\widetilde Q\) is closed. The smooth three-dimensional Poincaré theorem gives \(\widetilde Q\cong S^3\). This argument does not assume that the orbit orbifold is good before lifting the action. [Perelman, *Finite extinction time*, v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0307245)

A principal real-line bundle is trivial. Write the deck generator in that trivialization as \(t(x,r)=(g(x),r+\phi(x))\). Since \(t^8=h\), one has \(g^8=\operatorname{id}\) and \(\sum_{j=0}^7\phi(g^j x)=1\). The sum-zero part \(\psi=\phi-1/8\) is the coboundary \(u\circ g-u\) for \(u=(1/8)\sum_{j=1}^7 j\psi\circ g^j\). Changing the line coordinate by \(r\mapsto r-u(x)\) gives constant translation \(1/8\). Thus \(W_{-1}\) is the mapping torus of \(g\), which preserves orientation because both the deck map and its real-line action do. Hatcher's theorem implies that an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of \(S^3\) is isotopic to the identity. Therefore \(W_{-1}\cong S^3\times S^1\). The surgery circle \(\delta\), whose degree is one, is freely homotopic and hence ambiently isotopic to \(\{\mathrm{pt}\}\times S^1\), by the same dimension-four argument. [Hatcher, *A proof of the Smale Conjecture*, p. 553 and Appendix](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/Papers/SmaleConjecture.pdf)

The product-framed surgery on that generator gives the rounded boundary of \(D^3\times D^2\), using the pieces \(D^3\times S^1\) and \(S^2\times D^2\). It is standard. A loop of rotations in the stabilizer \(SO(3)\subset SO(4)\) of the chosen point of \(S^3\) represents the nontrivial element of \(\pi_1 SO(3)\). Acting by this loop in the \(S^3\) factor while advancing around the base circle gives a global diffeomorphism of \(S^3\times S^1\) fixing the generator and switching its oriented normal framing. The actual reverse surgery is therefore standard for either framing.

For completeness the finite-group averaging extends to the dihedral intermediate. Its lifted real action has the same freeness and properness because \(h\) has infinite order, and the transverse quotient is again \(S^3\). Here \(\deg h=2\) after orienting the fibres. For a deck element \(\gamma\) subtract \(\deg(\gamma)/2\) from its line shift. The resulting cocycle vanishes on \(h\) and factors through \(S_3\). Finite-group averaging removes it, so the commutator \(C_3\) acts without line translation and hence freely on the transverse \(S^3\). The intermediate is the mapping torus of an orientation-preserving involution of the lens quotient \(S^3/C_3\) inducing inversion on its fundamental group. This agrees with \(C_3\rtimes_{\rm inv}\mathbb Z\). The dihedral recognition itself uses the exceptional-orbit argument above.
