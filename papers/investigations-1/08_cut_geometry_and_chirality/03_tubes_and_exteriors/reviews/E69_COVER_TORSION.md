## The exact covering character and core value

Write $L=S_0\cup U\subset S^3$ in the ordinary meridional filling
and $Z=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu L$. Orient the two knots
so their linking number is $+3$. In $H_1(Z)$ use the two positive
meridians $\sigma$ of $S_0$ and $a$ of $U$, so the preferred zero
longitudes satisfy $[\lambda_S]=3[a]$ and $[\lambda_U]=3[\sigma]$.
The E39 meridian in the unfilled orbifold is
$[\mu]=-3[a]+3[\sigma]$. Its sign under the old twofold cover
character is $+1$. The sign character $\varepsilon$ sends each of
$\sigma,a$ to $-1$ in this exterior. The twofold unbranched
cover of $Z$ from $\varepsilon$, followed by branching along the
old $S_0$ tube only, is $E'$. It is the exterior of the single
degree-two lift of the regular marked knot in
$Y_+=S^3/(T^*\times C_{17})$. Its remaining boundary lattice
is generated by $2a$ and $\mu$. [E54 peripheral relation](../notes/E54_UNDERLYING_SLOPES.md#the-original-singular-knot-in-the-marked-exterior)
· [E65 covering lattice](E65_SIMULTANEOUS_CYCLIC.md#the-marked-link-determinant)

Over $F=\mathbb Q(y)$ use the character
\[
 \chi(\sigma)=-1,\quad \chi(a)=y,\qquad
 (\chi\varepsilon)(\sigma)=1,\quad(\chi\varepsilon)(a)=-y .
\]
In the covering subgroup, if $n$ is the exponent of $a$,
the exponent of $\sigma$ is congruent to $n$ modulo two.
Thus the restriction sends that element to $(-y)^n$.
On the lifted old tube, the primitive meridian projects to
$2\sigma$ and a core longitude projects to $\lambda_S+\sigma$.
These generate the index-two boundary lattice since the
zero longitude and meridian both have odd parity. This restricted
character extends over the branched filling and has old core
value $-y^3$. Changing its longitude by a lifted meridian
leaves the value unchanged. Reversing the core inverts the value
and changes its circle torsion by an integral Laurent unit.

On the remaining boundary take $\gamma=2a+\mu$. One has
$\chi(\mu)=-y^{-3}$ and $\chi(\gamma)=-y^{-1}$, so
$t=-y^{-1}$ is the variable corresponding to $\gamma$.
In particular $\gamma$ is primitive in the free quotient of
$H_1(E')$. The unfilled ambient $Y_+$ is a rational homology
sphere and $\mu$ is the filling meridian back to it, so
$H_1(E')$ has rank one. Since $\mu$ has free index three by the
character calculation and $|H_1(Y_+)|=51$, its torsion has
order seventeen. Thus $H_1(E')=\mathbb Z\gamma\oplus C_{17}$,
and the restriction of $\chi$ is the ordinary infinite cyclic
character, trivial on this finite summand.

## Induction, gluing, and the integral unit

Use the torsion convention in which a circle of monodromy $u\ne1$
has torsion $(u-1)^{-1}$. In the twofold unbranched cover of $Z$,
induction of $\chi|_{\ker\varepsilon}$ is $\chi\oplus\chi\varepsilon$.
This follows by tensoring the trivial and sign splitting of the
permutation representation by $\chi$. With a consistent pair of
coset lifts for each cell, the change from the two coset vectors
to the two eigenvectors has a single constant determinant repeated
for each cell. Its torsion power is the alternating cell count
$\chi_{\rm Euler}(Z)=0$. In particular its possible factor two or
one-half cancels exactly. Choices of lifts and orientations leave
only factors $\pm y^k$. This argument uses a cellular splitting
over characteristic zero. At odd primes the same splitting is
available for homology.

For an ordinary two-component link exterior, the two-variable
Alexander polynomial is its abelian torsion. The specializations
at $(-1,y)$ and $(1,-y)$ therefore give the two torsion factors
$\Delta_L(-1,y)$ and $\Delta_L(1,-y)$ whenever the indicated
systems are acyclic. One can verify specialization directly:
on a one-vertex two-dimensional spine, the signed maximal
minors of the lifted second boundary matrix are the entries of the
first boundary row times a common factor. The two-variable
greatest common divisor of the first boundary entries is one,
so the common factor is the integral two-variable Alexander
polynomial up to a unit. At a nontrivial character one chooses
a nonzero first boundary entry and the corresponding cofactor.
This argument gives acyclicity when that specialized polynomial
is nonzero and fixes its normalization even over an odd
characteristic field. Here $\Delta_L(-1,y)\ne0$ already follows
from its nonzero specialization at $y=1$ via the knot determinant.
For the other factor, Torres with $U$ unknotted gives
\[
 \Delta_L(1,-y)\doteq 1-y+y^2=\frac{1+y^3}{1+y}.
\]
The specialization at $y=1$ for the first factor is similarly
$\Delta_{S_0}(-1)$ up to sign. The equality of torsion and Alexander
polynomial, and the normalization as integral monomial units,
can also be read directly in the cited primary statements.
[Turaev, *Reidemeister torsion in knot theory*, Theorems 1.1.1–1.1.3](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/rm1971)
· [Torres, *On the Alexander polynomial*, Chapter III, Theorem 3](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/torres.pdf)

The old boundary torus and the attached solid torus are acyclic:
their lifted longitudinal character is $-y^3$, distinct from
one. The old meridional value is one. In a product cell structure
on the boundary torus the cancelling two copies of the
longitudinal differential give torsion $\pm1$ in product bases, up to a
character value.
The attached solid torus retracts to its core with torsion
$(-y^3-1)^{-1}$. Multiplicativity for the based gluing sequence
then gives
\[
 \tau(E',\chi)\doteq
 \frac{\Delta_L(-1,y)\Delta_L(1,-y)}{1+y^3}
 \doteq\frac{\Delta_L(-1,y)}{1+y}.
\]
Every equality marked $\doteq$ here permits only a sign and a
Laurent monomial. In particular there is no factor arising
from the degree two of the old meridian. That degree determines
which slope is killed, while the torsion of the attached
solid torus comes from its primitive longitude.
[Turaev, Theorem 0.2.3 and Lemma 1.3.3](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/rm1971)

For the infinite cyclic chain of $E'$ there is again a finite
two-dimensional spine. The zeroth module has order $t-1$.
The second module is zero since it is a submodule of a free
module and its fraction-field rank is zero by the preceding
acyclicity. Thus $\tau(E',t)\doteq \Delta_{E'}(t)/(t-1)$ with
$\Delta_{E'}$ the integral order of its first Alexander module.
For an integral verification of the constants, localize the
finite based free Laurent chain at each height-one prime of
$\mathbb Z[t,t^{-1}]$. The Smith orders over each resulting
discrete valuation ring give the torsion valuation as the
alternating sum of the module orders, including integer primes.
The resulting identities of valuations leave only the integral
units $\pm t^k$. Since $t-1=-(1+y)/y$, the conclusion is
\[
 \boxed{\ \Delta_{E'}(t)\doteq
 \Delta_L(-1,-t^{-1})\doteq\Delta_L(-1,-t)\ } .
\]
The final equivalence uses link Alexander reciprocity, or
equivalently changes which free generator is assigned $t$.

## Specializations as homology, and the E68 replay

Let $A=H_1(\widetilde E';\mathbb Z)$ be the integral infinite
cyclic Alexander module. Over an odd-characteristic field $k$
and at $t_0\ne1$, the rank-one twisted first homology of $E'$ is
$A\otimes_{\mathbb Z[t^{\pm1}]}k_{t_0}$. Indeed after inverting
$t-1$ the first cellular boundary surjects to the free zeroth
chain module and splits. The remaining first homology is a
cokernel, which commutes with this specialization.

At $y=1$ or $y=\pm i$ the other induced character $(1,-y)$ is
acyclic in odd characteristic: its Torres factor $1-y+y^2$
is respectively $1$ or $-y$. The old branched fill still
induces an isomorphism on twisted homology because its core
$-y^3$ remains distinct from one. For $y=1$ the character $(-1,1)$
extends over the meridional filling of $U$, whose core has
character $-1$ and whose torus is acyclic. For $y=\pm i$, the
characters $(-1,y)$ extend over the $+2$ filling because
$y^2(-1)^3=1$. Their core character is $y$ or $y^{-1}$, again
distinct from one. The ordinary twofold cover of the lens
filling exterior has the sign local system of the lifted
knot $K_1$, and induction splits it into exactly these two
characters on the downstairs exterior. Finally the sign
homology of a knot exterior over a field of odd
characteristic agrees in degree one with the homology of
its double branched cover: the invariant part descends to
the $S^3$ quotient, and the boundary torus and solid torus have
trivial deck action on homology. This gives
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 H_1(\Sigma_2(S_0);k)&\simeq A\otimes k_{-1},\\
 H_1(\Sigma_2(K_1);k)&\simeq (A\otimes k_i)\oplus(A\otimes k_{-i})
       \qquad(i^2=-1\text{ in }k).
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second equation can use a finite extension of the given
odd residue field and preserves its dimension test.

## The degree-four symmetry and the infinite-group consequence

There is a topological check on the elementary divisors of
the second group. In the native second orbifold group $G_1$,
the index-two subgroup comes from the ordinary lens cover of
the underlying $\mathbb{RP}^3$. It is the orbifold group of
$(S^3,K_1;2)$. Its deep subgroup
$\pi_1(\Sigma_2(K_1))$ is characteristic there because the
branched character is its unique index-two character.
It is therefore normal in $G_1$. The resulting order-four
quotient is $C_4$, since $G_1^{\rm ab}=C_4$. Its generator
$D$ acts on the smooth double branched cover by an
orientation-preserving deck transformation, and $D^2$ is the
branch involution. Over odd primary coefficients the invariant
part for this involution descends to $S^3$ and is zero in
degree one. For example choose an admissible equivariant
triangulation and apply averaging to identify invariant and
coinvariant chains, the latter being the quotient chains.
Because the group order is invertible at odd primes, this
also identifies their invariant homology. Hence $D^2=-1$
on the finite odd homology group. The $C_4$ action preserves
its nonsingular torsion linking form.

It follows in general for this marked $+2$ tower that its
finite homology is $B\oplus B$ as an abelian group. At a
prime $p\equiv3\pmod4$, use the action of $D$ to make its
primary group a module over $\mathbb Z_p[i]$, the unramified
quadratic discrete valuation ring. Each cyclic
$\mathbb Z_p[i]/(p^k)$ summand is two equal cyclic summands
over $\mathbb Z_p$. For $p\equiv1\pmod4$, the action splits
into eigensummands with eigenvalues $\pm i$ over $\mathbb Z_p$.
The linking pairing vanishes within either eigensummand
because $i^2=-1$ and two is invertible. Its cross pairing
is perfect, so the two summands are dual and have the same
elementary divisors. Odd order is automatic for a double
branched cover of a knot in $S^3$. At the actual prime
seven this symmetry already turns order $49$ into $C_7^2$
without using the Fox ranks.

The smooth closed oriented three-manifold $\Sigma_2(K_1)$
has infinite fundamental group. If its group were finite,
elliptization would give a free orientation-preserving
linear action on the round $S^3$. An odd Sylow subgroup of
such a group lifts isomorphically through
$SU(2)\times SU(2)\to SO(4)$ by taking an odd Sylow of its
preimage. Each factor projection is a cyclic odd
$p$-group: a nontrivial central element of order $p$ in an
odd $p$-group in $SU(2)$ has centralizer a circle. Thus the
lifted Sylow is abelian. A finite odd abelian group has
real orthogonal irreducible representations of dimension
one or two, and here freeness forces every nontrivial
rotation-plane character to be injective. Such a group
is cyclic. The image of a Sylow subgroup surjects onto
the corresponding primary part of the abelianization,
which therefore cannot be $C_7^2$. This proves infinitude
of the smooth branch cover group and hence of $G_1$.
For $s=0$, the order-six odd-dihedral candidate requires
the branched cover fundamental group itself to be cyclic.
The order-three homology does not settle that condition.
