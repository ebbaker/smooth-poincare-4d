# E89 · The standard cable meridian and Teragaito’s classification

27 November 2026. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI assistance. [Frozen E89 request](../research-progress/handoffs/E89_REQUEST_R1.md), baseline 72068cef1973d23aee808fe66476c44ed4b99cc5. Bounded Sol/ultra requested. Actual serving settings are unavailable. The marked input is [E84, §§1–3](../notes/E84_ODD_INTERMEDIATE.md). All four-manifolds and knots in the calculation are smooth. Orientations on the meridians are chosen for the presentations below.

Teragaito’s standard 2-cable about a lens-space twist spin has central fourth meridian power. The same property holds for a connected sum of two of the listed single-lens knots. The actual odd reverse meridian has noncentral fourth power. This excludes every exterior diffeomorphism to the listed knots, including one that changes fibrations. An independent choice of section of a standard cable’s capped bundle gives the same discrepancy and shows that the knot-classification clause on p. 61 fails under its unrestricted printed hypotheses. The smooth ambient type is still open in this comparison.

## 1. The exact pattern and its meridian

Use Teragaito’s definition in §4. Let \(K_0\subset S^4\) be the unknotted \(2\)-sphere with exterior \(S^1_d\times D^3\). In \(D^3\subset\mathbb C\times\mathbb R\), choose a small positive \(\epsilon\) and use the embedded circle
\[
\gamma(t)=(e^{2it},\epsilon e^{it},0),\qquad t\in\mathbb R/(2\pi\mathbb Z).
\]
This represents \(d^2\). The pattern is \(K_0\) in \(S^4\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu\gamma\), identified with a companion tube \(S^2\times D^2\). The circle \(\gamma\) is unknotted in \(S^4\) by general position for an isotopy of circles in dimension four. Its complement has the required product type. The group of the pattern exterior
\[
P=(S^1_d\times D^3)\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu\gamma
\]
is still \(\langle d\rangle\cong\mathbb Z\). Loops and disks between loops can be made disjoint from the removed circle by dimension counting. The inclusion from \(\partial\nu K_0\) sends a positive meridian to \(d\). The inclusion from \(\partial\nu\gamma\) sends the product generator to \(d^2\), since its push-off represents \(\gamma\) before removal. The two normal framings of \(\gamma\) give the same group maps. [Teragaito, *Fibered 2-knots and lens spaces*, §4, pp. 59–60](https://doi.org/10.18910/5691) · [Kanenobu, *Higher dimensional cable knots and their finite cyclic covering spaces*](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-8641(85)90065-3)

For the standard companion \(J\) take a \(2\)-twist spin of a \(2\)-bridge knot with lens cover \(L(p,q)\), \(p\) odd. With \(m\) the companion meridian and \(x\) a generator of \(C_p\), its group is
\[
\langle x,m\mid x^p=1,\ m x m^{-1}=x^{-1}\rangle.
\]
The return on the capped fibre is the double-cover deck map. Its action on \(C_p\) has square one, and its coinvariants are zero since the exterior has \(H_1=\mathbb Z\) generated by \(m\). Thus if the action is multiplication by \(a\), \(a-1\) is a unit modulo \(p\), and \((a-1)(a+1)=0\) forces \(a=-1\). [Zeeman, *Twisting spun knots*, §6, Main Theorem (1)–(2)](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9947-1965-0195085-8) · [Teragaito, §§2–3](https://doi.org/10.18910/5691)

In gluing \(P\) to the companion exterior, \(m=d^{\pm2}\) up to paths to basepoints. Conjugation by either sign of \(m\) inverts \(x\). Van Kampen gives the group with its cable meridian:
\[
\bigl(\langle x,d\mid x^p=1,\ d^2xd^{-2}=x^{-1}\rangle,[d]\bigr).
\]
Here \([d]\) denotes a conjugacy class, with inversion allowed upon reversing the meridian. Putting \(y=d x^{-1}d^{-1}\) exhibits the degree-zero subgroup as \(C_p*C_p\) and gives the based action
\[
dxd^{-1}=y^{-1},\qquad dyd^{-1}=x .
\]
In particular \(d^4\) is central. The two positive orders of a reversed or a mirrored peripheral generator give the same centrality. For a connected sum of two single-lens twist spins, van Kampen uses the common meridional \(\mathbb Z\) in the two exterior groups. Each lens generator is inverted by the common meridian and the square of this meridian is central. Fibre prime decomposition permits only a single 2-cable or a sum of two single-lens knots for two nontrivial lens factors. Adding trivial \(S^3\) factors has no effect. [Teragaito, §4, definition of types \(L\) and \(L^*\) and Theorem, p. 61](https://doi.org/10.18910/5691)

## 2. The actual odd marking

In E84 the orbit quotient is \(G_1=\langle q,c\mid q^4=c^2=(cq^2)^7=1\rangle\). Put \(x=cq^2\) and \(y=qxq^{-1}\). The quotient has \(qxq^{-1}=y\) and \(qyq^{-1}=x^{-1}\), and the kernel of its degree map to \(C_4\) is \(C_7*C_7\). In the pullback total group use the degree-one element \(d=(q^{-1},1)\). It acts by \(x\mapsto y^{-1}\), \(y\mapsto x\), so \(d^4\) is central. The oriented reverse section projects to \(qc=yq^{-1}\), making its degree-one peripheral representative \(\delta=yd\). Multiplication in the semidirect product now gives
\[
\delta^4
=y(dyd^{-1})(d^2yd^{-2})(d^3yd^{-3})d^4
=yx y^{-1}x^{-1}d^4 .
\]
The commutator is a nontrivial reduced word in \(C_7*C_7\), whose center is trivial. Thus \(\delta^4\) is noncentral in the total group. Passing from the capped bundle to the exterior of the actual section preserves the group by codimension-three general position, and the peripheral generator maps to the section class. [E84, §§1 and 3](../notes/E84_ODD_INTERMEDIATE.md)

Any diffeomorphism of compact exteriors induces an isomorphism on the fundamental group of their \(S^2\times S^1\) boundaries. It sends the generator to the positive or negative generator. By inclusions it sends the peripheral element to a conjugate of the target meridian or its inverse. Centrality of the fourth power is invariant under all these operations. This rules out a change of fibration as a way of identifying the two knot exteriors. The unmarked groups are isomorphic in the cable case, because replacing \(\delta\) by \(d=y^{-1}\delta\) gives the cable presentation. Isomorphism of the unmarked groups does not preserve the peripheral element. [Hillman, *Four-Manifolds, Geometries and Knots*, §14.7, Theorem 14.1 and the following paragraph, pp. 275–276](https://www.kurims.kyoto-u.ac.jp/EMIS/journals/GT/ftp/main/m5/m5-part3.pdf)

## 3. The printed hypotheses and an independent test

Teragaito’s definition of cyclic monodromy on p. 58 requires a permutation of actual punctured lens pieces \(L_i^\circ\). Lemma 2, pp. 60–61, assumes cyclic monodromy. If the return interchanges actual punctured lens pieces in a decomposition of the punctured two-summand fibre, their complement is a simply connected three-holed \(3\)-sphere. Choose paths from the fixed outer boundary basepoint to the pieces within this complement. The return then interchanges the actual based factor subgroups. For lens orders seven in the homology-sphere setting, the composite on a factor is inversion by the linking-pairing and coinvariant calculation in Lemma 2. Its fourth power on the free product is the identity. The infinite order of \(\operatorname{Ad}_{\delta}\) on \(C_7*C_7\), shown by the commutator above, excludes such a cyclic representative for the actual section. Permuting conjugacy classes of factors in the capped fibre is strictly weaker. [Teragaito, §2 and Lemma 2](https://doi.org/10.18910/5691)

The displayed Theorem on p. 61 quantifies over \(M^\circ\)-fibred knots in a homology four-sphere. The convention on p. 57 requires odd lens orders and imposes no restriction on the section or its framing. The sentence immediately preceding the theorem assumes that the return preserves a particular disk sum. The §2 reduction from prime uniqueness does not derive this claim for arbitrary relative-boundary returns. Even when the capped monodromy is represented by a finite-order permutation, moving the puncture around a fibre loop adds an inner automorphism to the based return. The exact hypotheses of Lemma 2 would exclude the current marking if taken as implicit restrictions on the final theorem. McCullough’s separate decomposition theorem likewise includes slide maps as additional generators alongside permutations and maps that preserve summands. [Teragaito, pp. 57–58, 60–61](https://doi.org/10.18910/5691) · [McCullough, *Mappings of reducible 3-manifolds*, §2, pp. 68–70](https://math.ou.edu/~dmccullough/research/pdffiles/reducible.pdf)

One can test the unrestricted knot conclusion without any of the E70 or E84 surgery inputs. Take the capped bundle \(W\) of the standard \(2\)-cable about the \(2\)-twist spin of \(K(7,1)\). Its fibre is \(L(7,1)\#L(7,1)\), by the cable proposition at the start of Teragaito’s §4. The capped group and its degree-one meridian \(d\) have the presentation of §1 above. Choose the new degree-one section \(\sigma=yd\) by tracing a based loop representing \(y\) in the fibre during one turn of the base. The projection of this section is the identity, so it is embedded even if the fibre loop is not embedded. Its oriented vertical normal bundle over the base circle is trivial. Removing a fibrewise ball bundle about \(\sigma\) and gluing \(D^2\times S^2\) with either framing produces a smooth knot whose exterior still fibres over \(S^1\), with closed fibre \(L(7,1)\#L(7,1)\) after capping and meridian \(\sigma\). Killing \(\sigma\) substitutes \(d=y^{-1}\). The relations \(dxd^{-1}=y^{-1}\) and \(dyd^{-1}=x\) then give \(xy=1\) and \(y=x\). Together with \(x^7=1\) these kill the whole group. The resulting closed oriented manifold has Euler characteristic two, since the closed bundle has Euler characteristic zero and circle surgery adds two. Simple connectedness and duality give the integral homology of \(S^4\), and the Hurewicz and Whitehead theorems give its homotopy type. As in §2, \(\sigma^4=[y,x]d^4\) is noncentral. Neither framing gives a knot of the listed types or a sum of such knots with this fibre. [Teragaito, §4, Proposition, pp. 59–60](https://doi.org/10.18910/5691)

The 1989 addendum repairs Lemma 1 for one lens space. Its key section step uses \(\alpha^it\sim t\) under conjugation in \(C_p\rtimes_{-1}\mathbb Z\), by invertibility of \(2\) modulo odd \(p\). The preceding construction gives two weight-one section classes in the two-summand group that cannot even be related by a group automorphism. The addendum does not address Lemma 2 or the two-summand extension. A bounded literature search found no further correction addressing this issue. The independent construction tests the knot conclusion only, and gives no distinction between the two ambient smooth types in the odd surgery problem. [Teragaito, *Addendum to Fibered 2-knots and lens spaces*, p. 953](https://doi.org/10.18910/12904)
