The two native ordinary-core orbifold groups cannot both be cyclic by this solid-torus surgery comparison. The separate [E66 Alexander parity test](E66_ALEXANDER_REPLAY.md) is stronger and excludes cyclicity of the first group alone. The braid calculation here checks a different geometric restriction on the marked link and leaves the mixed odd-dihedral and cyclic recognition route open.

## The marked link determinant

Write \(S_0\cup U\subset S^3\) for the old singular knot and the surgery dual unknot in the \(s=0\) ordinary filling. The oriented surgery \(+2\) on \(U\) is the \(s=1\) ordinary filling, with old knot \(S_1\subset\mathbb{RP}^3\). By E54 the preferred meridian and longitude of \(U\) are \(a,-3a-\mu\), the link has linking magnitude three, and in the common ordinary exterior \(Z\) of the two knots one has
\[
 H_1(Z)=\langle a,\mu,\sigma\mid3a+\mu-3\sigma=0\rangle ,
\]
with \(\sigma\) the old singular meridian.

On \(Z\), the character for the double branch over \(S_0\cup U\) sends \(a,\sigma\) to \(1\) and \(\mu\) to \(0\). It is therefore the same character as that of the original free \(\Lambda^+\) subgroup in the unfilled orbifold, after deleting \(\ell\) and the original singular locus. Filling over the lift of \(\sigma\) with branching gives the exterior \(E'\) of the single degree-two lift of \(\ell\) in \(Y_+=S^3/(T^*\times C_{17})\). On its boundary set \(\widetilde a=2a\); the actual cover lattice is generated by \(\widetilde a,\mu\). Filling along \(\mu\) gives \(Y_+\), while filling along \(\widetilde a\) gives \(\Sigma_2(S_0\cup U)\), with meridian degree two at the new core after the latter filling.

In \(H_1(E')\), the meridian \(\mu\) generates the infinite cyclic kernel of the filling to \(H_1(Y_+)=C_3\times C_{17}\). This follows from the pair sequence since the closed filled manifold is a rational homology sphere. The image of \(\widetilde a\) is the class of \(J^{-2}\) in \(H_1(Y_+)\); it has order three, as its right factor is central and its left projection in \(T^*/Q_8\) generates \(C_3\). Independently, the full 68-circle link upstairs is still one orbit under \(\Lambda^+\): the full stabilizer of the reference circle contains the odd element \(J\). Each upstairs meridian pushes to \(\mu\), and the signed row sum of E48 is \(16+3(6-11)=1\). The upstairs zero longitude therefore pushes to \(\mu\). In the boundary lattice it pushes to \(6\widetilde a+5\mu\). Thus \(6\widetilde a=-4\mu\), and \(3\widetilde a=-2\mu\) because \(3\widetilde a\) lies in the torsion-free meridian kernel.

The order of a finite quotient of a rank-one abelian group by a single element is the torsion order times the magnitude of the element's free coordinate. Comparing these two fillings gives
\[
 \left|H_1\bigl(\Sigma_2(S_0\cup U)\bigr)\right|
 =\left|H_1(Y_+)\right|\frac{2}{3}
 =34.
\]
This is the determinant of the exact marked link in the \(S^3\) filling, with branching over both components.

## What the two cyclic assumptions would give

If \(G_0\) is cyclic, E61 gives \(G_0=C_2\) and \(\Sigma_2(S_0)\) simply connected, hence standard \(S^3\) in dimension three. Its smooth involution linearizes by Dinkelbach–Leeb, and the branch circle of a nontrivial orientation-preserving involution with fixed points on round \(S^3\) is an unknot. Thus \(S_0\) is an unknot and its exterior \(V=S^3\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu S_0\) is the standard solid torus in this marked \(S^3\).

If \(G_1\) is cyclic, it is \(C_4\). Its orbifold universal cover is the smooth \(S^3\) supplied by the E61 covering tower, with local order-two isotropy surviving in the deck group. The same linearization theorem applies. An order-four generator acts by angles \(\pi,\ \pm\pi/2\) in orthogonal planes: its square fixes one circle and it has no fixed point itself. After removing an invariant tube of that fixed axis, the quotient is a disk mapping torus with rotation monodromy, so the exterior of \(S_1\) is a solid torus. These deductions use smooth finite actions on an elliptic three-manifold. [Dinkelbach–Leeb, Theorem E, published paper pp. 1129 and 1165–1166](https://msp.org/gt/2009/13-2/gt-v13-n2-p15-p.pdf)

The knot \(U\subset V\) has absolute winding three. The \(+2\) slope on its inner boundary is nonmeridional on the actual \(U\), and surgery in \(V\) along it gives the exterior of \(S_1\), a solid torus under the second assumption. Thus this is a nonmeridional solid-torus filling on the **inner** boundary of a winding-three knot exterior. The solid-torus surgery theorem of Gabai gives a \(0\)- or \(1\)-bridge braid. The nonzero wrapping hypothesis in the published restatement applies because the algebraic winding has magnitude three. A knot localized in a ball would have winding zero. The theorem used has target a solid torus itself. Cable alternatives in broader boundary-reducible-surgery statements with a nontrivial connected summand do not enlarge this case. The theorem permits the genuine torus-curve case. [Gabai, *Surgery on knots in solid tori*, Topology 28 (1989), Theorem 1.1](https://doi.org/10.1016/0040-9383(89)90028-1) · [Wang, *Cyclic surgery on satellite knots*, Theorem 3](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/686C4102D5005076CCC1F8F311E749C3/S0017089500008144a.pdf/cyclic-surgery-on-satellite-knots.pdf) · [Hom–Lidman–Vafaee, p. 3746 and Figure 1](https://msp.org/agt/2014/14-6/agt-v14-n6-p21-s.pdf)

For the original proposed reduction, the \(1\)-bridge normal word is \((\sigma_b\cdots\sigma_1)(\sigma_{w-1}\cdots\sigma_1)^t\), with \(1\le b\le w-2\) in the genuinely nonboundary-parallel case. Here \(w=3\), so \(b=1\). Its permutation has odd sign for all \(t\), since its exponent sum is \(2t+1\). An oriented single-component closed three-braid needs a three-cycle, with even sign. Thus the genuine \(1\)-bridge case is impossible. The boundary-parallel case is a \((3,q)\) curve measured in the **actual** preferred disk framing of \(V\), regardless of the possible disk twist used to normalize general braid parameters. Since \(U\) itself is an unknot in the ambient \(S^3\), the ordinary torus knot has \(q=1\) or \(q=-1\). Its braid matrix at \(-1\) has trace one, giving determinant six with its axis as below. This check does not identify a twist normalization with the actual surgery slope. [Gabai, *1-bridge braids in solid tori*, Topology Appl. 37 (1990)](https://doi.org/10.1016/0166-8641(90)90021-S) · [Wu, §1 and Corollary 2.7](https://homepage.divms.uiowa.edu/~wu/papers/p42.pdf) · [Hom–Lidman–Vafaee, pp. 3746–3747 and footnote 2](https://msp.org/agt/2014/14-6/agt-v14-n6-p21-s.pdf)

## A stronger three-braid determinant test

There is a determinant contradiction even without narrowing the winding-three braid to a torus curve. Suppose any closed three-braid \(K=\widehat\beta\) in this standard solid torus is an unknot, with axis \(A\). Let \(B\) be its reduced Burau matrix at \(t=-1\). It has determinant one, since each generator has determinant \(-t=1\) there. The axis formula and Torres specialization give, up to units,
\[
 \Delta_{K\cup A}(t,x)=\det(I-x B(t)),\qquad
 \Delta_K(t)=\frac{\det(I-B(t))}{1+t+t^2}.
\]
The link determinant is \(2|\Delta_{K\cup A}(-1,-1)|\) in this two-variable normalization. Since \(K\) is an unknot, \(|2-\operatorname{tr}B|=|\det(I-B)|=1\). Thus \(\operatorname{tr}B=1\) or \(3\), and the determinant of \(K\cup A\) is \(2|\det(I+B)|=6\) or \(10\). For \(\beta=(\sigma_1\sigma_2)^{\pm1}\), the two standard matrices at \(-1\) are positive Dehn twists on two transverse page curves, with product of trace one and determinant six for the link. [Morton, *The multivariable Alexander polynomial for a closed braid*, introduction and Theorem 1 with its Torres application](https://www.liverpool.ac.uk/~su14/papers/multiburau.pdf)

The factor two can also be checked directly by covering pages. The double cover of a disk page branched at three points is a once-punctured torus \(F\). The lift of the braid that fixes the boundary induces \(B\) on \(H_1(F)\), as adjacent half twists lift to Dehn twists on spanning curves. The page deck involution acts by \(-I\) on \(H_1(F)\), for instance by following those two spanning curves. For branching over \(K\) alone the monodromy is the boundary-fixing lift and the axis-meridian fill has base degree one. For branching over both \(K\) and \(A\) the parity on the axis meridian switches that lift, giving monodromy \(-B\) on \(H_1(F)\), and the primitive lifted filling meridian has base degree two. In either mapping torus the first homology splits as one base copy of \(\mathbb Z\) and the monodromy cokernel. Filling a slope of base degree \(d\) multiplies the finite cokernel order by \(|d|\), independently of its torsion component. This recovers the knot and link determinant factors directly.

Either computation contradicts the actual determinant \(34\). In particular the marked link cannot be an axis and unknotted three-braid pair. Under the two cyclic hypotheses the required solid-torus surgery would put it in that class. The argument therefore excludes simultaneous cyclicity and, equivalently here, the combination of an unknotted \(S_0\) and a solid-torus exterior of \(S_1\). It does not place \(U\) in a solid torus when \(S_0\) is a nontrivial knot with odd-dihedral orbifold group, so it leaves that mixed recognition case open.

For a different conditional restriction, suppose a future marked calculation places \(S_0\) itself as a closed three-braid about the actual unknot \(U\). Its Burau matrix at \(-1\) would satisfy \(|2+\operatorname{tr}B|=17\), forcing \(\operatorname{tr}B=15\) or \(-19\) and \(\det S_0=13\) or \(21\). If the first orbifold group were odd-dihedral under that additional braid premise, its rotation subgroup would have that order. The present link comparison supplies neither the braid placement with \(U\) as axis nor the assertion that the first branched cover has cyclic fundamental group.
