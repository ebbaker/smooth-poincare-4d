The E62 crossing, cone labels and native slopes replay. The two smooth filled covers have \(H_1=C_{34},C_{68}\), with self-linking of specified cyclic generators \(3/34,-11/68\), respectively. Their fundamental groups cannot both be cyclic by cyclic surgery on the actual lifted exterior. These covers branch over both the old singular knot and the new dual curve. ## Exact diagram and branching

Put \(M^+=S^3/(T^*\times C_{17})\). The image \(K\) of the chosen great circle \(p_0e^{jx}\), \(p_0jp_0^{-1}=n=(1,1,1)/\sqrt3\), is connected, and maps two to one to the marked regular knot in the unfilled orbifold. In fact its stabilizer in the free even subgroup is \(\langle J^2\rangle\) of order six. Since \(J^2=(b^2,-1)=(b^{-1},1)\) in the central product and \(b=e^{n\pi/3}\), the positive parameter step \(\pi/3\) has deck word \((b,1)\). It represents the generator of the \(C_3\) summand of \(H_1(M^+)\), with zero \(C_{17}\) component.

For the Hopf starting point \(x_0=(1,-1,0)/\sqrt2\), differentiation of \(x(x)=\cos(2x)x_0+\sin(2x)(n\times x_0)\) gives at the second crossing preimage \(x=\pi/6\) the point \((1,0,-1)/\sqrt2\) and unit tangent \((-1,2,-1)/\sqrt6\). Applying \(\operatorname{Ad}_{kb}\) brings it to \(x_0\) with tangent \(t_1=(1,1,2)/\sqrt6\). The first tangent is \(t_0=(1,1,-2)/\sqrt6\). Their determinant in the oriented base is \(x_0\cdot(t_0\times t_1)=2\sqrt2/3\). No other intersections occur because an even sign change preserves the equator condition only at its zero-coordinate points, which form this single orbit. In the two octant charts the two lobes surround the different multiplicity-three points, with orientations negative and positive. The multiplicity-two point is in the remaining region. The folded edges have angular side \(\pi/3\), so the unsigned area per lobe is \(\Omega=\arccos(1/3)-\pi/3\).

The regular period is \(P=\pi/17\). With \(q_1=p_0e^{j\pi/6}\), multiplication by \(-kb\) gives \(q_1\mapsto p_0e^{i\vartheta}\), \(\vartheta=\arccos(1/\sqrt3)\). This follows from \(p_0^{-1}kp_0=j/\sqrt3+\sqrt{2/3}\,k\) and \(bq_1=p_0j\). The second height is therefore \(h=\vartheta-5P\in(0,P/2)\). Curvature \(-\omega/2\) of the unit-angle connection and the signed angular areas give the two horizontal phases \(\pm(\pi/3-\Omega/2)=\pm\vartheta\), with the unwrapped lifts determined by \(i^{-1}bi=-kb\) and \(k^{-1}bk=-jb\). In particular no full turn is missing when the phases are reduced modulo \(P\).

Use \(A=2a\) for the primitive longitude in the even boundary lattice and \(\mu\) for its positive meridian. In E39 normal coordinates \(A\) advances the normal angle by \(-5\pi/3\) while the right Hopf push advances it by \(+\pi/3\) over the same interval. Hence \(\lambda_{\mathrm H}=A+\mu\). The downstairs marked slope \(\eta_s=a+s\mu\) has parity one, so its first power does not lift to the closed boundary torus upstairs. The primitive slope upstairs is
\[
 A+2s\mu=\lambda_{\mathrm H}+(2s-1)\mu .
\]
In meridian–longitude coordinates the covering of the attached solid tori is \((w,\theta)\mapsto(w^2,\theta)\), after choosing a longitude with trivial character. Thus \(Y_s=(M^+\setminus\nu K)(A+2s\mu)\) branches also over the new dual curve. The corresponding downstairs orbifold is \(Q_s^{(2)}\), with orders two on both branch curves. In particular \(Y_0\) is the double branched cover of the actual two-component link \(S_0\cup U\) in \(S^3\), with \(U\) the new dual unknot. \(Y_1\) double branches over both curves in the actual underlying \(\mathbb{RP}^3\). The single-knot orbifold \(Q_s\) from E54 has regular new core.

## Removing the cone fibres before ordinary surgery

One can keep all cone attachments in an ordinary diagram as follows. Remove disjoint disks around the three cone points, away from the immersed curve, to obtain a pair of pants. Trivialize its oriented circle bundle, write \(f\) for the positive fibre, and let \(d_i\) be section loops in the positive orientations around the removed disks. The local diagonal cone action has fractions \(\beta_i/\alpha_i=1/2,2/3,2/3\), at \(2,+,-\) respectively. In a radial trivialization around each cone disk, the section lift advances the reduced local fibre by \(\beta_i/\alpha_i+n_i\) turns for an integer \(n_i\). Its boundary connection integral is this number plus the disk curvature integral. Applying Stokes to the complement, whose oriented boundaries are the negatives of the disk boundaries, gives
\[
 -17/6=\int_{S^2/T}F=-\sum_i(\beta_i/\alpha_i+n_i),\qquad
 n_2+n_++n_-=1 .
\]
A change of section can redistribute the integers with fixed sum. Choose \((n_2,n_+,n_-)=(1,0,0)\). The three meridians of the original fibred solid tori are now \(2d_2-3f, 3d_+-2f, 3d_--2f\). This follows by going \(\alpha_i\) times around a section boundary circle and subtracting its net fibre degree.

On each positively oriented multiplicity-three lobe the section integral is \(2/3-\Omega/(2P)=h/P\), with the opposite sign on a negatively oriented lobe. The horizontal knot heights in this product chart thus have actual increments \(+h/P,-h/P\), matching at the single crossing. Fix the section value to be \(p_0\) at the crossing. On each open lobe, a real-valued gauge change supported away from the crossing can move its intermediate section heights into an interval containing the two crossing heights and of length less than one full turn. Indeed the real lifted height closes at the prescribed height on both ends of each arc. The two supports have disjoint tubular neighborhoods on the base. Applying the change of section on those neighborhoods extends it without changing the cone meridians.

Replace the cone tori temporarily by ordinary product tori with meridians \(d_i\). The ambient becomes \(S^2\times S^1\), with the three ordinary core fibres marked. The knot is in a ball in a product height chart and has a single negative crossing there, since \(t_1\) is over \(t_0\) and \(\det(t_1,t_0)\) in the oriented angular plane is negative. Its unframed one-crossing diagram in the ball is an unknot. The vertical push gives the diagram framing. For example, pushing finitely less than the height difference gives the two mixed projected crossings of the same negative sign, hence linking \(-1\) in the ball. Thus the knot's surgery coefficients are \(-2,0\) in the ball's zero framing, with the three vertical fibres retained and their surgery coefficients \(-2/3,-3/2,-3/2\) relative to product meridian and longitude. The diagram winds with opposite signs around the two multiplicity-three cores and has zero winding around the third. One can represent the product ambient by zero surgery on an unknot whose meridians are those three fibres. At the level of the rational linking matrix, eliminating the three cone coefficients leaves diagonal entries \(17/6\) and \(n+4/3\) for the original product surgery and the marked knot surgery \(n=-2,0\). The off-diagonal cancels because the windings are opposite. Multiplying the determinant by the denominators of the original rational surgery coefficients gives the orders \(34,68\) found independently below.

The one-crossing reduction in the ball alone allows isotopies through the other three components. It does not simplify the actual surgery link to a Seifert-fibre surgery. Turaev's ordinary-circle-bundle shadow projection requires a bundle over a smooth oriented surface, so it cannot directly treat the cone-labelled projection as an ordinary shadow with just the crossing. The deleted cones instead give the explicit three attachments above. Turaev's link diagrams on genuine oriented spines and surgery on shadowed polyhedra keep the framing and attaching data. No sequence of shadow or Kirby moves identifying either \(Y_s\) is concluded here. [Turaev, *Shadow links and face models of statistical mechanics*, scope of the circle-bundle construction](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214448442) · [Turaev, *Quantum Invariants of Knots and 3-Manifolds*, VIII §4 and IX §3](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/turaev5.pdf)

## Integral homology, linking and the orbifold group test

Put \(X=M^+\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu K\). The meridian exact sequence is \(0\to\mathbb Z\mu\to H_1(X)\to C_{51}\to0\). The E48 row-sum identity pushes \(\lambda_C\) to \(\mu\) in \(X\), because every meridian of the link in \(S^3\) pushes to the same oriented \(\mu\). The boundary coordinates give \(\lambda_C=6A+5\mu\). Since \(A\) maps to an element of order three, \(3A\) already lies in the meridian kernel. Thus \(3A=-2\mu\) and \(\lambda=3A+2\mu\) is primitive and zero in \(H_1(X)\). With \(\gamma=A+\mu\), the pair \((\lambda,\gamma)\) is a positive basis of \(\partial X\), and \(\mu=-\lambda+3\gamma\).

For the torsion, rational linking with \(K\) defines a map \(H_1(X)\to\mathbb Q\) sending \(\mu\) to one. Its reduction modulo integers is the torsion linking in \(M^+\) against \([K]\) on \(C_{51}\). An element of the latter group lifts to a torsion class precisely when this reduction is zero, since one can then adjust its linking by an integer multiple of \(\mu\). On a rank-one group the kernel of the rational linking map is the torsion subgroup, so it is \(C_{17}\) by nonsingularity of the closed linking form and the order-three class of \(K\). The free class \(\gamma\) is primitive because \(\mu\) has free index three. The two surgeries kill
\[
 A+2s\mu=(1-2s)\lambda+(6s-2)\gamma .
\]
It follows that \(H_1(Y_s)=C_{17}\oplus C_{|6s-2|}\), with \(\gamma\) of order \(|6s-2|\). In particular the two-component branch link for \(s=0\) has determinant 34.

Linking on the \(C_{17}\) summand is unchanged by filling because a representative and its multiple's bounding chain can lie in \(X\). The right cyclic quotient \(S^3/C_{17}\) has generator linking \(-1/17\), since inversion of the quaternion sphere swaps it with the left scalar quotient and reverses orientation. The latter is the boundary of the complex disk bundle of Euler \(-17\), with pairing \(+1/17\). The degree-24 covering from the right cyclic quotient to \(M^+\) sends its generator to the right deck generator \(z\), and transfers \(z\) to 24 times the cyclic generator in the domain. This uses the projection formula for the linking pairing by lifting bounding chains. Hence the \(C_{17}\) self-linking is \(-24/17=-7/17\) in \(\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z\). The 2-primary pairing on \(\gamma\) is \(-(1-2s)/(6s-2)\) in the oriented boundary basis, by subtracting the integral bounding chain for \(\lambda\) from the meridian disk. As a sign check, the \(\mu\)-filling has order-three pairing \(+1/3\), also obtained by transfer from the left order-three subgroup of \(T^*\). Distinct primary summands are orthogonal. Thus the self-linking of the generator \(\gamma+z\) is \(3/34\) or \(-11/68\) as stated. These pairings do not by themselves identify the filled manifolds as lens spaces.

The odd deck involution fixes \(\gamma\) on homology because on the peripheral torus it is the half-translation. It sends \(z\) to \(z^{-1}\) by conjugation with the odd right factor. Write \(G_X=\pi_1^{\mathrm{orb}}(O\setminus\nu\ell)\). Then
\[
  G_s^{(2)}=G_X/\langle\!\langle\eta_s^2\rangle\!\rangle
       \cong \pi_1(Y_s)\rtimes C_2 .
\]
The extension splits by an actual old order-two meridian \(\sigma\) of odd parity. In the coinvariants of the abelianized kernel, the difference \(\eta_s-\sigma=-A+(s-1)\mu\) is \((3s-1)\gamma\), since E48 gives \([\sigma]=3a+\mu\). The \(C_{17}\) component is killed in coinvariants by inversion. Killing the odd new meridian itself produces \(G_s=\pi_1^{\mathrm{orb}}(Q_s)\), whose abelianization is \(C_2\) or \(C_4\). If \(\pi_1(Y_s)\) were cyclic the same calculation in the semidirect group would give \(G_s\) cyclic, since imposing \(\eta_s=1\) forces \(z=z^{-1}\). The reverse implication does not follow. The local old double cover character is destroyed when its odd new meridian is killed, so \(Y_s\) is not the smooth branched cover of the single remaining knot used in E54.

For a further ordinary check, the \(C_{17}\)-projection of the unfilled exterior extends to both \(Y_s\). Its 17-fold unbranched cover is obtained by filling the 17 lifted components in \(S^3/T^*\). The push of the E48 linking vector to distinct right-axis columns is \(4-6D_j-\delta_{j0}\), where \(D_j=1\) at cyclic distance at most five and zero elsewhere. Thus \(6A+5\mu\) gives this vector, and three times the columns of the filling slopes in the meridian lattice form \(N_s=(6s-3)I+2J_{17}-3D\). The meridian lattice has index three before filling. The new homology order is \(|\det N_s|/3^{16}\), which is \(2,4\), since \(2N_s\) is the E48 equal-row block. This covering check does not distinguish cyclicity of either original group.

## The two smooth branched cover groups cannot both be cyclic

The compact orientable exterior \(X\) is irreducible. A sphere in it bounds a ball in spherical \(M^+\), and the ball cannot contain \(K\) because \([K]\ne0\) in \(H_1(M^+)\). Its sole boundary component is a torus. If \(X\) were Seifert fibred and \(\mu\) were a fibre slope, its filling group would be the base orbifold group of a compact surface with boundary and cone points. Capping the boundary torus along \(\mu\) then kills the regular fibre in the Seifert exact sequence. The resulting group is a free product of cyclic cone groups and a free group, which cannot be finite noncyclic \(T^*\times C_{17}\). The orientable total space excludes reflector boundary in a Seifert manifold, because an orientation-reversing reflection on the base would have to reverse the local circle to preserve total orientation and then could not act freely. For every other meridian slope the attached solid torus extends the Seifert fibration and its core is a fibre. [Scott, Lemma 3.2 and fibred solid-torus description](https://sschleimer.warwick.ac.uk/Teach/2021_3MFDS/References/1983the_geometries_of_three-manifolds.pdf)

In any Seifert fibration of \(M^+\), the base is orientable because its orientation character otherwise gives a nontrivial homomorphism from \(H_1(M^+)=C_{51}\) to \(C_2\). The regular fibre subgroup is then central. If its order were prime to 17, the finite base orbifold group would have order divisible by 17 and would also have \(A_4\) as a quotient, since the center of \(T^*\times C_{17}\) is \(C_2\times C_{17}\). Finite oriented spherical two-orbifold groups with order divisible by 17 are cyclic or dihedral. This follows from the finite rotation group classification, with the closed bad orbifolds also giving cyclic groups. No cyclic or dihedral group has \(A_4\) as a quotient. Therefore the regular fibre subgroup projects nontrivially to \(C_{17}\). An exceptional core has a power equal to the regular fibre in its solid torus, so every fibre has nontrivial projection. The actual knot has deck word \(b\) with trivial \(C_{17}\) projection. This rules out the proposed Seifert exterior. [Scott, §2 spherical orbifolds and §4 spherical rotation groups](https://sschleimer.warwick.ac.uk/Teach/2021_3MFDS/References/1983the_geometries_of_three-manifolds.pdf)

The actual two slopes \(A,A+2\mu\) have geometric distance two. The Cyclic Surgery Theorem for a compact connected orientable irreducible non-Seifert exterior with torus boundary gives distance at most one between cyclic fillings. Hence at least one of \(\pi_1(Y_0),\pi_1(Y_1)\) is noncyclic, and at least one filled cover is not a lens space. This does not identify which one and does not decide either single-knot orbifold group \(G_s\), because quotienting by the odd new meridian can kill a nonabelian kernel. [Culler–Gordon–Luecke–Shalen, *Dehn surgery on knots*, Cyclic Surgery Theorem, p. 237](https://marc-culler.info/static/home/papers/CyclicSurgery.pdf#page=2)
