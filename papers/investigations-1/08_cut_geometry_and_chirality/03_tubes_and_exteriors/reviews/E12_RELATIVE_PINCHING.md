## 1. Relative test and normalization

Let \(X\) be compact, connected, smooth and oriented, with rational circle homology and connected umbilic boundary \(\Sigma\cong S^1\times S^2\). Let \(D\) be the oriented identity double after a conformal change of \(X\) making \(\Sigma\) totally geodesic. Put \(Y_D=Y(D,[g])>0\), \(e_X=(E_+(X)+E_-(X))/2\), and
\[
m_W=\max\{\lambda_{\max}(W^+),\lambda_{\max}(W^-)\},\qquad
K_D=36\int_D m_W^2\,dV.
\]
The eigenvalues refer to the self-adjoint Weyl blocks on the oriented Riemannian \(\Lambda^\pm\) with unit-sphere curvature operator the identity. Chen–Zhu Theorem 1.4, Eq. (1.5), applies to a smooth compact four-manifold with \(Y>0\) and \(K_D<Y_D^2\). Its \(1/36\) uses the eigenvalues themselves. Equation (1.9) computes the corresponding squared Weyl block norm as the sum of the three squared eigenvalues. The proof on pp. 823–824 uses the positive modified Yamabe gauge \(R-6m_W>0\) and the smooth positive-isotropic-curvature classification. The cylinder quotients in the conclusion are free quotients, as specified in the underlying Chen–Tang–Zhu Main Theorem. [Chen–Zhu, published CAG 22 (2014), pp. 814–816 and 823–825](https://intlpress.com/api/bgcloud-front/resource/pdf/volume/1805784609151475713-1805784609151475713-dfd4e7a2ab88de50d337d60e7620a3c8.pdf) · [Chen–Tang–Zhu, published JDG 91 (2012), pp. 41–44 and §5](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/21/926-JDG-91-1-a2-che.pdf).

Reflection exchanges chiral blocks and preserves \(m_W\), so \(E_\pm(D)=2e_X\) and \(K_D=72\int_X m_W^2\,dV\). All these integrals are conformally invariant. For any traceless symmetric three by three matrix \(B\), if \(b=\lambda_{\max}(B)\) then \(|B|^2_{\rm HS}\ge b^2+b^2/2\). Using the stronger pointwise maximum of the two block norms gives
\[
K_D\le48\int_X\max\{|W^+|_{\rm HS}^2,|W^-|_{\rm HS}^2\}\,dV
=96e_X-48\int_X\min\{|W^+|_{\rm HS}^2,|W^-|_{\rm HS}^2\}\,dV.
\]
Consequently \(96e_X<Y_D^2\) suffices for the spectral test. The integral of the minimum gives a refinement even for weak doubles, independently of the equality argument below. For example, if both blocks are nonzero on a common open set, the upper bound for \(K_D\) is strictly less than \(96e_X\). The ratio \(K_D/Y_D^2\) is unchanged under constant rescaling because the dimension is four.

## 2. Smoothing and attainment of the strict test

In signed Gaussian distance the totally geodesic gauge gives \(g=dt^2+h(|t|)\) with \(h'(0)=0\). The usual \(C^{1,1}\) description suffices. In this construction smoothness up to the boundary actually gives \(C^{2,1}\) in the reflected collar because the second derivatives match. Smooth approximations with a collar cutoff can be chosen to converge in \(C^1\) and in \(W^{2,p}\) for every finite \(p\), with bounded second derivatives. One may convolve the even family in the signed normal coordinate, then blend on the smooth part of the collar. There is no curvature measure supported on the seam. The scalar and Weyl tensors converge in \(L^2\). In oriented orthonormal frames obtained from the metrics, the \(\Lambda^\pm\) identifications converge uniformly. The maximal eigenvalue of a real symmetric block is Lipschitz in its operator norm, so \(m_W\) also converges in \(L^2\).

The Yamabe numerator difference has an upper bound of the form \(o(1)(\|\nabla u\|_2^2+\|u\|_4^2)\) by uniform coefficient convergence and Hölder applied to the scalar terms. The \(L^4\) denominators are uniformly equivalent with factor \(1+o(1)\). For normalized near minimizers, \(Q(u)\) is bounded above by evaluation on a fixed smooth test function. Uniform \(L^2\) bounds on scalar curvature then bound the gradient term by the same estimate. Comparing near minimizers in both directions gives convergence of Yamabe constants. Thus positive Yamabe sign and the strict inequality survive smoothing on the same smooth double. If two totally geodesic gauges on \(X\) are used, their conformal factor has zero normal derivative by the conformal second fundamental form formula. Its reflection gives a positive weak conformal rescaling, under which the weak doubled Yamabe form obeys conformal covariance by the same matched-trace integration by parts as in E03.

The smoothing argument transfers an open strict inequality. Even \(C^2\) convergence gives no direction of approach for the difference \(Y_D^2-K_D\) at equality. The original weak doubled metric generally remains below the smooth regularity assumed in Chen–Zhu Theorem 1.6. A regularity and nonnegative-isotropic-curvature argument would therefore be needed for extending the endpoint below to all such doubles.

## 3. Marked group and the unmarked double

Poincaré–Lefschetz duality gives \(H_2(X,\Sigma;\mathbb Q)\cong H^2(X;\mathbb Q)=0\) and \(H_1(X,\Sigma;\mathbb Q)\cong H^3(X;\mathbb Q)=0\). Thus \(H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Q)\to H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\) is an isomorphism. A nonzero power of the peripheral generator cannot map to the identity in \(G=\pi_1(X)\), so the boundary homomorphism is injective. Write \(H\cong\mathbb Z\) for its image. Mayer–Vietoris and orientable duality give \(b_1(D)=1\), \(\chi(D)=0\) and \(b_2(D)=0\). Van Kampen with the actual identity marking gives
\[
\pi_1(D)=G*_H G.
\]

Under the strict pinching, orientability of \(D\) excludes \(\mathbb{RP}^4\) from the Chen–Zhu decomposition. A smooth cylinder quotient has zero Euler characteristic since the product curvature has no line entries and thus has zero Euler density. The connected sum of \(k\) such quotients with \(S^4\) has \(\chi=2-2k\), so \(k=1\). The cocompact discrete deck group in \(\operatorname{Isom}(\mathbb R)\times O(4)\) has discrete projection to the line isometry factor and finite kernel by compactness of \(O(4)\). The projected group is cocompact and contains the translations as an infinite cyclic subgroup of index at most two. A lift of a translation generator gives an infinite cyclic subgroup of finite index in the deck group.

If \(G\ne H\), choose \(a\in G\setminus H\). With subscripts for the two copies, \((a_1a_2^{-1})^n\) is reduced and its powers give distinct left cosets modulo \(H\) in \(G*_H G\). An infinite subgroup of a virtually infinite cyclic group has finite index, as seen by intersection with a finite-index infinite cyclic subgroup. This contradiction forces \(G=H\). Thus the boundary generator maps to a generator of both \(\pi_1(X)\) and \(\pi_1(D)\), up to orientation and base point. In particular a noncyclic \(G\) requires \(Y_D^2\le K_D\). Within \(e_X<8\pi^2/3\), it follows that \(Y_D<16\pi\). This test applies conditionally to the noncyclic groups of the two native disk exteriors. Their group data give neither a metric nor a lower bound on \(Y_D\). [Marked disk data](../../04_slide_filters/research-progress/handoffs/TO_TUBE_NATIVE_DISK_DOUBLE_20261127.md).

The \(\mathbb Z\) deck group of the recognized free cylinder quotient has trivial finite projection kernel and projects to translations, since a nontrivial line reflection has order two. Orientability then makes the spherical factor of its generator an element of the connected group \(SO(4)\). Its mapping torus is diffeomorphic to \(S^1\times S^3\) by untwisting this rotation along a path in \(SO(4)\). This diffeomorphism classifies the unmarked double.

## 4. Strict obstruction at the spectral endpoint for smooth doubles

Assume here that the reflected conformal class has a smooth representative. Also assume the peripheral generator normally generates \(G\), as for a homotopy-sphere filling. If \(G\ne H\) then \([G:H]\ge3\), since a subgroup of index two is normal and would leave a nontrivial meridian quotient. Choose two distinct nontrivial representatives of cosets \(aH,bH\) in the first copy and a fixed nontrivial coset representative in the second copy. Alternating \(n\) first-copy choices with the fixed second-copy representative yields \(2^n\) distinct reduced words by the amalgam normal form, each of word length \(O(n)\). Thus \(\pi_1(D)\) has exponential growth.

At \(Y_D^2=K_D\), Chen–Zhu Theorem 1.6 has three alternatives. The connected-sum class has the same virtually cyclic group when \(\chi=0\). The \(\mathbb{CP}^2\) alternative contradicts \(b_1=1\). In the remaining alternative the proof of Theorem 1.6 supplies a conformal metric on \(D\) whose lifted metric is isometric to a product of complete simply connected surfaces of constant Gaussian curvatures \(k_1,k_2\) with \(k_1+k_2>0\). The Euler density of the product is \(k_1k_2/(4\pi^2)\), as follows by taking the product of the two normalized curvature forms. Its integral on the compact quotient is zero, so the universal covering metric is \(S^2\times\mathbb R^2\), with a suitable round sphere radius. A free properly discontinuous isometric action on this product has at most quadratic growth: finite generators give a linear bound on orbit displacement, the orbit points are uniformly separated, and balls in the product have at most quadratic volume growth. This contradicts exponential growth. [Chen–Zhu Theorem 1.6 and proof, pp. 816 and 824–825](https://intlpress.com/api/bgcloud-front/resource/pdf/volume/1805784609151475713-1805784609151475713-dfd4e7a2ab88de50d337d60e7620a3c8.pdf).

It follows that a smooth reflected double with noncyclic \(G\) normally generated by the meridian satisfies the strict obstruction \(Y_D^2<K_D\le96e_X\). Under this smoothness and normal-generation hypothesis, the recognition test itself can be inclusive, \(K_D\le Y_D^2\). For arbitrary rational-circle exteriors the index-two case is possible. For weak reflected metrics the argument in §2 transfers only the strict recognition test.

## 5. A marked consequence for the two smooth fillings

Use \(G=H\) and \(D\cong S^1\times S^3\), as obtained under the strict test or the inclusive test with the additional hypotheses in §4. Let \(M_j\) be the closing by \(D^2\times S^2\) using the given product marking composed with \(j\) rotations of \(S^2\) along the boundary circle, \(j=0,1\) up to parity. Factor orientation changes extend over the cap. The generator \(\mu=S^1\times\{p\}\) in the actual seam is a smooth embedded generator circle in \(D\).

Choose its normal \(D^3\) in \(D\) so that the seam passes through the equatorial \(D^2\). The two smooth circle-surgery framings in \(D\) can be represented by zero or one full rotation about the normal axis to the equatorial disk, since \(\pi_1(SO(3))=\mathbb Z/2\) and a full rotation in the equatorial plane generates it. These attaching maps preserve each hemisphere of the normal sphere and commute with reflection across the equator. When \(S^1\times D^3\) is replaced by \(D^2\times S^2\), the two halves each receive one meridional two-handle \(D^2\times D^2\). Removing the old half-tube from either half and rounding its corners only changes the boundary collar. The compressed seam is the union of the solid tori \(S^1\times D^2\) and \(D^2\times S^1\). Their meridians intersect once for both parity choices, since the shear sends the second to a slope of the form \((1,j)\) and the first has slope \((0,1)\). Thus the compressed seam is \(S^3\).

Each half is \(M_j\) minus the interior of a four-ball. Indeed \(D^2\times S^2\) decomposes along the two hemispheres about \(p\) into the meridional two-handle already attached and a second \(D^2\times D^2\). The second piece attaches along its entire rounded boundary to complete \(M_j\). Reflection interchanges the corresponding halves of the surgered double.

A smooth homotopy between two embedded generator circles in dimension four can be perturbed rel endpoints to an isotopy. Immersion failure has codimension four in the two-dimensional parameter space, and coincidences at equal time have codimension four in the three-dimensional parameter space of distinct pairs and time. Parametric transversality avoids both, and isotopy extension makes the isotopy ambient. Thus in \(D\cong S^1\times S^3\) the marked \(\mu\) is ambient isotopic to the product circle, possibly after reversing the parameter. Both circle surgeries on the product circle give \(S^4\). The untwisted one is \(\partial(D^2\times D^3)\), and the other boundary rotation extends over the complementary \(S^1\times D^3\). Therefore, as smooth oriented connected sums,
\[
M_0\#\overline{M_0}\cong S^4,\qquad
M_1\#\overline{M_1}\cong S^4.
\]
Here the complement of each marked compressed three-sphere consists of two punctured copies with opposite orientation. Recognizing an individual \(M_j\) from this result requires smooth recognition of its actual punctured half as a standard four-ball, a smooth Schoenflies-type conclusion. The unmarked product classification alone supplies no such recognition.

The quotient \(G/\langle\!\langle\mu\rangle\!\rangle=1\) only checks the fundamental group of a closing. The two-twist-spun trefoil, with \(G=C_3\rtimes_{-1}\mathbb Z\), has this quotient and has a knotted core. Even the stronger \(G=\mathbb Z\) is a group criterion for topological locally flat unknotting, for which the smooth analogue is a separate question. The distinction is stated for smooth two-spheres in the introduction of the cited paper. [E04 topology check](E04_PARENT_AND_BOUND.md#6-smooth-action-and-topology) · [Daemi and Scaduto, *Chern–Simons functional, singular instantons, and the four-dimensional clasp number*, introduction, p. 5](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/31411).

## 6. Review of the residual question

The [marked exterior question](../brainstorm/notes/POSITIVE_ENERGY_IMPASSE.md) has the right topological scope. With integral circle homology and the peripheral normal-generation assumption, both framings close to homotopy four-spheres by van Kampen, Euler characteristic and duality. Conversely an actual normal sphere tube in a homotopy four-sphere has the indicated integral-homology exterior by Mayer–Vietoris, and van Kampen supplies normal generation. For the geometric hypothesis, it would help to state whether positive reflected double means a weak identity double in a totally geodesic gauge or a smooth representative of the reflected conformal class. Both support the strict test. The endpoint in §4 uses the latter.

The [early comparison](BEFORE_IMPASSE.md) accurately distinguishes the tested collapse from a general stability claim. In the odd-lens examples of E05 a fixed fibre metric yields \(e_X=c\ell>0\) and the constant Yamabe test gives \(Y_D=O(\sqrt\ell)\). The double group is \((C_m*C_m)\rtimes\mathbb Z\) with the simultaneous order-two automorphism on the two cyclic factors. A reduced product of nonidentity letters from opposite factors has infinite order and commutes with the square of the meridian. Its subgroup together with that square is \(\mathbb Z^2\). Thus the tests show that the absolute budget permits doubles far from the equality topology, even with actual homotopy-sphere fillings. Their fillings are recognized by the separate periodic-action construction. They give neither a counterexample to the residual question nor the relative hypothesis of §3.

The fixed-path shear obstruction in E05 depends on its constant lapse, zero shift, product puncture and invariant density for the Yamabe calculation. It does not apply to arbitrary scale-dependent metrics. For a meridian-dual section, the integral generator permits a smooth map to \(S^1\) with the marked boundary projection by a collar homotopy. A regular value then gives exactly the marked spherical boundary section, possibly with additional closed components. Its existence gives no product structure on the cut manifold or control of the return map. The still-useful improvements to the early impasse are therefore the smooth-double endpoint in §4 and the marked, doubled-filling conclusion in §5. A general positive-energy smooth closing remains outside those conclusions.
