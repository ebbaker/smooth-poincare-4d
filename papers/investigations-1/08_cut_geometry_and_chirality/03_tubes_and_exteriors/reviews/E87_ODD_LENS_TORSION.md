## The drilled tangle before the outer attachment

Let $P_{\rm in}$ denote the E70 ball with the two old
weight-two strands, after the ordinary fillings on its
$F$ and $L$ components. Use the native $s=1$ slope,
so the two attached meridians are
$F^3\lambda_F^{-2}$ and $\lambda_L$. Leave the outer
pillowcase boundary unfilled. The Wirtinger calculation
in E70 can be stopped at this point. Namely
$\lambda_L=b_1^{-1}F^{-1}=1$ forces $F=b_1$ because
$b_1^2=1$. With $q=\lambda_F$, the relation
$F^3=q^2$ becomes $F=q^2$, and $q^4=1$. Then
\[
 L=qc,\qquad b=cq^2c,\qquad
 b_{\rm top}=q^2,\qquad c_{\rm top}=q^2cq^2 .
\]
Conversely these substitutions solve the two
longitude equations, both longitude commutators, the
old meridian squares, and the $F$ filling relation
when $q^4=c^2=1$. For example $b_1=LbL^{-1}=q^2$,
$\lambda_F=c^{-1}b^{-1}L^{-1}=q$, and the boundary
pillowcase product $b_{\rm top}c_{\rm top}cb$ is one.
There is therefore no outer tangle meridian relation
at this stage, and
\[
 \pi_1^{\rm orb}(P_{\rm in})=\langle q,c\mid q^4=c^2=1\rangle
 =C_4*C_2.
\]
In the actual boundary translation basis of E70 the
same substitutions give
\[
 r=cq^2,\qquad d_2=b\,b_{\rm top}=r^2,\qquad
 f=c_{\rm top}b_{\rm top}=r^{-1}.
\]
Here $r$ has infinite order before the outer attachment,
since its powers are reduced alternating words in
$C_4*C_2$. [E70, §§3–4](../notes/E70_EQUIVARIANT_SURGERY.md#3-an-eight-crossing-projection-and-its-group)

The native cover is defined by the $C_4$ quotient
$q\mapsto1,\ c\mapsto2$. This quotient restricts
surjectively to the inner orbifold group. Each old
local subgroup of order two maps injectively, so
the restricted connected cover $M_{\rm in}$ is a
smooth compact oriented manifold with boundary.
The boundary pillowcase image in $C_4$ is its
order-two subgroup: its half-turns map to $2$,
and its translations to $0$. Consequently
$\partial M_{\rm in}$ has two tori, each with
the translation lattice generated by $(d_2,f)$.
This is the twofold torus cover of the sphere
branched at its four pillowcase points.

The kernel of $C_4*C_2\to C_4$ is free of rank
two. This can be seen by rewriting with
transversal $1,q,q^2,q^3$: the $q$ edges give no
kernel generator, and the $c$ edges give four
generators $r_j=q^j c q^{-(j+2)}$, with exponents
of $q$ taken modulo four. The square relation
pairs $r_{j+2}=r_j^{-1}$. Thus the two free
generators may be taken as $r_0=r$ and
$r_1=qrq^{-1}$. On the first boundary torus
the translation $m d_2+n f$ has image $r_0^{2m-n}$.
Choosing the other sheet conjugates this marking by
$q$ and uses $r_1$ on the second torus. In each case
the primitive kernel slope is
\[
 \alpha=d_2+2f .
\]

## Compressing the two boundary tori

The simple curve $\alpha$ is null-homotopic in the
smooth $M_{\rm in}$ on each boundary torus. Dehn's
lemma gives a properly embedded meridian disk on
the first torus. The disk is nonseparating because
a curve in its boundary torus can intersect its
primitive boundary once, and can be pushed in to
intersect the disk once. Cutting it off gives a
connected manifold whose fundamental group
injects into the original group: the inverse
operation is a single 1-handle attachment. Thus the
second $\alpha$ is still null-homotopic after this
cut. Dehn's lemma again gives an embedded disk,
disjoint from the first. This second disk also
does not separate. [Papakyriakopoulos, *On Dehn's
lemma and the asphericity of knots*, first theorem](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/papa.pdf)

Cutting both disks gives a connected smooth
manifold $M'$ with two sphere boundaries. By
reversing the two 1-handle operations,
\[
 F_2\cong\pi_1(M_{\rm in})\cong\pi_1(M')*F_2 .
\]
The first factor embeds in a free group and is
therefore free, and the rank of the abelianization
forces it to have rank zero. Capping $M'$ with
two ordinary balls therefore gives $S^3$ by
the three-dimensional Poincaré theorem in the
smooth category. The complement of the two
capping balls is $S^2\times I$. For example one
can shrink the two smoothly parametrized balls
into disjoint coordinate balls by ambient
isotopy. Reattaching one 1-handle at each end
identifies $M_{\rm in}$ as the connected sum
of two solid tori. The separating sphere may
be taken in the interior product, keeping the
two boundary identifications unchanged.
The two meridians of these solid tori are both
the marked $\alpha$ above. This argument does
not require an invariant choice of the two
compression disks. [Perelman, finite extinction](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307245)
· [Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton, Theorem 1.7.3
and Corollary 1.7.4](https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/Aschenbrenner-Friedl-Wilton-3-manifold-groups-final-version-031115.pdf)

## Outer meridian, orientation and the lens type

The E70 outer rational tangle lifts to two
separate solid tori in this fourfold cover.
Its pillowcase image in the cyclic group is
again order two, so each component is the
usual double branched solid-torus cover of that
rational tangle. The covering meridian is fixed
by the actual two solid-torus angles in E70:
in the geometric boundary coordinates they
are $2x_0+\theta,3x_0+2\theta$. Thus the two
slopes on the outer cap are
\[
 \beta=2d_2-3f\quad\text{(meridian)},\qquad
 \delta=-d_2+2f\quad\text{(longitude)}.
\]
Indeed these coefficients give respectively
$(1,0)$ and $(0,1)$ in the solid-torus angles.
They are valid on both components by the
orientation-preserving deck identifications.
[E70, outer cone chart](../notes/E70_EQUIVARIANT_SURGERY.md#2-the-marked-quotient-three-ball-and-the-three-attachments)

The outward boundary torus of the *inner*
piece has negative basis $(d_2,f)$. One direct
check is on the right edge of E70's positive
$(x,v)$ chart, where the outer normal is
$+\partial_x$ and the $d_2$ arc runs downward.
Equivalently E68 identifies $d_2=-\lambda_B$,
$f=\mu_B$, where $(\lambda_B,\mu_B)$ is
positive on the exterior of the ordinary
surgery component $B$. Consequently
$(\alpha,\ell)=(d_2+2f,-f)$ is the positive
meridian–longitude pair on each inner solid
torus. In that basis the outer slopes are
\[
 \beta=2\alpha+7\ell,\qquad \delta=-\alpha-4\ell .
\]
The two Heegaard gluings are therefore both
$+7/2$ surgeries on the unknot in E54's
orientation convention. In particular the
core $\ell$ represents $r_j$ in the relevant
factor, and the outer core represents $-4\ell$
in its first homology. The linking form on
that inner core, with E54's sign, is $-2/7$.
Changing the preferred longitude shifts the
printed coefficient $2$ by a multiple of
$7$. Exchanging the two solid tori gives
the inverse parameter $4$ modulo seven, and
orientation reversal negates the parameter.
The explicit signed slope fixes these
ambiguities. Thus the smooth oriented cover
is
\[
 X\cong L(7,2)\mathbin{\#}L(7,2)
 \quad\text{with }L(7,2)\text{ defined by }+7/2\text{ surgery}.
\]

## Reidemeister torsion and what the presentations supply

Let $\zeta$ be a nontrivial seventh root of unity.
Take the character nontrivial with value $\zeta$
on the marked generator $\ell$ of one summand
and trivial on the other. The genus-one solid
torus computation uses the two actual core
values $\zeta$ and $\zeta^{-4}$. In the
convention that a nontrivial circle has
torsion $(z-1)^{-1}$, this gives for the
nontrivial summand
\[
 \tau_{\rm lens}(\zeta)\doteq
       \frac{1}{(\zeta-1)(\zeta^{-4}-1)} .
\]
Here $\doteq$ allows multiplication by $\pm\zeta^j$.
This is the classical lens torsion formula
with inverse parameter $4$, and the sign of
the outer core is harmless for that formula.
[Massuyeau, Lemma 5.7 and its Heegaard
basis convention](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.5802/ambp.294.pdf)

The chosen character is acyclic on $X$.
The nontrivial punctured summand has twisted
$H_2$ generated by its boundary sphere and
no other twisted homology. The trivial
punctured summand has ordinary rational
$H_0$ only. The corresponding maps from
the gluing sphere's homology are isomorphisms
in degrees two and zero. With those bases the
homology-sequence torsion in the gluing formula
is a unit. The punctured nontrivial summand's
torsion with that boundary generator is its
closed lens torsion, by capping with one
3-ball. The trivial punctured summand has
torsion $7$ with the primitive ordinary
$H_0$ basis, because its single torsion
homology group is $H_1=C_7$. Consequently
\[
 \tau_X(\zeta,1)\doteq
 7\,\tau_{\rm lens}(\zeta)
 =\frac{7}{(\zeta-1)(\zeta^{-4}-1)} .
\]
A character nontrivial on both summands would
instead have nonzero twisted $H_1,H_2$ from
the connected sum and would need homology bases.
[Turaev, multiplicativity of based torsion, Theorem
0.2.3](https://www.mathnet.ru/eng/rm1971)

The products of absolute values distinguish
the two parameters even with unordered choices
of generators. For a fixed primitive seventh
root put $A_j=|1-\zeta^j|^2$, with representatives
$j=1,2,3$ and $\zeta=e^{2\pi i/7}$.
They satisfy $0<A_1<A_2<A_3$. In the family
of all nontrivial characters, the squared
denominator for parameter $1$ ranges over
$A_j^2$. For parameter $2$, multiplication
by $4$ permutes the three sign classes in
a 3-cycle, so the squared denominators
are the three $A_jA_k$ with distinct
indices. Their minimum is $A_1A_2>A_1^2$.
The units $\pm\zeta^j$ have modulus one.
These absolute values do not distinguish
an orientation reversal. The oriented
meridian and longitude computation does.

A bare Fox presentation of $C_7*C_7$
misses the relevant attaching information.
Even for a single lens factor, its 1-cell
and 2-cell differentials from $g^7$
are common to all lens parameters,
whereas the remaining 3-cell differential
depends on the second core exponent.
The E68 free Alexander character and the
printed E72 group relators do not by
themselves supply this differential on
the actual smooth index-four cover.
Here the two marked meridian slopes in
the E70 tangle give a geometric replacement
for that missing computation. The result
identifies the oriented lens factors of
the cover. It does not identify its
deck action on an invariant reducing
sphere or determine the original
four-dimensional framing's smooth type.
