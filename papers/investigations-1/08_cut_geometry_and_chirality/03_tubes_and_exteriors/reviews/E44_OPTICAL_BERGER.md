## Radial optics and the round shell

In the constant-lapse optical gauge \(N^{-2}(N^2L^2dt^2+h_t)=L^2dt^2+\widehat h_t\), conformal invariance preserves the chiral Weyl costs. The stationary umbilic seam is geodesic because its \(t\)-axis principal curvature vanishes. The spatial radius \(f\) consequently has \(f'(0)=0\), and the round outer data are \(f(S)=\sin r_0,\ f'(S)=\cos r_0\). The radial and spherical Ricci eigenvalues differ by \(-f''/f-(1-(f')^2)/f^2\). Their square deviation from the mean is \(2/3\) times the square of the difference, giving
\[
 A=\frac{2\pi}{3}\int\left[f''+\frac{1-(f')^2}{f}\right]^2 ds.
\]
Use \(dv=ds/f^2,\ y=f^{-2}\). Then \(y_v=-2f'/f\) and \(q=1-y_{vv}/2=1+f f''-(f')^2\). The sign of the outer derivative gives \(\int q\,dv=V+\cot r_0\) with \(V=\int dv>0\). Thus
\[
 A\ \ge\ \frac{2\pi}{3}\frac{(V+\cot r_0)^2}{V}
 \ \ge\ \frac{8\pi}{3}\cot r_0 .
\]
This computation uses \(f>0\) and both jets and can start at the last zero of \(f'\) as well as at the seam. In the equality profile \(f=a_*\cosh(s/a_*)\) with \(a_*=\sin r_0/\sqrt{1+\cos^2r_0}\), the outer radius and derivative are attained at \(s=a_*\operatorname{arsinh}(\cos r_0)\). It gives \(V=\cot r_0,\ q=2\). Exact roundness near the end would give \(q=0\) there. Matching the cosh profile to the exact round metric just inside \(r_0\) produces a continuous first derivative and bounded one-sided second derivatives. Interpolating the two extensions in a shrinking fixed-radius junction preserves \(C^1\) convergence and bounds their second derivatives, since the two profiles differ by a second-order term at the match. Hence the integral converges for each matching radius, and those radii can tend to \(r_0\). This establishes sharpness over the free optical profiles. A fixed physical radial modulus is a further restriction.

The static and time-dependent supports are disjoint, so \(B\ge0\). With \(d=\pi/6,\ x_0=2\tan(r_0/2)\), the resting steady stress estimate gives \(C\ge(32\pi/5)d^2x_0\cos^6(R/2)\). The minimum of \(A_0 L+C_0/L^3\) has fourth power \(256 A_0^3C_0/27\). Normalizing by \(8\pi^2/3\) gives a fourth-power ratio at least \((256/(405\pi^2))x_0\cot^3 r_0\cos^6(R/2)\). This exceeds \(32/25\) on \(0<r_0<R\le\pi/17\) by \(x_0\ge r_0,\ \sin r_0\le r_0,\ \cos^3r_0,\cos^6(R/2)>9/10,\ \pi^2<10\).

For the divergence-free time-changing case the entire bend can be written \(\widehat h=w^4g_{\rm rd}\), with \(w^2=f/\sin\rho\) and \(\partial_s\log w=(f'-\cos\rho)/(2f)\). The test \(u=1/w\), extended by one off the bend, descends because the bend coordinate is preserved by the marking. The reflected test has finite \(H^1\) norm even when its normal derivatives do not agree. The three-dimensional coefficient-eight conformal form transforms to \(6\operatorname{Vol}_{\rm rd}(B_{r_0}\setminus B_{\rho_i})\) plus a boundary term. Its outer term is zero. At the inner face the outward direction is \(-\partial_s\), hence \(u\partial_\nu u=-u^2\cos\rho_i/(2f)\) and the boundary contribution is \(-16\pi\sin\rho_i\cos\rho_i\). Lowering the gradient coefficient to six gives \(S[u]\le 6\operatorname{Vol}(Y)=3\pi^2/34\). This also bounds the conformal radius \(\rho_i\) in a positive class when the time form is nonpositive. Since \(H=0\), \(T[u]=-\int|K|^2\), and the inner bend adds no shear. The instantaneous stress estimate gives \(-T[u]\ge(32\pi/15)d^2x_0\cos^6(R/2)\). Strict positivity yields
\[
 \left(\frac{e_X}{8\pi^2/3}\right)^2>
 \frac{272}{405\pi}\,x_0\cot^2r_0\cos^6(R/2)
 \ \ge\ \frac{4624}{405\pi^2}\cos^2 r_0\cos^6(R/2)>1 .
\]
This use of the time form depends on divergence-free transport and applies to compatible nonresting matched jets as well as resting paths. [Round stress and length form](E33_VOLUME_PRESERVING.md)

For the counterexample to the areal radius restriction, put \(m=(\sin r_0)^3\). A product collar of radius \(m\), the scalar-flat branch \(f'^2=1-m/f\), and the round exit have matching first jets. ## Quotient and Berger curvature

Let \(h_\epsilon\) be left invariant with lengths \((\epsilon,1,1)\) on \(i,j,k\), \(0<\epsilon\le1\). Right multiplication by \(\zeta=e^{2\pi i/17}\) and by \(j\) preserves the inner product by the adjoint action. The left factors are isometries, so the free \(Q_8\times C_{17}\) quotient and the marked \(J\) both retain the metric. On the radius-\(1/2\) Hopf base a lift \(p\mapsto A p B^{-1}\), \(A=q a_q,\ B=\zeta^m j\), induces \(-\operatorname{Ad}_A\). All these \(A\) have real part \(\pm1/2\), giving \(y\cdot(-\operatorname{Ad}_A y)=1/2-3c^2/2\) for the unit base coordinate \(y\) with axis component \(c\). The projected displacement is at least \(\pi/6\). Conjugate \(A\) to \(\exp(\alpha B)\), where \(\alpha=\pi/3\) or \(2\pi/3\). The lift at that point traverses a horizontal subgroup arc of absolute length \(|\alpha-\pi/2|=\pi/6\), which gives equality. In the prescribed moving chart the endpoint difference of the pure left translation and \(J\) is \(\operatorname{Ad}_j\), preserving the polar metrics and fixing the direction \(j\) used to mark the meridian.

The orthonormal left brackets have coefficients \((2\epsilon,2/\epsilon,2/\epsilon)\). Substitution in Koszul gives Ricci eigenvalues \(2\epsilon^2,4-2\epsilon^2,4-2\epsilon^2\), sectional eigenvalues \(\epsilon^2,\epsilon^2,4-3\epsilon^2\), and scalar \(8-2\epsilon^2\). The Ricci square deviation is \((32/3)(1-\epsilon^2)^2\). The quotient order is \(136\), so its volume is \(\pi^2\epsilon/68\) and the Ricci square deviation integral is \(8\pi^2\epsilon(1-\epsilon^2)^2/51\).

For the global minimum deck displacement, the round left angles are \(0,\pi/2,\pi\), with closest nonidentical right angles producing minimum gap \(\pi/34\). The Berger comparison gives a lower bound \(\epsilon\pi/34\), attained by a vertical arc at alignment. The central element \((-1,\zeta^8)\) produces an arc of length \(\epsilon\pi/17\) at every point. From the Euler equation the geodesic starting with body velocity \(u+v i\), \(u\perp i\), is
\[
 g(t)=\exp(t(u+\epsilon^2 v i))\exp(t(1-\epsilon^2)v i).
\]
If \(u\ne0\), a nontrivial return to \(1\) requires \(t|u+\epsilon^2 v i|\ge\pi\), and the length is at least this quantity. A vertical loop has length at least \(2\pi\epsilon\). The upper curvature bound \(4\) gives conjugate radius at least \(\pi/2\) by the index form. The short loop alternative on this compact homogeneous cover gives injectivity radius at least \(\min(\pi/2,\pi\epsilon)\). Because the maximal relevant half deck displacement is \(\epsilon\pi/34\), balls downstairs are first constrained by the deck action. Thus \(\inf\operatorname{inj}_Y=\epsilon\pi/68\) and every radius \(R=\epsilon/32\) in the moving chart is embedded.
