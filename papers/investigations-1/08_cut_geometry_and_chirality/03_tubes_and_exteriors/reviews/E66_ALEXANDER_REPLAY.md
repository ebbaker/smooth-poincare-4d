This is an independent analytical replay of the E64 covering number and the resulting determinant test in the smooth oriented three-dimensional marked model.

The old order-two knot \(S_0\) in the actual first underlying filling \(S^3\) has determinant congruent to \(3\) or \(5\pmod 8\), so \(\operatorname{Arf}(S_0)=1\). The actual first orbifold group therefore cannot be cyclic. If the group is odd dihedral, its cyclic rotation subgroup has order \(\det S_0>1\). The computation leaves the fundamental group of the double cover branched only along \(S_0\) unresolved.

## Identify the comparison covering and compute its first homology

Let \(O=S^3/\Lambda\) be the unfilled orbifold, \(M=|O|\) its underlying oriented lens space, \(S\) its old order-two singular knot, and \(\ell\) the projected marked regular knot. E39 fixes the peripheral longitude \(a\) and positive meridian \(\mu\) on \(\partial\nu\ell\). The marked-core verification identifies the underlying \(a\)-filling with \(S^3\) and the new dual core \(U\) with an unknot. Write \(S_0\) for the surviving old knot in that filling and take the two-component link \(L=S_0\cup U\). Its exterior is the unfilled \(Z=M\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu S\cup\nu\ell)\), with meridians \(\sigma\) of \(S_0\) and \(a\) of \(U\). The winding calculation gives \(|\operatorname{lk}(S_0,U)|=3\). Choose component orientations to make the linking number \(+3\) for the polynomial calculation. This choice does not affect any meridian parity. [Marked core and dual unknot](../notes/MARKED_LENS_CORE.md) · [E54, winding and peripheral calculation](../notes/E54_UNDERLYING_SLOPES.md#the-original-singular-knot-in-the-marked-exterior) · [E61, independent winding check](E61_MARKED_PATTERN.md#integer-slopes-connected-lifts-and-group-decisions)

The double cover of \(S^3\) branched along both components corresponds on \(Z\) to the character sending \(a,\sigma\) to \(1\in C_2\). The peripheral homology relation \(3a+\mu-3\sigma=0\) forces \(\mu\) to \(0\). This is exactly the restriction of the parity cover of the unfilled orbifold. Indeed the even-right subgroup \(\Lambda^+=T^*\times C_{17}\) acts freely, the old singular meridian is odd, the lift of \(a\) traverses the odd stabilizer generator of the marked orbit up to inversion, and \(\mu\) bounds the original regular meridian disk. Thus the covering is branched along \(S\) in the unfilled ambient manifold. Its smooth total space is \(X_0=S^3/\Lambda^+\). The inverse image \(\widehat\ell\) is connected, and that of the boundary torus has lattice \(\langle 2a,\mu\rangle\). Set \(\widehat E=X_0\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu\widehat\ell\). [E39, deck group and marked torus](../notes/E39_PRISM_FILLINGS.md#the-canonical-suspension-and-the-actual-marked-torus) · [E62, the actual connected lift](../notes/E62_FIGURE_EIGHT_LIFT.md#the-covering-knot-and-projection)

Filling \(\widehat E\) along the primitive slope \(2a\) extends the map as a double cover branched at \(U\) as well as \(S_0\). The meridian maps with degree two. In the \((a,\lambda_0)\) basis of the torus of \(U\), with \(\lambda_0=-3a-\mu\), the second basis element of the upstairs lattice may be taken as \(a+\lambda_0\). The corresponding twist is realized by the local solid-torus branched map \((e^{\mathrm i\theta},z)\mapsto(e^{\mathrm i\theta},e^{\mathrm i\theta}z^2)\) in that basis. Accordingly the smooth filled cover is \(\Sigma_2(L)=\widehat E(2a)\).

The ambient \(X_0=\widehat E(\mu)\) has fundamental group \(T^*\times C_{17}\) and first homology \(C_3\times C_{17}\). Indeed \(T^*\) has normal quaternion subgroup \(Q_8\) and cyclic quotient of order three permuting the three nonzero classes of \(Q_8^{\rm ab}\), with trivial coinvariants. Thus \(\lvert H_1(X_0;\mathbb Z)\rvert=51\). Put \(E=M\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu\ell\). The knot exteriors \(\widehat E,E\) in the two rational homology spheres have rational first Betti number one, generated over \(\mathbb Q\) by the meridian, by the pair sequence and excision. The map \(\widehat E\to E\) takes \(\mu\) to \(\mu\), so it induces an isomorphism on rational first homology. The downstairs rational longitude is \(\lambda=3a+\mu\). Its rational line intersects the upstairs lattice in the primitive line generated by
\[
 \widehat\lambda=3(2a)+2\mu.
\]
For a primitive fill slope with nonzero distance from the rational longitude, the order of first homology is proportional to that distance. In fact the filling quotients the integral first homology of the exterior by the slope image. The image in its free cyclic quotient is a fixed integer multiple of the signed distance, and the torsion factor survives unchanged because this image is nonzero. The distances from \(\widehat\lambda\) to \(\mu\) and to \(2a\) are \(3\) and \(2\). This independently gives
\[
 \lvert H_1(\Sigma_2(L);\mathbb Z)\rvert=51\frac{2}{3}=34.
\]
This is the cover branched along the two-component link in the first underlying filling. The cover used to compute the actual orbifold group is branched along \(S_0\) alone. E65 independently obtains the same order using the integral lifted meridian kernel and the linking of the ambient great circles. ## Center the two-variable polynomial before testing parity

Let \(D(x,y)=\Delta_L(x,y)\) be the classical two-variable Alexander polynomial, with \(x\) for \(S_0\) and \(y\) for \(U\), defined up to a unit \(\pm x^i y^j\). Let \(d(t)\) be the single-variable polynomial for total meridian linking, with Seifert-matrix convention \(d(t)\doteq\det(tV-V^{\mathsf T})\). The normalizations give
\[
 d(t)\doteq (t-1)D(t,t),\qquad
 \lvert H_1(\Sigma_2(L);\mathbb Z)\rvert=\lvert d(-1)\rvert.
\]
For the first identity, the deleted Wirtinger column in the multivariable Alexander matrix gives a minor equal up to a unit to \((x_i-1)D\), where \(x_i\) is the component of the column. On diagonal specialization the common factor is \(t-1\); the resulting one-variable presentation computes the total-linking Alexander polynomial. For the second identity, take a connected oriented Seifert surface for \(L\) and the double cover of \(B^4\) branched along its pushed-in surface. The two-ball construction gives \(H_1=0\) by Mayer–Vietoris since the gluing neighbourhood is connected. Its intersection matrix is \(V+V^{\mathsf T}\), which therefore presents the first homology of its boundary \(\Sigma_2(L)\). Its determinant has the same magnitude as \(d(-1)\). In particular
\[
 |D(-1,-1)|=17.
\]
[Torres, *On the Alexander Polynomial*, Chapter I §3 and Chapter II §3, cofactor calculation](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/torres.pdf) · [Gordon–Litherland, *On the Signature of a Link*, Theorem 3, relation-matrix discussion, §5 extension to links](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/gordlith.pdf)

Torres reciprocity for two components gives \(D(x^{-1},y^{-1})=x^p y^q D(x,y)\) with plus sign and integer exponents \(p,q\). The two axis specializations are
\[
 \begin{aligned}
 D(x,1)&\doteq(1+x+x^2)\Delta_{S_0}(x),\\
 D(1,y)&\doteq 1+y+y^2 .
 \end{aligned}
\]
The second line uses the dual unknot and both use linking number three. A knot polynomial has an integral reciprocal centre. Multiplication by \(1+x+x^2\), and any allowed unit, keep that centre integral. Both specializations are nonzero. Restricting the reciprocal identity to each specialization therefore shows respectively that \(p\) and \(q\) are even. Multiplying by \(x^{p/2}y^{q/2}\) makes reciprocity exact, with zero centre. Continue to call the centered representative \(D\). In particular its \(x=1\) specialization is \(\pm(y+1+y^{-1})\), since a remaining nonzero monomial shift would break symmetry. [Torres, Chapter II Theorems 2 and 3 and the knot normalization in the introduction](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/torres.pdf)

Set \(P(y)=D(-1,y)\). Then \(P(y^{-1})=P(y)\) and \(P(y)\equiv D(1,y)\equiv y^{-1}+1+y\pmod2\). Write \(P(y)=c_0+\sum_{j\ge1}c_j(y^j+y^{-j})\). Only \(c_1\) is odd among the positive-index coefficients, so
\[
 P(1)-P(-1)=4\sum_{\substack{j>0\\j\ {\rm odd}}}c_j\equiv4\pmod8.
\]
Since \(P(-1)=\pm17\), it follows that \(P(1)\equiv3\) or \(5\pmod8\). The first axis specialization, evaluated at \(x=-1\), gives \(|P(1)|=|\Delta_{S_0}(-1)|=\det S_0\). Both the sign of the representative and taking the absolute value preserve the residue pair \(\{3,5\}\). The auxiliary factor in that specialization has magnitude one at \(-1\). Murasugi's knot criterion gives \(\operatorname{Arf}(S_0)=1\), and \(\det S_0\ge3\) also directly excludes the unknot. [Murasugi, *The Arf invariant for knot types*, Proc. AMS 21 (1969), 69–72, Theorem 2, author-posted scan](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/243054018_The_Arf_invariant_for_knot_types)

The signed residues also check after choosing \(D(1,1)=3\) and the centered knot representative \(\Delta_{S_0}(1)=1\). Then \(D(x,1)=(x^{-1}+1+x)\Delta_{S_0}(x)\), so \(P(1)=-\Delta_{S_0}(-1)\equiv-1\pmod4\) by knot reciprocity. Thus \(P(-1)=-17\) for this sign convention, \(P(1)\equiv3\pmod8\), and the normalized signed knot evaluation is \(\Delta_{S_0}(-1)\equiv5\pmod8\).

## Consequence for the actual first orbifold

The first actual filled orbifold has underlying \(S^3\) and only the old weight-two knot \(S_0\). Its group \(G_0\) imposes the square of \(\sigma\) in the knot exterior group. The mod-two meridian character has kernel \(\pi_1(\Sigma_2(S_0))\) after this relation, since the lifted branched solid torus fills along the lift of \(\sigma^2\). Its abelianization has order \(\det S_0>1\) by the knot case of the determinant formula. Thus the kernel is nontrivial. As \(G_0^{\rm ab}=C_2\), a cyclic \(G_0\) would be \(C_2\) and have trivial kernel. If \(G_0\) is an odd dihedral group of order \(2m\), its meridian-parity kernel is the cyclic rotation subgroup of order \(m\), so \(m=\det S_0\equiv3\) or \(5\pmod8\). The determinant calculation does not identify that kernel with a cyclic fundamental group. Odd-dihedral recognition of this orbifold, and the corresponding four-dimensional smooth recognition step, remain open.
