The Osaka Journal scans of the article and addendum were inspected directly.

The actual odd reverse surgery gives a fibred knot with punctured lens-connected-sum fibre, within the literal hypotheses of Teragaito’s general theorem on p. 61. Its actual meridian has infinite order modulo the group centre. Every knot in the theorem’s stated list has a meridian with a central power. The knot-classification conclusion therefore fails here, and a separate construction below gives the same conflict without using the prism data. The failure occurs in the reduction to a relative punctured prime system. Teragaito’s single-lens addendum does not provide that reduction or establish standardness of the odd ambient sphere.

Uniqueness of the diffeomorphism types of the closed primes does not give the asserted relative-boundary permutation. Slide maps are independent operations in the mapping class group of a reducible three-manifold. Even a closed-fibre diffeomorphism isotopic to a specified one can change the punctured return by pushing the ball along a loop. McCullough gives the geometric prime-slide construction and the generation theorem in §3. ## Relative prime system and the marked return

For the actual basic odd construction, the geometric reverse circle \(\delta\) is a section of the capped bundle \(W_1\) of E84. Its fibre \(X\) is a connected sum of two order-seven lens spaces. Remove a fibrewise ball tube around \(\delta\) and attach \(D^2\times S^2\) with the reverse framing. The resulting core knot has punctured fibre \(X^\circ\), and its meridian in the exterior is the class of \(\delta\), up to inversion and conjugacy. The ambient \(\Sigma_1\) is already a smooth homotopy four-sphere by the original marked closing. These are the printed general hypotheses, independently of the lens parameters. [E84 actual fibre and section](../notes/E84_ODD_INTERMEDIATE.md#2-a-smooth-fibration-with-the-actual-reverse-circle-as-section) · [E39 homotopy sphere](../notes/E39_PRISM_FILLINGS.md#the-canonical-suspension-and-the-actual-marked-torus)

Use the E84 signed splitting \(F=\langle x\mid x^7=1\rangle*\langle y\mid y^7=1\rangle\), with \(x=r,y=r_1\) there, and stable lift \(t=(q^{-1},1)\). Write \(\beta=\operatorname{Ad}_t|_F\). The relations are
\[
 \beta(x)=y^{-1},\qquad \beta(y)=x,\qquad \delta=yt,\qquad
 \delta^4=(yx y^{-1}x^{-1})t^4.
\]
Here \(t^4\) is central, and \(w=yx y^{-1}x^{-1}\) is a cyclically reduced word of length four in \(F\). In particular every nonzero power of \(w\) is nontrivial. The based return for the actual section is \(\operatorname{Inn}(y)\beta\) or its inverse, according to the base orientation convention. Its fourth power is \(\operatorname{Inn}(w)\) in the displayed convention. The centre of \(F\) is trivial. If \(\delta^n\) were central for \(n\ne0\), then \(\delta^{4n}\) would act trivially on \(F\), forcing \(w^n=1\). Hence the actual meridian has infinite order modulo the centre of the exterior group. [E84 pointed return and peripheral marking](../notes/E84_ODD_INTERMEDIATE.md#3-prime-factors-and-the-marked-pointed-return)

If a boundary-fixed return permutes interior embedded punctured primes forming a connected-sum system, its complementary punctured three-ball is simply connected. Paths in that complement from the cap-sphere basepoint to the prime boundaries give a free product of actual based factors. The return permutes these based factors, with maps given by isomorphisms of the finite factor groups. Its automorphism on the free product then has finite order, since it is determined by the finite permutation and the factor isomorphisms. This conflicts with the computed fourth power. No such relative prime system is preserved by the actual pointed return. In particular the unpointed order-four return of E84 cannot be used as evidence for the cyclic hypothesis of Lemma 2. In a trivialization of the capped mapping torus, paths for the two sections \(t\) and \(yt\) differ by the fibre loop \(y\). Transporting the cap ball along this difference changes the based automorphism by \(\operatorname{Inn}(y)\). The reduced commutator in the fourth return is the obstruction to keeping a permuted system relative to that ball.

## Test of the knot conclusion

For a type L knot, the meridian acts by inversion on the odd cyclic fibre group, so its square is central. For an \(r\)-cable of a type L knot, use the pattern in Teragaito’s §4. Before inserting the companion, the exterior of the trivial pattern knot has group \(\mathbb Z\) with generator its meridian \(m\). Removing the disjoint tubular neighbourhood of the pattern circle preserves this group by codimension-three general position. On its boundary, the generator represents \(m^r\). Inserting the companion exterior identifies this with the companion meridian \(t_L\), up to orientation. Van Kampen gives the cable group by adjoining \(m\) to the companion group with \(m^r=t_L^{\pm1}\). Thus \(m^{2r}\) is central, since it commutes with both the companion group and \(m\). For a connected sum the exterior groups amalgamate along the common meridian. Any common multiple of the central exponents is central in the whole group. This condition applies to every knot on the general theorem’s list and is invariant under equivalence of pairs, allowing reversal of the meridian. It fails for \(\delta\). [Teragaito, p. 59–61, cable definition and types L and L*](https://ir.library.osaka-u.ac.jp/repo/ouka/all/5691/ojm26_01_02.pdf)

For an independent example within the printed hypotheses, let \(X=L(p,q)\#L(p,q)\) with odd \(p>1\) and \(p>q>0\). Use a ball with two holes and attach the two punctured lens copies. A half-turn near the two holes, interpolated to the identity near the outer boundary, interchanges the copies using the matching boundary parametrizations. Invert the fundamental group of one lens copy by complex conjugation on its standard round quotient. Complex conjugation preserves the three-dimensional orientation and fixes a point, and multiplying by a local rotation near that point makes the diffeomorphism the identity on a chosen ball while retaining its action on the fundamental group. Composing the exchange and this inversion in the appropriate order gives the exact based action \(\beta(x)=y^{-1}\), \(\beta(y)=x\) on \(C_p*C_p\). The paths defining the two factors run in the simply connected ball with holes. Cap the outer boundary by the identity and take its mapping torus, with stable letter \(t\).

A degree-one section representing \(yt\) is obtained by choosing its fibre path over a base interval with one extra loop \(y\); it is embedded because it meets each fibre once. Circle surgery with a fibrewise normal framing gives a smooth homotopy four-sphere. In the group, killing \(yt\) sets \(t=y^{-1}\); the relation \(t y t^{-1}=x\) gives \(x=y\), and \(t x t^{-1}=y^{-1}\) then gives \(y^2=1\). The odd order relation kills \(y\), so the ambient is simply connected. Circle surgery adds two to the mapping torus’s zero Euler characteristic. Duality and the homotopy criterion for a simply connected homology four-sphere give the assertion. The surgery core knot has punctured \(X\) fibre and meridian \(yt\), with fourth power \(w t^4\) as above. This independently contradicts the knot-classification part without requiring any decision about the ambient smooth type.

The ambient and pair assertions are distinct. A different knot on standard \(S^4\) could have exactly this meridian behaviour. The printed proof of ambient standardness proceeds through the relative splitting and cable identification that fail for the actual section, and the addendum’s single-lens conjugacy argument cannot change \(yt\) to \(t\). The odd ambient diffeomorphism type remains undecided by this source and these calculations.
