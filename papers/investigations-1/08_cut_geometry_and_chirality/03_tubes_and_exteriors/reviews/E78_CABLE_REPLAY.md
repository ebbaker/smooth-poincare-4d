## Stable class in the suspension

Use the conventions of E39. On the universal cover \(\mathbb R\times S^3\), the degree-one deck generator associated with the actual section is \(t:(r,q)\mapsto(r+1,J^{-1}q)\). In particular \(J^6\) acts by the antipode. Write \(c\) for the left deck element \(-1\in Q_8\), so the principal orbit represents \(f=ct^6\). The map \(R_i:q\mapsto qi\) normalizes \(\Gamma\) and conjugates \(J\) to \(cJ\), which gives an orientation-preserving involution of \(N\) commuting with the suspension action. It carries the original geodesic circle to its orthogonal circle and sends \(t\) to \(ct\). Its marking is the image of \((a,\mu)\) under \(R_i\). In the full orbit group, the original positive longitude maps to \(J^{-1}\) and its orthogonal counterpart to \(J^5\). The right factors of these deck actions differ by the central antipode. With the right-\(i\) markings, the two regular exterior orbifolds are identified by \(R_i\).

Take a cable of winding \(w>0\) and meridional exponent \(k\), with \(\gcd(w,k)=1\). In core-product coordinates \((x,z,\phi)\in S^1\times D^2\times S^1\), a lift of its section can be written \((ws,\rho e^{2\pi i k s},ms)\) for \(s\in\mathbb R/\mathbb Z\), with integer fibre winding \(m\). The lift is embedded because the cable is embedded. Its degree is \(w+6m\), so degree one requires \(m=(1-w)/6\). In the original product its class is \(c^m t\). In the orthogonal product it is \(c^{w+m}t\). The two degree-one classes \(t,ct\) are not conjugate in \(\Gamma\rtimes\mathbb Z\): conjugation to \(ct\) would require an element of \(Q_8\) to be sent to its negative by the order-three cycle on its imaginary axes. Neither a central element nor a signed imaginary unit has that property. Therefore a positively oriented lift must have \(w\equiv1\pmod{12}\) in the original product and \(w\equiv7\pmod{12}\) in the orthogonal product. Allowing reversal of orientation gives the residue classes \(\pm1\) and \(\pm5\), respectively. A generic homotopy between loops in a four-manifold can be taken through embeddings: equal-time coincidences have three-dimensional domain, and tangent vanishing also has codimension four. Thus the class equality gives smooth ambient isotopy of the unframed sections. For the orthogonal \((7,2)\) cable, \(m=-1\) gives the exact class \((ct)^7f^{-1}=t\).

## Companion slopes and the torus away from distance one

Write \(q\) for the integer coefficient of the positively oriented cable meridian in the slope \(l_C+q\mu_C\), using the preferred longitude in the standard zero-framed solid torus model for \((a,\mu)\). The annular surface framing of the cable is \(l_C+wk\mu_C\). The filling distance from this slope is \(d=|q-wk|\). A cable space is Seifert fibred over an annulus with one exceptional fibre of order \(w\). For \(d>0\), the inner filling gives a disk base with the existing order-\(w\) fibre and a fibre of multiplicity \(d\). In particular \(d=1\) gives a solid torus. [Gordon, *Dehn surgery and satellite knots*, Lemma 7.2, as stated and used on p. 254 by Ni and Zhang](https://www.its.caltech.edu/~yini/Published/BoundSlope.pdf) · [Meier, published cable-space argument, pp. 445–446](https://msp.org/agt/2014/14-1/agt-v14-n1-p16-s.pdf)

The inner filling imposes \(w[a]+q[\mu_C]=0\) in the cable-space homology, since \([l_C]=w[a]\) and the outer meridian satisfies \([\mu]=w[\mu_C]\). When \(d=1\) the primitive outer meridian is therefore \(w^2a+q\mu\). Its coefficients are coprime because \(q=wk\pm1\). This sign agrees with the cabling annulus, whose outer boundary is \(w a+k\mu\).

The orbifold exterior torus of the original companion is incompressible. Its double cover E62 has ordinary exterior \(E'\) in the spherical manifold \(S^3/(T^*\times C_{17})\), and its drilled core is homologically nonzero in that manifold: in the E68 free quotient \(\mu\) represents three times the generator and \(\lambda_H\) represents the generator. Reduction modulo three therefore kills the meridian and detects the core. This excludes a ball containing the core, so \(E'\) is irreducible. Its nonconstant E68 Alexander polynomial excludes a solid torus. The loop theorem and the usual compression of an irreducible torus-boundary manifold imply that its boundary group injects. The index-two peripheral sublattice then gives injection downstairs, since the square of any nontrivial kernel element would lie in that sublattice. The same holds for the orthogonal companion via \(R_i\). [E62 cover and marking](../notes/E62_FIGURE_EIGHT_LIFT.md) · [E68 free character and polynomial](../notes/E68_CROSSING_ALEXANDER.md)

When \(w>1\) and \(d>1\), the filled cable side has injective outer boundary as well. In a Seifert presentation the fibre has infinite order, as seen by mapping the fibre to \(wd\in\mathbb Z\) and solving the two exceptional power relations there. Modulo the fibre, the outer boundary projects to the product of the two cone generators, an infinite-order word in \(C_w*C_d\). These two facts give injection of the full peripheral \(\mathbb Z^2\). The amalgamated product of the orbifold exterior and cable filling then contains \(\mathbb Z^2\), which excludes both finite recognition groups. This uses the orbifold exterior retaining the old singular knot.

When \(d=0\), the filling kills the Seifert fibre. A presentation over the annulus with one order-\(w\) point gives \(C_w*\mathbb Z\) after this relation. The image of the outer peripheral group is the free cyclic factor after choosing the outer base boundary as its cyclic generator. Its kernel is the slope \(w a+k\mu\) of the cabling annulus. Gluing thus gives \(C_w*G(w,k)\), where \(G(w,k)\) is the native companion filling group at \(w a+k\mu\). Its abelianization has order \(2|3k-w|>1\): start with the two link meridians from E74, impose the weight-two old meridian and the companion slope. The winding classes above give \(3\nmid w\). Thus the free product is infinite. Winding one is a core pattern with a product cable exterior, so its ordinary integer core slopes reduce to the E74 calculation.

## The unique candidate and its actual filling word

For \(w>1\) and \(d=1\), put \(p=|3q-w^2|\), the order of the ambient lens group. Its old knot lifts as a single knot to the ordinary \(p\)-fold cover since \(3\nmid w\). The E74 determinant calculation extends directly to the primitive companion pair \((w^2,q)\). In its proof replace \(3s-1\) by \(3q-w^2\) and \(s\) by \(q\); the core-character product is unchanged because the pair is primitive and \(3q-w^2\equiv q+1\pmod2\). It gives
\[
 \prod_{y^p=(-1)^q}|7-5(y+y^{-1})|.
\]
For even \(q\) this contains the factor \(3\). For odd \(q\), E74's recurrence shows that it equals one precisely at \(p=4\). Cyclicity requires this determinant condition. Odd-dihedral recognition demands abelianization \(C_2\), hence \(p=1\), since the native abelianization order is \(2p\).

With \(q=wk+\varepsilon\) and \(\varepsilon=\pm1\), the condition \(p=1\) would give \(w\mid 2\) or \(w\mid4\), impossible for an admissible odd \(w>1\). The condition \(p=4\) gives \(w\mid1\) or \(w\mid7\), so \(w=7\). Its sole integer solution is \(k=2,\varepsilon=1,q=15\), with \(3q-w^2=-4\). Winding seven is admissible on the orthogonal companion. The induced slope is \(-4a+15\lambda_U\) up to sign in the dual unknot basis of E74. It is a different slope from the previously tested \(+4\) surgery, even though both have ambient lens order four and determinant one.

In the E72 based presentation write \(P=g_9^{-1}h\), where \(h\) is the reflection and \(g_{16}\) is the meridian \(\mu\). E72 gives \(a=P\mu^{-1}\) in the commuting peripheral group. Thus the actual new relator is \(P^{49}\mu^{-34}\). The E72 \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_{29})\) witness for \(a-\mu\) has projective \(\mu\) of order 14, with representative \(\begin{pmatrix}6&22\\18&13\end{pmatrix}\). In that witness \(P=\mu^2\), so the new relator evaluates to \(\mu^{64}\ne1\).

The E77 witness uses the independent E70 tangle conventions, where the same slope word is \(\lambda_L^{49}L^{-34}\). A direct replay with its first four determinant-one matrices modulo 31 gives the slope, the \(F\)-surgery word and both old-meridian squares as \(-I\). The outer slope, longitude commutators and pillowcase product give \(I\), and \([F,L]=(21,28;28,30)\) is noncentral. The E77 checker (`c86208a7e307ba85`) traverses the two proposed images and gets 14,880 projective elements in each, the full simple group \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_{31})\). [E77 exact matrices and checker](../notes/E77_ORTHOGONAL_CABLE.md#3-finite-nonabelian-image-of-the-actual-group)

The ordinary fourfold lens cover followed by the double cover branched over its one lifted old knot gives the index-eight smooth manifold subgroup of the native group. This subgroup is normal by uniqueness of the branch character in the single-knot cover. The order-eight quotient has a central \(C_2\) with cyclic quotient \(C_4\) and is therefore abelian. The native abelianization \(C_8\) maps isomorphically to it. The deep subgroup is thus the commutator subgroup and still surjects onto the simple perfect group \(\operatorname{PSL}_2(\mathbb F_{31})\). If it were finite, elliptization would embed it as a free spherical group in \(SO(4)\). A finite subgroup of \(SO(4)\) has only \(A_5\) as a possible nonabelian simple composition factor, by projection to \(SO(3)\times SO(3)\) with central kernel and the classification of finite rotation groups in each factor. This excludes the simple quotient of order 14,880. Hence both the deep group and native group are infinite, despite determinant one. ## Spin comparison of the full four-dimensional framing

Use the left-invariant spatial frame \(q\mapsto(qi,qj,qk)\) on \(\mathbb R\times S^3\), with the time direction. A deck action with right quaternion factor \(B\) rotates this frame by \(\operatorname{Ad}_B\). The spin structure obtained by lifting the fibre deck map \((A,z^r)\mapsto z^r\) admits either lift \(\pm j^{-1}\) of \(t\). These exhaust the two spin structures of \(N\), since the order-three monodromy on \(H^1(Y;\mathbb F_2)\) has no invariant difference. The left antipode \(c\) has trivial spin lift. Thus the pure time translation \(f=ct^6\) has spin lift \(-1\) in a suspension-transported product frame. Right multiplication by \(i\) conjugates the lift of \(t\) to its negative, while the downstairs generator changes to \(ct\). It follows that \(R_i\) swaps the two spin structures. In particular the framed orthogonal centre, with its pushed-forward product frame, has spin bit \(\epsilon_a+1\) when the original product-framed centre has bit \(\epsilon_a\). A bit records the sign of the lift of the adapted tangent-plus-normal frame around the circle.

In the pushed-forward trivialization on the orthogonal product, the cable has product-frame bit \(w(\epsilon_a+1)+m\). The adapted torus framing adds \(k\) to this bit. To see the full linear comparison, use the disk radial vector and the tangent vector perpendicular to the cable in the companion boundary torus as the two transverse base directions. The adapted tangent and these two directions undergo the disk rotation through \(2\pi k s\). After factoring out that rotation, they and the fibre direction are related to the constant product frame by a constant matrix. The vertical velocity \(m\) contributes a constant shear when the fibre chart is adjusted to the lifted section. It adds no extra frame rotation. Changing from the torus surface framing to \(l_C+q\mu_C\) adds \(q-wk\) disk turns. Consequently the difference from the original framed section, for the same ambient spin structure, is
\[
 w(\epsilon_a+1)+m+k+(q-wk)-\epsilon_a\pmod2.
\]
At \((w,m,k,q)=(7,-1,2,15)\) this equals one. Once the unframed sections are isotoped to one another, the spin bit distinguishes the two full normal three-framings because a generator of \(\pi_1SO(3)\) changes the bit. Thus the group obstruction here applies to a construction of the original odd framed surgery. The classification concerns the two specified regular companions and their torus cables.
