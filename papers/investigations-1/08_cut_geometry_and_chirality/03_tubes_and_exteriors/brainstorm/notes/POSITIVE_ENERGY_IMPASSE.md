# The remaining marked exterior statement

Scope is the smooth homotopy-sphere closing of an actual normal sphere tube; in particular its peripheral circle normally generates the exterior group.

**Remaining question.** Let \(X\) be a compact smooth oriented exterior with integral homology of the circle and marked boundary \(S^1\times S^2\). Let the meridian normally generate \(\pi_1 X\), and suppose \(X\) has a smooth conformal class with umbilic boundary, positive reflective double and \(0<e_X<8\pi^2/3\). Must the two homotopy-sphere fillings by \(S^2\times D^2\) be smooth standard spheres? Integral homology is forced for the exteriors of homotopy-sphere closings by Mayer–Vietoris or Alexander duality. The assumptions on an entering assembly may further constrain the possible \(X\); no converse construction from the exterior inequalities is claimed.

- Equality, [E03](../../notes/E03_EQUALITY.md). Conformal developing charts classify the reflection and give the marked product at zero energy. With positive Weyl there is no flat conformal structure to reflect.
- Stability and twisted examples, [E05](../../notes/E05_TWISTED_EXTERIORS.md). The homotopy-filling odd-lens examples have noncyclic groups and doubles containing a rank-two free abelian subgroup, as energy tends to zero. They disprove a topology-near-equality approach without a scale-invariant lower bound, and their periodic action recognizes their own fillings. Dropping the homotopy hypothesis instead gives trivial counterexamples as described there.
- Marked nonperiodic monodromy. The naive fixed-path collapse with an invariant density has negative double Yamabe sign at small length when the shear is nonzero. A metric-preserving punctured monodromy gives a periodic representative up to boundary-preserving isotopy. An unpointed finite-order action on a closed fiber cannot be substituted for one on the puncture.
- Relative curvature pinching, [group test](../../notes/RELATIVE_PINCHING.md). A genuinely stronger budget in units of \(Y_D^2\) forces a cyclic peripheral group and a standard unmarked double. The absolute bound lacks this ratio, and even the unmarked classification supplies no diffeomorphism of the half.
- A section dual to the meridian. An integral generator gives a map \(X\to S^1\); since the boundary restriction is primitive for a homotopy-sphere exterior one can arrange the projection on the boundary, then take a smooth regular level. The component meeting the boundary has an \(S^2\) end and other closed components may occur. Even in the product fibration of E05 the capped section can have nontrivial finite fundamental group. A single regular or minimizing section gives neither the diffeomorphism type of the cut cobordism nor the return map. The absolute energy inequality tested above has no term controlling their handles.

A direct construction on a named nonperiodic marked exterior would reopen the question with an umbilic collar, a proved positive first conformal-Laplacian eigenvalue and a whole-exterior upper energy bound, or a relative compactness/recognition theorem could replace that construction. The positive Bach-flat attainment result in 06 concerns a closed homotopy-sphere class after entry. Its hypotheses do not supply a reflection-preserving metric on the marked double with Euler characteristic zero. An unconstrained search over closed metrics would belong to 07, and could not verify the fixed exterior or its marking by itself.

The [E09 independent reading](../../reviews/E09_EXTERIOR_REPLAY.md) tested an omitted bounded variable-lapse collapse by absorbing the lapse into a conformal scaling. The [E12 review](../../reviews/E12_RELATIVE_PINCHING.md) tested the meridian surgery in the *unmarked* standard double obtained under relative pinching. It gives \(M_j\#\overline{M_j}\cong S^4\) for either framing, locating a smooth Schoenflies problem on the actual punctured filling. A statement that the undoubled \(M_j\) is standard needs the smooth type of that half. Neither branch applies a cancellation law for connected sums of smooth homotopy spheres.

**Fibered exteriors.** A regular fiber class carries more information than an arbitrary section: its cyclic cover is a product with the closed doubled fiber. The stable hypersurface argument tested on that cover excludes every aspherical prime in the capped fiber, independently of length, shear, or a Weyl bound. Conversely a positive *reflected* double exists for every surviving prime type by choosing a path of doubling three-metrics and traversing it sufficiently slowly. The mean energy at finite length is governed by an exact three-coefficient test. In the subclass of one-sided homogeneous spherical fibers the actual punctured monodromy has a periodic representative, including point pushes, so both homotopy fillings are standard. A marked type-II double-action prism fiber survives these exclusions; the metric path and measured coefficients meeting the absolute bound have not been supplied.
