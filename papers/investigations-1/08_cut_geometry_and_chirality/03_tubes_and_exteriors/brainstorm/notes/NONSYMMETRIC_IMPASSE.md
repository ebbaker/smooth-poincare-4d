# Which nonsymmetric tubes are still untested

27 November 2026 for Edward B. Baker III, OpenAI assistance. Inputs bc2c1bb, the [earlier assessment](../../reviews/BEFORE_IMPASSE.md), and new returns from LED, SEAM, SLIDE and GEO. These are obstructions to particular constructions. The unrestricted existence question has no global negative answer from these estimates.

**Nonabelian rotations.** On a Fuchsian compactified round-fiber exterior over a surface with boundary, one surgery cannot kill free rank greater than one. On the two actual other native exteriors from SLIDE, the indicated projective images really are nonabelian and their meridians give a quantitative cap budget. Both fillings are independently recognized by the smooth Gluck extension. A flat bundle over a four-dimensional exterior is not an identification of the metric on that four-manifold with a two-dimensional base and sphere fibers. Meridional curvature flux can constrain a prescribed metric but supplies no such identification. A new example would require a marked exterior outside those known fillings with a geometric realization of the peripheral connection.

**Positive exterior Weyl energy.** The precise recognition problem with an umbilic sphere tube and positive doubles is isolated in the [positive-energy impasse](POSITIVE_ENERGY_IMPASSE.md). For nonumbilic tubes the Gauss–Bonnet boundary curvature need not even have the intended sign, as in the exact round join. Unconstrained perturbation of a known standard model can produce positive energy without producing a new smooth type.

**Projective-plane tubes.** A locally conformally flat one-sided collar, or a regular half-flat Einstein one, is excluded at every umbilic quaternionic face in the band. An intrinsically admissible Berger face with a long collapsing axis forces a supercritical sign for all aligned biaxial paths. The same moment estimate acquires an indefinite correction in the triaxial problem, and testing that correction on supported perturbations gives both signs. With a short collapsing axis, positive-scalar fillings with the prescribed exact face exist. Passing through an intermediate round face in the constructed cap already loses the budget. The direct triaxial trial changes that premise and tests a family avoiding that intermediate face. The switched two-disk model is a standard-sphere control. To test an unrecognized type, its complement would have to differ from a second copy of the standard disk bundle or another recognized filling. Budget and boundary maps on a specified nonstandard-unknown complement are missing.

For the concrete short-axis filling question, the input that would decide the open comparison is a smooth marked filling with positive Neumann conformal Laplacian and mean \(e_W<4\pi^2\), or a uniform lower bound for that entire filling class. At \(|\eta|=5/12\) the signed difference has magnitude \(5\pi^2\). Passing to the connected double cover of the disk bundle and doubling it gives a positive four-manifold with Euler characteristic four and second Betti number two. The positive-double inequality on that cover gives \(e_W\ge 8\pi^2/3\). Consequently the smaller signed energy of a qualifying cap is in \([\pi^2/6,3\pi^2/2)\), the larger in \([31\pi^2/6,13\pi^2/2)\). In particular a globally half-flat cap with this boundary cannot have a positive double, although a local half-flat collar exists. A generic width search on the raw round model, which has scalar-flat nonumbilic principal orbits, does not test this filling problem. The exact cap test and any subsequent closed trial have distinct owners.

On the incoming concrete model the [direct test](../../notes/E17_DIRECT_TRIAXIAL.md) gives a positive Neumann cap and \(8/3\le F_W(h_{\sqrt3})/\pi^2\le7.5575\). The two profiles are separated by the presence or absence of an intermediate round face. A different analytic route from the [collar reading](../../reviews/E15_QUATERNIONIC_COLLAR.md) gives an explicit half-flat positive-scalar germ with the required induced metric. Following it all the way to a smooth core while keeping it half-flat would contradict the positive-double lower bound above; a useful deformation would have to acquire controlled energy in the smaller sign before the core. The actual [two-disk smooth type](../../notes/E18_QUATERNIONIC_PAIR.md) is standard for every distinct-kernel marking. Thus improving the short-axis cap would establish a new marked geometric model, with no new recognition of a homotopy sphere.

The positive half-flat germ has two explicit maximal directions in the displayed gauge. In increasing \(v\) its axis length tends to one as \(v\to+\infty\), at infinite arclength. Continuing backwards with \(x=e^{-2v}\), its squared axis length is \(1+4x-2x^2\). It first vanishes at \(x=1+\sqrt6/2\), where \(c_s=\dot z/2=4x(x-1)=6+2\sqrt6\). The corresponding finite-distance cone has the wrong slope even for collapse of that long axis (the smooth slope is four). The desired disk collapses a different, short axis. The explicit half-flat solution thus cannot be used unchanged as a core at either end. Departing from it carries a new chiral energy cost.
