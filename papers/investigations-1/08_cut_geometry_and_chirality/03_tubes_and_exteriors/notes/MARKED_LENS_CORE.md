**Result.** The regular knot \(\ell\) obtained by projecting the marked section is a Heegaard core of the order-three lens space \(|S^3/\Lambda|\). Its two basic underlying fillings are \(S^3\) and \(\mathbb{RP}^3\). The original singular knot survives in both.

Use \(q\mapsto q i q^{-1}\). The left tetrahedral rotations act by even coordinate sign changes and cyclic permutations. The right binary-dihedral factor adds the antipodal map. The underlying base is the spherical octant modulo cyclic permutation, a disk. Its center is an order-three cone point and its boundary is a mirror with one order-two corner. On the marked great circle \(C=p_0e^{js}\), the Hopf projection goes twice round the equator perpendicular to \((1,1,1)\). Taking absolute coordinates sends this equator to the medial triangle where the largest coordinate is the sum of the other two. Each point in its cyclic quotient has six preimages on the equator, including the single ordinary mirror contact. The Hopf map on \(C\) has degree two, so the 12 preimages are precisely its \(\langle J\rangle\)-orbit. Thus \(\ell\) maps bijectively to the projected simple loop. The loop misses the center and the corner and surrounds the center once.

At the lift \((1,-1,0)/\sqrt2\) of the contact, take the tangent \((1,1,-2)\). After folding the second coordinate, its component along the ordinary mirror is \((1,-1,0)\). In a short mirror chart the total underlying space is a product of that longitudinal coordinate with
\[
 D=\big([-\delta,\delta]\times S^1\big)/((y,\phi)\sim(-y,-\phi)).
\]
This orientable quotient has one boundary and Euler characteristic \((0+2)/2=1\), hence is a disk. The marked arc is a graph over the longitudinal coordinate. Replace it rel ends by a graph that projects over an interior smoothing of the contact, joining the required circle lifts. Paths in \(D\) with the same endpoints are homotopic rel ends, and every intermediate graph is embedded. Smooth the graph in the ordinary smooth structure. The resulting knot is a section over an interior simple loop. This operation can cross a strand of the singular knot in the disk chart.

The torus over the smoothed loop bounds a solid torus on the central side. Its single exceptional fibre has multiplicity three. The element \(e^{n\pi/3}\), \(n=(i+j+k)/\sqrt3\), rotates the generic Hopf fibre through \(\pi/3\) modulo its period \(\pi/17\) at the central point, giving order three. A radial segment on the outside to a noncorner mirror point has the disk \(D\) as inverse image. Its boundary is the regular fibre \(f\). Compress along this disk. The sphere bounds a ball by irreducibility of the lens space. The central solid torus with the 2-handle attached has \(H_1=C_3\) because \(f\) winds three times there, so the ball is on the other side. Undoing the compression gives an outer solid torus with meridian \(f\). The smoothed knot on the boundary torus meets \(f\) once. It is therefore isotopic to the outer core. This argument handles the corner by choosing the disk away from it. The quotient and tangle framework are described by [Mecchia–Seppi, §5](https://arts.units.it/retrieve/handle/11368/2838654/163924/mecchia-seppi-revised.pdf).

## The remaining singular knot and its cyclic lifts

Use \(\mu_0=a,\ \lambda_0=-3a-\mu\), oriented meridian and zero longitude of the dual unknot in the basic \(S^3\) filling. The native slopes are \(\eta_s=a+s\mu\); up to overall sign they are \((3s-1)\mu_0+s\lambda_0\). Thus the ordinary filling for every integer \(s\) is \((3s-1)/s\) surgery on the unknot, with \(s=0\) the unfilled-meridian convention. The original filling is \(+3\), and \(s=1\) gives \(+2\). E48 computes the abelianization of the orbifold with its regular dual core as \(C_{2|3s-1|}\). This differs from the degree-12 branched completion of the 68-link surgery.

Let \(S\) denote the original singular knot carried along by ambient isotopy into this unknot exterior \(V\). Its winding has magnitude three, by E54. Let \(p=|3s-1|\); since \(\gcd(3,p)=1\), the full inverse image of \(S\) in the cyclic degree-\(p\) cover of \(V\) is one knot. Filling that cover along the primitive lift \({\rm sgn}(3s-1)\widetilde\mu_0+s\widetilde\lambda_0\) gives \(S^3\), the ordinary universal cover of the lens filling, and leaves a single knot \(K_s\) of weight two. Here \(\widetilde\mu_0\) maps to \(p\mu_0\), and \(\widetilde\lambda_0\) to \(\lambda_0\). For \(s=1\) this is the double cyclic lift followed by a \(+1\) filling. The orbifold group fits into extensions with successive kernels
\[
 \pi_1\Sigma_2(K_s)\ \triangleleft\ \pi_1^{\rm orb}(S^3,K_s;2)\
 \triangleleft\ \pi_1^{\rm orb}(O(\eta_s))
\]
and respective quotients \(C_2,C_p\). This follows by ordinary covering on the ambient lens space, then the twofold branched cover on its knot. Hence the outer group is cyclic iff \(\pi_1\Sigma_2(K_s)=1\): if the kernel is trivial the order-two group is central and its quotient is cyclic, while the converse uses the computed full abelianization. For \(s=0\), a nontrivial odd cyclic group of the double branched cover instead gives the odd-dihedral case: the meridian splits the order-two quotient, and its action is inversion because the full abelianization is \(C_2\). A determinant alone does not establish either fundamental group.

The next calculation needs the three-dimensional pattern \(S\subset V\). A drawing or triangulation must carry the two fixed points in the mirror disk during the isotopy. Erasing these strands and putting back a singular core would change the problem. Recognition of a filled orbifold still needs the remaining hypotheses of the four-dimensional surgery construction.
