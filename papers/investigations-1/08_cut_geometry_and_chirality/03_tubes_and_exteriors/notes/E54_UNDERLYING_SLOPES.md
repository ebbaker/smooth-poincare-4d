# SP4-CUT-TUBE-E54 — The underlying lens space and the marked slopes

27 November 2026. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI assistance. Analytical follow-up to the fixed [E39 marking](E39_PRISM_FILLINGS.md) and [E48 linking and slope calculation](E48_LINK_SURGERY.md), investigation baseline bc2c1bbccc1d244a9d456ffe2fcaf64e368290b0. Actual helper model and effort are unavailable.

The underlying orbit space is the oriented lens space given by \(+3\) surgery on the unknot. The two marked fillings are the meridional and \(+2\) fillings of a single dual knot in an integral homology sphere. The singular locus is one order-two knot of known homology in the marked exterior and in both fillings. The cyclic or odd-dihedral condition for the first orbifold would force the marked knot to be a Heegaard core of the unfilled lens space. The second orbifold is cyclic exactly when the singular knot in that filling has solid-torus exterior. The computations below leave both knot types open.

## Underlying quotient and its singular circle

Write \(O=S^3/\Lambda\) for the orbifold, \(M=|O|\) for its underlying oriented manifold and \(\Lambda=T^*\times_{\{\pm1\}} Q_{68}\), acting by \(q\mapsto A q B^{-1}\) as in E39. Let \(z=e^{2\pi i/17}\), so \(Q_{68}=\langle z,j\rangle\). The even right subgroup acts freely. A fixed-point element therefore has the form
\[
[(A,B)],\qquad A\in\{\pm i,\pm j,\pm k\},\quad B\in z^{\mathbb Z}\{\pm j\}.
\]
The condition is equality of left and right real parts, so all these elements have a fixed circle. There are \(6\cdot34/2=102\) such involutions in the central product. Two of their fixed circles cannot meet, because their product would give a fixed point for a nonidentity element with even right factor. They form one conjugacy class: \(T^*\) permutes the cyclic quaternion axes and both signs, and conjugation by \(z^u\) sends \(z^r j\) to \(z^{r+2u}j\). A simultaneous sign can be absorbed in the central product. The underlying singular set is thus one knot \(S\), with rotation isotropy \(C_2\). The local half-turn quotient in the normal disk is a topological disk, with the smooth underlying chart given by squaring the normal complex coordinate.

The normal subgroup generated by the stabilizers is \(K=Q_8\times_{\{\pm1\}}Q_{68}\). All fixed-point elements belong to \(K\). The product of those represented by \((i,j)\) and \((i,zj)\) gives the class of \((1,z^{-1})\). Products with the same right factor and distinct quaternionic left axes generate the left \(Q_8\); multiplying one of the original involutions by a left factor then gives \([(1,j)]\). Hence these elements generate \(K\). Killing the singular meridians in the orbifold group gives the ordinary underlying group by van Kampen on the singular solid torus, so
\[
\pi_1(M)=\Lambda/K\cong C_3.
\]
By elliptization \(M\) is a spherical three-manifold. A free orthogonal cyclic action of order three is a rotation in each of two orthogonal planes, so the underlying manifold is a lens space of order three. Its oriented type is specified by the linking calculation below. [Perelman, finite extinction](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0307245) · [Elliptization statement in Aschenbrenner–Friedl–Wilton, Theorem 1.7.3](https://www.maths.gla.ac.uk/~mpowell/Aschenbrenner-Friedl-Wilton-3-manifold-groups-final-version-031115.pdf).

## Peripheral basis, distance and linking forms

Let \(E=M\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu\ell\), with the E39 peripheral basis \((a,\mu)\). Here \(a\) is the marked longitude from the stabilizer step and \(\mu\) is the positive normal meridian. With the chosen \(S^3\) orientation, \((a,\mu)\) is positive on the boundary of \(E\). This follows locally from the oriented coordinates \((x,r,\theta)\) and the exterior outward normal \(-\partial_r\). By E48, the orbifold exterior has abelianization \(\langle a,\mu\mid 6a+2\mu=0\rangle\). An old singular meridian is the element \(3a+\mu\), so forgetting the isotropy gives
\[
H_1(E)=\mathbb Z[a],\qquad [\mu]=-3[a],\qquad
\lambda=3a+\mu.
\]
The last curve is the primitive rational longitude of \(\ell\). In particular \([\ell]\) generates \(H_1(M)\) and has order three.

The first marked filling is \(M_0=E(a)\). For its dual knot orient the peripheral basis by \(\mu_0=a\), \(\lambda_0=-\lambda\), so \((\lambda_0,\mu_0)\) is positive on \(\partial E\) and \(\lambda_0\) bounds in \(E\). In this convention the slopes are

| Manifold | Slope in the given basis, up to overall sign | Surgery relative to the dual knot in \(M_0\) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| \(M_0\) | \(a\) | \(\infty\) |
| \(M_1\) | \(a+\mu\) | \(+2\) |
| \(M\) | \(\mu\) | \(+3\) |

In general the marked slope \(a+s\mu\) in this surgery convention has coefficient \((3s-1)/s\) for nonzero \(s\). All three displayed slopes have pairwise distance one on \(\partial E\). The distances from \(\lambda\) are \(1,2,3\) respectively. Upstairs the two primitive marked slopes have distance twelve in each \(S^3\) torus, with distances two and six in the two intermediate lattices of E48. The ordinary exterior homology gives \(H_1(M_0)=0\), \(H_1(M_1)=C_2\), and \(H_1(M)=C_3\). The Cyclic Surgery Theorem has a distance-at-most-one conclusion on an irreducible non-Seifert exterior. Thus the measured distances meet its conclusion whenever its hypotheses hold and cannot obstruct the proposed simultaneous cyclic underlying fillings. [Culler–Gordon–Luecke–Shalen, introduction and Cyclic Surgery Theorem](https://marc-culler.info/static/home/papers/CyclicSurgery.pdf).

For a slope \(p\mu_0+q\lambda_0\), the linking form on the surviving \(\mu_0\) is \(-q/p\) modulo \(1\). Indeed subtract \(q\) copies of a bounding 2-chain for \(\lambda_0\) from the attached meridian disk to bound \(p\mu_0\). A push of \(\mu_0\) into \(E\) meets the first chain with algebraic intersection \(+1\), by the positive boundary basis, and misses the disk. For the two nontrivial homology groups the pairings on a generator are \(-1/2\) and \(-1/3\), respectively. The latter selects the orientation of \(M\) equal to \(+3\) surgery on the unknot, since the two orientations of the order-three lens space have distinct linking forms. Here \(L(3,1)\) means this \(+3\) convention. [Surgery orientation convention in Kronheimer–Mrowka–Ozsváth–Szabó, §1](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~kronheim/lens.pdf).

## The original singular knot in the marked exterior

Use \(F=\{q:q j q^{-1}=i\}\), the fixed circle of \([(i,j)]\) upstairs, oriented by \(q'=qj\). Its stabilizer is its involution's centralizer, since the different fixed circles are disjoint. A right factor can send \(j\) to \(+j\) only when it belongs to \(\langle j\rangle\), and it cannot send \(j\) to \(-j\) since the relevant rotation angles are integral multiples of \(2\pi/17\). Thus the stabilizer is \(\langle i\rangle\times_{\{\pm1\}}\langle j\rangle\), of order eight, and \(F\to S\) has degree four with the resulting orientation of \(S\). This stabilizer lies in \(K\), so the unbranched universal cover of \(M\) has three lifts of \(S\). The class of \(S\) also vanishes in \(\pi_1 M\).

For each oriented lift \(C_{u,v}\) of \(\ell\), the normal-projection calculation of E48 gives
\[
\operatorname{lk}(F,C_{u,v})=\operatorname{sign}(i\cdot v-j\cdot u).
\]
Two tetrahedral rows have \(i\cdot v=1/\sqrt3\), and two have \(i\cdot v=-1/\sqrt3\). In a positive row the negative links occur at cyclic distances \(0,1,2\), giving \(12\) positive and \(5\) negative entries. In a negative row the positive links occur at \(6,7,8\), giving \(6\) positive and \(11\) negative entries. The thresholds follow from
\(\cos(4\pi/17)>1/\sqrt3>\cos(6\pi/17)\) and
\(\cos(10\pi/17)>-1/\sqrt3>\cos(12\pi/17)\).
For the last strict comparison \(\cos(5\pi/17)>\sin(\pi/5)>1/\sqrt3\). The sum of all signed links is \(2(12-5)+2(6-11)=4\). In \(H_1(E)\), each oriented meridian upstairs pushes to \(\mu\) and \(F\) pushes to \(4S\). Therefore
\[
[S]=[\mu]=-3[a]\quad\text{in }H_1(E).
\]
In particular the original singular knot is null-homologous in \(M\) and represents the generator in \(H_1(M_1)\). With the orientation above its rational linking with \(\ell\) in \(M\) is \(+1\), since pairing with \(\ell\) sends the positive meridian to \(1\).

There is an integral peripheral consequence on the complement of both knots. Let \(Z=M\setminus\operatorname{int}(\nu\ell\cup\nu S)\), and let \(\sigma\) be the positive meridian of \(S\). The pair sequence gives the subgroup \(\mathbb Z^2[\mu,\sigma]\) and quotient \(C_3[a]\). The bounding relative chain in \(E\) with boundary \(\lambda\) pairs to \(-1\) with a push of \(a\), using the ordered boundary basis \((a,\mu)\). Since \([S]=-3[a]\), its intersection with \(S\) is \(+3\). Removing those intersections gives the relation \(\lambda-3\sigma=0\) in \(Z\), yielding
\[
H_1(Z)=\langle a,\mu,\sigma\mid 3a+\mu-3\sigma=0\rangle,\qquad
H_1(M_s\setminus\operatorname{int}\nu S)=
\langle\mu,\sigma\mid(1-3s)\mu-3\sigma=0\rangle .
\]
The latter is infinite cyclic, with \(\sigma\) having index \(1\) at \(s=0\) and index \(2\) at \(s=1\). Adding \(2\sigma=0\) recovers the E48 orbifold abelianizations and the nonzero order-two isotropy classes.

## Effect on cyclic and odd-dihedral recognition

Let \(Q_s=O(a+s\mu)\) retain the old order-two knot and give the new core trivial isotropy. Its abelianization has order two at \(s=0\) and order four at \(s=1\). The local isotropy survives in each abelianization. The recognition inputs are finite cyclic orbifold group, or odd-dihedral group with an actual order-two exceptional orbit, through the two-stage construction of E39. [Jabłonowski, preprint, Propositions 8.4, 8.7 and 8.8](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.29839).

- For \(s=0\), a cyclic group must be \(C_2\). An odd-dihedral group has the old singular meridian represented by a reflection, since it is an involution with nonzero abelianization. Killing that meridian makes the underlying group trivial in either case, so \(M_0\cong S^3\). The actual dual knot in this \(S^3\) has \(+3\) surgery orientation-preservingly diffeomorphic to \(+3\) surgery on the unknot. Kronheimer–Mrowka–Ozsváth–Szabó, Theorem 1.1, then forces that knot to be unknotted. Hence the sought group at \(s=0\) requires \(E\) to be a solid torus and \(\ell\) to be a lens Heegaard core. Conversely a solid torus \(E\) gives \(M_0=S^3\) by its longitude distance one. For group recognition one must still determine the singular knot. More precisely the unique index-two orbifold cover of \(Q_0\) is the smooth double branched cover of \(M_0\) along \(S\). If it has finite cyclic fundamental group, its local involution splits the extension, and \(H_1^{\rm orb}(Q_0)=C_2\) forces inversion on the cyclic kernel. The order is odd since the coinvariants vanish. This gives the cyclic order-two or odd-dihedral group as desired. Conversely these groups give that cover a finite cyclic group. [Kronheimer–Mrowka–Ozsváth–Szabó, Theorem 1.1](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2007/165-2/p03).
- For \(s=1\), the abelianization excludes odd dihedral and the cyclic candidate is \(C_4\). In that case the underlying quotient group is \(C_2\), so \(M_1\cong\mathbb{RP}^3\). In fact \(\pi_1^{\rm orb}(Q_1)\cong C_4\) is equivalent to \(S\) in \(M_1\) having solid-torus exterior. For sufficiency the ordinary exterior group is \(\mathbb Z\), in which the meridian has index two as above, so imposing its square yields \(C_4\). For necessity the local order-two subgroup injects, hence the universal orbifold cover is a compact simply connected smooth three-manifold, thus \(S^3\). Dinkelbach–Leeb give smooth orthogonal realization of its finite action. A cyclic generator acts in two orthogonal planes by angles \(\pi\) and \(\pm\pi/2\), because its square fixes exactly one circle and the generator fixes none. The quotient of the complement of the first axis's invariant solid torus is a disk mapping torus, which is a solid torus. [Dinkelbach–Leeb, Theorem E](https://msp.org/gt/2009/13-2/gt-v13-n2-p15-p.pdf).

The first recognition test therefore needs the concrete underlying knot exterior and then the double branched cover of the singular knot after filling. The second needs the actual singular-knot exterior after its slope. The distance and homology data determine neither of these exteriors.
