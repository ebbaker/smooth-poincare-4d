## 1. Exact words for the actual meridian

Use \(z=e^{2\pi i/17}\) for the right cyclic factor and keep \((s+1,q)\sim(s,Jq)\) for the mapping torus convention. The positive meridian \(t\) is the actual oriented section from E39. Its lift shifts time by \(+1\) and acts spatially by \(J^{-1}\). Thus conjugation by \(t\) on \(\Gamma=Q_8\times C_{17}\) acts by \(i\mapsto k\mapsto j\mapsto i\), and takes \(z\mapsto z^{-1}\). The complement of the capped circle has the same fundamental group as the capped mapping torus, since the circle has codimension three. Its peripheral circle represents \(t\) through the given section marking. Take
\[
u=(-i,z)\in Q_8\times C_{17}.
\]
In the action on \(S^3\) this is \(q\mapsto(-i)qz^{-1}\). It has order \(68\), with \(u^{17}=-i\) and \(u^4=z^4\). The two relations are
\[
t u^2 t^{-1}=u^{-2},\qquad
t^2u^{17}t^{-2}=u^{17}\,t u^{17}t^{-1}.
\]
The first holds since \(u^2=-z^2\). In the second, the three quaternionic words are \(x=u^{17}=-i,\ y=t x t^{-1}=-k,\ t^2 x t^{-2}=-j=xy\). Together \(x,y,z^4,t\) generate the whole semidirect group.

To prove there are no hidden relations in the presentation, now work in its abstract group. Put \(v=u^2,\ x=u^{17},\ y=txt^{-1},\ c=v^{17}\). Since \(tvt^{-1}=v^{-1}\) and \(x\) commutes with \(v\), also \(y\) commutes with \(v\). The squares satisfy
\[
x^2=c,\qquad y^2=c^{-1},\qquad (xy)^2=c.
\]
The last identity is \(t^2x^2t^{-2}=c\). As \(c\) commutes with \(x,y\), these give \(yx=cxy\) and \(yxy^{-1}=cx\). Conjugating \(x^2=c\) by \(y\) gives \(c=c^3\), hence \(c^2=1\). Thus \(x,y\) are a quotient of quaternionic generators. The element \(Z=u^4=v^2\) commutes with them and satisfies \(Z^{17}=1\). It is inverted by \(t\). Conjugation by \(t\) preserves \(\langle x,y\rangle\), carrying \(x\mapsto y\mapsto xy\mapsto x\). Finally \(u=xZ^{-4}\). Consequently the abstract group is the semidirect product of \(\langle x,y,Z\rangle\), of order at most \(8\cdot17\), with the group generated by \(t\). The time-degree map exists because the relators have zero \(t\)-exponent sum. The already verified map onto the actual group is an isomorphism on the subgroup of order \(136\) and on the time quotient, so is a marked isomorphism.

Yoshikawa's two fibre generators in theorem part (B) can also be matched algebraically. Write \(a_{\rm Y}=-j=xy,\ b_{\rm Y}=iz=u^{35}\) for his \(n=17\) words. They give \(b_{\rm Y}^2=(a_{\rm Y}b_{\rm Y})^2\) and \(a_{\rm Y}^2b_{\rm Y}^{34}=1\). The actual action sends them to \(b_{\rm Y}^{-17}\) and \((b_{\rm Y}a_{\rm Y})^{-1}\), respectively. This matches his displayed automorphism on the fibre group. [Yoshikawa, *On 2-knot groups with the finite commutator subgroups*, theorem part (B), pp. 327–328](https://da.lib.kobe-u.ac.jp/da/kernel/E0001550/E0001550.pdf).

## 2. Edition check

Hillman's *Optimal presentations for solvable 2-knot groups*, p. 132, gives the two-generator argument for \(Q(8)\times C_n\) and the second relation in the meridional form above. In reading the first displayed relation, its proof uses \(t u^2 t^{-1}=u^{-2}\), the form checked directly in §1. This distinction matters because a literal double inverse power of \(t\) in the first conjugation would have nonzero \(t\)-exponent sum. [Hillman, *Optimal presentations for solvable 2-knot groups*, Bull. Austral. Math. Soc. 57 (1998), pp. 129–133](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/202FC5A4B7C916D90FF714224D9A9063/S0004972700031476a.pdf/optimal_presentations_for_solvable_2knot_groups.pdf#page=4).

The published 2002 monograph §15.4, p. 305, instead writes its time generator as \(s\) here, with \(s x s^{-1}=x s^2 x s^{-2}\), and then identifies \(s x\) as the meridional choice. This agrees with the present marking by taking \(s=t x^{-1}\). Indeed \(s x s^{-1}=y\) and \(s y s^{-1}=(xy)^{-1}=x^{-1}y\). The latter uses the quaternionic relations already proved. The 2022 and 2023 author-hosted revisions give a three-generator deficiency-minus-one construction from a balanced fibre presentation in §15.1, pp. 295–296. The frozen locator §15.5, pp. 222–223, did not identify the two-generator display in those revisions. The marked proof in §1 establishes the order-seventeen presentation independently of that edition difference. [Hillman, 2002 Monograph, §15.4](https://msp.org/gtm/2002/05/gtm-2002-05-003p.pdf) · [2022 revision](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/jonh/fmgk22.pdf#page=309) · [2023 revision](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/jonh/fmgk23.pdf#page=309).

## 3. Relative free-group slide sequence

Use the ordered relators
\[
R_1=t u^2t^{-1}u^2,\qquad
R_2=t^2x t^{-1}x^{-1}t^{-1}x^{-1},\qquad
T=t,\quad x=u^{17}.
\]
Here \(R_2\) is the freely reduced word obtained by multiplying the second relation's left side by the inverse of its right side. To delete a displayed occurrence \(t^\epsilon\) in a relator \(W=Xt^\epsilon Y\), right multiply \(W\) by \(Y^{-1}T^{-\epsilon}Y\), yielding \(XY\). In Andrews–Curtis operations one temporarily inverts and conjugates \(T\) as needed, performs the multiplication, then restores \(T\). The relator \(T\) is unchanged after every such step, and neither generator changes.

| Relator | Successive words after deleting marked letters and freely reducing |
| --- | --- |
| \(R_1\) | \(u^2t^{-1}u^2\), \(u^4\) |
| \(R_2\) | \(t x t^{-1}x^{-1}t^{-1}x^{-1}\), \(x t^{-1}x^{-1}t^{-1}x^{-1}\), \(t^{-1}x^{-1}\), \(x^{-1}=u^{-17}\) |

Next right multiply the second relator by the first four times. Its exponents go from \(-17\) through \(-13,-9,-5,-1\). Invert it to \(u\). Right multiply the first relator by the inverse of this second relator four times, taking exponent \(4\) through \(3,2,1,0\). The endpoint is \((1,u,t)\) in the original free group on \((t,u)\), using the meridian relation only as a relator, with no quotient substitution assumed during the sequence. The trivial relator records the surplus presentation cell after the two generator–relator pairs are deleted. This is an algebraic calculation for the same marked meridian in both smooth framings.
