**Result.** Two copies of the standard normal disk bundle of a projective plane, with any smooth boundary identification, give exactly two smooth types. Equal transported peripheral kernels give the identity double with fundamental group \(C_2\). Distinct kernels give the standard \(S^4\), with the two disk-bundle pieces identified with the two round matrix-sphere chambers. SEAM's exact \(i,j\) switch is the distinct-kernel representative. This classification assumes both sides are the standard disk bundle.

## 1. The smooth normal slice and the two circle descriptions

Let \(V=\operatorname{Sym}_0(3,\mathbb R)\) with Euclidean form \(\operatorname{tr}(AB)\). The unit sphere in \(V\) is the round \(S^4\), with the \(SO(3)\) action \(A\mapsto gAg^{-1}\). Unit quaternions \(G=SU(2)\) act through their adjoint rotations on the imaginary axes \(i,j,k\). At a distinct-eigenvalue diagonal matrix the effective isotropy is the group \(V_4\) of diagonal sign matrices of determinant \(+1\), with inverse image \(Q_8\) in \(G\). At a singular orbit with simple eigenline the \(i\)-axis, the effective isotropy preserves this unoriented line and is \(O(2)\). Its inverse image in \(G\) is
\[
C_i=\{\exp(\alpha i)\},\qquad H_i=C_i\cup jC_i .
\]
The singular orbit is the space \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) of unoriented simple eigenlines. Its tangent variations mix \(i\) with the repeated eigenspace \(i^\perp\). Its normal two-plane within \(S^4\) is the space of traceless symmetric forms on \(i^\perp\).

Write such a form in an oriented basis of \(i^\perp\) as a linear combination of \(\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)\) and the symmetric off-diagonal matrix. A physical rotation of the plane by angle \(\lambda\) rotates these coordinates by \(2\lambda\). The quaternion \(\exp(\alpha i)\) turns the physical plane by \(2\alpha\). Thus the slice representation \(\rho:H_i\to O(2)\) sends it to rotation by \(4\alpha\). The quaternion \(j\) gives a reflection in the slice. Choose the real unit normal vector \(z_0\) fixed by this reflection. The stabilizer of \(z_0\) in \(H_i\) is \(Q_8\) and the connected rotation kernel is \(C_4=\{\pm1,\pm i\}\). The circle of actual normal directions has period \(\pi/2\) in \(\alpha\), so its ordinary polar angle is \(4\alpha\). In the unit-quaternion orbit length convention, smooth collapse has length coefficient with initial derivative \(4\), since a small normal circle of radius \(r\) has circumference \(2\pi r+O(r^3)\). The effective \(SO(3)\) circle has normal weight two. The \(SU(2)\) weight four uses the entire lifted stabilizer.

Use the left quotient convention \(Y=Q_8\backslash G\). The associated disk model is
\[
W_i=(G\times D^2)/\bigl((g,z)\sim(hg,\rho(h)z),\ h\in H_i\bigr),
\qquad
[g]\in Y\longmapsto [g,z_0]\in\partial W_i .
\]
The diagonal action is free on the \(G\) factor, so \(W_i\), including its zero section, is a smooth disk bundle. The nontrivial component of \(H_i\) reverses the normal fibre orientation and the base orientation. The total space is orientable, as also follows from its embedding in the matrix sphere. The boundary projection to the core is \([g]\mapsto[g^{-1}ig]\), with ordinary circle fibre \(Q_8\backslash H_i\). The inclusion \(q_i:\pi_1(Y)\to\pi_1(W_i)\) is the quotient \(Q_8\to C_2\) with kernel \(\langle i\rangle\). Indeed a fibre loop is \([\exp(\alpha i)g]\) for \(0\le\alpha\le\pi/2\); its lift ends at the generator of that subgroup. The oriented-base double cover of the circle bundle has total space \(C_4\backslash G\to S^2\), so its Euler number has magnitude four by the homotopy sequence. Pullback doubles the Euler evaluation in the orientation local system. Thus the normal Euler number downstairs has magnitude two, the standard projective-plane normal bundle.

There is also a different, Seifert, circle action. Right multiplication by a fixed circle \(C_i\) commutes with the left \(Q_8\) action and descends to \(Y\). Its generic kernel is \(\{\pm1\}\). The effective base orbifold is \(S^2/V_4\), with exactly three order-two cone points corresponding to the three unoriented axes. Indeed the right-circle quotient upstairs is \(G/C_i\cong S^2\), and the induced left action is \(V_4\) acting by half turns. On an exceptional fibre the lifted right-circle stabilizer has order four, so the effective stabilizer has order two. The short loop lifts to an element of the corresponding order-four axis subgroup in \(Q_8\). The three Seifert labels therefore detect \(\langle i\rangle,\langle j\rangle,\langle k\rangle\). The cone isotropy is isotropy of this circle action on the smooth link, and the collapsing circle of the disk bundle uses the ordinary fibration over \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) described above. The orbit-space endpoint in the matrix chamber is the radial quotient of the weight-four slice, with a reflection on an extended normal geodesic.

## 2. The exact SEAM switch

Fix the midpoint diagonal matrix and the positive-side endpoint
\[
D=\frac{\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0)}{\sqrt2},
\qquad
A_i=\frac{\operatorname{diag}(2,-1,-1)}{\sqrt6}.
\]
The fixed middle eigenline at \(D\) is the \(k\)-axis. In the chamber from \(A_i\) to \(D\), the \(j\) and \(k\) eigenvalues split and the normal disk collapses around the \(i\)-axis. The adjacent endpoint is \(A_j=\operatorname{diag}(1,-2,1)/\sqrt6\). Each sector cut off by the orbit of \(D\) has angle \(\pi/6\) in the unit diagonal circle. For example \(\operatorname{tr}(A_iD)=\sqrt3/2\). An explicit diffeomorphism from the radius-\(\pi/6\) associated disk to the \(A_i\) chamber is
\[
[g,z]\longmapsto
g^{-1}\left(\cos|z|\,A_i+
                  \frac{\sin|z|}{|z|}\,z\right)g .
\]
Here \(z\) is an actual traceless symmetric normal matrix with its Euclidean norm, and the expression at zero is smooth. Take the positive real direction to be \(\operatorname{diag}(0,-1,1)/\sqrt2\), so the boundary real vector maps to \(D\). The formula is invariant under the associated-bundle relation. It is the normal exponential near the zero section. Away from that section the ordered eigenvalues determine a unique radius, and the stabilizer is exactly \(Q_8\) on the standard diagonal ray. The radial coordinate and principal orbit provide a smooth inverse there by the smooth distinct-eigenvalue and eigenspace projections. Thus this is a smooth chamber chart up to the principal boundary. Applying the same construction at \(A_j\) gives the other chamber.

Let \(u=(i+j)/\sqrt2\) and \(P=\operatorname{Ad}_u\in SO(3)\). Then \(P\) swaps \(i,j\), negates \(k\), and satisfies \(PDP^{-1}=-D\). The round sphere isometry
\[
J(A)=-PAP^{-1}
\]
exchanges the two chambers and fixes \(D\). It reverses ambient orientation because \(-I\) has determinant \(-1\) on the five-dimensional space \(V\), and the conjugation action of the connected group \(SO(3)\) has determinant \(+1\). It is an involution. At a principal point \(g Dg^{-1}\) it sends the parameter \(g\) to \(PgP^{-1}\). Inversion converts the natural right quotient for \(gDg^{-1}\) to the left quotient used above and commutes with this conjugation. Hence the boundary map in the SEAM convention is exactly
\[
\phi:[g]\longmapsto[ugu^{-1}],
\]
interchanging the \(i\) and \(j\) kernels and negating the remaining axis in the frame. Choose the ambient orientation so that the first chamber, a copy of \(W_i\), induces the fixed orientation of \(Y\). The restriction of \(J\) identifies its orientation-reversed copy with the second chamber. The ambient identity gluing therefore reads \(W_i\cup_\phi(-W_i)\) since \(\phi^{-1}=\phi\). This identifies the actual marked assembly and both zero sections smoothly, without using a recognition result for a homotopy four-sphere. The right-invariant lengths \((1,1,t)\) are preserved by this boundary map. In the round model the induced \(t=2\) midpoint is scalar flat and bent, so the identification supplies topology for SEAM's modified conformal boundary datum and leaves its filling estimates as separate requirements. ## 3. All smooth boundary identifications

Choose the constant-curvature-one round quotient metric on the reference \(Y\). Bamler–Kleiner give a homotopy equivalence \(\operatorname{Isom}(Y)\hookrightarrow\operatorname{Diff}(Y)\) with the smooth topology. The quaternionic quotient is compact, connected, smooth and spherical, with \(|\pi_1|=8\), within the hypotheses of their 2017 Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.2 and of their 2019 Theorem 1.3. They act on the full groups, including orientation components. Thus every smooth boundary diffeomorphism has an isotopic round isometry representative. A path in the smooth topology can be replaced by a smooth isotopy, by subdividing into small maps in local \(C^1\) exponential charts and joining them by smooth interpolations. A smooth isotopy extends into a boundary collar, so replacing the gluing by the representative preserves the smooth glued pair. [Bamler–Kleiner, *Ricci flow and diffeomorphism groups of 3-manifolds*, Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 2.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1712.06197) · [Journal version](https://doi.org/10.1090/jams/1003) · [Bamler–Kleiner, *Ricci flow and contractibility of spaces of metrics*, Theorem 1.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08710).

An orientation-preserving isometry of the round cover has the form \(g\mapsto a g b^{-1}\) for \(a,b\in SU(2)\). It normalizes the left deck group exactly when \(a\in N_{SU(2)}(Q_8)\). Normalizing \(Q_8\) means that \(\operatorname{Ad}_a\) is a signed permutation of the three imaginary coordinate axes with determinant \(+1\). The inverse image in \(SU(2)\) of the determinant-one diagonal sign changes is exactly \(Q_8\). Therefore
\[
N_{SU(2)}(Q_8)/Q_8\cong S_3.
\]
The right factor is connected and acts trivially on the permutation. An orientation-reversing round isometry of \(S^3\) has the same form composed with inversion. Inversion sends the left deck group to a right deck group, and noncentral right multiplications never coincide with left multiplications. Such an isometry cannot normalize the left \(Q_8\) action. Lifting quotient isometries to the universal cover proves that all round quotient isometries preserve orientation, hence so do all self-diffeomorphisms of the reference \(Y\). To glue with the outward-normal-first orientation, orient the two copies as \(W_i\) and \(-W_i\). The reference orientation-preserving map is then orientation reversing as a map of their two induced oriented boundaries. The underlying gluing remains orientable when both copies had originally been given the same reference orientation because one can reverse the orientation choice on a whole piece.

The kernel-preserving permutations are the \(S_2\) fixing the \(i\)-axis. They extend smoothly across \(W_i\). For an explicit extension, first extend every right translation by \([g,z]\mapsto[g b^{-1},z]\). If \(a\) preserves the axis, then \(a\in H_i\). Since \(a\) normalizes \(Q_8\), \(\rho(a)\) preserves the fixed line of \(\rho(Q_8)\), giving \(\rho(a)^{-1}z_0=\epsilon z_0\) for \(\epsilon\in\{+1,-1\}\). Hence \([ag,z_0]=[g,\epsilon z_0]\), and its left action on the boundary extends by the globally defined fibre map \([g,z]\mapsto[g,\epsilon z]\). This map preserves the orientation of \(W_i\) and fixes the zero section. A general smooth axis-preserving boundary map extends by composing with the collar isotopy. Conversely any extension across \(W_i\) preserves \(\ker q_i\) by functoriality. Thus the extendible subgroup of boundary isotopy classes is precisely the stabilizer of this axis.

The smooth type of the glued pair is constant on double cosets of this subgroup because each gluing can be changed independently by extending maps on the two sides. There are exactly two double cosets of \(S_2\) in \(S_3\), one fixing the \(i\)-axis and the other moving it. The latter contains \(\phi\). By the explicit chamber identification, all the distinct-kernel gluings give standard smooth \(S^4\) with the corresponding smooth pair of disk chambers. In the same-kernel case the gluing is absorbed by an extension and the result is the smooth identity double \(DW_i=W_i\cup_{\mathrm{id}}(-W_i)\). It can also be written as the sphere bundle \(S(E_i\oplus\underline{\mathbb R})\to\mathbb{RP}^2\), where \(E_i\) is the normal plane bundle. The hemispheres in each fibre give this smooth sphere-bundle description by a smooth radial identification. Pullback of \(E_i\) to \(S^2\) has Euler magnitude four. Its stabilization is trivial, since its clutching map has even degree in \(\pi_1SO(2)\) and maps to zero in \(\pi_1SO(3)=C_2\). Thus the universal cover of the double is diffeomorphic to \(S^2\times S^2\).

For a direct group check, pull the second quotient \(q_i\) back by the chosen boundary gluing and compare it with the first \(q_i\). Van Kampen gives \(C_2*_{Q_8}C_2\) with these two surjections. If the kernels agree, the pushout is \(C_2\). If they differ, an element in either kernel with nontrivial image in the other quotient kills the other copy of \(C_2\). Thus the group is trivial. The map from \(H_1(Y;\mathbb Z)=C_2^2\) to the sum of two copies of \(C_2\) has rank one in the first case and is an isomorphism in the second. Because each side retracts onto \(\mathbb{RP}^2\), Mayer–Vietoris then gives \(H_2=C_2\) for the double and \(H_2=0\) for the distinct-kernel case. In either case \(H_3=0\) by oriented duality and \(H_4=\mathbb Z\). The different groups distinguish the two smooth outcomes.

## 4. Primary Price passages and orientation scope

T. M. Price, *Homeomorphisms of quaternion space and projective planes in four space*, J. Austral. Math. Soc. Ser. A 23 (1977), 112–128, was checked in the published scan. On p. 113 the symbols for homeomorphism groups are defined using orientation-preserving maps. Theorem 1 and its corollary on pp. 122–124 identify their isotopy classes with \(\operatorname{Out}(Q_8)\cong S_3\). On pp. 116–117 the \(g_1\) representative preserves the meridional circle bundle and extends over the disk bundle. Section III and Theorem 2, p. 124, give the kernel stabilizer as the extendible classes. Theorem 4, p. 127, distinguishes the two gluings in the homeomorphism category using their groups and the linked projective-plane pair. These passages agree with the orientation-preserving kernel calculation and the two outcomes. Smoothness here comes from the separate smooth normal model, the explicit extensions and Bamler–Kleiner. [Price, published scan](https://www.cambridge.org/core/services/aop-cambridge-core/content/view/C8290EA8E6D966A19FF658A7AAD11334/S1446788700017407a.pdf/homeomorphisms-of-quaternion-space-and-projective-planes-in-four-space.pdf).

For provenance, the calculation does not import Price's construction of an orientation-reversing \(r\) on pp. 117–118. The scans of pp. 113, 117–118 and 127 were checked against the searchable text. The displayed covering and attaching maps give the following specific discrepancy in his solid-torus extension argument. In the actual cover \(F\) of the Klein-bottle boundary, defined by his formula on p. 113, the deck involution on \(T^2=S^1\times\partial D^2\) is \((\theta,(x,y))\mapsto(\theta+\pi,(-x,y))\). In the longitude and fibre-angle coordinates this has induced homology \(\mathrm{diag}(1,-1)\), whereas the proposed extension treats the map as a half translation isotopic to the identity on \(T^2\). The meridian of the filling solid torus, under the attaching map \((\alpha,z)\mapsto(\alpha,\rho_{2\alpha}z)\) on p. 113 with \(\rho_{2\alpha}\) the displayed planar rotation, has slope \((1,2)\) in these coordinates. The deck involution takes it to \((1,-2)\), which is neither sign of the original meridian. Hence this map does not extend over the filling solid torus. The smooth round-normalizer proof gives the needed orientation conclusion independently. It uses the fixed reference link, so it is consistent with \(J\) reversing orientation between the two matrix chambers while inducing an orientation-preserving self-map in the reference boundary marking.

The classification concerns gluings of two actual standard disk bundles and extends to either chosen orientation of the resulting standard sphere. It supplies no recognition statement for a knotted projective-plane complement or for arbitrary fillings of the quaternionic link. No metric or numerical evidence beyond the smooth matrix-sphere model enters the topology argument.
