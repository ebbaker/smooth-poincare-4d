## 1. Inner surgery with a marked pillowcase boundary

Use the E70 quotient three-ball and perform the ordinary fillings \(F^3\lambda_F^{-2}=1\) and \(\lambda_L=1\), leaving the outer order-two rational tangle unattached. Call this compact oriented orbifold \(B'\). Its singular arcs are the two original local strands \(+\) and \(-\), with their four boundary endpoints on the labelled pillowcase. Put \(q=\lambda_F\) and let \(c\) be the bottom meridian of the old \(-\) strand. The E70 eliminations up to its outer relation give
\[
\pi_1^{\mathrm{orb}}(B')=\langle q,c\mid q^4=c^2=1\rangle=C_4*C_2.
\]
Indeed \(F=q^2\), \(L=qc\), and all the E70 inner relations are recovered from these substitutions and the displayed squares. In particular the endpoint meridians are
\[
 b_{\rm top}=q^2,\qquad c_{\rm top}=q^2cq^2,\qquad
 b=cq^2c,\qquad c .
\]
The oriented translations of the marked pillowcase are \(d_2=b\,b_{\rm top}\) and \(f=c_{\rm top}b_{\rm top}\). Their images in \(\pi_1^{\rm orb}(B')\) are
\[
 d_2=(cq^2)^2,\qquad f=(cq^2)^{-1}.
\]
The product \(cq^2\) has infinite order in the free product. Hence the exact kernel on the pillowcase translation lattice is generated by the primitive slope
\[
 m=d_2+2f.
\]
This step uses the boundary words of the actual tangle, including the signs of \(d_2\) and \(f\).

Take the fourfold cover of \(B'\) from \(q\mapsto1,\ c\mapsto2\) in \(C_4\). Every old-strand meridian maps to \(2\). The cover is smooth along the branch arcs, since over each order-two rotation chart it takes the ordinary smooth normal disk with its half-turn action. Denote it by \(P\), and denote a generator of the deck group by \(g\). It has two boundary tori \(T_0,T_1\), because the pillowcase image has order two. Each boundary restriction is the usual double cover of the pillowcase, with torus lattice \(\langle d_2,f\rangle\). The subgroup generated by \(g^2\) acts there by the negative identity in affine torus coordinates based at a fixed point, and \(g\) interchanges the tori. In \(P\) the boundary slope \(m\) is null-homotopic on each torus by the exact boundary calculation.

The fundamental group of \(P\) is free of rank two. The kernel of \(C_4*C_2\to C_4\) intersects both vertex groups trivially, so it acts freely on the free-product tree. Its quotient graph has one vertex of type \(C_4\), two of type \(C_2\), and four edges. Its free rank is \(4-3+1=2\).

## 2. Equivariant compressions and the separating sphere

On \(T_0\) choose positively coordinated affine circles in the slope \(m\) at transverse parameters \(+\epsilon\) and \(-\epsilon\), \(0<\epsilon<1/4\), relative to a torus origin fixed by \(g^2\). Transport the pair to \(T_1\) by \(g\). The four simple curves are disjoint and null-homotopic in \(P\). The deck group acts freely on their union because \(g\) exchanges tori and \(g^2\) exchanges the two curves on each torus. The equivariant Dehn lemma for disks applies to this compact orientable smooth \(P\), its orientation-preserving finite action, and this boundary collection. It gives four pairwise disjoint properly embedded smooth disks with the chosen boundaries and invariant as a collection. [Meeks–Yau, *The equivariant Dehn's lemma and loop theorem*, Theorem 5, pp. 234–235](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/59/11077-Shing-Tung_Yau_126.pdf).

For each \(T_i\) thicken its collar and both associated compression disks equivariantly to obtain a region \(U_i\). This is a solid torus punctured at two disjoint balls. Keeping \(T_i\) as its outside torus and compressing along two parallel meridians creates the two inside sphere boundaries. The two annuli between the parallel curves on \(T_i\) are each preserved by \(g^2\). In affine coordinates \((s,t)\) with \(s\) along \(m\), the annuli contain the invariant levels \(t=0,1/2\), respectively. Each contains exactly two fixed points of \((s,t)\mapsto(-s,-t)\). Each inside sphere of \(U_i\) is the corresponding collar annulus capped by the two disk faces. The disk faces are exchanged freely. Thus each inside sphere is preserved by \(g^2\) and has exactly two transverse branch intersections. The collar product and the disjoint disk caps show that the intersections are on the actual lifted old strands.

Cutting \(P\) along the four inside spheres gives \(U_0,U_1\) and complementary pieces. The dual graph is a tree. In fact both \(U_i\) contribute one free generator to \(H_1(P)\), which already exhausts its rank two. Gluing along spheres would add a free rank for any cycle. There are four edges and hence five vertices. The three complementary pieces have degrees \(1,1,2\), since \(U_0,U_1\) each have degree two and every cut sphere faces one complementary piece. The degree-two piece meets both \(U_i\). All complementary pieces are simply connected: van Kampen gives their groups as free factors of the free rank-two group of \(P\), and each has zero first homology and hence trivial free group. Capping their sphere boundaries and using three-dimensional Poincaré shows that the two leaves are balls and the middle piece is \(S^2\times I\).

Consequently the two spheres adjacent to the middle piece are the two essential frontiers in the smooth cover, and \(g\) interchanges them. Their common quotient is an embedded sphere with two order-two points. It is separating in the filled orbifold: the components on the two outside sides are interchanged by \(g\), while the middle piece is preserved. The other two frontier spheres bound the leaf balls. The construction uses marked boundary compressions and keeps their deck action. It does not select a sequence of local Kirby moves in the eight-crossing picture.

## 3. Oriented outer filling on each compressible torus

The fourfold cover extends over the outer tangle. The boundary group of that tangle surjects onto its orbifold group, and its image in \(C_4\) is \(C_2\). Thus there are two lifted outer fillings, each the smooth solid-torus double cover of that rational tangle. Their meridian in the E70 basis is
\[
 \eta=2d_2-3f .
\]
The orbit construction orients the smooth boundary as retained base then fibre. The curve \(d_2\) has the orientation of the removed disk boundary, opposite to the positive boundary of the retained base. Thus \((d_2,f)\) is negative on each inner torus, and the meridian-longitude basis \((m,l)=(d_2+2f,-f)\) is positive. In this basis the closing meridian is
\[
 \eta=2m+7l .
\]
After restoring the leaf balls, each \(U_i\) is a solid torus punctured at one ball, with meridian \(m\). Attaching its lifted outer solid torus therefore gives the punctured \(+7/2\) unknot surgery. Equivalently the rational tangle meridians \(m,\eta\) have geometric intersection seven, with the second slope having meridian coefficient two in the indicated positive basis. The torsion self-linking of the generator \(l\) is \(-2/7\) with the same orientation: subtract two meridian disks of the inner torus from an outer meridian disk to bound \(7l\), and push \(l\) into the inner torus. This records the oriented parameter distinctly from its opposite. The deck transformation \(g\) is orientation preserving and interchanges the two filled pieces, giving the connected sum of two equally oriented \(L(7,2)\) factors. [Oriented unknot surgery convention in Kronheimer–Mrowka–Ozsváth–Szabó, §1](https://people.math.harvard.edu/~kronheim/lens.pdf).

The result specifies a reducing sphere for the marked odd orbit orbifold and the prime factors of its smooth fourfold cover. It gives no placement of the actual inverse four-dimensional surgery circle into a local handle diagram of a single summand, and the odd smooth closing is not identified here.
