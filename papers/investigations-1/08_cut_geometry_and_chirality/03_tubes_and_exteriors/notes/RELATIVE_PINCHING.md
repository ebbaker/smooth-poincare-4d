# A Yamabe-ratio obstruction for a noncyclic exterior

27 November 2026, for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI assistance. Lead actual setting unavailable. Inputs bc2c1bb, [equality](E03_EQUALITY.md) and [positive-energy collapse](E05_TWISTED_EXTERIORS.md). New branch deduction using Chen–Zhu's Theorem 1.4. Let \(X\) have the rational homology of a circle, connected umbilic \(\Sigma=S^1\times S^2\), and positive reflected Yamabe constant \(Y_D\). Denote \(D=DX\) and \(e_X=(E_+(X)+E_-(X))/2\).

**Claim.** If \(\pi_1(X)\) is noncyclic, then \(Y_D^2\le96e_X\). In fact the potentially stronger test
\[
36\int_D \max\{\lambda_{\max}(W^+),\lambda_{\max}(W^-)\}^2\,dV<Y_D^2
\]
forces the peripheral inclusion to identify \(\pi_1(X)\cong\mathbb Z\), and \(D\) is diffeomorphic to \(S^1\times S^3\). The group conclusion is marked and the diffeomorphism conclusion is unmarked.

For a trace-free symmetric three by three matrix \(B\), \(\lambda_{\max}^2\le(2/3)|B|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\) by minimizing the squares of the remaining eigenvalues with their sum fixed. The full Hilbert–Schmidt Weyl integral on the double is \(4e_X\). Thus \(96e_X<Y_D^2\) suffices for the displayed test. Smooth the weak double in its totally geodesic gauge in a shrinking collar. The approximants converge in \(C^1\), have uniformly bounded second derivatives and converge in \(W^{2,p}\) for finite \(p\). Scalar and Weyl tensors converge in \(L^2\), hence so do the maximal eigenvalues. For the Yamabe constants, the numerator difference is bounded by \(o(1)(\|du\|_2^2+\|u\|_4^2)\) and denominators are uniformly equivalent with factor \(1+o(1)\). On normalized near minimizers in either direction the gradient stays bounded by the uniform scalar \(L^2\) norm and a fixed test function. Therefore \(Y_{D,j}\to Y_D\) and a strict inequality survives.

Chen and Zhu's smooth compact four-dimensional classification under this precise strict eigenvalue inequality and \(Y>0\) identifies \(D\) as a connected sum of standard spheres, possibly \(\mathbb{RP}^4\), and compact free cylinder quotients. Orientability excludes a nonorientable summand. Each cylinder quotient has Euler characteristic zero (a finite product cover, or its Gauss–Bonnet density). Connected-sum additivity and \(\chi(D)=0\) leave exactly one such summand. Its fundamental group is virtually infinite cyclic; projection to the line is discrete with finite kernel. [Chen–Zhu, Theorem 1.4, pp. 813–814](https://intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2014/0022/0005/CAG-2014-0022-0005-a002.pdf) · [Chen–Tang–Zhu, main theorem and Corollary 1 proof, p. 44](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/21/926-JDG-91-1-a2-che.pdf).

Let \(G=\pi_1(X)\) and let \(H\) be the image of the boundary generator. The rational long exact sequence as in E03 gives \(H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Q)\cong H_1(X;\mathbb Q)\), so \(H\) is infinite cyclic. By van Kampen \(\pi_1(D)=G*_H G\) because the two boundary homomorphisms are identical in the doubled marking. If \(g\in G\setminus H\), the element \(w=g_1g_2^{-1}\) from the two copies has reduced alternating word length \(2\) and \(w^n\) has length \(2n\). The cosets \(w^nH\) are therefore distinct. But in a virtually cyclic group every infinite subgroup has finite index (intersect with a finite-index infinite cyclic subgroup). Hence \(G=H=\mathbb Z\) and \(\pi_1(D)=\mathbb Z\). An orientable free cylinder quotient with deck group \(\mathbb Z\) has no finite projection kernel; its generator translates on the line and acts by a rotation in \(SO(4)\) on the sphere. It is diffeomorphic to \(S^1\times S^3\).
