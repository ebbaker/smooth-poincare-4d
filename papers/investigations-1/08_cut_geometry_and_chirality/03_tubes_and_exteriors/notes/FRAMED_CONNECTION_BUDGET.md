# The cost of the lifted boundary holonomy

27 November 2026, for Edward B. Baker III, OpenAI assistance. Serving lead identifier and effort unavailable. Inputs bc2c1bb, [07’s disk calculation](../../../07_positive_subcritical_entry/notes/MARKED_CONNECTION_ASSEMBLY.md) and [curvature derivation](../../../07_positive_subcritical_entry/notes/CONNECTION_CURVATURE.md). This concerns any smooth metric conformal to a round two-sphere connection metric over an oriented disk with base-dependent radius. The connection may be nonabelian, vary in both base coordinates and have no circle symmetry. After division by the fiber radius squared, denote the normalized base metric by \(q\), its curvature by \(K\), and the curvature vector of the connection by \(b=*_{q}F_A\), using unit angular speed as norm in \(\mathfrak{so}(3)\).

Put \(d\mu=dA_q/(2\pi)\), \(U=\int d\mu\), \(D=\int Kd\mu=1-(2\pi)^{-1}\oint k_q\,d\ell_q\), \(z=|b|^2\). Boundary curvature uses the disk Gauss–Bonnet sign. For each chirality the axial Weyl diagonal is \((K+1-|b\times p|^2)/3\mp b\cdot p\), \(p\) on the unit sphere. Tracelessness bounds the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm below by \(3/2\) times the squared diagonal. The sphere averages of \(1-(p\cdot\hat b)^2\), its square and \((p\cdot\hat b)^2\) are \(2/3,8/15,1/3\). The odd cross term integrates to zero. Hence for each sign
\[
 J_\pm=E_\pm/\pi^2\ \ge\ \int\!\left\{\tfrac43(K+1-\tfrac23 z)^2+4z+\tfrac{16}{135}z^2\right\}\,d\mu.
\]
Completing the square against \(K-1-2z/3\) gives \(16D/3+(4/9)\int z+(16/135)\int z^2\) as a lower bound. The same completion with the derivative term retained is an identity for the circle ansatz. Equality in its basic \(16D/3\) bound requires \(F=0,\ K=1\), hence \(a=\sin s,\ a(S)^2+a'(S)^2=1\).

Lift the disk bundle connection to \(SU(2)\) over the disk. The lift of parallel transport on the boundary in a disk-extending trivialization has endpoint \(h\). Use the bi-invariant metric on the group for which the covering map to \(SO(3)\) preserves the angle norm locally. Set \(\delta=d(1,h)/(2\pi)\). To prove the flux inequality sweep out the disk by concentric loops, use radial parallel transport to trivialize, and differentiate the holonomy of each loop. Its right-translated derivative is the loop integral of \(F_{r\varphi}\) conjugated by parallel transports. This follows directly by differentiating the parallel-transport ODE and integrating along the loop. Conjugation is isometric; integrating the speed in the radial variable bounds the endpoint distance by \(\int |b|\,dA_q\). The point loop has holonomy one. Thus \(\int z\,d\mu\ge\delta^2/U\) and \(\int z^2\,d\mu\ge\delta^4/U^3\). For a boundary coefficient \(\beta J\,d\psi\) in the prescribed disk marking, \(\delta=\min_{n\in\mathbb Z}|\beta-2n|\); an odd winding of the boundary frame changes the lift. Curvature determines a conjugacy class in the lift; one must specify the boundary marking to use the formula for \(\beta\).

For every \(0\le\lambda\le16/3\), the tangent bound for the square and arithmetic–geometric mean yield the area-independent inequality
\[
 J_\pm\ \ge\ \lambda D+
 2\delta\sqrt{(\lambda-3\lambda^2/16)(4-2\lambda/3)} .
\]
Indeed the remaining integrated terms dominate
\((\lambda-3\lambda^2/16)U+(4-2\lambda/3)\delta^2/U\). This also gives an obstruction for a positive-energy exterior by adding its *corresponding signed* energy to each side before testing the closed wall. The mean energy gives a weaker necessary test when only the mean is supplied.

For example prescribe \(D=147/100,\ \beta=-3/4\); Hopf end data achieve this by setting \(\ell=2\pi(47/100)\tan\theta\), \(0<\theta<\pi/2\). At \(\lambda=26/5\) each cap energy divided by \(\pi^2\) is at least \(1911/250+(3/2)\sqrt{26/375}>8\). This obstructs **every** round-fiber connection cap with that marked germ. The ordinary \(SO(3)\) endpoint has the same principal angle as for \(+1/4\). For that other marking this particular bound at \(26/5\) is below eight; it supplies no upper bound or positive closed example. At a sweepout tube with no round-fiber model the substitution of its radius into this inequality has no justification.

For an exterior made by deleting one circle from the boundary of an oriented disk three-bundle over a compact orientable surface with boundary, the projection identifies the boundary fundamental group with the free group of the surface: the horizontal sphere fiber is simply connected and the vertical solid disk bundles along the boundary glue without changing the group by van Kampen. Removing a circle in dimension four also preserves \(\pi_1\) by transversality. One surgery kills one normal generator; its abelianization can vanish only when the free rank is at most one. Therefore a representation *of that entire exterior* giving nonabelian \(SO(3)\) holonomy cannot give a one-surgery homotopy-sphere example in this Fuchsian compactification model. A quaternionic certificate of a piece in a multi-relator diagram does not by itself supply this peripheral representation. On any homotopy-sphere closing the meridian normally generates the exterior group, so a representation trivial on it is trivial. These tests leave general groups with normally generating, nontrivial meridian holonomy open.
