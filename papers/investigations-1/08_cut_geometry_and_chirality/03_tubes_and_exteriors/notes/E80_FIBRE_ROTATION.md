The derivative of every loop of diffeomorphisms fixing the puncture represents zero in \(\pi_1SO(3)\). More generally every diffeomorphism preserving the marked punctured-fibre bundle has even boundary rotation, including one reversing the base orientation. The argument concerns this fibration and leaves the general exterior diffeomorphism question open.

## The actual capped and pointed monodromies

Write \(Y=S^3/\Gamma\) for unit quaternions with the deck action
\[
 (u,z^r)q=uqz^{-r},\qquad \Gamma=Q_8\times C_{17},\qquad z=e^{2\pi i/17}.
\]
In the convention \((t+1,q)\sim(t,Jq)\), the capped monodromy is \(J(q)=bqj^{-1}\), with \(b=e^{n\pi/3}\), \(n=(i+j+k)/\sqrt3\). The actual marked section has lift \(p_t=p_0e^{j\pi t/6}\), \(p_0jp_0^{-1}=n\). In particular \(Jp_1=p_0\) as an equality on \(S^3\). Set \(p=[p_0]\). Choose a small standard ball \(B\) at \(p\) and an ambient transport \(U_t\) starting at the identity, carrying its parametrization along a smoothly framed family of balls on the actual section. The frame along the section can be chosen so \(U_1|_B=J^{-1}|_B\), by endpoint matching in the oriented tangent bundle along the loop and isotopy extension. The marked punctured bundle is the mapping torus of \(\phi=JU_1\) on \(P=Y\setminus\operatorname{int}B\). We take a representative with \(\phi\) identity on a collar of its spherical boundary, by choosing the transported balls a little larger. The cap framing used for this trivialization may carry either absolute parity. Whether a self-map exchanges the two parities is independent of this initial choice. [E39, marked section and suspension](E39_PRISM_FILLINGS.md#the-canonical-suspension-and-the-actual-marked-torus)

The lift of \(U_t\) to \(S^3\) starting at the identity commutes with \(\Gamma\), since its conjugation acts continuously on a finite set of deck maps. It carries \(p_0\) along the specified lift \(p_t\). Thus the lift \(J\widetilde U_1\) fixes \(p_0\) and gives exactly the based monodromy automorphism \(\alpha(u,z^r)=(bub^{-1},z^{-r})\). This establishes the point-push choice. Conjugating by a different based loop could change that marked lift in an unpointed calculation.

## Round circle, evaluation and derivative

The covering \(SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\to SO(4)\) has kernel generated by \((-1,-1)\), with action \((A,B)q=AqB^{-1}\). The full inverse image of \(\Gamma\) is \(Q_8\times C_{34}\), where \(C_{34}=\langle e^{i\pi/17}\rangle\). Its normalizer in the two factors is
\[
 \mathcal N=2O\times\operatorname{Pin}(2)_i,\qquad
 \operatorname{Pin}(2)_i=S^1_i\cup jS^1_i .
\]
Indeed the left factor lifts the rotations permuting the three unoriented coordinate axes, and the right factor preserves the line spanned by \(i\). It follows that \(\operatorname{Isom}_0(Y)\) is the right circle with effective period \(\pi/17\), and \(\pi_0\operatorname{Isom}^+(Y)\cong S_3\times C_2\). There is no orientation-reversing isometry. Up to an orientation-preserving isometry any reversing lift interchanges the left and right factors, which would interchange their projection subgroups of unequal order. The round spherical Smale theorem transfers these component and fundamental group statements to \(\operatorname{Diff}(Y)\), including absence of orientation-reversing diffeomorphisms. The action of \(S_3\times C_2\) on \(\operatorname{Out}(\Gamma)\) is faithful, as the factors record the permutation of the quaternion lines and inversion on \(C_{17}\). In particular \([\phi]\) has unpointed class \((\text{3-cycle},\text{inversion})\). [Bamler–Kleiner, *Ricci flow and contractibility of spaces of metrics*, Theorem 1.3, authors' 2019 version](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1909.08710v1)

Right multiplication by \(e^{i\theta}\) commutes with \(J\) only at the two inversion-fixed parameters of the effective circle. The effective circle acts freely on the chosen \(p\). An extra stabilizer would require a noncentral \(u\in Q_8\) on the axis \(p_0 i p_0^{-1}\). This vector is orthogonal to \(n\), whereas none of the three quaternion axes is orthogonal to \(n\). Thus the only right-circle isometry fixing this puncture is the identity. The same reasoning holds at each \(p_t\).

Let \(D_0=\operatorname{Diff}_0(Y)\), and \(D_{0,p}\subset D_0\) denote the point-fixing fibre, with homotopy groups at the identity. Point evaluation is a fibration by local supported translations. A generator of \(\pi_1(D_0)\cong\mathbb Z\) is represented by the right rotation from angle \(0\) to \(\pi/17\). Its evaluation has endpoint \(p_0e^{i\pi/17}=(-1)p_0z^{-8}\) in the covering chart; its deck class is \((-1,z^8)\), generating \(Z(\Gamma)\) of order 34. Since \(\pi_2(Y)=0\), the evaluation sequence gives
\[
 \pi_1(D_{0,p})\hookrightarrow\pi_1(D_0),\qquad
 \operatorname{im}(\pi_1(D_{0,p}))=34\mathbb Z,\qquad
 \pi_0(D_{0,p})\cong\Gamma/Z(\Gamma)\cong C_2^2.
\]
On the component group the monodromy acts by a 3-cycle on the nonzero classes.

Use framed evaluation \(f\mapsto(f(p),df_p)\) with a chosen initial oriented frame. The \(34\)-fold generator in \(D_0\) lifts in \(S^3\) to \(p_0e^{i\theta}\) for \(0\le\theta\le2\pi\), and the derivatives of the right translations preserve the right-invariant frame \((i q,j q,k q)\). Thus its framed evaluation is nullhomotopic on the frame bundle of \(S^3\) and descends to a nullhomotopic framed loop on \(Y\). The inclusion of the linear frame fibre \(\operatorname{GL}^+(3)\) into the full frame bundle is injective on \(\pi_1\), again by \(\pi_2(Y)=0\). A point-fixing loop evaluates in that fibre, so its image under the derivative in \(\pi_1\operatorname{GL}^+(3)\cong C_2\) is zero. This excludes the proposed loop even before imposing commutation with \(\phi\).

## Relative ball group and all fibre-preserving maps

For the exact bundle test use the relative mapping class group
\[
 \mathcal A=\pi_0\operatorname{Diff}(P\ {\rm rel}\ \partial P)
 \cong\pi_0\operatorname{Diff}(Y,B).
\]
The equality of components uses collar straightening rel boundary, followed by the identity extension across \(B\). The collar construction works in compact parameter families, with an isotopy extension using local cutoffs, so it also identifies relations of isotopy. The restriction fibration over oriented embeddings of \(B\) compares this ball group to the fibre of framed evaluation. The derivative-at-centre map from those embeddings to oriented linear frames is a weak homotopy equivalence. One may shrink a compact family of embedded parametrized balls uniformly, and in exponential coordinates interpolate to small linear embeddings with the same centre and derivative. For sufficiently small uniform radii the derivative is uniformly close, after linear rescaling, to the identity, so the interpolation remains embedded. Small exponential balls provide a reverse construction, with the derivative corrected by positive scalar interpolation. This gives the comparison also relative to compact parameter boundaries. [Palais, restriction fibration for a compact embedded submanifold](https://www.e-periodica.ch/cntmng?pid=com-001%3A1960%3A34%3A%3A24) · [E25, collar straightening](../reviews/E25_HOMOGENEOUS_REPLAY.md#1-the-puncture-and-its-smooth-blow-up)

For the group calculation pull framed evaluation back along the universal cover of the frame bundle. This pullback is a topological group: a point over \(f\) specifies a lift of its derivative action on the frame bundle, uniquely determined by the prescribed image of the fixed lifted base frame. The group of diffeomorphisms fixing the frame includes by the lift fixing that frame. The lifted evaluation is still a fibration, and its base is simply connected, so this inclusion induces an isomorphism on path components. On round orthonormal frames the base is \(\Gamma\backslash SO(4)\); adding the contractible positive symmetric linear factor gives the oriented linear version. The pullback of the isometry group to the universal frame cover is exactly \(\mathcal N\) above. The Smale equivalence induces a bijection on components of these pullback covers as well, by the lifting of paths and loops. All the identifications on components respect the group law. Therefore
\[
 \mathcal A\cong\pi_0\mathcal N\cong 2O\times C_2 .
\]

A \(2\pi\) rotation of the boundary sphere lifts to a collar isotopy whose endpoint fixes the boundary. Let \(\delta\) denote its class in \(\mathcal A\). To see its framed class directly, extend the collar isotopy over the ball by the same rotation at each time. The endpoint is the identity on the ball, and the path of frames goes once around the \(SO(3)\) frame fibre. Its lift in \(SU(2)_L\times SU(2)_R\) ends at \((-1,-1)\), yielding
\[
 \delta=(-1,0)\in 2O\times C_2 .
\]
The other boundary homotopies are exhausted by this parity, since Smale's sphere theorem gives \(\pi_1\operatorname{Diff}^+(S^2)=C_2\). The parity is the Gluck boundary class, also for base reversal, which reverses a loop in this group without changing its class. Constant boundary rotations and base maps extend over the product cap. [Smale, *Diffeomorphisms of the 2-sphere*, Theorem A, reproduced first page](https://math.tecnico.ulisboa.pt/seminars/download.php?fid=2693) · [Hatcher, *On the Diffeomorphism Group of \(S^1\times S^2\)*](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/Papers/newDiffS1xS2.pdf)

Let \(m\in\mathcal A\) be the class of \(\phi\). Lift the transport \(U_t\) to the framed lift group starting at the identity. Its endpoint lies in the identity component, so the product with the lift \((b,j)\) lies in component \((b,1)\). The underlying lift to \(S^3\) of this product fixes \(p_0\), exactly as does the canonical framed lift of the relative-ball diffeomorphism \(\phi\). Their difference is therefore in the kernel over the \(S^3\) action, namely \(1\) or \((-1,-1)\). Hence \(m\) has \(2O\) entry \(b\) or \(-b\). This accounts for the remaining choice of tangent framing without replacing the actual moving section by an unpointed isotopy.

Now let a self-diffeomorphism of the punctured bundle preserve fibres, with base degree \(\epsilon=\pm1\). In interval coordinates its fibre maps satisfy
\[
 A_1=\phi^{-\epsilon}A_0\phi.
\]
Every fibre map is orientation preserving: an orientation-reversing punctured-fibre map can be capped across the ball after collar straightening and an isotopy of its boundary to a reflection, which would give an orientation-reversing diffeomorphism of \(Y\). The family of boundary sphere maps is a loop because \(\phi\) is the identity there. First compose with a fixed collar extension of \(A_0|_{\partial P}^{-1}\) to base the loop at the identity. Choose its support in the collar where \(\phi\) is the identity, so the twisted endpoint condition remains valid. If the boundary parity is \(\omega\), lift this loop into a collar isotopy starting at identity and ending in the relative class \(\delta^\omega\). Dividing by that collar path gives a path fixing the boundary. Writing \(g\) for the resulting initial relative class, the endpoint relation becomes
\[
 g m g^{-1}=\delta^\omega m^\epsilon \qquad\text{in }\mathcal A .
\]
Quaternion conjugation and inversion preserve the real part of the \(2O\) entry of \(m\), which is \(1/2\) or \(-1/2\). Multiplication by the \(2O\) entry of \(\delta\) reverses that sign. Therefore \(\omega=0\) for either base degree. The conjugacy test uses the marked relative-ball monodromy and applies to all components of the fibre map group, beyond loops in \(D_{0,p}\).

## The periodic suspension and the remaining question

The unpointed capped monodromy \(J\) has order six, but its unpointed conjugacy class cannot contain a finite-order diffeomorphism fixing a point. First, an isometry in this class has the left lift in \(Q_8 b^{\pm1}\), with real part \(\pm1/2\), and the right lift in \(jS^1_i\), with real part zero. Multiplying by any deck elements retains these two scalar types. A fixed point would require the two lift factors, after such multiplication, to be conjugate in \(SU(2)\). This is impossible by real parts. A finite-order diffeomorphism of the spherical \(Y\) is smoothly conjugate to such an isometry for the fixed round metric by elliptic finite-action linearization. Its unpointed conjugacy class and fixed-point existence are preserved by that conjugacy. Consequently the marked pointed class has no periodic representative, even allowing the boundary sphere to move. To see the last statement, a finite-order boundary-setwise representative on \(P\) has an invariant collar obtained by averaging a metric. Its spherical boundary action is smoothly conjugate to rotations, by uniformization of an invariant metric and conjugating the finite Möbius group into rotations. It extends equivariantly over the ball with fixed centre. The unpointed outer action on \(\Gamma\) is unchanged by the capping choices, and that outer action detects the unpointed class here. Equivalently a circle suspension preserving the section and covering base rotation with nonzero degree would give a finite-order first return on a punctured fibre and so meet the same obstruction. [Dinkelbach–Leeb, Theorem E, final paragraph of its proof](https://msp.org/gt/2009/13-2/gt-v13-n2-p15-p.pdf) · [Finite Möbius action and round linearization in E15](../reviews/E15_QUATERNIONIC_COLLAR.md#the-compact-link-and-its-one-sided-collars)

The obstruction addresses the direct fibre rotation and a section-compatible periodic suspension. An arbitrary exterior diffeomorphism acts by a sign on the base cohomology generator, but that information alone supplies no isotopy to a diffeomorphism preserving the fibration. The calculation does not determine that isotopy question or the smooth type of the odd Gluck twin.
