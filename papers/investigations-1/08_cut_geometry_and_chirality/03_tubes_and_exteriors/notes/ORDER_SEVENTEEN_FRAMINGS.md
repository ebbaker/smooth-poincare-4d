## Why the even group recognizes the four-manifold

After the original surgery, compress the marked torus to \(S_T^2\); the local product gives a trivial normal bundle. Surgery on this sphere produces \(W_0\) with a locally free smooth circle action over the indicated \(G_0\)-orbifold. On its common exterior the meridian of \(S_T\) generates \(H_1\), and the reversed surgery circle \(\delta\) represents that generator in \(H_1(W_0)=\mathbb Z\).

For \(\Pi=\pi_1W_0\) the principal orbit \(h\) is central and \(\Pi/\langle h\rangle=S_3\). Its degree in \(H_1(W_0)\) has magnitude two. The commutator subgroup of \(\Pi\) injects into \(S_3\) because it misses \(\langle h\rangle\), and maps onto \(C_3\). Thus \(\Pi=C_3\rtimes_{\rm inv}\mathbb Z\). Every degree-one element is conjugate to every other, since squaring is bijective in \(C_3\). The surviving exceptional orbit of weight two has degree one after orienting. It is freely homotopic and hence smoothly isotopic to \(\delta\) by one-parameter general position for circles in four dimensions.

Transport the actual reverse-surgery framing by that isotopy. Either normal framing on the exceptional orbit permits a smooth equivariant cap: changing the framing once adds the isotropy order to the lifted rotation weight. The resulting smooth effective circle action is on the original even homotopy sphere. The curvature-free circle-action classification for closed simply connected smooth four-manifolds then gives the standard \(S^4\). The two allowable framings at this last *inverse* surgery do not identify the two framings of the *initial* section. ## Extent of the odd-framing obstruction

For the ordinary \(p=|3s-1|\) lift of the old knot, the determinant of its smooth branch double is
\[
\prod_{y^p=(-1)^s}\big|7-5(y+y^{-1})\big|.
\]
The Alexander specialization comes from the drilled marked smooth cover. Torsion gluing uses the core character of the *filled* solid torus and accounts for the factor two in the sign-character normalization. The product is one only for \(s=-1\). At odd \(s\ne-1\) the order-four action and the linking pairing give a nontrivial repeated odd-primary summand. This excludes a spherical finite group. At \(s=-1\) the explicit projective image has order 12,180, and the deep branch double group also surjects onto it. The spherical three-manifold group classification excludes this simple quotient. Therefore **every odd integer slope on the original marked torus has infinite orbit group**. The only possible odd-dihedral case lies at \(s=0\), by abelianization. ## The odd Gluck twist as a boundary map

For the two regular companions, the cable-space calculation includes distance zero, distance greater than one, and the primitive distance-one fillings. The exceptional \((7,2)\) cable realizes the initial *odd* normal frame by spin transport. The result concerns genuine torus cables of these two companions. [Cable replay](../reviews/E78_CABLE_REPLAY.md).

A different obstruction comes from the relative mapping class group of the punctured prism. Its oriented ball-fixing group of components is \(2O\times C_2\), where \(2O\) is the binary octahedral group. The full boundary-sphere rotation is the central \((-1,0)\). In E81’s native oriented frame, the monodromy has left component \(b=(1+i+j+k)/2\), and after the twist it is \(-b\). Multiplying by the central twist changes that real part. Conjugacy and inversion preserve it. A fibre-preserving diffeomorphism extending the Gluck twist would give such a conjugacy (with inversion if the base is reversed), so this route cannot realize the odd filling from the even exterior. The remaining smooth question allows changes of handle description and exterior maps that do not preserve the fibration.
