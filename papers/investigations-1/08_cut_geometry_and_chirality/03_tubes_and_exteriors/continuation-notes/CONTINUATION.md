## After the first horizon

The rows above are the first horizon. The directions below serve the components and are listed in the order in which they are likely to matter; the lead may replace them. A statement marked † was derived while this note was written and has not been read independently: it is a first packet for a Sol reader, not an input.

- **A lower bound within the ansatz** (T2). † For every cap (6.2), \(\int_0^SaK\,ds=1-a'(S)\), and the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality applied to (6.3), after the terms in \(F^4\) and \(F'^2\) are dropped, gives \(e_{\rm cap}\ge\tfrac43T\,(1-a'(S))\); with the matching conditions this is \(e\ge\tfrac43T\bigl(1+\tfrac{\ell}{2\pi}\cot\theta\bigr)\). For caps of the form (6.2) it is the bound of Proposition 6.2(1) at \(\theta=\pi/2\) without the hypothesis on the double. It lies below every row of [check_02](../../../../master/brainstorm/notes/claude/USES_OF_THE_FIRST_ORDER_AND_MARKED_CUT_TOOLS_20261126_checks/check_02_cap_costs.py) and equals the first. It shows that a circle-symmetric tube in the band has \(\tan\theta>\ell/\pi\), and that the flat exterior of Remark 6.3 has no such cap in the band when the radius is below \(1/\pi\) times the length. The flux condition is not used; using it should separate the two framings.
- **The case \(e_X=0\)** (T1). † By the equality case of Gursky's inequality the double is then covered by \(\mathbb R\times S^3\), and the reflection has to fix a connected separating \(S^1\times S^2\). The expected conclusion is \(X=S^1\times B^3\) with a standard closing for both framings. TUBE-E03 takes each step from the primary sources, including the passage from the \(C^{1,1}\) doubled class to the smooth statement.
- **From \(e_X=0\) outward** (T1). The plan row names the approaches: stability of the equality case, a classification that starts at \(e_X=0\), and a counterexample from a twisted exterior. The attained minimizers of 06 are a possible source of compactness for the second. A failed approach is recorded for the class examined, and the next is taken.
- **The least cost as a function of the data** (T2). Existence and uniqueness of the minimizer for given \((\ell,\theta,\beta)\), monotonicity in \(\theta\), and the thin-tube limit, where the rows of Remark 6.3 approach the wall. Decide whether the limit is \(2T\) and with what leading correction: that decides which knotted tubes are in the band at all.
- **Tubes without symmetry** (T3). An exterior with Weyl energy leaves the cap less than \(2T-e_X\), which the lower bound above constrains. Where a representation recorded by the slide enumeration of 05 is a representation of the group of an exterior, it is a candidate holonomy for the first class; [slide filters](../../04_slide_filters/README.md) is asked for that list, and nothing is enumerated here. For tubes around a projective plane the seam is a quotient by the quaternion group and the eta bound requires \(\lvert\eta\rvert<\tfrac23\); the [chirality ledger](../../01_chirality_ledger/README.md) returns the admissible structures.
- **Events of sweepouts.** [Cuts from geometry](../../05_cuts_from_geometry/README.md) sends the exterior of each tube that collapses in a sweepout of an example. Those tubes are thin and far from umbilic, so the atlas does not apply to them as stated; say what the cap functional and its bounds do give.
