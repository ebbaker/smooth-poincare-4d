# Signature eta on left-invariant quaternionic links

27 November 2026 (UTC). Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI assistance in Codex. Actual model and effort of this execution are unavailable to the author of this note. Frozen task [SP4-CUT-LED-E04, request 1](../research-progress/handoffs/E04_REQUEST_R1.md), checkout `bc2c1bbccc1d244a9d456ffe2fcaf64e368290b0`, with the [E01 relative Chern–Simons calculation](FS_BALLS.md). This is an exact calculation and complete-cone inequality for the indicated family. An [exact arithmetic script](../numerics/check_quaternionic.py) checks the algebra.

Let \(Y=Q_8\backslash SU(2)\), with the action on the unit quaternions by left multiplication. Orient the cover \(S^3\) as the boundary of the unit ball in the positively oriented real quaternion vector space with basis \((1,i,j,k)\), and give \(Y\) the quotient orientation. For a smooth metric on \(Y\) whose lift to \(SU(2)\) is left-invariant, let \(A,B,C>0\) be the squared lengths of its three principal left-invariant axes, measured in the unit round frame. Put \(s_1=A+B+C\), \(s_2=AB+BC+CA\), \(s_3=ABC\). In the parent odd signature normalization,

\[
 \eta(Y,h)=-\frac34-\frac{s_1^3-4s_1s_2+9s_3}{12s_3}.
\]

It follows that \(|\eta(Y,h)|\ge3/4\), with equality precisely for a round metric up to scale. The region \(|\eta|<2/3\) is empty in this entire family, in either orientation.

## Orientation, metric reduction and the round offset

Let \(\sigma_i\) be the coframe dual to the round left translates of \((i,j,k)\). In this orientation \([i,j]=2k\) cyclically, so \(d\sigma_i=-2\sigma_j\wedge\sigma_k\) for cyclic \((i,j,k)\). The coframe descends to \(Y\), where \(\int_Y\sigma_{123}=2\pi^2/8\). This Maurer–Cartan sign is part of the orientation choice. In the positive frame of the Fubini–Study balls in E01, the Maurer–Cartan sign was the opposite one.

An arbitrary metric of the indicated class is a positive symmetric real matrix on the left frame. For every \(b\in SU(2)\), right multiplication by \(b\) commutes with the deck action of \(Q_8\) and induces an orientation-preserving diffeomorphism of \(Y\). Its derivative in the left frame is \(\operatorname{Ad}_{b^{-1}}\). These transformations give all of \(SO(3)\), so the spectral theorem diagonalizes the matrix by a pullback on the actual quotient. If an orthonormal eigenbasis initially has negative orientation, changing the sign of one vector gives an \(SO(3)\) diagonalization. The pullback preserves the oriented eta invariant. Thus all such metrics are represented by \(A\sigma_1^2+B\sigma_2^2+C\sigma_3^2\), and permutations of the three entries can also be realized by an orientation-preserving signed permutation.

Here is a round sign check using only cyclic quotients and averaging. The round oriented sphere has \(\eta=0\) by its reflection symmetry. For the cyclic group generated by the left multiplication by \(i\), its generator rotates both oriented planes \((1,i)\) and \((j,k)\) by \(+\pi/2\), with these plane orientations inducing the chosen ambient orientation. The same holds for \(j\) and \(k\) in cyclically permuted quaternion bases. [Atiyah–Patodi–Singer II, Proposition (2.12), p. 412](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps002.pdf#page=8) gives the eta invariant of the standard oriented quotient of the sphere by a rotation with this plane-angle data. It is \(-\tfrac14[\cot^2(\pi/4)+\cot^2(\pi/2)+\cot^2(3\pi/4)]=-\tfrac12\) for each of the three order-four subgroups. For the central subgroup \(\{\pm1\}\), both angles are \(\pi\), so the quotient invariant is zero.

For an isometry \(g\) of the round sphere in the deck group, write \(a_g\) for its equivariant eta value. For a finite subgroup \(G\), the projection to \(G\)-invariant vectors in each eigenspace of the odd signature operator is the average of the group actions. Taking the trace first for the absolutely convergent eta series and then continuing it to zero yields \(\eta(G\backslash S^3)=|G|^{-1}\sum_{g\in G} a_g\). This uses the equivariant spectral sum of APS II, equation (2.13), on the same page as the cyclic formula. The trivial and central order-two subgroup calculations give \(a_1=a_{-1}=0\). Each order-four subgroup calculation gives \(a_u+a_{-u}=-2\) for \(u=i,j,k\). Therefore

\[
 \eta(Y,h_{\rm rd})=\tfrac18\bigl(0+0-2-2-2\bigr)=-\tfrac34.
\]

## Relative signature Chern–Simons integral

Write \((A_1,A_2,A_3)=(A,B,C)\), \(a_i=\sqrt{A_i}\) and \(e^i=a_i\sigma_i\). With \(d_i=a_i/(a_j a_k)\) for cyclic \((i,j,k)\), the structure equations are \(de^i=-2d_i e^j\wedge e^k\). For the skew connection matrix the entries \(\omega^j{}_k=u_i e^i\) solve \(u_j+u_k=-2d_i\), hence \(u_i=d_i-d_j-d_k\). Equivalently, \(\omega^j{}_k=f_i\sigma_i\), where

\[
 f_i=\frac{2A_i-s_1}{\sqrt{A_jA_k}}.
\]

The coefficients are constant on each link. With \(\sigma_{123}\) positive, the two traces are \(\operatorname{tr}(\omega\wedge d\omega)=4\sum_i f_i^2\,\sigma_{123}\) and \(\operatorname{tr}(\omega^{\wedge3})=6 f_1f_2f_3\,\sigma_{123}\). The first sign follows from the negative Maurer–Cartan sign and skewness. The cubic trace has six terms of the same sign from the cyclic and reversed orders. Thus the Pontryagin Chern–Simons integral in this frame is

\[
 \int_Y\operatorname{CS}_{p_1}(\omega)=-\frac18 F,\qquad F=\sum_i f_i^2+f_1f_2f_3.
\]

On a cylinder oriented by \(dt\wedge\sigma_{123}\) take a smooth path of positive diagonal metrics, constant and round near \(t=0\) and constant and equal to \(h\) near \(t=1\). Its product-ended metric \(dt^2+h(t)\) has a global orthonormal frame extending the two normalized left frames. The signature of the cylinder is zero, since \(H^2(Y;\mathbb R)=0\) by pullback to the finite cover \(S^3\). Apply [Atiyah–Patodi–Singer I, Theorem (4.14), p. 66](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf#page=24) and Stokes in this common frame as in E01. The relative eta is one-third the difference of the two Chern–Simons integrals, with the round integral subtracted. At the unit round metric \(f_i=-1\), so \(F_{\rm rd}=2\). This gives

\[
 \eta(Y,h)-\eta(Y,h_{\rm rd})=-\frac{F-2}{24}.
\]

The expression is real, with its sign fixed by the common frame and the boundary orientation. The cylinder calculation uses no extrinsic term from any filling of \(Y\).

## Exact rational expression and the full positive cone

In the expression for \(F\), the square roots cancel in the squares and in the triple product. The numerator over \(s_3\) is

\[
 \sum_i A_i(2A_i-s_1)^2+\prod_i(2A_i-s_1)
 =2s_1^3-8s_1s_2+20s_3.
\]

Indeed the sum is \(s_1^3-4s_1s_2+12s_3\) by expanding and collecting power sums. The product is \(s_1^3-4s_1s_2+8s_3\). Subtracting the round value thus gives \(F-2=2(s_1^3-4s_1s_2+9s_3)/s_3\) and the claimed eta formula. For a Berger metric with \(A=B=1\) and \(C=v\), the correction is \(-(1-v)^2/12\). This is one eighth of the correction on the cover with the quaternion orientation, which has the opposite sign to the E01 correction.

By symmetry order \(A\ge B\ge C>0\). Expanding or grouping the first two terms of \(\sum_{\rm cyc} A(A-B)(A-C)\) gives

\[
 s_1^3-4s_1s_2+9s_3
 =(A-B)^2(A+B-C)+C(A-C)(B-C).
\]

Since \(A+B-C\ge A>0\) and \(C>0\), this is nonnegative. Its first term vanishes only if \(A=B\), and then the second vanishes only if \(A=C\). This proves the asserted result at every point of the open positive cone, including repeated axes. In conjunction with the parent umbilic cut bound of Corollary 3.3, it excludes a cut with any of these conformal data from occurring umbilically in an entering class on a smooth homotopy four-sphere.
