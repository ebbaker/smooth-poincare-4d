# A quaternionic link realized inside the round sphere

27 November 2026. For Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI assistance, lead identifier and effort unavailable. E11, checkout bc2c1bb. This calibration uses the signed quotient formula in [E04](QUATERNIONIC.md) and its [marking](QUATERNIONIC_MARKING.md). It identifies the two circle directions on one actual embedded link.

The trace-free symmetric real three-by-three matrices with inner product \(\mathrm{tr}(AB)\) form a Euclidean five-space. Its unit sphere is the round \(S^4\). Conjugate by \(SO(3)\) and use an ordered diagonal matrix \(D=\mathrm{diag}(d_1,d_2,d_3)\), \(d_1>d_2>d_3\), as a marked point. Its stabilizer consists of the four diagonal rotations. Its inverse image in the unit quaternions is \(Q_8\). The principal orbit is the *right* quotient \(S^3/Q_8\), with left-invariant metric upstairs. Inversion identifies this with the left quotient carrying a right-invariant lift in E10, with orientation reversed upstairs. The eta at these particular orbits will vanish in either orientation.

For quaternionic infinitesimal generators \(i,j,k\) the rotations in three-space have twice the elementary angular speed. The variation at the marked point is \([X,D]\). Its squared length for rotation in the \((j,k)\)-coordinate plane is \(8(d_j-d_k)^2\); distinct planes are orthogonal by their matrix supports. The length about the middle eigenaxis is therefore the sum of the other two lengths. Let \(A,B,C\) denote the three squared lengths, \(p=A+B+C, q=AB+BC+CA, r=ABC\). The sum-of-lengths relation gives \(p^2=4q\), hence \(p^3-4pq+9r=9r\). In E10 orientation the homogeneous quotient formula gives \(-3/4+9/12=0\). Independently the ambient Weyl tensor is zero, so Codazzi gives \(\operatorname{symcurl} B^\circ=0\), the transgression vanishes, and the ledger on a side gives the same eta.

At equal successive eigenvalue gaps the metric is proportional to the Berger triple \((1,1,2)\) of **lengths**, after ordering the two shorter axes first. Thus E10's \(t=2\) scalar-flat example is realized; a point of the strict positive-scalar interval in E10 changes this induced metric. In fact the whole round orbit family has scalar curvature zero: for scale-one Lie brackets \([i,j]=2k\), contraction of the three-dimensional Koszul curvature gives \(R=2(4q-p^2)/r\). One can instead use the Gauss equation after differentiating the three metric lengths along the unit diagonal circle. A totally umbilic hypersurface of the unit sphere has scalar curvature \(6+2H^2/3>0\); none of these orbits is umbilic.

The orbit space is the closed arc of the unit diagonal circle with ordered eigenvalues. At one endpoint \(d_1=d_2\), rotations about the third eigenaxis stabilize the matrix. At the other \(d_2=d_3\), rotations about the first axis stabilize it. Each singular orbit is the space of unoriented simple eigenlines, hence \(\mathbb{RP}^2\); the shrinking circle on a principal level is the corresponding stabilizer circle modulo its finite intersection with the principal isotropy. This circle is the normal circle of the singular orbit (the traceless symmetric variations in the repeated eigenspace form its normal two-plane). At the midpoint the two normal circles point along the two *short* axes. The long axis about the middle eigenline never collapses in this marked chamber. Relabelling a fibre direction without transporting the boundary maps can therefore change the geometric problem. Nothing here realizes an umbilic principal orbit or certifies a new closed metric on either exterior.
