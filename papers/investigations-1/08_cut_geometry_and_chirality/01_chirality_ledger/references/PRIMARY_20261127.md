## APS signature normalization

**Atiyah, Patodi and Singer, *Spectral asymmetry and Riemannian
Geometry. I*, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 77 (1975), 43–69.**
Checked §§1 and 4, equations (1.2), (1.5), (1.7), (4.9)–(4.11) and
Theorem (4.14), especially p. 66. The signature is the signature of
the nondegenerate form on \(\operatorname{im}(H^2(V,\partial V;\mathbf R)
\to H^2(V;\mathbf R))\). For a smooth compact oriented Riemannian
four-manifold with a product metric in a boundary neighborhood, the
theorem gives
\[
  \tau(V)=\frac{1}{3}\int_Vp_1-\eta_{\rm sig}(\partial V).
\]
Here \(\eta_{\rm sig}\) is the continued value at zero of the signed
nonzero spectrum on even-degree forms. On a three-dimensional
boundary the operator in (4.14) acts by
\((-1)^r(*d-d*)\) on \(2r\)-forms. In particular, the signature
formula uses the unhalved even-form invariant, without a kernel
correction. The kernel term in the preceding signature-operator index
calculation is eliminated when passing from the index to the topological
signature. The orientation is the induced boundary orientation, i.e.
outward normal first. The inward collar variable in the general index
formula describes the differential operator near the boundary. Reversing
the boundary orientation negates its Hodge star and hence
\(\eta_{\rm sig}\). For disconnected boundary the values add.
[APS I, journal-page scan][aps1]

The parent writes \(p_1=-\operatorname{tr}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)/(8\pi^2)\)
with \(\Omega\) the real skew connection curvature matrix. Its
curvature-operator convention gives
\(p_1=(|W^+|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2-|W^-|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2)dV/(4\pi^2)\).
Indeed \(p_1\) in an oriented orthonormal frame is the sum of the squares
of the curvature two-forms over \(i<j\), divided by \(4\pi^2\).
The corresponding Hodge-star trace is
\(\operatorname{tr}(*\mathcal R^2)\), in which the trace-free Ricci
off-diagonal blocks cancel and the equal scalar parts cancel. Thus
the product identity is \(E_+-E_-=12\pi^2(\tau+\eta_{\rm sig})\).
For a nonproduct metric, applying APS to a product replacement requires
the separate Pontryagin transgression of the parent ledger. Its sign
is outside this source check.

**Atiyah, Patodi and Singer, *Spectral asymmetry and Riemannian
geometry. II*, Math. Proc. Cambridge Philos. Soc. 78 (1975), 405–432.**
Checked §2, (2.1)–(2.3), and §4, (4.6)–(4.7), pp. 419–421.
Equation (4.7) gives the ordinary eta difference for a product-ended
cylinder, oriented by increasing collar coordinate first:
\[
  \eta_{\rm sig}(Y,h_1)-\eta_{\rm sig}(Y,h_0)
  =\frac{1}{3}\int_{[0,1]\times Y}p_1.
\]
This also gives conformal invariance in dimension four. If
\(h_1=e^{2u}h_0\), take \(e^{2\chi(t)u}(dt^2+h_0)\) with \(\chi=0,1\)
near the ends. Near each end, the induced tangent connection is the
product connection for that end metric and the second fundamental form
is zero. Its actual metric need not have a product collar. Replace it
by one that is product at both ends and use (4.5) with the agreeing
boundary connections, or equivalently use the transgression with zero
boundary term. To compare the connections as endomorphisms, identify
the normal lines by matching their unit normals and extend this bundle
identification across the cylinder. The conformally scaled metric has
zero Pontryagin form because \(p_1\) is conformally invariant and the
unscaled product has zero \(p_1\) pointwise. The conformal invariance
of Pontryagin forms is explicitly used in the footnote on p. 421.
The metric-independent twisted difference in Theorem (2.4) concerns
the rho invariant. [APS II, journal-page scan][aps2]

## Fixed points, round quotients and handedness

**APS II, §2, Proposition (2.12), p. 412, and (2.13)–(2.16),
pp. 412–413.** For a cyclic action of order \(m\) rotating two ordered
oriented planes by \(2\pi a/m\) and \(2\pi b/m\), free on the unit
sphere, the oriented lens quotient of the round sphere has
\[
  \eta_{\rm sig}=-\frac1m\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}
      \cot(\pi ak/m)\cot(\pi bk/m).
\]
The two planes together carry the ambient orientation. In the proof
the ball has only the fixed origin for each nonidentity element and
has signature zero. Its fixed-point factor over the two planes is
\((-i)^2\cot(\theta_1/2)\cot(\theta_2/2)\). The formula assumes the
standard round quotient metric. The ordinary eta average for a general
finite group of free orientation-preserving round isometries follows
by taking the invariant subspace on each nonzero even-form eigenspace.
The projector is \(|\Gamma|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\gamma^*\).
The signed trace identity holds in the absolutely convergent
half-plane, then for the continued value at zero. The identity term
is zero by the orientation-reversing round sphere isometry. Each
remaining trace is the same cyclic equivariant term computed from
the ball fixed point in the proof of (2.12), or from (2.16) and
the fixed-point discussion immediately after it. For this action the
fixed-point calculation uses the smooth ball with a group action.
The quotient of the ball has a singular origin when \(\Gamma\ne 1\).
[APS II, pp. 411–413][aps2]

**Independent orientation calculation.** Orient \(\mathbb H\) by
\((1,i,j,k)\) and \(S^3\) by applying the outward-first rule to its unit
ball. For conjugacy one can choose \(\gamma=\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\)
with \(0<\theta\leq\pi\) using an orientation-preserving quaternionic
linear isometry. In the oriented planes \((1,i)\) and \((j,k)\), left
multiplication rotates by \((\theta,\theta)\) since \(ij=k\).
Right multiplication rotates by \((\theta,-\theta)\) since \(ji=-k\).
Writing the right group action using \(\gamma^{-1}\) changes both
angles' signs and preserves their cotangent product. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
 \eta_{\rm sig}(\Gamma\backslash S^3)
   &= -|\Gamma|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\ne1}\cot^2(\theta_\gamma/2),\\
 \eta_{\rm sig}(S^3/\Gamma)
   &= +|\Gamma|^{-1}\sum_{\gamma\ne1}\cot^2(\theta_\gamma/2),
 \qquad \cos\theta_\gamma=\operatorname{Re}\gamma .
\end{aligned}
\]
Here the first quotient uses left orbits, and the second uses right
orbits. Quaternionic conjugation interchanges these actions and
reverses the orientation. The usual complex determinant-one action
on \(\mathbb C^2\) with its complex orientation has eigenangles
\((\theta,-\theta)\) and gives the positive sign. For example, with
\(q=z_1+z_2j\) the complex orientation is \((1,i,j,k)\), and right
multiplication gives the complex determinant-one subgroup. The term
\(\gamma=-1\) vanishes in either handedness. A radial exterior of a
round model induces the reversed orientation on its inner boundary.

| Round left quotient in the stated orientation | \(\eta_{\rm sig}\) |
| --- | ---: |
| Cyclic order \(2,3,4,5\), respectively | \(0,\,-2/9,\,-1/2,\,-4/5\) |
| \(Q_8\) | \(-3/4\) |
| Binary tetrahedral group | \(-49/36\) |

For cyclic order \(m\), the identity
\(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\cot^2(\pi k/m)=(m-1)(m-2)/3\) follows by
differentiating the cotangent multiplication identity and taking the
constant term at zero. For \(Q_8\) there are six purely imaginary
terms of value \(1\). For the binary tetrahedral group these six
terms are supplemented by eight with real part \(1/2\), each of value
\(3\), and eight with real part \(-1/2\), each of value \(1/3\).
The signed result agrees with the cyclic-subgroup calculation in the
[marked-neck manuscript, Proposition on the quotient obstruction][neck].
The parent uses the absolute values. Use the sign tied to the action and
orientation when inserting a model in a signed ledger.

## Half-flat filling and the boundary datum

**Claude R. LeBrun, *H-Space with a Cosmological Constant*, Proc.
R. Soc. London A 380 (1982), 171–185.** The abstract and the author's
later restatement in *On complete quaternionic-Kähler manifolds*,
Duke Math. J. 63 (1991), p. 742, were checked. For a real-analytic
three-manifold with a real-analytic Riemannian conformal structure,
the result gives a local germ-unique real-analytic self-dual Einstein
conformal infinity with negative cosmological constant in the stated
normalization. This is the umbilic case \(B^\circ=0\) after conformal
compactification, corresponding to the regular compactifying
representative, while the Einstein representative has an order-two
pole. The checked 1982 statement specifies only the conformal
metric as boundary data. The publisher text beyond the abstract was
unavailable. Biquard's introduction credits both this article
and LeBrun's *Foliated CR Manifolds*, J. Differential Geom. 22 (1985),
81–96, for the general local \([h,B^\circ]\) problem, and his
Theorem 1.1 refers specifically to the latter's Theorem 7 for the
foliated CR correspondence. The 1985 full text was unavailable
through the journal link.
A citation to the 1982 article alone in Remark 3.6 supports the
umbilic Einstein germ assertion at the inspection level stated here.
[1982 article DOI][leb82] · [1982 abstract copy][leb82abs] ·
[LeBrun's 1991 published text, author copy][leb91] · [1985 article DOI][leb85]

**LeBrun, *Spaces of Complex Geodesics and Related Structures*,
Oxford D.Phil. thesis (1980).** Section 3.1 describes the second
conformal fundamental form as the trace-free symmetric part of the
second fundamental form with conformal weight one. With a chosen
unit normal this is the parent's \(B^\circ\), and the sign changes
when the chosen normal is reversed. Theorem (3.6.1), pp. 101–102,
gives the general-pair germ. A real-analytic positive-definite
conformal metric and real-analytic real second conformal fundamental
form on a real-analytic three-manifold are locally uniquely realized
as a real-analytic hypersurface in a real-analytic half-flat
positive-definite conformal four-manifold. The analytic uniqueness
is a local conformal germ assertion with the datum and orientation
fixed. The sign \(W^-=0\) or \(W^+=0\) is specified by the ambient
orientation. Applying the theorem to one side supplies a local
collar. [Oxford thesis, §§3.1 and 3.6][leb80]

**Olivier Biquard, *Métriques autoduales sur la boule*, Invent. Math. 148
(2002), 545–607.** Checked the publisher abstract and author preprint
`arXiv:math/0010188v3`, revised 16 November 2000, introduction
(Problem 0.1 and Theorems 0.3–0.6), Theorem 1.1, Corollary 4.2 and
§9 through Theorem 9.3 and Remark 9.4. The preprint explicitly defines
Riemannian **self-dual** as \(W^-=0\), and **anti-self-dual** as
\(W^+=0\), the convention also used by 03 for ASD. Its pair
\([h,\mathcal Q]\) records the boundary conformal metric and trace-free
second fundamental form. With a normal fixed, the conformal
representatives of the datum satisfy
\((h,\mathcal Q)\sim(e^{2u}h,e^u\mathcal Q)\).

For boundary data on \(S^3\) close to \([h_{\rm round},0]\), Theorem 0.4
gives the whole-standard-ball filling criterion via the associated
integrable CR twistor structure and Theorem 0.3. In the appropriate
contact circle action, the deformation must have only nonnegative
Fourier modes. The inclusion of the zero mode is explicit in
Corollary 4.2 and §9.2. The theorem distinguishes the special
\(\mathcal Q=0\) Einstein filling, with \([h]\) at conformal infinity.
The analytic neighborhoods in §9 use sufficiently high
Folland–Stein \(\mathcal H^k\) regularity after lifting the data
to the twistor space, as defined in §5 and at the start of §9.
Closeness of data in a low-order hypersurface norm alone was
not an input to this theorem. Theorem 0.5 describes the two
chiral filling loci on the same marked near-round boundary as
transverse modulo the reduced boundary diffeomorphism group.
Their intersection consists of data from deforming the sphere
in flat \(\mathbb R^4\), with coincident conformally flat fillings.
Theorems 0.6 and 10.1 describe the corresponding local loci for
Einstein conformal infinities. This is a whole-ball result
with a local restriction in the space of boundary data.
[Publisher record][biqpub] · [Author preprint][biq]

For a cut \(V\cup_\Sigma V'\) in one oriented four-manifold, the two
induced orientations on \(\Sigma\) are opposite, and the outward
second fundamental forms of one smooth metric differ by sign under
the boundary identification. With outward normals the two data are
therefore \((\Sigma,[h],B^\circ)\) and
\((-\Sigma,[h],-B^\circ)\) in the same conformal scale. If one reverses
the orientation of \(V'\) to compare its filling at the same
oriented boundary, its chiral labels swap. In the umbilic case
the outward \(\mathcal Q\) on both sides is zero. Opposite
chirality in the common ambient orientation then gives the same
chirality after identifying the two boundary orientations. Thus
the \( \mathcal B_+\cap\mathcal B_- \) statement in Theorem 0.5
has a different orientation hypothesis from the opposite-chirality
cut in Corollary 3.5. The existence of compatible smooth jets
and a whole filling on the actual cut-open smooth piece are
independent of the whole-standard-ball criterion.

## Actual half-flat pieces in investigation 03

- [SEC-E07][e07] constructs a smooth closed Taub–NUT coordinate ball
  with \(W^+=0\) in the hyperkähler orientation. Its given datum
  is induced at finite radius \(R=(2\ell)^{-1}\). The ball embeds
  by an oriented chart on any smooth oriented homotopy four-sphere
  and the metric extends smoothly by a cutoff. The outward
  second fundamental form in the source has principal curvatures
  \(a'/a,a'/a,c'/c\), with \(a/c=rV=\ell r+1/2\). Thus
  \((a'/a)-(c'/c)>0\) for \(\ell>0\) and positive finite radius.
  Its boundary datum is nonumbilic. The global extension permits
  unrestricted exterior curvature.
- [SEC-E28][e28], equations (2) and (7), supplies a nonconformally-flat
  ASD southern cap on the known smooth \(S^4\). For the fixed small
  nonzero prescribed seed the positive Weyl block vanishes
  in a neighborhood of the closed southern cap and the opposite
  block has a positive lower bound there. The cap carries the
  datum induced by the constructed global metric. Its umbilicity
  and a matching opposite-chirality fill are not established.
- [SEC-E24][e24] supplies an independent input for the lead's scope
  decision. The Weyl first variation implies local Bach-flatness
  where either chiral block vanishes. The checked analytic
  representative in [Lassas–Liimatainen][ll], Theorem 1.3(c)
  (see also Theorem 3.7(c)), propagates vanishing of a chiral
  block via the analytic Hodge projector as shown in E24.
  Theorem 1.4 of that source addresses the full Weyl tensor.
  This input concerns a smooth metric across the interface.

The inspected results contain actual single pieces, including a
nonumbilic core available on any smooth type and a cap with its
induced datum on the standard type. A specified umbilic datum with
a compatible smooth opposite-chirality exterior on an arbitrary
marked smooth type is absent from these 03 results.
