## E02 · Source scope and face estimates

In a marked unit round product window, ambient \(C^2\) error \(\epsilon\) gives \(C^2\) intrinsic error and \(C^1\) error \(O(\epsilon)\) in \(B^\circ\) by the connection and unit-normal formulas. The transgression integral is \(O(\epsilon^2)\). Interpolation of the intrinsic metrics on a fixed product-germ strip has Weyl \(O(\epsilon)\), since the round product is conformally flat. The cylinder has signature zero and opposite ends. Product APS gives the intrinsic eta difference \(O(\epsilon^2)\), also for the round quotient with its own eta offset. This argument uses bounded fixed-window volume. For conformally flat product faces, the two errors are exactly zero relative to their round model, even when the interior of the collar is not product.

For a weak splice leaving an unmodified raw germ and an exact product germ, top and bottom contributions differ by the chiral difference across that splice. This gives
\[
\left|12\pi^2(\eta_{\rm raw}-\eta_{\rm model})+\mathcal T_{\rm raw}\right|
 \le E_+({\rm splice})+E_-({\rm splice}).
\]
The proof requires that the splice actually retain the raw face on a germ, have both chiral masses tending to zero, and use the same marking and eta offset as the original collar. The estimate concerns this signed combination. Two such splices to the same round model suffice for chiral transfer on the original collar even when neither individual raw end term is controlled.

**Separated estimates at a fixed face under strong \(W^{2,2}\).** Assume smooth uniformly elliptic metrics with strong \(W^{2,2}\) convergence on a neighborhood of the face to a smooth model. In a fixed smooth underlying bundle frame the intrinsic connection difference \(A\) of the leaves and the extrinsic tensor \(B^\circ\) are functions of the metric and its first jet. For the extrinsic wedge pairing one may transport orthonormal frames using the positive square root of the tangential metric relative to the model. Each jet expression converges strongly in \(W^{1,2}\) in the window. To see this, estimate terms with second derivative differences in \(L^2\), quadratic first derivative differences using strong \(W^{1,4}\), and bounded zero-order coefficient differences against the smooth fixed jets by convergence in measure and dominated convergence. Differentiating the frame maps has the same structure. Tracing yields strong \(H^{1/2}\) on the fixed face. The quadratic terms \(\int\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge d_0A)\) and \(\int\sum_i B_i^\circ\wedge d_0 B_i^\circ\) are bounded by \(H^{1/2}\) against \(H^{-1/2}\) duality, and cubic terms by \(H^{1/2}\hookrightarrow L^3\). The model curvature is smooth in the linear term. Thus the signed eta difference and the extrinsic integral tend to zero at an umbilic model. This proof estimates signed integrals without estimating a separate raw curl \(L^{3/2}\) norm.

**Separate estimates by moving each face.** Pull back to fixed bounded product windows and use one background measure for the argument, equivalent to the source measures by ellipticity. For
\[
 f_i(s)=\int_{\Sigma_s}|\nabla(G_i-G_0)|^4,\qquad
 g_i(s)=\int_{\Sigma_s}|\mathrm{Rm}_{G_i}|^2 ,
\]
the respective window integrals tend to zero and stay uniformly bounded. Choosing thresholds four times their means, the two bad sets together have at most half the window length. A slice outside them has \(A,B^\circ=o(1)\) in \(L^3\), from \(C^0\) convergence and the selected \(L^4\) first-jet error. The entire second fundamental form stays \(L^3\)-bounded. Gauss then bounds intrinsic curvature and Codazzi bounds \(\operatorname{sym}\operatorname{curl}B^\circ\) in \(L^{3/2}\) on that slice. Rewrite the connection transgression integrand in E03 as \(\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge(\Omega+\Omega_0)-A^{\wedge3}/3)\); its bound is \(C(\|A\|_3(\|\Omega\|_{3/2}+\|\Omega_0\|_{3/2})+\|A\|_3^3)\). The extrinsic bound follows by Hölder. The proof applies once at each end, with the same oriented round marked model. The changed collar needs an energy estimate on its changed domain, which follows from any preceding estimate on a larger collar containing both selected faces. The end windows must permit their movement without crossing marks.

**Fixed faces in the compact physical-window route.** The proof of [03 physical window][window] explicitly retains \(C^1\) convergence in the assembled charts (lines 86–88) and assumes a uniform ambient pointwise curvature bound on the physical band (lines 13–17). With the additional conditions needed for one of the [radial tests][shell] to identify a fixed marked cone, these estimates hold on both original fixed faces in a compact comparison interval. Work in the cone gauge itself, where the model faces are round up to scale and umbilic. Then \(A,B^\circ=o(1)\) in \(L^\infty\) by \(C^1\) convergence, the full \(B\) stays bounded, and Gauss and Codazzi give uniform intrinsic curvature and symmetric-curl bounds from the pointwise ambient curvature. The same transgression and Hölder estimates as in the movable-face case now give separate vanishing fixed-face errors without choosing different slices. Conformal invariance transfers the integrals to cylindrical gauge. This also avoids requiring a pointwise curvature claim in a different nonconstant gauge.

## E03 · Variation and coefficient

The checked primary input is [APS I, Theorem (4.14), p. 66](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf), on oriented product collars with \(\eta(0)\) on even forms. [APS II, equations (4.6)–(4.7), p. 420](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps002.pdf) uses the same relative sign on a cylinder. Using the orientation \(ds\wedge dV_\Sigma\), this gives \(3(\eta_1-\eta_0)=\int p_1\). Replacing the cylinder connection by the tangent connection path plus a trivial normal line is valid because the boundary pullbacks agree. In the curvature square the one-\(ds\) terms add with the same sign. Using \(p_1=-\operatorname{tr}\Omega^2/(8\pi^2)\) and integrating in the path parameter gives the coefficient \(-1/(24\pi^2)\), with the three transgression terms weighted \(2,1,2/3\).

For \(v=\partial_s h_s\), the linearization of the transgression is \(-\int\operatorname{tr}(\dot D\wedge\Omega)/(12\pi^2)\). In a frame satisfying the note's convention \(\Omega^j{}_i=e^j\wedge P_i+P^j\wedge e_i\), \(\sum_j\dot D^i{}_j\wedge e^j=0\) by symmetry in the two lower indices. The remaining volume coefficient is \(\varepsilon^{kli}\dot D^i{}_{kj}P^j{}_l\). Inserting the standard three gradient terms of \(\dot D\), its middle term vanishes by antisymmetry in \(k,i\), and the other two equal each other after exchanging \(k,i\). Integration by parts gives \(-\int\varepsilon^{ikl}v_{ij}\nabla_kP_{lj}\,dV\). The sign and coefficient of E03 equation (2) therefore agree with \(Y_{ij}=\varepsilon_i{}^{kl}\nabla_kP_{lj}\). Reversing orientation negates both \(\eta\) and \(Y\).

Independently, on the positive-\(d\sigma\) Berger coframe set \(e^1=\sigma_1,e^2=\sigma_2,e^3=t\sigma_3\). Cartan gives the connection coefficients \((2/t-t),t,t\) in the cyclic pairs \(12,23,31\), followed by \(K_{12}=4-3t^2\), \(K_{23}=K_{31}=t^2\). The two Schouten eigenvalues are \(a=2-3t^2/2\) and \(b=-2+5t^2/2\). The antisymmetrized derivatives entering \(Y_{33}\) are \(t(b-a)-[-t(b-a)]=2t(b-a)\). With \(v_{33}=2/t\), volume \(2\pi^2 t\) and round eta zero, the formula integrates to \(2(1-t^2)^2/3\), as in the frozen signed test. For a foliation with positive lapse \(N\), coarea and the two correctly oriented boundary terms give the derivative of \(12\pi^2\eta+\mathcal T\) as \(-4\int N\langle\mathcal E,\mathcal H\rangle\,d\sigma\). This sign takes the magnetic tensor as defined in parent §2.

For the quantitative bound, the round term \(\operatorname{tr}(A\wedge\Omega_0)\) vanishes pointwise by torsion freeness. Put \(k=h-h_0\) in the fixed marked unit gauge, with Hilbert–Schmidt pointwise derivatives up to two bounded by \(\delta\le1/2\). Contraction of the symmetric three-term difference formula with \(h^{-1}\) gives \(|A|\le 3\delta/[2(1-\delta)]\le3\delta\). Differentiation puts one derivative on \(k\) or \(h^{-1}\), giving \(|D_0 A|\le (3/2)(\delta/(1-\delta)+\delta^2/(1-\delta)^2)\le6\delta\). Exterior antisymmetrization with unit simple two-forms gives \(|d_0A|\le\sqrt2|D_0A|\). The pointwise quadratic trace is at most \(18\sqrt2\,\delta^2\). The cubic trace is at most \(6|A_1||A_2||A_3|\le 2|A|^3/\sqrt3\), so its weighted contribution is at most \(12\sqrt3\,\delta^3\le6\sqrt3\,\delta^2\). Division by \(24\pi^2\) and insertion of volume \(2\pi^2/|\Gamma|\) yields exactly \(K_{|\Gamma|}=(3\sqrt2+\sqrt3)/(2|\Gamma|)\). The marked representative is a premise; the resulting eta difference is a conformal invariant.

## E08 · Deficit, thresholds and topology

For a cut in the stated smooth homotopy sphere, the relative-to-absolute \(H^2\) maps factor through \(H^2(M)=0\) by excision, so both signatures vanish. The two cut orientations give opposite \(\eta\) and \(\mathcal T\). If \(D=12\pi^2\eta+\mathcal T\ge0\), use \(E_+(V)+E_+(V')=e\) to obtain \(e-D=E_-(V)+E_+(V')\). If \(D\le0\) the other orientation gives the switched identity. Equality in \(e\ge|D|\) is exactly the vanishing of the corresponding two nonnegative block integrals, and smoothness makes these pointwise vanishings on the open interiors. Following [E07](../notes/HALF_FLAT.md), Bach-flatness from compactly supported variations on each side and smooth propagation across the cut force conformal flatness. This last implication uses the smoothness and connectedness in E07. The deficit identity uses neither propagation nor smoothness across the interface if the piecewise smooth metrics have canceling boundary data and global energies defined by summing the piece integrals.

The product \(J=\|B^\circ\|_3\|\operatorname{sym}\operatorname{curl}B^\circ\|_{3/2}\) is an invariant upper bound on \(|\mathcal T|\), by the respective pointwise weights \(-1,-2\). Using \(|\eta|\ge|\eta_0|-K_q\delta^2\), divide by \(T=4\pi^2\) to get \(e/T\ge3(|\eta_0|-K_q\delta^2-J/(12\pi^2))\). A model margin \(m=|\eta_0|-2/3>0\) allows the simultaneous conditions \(K_q\delta^2\le m/2\) and \(J\le 6\pi^2m\). Their equality still excludes \(e<2T\). At zero model margin the bound alone gives no positive error tolerance uniformly for the whole open band. A fixed ambient energy margin permits positive tolerance. The proof of \(e<T\) uses the same estimate with threshold \(1/3\).

I checked the round values using [APS II, Proposition (2.12) and equations (2.13)–(2.16), pp. 412–413](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps002.pdf). In the quaternion orientation, both signed rotation planes for a left element have the same angle up to conjugation, so the summand is minus a cotangent square. Non-left actions require the actual two signed angles in the oriented four-space before taking absolute values. The cyclic sum is \((q-1)(q-2)/(3q)\) by the stated cosecant identity. For \(Q_8\) the six order-four elements contribute one each and \(-1\) contributes zero. For \(2T\), the additional \(8+8\) elements contribute \(3\) and \(1/3\), respectively. The resulting \(3/4\) and \(49/36\), numerical energy bounds, and their cutoff margins agree with E08.

In the rational homology-sphere case \(H_2(Y;\mathbb Z)=0\). Mayer–Vietoris over the integers gives \(H_2(V)=H_2(V')=0\) and \(H_1(Y)\cong H_1(V)\oplus H_1(V')\). The pair sequence and surjectivity to \(H_1(V)\) identify \(H_1(V')\cong H_2(V,Y)\cong H^2(V)\), hence abstractly with the finite \(H_1(V)\) by the universal coefficient theorem. Thus the group is a direct sum of two isomorphic groups, excluding all nontrivial cyclic quotients. The same obstruction also excludes the binary tetrahedral quotient as an actual cut. In \(2T=Q_8\rtimes C_3\), the commutator contains \(-1\), and the order-three action on \(Q_8/\{\pm1\}\) cyclically permutes its three nonzero elements. Its difference with the identity is invertible on that vector space over \(\mathbb F_2\), so the commutator surjects onto it, and hence contains all of \(Q_8\). Since the quotient by \(Q_8\) is cyclic of order three, \(H_1(S^3/2T)\cong C_3\), which fails the direct-sum condition. The eta numbers in these excluded rows are still valid for the abstract model and carry no least-energy claim for cuts.

For the weak Pedersen double added to the final E08 note, the compactified boundary coefficient from E07 has \(q(1)=(1+k)/(1+k)^2=1/(1+k)\). Thus E03's Berger formula gives eta magnitude \(\tfrac23(k/(1+k))^2\), and the cut is umbilic. The ball has signature zero. Its one surviving Weyl block therefore has energy \(12\pi^2|\eta|=8\pi^2(k/(1+k))^2\). Reflection switches the blocks as measured with the fixed orientation of the double, so each global chiral energy is exactly this value. It is strictly subcritical for every finite \(k>0\). [Independent E07 replay](HALF_FLAT_REPLAY.md) verifies the compactification and third-jet jump that put this metric outside smooth propagation.
