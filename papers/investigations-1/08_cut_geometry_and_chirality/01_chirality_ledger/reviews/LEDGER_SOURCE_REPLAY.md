## Sections 3.1–3.5

**Ledger normalization.** APS I, Theorem (4.14), printed p. 66, assumes a compact oriented Riemannian \(4k\)-manifold with exact product metric near its boundary and uses the odd signature operator on even forms. In dimension four its correction is the ordinary even-form \(\eta(0)\), with outward-first orientation and without a kernel shift. Directly, \(-\operatorname{tr}(\Omega\wedge\Omega)=2\sum_{a<b}\Omega^a{}_b\wedge\Omega^a{}_b\). Pairing each curvature two-form with its Hodge star gives \(p_1=(4\pi^2)^{-1}\operatorname{tr}(*\mathcal R^2)\,dV\). The off-diagonal Ricci blocks cancel and each scalar block has equal dimension and zero pairing with its trace-free Weyl block. On replacing the metric by a product in its Gaussian collar, both metric connections have the same boundary orthonormal frame. Their difference has only mixed entries \(B_i\) and \(-B_i\), so the only nonzero boundary trace term is \(\operatorname{tr}(\theta\wedge d_0\theta)=-2\sum_i B_i\wedge d^\nabla B_i\). The extra minus in \(p_1\) makes the boundary coefficient \(+1\) in (3.2). Projecting to the trace-free form removes only pointwise zero contributions. The statement in the baseline after the proof that no later statement depends on this sign should be removed, since the signed collar equality does. [APS I][aps1]

**Electric/magnetic signs.** Write \(\alpha_i=e^i\wedge\nu^\flat\), \(\beta_i=\tfrac12\varepsilon_{ijk}e^j\wedge e^k\). In the outward-first volume, \(\alpha_i\wedge\beta_i=-dV\), so \(*\alpha_i=-\beta_i\). The Weyl endomorphism commutes with star and takes the form
\[
\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal E&\mathcal H\\ \mathcal H&\mathcal E\end{pmatrix}.
\]
This gives the positive double-epsilon reconstruction for \(W_{ijkl}\), \(W^+=\mathcal E-\mathcal H\), \(W^-=\mathcal E+\mathcal H\) in the orthonormal Hodge bases, and the difference of squared norms \(-4\langle\mathcal E,\mathcal H\rangle\). The pure-electric test \(\mathcal E=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0)\) gives \(W_{2323}=1\) and Ricci trace zero. The baseline minus sign gives \(\operatorname{Ric}_{11}=2\) for that tensor. With \(R_{abcd}=\langle R^\nabla(e_a,e_b)e_d,e_c\rangle\) sphere-positive, Codazzi gives \(R_{jki\nu}=\nabla_jB_{ik}-\nabla_kB_{ij}\). Its Schouten contribution and the derivative of the trace part of \(B\) both dualize to skew forms. Thus \(\mathcal H=\operatorname{sym}\operatorname{curl} B^\circ\).

**Normals and conformal weights.** Reversing the ambient orientation with normal fixed negates \(\varepsilon,\mathcal H,\mathcal T,\eta\) and swaps \(W^\pm\). Reversing the normal with ambient orientation fixed also reverses the induced orientation. Then \(B^\circ\) and \(\varepsilon\) both negate, \(\mathcal H\) and the symmetric curl stay the same, and \(\mathcal T\) and \(\eta\) negate. This gives the correct subtraction of the bottom end in Corollary 3.4. In a rescaled orthonormal frame the curl transforms with scale \(e^{-2u}\) and a skew addition from \(u_j e^i-u_i e^j\), so the symmetric curl has weight \(-2\), while \(B^\circ\) has weight \(-1\) and area weight \(3\). The \(L^3\) norm of \(B^\circ\) and the \(L^{3/2}\) norm of the symmetric curl are invariant and give the sharper Hölder bound. A flat torus with \(B^\circ=\operatorname{diag}(1,-1,0)\) and \(u=u(x^1)\) indeed acquires a nonzero raw curl where \(u_1\ne0\). Eta is conformally invariant by transgression on the rescaled cylinder, where at each slice reflection in that slice for the unscaled product exchanges the Weyl blocks. APS II (4.7) gives the real eta difference. Its conformal proof on p. 421 is written in the Dirac discussion, so the product replacement or Theorem 3.1 supplies the signature argument independently. [APS II][aps2]

**Energy bounds.** For a homotopy-sphere cut the signatures of the pieces vanish. The inclusion into the closed sphere preserves the intersection pairing of interior representatives, and the target middle homology is zero. With \(L=E_+(V)-E_-(V)\) the opposite side has signed energy \(-L\) and \(e\ge |L|\). At equality the two sides vanish in opposite blocks. In the smooth class this forces zero Weyl by the across-cut argument in [E07][e07]. For an individual umbilic half-flat filling the calculation is \(e_V=\frac12|L|=6\pi^2|\tau+\eta|\). For a smooth opposite pair along the umbilic cut, Corollary 3.5 therefore gives \(e=12\pi^2|\eta|=0\). The strict model tolerance in E05 follows from the triangle inequality and requires \(|\eta_m|>2/3\) for a positive error tolerance for the whole open band. Its cyclic nonembedding check uses integral Mayer–Vietoris, Poincaré–Lefschetz duality and \(\operatorname{Ext}(A,\mathbb Z)\cong A\) for finite abelian groups. Those steps check in the stated rational homology sphere case.

**Round handedness.** APS II, Proposition (2.12), p. 412, gives the fixed-point contribution \(-\cot(\theta_1/2)\cot(\theta_2/2)\) with the two oriented planes ordered in the ambient orientation. The cyclic result extends by the invariant projector on eigenspaces and the equivariant formula (2.13)–(2.16), with zero identity contribution from the round sphere. In \(\mathbb H\) oriented by \((1,i,j,k)\), left multiplication by \(\cos\theta+i\sin\theta\) rotates both \((1,i)\) and \((j,k)\) by \(+\theta\). Right multiplication rotates the second plane by \(-\theta\). Quaternionic conjugation has negative real determinant and interchanges the left and right actions, independently checking the sign reversal. Thus the left quotient values in E06 are negative with the ordinary outward orientation and the right quotient values are positive. The parent uses their absolute values. The \(Q_8\) sum is \(6/8\) and the binary tetrahedral sum is \((6+8\cdot3+8/3)/24=49/36\), with the central element \(-1\) contributing zero. No quotient ball with a singular center is used as a smooth APS manifold in this argument. [APS II][aps2]

## Remark 3.6

**Analytic collar.** The baseline citation to LeBrun's 1982 *H-Space* paper, checked at the publisher preview, supplies an analytic self-dual Einstein conformal infinity from an analytic conformal metric. The general pair has a direct primary source in LeBrun's 1980 Oxford D.Phil. thesis, *Spaces of Complex Geodesics and Related Structures*, §3.6, Theorem (3.6.1), pp. 101–102. It gives local unique realization of the real-analytic positive-definite conformal metric and real-analytic second conformal fundamental form by an analytic hypersurface in a positive-definite half-flat conformal four-fold. The `Foliated CR Manifolds` article is cited by Biquard, and its full theorem text was unavailable in this replay. The thesis supplies the intended general-pair reference. [1982 publisher preview][leb82] · [Oxford thesis][lebt]

The E05 independent proof of the analytic result also checks. In an existing analytic representative \(g_*\), the equation \(\operatorname{div}_{g_*}(|dt|_{g_*}^2\nabla_{g_*}t)=0\) with \(t|_\Sigma=0\) and a prescribed nonzero normal derivative is noncharacteristic. The coefficient in an adapted orthonormal frame is \(|dt|^2+2(dt(\nu_*))^2=3|dt|^2\). Choose the derivative in the marked normal direction to realize the selected \(h_0\) on rescaling. For \(\widehat g=|dt|_{g_*}^2g_*\), the resulting \(t\) is unit-speed and harmonic, so Gaussian normals give \(\partial_t\det h_t=0\). The scalar trace condition fixes \(\operatorname{tr}_{h_t}h''=\operatorname{tr}((h_t^{-1}h')^2)\), and the electric tensor contains \(-\frac14(h'')^\circ\) as its pure second normal term. The magnetic tensor contains only first normal and tangential derivatives. After fixing the trace, each chirality equation is noncharacteristic for the trace-free logarithm of \(h_t\), with initial derivative \(2h_0^{-1}B_0^\circ\). Cauchy–Kowalevski supplies existence and uniqueness in this gauge. Reversing the normal changes the sign of the marked second datum and reversing the ambient orientation swaps the chosen block. The construction has the local analytic scope claimed in E05.

**Whole-ball theorem.** Biquard's author preprint arXiv:math/0010188v3 (16 November 2000) defines self-dual by \(W^-=0\) in the introduction, and the weighted pair in §1.1 (1.2). Theorems 4.3 and 4.4 specify the contact circle action, its relation \(\tau u=\bar u\tau\) to the real structure, and the nonnegative Fourier modes of the CR structure. Theorem 4.3 concerns a whole standard ball with a marked near-round \(S^3\) datum. Theorem 4.4 concerns the Einstein conformal infinity. Theorem 9.3 and Remark 9.4 use high Folland–Stein regularity of the lifted geometry as set up at the start of §9. Theorem 0.5 concerns the intersection of opposite filling labels for the same oriented marked datum. Both sides of an ambient cut have opposite outward-first boundary orientations. To compare their fillings on one common oriented model ball using the same boundary marking, the second orientation must be reversed. Thus opposite ambient blocks become the same filling label after that comparison. For a nonumbilic cut the attached outward second tensor must also be negated across the boundary identification. This confirms E06's handedness distinction and excludes applying Theorem 0.5 directly to a smooth opposite-chirality cut. [Biquard, author preprint][biq] · [Publisher record][biqp]

The checked material in 03 supplies single actual half-flat examples. The Taub–NUT cap's principal curvatures differ by \(\partial_{\rm dist}\log(a/c)\ne0\) because \(a/c=\ell r+1/2\) at finite positive radius. The southern cap in SEC-E28 is on the standard sphere, with its own induced datum and no condition of opposite half-flatness on the other side. Neither gives the global prescribed-datum assertion left open in the baseline. [SEC-E07][sec7] · [SEC-E28][sec28]

## Disposition

The E05 corrections and E06 source conclusions pass at the scopes stated above. The baseline manuscript still carries its former signs, raw-curl invariance claim and limited LeBrun citation in the hashed version reviewed. The precise correction to the latter is LeBrun's thesis Theorem (3.6.1), or the checked local analytic proof with the marked normal. The Biquard statement should name a fixed chirality on the near-round standard ball and remain distinct from smooth matching of the actual cut pieces.
