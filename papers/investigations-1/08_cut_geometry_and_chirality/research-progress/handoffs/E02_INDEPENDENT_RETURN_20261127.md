The corollary supplies no converse for fixed-metric positivity or the stronger \(\sigma_2(B)\ge0\) requirement. This repairs the sentence claiming equivalence for that stronger requirement.

**Proposition 7.4.** Choose the standard double decomposition of \(\#_\ell(S^1\times S^3)\) by two 1-handlebodies \(N_0,N_1\). The inclusion of their common boundary \(Y\) induces an isomorphism on fundamental groups. Represent the relators by disjoint embedded circles in \(Y\). In the ambient four-manifold a generic one-parameter homotopy of finitely many circles in the prescribed free homotopy classes has no equal-time double points (dimension \(1+1+1<4\)) and no derivative zeros, so it gives an isotopy of their ordered union to these circles. Transport their normal framings. Each of the two rank-three normal framing classes is realized by an integral framing in \(Y\), since \(\pi_1(SO(2))\to\pi_1(SO(3))\) is reduction mod two. A framed circle surgery in a product collar \(Y\times I\) has the relative handle presentation with a 2-handle along \(K_i\) at that integral framing and a 2-handle along its zero-framed meridian \(U_i\). This follows by writing the inserted \(D^2\times S^2\) as the two disk hemispheres of the \(S^2\) factor: the lower trace is the Dehn surgery and the reverse of the other trace attaches the dual circle, represented by the meridian in the original surgery diagram. This is the usual 2-handle and meridian replacement. The \(2\ell\) handles are attachable in the specified order since the entire framed link is in \(Y\).

The complement of the surgery loops has the original ambient fundamental group by general position for loops and homotopy disks in codimension three. Van Kampen for each \(D^2\times S^2\) kills its relator, giving trivial \(\pi_1\). Each surgery increases \(\chi\) by two, so the final \(\chi\) is two. Integral duality and the universal coefficient theorem give the sphere's homology, and Hurewicz and Whitehead give the homotopy type.

After the \(U_i\) are attached, the induced level is \(\#_{2\ell}(S^1\times S^2)\). In the surgered three-dimensional boundary the meridian disk of the filling solid torus caps the disk \(D_i\) cut off at the tube of \(U_i\), giving the sphere \(S_i\) meeting \(K_i\) once. This is a boundary disk in the new solid torus, a parallel of the 2-handle cocore, rather than the four-dimensional handle core. The spheres can be kept disjoint from the other \(K_j\). For a connected closed three-manifold \(Y_1\) and \(K\) meeting a sphere once, removing the tube of \(K\) punctures that sphere to a disk with boundary the meridian \(\mu_K\). A regular neighborhood of the boundary torus and this compressing disk is a punctured solid torus with meridian slope \(\mu_K\), since compression caps the torus to a sphere. Its complement is a punctured closed \(Y_0\). Filling along \(\mu_K\) gives \(Y_0\#(S^1\times S^2)\). Every integral surgery slope meets \(\mu_K\) once, so filling along that slope gives \(Y_0\#S^3\cong Y_0\). The prime decomposition identifies \(Y_0\) here as the connected sum with one fewer \(S^1\times S^2\) factor. This argument keeps each later \(S_j\) in the region outside the current surgery. Induction gives each intermediate level claimed in the proposition. The standard handles inside \(N_0\) and \(N_1\) give the connected levels at the two ends.

The post-proposition Andrews–Curtis assertion has a proof for this **circle-surgery model**: multiplication by a conjugate relator is a framed slide in the surgery trace, inversions and conjugations change orientations and base paths, and if using generator changes, the free-basis moves are induced by the standard ambient 1-handlebody moves. Codimension-three isotopy brings the final circles to the disjoint standard generators, with either framing in each summand. Surgery on the standard factor of \(S^1\times S^3\) with product framing gives \(\partial(D^2\times D^3)\); the framing twist extends across the complementary \(S^1\times D^3\) by rotation, so either framing gives \(S^4\). The published Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson passage in §7 gives the presentation associated to a surgery link and the Andrews–Curtis move induced by a dual handle slide. It does not by itself say that a presentation extracted from an arbitrary Property \(nR\) link with trivial Andrews–Curtis class certifies that link. Inserting the circle-surgery argument avoids that inference. The topological level list of the proposition occurs even on standard examples with index-two critical points, and a recognition assertion based solely on this list would have to settle the presented family. It is not refuted by an established exotic member of that family. The nearby marked two-middle-handle recognition is a companion working result with its specific hypotheses, not a claim about arbitrary balanced presentations. [Gompf–Scharlemann–Thompson, §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1103.1601).

**Min-max premise and Proposition 7.5.** The 2015 Zhou Theorem 1.1 assumes a connected closed orientable ambient manifold of dimension \(n+1\), \(2\le n\le6\), and \(\mathrm{Ric}>0\). For the fundamental-class width its embedded support is orientable with multiplicity one and index one, or nonorientable with multiplicity two. State multiplicity two as a varifold, not a claim that the oriented double cover is a stable two-sided immersion for all variations. The later paper states stability in the one-sided normal-variation sense, as seen by the odd functions on the orientation cover in its proof. The homotopy sphere has no one-sided embedded cut, so only the first alternative occurs here. No index-one hypothesis for an arbitrary critical cut follows from the existence theorem.

For a two-sided minimal hypersurface, with \(\Delta=\mathrm{div}\nabla\), \(J=-\Delta-(\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|B|^2)\). Gauss at \(H=0\) gives \(2(\mathrm{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+|B|^2)=R-R_\Sigma+|B|^2\). Hence \(-8\Delta+R_\Sigma=2J-6\Delta+(R+|B|^2)\), with \(-6\Delta\) nonnegative as a form. On the orthogonal complement of the negative eigenspace of \(J\), of codimension at most one, this form is strictly positive by \(R>0\) on the compact hypersurface. The nonpositive spectral subspace of the conformal Laplacian has dimension at most one. This leaves the first eigenvalue undetermined when the index is one. [Zhou 2015, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2112) · [Zhou's later Theorem 1.1 and proof of (ii)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1504.00966).

## §8 · Free cuts (lines 830–867)

**Proposition 8.1(1).** Van Kampen gives \(\pi_1(V)*\pi_1(V')=\pi_1(M)=1\) because \(\Sigma=S^3\), so both sides are simply connected. The integer homology sequence for the cut and the isomorphism on top boundary fundamental classes give \(H_j(V)=H_j(V')=0\) for \(j>0\). Thus the two smooth caps \(\widehat V,\widehat V'\) are homotopy spheres. Removing the cap balls and using the original boundary map gives \(M\cong\widehat V\#\widehat V'\). A change of oriented gluing compares two maps of \(S^3\) whose composite is orientation preserving and extends over a ball by Cerf. Choose the boundary round scale and \(\nu(u)=-H/3\) simultaneously to make each side totally geodesic. Attaching a hemisphere now matches induced metric and its first normal derivative, hence gives a piecewise smooth \(C^{1,1}\) metric with no curvature measure on the seam. Its exact chiral energies are those of the original side: the cap Weyl curvature vanishes and the seam has zero volume. They agree because the side signatures vanish and the round eta is zero, or by taking smooth approximants and applying the closed signature equality. The common value is \(e_V\) for \(\widehat V\) and \(e_{V'}\) for \(\widehat V'\). The split \(e=e_V+e_{V'}\) is exact on \(M\).

The smooth cap metrics from the §5 approximation have energies converging to the two specified values. Matching a first jet does not imply any exact equality in these smooth classes. For example a round totally geodesic hypersurface can have nonzero electric Weyl curvature on it by prescribing a nonzero trace-free second normal metric derivative in a product collar. A smooth metric agreeing with that side at the cut cannot attach an exactly conformally flat cap across it without alteration. A smooth conformally flat matching collar would be an extra hypothesis under which an exact smooth realization can be sought directly.

**Proposition 8.1(2).** Interpret the two positive sign hypotheses as strict positivity of the matched conformal Yamabe quotients. This guarantees positive smooth approximating classes by continuity in §5. Positivity of the original ambient class or of the induced round boundary does not by itself supply either cap sign in this proof. From \(e=e_V+e_{V'}<2T\) one side has limiting energy \(<T\), and the corresponding approximating positive metrics have energies \(<T\) for all sufficiently large indices. Chang–Gursky–Yang 2003, Theorem A, assumes a smooth closed positive Yamabe class with four-index \(\int |W|^2<16\pi^2\chi\). On a homotopy four-sphere \(\chi=2\) and the integral is \(8e\), giving exactly \(e<T\), strictly. The theorem gives \(S^4\) or \(\mathbf{RP}^4\), and the latter is excluded by simple connectedness. One summand is standard and can be removed from the connected sum. Both individual strict inequalities give two standard summands. Equality at \(T\) is outside this limiting argument. [Chang–Gursky–Yang 2003, Theorem A and the norm discussion on p. 107](https://numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf).

**Proposition 8.1(3) and final paragraph.** In addition to the two positive cap signs and \(e<2T\), suppose \(M\) is nonstandard and \([g]\) is a smooth minimizer in the positive classes on \(M\). Label the standard side cap \(\widehat V\). Then \(\widehat V'\cong M\) and its positive smooth capped approximation has energies \(e_{V'}+o(1)\). Each is at least \(e\) by the infimum property. Hence \(e_{V'}\ge e\), and \(e_V=0\). Each Weyl block vanishes on \(V\) by smoothness and nonnegative integral. The parent attainment manuscript offers such a minimizer on an entered type as a separate working theorem, which is not used to infer a cap sign or invoked in this conditional argument. [Companion attainment statement, blob `bcc19e67ef1aed4890cf391d87a384a98337bfab`](../../../06_bour_flow_and_fixed_type_attainment/manuscript/fixed_type_attainment.tex).

The conformally flat side with spherical umbilic boundary develops onto a round ball. One way to justify this step is to use the flat conformal structure on the simply connected compact side, with its smooth flat Cartan connection up to the boundary, to develop locally diffeomorphically into \(S^4\). In these charts umbilic connected boundary patches lie in round spheres, hence the connected boundary maps into a single round sphere. Its boundary developing map is a covering of that sphere and therefore a diffeomorphism. The adjacent side near the boundary is well-defined by the normal and connectedness. Fill the other side of that round sphere by the corresponding round ball and extend the developing map over it by the model inclusion. The seam's developing charts make this a local diffeomorphism, now from a compact connected closed manifold onto \(S^4\), hence an isomorphism of conformal structures by the covering argument. The original side is then conformally equivalent to the round ball. This claim is about the conformal class.

The manuscript's phrase “every free cut” in the final paragraph drops the positive cap signs. Keep those hypotheses explicitly.
