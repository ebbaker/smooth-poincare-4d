## A simultaneous bound in the physical band

Use \(I=[a,b]\), \(a<b\), a fixed product marking \(Z\times I\), the induced metric \(h_t\), and the increasing unit normal \(\nu\). Set
\[
 V_t=R_g-2\operatorname{Ric}_g(\nu,\nu)+\tfrac23H^2,\qquad
 \mathcal F_t(u)=\int_Z(8|du|_{h_t}^2+V_tu^2)\,d\mu_{h_t}.
\]
The scalar Gauss equation in the [parent Theorem 4.1](../../manuscript/cuts_of_subcritical_conformal_classes.tex) gives \(R_{h_t}=V_t-|B^\circ|_{h_t}^2\), with \(B(Y,W)=g(\nabla_Y\nu,W)\) and \(H=\mathrm{tr}_{h_t}B\). In an entering gauge the positive minimum of \(R_g-2\operatorname{Ric}_g(\nu,\nu)\) on the compact band gives a positive lower bound for \(V_t\). The proof below needs only the stated lower bound for \(V_t\) in the representative actually measured.

Assume the fixed coordinate charts and fixed smooth \(\chi_j\) in the request, with each support compactly contained in its chart, \(\sum_j\chi_j^2=1\), \(mI\le h_t\le MI\) in coordinates, and \(\sum_j|d\chi_j|_{h_t}^2\le L\) for every \(t\). Let \(S_{\mathrm E}\) denote any Euclidean constant in the convention \(\|v\|_{L^6(dx)}^2\le S_{\mathrm E}\int_{\mathbf R^3}|Dv|^2dx\), for compactly supported tests. For \(v=\chi_j u\), zero extension gives such a test in the reference coordinates. Write \(d\mu_{h_t}=\omega\,dx\) with \(\omega=\sqrt{\det h_t}\). The upper bound \(\omega\le M^{3/2}\) takes power \(1/3\) under the \(L^6\) norm squared, giving \(\|v\|_{6,h_t}^2\le M^{1/2}\|v\|_{6,dx}^2\). The pointwise Euclidean energy conversion is
\[
 |Dv|^2\,dx\le \frac{\lambda_{\max}(h_t)}{\omega}\,|dv|_{h_t}^2\,d\mu_{h_t}
 \le\frac{\sqrt M}{m}\,|dv|_{h_t}^2\,d\mu_{h_t}.
\]
Here \(\det h_t\ge m^2\lambda_{\max}(h_t)\), since the two other eigenvalues are at least \(m\). Thus a valid chart constant is \(D=S_{\mathrm E} M/m\). Using separately the coarse inequalities \(\omega\ge m^{3/2}\) and \(\lambda_{\max}\le M\) would instead give \(S_{\mathrm E}(M/m)^{3/2}\), which is valid but weaker.

Since \(\|u\|_6^2=\|u^2\|_3\), the \(L^3\) triangle inequality gives \(\|u\|_{6,h_t}^2\le\sum_j\|\chi_j u\|_{6,h_t}^2\). In the sum of the localized Dirichlet energies the derivative cross term vanishes by \(\sum_j\chi_jd\chi_j=0\). The exact integrand before bounding \(\sum_j|d\chi_j|^2\) is \(|du|_{h_t}^2+u^2\sum_j|d\chi_j|_{h_t}^2\). This handles chart overlaps without a separate overlap multiplicity. Therefore
\[
 \|u\|_{6,h_t}^2\le D\left(\int_Z |du|_{h_t}^2\,d\mu_{h_t}+L\|u\|_{2,h_t}^2\right).
\]
All these comparisons are simultaneous in \(t\) and apply by density to \(H^1\) tests. If \(V_t\ge q_*>0\), compare the coefficients of the two terms to obtain
\[
 \inf_{u\ne0}\frac{\mathcal F_t(u)}{\|u\|_{6,h_t}^2}\ \ge\
 C_{\mathrm{loc}}=\frac{m}{S_{\mathrm E} M}\min\{8,q_*/L\}.
\]
If \(L=0\), the second term of the localized bound vanishes and the minimum is read as \(8\).

One can take \(S_{\mathrm E}=4/3\) without using the optimal Euclidean result. For \(f=|v|^4\), along each full coordinate line through \(x\) the total variation is at least \(2f(x)\). Set \(F_i\) equal to the integral of \(|\partial_i f|\) over that coordinate line, which is a function of the remaining two coordinates. Then \(f^{3/2}\le 2^{-3/2}(F_1F_2F_3)^{1/2}\). Cauchy–Schwarz in the first coordinate and then in the other two gives \(\int (F_1F_2F_3)^{1/2}\le\prod_i\|\partial_i f\|_1^{1/2}\). Taking power \(2/3\) and using Hölder on \(4|v|^3|\partial_i v|\) gives \(\|v\|_6\le2\prod_i\|\partial_i v\|_2^{1/3}\) for \(v\ne0\). Square and use arithmetic-geometric mean.

**Nonnegative floor.** \(V_t\ge0\) alone is insufficient. If \(V_t\) vanishes identically on a component for some \(t\), the constant on that component extended by zero on all others has zero quotient. With \(V_t\ge0\), an independently verified common spectral estimate \(\mathcal F_t(u)\ge\lambda_*\|u\|_2^2\) for \(\lambda_*>0\) gives instead \(\{D(1/8+L/\lambda_*)\}^{-1}\). For example, if smooth \(V_t\ge0\) is nonzero somewhere on every connected component at every \(t\) on the compact parameter interval, such a \(\lambda_*\) exists. To see uniformity, assume a sequence of \(L^2\)-normalized tests with form tending to zero and take a convergent subsequence of times. Uniform ellipticity, \(V_t\ge0\), and compactness in \(L^2\) imply a nonzero limit function which is constant on each component. Its integral against the limiting \(V_t\) has to vanish, a contradiction. This existence argument alone supplies no measured numerical \(\lambda_*\).

## Cubic bending and the parameter

Write the metric as \(N^2dt^2+h_t(dx+X\,dt,dx+X\,dt)\), so that \(N>0\) and \(\nu=N^{-1}(\partial_t-X)\). The tangential components of the Lie derivative along \(\nu\) are \(2B=N^{-1}(\partial_t h_t-\mathcal L_X h_t)\), because terms differentiating the \(N^{-1}\) coefficient pair with the normal and vanish. Set \(A_t=(\partial_t h_t-\mathcal L_X h_t)^\circ\), with the trace removed using \(h_t\). Then
\[
 K(t):=\int_Z|B^\circ|^3\,d\mu_{h_t}=\tfrac18\int_ZN^{-3}|A_t|_{h_t}^3\,d\mu_{h_t},\qquad
 \frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^bK(t)\,dt
 =\frac{1}{8(b-a)}\int_{Z\times I}N^{-4}|A_t|_{h_t}^3\,d\mu_g.
\]
In the right volume expression \(d\mu_g=N\,dt\,d\mu_{h_t}\); the shift has no determinant factor. The factor \(1/8\) is from \((2N)^{-3}\).

By Hölder \(\int_Z |B^\circ|^2u^2\,d\mu_{h_t}\le K(t)^{2/3}\|u\|_{6,h_t}^2\). Hence the Yamabe form is at least \((C_{\mathrm{loc}}-K(t)^{2/3})\|u\|_6^2\). If \(\frac1{b-a}\int_a^b K(t)\,dt<C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{3/2}\), some interior slice satisfies the strict inequality \(K(t)<C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{3/2}\) by continuity or by averaging. On that slice the first eigenvalue of the conformal Laplacian is positive, since \(\|u\|_6^2\ge \operatorname{Vol}(Z,h_t)^{-2/3}\|u\|_2^2\). A positive first eigenfunction on each connected component gives positive scalar curvature in the induced conformal class by the dimension-three conformal law. This argument uses the quotient of the actual slice, in the convention of the [parent marked bending calculation](../../notes/MARKED_BAND_AND_MINIMIZER_20261127.md).

- **Constant ambient scaling, \(\tilde g=\lambda^2g\).** The ellipticity endpoints become \(\lambda^2m,\lambda^2M\). Both \(L\) and \(q_*\) scale by \(\lambda^{-2}\), so \(C_{\mathrm{loc}}\) is unchanged. In the same coordinates \(N\) is multiplied by \(\lambda\), \(X\) stays fixed and the covariant \(A_t\) is multiplied by \(\lambda^2\) while its \(h_t\)-norm stays fixed. The \(N^{-3}\) factor and \(d\mu_{h_t}\) scale oppositely. The three-dimensional quotient and \(K(t)\) are unchanged.
- **Height changes, \(t=f(s)\) with \(f'>0\).** At corresponding points \(N_s=f'N_t\), \(X_s=f'X_t\), \(A_s=f'A_t\), and the slice quantities and \(K\) agree. For constant \(f'\), normalized parameter averages agree. For general \(f'\) the normalized measure on the same family is reweighted, so the average is a certificate for the selected height. The increasing coorientation is preserved. A reversal must also reverse the assignment of face orientations.
- **Moving slice coordinates, \(x=\phi_t(y)\).** Let \(Y_t=\dot\phi_t\circ\phi_t^{-1}\). The pullback metric gives \(\tilde h_t=\phi_t^*h_t\), \(\tilde N=N\circ\phi_t\) and \(\tilde X=(D\phi_t)^{-1}(X\circ\phi_t+\dot\phi_t)\). Therefore \(\partial_t\tilde h_t=\phi_t^*(\partial_t h_t+\mathcal L_Yh_t)\) and \(\mathcal L_{\tilde X}\tilde h_t=\phi_t^*(\mathcal L_{X+Y}h_t)\), so \(\tilde A_t=\phi_t^*A_t\). Transporting the native projection means composing it with \((t,y)\mapsto(t,\phi_t(y))\). The scalar field \(V_t\) and its floor pull back, and the geometric quotient and \(K(t)\) are unchanged. For a fixed remeasured chart atlas in the new product coordinates, the ellipticity and partition constants can change. A large \(\partial_t\tilde h_t\) alone does not measure bending.
- **General conformal representatives.** If \(\hat g=e^{2w}g\), then \(\hat B^\circ=e^wB^\circ\) as a covariant tensor and the cubic bending density is unchanged on the fixed slice. The quotient with \(V_t\) is invariant as well: the Gauss equation writes its numerator as the Yamabe form plus \(\int |B^\circ|^2u^2\), and \(\hat u=e^{-w/2}u\) preserves both integrals and \(\|u\|_6^2\). The coordinate \(m,M,L\), the computed \(q_*\) and the numerical chart lower bound depend on the chosen representative and chart data. In particular a positive potential floor in one gauge cannot simply be transferred to another. The Euclidean \(S_{\mathrm E}\) is fixed. [Representative comparison in SEAM E05](../../02_seams_and_marked_cuts/notes/E05_UNIFORM_BENDING.md).

## Variable potential deficit and the averaging measure

Choose a positive reference level \(q_0(t)>0\), which need not be a pointwise floor for \(V_t\), and set
\[
 C_t=D^{-1}\min\{8,q_0(t)/L\},\qquad
 D_t=\|(q_0(t)-V_t)_+\|_{L^{3/2}(h_t)} .
\]
The partition estimate applied to \(8\int|du|^2+q_0(t)\|u\|_2^2\), followed by Hölder for the potential deficit, gives \(\mathcal F_t(u)\ge(C_t-D_t)\|u\|_6^2\). The convention at \(L=0\) is the same as above. Hence the induced Yamabe quotient is bounded below by \(C_t-D_t-K(t)^{2/3}\). Any nonpositive-sign slice necessarily satisfies
\[
 K(t)\ge T_t:=[C_t-D_t]_+^{3/2}.
\]
The positive part reflects the absence of a positive threshold when \(C_t\le D_t\).

Let \(\mu\) be any chosen probability measure on the height interval, with the quantities measurable and integrable, and with no endpoint atoms if the averaging argument itself must select an interior height. If \(\int K\,d\mu<\int T_t\,d\mu(t)\), the set \(K<T_t\) has positive \(\mu\) measure and consists of positive slices. The test is invariant under a relabeling when transporting that same measure. If \(t=f(s)\), the corresponding measure in \(s\) is \((f^{-1})_\#\mu\) and all scalar integrands are composed with \(f\). This is different from choosing normalized Lebesgue measure anew after a nonlinear relabeling. When \(C_t-D_t\ge c>0\) uniformly, the nonpositive slice set has \(\mu\) measure at most \(\int K\,d\mu/c^{3/2}\). In particular \(\int K\,d\mu<c^{3/2}\) gives a positive lower bound \(1-\int K\,d\mu/c^{3/2}\) on the fraction of positive slices, measured using the chosen \(\mu\). More generally a strict integral margin \(\delta=\int(T_t-K(t))\,d\mu(t)>0\) yields the lower bound \(\delta/\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_\mu T_t\) when the latter supremum is finite, since \((T_t-K)_+\le(\operatorname*{ess\,sup}T_t)\mathbf 1_{\{K<T_t\}}\).
