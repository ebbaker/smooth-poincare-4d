# A positive sweepout through the actual first-width cut near the round metric

27 November 2026. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI Codex, selected Astra/ultra, actual lead settings unavailable. GEO-E12–E13. Smooth Riemannian signature, unit round radius. The assumptions below concern convergence on a metrically identified S⁴. The first fundamental-class width is unrestricted and integral.

**Alignment lemma.** Let \(g_j\) converge smoothly to the unit round metric and let smooth closed embedded hypersurfaces \(\Sigma_j\) converge as single graphs in at least \(C^2\) to a great equator E. For all sufficiently large j a construction using the pair \((g_j,\Sigma_j)\) yields a Morse function with precisely two critical points, zero set exactly \(\Sigma_j\), and strictly positive induced scalar curvature on every regular level. It is immaterial whether a particular first nonconstant eigenfunction points in the normal direction to the limiting equator.

**Selecting the normal direction.** Use the direct sum V of the real function eigenspaces of \(-\Delta_{g_j}\) with eigenvalues in (2,7). On the round sphere the eigenvalues through the second nonconstant cluster are 0, 4, 10. The 4-eigenspace consists of the five linear coordinate heights restricted from R⁵. On nearby metrics V has dimension five: the min-max characterization yields eigenvalue convergence, and uniform elliptic estimates for normalized eigenfunctions yield subsequential convergence in C² to an orthonormal basis of the height space. On its unit sphere in ambient volume L² minimize \(\int_{\Sigma_j}u^2\,dA_j\). On E the associated symmetric quadratic form has a one-dimensional kernel, the linear normal coordinate, and is positive on its orthogonal complement. The restriction matrices converge in the above bases. Thus the smallest eigenvalue is simple for large j, and the selected u tends up to sign in C² to the normal height. Rotating the limiting sphere only changes these coordinates. No choice within a split first eigenvalue is required.

**Putting the given hypersurface at level zero.** On a fixed band \(|u|<3\delta\) about its zero set, with \(0<\delta\) small, use the flow of \(X=\nabla u/|\nabla u|^2\). The time parameter is exactly the change of u. This gives coordinates \((y,t)\), where \(y\in u^{-1}(0)\) and \(t=u\). The global single graph hypothesis and transversality imply \(\Sigma_j=\{t=v_j(y)\}\) there, with \(v_j\to0\) in C². For the C² control of these coordinates use C³ control of u from its eigenfunction equation. Choose a smooth fixed cutoff equal to one for \(|t|\le\delta\) and supported in \(|t|<2\delta\); set \(f=t-\chi(t)v_j(y)\) inside and \(f=u\) elsewhere. For large j, \(\partial_t f=1-\chi'v_j>0\). It follows that the zero level is precisely the prescribed cut and that the change is C²-small. A choice of sign is equivalent to interchanging the sides. The procedure uses an auxiliary fixed cutoff and angular orientation only up to sign.

**Regular scalars and the endpoints.** The transformed f converges in C² to a round linear height. On the compact middle region, gradients are bounded away from zero and the tangential Gauss expression for each regular level converges uniformly to the round expression, which is at least 6. In two geodesically convex neighborhoods of the poles the Hessian of a height is uniformly positive or negative definite. This persists, as does positivity of \(R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)\) for every unit normal. Thus the Gauss expression is strictly positive also on every regular leaf in these two neighborhoods. Strict convexity or concavity there gives at most one critical point in each; existence follows from the maximum and minimum of f and the nonvanishing gradient elsewhere (or the definite Hessian and the boundary value separation). The normalized gradient away from the two poles sweeps each side from \(f=0\). This construction specifies a gradient flow, without requiring a regularity theorem at a singular mean curvature flow.

**Application to the width.** For the smooth topology the equatorial limit also follows directly from the index. Round latitudes bound the widths by a fixed area, using the same smooth sweep for the converging metrics. Sharp, Appendix A, Theorem A.6 and Corollary A.7 give one-sheet smooth graphical convergence along a subsequence and index at most one in the positive-Ricci round limit. A closed embedded limiting hypersurface in S⁴ is two-sided. On a round minimal hypersurface let ν be its normal in the sphere and let a be a constant vector in R⁵. In a geodesic tangential frame the Euclidean Laplacian of ν equals −|B|²ν: the tangential term is the divergence of the shape operator (zero by Codazzi and minimality), the radial term is its trace (zero), and the remaining normal term is −|B|²ν. Thus the Jacobi operator J=−Δ−3−|B|² maps ⟨a,ν⟩ to −3⟨a,ν⟩. Some such function is nonzero. If B is nonzero somewhere, the constant Rayleigh test gives the lowest eigenvalue strictly below −3. The −3 mode then gives a second negative eigenvalue. Hence B vanishes, and by connectedness, completeness and embeddedness the limit is a great equator. [Sharp, Appendix A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1501.02703#page=16) · [Zhou, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2112v2#page=2)

Passing to a subsequence and a rotation gives exactly the graph hypothesis above. A contradiction sequence therefore gives one neighborhood in the smooth topology on which the conclusion holds for every such cut, with no uniqueness hypothesis on width realizers. Equivalently one may pull back by a diffeomorphism and normalize the scale first. The C² graph hypothesis by itself is enough for the alignment lemma with smooth metric convergence. The pinching inequality provides neither this global convergence nor a marked equator on an unknown smooth type.
