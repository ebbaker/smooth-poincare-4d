# The remaining first-width comparison

27 November 2026. Prepared for Edward B. Baker III with OpenAI Codex, lead selected Astra/ultra, serving settings unavailable. Input and scope as in [GEO-E04](../../research-progress/handoffs/E04_MINMAX.md) and [GEO-E06](../../research-progress/handoffs/E06_GERM.md). Smooth connected closed oriented homotopy four-sphere, fixed smooth entering gauge. This is the reduction proposed for G3 after the [failed-route assessment](../../reviews/LOCAL_VERSUS_WIDTH_20261127.md).

**One open statement.** For every closed embedded connected two-sided minimal hypersurface in this gauge whose area equals the fundamental-class width, the scalar Schur complement below is strictly positive. Equivalently, every embedded two-sided index-one minimal hypersurface for which the scalar is nonpositive has a competitor which is a closed embedded minimal hypersurface of strictly smaller area. Ties matter. The first formulation quantifies over width realizers; in the second the scalar test applies to index-one candidates, which suffice among possible width realizers. The equivalence uses Zhou’s characterization and the sweepout associated to each two-sided minimal hypersurface. Selecting some favorable member of a symmetry orbit alone gives no assertion about other realizers.

Let φ>0 be the normalized Jacobi ground state of an index-one candidate. Put L=−8Δ+RΣ, C the compression of L to φ-perp, w the projection of Lφ onto that space. The tested number is
\[
 S=\langle\phi,L\phi\rangle-\langle w,C^{-1}w\rangle.
\]
On φ-perp the form L=2J+6(−Δ)+R+|B|² is bounded below by min R>0. Completing the square on the form domain proves the exact assertion, including equality. In the homogeneous germ the cross term is zero and S is literally the negative constant intrinsic scalar. This formulation specifies one positive elliptic solve and one scalar comparison on the candidate and a possible global certificate excluding it.

**Approaches tried.**

1. The parent’s index bound and orthogonal balancing give a coercive complement. A second test function only lowers the Schur complement after minimization. The flat and negative Berger collars explicitly realize the remaining sign possibilities.
2. In the parent Hölder estimate at H=0, use the constant test on the homogeneous slice. There P is constant and |B|²=P−RΣ, so when RΣ≤0 the strict smallness condition fails even against the upper bound P Vol^(2/3) for the Sobolev threshold. Shrinking to a thinner collar preserves its central data. This calculation and E06 defeat inference from the local entering cone. A same-cut global integral estimate from SEAM would be new input.
3. Compare total areas to the actual global width. On the round join the closed minimal meridian with area 2π² strictly beats the orbit of area 16π²/(3√3). On an untwisted symmetric positive-Ricci join the meridian still gives the explicit integral upper bound in the orbit note. An arbitrary low-area positive cut must also be minimal (or come with an entire sweepout of smaller maximal area) for this comparison. Neither the Berger germ nor the unrestricted first-width cut comes with such a competitor.
4. Try continuing the Berger model as an unaltered homogeneous cylinder to obtain a closed candidate. In its Gaussian collar H(s)=−qs with q=3c−RΣ>0. The explicit exponentials have strictly positive orbit sizes at every finite s. Extending the same formula to either end therefore supplies no smooth pole. Periodic matching also fails since H is strict monotone there. Even if new cap functions close the two ends preserving strict pinching, comparing with nonsymmetric sweepouts remains necessary.
5. On an invariant cut in a one-tube metric a favorable example is the totally geodesic meridian in an invariant entering gauge. The principal orbit in any such smooth untwisted join has intrinsic positive scalar. In the raw equality model an induced meridian is conformal to a spherical metric, but the ambient metric, selection rule, and regularity required by Zhou still have to be checked before designating any of these as the unrestricted first-width cut.

**Alternatives and reopening inputs.** Test whether an actual closed filling of the Berger jets admits a sweepout with smaller maximal area than the central slice. Shooting a family of normal profile ODEs by itself cannot certify all unrestricted competitors; an area calibration, rigidity theorem, or lower bound for all sweepouts could. A global integral inequality using a two-sided harmonic extension may control the Schur correction; here no boundary sign for the corresponding second fundamental form is supplied. A perturbative compactness argument near a metrically identified round sphere can decide a subclass, with no uniform closeness estimate furnished by the open Schouten cone. The independent reading will test additional approaches before this reduction is assessed.

**Independent-reader proposal tested.** E11 proposed a quaternionic angular competitor on two-pole SU(2) fillings. The [global area test](../../notes/GLOBAL_AREA_TEST_20261127.md) constructs the closed minimal S³, computes its area and evaluates the comparison on the round sphere and on the actual part of the Berger germ. Both cap integrals are still needed on the triaxial completion. The alternative of a biaxial completion fails for a stronger reason: at peak squashing the orbit is umbilic, so strict ambient entry prohibits any orbit of nonpositive scalar. In the near-round class [E12](../../research-progress/handoffs/E12_ROUND.md) resolves the actual width question, and [E13](../../notes/ALIGNED_FIRST_WIDTH_20261127.md) places each chosen width surface at the center of a positive sweepout.

**Received from SEAM.** The [fixed-gauge result](../../../02_seams_and_marked_cuts/research-progress/handoffs/TO_GEO_MINIMAL_CUT_20261127.md) gives a positive lower Sobolev constant simultaneous over smooth embedded cuts of one entering gauge via a fixed isometric Euclidean embedding. The upper bound on the bending of the same first-width cut is the remaining estimate in this approach. In E06 the central constant test already precludes the required strict smallness locally. The [actual trefoil calculation](../../notes/TREFOIL_RADIAL_CUT_20261127.md) also distinguishes an index-one positive radial orbit from a closed first-width selection.
