# To the parent • proposed corrections to Sections 4, 6 and 7

1. In the remark after Theorem 4.1 require strict σ₂(B)>0 for strict two-convexity. If H>0 and σ₂(B)≥0 the pairwise sums are nonnegative, and (H,0,0) is allowed. This equality is realized by radial cap leaves.
2. In the introduction state the two strict implications of Theorem 7.2. At equality a Hessian-only classification of a uniform lower bound is false. The fixed Hessian (1,1,1,−1/2) and the cubic term cxw²/2 yield regular scalars 4c/x on the x-axis. The Morse theorem as stated survives.
3. After Corollary 7.3 use uniform lower boundedness of regular scalar as the recognition hypothesis; σ₂(B)≥0 is sufficient in an entering metric. The converse for lower boundedness on a standard sphere holds for every smooth metric by any smooth two-extrema Morse height. Necessity of the stronger inequality for the prescribed gauge does not follow.
4. In the paragraph before Proposition 7.5 cite Zhou's 2015 result for the orientable, multiplicity-one, index-one case. The early theorem states multiplicity two on a nonorientable support. If stability of that one-sided support is mentioned, cite the later paper and specify normal-bundle variations; positivity of Ric rules out stability on *all* scalar functions of its orientable cover. The homotopy sphere excludes the one-sided case. The statement and form proof of Proposition 7.5 pass.
5. For the equality example of Section 6 the two one-sided normal Ricci curvatures are +1/−1 in the raw product and +1/+2 in the indicated compact representative. The seam has no classical two-sided area Hessian. Neither representative is the smooth entering gauge. Start ordinary flow at perturbed mean-convex leaves and distinguish the unrestricted width from invariant critical orbits. On the round join that orbit has full index 6. At a singular critical surface distinguish a rank-two definite normal Hessian from an isolated Morse saddle. A two-sphere collapse is compatible with strictly positive scalar on every regular principal leaf.
