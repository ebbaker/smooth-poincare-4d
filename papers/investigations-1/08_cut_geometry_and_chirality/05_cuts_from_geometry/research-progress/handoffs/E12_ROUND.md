Inputs are the unit round metric \(\gamma\) on the identified standard smooth \(S^4\), the parent manuscript at `bc2c1bbccc1d244a9d456ffe2fcaf64e368290b0`, and the integral width convention checked in [GEO-E04](E04_MINMAX.md). These are analytic arguments with no numerical inputs.

For sufficiently small \(C^k\) distance from \(\gamma\), \(k\ge4\), any connected embedded index-one realizer of the one-parameter integral fundamental-class width is a small normal graph over some round equator after identifying the sphere. Its induced scalar curvature is positive. Separately, sufficiently small \(C^2\) distance suffices for every real first nonconstant eigenfunction to have exactly two nondegenerate critical points and regular \(S^3\) levels with sectional curvature uniformly bounded below by a positive constant. The two tests are statements in the same metric, with no identification of the width cut as an eigenfunction level.

## Width bound and compactness with varying metrics

Let \(g_j\to\gamma\) in \(C^k\), \(k\ge4\), with smooth \(g_j\), and take any sequence of connected, closed, embedded index-one width realizers \(\Sigma_j\). Zhou's positive Ricci theorem supplies such a \(\Sigma_j\) for all large \(j\), with multiplicity one and least area among all closed embedded minimal hypersurfaces. Smoothness, simple connectivity, orientation and \(n=3\) verify the remaining hypotheses. All such hypersurfaces are two-sided on \(S^4\). The round latitudes of one coordinate function, oriented as boundaries of its sublevel sets, form a flat-continuous sweepout from the empty set to the whole sphere. They have no concentration of mass. For example their round area measures depend continuously, in the weak topology, on the parameter, including zero mass at the poles. The parameter interval is compact and those measures are atomless. Uniform comparison of the \(g_j\) and \(\gamma\) three-dimensional area elements transfers this property to each \(g_j\). Discretization preserves the fundamental class and bounds its width by the maximum of the continuous areas. Uniform comparability of the induced three-dimensional area elements gives

\[
\operatorname{area}_{g_j}(\Sigma_j)
=\mathcal W(g_j)\le (1+o(1))\operatorname{area}_\gamma(S^3)
=(1+o(1)),2\pi^2 .
\]

This comparison uses latitudes as sweepout competitors. There is no need for a particular latitude to be \(g_j\)-minimal. [Zhou, Theorem 1.1, Proposition 5.1 and Theorems 5.5 and 5.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2112v2)

Sharp's changing-metric Theorem A.6 and Corollary A.7 assume smooth convergence of the ambient metrics. With \(C^\infty\) convergence they apply directly here with \(n=3\), bounded area and index, and positive Ricci curvature of the round limit. They give subsequential single-sheeted smooth graphical convergence. The stated theorem alone does not supply the finite-\(k\) conclusion. For finite \(k\), use its local stable input, Theorem A.3 and Remark A.4, and the following argument. [Sharp, *Compactness of minimal hypersurfaces with bounded index*, Appendix A, accepted author text, pp. 14 and 16](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/129477/7/WRAP-compactness-minimal-hypersurfaces-index-Sharp-2016.pdf) · [J. Differential Geom. 106 (2017), 317–339](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1497405628)

Regard the \(\Sigma_j\) as round-background varifolds \(V_j\). Their bounded mass gives a weakly convergent subsequence with limit \(V\). The \(g_j\) and \(\gamma\) area-density ratio tends to one uniformly on the Grassmann bundle. The first variation integrands for any fixed test vector field converge uniformly there by \(C^1\) convergence of the ambient metrics. Thus \(V\) is round-stationary. The ambient sectional curvatures and injectivity radii are uniformly controlled on the compact sphere. The Riemannian monotonicity inequality gives uniform bounds \(c s^3\le\operatorname{area}_{g_j}(\Sigma_j\cap B_s^{g_j}(q))\le C s^3\) at \(q\in\Sigma_j\) for all sufficiently small \(s>0\). The upper bound is the comparison with mass in a fixed radius, and the lower bound follows from the density one at \(q\). In particular \(V\) is nonzero and has no atoms, and any sequence of points of the \(\Sigma_j\) accumulates only at points of \(\operatorname{spt}V\). [De Lellis–Ramic, §7.2, (7.1)](https://www.numdam.org/article/AIF_2018__68_5_1909_0.pdf#page=31)

After a further diagonal subsequence, each ball from a countable coordinate basis is eventually either stable or unstable for the area second variation of \(\Sigma_j\), tested by variations compactly supported in that ball. Two disjoint unstable balls for the same \(j\) give two independent negative directions with zero mixed term. Hence at most one point has no eventually stable neighborhood. On every eventually stable ball, Sharp's local A.3–A.4 applies to the area integrands of \(g_j\). In fixed charts the integrands are smooth functions of the metric matrices and the plane variable. The \(C^k\) convergence gives the \(C^3\) integrand convergence required there, on unit normals, with uniform ellipticity and derivative bounds on smaller coordinate balls. A linear normalization of the metric matrix at a center and localization on uniformly smaller balls give the area-integrand normalization in (8)–(12), with uniform constants. Each hypersurface is smooth and orientable in the ball, and the local mass bound is uniform. Local compactness therefore gives a smooth round-minimal limit with integer multiplicity on those balls. A diagonal choice can cover their union. The possible exceptional point carries no mass. Thus \(V\) is integral and has a smooth point of positive integer density. This uses the local stable theorem, without needing a finite-regularity version of the full changing-metric statement or removability of the exceptional point.

## The limit is a multiplicity-one equator

Form the four-dimensional radial cone \(C(V)\) in \(\mathbb R^5\). The cone is stationary away from the origin. Indeed for a test vector field decomposed into radial and spherical parts, its tangential divergence on the cone consists of a radial term \(r^{-3}\partial_r(r^3 X_r)\) and a spherical tangential divergence divided by \(r\). Integrating with \(r^3dr\,dV\) kills the radial term by compact support and the angular term by stationarity of \(V\). The same stationarity holds across the vertex by a radial cutoff since cone mass in a radius-\(s\) ball is \(O(s^4)\). At a smooth point \(q\in\operatorname{spt}V\subset S^4\) the cone has integer density at least one. With \(\omega_d\) denoting the volume of the Euclidean unit \(d\)-ball, its density at infinity about \(q\) is

\[
\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{\|C(V)\|(B_r(q))}{\omega_4r^4}
=\frac{\|V\|(S^4)}{4\omega_4}
=\frac{\|V\|(S^4)}{2\pi^2}\le1 .
\]

Here the mass formula at the origin is exact by radial integration, and shifting the center changes the bounding ball radii by at most \(|q|\). Monotonicity about \(q\) is therefore an equality at all scales. The equality case makes the cone homogeneous about \(q\). Since it is smooth with density one there, it is the multiplicity-one tangent plane translated through \(q\). Its original homogeneity about the origin forces this plane to contain the origin. Thus \(V=|E|\) for a round equator \(E\), and the area tends to \(2\pi^2\). This provides the equality case of the area comparison, including for the finite-regularity varifold limit. [White, *Lectures on Minimal Surface Theory*, Theorem 3 and density-at-infinity discussion](https://www.math.stonybrook.edu/~bishop/classes/math638.F20/White_Min_Surf_Survey.pdf) · [Mazet–Rosenberg, introduction](https://www.idpoisson.fr/mazet/docu/leastminsurf.pdf)

The equatorial limit is globally graphical in at least \(C^2\) at finite \(k\). To see the point that stable compactness alone leaves open, suppose \(a_j=\max_{\Sigma_j}|B_{g_j}|\to\infty\) and choose maximizing points \(q_j\to q\in E\). Blow up at \(q_j\) by \(a_j\). In fixed translated linear coordinates the rescaled metrics tend to the Euclidean metric on bounded balls in at least \(C^3\), and the rescaled curvature satisfies \(\max |B|=1\) and \(|B|(0)=1\). A local sheet through the base point has a uniform graphical radius under this curvature bound. One can project to its initial tangent plane and lift short radial segments while the tangent angle is small, using the curvature bound and ambient coordinate bounds to preserve transversality. The graph has a uniform second-derivative bound, and interior estimates for the minimal graph equation give subsequential \(C^2\) convergence on a smaller disk to a Euclidean minimal graph \(\Gamma\) through \(0\), with \(|B_\Gamma|(0)=1\). [Sharp, introduction and proof of Lemma 4.1](https://wrap.warwick.ac.uk/id/eprint/129477/7/WRAP-compactness-minimal-hypersurfaces-index-Sharp-2016.pdf)

For sufficiently small fixed \(s>0\), the area in \(B_s^{g_j}(q_j)\) tends to \(\operatorname{area}_\gamma(E\cap B_s^\gamma(q))\) because the limiting ball boundary has zero equator area. Let \(R>0\) lie within the graphical neighborhood at rescaled distance. Uniform ambient monotonicity gives, for all large \(j\),

\[
\frac{a_j^3\operatorname{area}_{g_j}(\Sigma_j\cap B_{R/a_j}^{g_j}(q_j))}{\omega_3 R^3}
\le e^{Cs}\frac{\operatorname{area}_{g_j}(\Sigma_j\cap B_s^{g_j}(q_j))}{\omega_3s^3} .
\]

Letting \(j\to\infty\) bounds the ratio for \(\Gamma\) at \(R\) by the limit of the right side. Letting \(s\downarrow0\) bounds it by one. The graph has density one at the origin, so the Euclidean equality case makes it flat near \(0\), a contradiction. There is thus a uniform unscaled curvature bound. Graphical compactness now applies at every point. The support approaches \(E\) by the lower area bound, and the single sheet of the limit forces one graph throughout a tubular neighborhood by locality and compactness. In more detail, every point of \(E\) has a small neighborhood in which the graph through it is the unique converging sheet, since any additional sheet through a converging sequence of points has the same uniform graphical radius and contributes positive multiplicity. Nearest-point projection to \(E\) is consequently a degree-one covering for large \(j\). The graph tends to zero in \(C^2\) and is ambient isotopic to \(E\). This gives the desired graph convergence with no appeal to a \(C^\infty\) hypothesis at finite \(k\).

For the central cut the contracted Gauss equation is \(R_{\Sigma_j}=R_{g_j}-2\operatorname{Ric}_{g_j}(\nu_j,\nu_j)-|B_{g_j}|^2\). Hence \(R_{\Sigma_j}\to 6\) uniformly along the subsequence. The assertion that any such realizer is close to some equator and has positive induced scalar curvature in a single metric neighborhood follows by contradiction from the preceding subsequence proof. Neither a unique equator nor a unique realizer is required. The positive Yamabe sign follows already from the positive induced scalar curvature.

## Every first nonconstant eigenfunction is a sweepout height

This test uses only \(C^2\) metric convergence to \(\gamma\). Let \(u_j\) be any real eigenfunction for the first positive eigenvalue \(\lambda_j\) of \(-\Delta_{g_j}\), normalized by \(\|u_j\|_{L^2(g_j)}=1\). Rayleigh comparison uses the common space of nonconstant functions and the denominator \(\inf_{c\in\mathbb R}\|u-c\|_{L^2(g_j)}^2\). Uniform metric and measure comparisons give \(\lambda_j\to\lambda_1(\gamma)=4\). For this last value the coordinate functions give the upper bound and the integrated Bochner identity with \(\operatorname{Ric}_\gamma=3\gamma\), together with the Hessian trace inequality, gives the lower bound. Equality gives \(\nabla^2_\gamma u=-u\gamma\) on the first eigenspace. Solving this equation along unit-speed round geodesics identifies every member of that eigenspace with a linear coordinate restriction from \(\mathbb R^5\).

The normalized eigenfunctions are precompact in \(C^2\). In fixed charts the \(H^1\) bounds follow from the energy identity. In dimension four the Sobolev embedding gives uniform \(L^4\) bounds. The local \(W^{2,4}\) estimate and the next \(W^{2,p}\) estimate for any fixed \(p>4\) yield uniform Hölder bounds. Schauder estimates then give a uniform \(C^{2,\alpha}\) bound for some \(\alpha>0\). The ellipticity and coefficient regularity constants on the finite chart cover are uniform under \(C^2\) metric convergence. Any \(C^2\) limit of a subsequence is a unit round first eigenfunction

\[
u(x)=c_4\langle x,v\rangle, \qquad |v|=1, \qquad
c_4=\sqrt{5/\operatorname{Vol}_\gamma(S^4)}.
\]

After a round rotation of the comparison charts one may therefore work with fixed north and south poles. Choose small round geodesically convex caps about them. On each cap \(\nabla^2_\gamma u_j\) is uniformly definite with the sign of \(-u\), and on the complement the gradient is bounded away from zero. A cap cannot have two critical points, since along the round geodesic joining them the second derivative would have one strict sign and the endpoint derivatives would both vanish. The maximum and minimum supply one critical point in each cap, and Hessian definiteness makes them nondegenerate. Every regular level is diffeomorphic to \(S^3\), first by the Morse lemma near an extremum and then by transport using \(\nabla u_j/|\nabla u_j|^2\) on a compact regular band.

The regular levels also have uniform positive sectional curvature. On the caps \(\nabla^2_{g_j}u_j\) is definite, so the determinant of \(B_{\mathrm{level}}=\nabla_{g_j}^2u_j|_{T\mathrm{level}}/|\nabla_{g_j}u_j|\) on every tangent two-plane is nonnegative. On the belt the gradient is bounded below and the Hessian is uniformly \(-u g_j+o(1)\), so this determinant is bounded below by \(-o(1)\). Gauss then gives sectional curvature at least \(1/4\) everywhere on every regular level for large \(j\), allowing levels that cross the transition. Scalar curvature is at least \(3/2\). The definite-Hessian argument handles the region where the level gradient can tend to zero. In a contradiction sequence the normalized \(u_j\) were arbitrary, so the conclusions hold uniformly over the full first eigenspace, including changes of multiplicity.

## Scope and sources inspected

Sharp's accepted author PDF was read at Theorem 2.3 and Lemma 4.1, Appendix A (8)–(12), Theorem A.3, Remark A.4, Theorem A.6 and Corollary A.7. The relevant portions of Zhou and the two monotonicity accounts are linked at use. The finite-regularity extraction, equality case, and spectral sweep proof are derived above. An independent check of the spectral proof also confirmed the cap and belt estimates.

Both statements transport under a supplied smooth diffeomorphism with standard \(S^4\) after applying the stated metric closeness to the pullback in fixed round coordinates. The width proof uses smooth embedded index-one width realizers and their area comparison, with positivity of Ricci curvature used to obtain them from Zhou. The eigenfunction proof uses only the near-round metric hypothesis in \(C^2\). No Weyl energy, chirality, entering-gauge pinching, Bach-flatness, bumpy genericity, curvature flow or energy descent is required in either proof. The conclusions give no implication from those weaker intrinsic hypotheses to a near-round neighborhood on an unidentified smooth homotopy sphere.
