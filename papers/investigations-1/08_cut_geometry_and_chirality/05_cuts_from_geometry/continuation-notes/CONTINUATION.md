## After the first horizon

The rows above are the first horizon. The directions below serve the components and are listed in the order in which they are likely to matter; the lead may replace them. A statement marked † was derived while this note was written and has not been read independently: it is a first packet for a Sol reader, not an input.

- **What the hypothesis constrains.** The scalar curvature of regular levels is bounded below away from the critical points for any function, so the hypothesis of Corollary 7.3 is a condition at critical points, and on the standard sphere a function that satisfies it exists in every metric. The route has content only when the function is produced by the geometry. State, for each proposed function, what produces it.
- **Critical surfaces** (G2). † For the tube of radius \(r\) about a surface \(F\), \(\sigma_2(B)=-\langle\vec H_F,n\rangle/r+O(1)\), with \(n\) the unit normal of \(F\) that points to the leaf. So leaves near a critical surface of normal index two have scalar curvature bounded below only if \(F\) is minimal, and in an entering gauge they have positive type only if \(F\) is a sphere or a projective plane, by the argument of Corollary 4.3. The core of the model class of GEO-E02 is totally geodesic. If GEO-E03 confirms this, the index-two events that survive are collapses of tubes onto minimal spheres.
- **What such events recognize** (G4). † A sweepout whose only index-two events are collapses of tubes onto spheres presents \(M\) as two boundary sums of copies of \(S^1\times B^3\) and \(S^2\times D^2\), glued along their boundary. The presentation spheres of Proposition 7.4 have this form, so the exclusion has to use the geometry of each collapse. Each event is a one-tube form: send its exterior to [tubes and exteriors](../../03_tubes_and_exteriors/README.md) and ask what the bounds of that child say about a thin tube.
- **The cut** (G3). † With \(H=0\), statement (3) of Theorem 4.1 says that the minimal cut is positive as soon as \(\lVert B\rVert_{L^3}^2\) is below the constant of that statement. Step 1 is then a bound on the total curvature of an index-one minimal hypersurface by the geometry of the gauge; ask [seams and marked cuts](../../02_seams_and_marked_cuts/README.md) for the constant. The search among metrics of cohomogeneity one in the plan row tests whether such a bound can hold.
- **A falsifiable first claim** (G4). State the claim of GEO-B01 so that the model class can refute it, test it on every class of Section 6 before any general argument, and change the cut before the flow: the symmetric flow from the totally geodesic seam of the model class already collapses onto the core.
- **Proposition 7.4 as a test.** An argument that uses only the diffeomorphism types of the leaves, in any order, proves nothing. Record for each proposed argument where it uses the metric at the events.
- **The immersion from 04** (G6). Write the request to 04 in the first session, so that GEO-E01 is not what the child waits for at the end.
