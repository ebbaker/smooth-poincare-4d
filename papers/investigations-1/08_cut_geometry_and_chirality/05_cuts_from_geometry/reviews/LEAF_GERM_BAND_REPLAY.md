## Morse–Bott level calculation

The [E03 return](../research-progress/handoffs/E03_LEAVES.md) passes. In a frame parallel along \(x=\exp_p(rv)\), set \(Q=\nabla^2 f(p)^\sharp\), \(D=(\nabla_v\nabla^2f)^\sharp\), \(a=Qv\), \(b=Dv\), \(n=a/|a|\). Integrating the radial derivative of the gradient gives \(ra+r^2b/2+O(r^3)\). Thus its squared norm is \(r^2(|a|^2+r\langle a,b\rangle+O(r^2))\), and its unit direction is \(n+r\xi+O(r^2)\), with \(\xi=(b-\langle b,n\rangle n)/(2|a|)\). In an orthonormal frame completing the unit \(n\), the trace and norm of the restricted Hessian give
\[
\Phi(Q,n)=\sigma_2(Q)-(\operatorname{tr}Q)\langle Qn,n\rangle+|Qn|^2.
\]
The contraction of the Gauss equation gives \(R_\Sigma=P+2\Phi(Q_x,\nu)/|\nabla f|^2\). Differentiating \(\Phi\) as a polynomial in \(Q\) and \(n\) yields the differential in E03, including the angular contribution \(2\langle(Q^2-(\operatorname{tr}Q)Q)n,\xi\rangle\). Multiplying out the inverse squared gradient gives the stated \(r^{-2}\) and \(r^{-1}\) coefficients. Negating \(v\) negates \(a,D,n\) and preserves \(b,\xi\), so only the second coefficient is odd.

Invertibility of the normal Hessian \(A\) gives every limiting normal by \(v=A^{-1}n/|A^{-1}n|\). The leading sign of \(f-f(p)\) is the sign of \(\langle n,A^{-1}n\rangle\). For an indefinite \(A\) the null cone meets the unit sphere transversely, so its directions are accessible as limits from either side. Over a compact patch the remainder is uniformly bounded. If \(C_0>0\), the minimum over \(z\in\mathbb R\) of \(C_0z^2+C_1z\) is \(-C_1^2/(4C_0)\). If this ratio diverges, parity permits choosing \(C_1<0\), and \(r=2C_0/|C_1|\to0\) realizes the divergent negative values. A negative \(C_0\) or nonzero \(C_1\) on its zero set likewise rules out a uniform lower bound when both opposite rays are included. For fixed \(p\) a semidefinite form controls squared distance to its null sphere, and a smooth function vanishing there is Lipschitz relative to that distance. Constant rank on a compact patch gives the same bound uniformly. In the four-dimensional Morse–Bott cases at issue, once the \(C_0\ge0\) condition holds, the ratio adds no further test. The codimension-four core is isolated, codimension three has definite normal Hessian, and codimensions one and two have \(C_0\equiv0\).

For codimension two the restricted form has eigenvalues \(0,0,\lambda\) with \(\lambda=\det(A)\langle n,A^{-1}n\rangle\). Only the tangent trace of \(D\) contributes to its first \(\sigma_2\) derivative. The angular derivative vanishes by the two-dimensional characteristic equation for \(A\). Commutation of third derivatives at the critical point and differentiation of \(\nabla^2f(v,Y)=0\) tangentially give \(D(X,Y)=-\langle Av,\mathrm{II}_F(X,Y)\rangle\). Thus the curvature coefficient is \(-2\lambda\langle\vec H_F,n\rangle/|Av|\). If \(\vec H_F\ne0\), the linear factor cannot vanish identically on any nonempty open sign cone, and one of each pair of opposite directions with nonzero coefficient makes it negative. Codimension one instead gives the finite second-form limit of the core because the restriction of \(Q\) to its tangent space vanishes to second order under the angular tilt.

The three flat cylinders have \(m-1\) principal curvatures \(r^{-1}\) and the rest zero, giving \((m-1)(m-2)/r^2\). The same rank-one restriction shows exactly zero scalar curvature for a constant elliptic codimension-two cylinder. For a spatially varying profile \(\rho=\varepsilon\beta\), set \(h_B=g_F+d\rho^2\) and \(k=m-1\). The mixed and vertical sectional curvatures give exactly
\[
R_{h_B}+k(k-1)(1-|\nabla\rho|_{h_B}^2)/\rho^2-2k\Delta_{h_B}\rho/\rho.
\]
This reproduces the stated bounded term. For \(\beta=1+c|y|^2/2\) in the flat codimension-two case, at \(y=0\) the graph term is \(2\varepsilon^2c^2\) and the Laplacian term is \(-4c\). The displayed tangential quadratic perturbations also reproduce the omissions in E03 by direct pairwise products. A separate equality check for isolated critical points uses \(f=(x^2+y^2+z^2-w^2/2)/2+c xw^2/2\). At \((x,0,0,0)\) the unscaled restricted \(\sigma_2\) is \(2cx\), so \(R_\Sigma=4c/x\). At \(c=0\) the same Hessian at the origin gives nonnegative scalar curvature on every regular leaf.

For an actual geodesic tube the columns for tangent-core variations start as \(I-rA_n-r^2\mathcal R_n/2\) on tangent components, and the angular variation starts as \(rw-r^3\mathcal R_nw/6\). Applying the radial derivative and inverting these columns gives \(B_{TT}=-A_n-r(A_n^2+(\mathcal R_n)_{TT})+O(r^2)\), \(B_{Tw}=O(r)\) and \(B_{ww}=r^{-1}+O(r)\). Inserting them into \(\det B_{TT}+B_{ww}\operatorname{tr}B_{TT}-|B_{Tw}|^2\) gives the stated finite scalar term. The outward circle normal of the flat cylinder example has core mean curvature vector pointing inward, consistent with the positive sign at the outer tube point. The equatorial sphere has zero second form and positive limiting scalar curvature \(2\) in unit \(S^4\).

For the Clifford torus take \(p=(e^{iu},e^{iv},0)/\sqrt2\) in \(S^4\subset\mathbb C^2\oplus\mathbb R\) and normal frame \((e^{iu},-e^{iv},0)/\sqrt2\), \((0,0,1)\). The two contributions to the trace in the first normal cancel, and the equatorial normal has zero shape. Put \(C=\cos r,S=\sin r\). The two circle factors of the tube embedding have metric lengths \((C\pm S\cos\theta)/\sqrt2\) and the normal-angle length is \(S\). With \(t=S\theta\), the sum of the two curvatures containing \(\partial_t\) is \(-2\cos^2\theta/(C^2-S^2\cos^2\theta)\). The remaining sectional curvature is \(\sin^2\theta/(C^2-S^2\cos^2\theta)\), giving exactly the mixed-sign scalar in E03. This also determines the induced conformal spectral sign. For \(0<r<\pi/4\),
\[
\int_{T^3}R_\Sigma\,dV=-4\pi^3S,\qquad
\operatorname{Vol}(T^3)=2\pi^3S(2C^2-S^2),\qquad
\lambda_1(-8\Delta+R_\Sigma)\le -\frac{2}{2C^2-S^2}<0.
\]
Here the volume density \(S(C^2-S^2\cos^2\theta)/2\) cancels the curvature denominator, and the variational test is the constant function. This sign follows directly without a classification of three-manifold scalar curvature. The calculation is for the induced metric on the explicit distance tubes. It requires a smooth Morse–Bott defining function or actual distance-tube description before making an inference about a flow.

## Minimal collar germs and the one spectral direction

The [E06 return](../research-progress/handoffs/E06_GERM.md) passes. In a metric \(ds^2+h_s\), the normal curvature operator is \(-S_s'-S_s^2\). Its trace yields \(\operatorname{Ric}_{ss}=-H_s'-|S_s|^2\). Adding tangential Gauss contractions gives \((\operatorname{Ric}_{TT})^\sharp=\operatorname{Ric}_{h_s}^\sharp-S_s'-H_sS_s\). The contracted Codazzi equation gives \(\operatorname{Ric}_{sT}=\operatorname{div} B-dH\). Differentiating the exponential diagonal metric gives \(S_0=\operatorname{diag}b_i\), \(S'_0=\operatorname{diag}(r_i-c)\). With \(\sum b_i=0\), \(\sum b_i^2=2c-R_h\) and zero mixed Ricci, this gives \(\operatorname{Ric}=cg\) on the slice. There \(R=4c\), \(A=(c/6)g\), and \(\sigma_2(A)=c^2/6\). The strict inequalities extend uniformly to a neighborhood by compactness. The Jacobi potential is \(q=c+|B|^2=3c-R_h\). Summing the three diagonal exponents gives \(H_s=-qs\) and the volume profile \(e^{-qs^2/2}\).

On the flat torus, \(b_1=-b_2=1/4,b_3=0\) gives \(q=3/16\) below its Fourier gap \(1\). The Jacobi index is one and nullity zero, while the induced conformal Laplacian has simple eigenvalue zero on constants and is at least \(8\) on their orthogonal complement. On the Hopf-stretched sphere, the cyclic brackets \((2t,2/t,2/t)\) give \(K_{12}=4-3t^2\), \(K_{13}=K_{23}=t^2\) by Koszul and curvature contraction. The diagonal momentum condition holds because \(\nabla_{e_i}e_i=0\) in this frame. The volume factor \(t\) is constant, and the inverse metric is at least \(t^{-2}\) times the inverse round metric. The first positive eigenvalue is therefore at least \(3/t^2\), where the round bound follows from the integrated Bochner inequality or first spherical harmonics. For \(2<t\le\sqrt{65}/4\), this yields \(q\le5/16<48/65\le\lambda_1(-\Delta_h)\). The constant scalar \(R_h=8-2t^2\) is strictly negative, and the eigenvalues of \(-8\Delta_h+R_h\) on the nonconstant modes are positive. These are different examples from the Clifford distance tubes. Torralbo–Urbano §2.1 defines the metric with vertical multiplier \(4\tau^2/\kappa\) after overall scaling and gives scalar \(2(\kappa-\tau^2)\), confirming \((\kappa,\tau)=(4,t)\). [Author PDF, §2.1](https://www.ugr.es/~furbano/papers/StableBerger.pdf)

At a minimal slice, Gauss gives the exact quadratic identity \(L=2J-6\Delta+R+|B|^2\). Restricting to the orthogonal complement of the negative Jacobi ground state proves parent Proposition 7.5 directly when the index is one. At index zero the same lower bound holds on the entire form domain. Writing the normalized Jacobi ground state as \(\phi\) and the compression of \(L\) as \(C\), one has \(C\ge\min(R+|B|^2)I>0\). The vector \(w=P_\perp L\phi\) is the projection of \((R+|B|^2+6(q+\mu_0))\phi\). Completing the quadratic form gives the stated Schur complement \(\langle\phi,L\phi\rangle-\langle w,C^{-1}w\rangle\). The change of variables \((x,v)\mapsto(x,v+xC^{-1}w)\) is bounded and invertible in \(L^2\), so a positive scalar also gives a positive spectral lower bound. A zero scalar gives a kernel and a negative scalar gives a negative test direction. Both germs have \(w=0\) and fail strict positivity. They do not supply a closed extension or a first-width realizer. Zhou’s Theorem 1.1 assumes a connected closed orientable ambient manifold of positive Ricci curvature and produces an orientable index-one multiplicity-one support or a nonorientable support of multiplicity two. The full source proof reading is in [E04](../research-progress/handoffs/E04_MINMAX.md). [Zhou, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2112v2#page=2)

## Prescribed cuts and marked bands

The [E07 return](../research-progress/handoffs/E07_BANDS.md) passes. For the outward normal of \(\Omega\), the flux term varies by \(\int h u\), and area by \(\int H u\). At \(H=h\) this yields the second variation \(\int(|\nabla u|^2-(q+\partial_\nu h)u^2)\) without a volume constraint. The outward divergence on the boundary of the entire band is \(-H_\nu\) at the lower end, and \(+H_\nu\) at the upper end, with \(\nu\) cooriented toward the upper end. Thus the strict barrier signs are \(H_\nu<h\) below and \(H_\nu>h\) above. Inserting Gauss into stability gives the exact coefficient \(R+h^2+|B|^2+2\partial_\nu h\). Replacing \(|B|^2\) by \(h^2/3\) gives the sufficient trace-only coefficient in E07. The normal derivative that helps stability carries the opposite benefit for the curvature bound. Räde’s convention coincides numerically with the one here, and his first-variation and second-variation formulas give these signs. The dimension-four result falls within the ambient regularity range \(n\le7\) of his construction. [Räde, Remark 4.1 and Lemmas 4.2–4.4 with (11)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.10120v4#page=12)

In the scaled metric \((\rho/12)g\), the product width bound with non-PSC base is \(\sqrt{\rho/12}\,d<\pi/2\), so its contrapositive includes equality at \(\rho d^2=3\pi^2\). The finite comparison requires no PSC separator and begins with \(m_-+m_+<0\). Scaled boundary means are \(\sqrt{12/\rho}\,m_\pm\). Solving the model end inequalities with means \(3\tan(2\ell_-)\) and \(-3\tan(2\ell_+)\) gives exactly E07’s arctangent interval. The finite comparison itself is non-strict. A round equatorial band has width \(2\varepsilon\), normalized squared width \(48\varepsilon^2\), and finite-end sum \(2\varepsilon\). This distinguishes the width and finite-end tests. [Räde, main theorem, Theorems 2.7–2.8 and Corollaries 2.9 and 2.25](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.10120v4#page=3)

The prescription \(h=H_r-\kappa(r-r_0)\) gives \(q+\partial_\nu h=-\kappa\) by the Riccati equation and the exact scalar coefficient \(R_{Y_{r_0}}-2\kappa\). The flux of \(\partial_r\) across the interior boundary is \(\int_\Omega H_r+A_-\), since its flux through the lower trace part is \(-A_-\). Subtracting the forcing integral gives a lower bound attained by the sublevel region, and the sign of the remaining linear integrand makes it unique up to measure zero. This is a prescribed minimizer for the chosen annulus and forcing. In smooth polar coordinates the radial Jacobi fields give \(S_r=r^{-1}I+O(r)\), so small spheres have \(6/r^2+O(1)\) intrinsic scalar curvature. Their homology class in any marked band containing the full ball is zero. An admissible band separator instead has the slice class by its oriented boundary chain, so its components have total projection degree one. The local cut therefore leaves the actual marked-band selection and the complement sweepout open.

For the round join in E07 coordinates the principal curvatures are \((-\tan r,\cot r,\cot r)\). They give \(|B^\circ|^2=2/(3\sin^2r\cos^2r)\), confirming the trace-only failure for \(\sin^2r\ge2/3\) even when the exact coefficient is positive. The finite-end derivative is \(2+3H'/(9+H^2)=(2-x)(1+x)/(x(9+H^2))\) with \(x=\tan^2r\). For ordinary mean curvature flow, \(y=\sin^2r\) solves \(y'=6y-4\), giving collapse to the circle for \(y_0<2/3\) and to the sphere for \(y_0>2/3\). The rates \(y\sim4(T-t)\) and \(1-y\sim2(T-t)\) agree with the cylinder radii in the E07 table. The latter family has regular-leaf scalar \(2/y>0\) through the last regular time. The orbit with \(y=2/3\) is stationary for unforced flow, even though it can be made strictly stable for its prescribed functional. The two nonstationary forward flows start at different leaves.

## Parent statements and requested corrections

- The algebra and conformal conclusion of Theorem 4.1 pass. The trace-free eigenvalue bound \(|Z(\nu,\nu)|\le(\sqrt3/2)|Z|<R/4\) implies \(P>0\). The strict \(L^3\) threshold in part (3) gives a lower bound \(\delta\|u\|_6^2\ge\delta\operatorname{Vol}(\Sigma)^{-2/3}\|u\|_2^2\). The conformal Laplacian’s positive first eigenfunction then gives the positive metric. The existence of a gauge for a smooth entering class is a separate global input. The 2003 paper restates the 2002 pointwise \(\sigma_2\) theorem in Theorem 1.3. For a connected representative with \(\sigma_2>0\), \(R\) cannot change sign, and positive Yamabe type excludes \(R<0\) everywhere by its constant-function test. The matched theorem statement and original 2002 abstract were checked, not the 2002 proof. [Chang–Gursky–Yang, Theorem 1.3](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf) · [Annals record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/2002/155-3/p02)
- In the remark following Theorem 4.1, its last sentence should require \(\sigma_2>0\) for **strict** two-convexity, or weaken the conclusion to nonnegative pair sums. Indeed, if \(t=\kappa_j+\kappa_k\), then \(\sigma_2\le Ht-3t^2/4\), which is negative for \(H>0,t<0\). For \(t=0\), \(\sigma_2=-\kappa_j^2\), so the rank-one case \((H,0,0)\) is allowed at equality.
- Theorem 7.2 itself passes. For index two the weak omission inequalities would force each positive eigenvalue smaller than both magnitudes of the negative ones and vice versa. For index one or three the pairwise reciprocal threshold follows by dividing the expression \(\alpha_j\alpha_k-\beta(\alpha_j+\alpha_k)\) by \(\beta\alpha_j\alpha_k\). The introduction’s Hessian-only equivalence for lower boundedness should be weakened. The equality example in E03 has fixed Hessian and different signs at order \(r^{-1}\). The criterion in the theorem itself states only the justified strict implications and the weak threshold.
- The text following Corollary 7.3 should distinguish the sufficient condition \(\sigma_2(B)\ge0\) from the lower-bound condition used for recognition. The claimed necessity of the former for each prescribed entering metric does not follow. The converse for lower boundedness on a standard \(S^4\) holds for any fixed smooth metric. A smooth two-critical-point height admits the positive omission test at both ends, and compactness supplies the bound on the remaining regular part.
- Proposition 7.5 passes. The preceding description of Zhou’s nonorientable case should say that the varifold has multiplicity two on a nonorientable support. Theorem 1.1 does not assert stability or represent the varifold statement as a stable double cover. The homotopy-sphere case excludes nonorientable embedded hypersurfaces, so this correction does not change Proposition 7.5 or the application to this branch. [Zhou, Theorem 1.1 and Remark 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1210.2112v2#page=2)

The returns establish local criteria and explicit examples in their stated families. A strictly stable positive prescribed cut does not imply that ordinary flow from its neighboring leaves avoids core-sphere collapse. Positive Ricci curvature and index one by themselves leave the induced conformal-Laplacian sign open for a fundamental-class width realizer. The width condition concerns the actual marked band, and the verified small sphere has no projection degree there when its full ball lies inside the band.
