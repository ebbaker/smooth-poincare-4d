## Replay

**Paired filling.** A smooth positive boundary ratio extends with vanishing normal logarithmic derivative, for example by extending its logarithm constantly in a collar and multiplying by a cutoff. In dimension four \(g\mapsto w^2g\) leaves the integrated Weyl energy invariant and preserves \(B=0\) under that condition. The Robin transformation in [E04](../notes/E04_ROBIN_DESCENT.md) then preserves the sign. Thus \(F_V\) depends on the boundary conformal class with its marking. For the forward implication the single ambient normal conformal jet kills both opposite trace mean curvatures of an umbilic cut. Restriction of the smooth metric gives two admissible competitors with precisely the identified boundary representatives. The actual conformal class of that boundary is positive by the Gauss equation in an entering gauge.

Conversely, choose actual smooth metrics with common \(h\) and strict total half-sum below \(2T\), each within the available strict margin of its infimum. In a common trace across the seam, the scalar quadratic form is the sum of the two positive Neumann forms because \(B=0\). Their \(H^1\) coercivity bounds the joined form. In geodesic collars \(h_s-h_0=O(s^2)\) and \(\partial_s h_s=O(s)\), including tangential derivatives of these identities. The productization by a cutoff at scale \(\epsilon\) changes the metric by \(O(\epsilon^2)\), its first derivatives by \(O(\epsilon)\), and its second derivatives by \(O(1)\) on a region of volume \(O(\epsilon)\). This gives the stated \(C^1\) and \(W^{2,p}\) convergence on the joined manifold and the positive individual doubles after smoothing. Specifically, the difference of scalar forms is \(o(1)\|u\|_{H^1}^2\) by \(L^2\) convergence of scalar curvature and \(H^1\hookrightarrow L^4\). Smoothness, total geodesy, the actual boundary map and the strict margin therefore survive. At equality the construction supplies only approximations of energy and sign. It neither produces a minimizer nor gives a strict upper bound from equality of infima. This validates the exact strict equivalence in B04. [E04 form and smoothing calculation](../notes/E04_ROBIN_DESCENT.md)

**Local collar.** For \(g=ds^2+h_s\) with \(h_s=h+s^2(\operatorname{Ric}_h-R_hh/2)\), the shape tensor vanishes at zero. Its derivative gives \(R_{i\nu j\nu}=-h_s''/2=R_hh/2-\operatorname{Ric}_h\) in the parent's sign convention. The tangential Ricci contracts the intrinsic Ricci plus this normal form, the normal Ricci is its trace \(R_h/2\), and Codazzi gives the vanishing mixed entries. Hence \(R_g=2R_h\) and \(Z_g=0\) there. For \(R_h>0\), compactness gives the strict entering curvature inequalities on a fixed smaller collar. The chiral integrands for this fixed smooth collar are bounded, giving shrinking-collar \(O(\epsilon)\) energies. Closing both remaining faces with the required energy is a separate condition.

**Marked quaternionic reduction.** In the standard projective-plane disk bundle \(W\), the disk fibre boundary subgroup is the cyclic order-four subgroup for the collapsing axis. Its image generates the kernel of \(Q_8\to\mathbb Z/2\). For the gluing swapping \(i\) and \(j\) and negating \(k\), the two such kernels generate \(Q_8\). The two quotient maps kill the two side generators in the pushout, and their direct sum on abelianizations is an isomorphism over \(\mathbb Z/2\). Both \(W\) and \(-W\) have the homotopy type of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\). Boundary Mayer–Vietoris gives integral sphere homology, so the smooth closed simply connected union is a homotopy four-sphere. The quaternion automorphism is orientation preserving on \(Y\); its use as a map to \(\partial(-W)\) reverses the induced boundary orientation. It preserves the metric of lengths \((1,1,t)\) in the right-invariant frame. The claimed reduction at this fixed datum to \(F_W(h_t)<T\) follows. The aligned biaxial exclusion assumes the third, long axis is the collapsing circle and supplies no bound for that test when both marked collapsing axes are short. ## Finite-cover eta and attained equality

For an oriented covering \(\widetilde V\to V\) of degree \(d\) extending a boundary covering, replace \(g\) by a product-ended comparison metric on \(V\) with the prescribed boundary metric, and pull it back. The integral of its signature density scales by \(d\). The product-ended APS theorem therefore gives
\[
 \tau(\widetilde V)+\eta(\partial\widetilde V)
       =d\big(\tau(V)+\eta(\partial V)\big),
\]
where \(\eta\) sums over boundary components and all metrics upstairs are pullbacks. This identity concerns the actual extending boundary cover; it allows the boundary defect to be nonzero. For the marked \(Q_8\) side it is connected of degree two with subgroup \(C_4\). In the quaternion orientation the round offsets are \(\eta(C_4\backslash S^3)=-1/2,\ \eta(Q_8\backslash S^3)=-3/4\). The defect \(\eta(\widetilde h)-2\eta(h)=1\) persists for any common pulled-back \(h\), by applying APS to a product-ended metric cylinder and its cover. The lifted \(W\) is a disk bundle over \(S^2\) of Euler magnitude four, with intersection form of sign \(-1\) in this outward orientation by the same identity. Thus for \(t=\sqrt3\) the lifted eta is \(1/6\) and the signed APS combination is \(-5/6=2(-5/12)\). On the other side the signs reverse. The lifted Weyl energies scale by the same degree. Applying the same energy-positivity argument on this cover reproduces the downstairs bound exactly. Independent metric constructions on the cover would need a descent or equivariance argument. [APS I, Theorem (4.14), printed p. 66](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf#page=24) · [APS II, Proposition (2.12), printed p. 412](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps002.pdf#page=8)

There is, independently, an obstruction to **smooth attained equality** in the \(Q_8\) cover bound. Suppose a filling of this marked \(W\) has \(B=0\), positive Robin sign, a smooth even double, and \(e_W=2T/3\). Take the double's degree-two universal cover, the double of the Euler-\(\pm4\) disk bundle. It has \(\chi=4\), \(\tau=0\) by orientation reversal, and \(b_1=0\), hence \(b_2^+=b_2^-=1\). Its Yamabe sign is positive, \(\int\sigma_2=2(T-e_W)>0\), and \(\int|W|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2=8e_W\). The ratio in Chang–Gursky–Zhang is then \(\beta=8\), so its conformal class is that of the equal-radius product on \(S^2\times S^2\).

The double reflection lifts to an involution fixing the connected \(C_4\)-cover of \(Y\): lift it at one boundary point, and use the connectivity of the lifted boundary and uniqueness of lifts. Its square is a deck transformation fixing a point. The lift preserves the product conformal class and is an isometry of the product metric. In fact if \(\widetilde g=f^{-2}g\) is its pullback of the product metric, both metrics are Einstein, so the trace-free Ricci transformation gives \(\nabla^2 f=(\Delta f/4)g\). Mixed components show that \(f(x,y)\) is a sum of functions on the two factors. The common pure-trace Hessian coefficient is consequently constant and vanishes on the closed product by integration. The function is constant, and equality of total volume makes the factor one. An orientation-reversing involution of the product with a three-dimensional fixed component must act by a great-circle reflection on one factor and as the identity on the other. To check this, the two simple lines in the image of the product curvature operator distinguish the two factor distributions up to interchange. An involution exchanging factors is orientation-preserving, and otherwise the fixed sets are direct products of the fixed sets of two involutive round sphere isometries. A three-dimensional fixed component therefore has \(S^2\times S^1\) topology. The actual component is the circle bundle of Euler magnitude four over \(S^2\), a lens space with \(H_1=\mathbb Z/4\). This contradiction proves \(e_W>2T/3\) for each filling with smooth even double.

The argument excludes attaining equality in that regularity class. A smooth filling with only \(B=0\) generally gives a \(C^{2,1}\) reflected double, so the cited smooth equality theorem does not by itself settle even attained equality for the full admissible class. Approximation gives the weak inequality and no uniform margin. Even if the equality theorem were extended, excluding an attained value alone would give no stronger infimum. An equivariant compactness or quantitative rigidity theorem would be needed for a gap, and a gap smaller than \(T/3\) would still leave a realization window. ## Missed construction test on a reducible lens seam

An explicit double of one punctured-lens interval costs less than \(T\) for its half, with a controlled Robin sign. It gives a finite test of the unresolved compatibility, independent of the quaternionic cap. Let \(P=L(3,1)\setminus B(r_0)\) with the unit round lens metric outside the deleted round ball and \(y=\cos r_0=8/15\). The shortest orbit displacement of the cyclic deck action is \(2\pi/3\), so \(r_0<\pi/3\) leaves an embedded collar beyond the puncture. Put a product \(S^1\times P\) metric with the circle radius \(\ell=\sqrt{161}/17\), and conformally rescale near the boundary by \(\csc^2 r\). The rescaled collar is
\[
 ds^2+a(s)^2d\theta^2+g_{S^2},\qquad ds=dr/\sin r,\quad
 a=\ell/\sin r,\quad a'/a=-\cos r.
\]
Time-independent positive factors extend smoothly away from the collar. The exterior is locally conformally flat since the unscaled product of a line and a round three-manifold is, and the Weyl tensor is conformally invariant. Attach \(S^2\) times a unit-curvature geodesic disk \(0\le s\le s_0\) with \(a=\sin s\), \(\sin s_0=15/17,\ \cos s_0=-8/17\). The radius and its normal derivative match. Both Weyl block norms squared of the positive cap product are \(2/3\), so on this whole doubled metric
\[
 E_+(\mathcal D V)=E_-(\mathcal D V)
 =\frac23(4\pi)(2\pi)(1-\cos s_0),\qquad
 e_V/T=50/51.
\]
Angular reflection fixes two copies of \(P\) joined via the sphere times a diameter, giving a totally geodesic \(L(3,1)\#(-L(3,1))\) after smoothing. Each half is a punctured-lens interval with its connector semicollar.

For scalar positivity restore the unscaled product outside the disk by a global conformal factor \(w\), equal to \(\sin r\) in the conformal collar. The initial normal matching for \(w\) on the disk requires \(w'/w=8/15\) at \(s_0\). Write \(z=(1-\cos s)/2\), so \(z_0=25/34,\ z'_0=15/34\). Take the degree-five polynomial \(p(z)=\sum_{n=0}^5a_nz^n\), with \(a_0=1\), \(a_{n+1}=((3/5)+n(n+1))a_n/(n+1)^2\). The disk radial Laplacian is \(z(1-z)\partial_z^2+(1-2z)\partial_z\). The recurrence gives \(\Delta p\le (3/5)p\) for \(z\ge0\). At the matching radius the exact difference \(z'_0p'(z_0)-(8/15)p(z_0)\) is \(85090847/43618007040>0\). A positive linear combination of \(1\) and \(p\), and a common scaling, matches the required logarithmic derivative and value while retaining \(\Delta w\le(3/5)w\). With disk-product scalar curvature \(4\), the conformal scalar numerator \(-6\Delta w+4w\) is strictly positive. Both pieces have a positive strict scalar margin in this new gauge and a \(C^1\) junction. Ordinary smoothing in the radial variable preserves the form sign by the \(C^1\) and \(W^{2,p}\) argument above. The scaled smoothings can be kept angular-reflection invariant, so each half has positive Robin sign. The energy tends to the displayed value.

**Actual matching test.** In the two-twist-spun construction the two semicircle trivializations of the punctured-lens exterior meet by the identity at one page and the branched deck involution \(J\) at the other. Its boundary action is rotation by \(\pi\) about the branch axis and its effect on the side \(\mathbb Z/3\) is nontrivial. Alternatively, nontriviality of the latter effect follows from the pushout: if the two identifications acted identically on \(\mathbb Z/3\), the glued manifold would still have that fundamental group. For a matched marked product trial the identification respects the two lens faces and their connector. Any conformal map between the round product connectors restricts to the same angular map at both ends. To see this, represent each connector as a concentric Euclidean annulus and apply Liouville on its interior. A Möbius map between the two annuli conjugates the inversions in their boundary spheres. The composition of these inversions is a nontrivial dilation with fixed pair \(\{0,\infty\}\), so the map preserves that pair and acts by a single angular orthogonal transformation. The two punctured-lens face maps from the fixed round model of one half to the fixed round model of the other must therefore have the same boundary restriction. If two such conformal maps had the same restriction, their quotient would fix the boundary sphere pointwise. In local round conformal charts the resulting Möbius map is the identity or the reflection in that sphere. Indeed conjugating the fixed sphere to a hyperplane makes the map a Euclidean similarity fixing the hyperplane pointwise. The reflection changes sides, so a map of the punctured lens to itself is the identity in a collar, and it is then the identity throughout by local Liouville uniqueness and connectedness. Thus the two maps induce the same action on \(\mathbb Z/3\), preventing this exact product pair from realizing the marked gluing even after a conformal change. This argument allows one fixed round model per half and uses the exact product conformal connector with its identified end faces. [Schoen–Yau, *Conformally flat manifolds, Kleinian groups and scalar curvature*, p. 47 and §1](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen-yau.conformal.pdf)

A remaining finite test allows variation of the lens geometry between the endpoints within a half, or a nonflat sphere connection in the connector carrying the marked twist. It must produce exact common boundary representatives up to a conformal change, the preserved Robin signs, and combined extra half-sum smaller than \(2T/51\) from this trial. The common marking and energy cost are the new issues for [E09](../research-progress/handoffs/E09_REQUEST_R1.md). This keeps the broad B04 reduction open independently of any negative result restricted to product gauges. ## Disposition

The paired filling equivalence, the smooth collar and the reduction to a short-axis quaternionic cap are verified. The remaining statement in B04 is appropriately quantified over actual marked pairs and common boundary classes. Positive intrinsic curvature and eta are compatible for the quaternionic datum, and the extending finite cover repeats its eta constraint. Smooth equality excludes only the exact lower endpoint of the projective-bundle filling estimate. The lens trial supplies one checked low-cost positive double and identifies the unverified marked pair as a bounded construction problem. No indicated passage or computation establishes the strict paired inequality for either residual case.
