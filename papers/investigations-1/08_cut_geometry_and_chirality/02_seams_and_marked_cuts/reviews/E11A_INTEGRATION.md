## The \(119T/60\) wall

The sharper bound holds for an entering smooth oriented homotopy four-sphere with a smooth connected umbilic PSC integral homology-sphere cut \(\Sigma\not\cong S^3\), under the two positive reflected Yamabe signs. Both sides \(U\) are integral homology balls by integral Mayer–Vietoris and the fundamental-class map to \(H_3(\Sigma)\). For each side set \(b=e_U\), \(D=DU\). The reflected limit has \(\chi(D)=2\), \(\tau(D)=0\), and \(E_\pm(D)=2b\), since reflection swaps chirality. Its integrated normalized Schouten \(\sigma_2\) is \(T-b\). A strict inequality \(b<T\) persists for smooth normalized approximants of positive Yamabe sign. CGY's positive-Ricci result and Bonnet–Myers give a finite group for that double. Equivalently an infinite double group forces \(b\ge T\). If the double group is finite, write \(P=\pi_1(U)\) and \(I\le P\) for the boundary image. Van Kampen gives \(P*_I P\) by passing to the common quotient of the boundary group on the two sides of an identity double. For \(I\ne P\) there are alternating reduced words of arbitrary length, so the finite case has \(I=P\) and double group \(P\). [CGY 2002, Theorem A, Corollaries B–C and (0.2)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583#page=4)

CMM's PSC homology-sphere theorem gives a nonempty signed sum of Poincaré spheres. Their Proposition 4.2 eliminates a smooth contractible filling of such a sum, so \(P\) cannot be trivial. Balance of the orientations follows separately by removing an interior ball from either homology ball, applying the correction term under the resulting integral homology cobordism, and using the nonzero correction term of a Poincaré sphere with additivity and orientation reversal. Balance is part of the surviving profile. The order argument only needs the nonempty Poincaré sum and the contractible obstruction. [CMM Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574v1#page=5) · [Ozsváth–Szabó v2, Theorems 1.2, 4.3, Proposition 6.3 and §8.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110170)

In the finite case the images of the binary icosahedral factors generate \(P\). Some such image is nontrivial. Write \(I^*\subset SU(2)\) for the double cover of the rotational icosahedral group \(A_5\subset SO(3)\), with central kernel \(C_2\). The group \(I^*\) is perfect, as it is the fundamental group of the Poincaré homology sphere. If a proper normal subgroup projects nontrivially to the simple quotient \(A_5\), it projects onto it, leaving a quotient of order at most two. Perfection excludes such a nontrivial quotient. Thus the nontrivial images of a factor have orders 60 and 120, and the only possible \(|P|<120\) is \(P\cong A_5\). The oriented sign of the corresponding Poincaré summand has no effect on its group. [Milnor, Brieskorn manifolds, §1 spherical triangle groups and §6](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/milnbries.pdf)

Attach cells of dimension at least three to the connected CW model of \(U\) to make \(K(P,1)\). The relative second homology vanishes, so \(H_2(U;\mathbb Z)\twoheadrightarrow H_2(P;\mathbb Z)\). In particular \(H_2(P;\mathbb Z)=0\). The extension of \(A_5\) by \(C_2\) with total group \(I^*\) is nonsplit, since a split central extension would be \(A_5\times C_2\), which is not perfect. It gives a nonzero class in \(H^2(A_5;C_2)\) for the trivial action. The universal coefficient sequence and \(H_1(A_5;\mathbb Z)=0\) then give \(H_2(A_5;\mathbb Z)\ne0\). This proves \(|P|\ge120\). [Hatcher, §4.2, Example 4.16 with \(n=1\), and §3.1 UCT](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf) · [Weibel, Classification Theorem 6.6.3, pp. 183–184](https://math.mit.edu/~hrm/palestine/weibel/06-group_homology_and_cohomology.pdf)

For a smooth positive approximant to \(D\), a volume-one Yamabe representative of a degree-\(d\) cover has scalar curvature \(Y\) with \(0<Y\le8\sqrt6\pi\). Its smooth Gauss–Bonnet identity is \(16\pi^2 d=4db_j+Y^2/24-\tfrac12\int|Z|^2\), where \(4b_j\) is the total Weyl energy on the smooth double. Passing to the limit gives \(b\ge(1-1/d)T\). The finite universal cover has \(d\ge120\), hence \(b\ge119T/120\). An infinite group satisfies the larger bound already established. Adding yields \(119T/60\le e<2T\). If either double group is infinite the lower bound becomes \(239T/120\). If both reflected classes are smooth, equality at \(119T/60\) is excluded by smooth equality in the spherical Yamabe bound on a cover of degree 120, which would have \(\chi=240\). For general reflected metrics the limit argument gives the stated weak inequality. [Sung, Lemma 2.1, pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808#page=6) · [Lee–Parker, Theorem 3.3, Lemma 3.4 and §11](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf) · [CGY 2003, (0.4)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0309287#page=4)
