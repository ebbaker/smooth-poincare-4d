## E04 · Robin sign and capped descent

1. **Form and reflection.** In a four-metric \(w^2g\), the scalar term for \(u\) is \(w^{-3}L_g(wu)\) with \(L_g=-6\Delta_g+R_g\) and \(\Delta_g=\operatorname{div}\nabla\). The trace curvature transforms as \(w^{-1}(H+3w^{-1}\partial_\nu w)\). Thus \(6\partial_{\tilde\nu}u+2\tilde H u=w^{-2}(6\partial_\nu(wu)+2Hwu)\). Integrating \(uL_gu\) by parts gives \(-6\int_{\partial V}u\partial_\nu u\), which is canceled by the boundary operator, leaving \(+2\int_{\partial V}Hu^2\). The volume weights are \(w^4,w^3\) and give the identity \(Q_{w^2g}(u)=Q_g(wu)\). The Robin Rayleigh infimum has the three claimed alternatives by the relative trace bound and compact bulk and trace embeddings. In the positive alternative a small multiple of the semibounded gradient estimate can be added to the positive \(L^2\) bound to give coercivity. On an umbilic side, setting \(\partial_\nu\log w=-H/3\) forces the full form \(B\) to vanish. The even \(h(|t|)\) in its Gaussian collar is \(C^2\): all mixed derivatives with one normal derivative vanish at the boundary as well. Restriction to the two sides gives the reflected first eigenvalue lower bound, and even extension gives equality, measured in this gauge. Coercivity and the closed \(H^1\to L^4\) estimate identify its sign with the Yamabe sign. A second gauge with \(B=0\) has an even \(C^2\) conformal ratio across the reflection in the original collar. Ambient reflection must fix the cut pointwise and exchange the entire halves to identify their doubles with the ambient conformal geometry. The local equator in 07 gives the totally geodesic condition, while its regular and long cuffs give three-dimensional positivity only. The short cuffs obstruct entry by the independent three-dimensional sign. The prescribed product-circle face has principal curvatures \((\pm\coth\rho,0,0)\) in its stated germ. The Mazur swap has the asserted global reflection property. Its strict entry obstruction and the unresolved separate eta-zero half-filling question agree with the 07 sources. 2. **Smoothing and the weak bound.** In Gaussian collars equality of the boundary metrics and opposite outward \(B\) ensure matching first derivatives. Smooth local approximants filtered through a fixed smooth partition of unity therefore converge in \(C^1\) and \(W^{2,p}\) for every finite \(p\). The piecewise second derivatives are the weak derivatives, since there is no first-derivative jump. In the local curvature formula, the coefficient of the second derivatives converges uniformly, the first-derivative terms converge uniformly, and \(p=2\) suffices for strong \(L^2\) convergence of curvatures. Chiral projections and volume densities converge uniformly; this gives convergence of both squared energy integrals. Across scalar forms, Hölder with the uniform \(H^1\to L^4\) estimate gives the asserted \(o(1)\|u\|_{H^1}^2\) control. A fixed test bounds the Yamabe infimum above. An approximate minimizing sequence of unit \(L^4\) norm has bounded \(L^2\) norm by finite volume and bounded gradient energy because \(|\int R u^2|\le\|R\|_2\|u\|_4^2\). Together with uniform convergence of the \(L^4\) density ratios this proves both limits for the infima at each sign. For the reflected double, \(\chi=2\chi(V)\) and total HS Weyl energy tends to \(4e_V\) after the two chiralities exchange on the reversed half. Using smooth unit-volume Yamabe minimizers in the closed Gauss–Bonnet formula gives \(16\pi^2\chi(V)\le4e_V+Y^2/24\). Thus \(e_V\ge T\chi(V)-Y^2/96\), and the lower bound on \(|Y|\) if \(e_V<T\chi(V)\) is valid with the negative sign when \(Y\le0\). Convergence at \(Y=0\) supplies no smooth strict sign.
3. **Capped positivity.** The prescribed boundary representative fixes the boundary value of the conformal factor. Its independent first normal derivative sets \(H=0\) on both sides because the outward normals are opposite. The inward cap derivative is then \(2B_{V_i}\) with \(B_{V_i}\) measured outward, as first-jet matching requires. Each [E02 trial cap](../notes/E02_CAP.md) with \(D_\sigma\le1/100\) has \(R\ge8\) and trace \(H=0\). The Robin boundary integrals of the matched forms cancel on a common \(H^1\) trace. Thus a positive cap and nonnegative side suffice. In the side-zero case, a closed null minimizer would vanish on the cap by its coercivity. Its side restriction would be a nonzero null minimizer with zero trace. Absolute value preserves this minimum and yields a smooth nonnegative first eigenfunction on the side, which is positive up to the boundary by the interior maximum principle and the boundary point lemma together with the Robin condition. This contradicts the zero trace. Smooth approximants consequently have positive Yamabe sign. The sign of the side Robin form is a sufficient input here.
4. **Threshold and minimizer.** Van Kampen across \(S^3\) gives a free product, so both sides have trivial fundamental group. The integral Mayer–Vietoris connecting map \(H_4(M)\to H_3(S^3)\) sends an oriented fundamental class to an oriented fundamental class. It gives \(H_3(V_i)=0\), and the adjacent ranges give \(H_1(V_i)=H_2(V_i)=0\). The closed capped \(X_i\) are simply connected integral homology spheres, hence homotopy spheres. The given markings realize \(M\cong X_1\#X_2\), with the \(S^3\) boundary map extending over the ball by Cerf and a linear reflection when needed. In the oriented capped limit, the closed signature identity gives \(E_\pm(V_i)+C_{i,\pm}=e_i+c_i=q_i\) for each chirality separately. The full covariant Weyl energy is \(8q_i\) at the limit. Since \(\chi(X_i)=2\), the strict Chang–Gursky–Yang threshold \(16\pi^2\chi(X_i)\) is \(q_i<T\); an eventual positive smooth approximant under this threshold suffices. Equality on an actual smooth positive metric is covered separately by Theorem B and is incompatible with its two residual alternatives on a homotopy sphere. A limit at equality alone gives no such representative. The same smooth Yamabe and Gauss–Bonnet calculation with \(\chi(X_i)=2\) gives \(q_i\ge 2T-Y(G_i)^2/48\). If \(X_i\) is standard and \(Y(G_j)>0\) with \(j\ne i\), approximants on \(X_j\cong M\) give \(I(M)\le q_j\), with no assumption that the infimum is achieved by an approximant. Subtracting \(E_\pm(V_j)\) from \(e\le q_j+\delta\) yields \(E_\pm(V_i)\le C_{j,\pm}+\delta\), with half-sum \(e_i\le c_j+\delta\). A true positive minimizer and a free cap therefore force the removed side to be Weyl-flat. A global exchange reflection of an entering free cut supplies both signs and forces \(e_i=e/2<T\).
5. **Exact energy and development.** The second normal derivative jump in the round test is \(2k\), giving the normal curvature change \(-k\), tangential Ricci change \(-k\), and zero normal Ricci and scalar changes by \(\operatorname{tr}k=0\). The electric Weyl change is thus \(-k/2\). This verifies the obstruction to preserving both given pieces smoothly up to the join. Proposition 8.1(1) should state exact equality for the first-jet-matched piecewise metric and convergence for smooth metrics. The strict inequalities and infimum comparison require only this convergence. For the development step, the standard tractor connection is flat on a Weyl-flat half-chart by the Cotton identity. A parallel initial null triple \((1,0,0)\) has \(\mu=d\sigma\), \(\nabla^2\sigma=-\sigma A-\rho g\), and \(|d\sigma|^2=-2\sigma\rho\), so \(A_{\sigma^{-2}g}=A+\sigma^{-1}\nabla^2\sigma-\tfrac12\sigma^{-2}|d\sigma|^2g=0\). The flat coordinates extend smoothly to the boundary. Monodromy on the simply connected interior gives the developing immersion, and Liouville extends it by a Möbius transformation on each half-chart. The umbilic boundary maps locally into a round \(S^3\), since a Euclidean umbilic hypersurface of dimension three has constant principal curvature by Codazzi. Open overlaps force one shared round sphere. Compactness and the simple connectivity of that target make the boundary map a diffeomorphism. Reflecting the developing map on the second copy gives a local homeomorphism on the compact double, and its image is open and closed in \(S^4\). It is a covering of the simply connected target, so the original side maps diffeomorphically and conformally to a round closed ball. This justifies the asserted development in the free-cut application with its capped sign hypotheses.

## E05 · Bending bound and marked inference

1. **Ambient and intrinsic terms.** In the normalized Schouten convention \(A=\tfrac12(\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/6)\), the entering condition on a homotopy sphere gives \(\int\sigma_2(A)=T-e/2>0\). Chang–Gursky–Yang's Schouten tensor equals \(2A\), so its \(\sigma_2\) is four times as large. Their positive Yamabe hypothesis gives \(R>0\) in their conformal solution, and the eigenvalue estimate for \(Z=\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/4\) or their Corollary B gives \(R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)>0\). Compactness gives \(p_g>0\). The embedded closed cut has zero mod-two homology class in the ambient sphere. A loop crossing once would exist if the cut were one-sided or nonseparating, so it is two-sided and separates. The Gauss scalar term is \(H^2-|B|^2=\tfrac23H^2-|B^\circ|^2\). In dimension three the conformal Laplacian coefficient is \(8\), and Hölder gives the bending subtraction \(\int|B^\circ|^2u^2\le\|B^\circ\|_3^2\|u\|_6^2\). A positive quotient bound gives a positive first \(L^2\) eigenvalue by Hölder, so its positive eigenfunction yields PSC in the induced conformal class.
2. **Extrinsic constant.** The compact smooth ambient four-manifold has a Nash embedding into \(\mathbb R^{46}\), and a chosen embedding can be padded to ambient dimension \(\ge5\) if needed. On every closed cut the Euclidean trace decomposes as \(-H\,dF(\nu)+\operatorname{tr}_{T\Sigma}\mathrm{II}_F\) into orthogonal components, so its squared norm is bounded by \(H^2+K_F^2\). In Brendle's Theorem 1 take \(n=3\), \(m\ge2\) and \(f=u^4+\varepsilon\). The boundary term is absent, and \(\varepsilon\downarrow0\) gives \(b_m\|u\|_6^4\le\int |u|^3(16|\nabla u|^2+|\mathcal H_E|^2u^2)^{1/2}\). By Cauchy–Schwarz and division by \(\|u\|_6^3\), the resulting squared form has gradient coefficient 16 and mean-curvature coefficient 1. Comparing with the Gauss form gives \(\max\{2,3/2,K_F^2/p_g\}=\max\{2,K_F^2/p_g\}\). This verifies \(S_g(\Sigma)\ge C(g,F)=b_m^2/\max\{2,K_F^2/p_g\}\) and \(Y(\Sigma,[h])\ge C(g,F)-\|B^\circ\|_3^2\). All quantifiers are simultaneous for one fixed gauge and embedding.
3. **Conformal invariance and embedding dependence.** Under \(\widehat g=e^{2w}g\), the pointwise bending norm scales by \(e^{-w}\) and the three-volume by \(e^{3w}\). With \(\widehat u=e^{-w/2}u\), both the \(|B^\circ|^2u^2\) term and the sixth-power norm are invariant, as is the three-dimensional Yamabe form. Thus the bound controls these intrinsic expressions in every representative of the same class. Constant scaling of the gauge and embedding multiplies \(p_g\) and \(K_F^2\) by the same factor. Across embeddings the helix argument checks: the map replacing a coordinate line has an isometric differential and is injective by its nonzero linear component. Its second derivative is perpendicular to that differential and contributes a trace of norm \(ak|\nabla_L f|^2\) orthogonal to the pushed-forward original normal component. This makes the numerical constants tend to zero along the constructed sequence. Uniformity for a bounded range of \(m\) and \(K_F^2/p_g\) follows directly from the formula. Moving a cut by smooth isotopy can change bending, while applicability of the universal-in-cuts estimate remains.
4. **Markings.** The actual lower winding-one trace in the reduced mixed presentation is simply connected with unit cellular boundary in degree two, hence contractible, and Lefschetz duality makes its boundary \(Z_a\) an integral homology sphere. The cut supplying a PSC metric can be any smooth embedded copy of \(Z_a\) in the fixed entering class. Transporting the metric and taking the identity yields the degree-one domination required in the PSC-domination theorem. For \(Z_a\cong S^3\) alone, the smooth contractible trace and the CMM passages suffice. The implication \(M\cong S^4\) uses the working recognition theorem for the actual presentation, with its lower and upper attachments and marks, as in the parent Corollary 4.4. The contrapositive bending infimum is therefore conditional on that theorem and supplies a constraint for each such hypothetical nonstandard presentation.
