## Both signs and the actual seam

Let \(W\) be the specified smooth projective-plane normal disk bundle with collapsing \(i\). Its induced totally geodesic datum is the right-invariant \(h=h_{\sqrt3}\) on \(Q_8\backslash S^3\), up to a constant scale. The signed ledger, with \(\tau(W)=0\), \(B=0\) and \(\eta(h)=-5/12\), gives \(E_-(W)=E_+(W)+5\pi^2\). Write \(x=E_+(W)\) and \(b=e_W=x+5\pi^2/2\).

Keep the marking \(\phi([q])=[uqu^{-1}]\), \(u=(i+j)/\sqrt2\), as a map from \(\partial W\) to \(\partial(-W)\). On the reference boundary it is an orientation-preserving isometry exchanging the short directions and negating the long direction. The target boundary orientation from \(-W\) is opposite to the reference orientation. In signed Gaussian collars the normal changes sign on crossing the seam. The first matching condition is \(\phi^*B_{-W}=-B_W\), which holds since both terms vanish. The two sides carry identical scalar forms if the metric is duplicated. With outward trace mean curvature the full Robin term is \(2\int H f^2\). Both signs here are precisely the positive Neumann signs of \(-6\Delta+R\) on the entire caps. Transforming conformally on either copy preserves each sign, and at \(H=0\) a constant boundary rescaling preserves the first matching condition.

Productize the first jets on shrinking collars while keeping \(h\) fixed on the seam, as in B04. Both independent identity doubles and the actual \(\phi\)-join are then smooth. The changes tend to zero in \(C^1\cap W^{2,p}\) for each finite \(p\), with changes of second derivatives bounded on collars of volume tending to zero. They preserve each strict Neumann sign and both signs on the reflected doubles. The closed form on the \(\phi\)-join is the sum of the two coercive side forms on matching traces, hence positive. The ledger difference stays exactly \(-5\pi^2\) on each copy in the reference orientation. If the cap is duplicated, the opposite copy in the ambient orientation exchanges its Weyl blocks. The smooth paired class consequently has each closed chiral integral tending to \(2x+5\pi^2=2b\). For two different admissible metrics at the same \(h\), the limit is \(x_1+x_2+5\pi^2\) in both chiralities. Therefore a successful pair at this datum yields a single cap with \(x<3\pi^2/2\), and one such cap gives a smooth successful pair by the strict margins. The two separate identity reflections fix their seams. The copy exchange in the actual twisted pairing restricts to \(\phi\) on its seam and should not be used as a pointwise reflection across it. The identity double \(D=DW\) has \(\pi_1=C_2\): both surjections from \(Q_8\) have kernel \(\langle i\rangle\). Its universal cover \(\widetilde D\) is the identity double of the disk bundle \(\widetilde W\to S^2\) with oriented Euler magnitude four and boundary \(\langle i\rangle\backslash S^3\). This follows from the connected pullback of the oriented base cover. The doubled disk fibres give the sphere bundle of the plane bundle stabilized by a real line. The clutching degree four maps to zero in \(\pi_1(SO(3))=C_2\), hence \(\widetilde D\cong S^2\times S^2\) smoothly. The actual \(\phi\)-pair instead has distinct C4 kernels, trivial group and the smooth matrix-sphere identification with \(S^4\). ## What the positive cover measures

The ordinary signature combination on the lifted half satisfies \(\tilde\tau+\tilde\eta=2(\tau+\eta)\) by the APS formula and pullback of the product-ended Pontryagin integral. Pulling back a relative metric cylinder shows that the round defect \(\eta(\tilde h)-2\eta(h)=1\) survives at the marked metric. Indeed the signed round values on C4 and \(Q_8\) are \(-1/2\) and \(-3/4\). Thus \(\tilde\eta=1/6\), \(\tilde\tau=-1\), and the signed half difference on \(\widetilde W\) is exactly \(-10\pi^2\), twice the downstairs difference. The switch sends the \(\langle i\rangle\) quotient on the cover to the \(\langle j\rangle\) quotient. It is not an automorphism of the given connected \(\langle i\rangle\) boundary cover in that marking. Furthermore the lens space in this cover has cyclic linking form with generator square \(\pm1/4\). Every unit modulo four has square one, so the linking form excludes an orientation-reversing self-diffeomorphism. Gluing two copies of this same oriented covered half by a boundary self-map to put the small blocks together is therefore unavailable. The identity-double energy necessarily sums opposite blocks. [APS I (4.14), p. 66](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf#page=24) · [APS II (2.12) and (4.7)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps002.pdf) · [Cover defect replay](E08C_REALIZATION.md)

The pulled-back reflection double has \(\chi=4\), \(\tau=0\), \(b_2^+=b_2^-=1\), and \(E_+(\widetilde D)=E_-(\widetilde D)=4b\). The limit Yamabe constant \(Y_c\) is positive. With smooth approximation followed by the volume-one Yamabe representative, Gauss–Bonnet gives \(Y_c^2\ge192(T-b)\). The standard closed harmonic-form inequality in either sign gives \(Y_c^2\le24 E_\pm(\widetilde D)=96b\). This is also the content of the CGZ/Gursky inequalities in this topology, and gives \(b\ge2T/3\), equivalently \(x\ge\pi^2/6\). If \(b<T\), the normalized Schouten integral on the cover is \(2(T-b)>0\), and CGZ's ratio is \(\beta=4b/(T-b)\). Their indefinite-form threshold \(\beta\ge8\) gives the same inequality. Smooth attained equality would force the equal-radius product class on \(S^2\times S^2\), inconsistent with the lifted reflection's fixed lens hypersurface. The product Einstein representative makes the reflection isometric by Einstein uniqueness and volume. An orientation-reversing product involution with hypersurface fixed set has a great-circle reflection on one factor and identity on the other, so that fixed hypersurface is \(S^2\times S^1\). This is an attained equality exclusion for a smooth even double. Mere \(B=0\) only supplies a \(C^{2,1}\) double in geodesic collars, and neither this limiting argument nor failure of smooth equality yields a numerical gap in the infimum. [CGZ, Theorem C and §2, (2.1)–(2.4)](https://web.math.princeton.edu/facultypapers/chang/CGZ-14-September.pdf) · [Smooth equality replay](E08C_REALIZATION.md)

The spherical upper estimate \(Y_c\le8\sqrt6\pi\) does not sharpen the half-sum lower bound. The value \(16\pi\) is the Yamabe constant of the equal-radius product class, and cannot be used as an upper for arbitrary classes on this smooth cover. In fact nearby unequal product radii have larger Yamabe constant. A smooth positive auxiliary metric on the closed cover gives no estimate on the specified cap unless it also realizes its lens cut, reflects as required and descends with the deck action. These closed statements concern \(b\) and do not by themselves control the small block. [Lee–Parker §§3 and 11](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf) · [LeBrun, *On the Scalar Curvature of 4-Manifolds*, §7, pp. 50–51](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10785)

## A relative harmonic test and its unresolved trace

Write \(Y_N\) for the interior \(L^4\) Neumann Yamabe quotient of \(\widetilde W\), with numerator \(\int(6|df|^2+Rf^2)\) since \(H=0\). Even extension of competitors gives \(Y_c\le\sqrt2 Y_N\), so if \(b<T\) then \(Y_N^2\ge96(T-b)\). Absolute and relative real harmonic two-forms on \(\widetilde W\) each have dimension one by Hodge–Morrey–Friedrichs and duality. The Hodge star exchanges the two boundary conditions. A chiral form satisfying either homogeneous condition has full boundary value zero. Extending a harmonic field with full zero trace by zero across a smooth extension of the metric gives a distributional closed and coclosed form, hence a smooth harmonic form that vanishes by unique continuation. In particular the two chiral projections of a nonzero absolute harmonic form are nonzero in the interior, and neither satisfies the homogeneous absolute condition. [Aronszajn–Krzywicki–Szarski, Introduction and theorem for closed and coclosed exterior forms](https://archive.ymsc.tsinghua.edu.cn/pacm_download/116/6988-11512_2007_Article_BF02591624.pdf)

For a nonzero closed self-dual two-form \(\omega\) on this disk, the refined Kato inequality gives \(|\nabla\omega|^2\ge\tfrac32|d|\omega||^2\). The Weitzenböck formula gives for \(f=|\omega|^{1/2}\) away from its zeros
\[
 f(-6\Delta+R)f\le6\lambda_{\max}(W^+)f^2.
\]
For justification across zeros use \(f_\epsilon=(|\omega|^2+\epsilon)^{1/4}\). The same calculation gives an error bounded by \(C\sqrt\epsilon\) on the compact disk because the regularized quadratic gradient remainder has nonnegative sign. Integrating gives the boundary contribution \(6\int_{\partial\widetilde W} f_\epsilon\partial_\nu f_\epsilon\). In an outward Gaussian collar at \(B=0\), write \(\omega=\nu^\flat\wedge a+*_ha\). The normal equation of \(d\omega=0\) then gives \(\partial_\nu a=*_h da\) and the tangential equation gives \(d(*_ha)=0\). Since \(|\omega|^2=2|a|^2\) and \(h'_0=0\), the normalized estimate is
\[
 Y_N\le \sqrt{48x}+6\frac{\displaystyle\int_{\partial\widetilde W}\frac{\langle a,*_h da\rangle}{|\omega|}\,dA}
 {\displaystyle\left(\int_{\widetilde W}|\omega|^2\,dV\right)^{1/2}} .
\]
The trace integrand is interpreted as zero at \(a=0\), and dominated convergence applies using the bound by \(|*_hda|/\sqrt2\). The coefficient of \(x\) follows from \(\lambda_{\max}^2\le2|W^+|^2/3\) and the degree-two integral on this half. The normals and the self-duality choice matter. The anti-self-dual equation changes the sign of the boundary curl. For the two projections of the absolute harmonic representative, the boundary norms agree and the two unnormalized boundary contributions are opposite. Cancellation in their sum does not discard the larger chiral energy from the bulk. Even at the fixed Berger metric the co-closed trace \(\sigma_i\) descends to the marked C4 quotient and has positive curl eigenvalue in the E19 orientation. Thus the umbilic boundary and the metric alone do not make the boundary contribution nonpositive for all co-closed traces. The needed trace is that of the actual harmonic field of the proposed interior metric. [Calderbank–Gauduchon–Herzlich, *Refined Kato inequalities and conformal weights*, harmonic two-form constant, §6](https://people.bath.ac.uk/dmjc20/Papers/kato.pdf) · [Al-Zamil–Montaldi, §2, ordinary absolute and relative decomposition](https://eprints.maths.manchester.ac.uk/1433/1/covered/MIMS_ep2010_31.pdf)

As a conditional improvement, a nonpositive actual self-dual harmonic flux gives \(Y_N^2\le48x\). Combining the preceding lower bound gives \(96(T-x-5\pi^2/2)\le48x\), hence \(x\ge\pi^2\). This still leaves \([\pi^2,3\pi^2/2)\). A stronger verified bound on the same trace and Yamabe quotient, or another geometric estimate, would be necessary to rule out the target by this test.

## No topology-only positive lower Yamabe bound

The fixed \(h\), \(B=0\) and topology do not force a uniform positive \(Y_N\) over all Robin-positive competitors without energy control. Start from E19 and change the metric inside a coordinate ball. In an annulus there put a product cylinder of any prescribed metric length \(L\), with homogeneous Berger \(S^3\) slices of negative constant scalar curvature \(R_0\), for example a lift of the displayed Berger family with \(t>2\). Smoothly interpolate on collars disjoint from a central length-\(L\) region, retaining the original metric outside the ball. A test function constant along the sphere and with sine profile along that region, extended by zero in \(H^1\), has Rayleigh quotient \(6\pi^2/L^2+R_0<0\) for large \(L\). Interpolating this metric and E19 as positive tensors keeps the full boundary germ fixed. The first Neumann eigenvalue varies continuously by the form estimate on this compact smooth path. Approach its first zero from the positive side. Normalized first eigenfunctions give \(0<Y_N\le\lambda_1\operatorname{Vol}^{1/2}\to0\), with volumes bounded along the path. The same conclusion for the cover follows by lifting the positive first Neumann eigenfunction, which is still its simple ground state, and the volumes multiply by two. Thus an estimate using only the face and the positive sign cannot provide the missing strict lower bound. This variation has no signed energy bound.

For a specified non-invariant trial on the downstairs disk the exact whole-side test is \(\lambda_1^N(-6\Delta+R)>0\). Equivalently require a positive first Dirichlet eigenvalue and positivity on every nonzero trace of \(6\Lambda_g\), where \(\Lambda_g f=\partial_\nu u_f\) for \((-6\Delta+R)u_f=0\), \(u_f|_{\partial W}=f\). Decompose every \(H^1\) function as \(u_f+v\) with zero trace \(v\). The cross term vanishes and the form is the sum of the Dirichlet form on \(v\) and \(6\int f\Lambda_g f\). Compactness excludes a zero first Neumann eigenvalue when both pieces are strictly positive. This criterion uses the entire interior and retains both paired signs if the candidate is duplicated. No source above supplies this boundary operator or the weighted harmonic trace for a competitor with \(x<3\pi^2/2\).
