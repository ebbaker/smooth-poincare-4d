## E03 atlas and torsion

In integral Mayer–Vietoris, the fundamental class maps to the oriented boundary class. This gives \(H_3(V)=H_3(V')=0\) and \(H_j(\Sigma)=H_j(V)\oplus H_j(V')\) for \(j=1,2\), with both inclusion maps onto. For \(k=b_1(\Sigma)\) and \(a=b_1(V)\), exactness identifies \(\ker i_*\) on \(H_1(\Sigma)\) with \(H^2(V)\), using duality and the surjectivity on \(H_2\). Its rank equals \(b_2(V)=k-a\). The vanishing intersection map proves each side's signature zero. The double folds to \(V\), and the signed diagonal calculation gives \(H_1(DV;\mathbb Z)\cong H_1(V;\mathbb Z)\). Thus \(b_2(DV)=2(k-a)\), \(\chi(V)=1+k-2a\), and the two side Euler characteristics sum to two. The reflected metric is \(C^{2,1}\) in signed geodesic coordinates. A convenient coordinate-independent formulation of its Yamabe constant is the sum of the two side forms on pairs of \(H^1\) functions with matching traces. This formulation directly proves invariance under even conformal rescaling and accommodates a change of geodesic collar. Smooth even mollification converges in \(C^2\), preserving a strictly positive sign and both limiting energies.

The torsion subgroups \(K_V,K_{V'}\) of the opposite inclusion kernels sum directly to all torsion \(G\) of \(H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)\). A torsion class in \(K_V\) is the boundary of a torsion class of \(H_2(V,\Sigma)\) by injectivity of the boundary map. In cohomology its dual therefore comes by restriction from torsion \(H^2(V)\). The Bockstein lift to \(H^1(V;\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)\) evaluates trivially on any other member of \(K_V\). This proves isotropy and the proof for \(V'\) is identical. Nonsingularity makes the off-diagonal pairing perfect, giving \(G\cong A\oplus A\) and complementary metabolizers, with \(\operatorname{Tor}H_1(U)\cong\operatorname{Tor}H_2(DU)\cong A\) for either side \(U\). The cyclic example fails outright. The form \(\operatorname{diag}(1/2,1/2)\) on the two order-two generators in the projective-space example has exactly one isotropic line. These checks establish the claimed obstruction even with free summands. [Kawauchi–Kojima, §6, Lemma 6.1, pp. 40–41](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/kawauchikojima.pdf#page=12)
