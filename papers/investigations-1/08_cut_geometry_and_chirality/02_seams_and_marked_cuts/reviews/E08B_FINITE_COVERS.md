## Double group, pinching and topological consequences

Write $D=DV$, $\chi=\chi(V)$ and $b=e_V$. A smooth representative on $V$ can be made totally geodesic by a normal conformal jet. In its own Fermi collar the reflection of $dt^2+h_t$ through $t=0$ is $C^{2,1}$ because $h'_0=0$. Smooth $C^2$ approximations on the fixed doubled manifold preserve the strict positive Yamabe sign and converge in Weyl energies. Its orientation-reversing reflection exchanges chiralities, so the limiting single-block energy is $2b$, the limiting sum of the two block integrals is $4b$, and $\tau(D)=0$. Also $\chi(D)=2\chi$.

With $A=(\operatorname{Ric}-Rg/6)/2$, the limiting integrated $\sigma_2(A)$ is $T\chi-b$. Thus $b<T\chi$ gives positive integrated $\sigma_2$ and positive class on a smooth approximant. Chang–Gursky–Yang Corollaries B and C give a smooth conformal positive Ricci metric on $D$, so compactness and Bonnet–Myers give finite $\pi_1(D)$. In the 2002 article the Schouten tensor is $2A$ and its $\sigma_2$ integral is four times the one used here. Its strict pinching criterion is unchanged by that conversion. The displayed limit only uses the characteristic-number identities and approximation. [CGY 2002, (0.2), Theorem A, Corollaries B and C, pp. 710, 712–713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583#page=4)

With $P=\pi_1(V)$ and $I$ the boundary image in $P$, van Kampen gives $\pi_1(D)=P*_I P$ by first factoring out the common boundary kernel on each inclusion. If $I$ is proper, alternating copies of a fixed element of $P\setminus I$ yield normal-form words of unbounded length. A finite double therefore forces $I=P$, so the double group is the side group. This proof requires neither residual finiteness nor any assumption about which covers exist when the group is infinite.

For a connected finite cover of degree $n$, pull back each smooth approximant. Its smooth PSC representative also pulls back, preserving positive Yamabe type. If $b_j=(E_+(D,g_j)+E_-(D,g_j))/4$, then a volume-one Yamabe metric on that cover satisfies
\[
 16\pi^2 n\chi=4nb_j-\tfrac12\int |Z|^2+Y_{\rm cov}^2/24 .
\]
The spherical upper bound has $Y(S^4)=12\sqrt{8\pi^2/3}=8\sqrt6\pi$. Hence $Y_{\rm cov}^2/24\le16\pi^2$ and $b\ge T(\chi-1/n)$ after passing to the limit. Taking the universal cover gives $n=|\pi_1(D)|$ in the finite case, and the previous pinching argument gives $b\ge T\chi$ in the infinite case. The argument applies to every finite connected cover with its pullback orientation, and makes no assertion of smooth equality rigidity for rough limit classes. The primary comparison is Sung's Lemma 2.1 in operator norms. He assumes a smooth closed oriented base and $Y\ge0$, proves the same cover bound in one chirality, and treats equality for smooth metrics. Lee–Parker derive the spherical upper bound with normal-coordinate tests. [Sung, Lemma 2.1, pp. 6–7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808#page=6) · [Lee–Parker, Theorem 3.3 and Lemma 3.4, pp. 50–51](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf#page=14) · [Yamabe attainment, §11](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf#page=49)

For $\chi=1$, the full four-index Weyl integral tends to $16b$, and the smooth positive approximants satisfy $16b_j<16\pi^2\chi(D)=32\pi^2$ eventually if $b<T/2$. Chang–Gursky–Yang's 2003 Theorem A gives $S^4$ or $\mathbb{RP}^4$, with orientation eliminating the latter. The fold map retracts $D$ to $V$. With $k=0$, integral Mayer–Vietoris gives $H_3(V;\mathbb Z)=0$ and $H_2(V;\mathbb Z)$ is a summand of $H_2(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)=0$. Simple connectivity kills $H_1$ and gives contractibility by Hurewicz and Whitehead. To invoke the PSC boundary classification, assume intrinsic PSC as supplied by entry and the Gauss theorem. CMM Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2 then give $\Sigma\cong S^3$, and the latter identifies $V$ as a topological ball. These steps give no smooth ball recognition for a general half. ## Residual profiles and the sharpened integral bound

- **Free rank zero with torsion.** When $e<2T$, one side has $b<T$. If a positive double admits a degree $n>1$ cover, $\chi_{\rm cov}=2n$ and signature is zero by pulling back the signature density and multiplying its integral by $n$. Thus $b^+_2=b_2/2\ge n-1>0$. The positive Yamabe self-dual bound on the smooth cover gives $2nb\ge (4\pi^2/3)(4n)$ in the limit, which is $b\ge2T/3$. In E03's torsion splitting, $A\ne0$ gives a side and double abelianization of order $q=|A|>1$ on each side. Its kernel gives a connected $q$-fold cover, hence both $b\ge2T/3$ and $b\ge(1-1/q)T$ per side. The smooth specialization of Sung's Theorem 1.4(v) proves the bound used here, with no need to invoke the equality classification. [Sung, Theorem 1.4(v), p. 5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808#page=5)
- **One spherical factor.** Hillman Theorem 5.2(1), p. 54, concerns a closed three-manifold of finite or solvable fundamental group embedded in a homology four-sphere in the book's locally flat setting. Its finite list is $1,Q_8,I^*$. This hypothesis is on the whole seam and its actual embedding. The $I^*$ case in the locally flat list uses Freedman's topological result. The smooth restriction follows from the vanishing correction term for the boundary of a smooth integral homology ball together with the nonzero correction term of $\Sigma(2,3,5)$. Hillman also gives the smooth $Q_8$ example as the boundary of a regular neighborhood of a projective plane in standard $S^4$. For this boundary, $H_1(\Sigma)=(\mathbb Z/2)^2$ forces $H_1(V)=H_1(V')=\mathbb Z/2$ by E03. A side with $b<T$ has a finite group that is a quotient of $Q_8$. Its abelianization $\mathbb Z/2$ rules out $1,Q_8$ and the sole order-four quotient $(\mathbb Z/2)^2$, leaving $\mathbb Z/2$. The same cover bound costs both sides at least $2T/3$. This leaves the marked filling problem for the right-invariant datum from the sibling ledger. [Hillman, Theorem 5.2(1), pp. 54–55, and §5.6, p. 67](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/jonh/embkDec24.pdf#page=64) · [Ledger marking calculation](../../01_chirality_ledger/notes/QUATERNIONIC_MARKING.md)
- **Free rank one.** For the side with $b_1=0$, $b_2=1$, $\chi=2$ and $b<2T$, the finite group of order $d$ is a quotient of the boundary group. The two estimates give $b\ge\max\{4/3,2-1/d\}T$, so the other side costs less than $\min\{2/3,1/d\}T$. For the pure $S^1\times S^2$ boundary, the $b_1=0$ side has zero integral $H_1$, since it is a rank-zero direct summand of $\mathbb Z$ by Mayer–Vietoris. The finite cyclic quotient of $\mathbb Z$ is therefore trivial. The other side has an infinite group since its $b_1=1$. This proof uses the actual boundary inclusion for the finite-side group.

For the remaining nonempty integral homology-sphere case, intrinsic PSC and CMM Corollary 2.3 reduce $\Sigma$ to a sum of positively or negatively oriented Poincaré spheres. Each side is an integral homology ball by Mayer–Vietoris. Removing an interior smooth ball gives a smooth integral homology cobordism to $S^3$. The spin structure extends across this punctured homology ball and gives the needed spin-c bordism. Ozsváth–Szabó Theorem 1.2 makes $d(\Sigma)=0$, Theorem 4.3 and Proposition 6.3 add and reverse the signs of the correction terms, and §8.1 computes a nonzero correction term for the Poincaré sphere. Thus the counts of the two orientations agree. CMM Proposition 4.2 adds a different input for **contractible** fillings, and its periodic-end obstruction to the balanced nonempty case requires that stronger premise. In particular a finite side group here cannot be trivial, because a simply connected smooth homology ball is contractible. [Ozsváth–Szabó v2, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110170#page=2) · [Theorem 4.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110170#page=22) · [Proposition 6.3](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110170#page=33) · [§8.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110170#page=46) · [CMM, Proposition 4.2 and proof](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574#page=10)

A finite double group is generated by the boundary images, so some $I^*$ factor has nontrivial image. The proper normal subgroups of $I^*$ lie in its center of order two: a subgroup with nontrivial projection onto simple $A_5$ projects onto all of $A_5$, and if that subgroup is normal the quotient has order at most two, hence is trivial by perfection of $I^*$. It follows that a nontrivial factor image has order $60$ or $120$. For finite $d$ this proves $d\ge60$, with $b\ge59T/60$. Infinite doubles give $b\ge T$. Summing verifies $e\ge59T/30$.

**Additional strengthening.** In the proof just given the only possible finite double group of order $<120$ is $A_5$, because its order would have to be a multiple of $60$ and the order-$60$ factor image would fill it. For an integral homology-ball side $V$ one can build $K(\pi_1V,1)$ from $V$ by attaching cells of dimensions at least three to kill higher homotopy. Hence $H_2(V;\mathbb Z)\to H_2(\pi_1V;\mathbb Z)$ is onto and the latter vanishes. The central extension $1\to\mathbb Z/2\to I^*\to A_5\to1$ is nonsplit, since splitting a central extension makes a direct product with $\mathbb Z/2$, whereas $I^*$ is perfect. Extension classification therefore gives $H^2(A_5;\mathbb Z/2)\ne0$. The universal coefficient theorem and $H_1(A_5;\mathbb Z)=0$ force $H_2(A_5;\mathbb Z)\ne0$. Since a finite double group agrees with the side group, order $60$ is excluded. Each finite group therefore has $d\ge120$, giving $b\ge119T/120$. An infinite group again gives $b\ge T$. The sharpened necessary condition is
\[
                         (119/60)T\le e<2T .
\]
This is a bound for the balanced nonempty PSC integral homology spheres on this positive-double splitting. It supplies no realization. [Weibel, Classification Theorem 6.6.3](https://math.mit.edu/~hrm/palestine/weibel/06-group_homology_and_cohomology.pdf) · [Hatcher, Algebraic Topology, §3.1 and §4.2](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/AT/AT.pdf)

## Gluing the calibrating models

The round equator has two round hemispheres, each Weyl-flat, with positive reflected classes, and the ambient round class enters. For the pure free-rank-one profile, use unit $S^2\times S^2_+$ and $S^1_{2\pi}\times S^3_+$ with their product metrics and the product identification of their boundary $S^2\times S^1$. The smooth marked assembly is the rounded boundary of $D^2\times D^3$. This follows by identifying $S^2_+$ with $D^2$ and $S^3_+$ with $D^3$ respecting the round boundaries, then matching the common corner with the product map. The orientation on the two faces is the boundary orientation of the product, so their copies of the common boundary have opposite outward orientation.

For the product half the curvature operator equals one on the two factor area forms and zero on mixed bivectors. Restricting to either chiral block and subtracting $R/12=1/3$ gives eigenvalues $(2/3,-1/3,-1/3)$. Their squared sum is $2/3$, and the half-product volume is $(4\pi)(2\pi)=8\pi^2$. Thus $e_V=16\pi^2/3=4T/3$. The other half is locally a subset of the cylinder $\mathbb R\times S^3$, which is conformally Euclidean in polar coordinates, so its Weyl cost is zero. The product scalar curvatures are $4$ and $6$. Both boundaries are totally geodesic and the original reflected metrics have positive scalar curvature.

The closed piecewise metric is $C^{1,1}$ through the product seam, where the first normal derivatives vanish. On each side replace its tangential metric $h_s$ in the collar by $h_0+\theta(s/\epsilon)(h_s-h_0)$ with $\theta$ zero by $s=\epsilon$ and one from $2\epsilon$ on. The estimates $h_s-h_0=O(s^2)$, $\partial_s h_s=O(s)$ and bounded second derivatives imply a $C^1$ change tending to zero and bounded second derivative errors supported on a layer of volume $O(\epsilon)$. The closed metric and both doubles are now smooth and remain positive in Yamabe sign: the original piecewise scalar form bounds the $H^1$ norm, and Sobolev controls the error from the scalar change in $L^2$ times $\|u\|_4^2$. The curvature integrals converge by the same uniform bounds and support. Hence the smoothed ambient shared energy tends to $4T/3<2T$, with an actual umbilic marking and positive doubles. At the limiting original pair the second jets need not join smoothly. This verifies the control as phrased in E06.
