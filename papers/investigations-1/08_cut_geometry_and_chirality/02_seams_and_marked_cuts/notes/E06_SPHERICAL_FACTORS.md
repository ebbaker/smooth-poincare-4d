## A bound that detects the double's fundamental group

Let \(D=\mathcal DV\), \(\chi=\chi(V)\), \(b=e_V\) and \(G=\pi_1D\). For a finite group let \(d=|G|\), and put \(1/d=0\) for an infinite group. Then
\[
                   b\ \ge\ T(\chi-1/d).
\]
If \(b<T\chi\), the boundary maps onto \(\pi_1V\) and \(G=\pi_1V\) is finite. These extra conclusions do not require residual finiteness.

Indeed \(E_+(D)+E_-(D)=4b\) and \(\chi(D)=2\chi\). Normalize the umbilic seam to totally geodesic. The even reflected metric is \(C^{2,1}\) in its smooth doubled collar. Smooth \(C^2\) approximations converge in Weyl energy and preserve the strictly positive Yamabe sign. The identity \(4\int_D\sigma_2(A)=16\pi^2\chi-4b\) in the limit forces positive integrated \(\sigma_2\) on sufficiently close approximations if \(b<T\chi\). Chang–Gursky–Yang, Corollary B and §8, then give positive Ricci curvature in one smooth class, hence finite \(G\). For finite \(d\), lift each smooth approximation to the universal cover; a positive scalar representative lifts, hence the cover class is positive. On its volume-one Yamabe metric,
\[
  16\pi^2 d\chi=d\!\int_D |W|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2
     -\tfrac12\int_{\widetilde D}|Z|^2+Y([\tilde g])^2/24 .
\]
The positive Yamabe constant is at most \(Y(S^4)=12\sqrt{8\pi^2/3}\). Dropping the nonpositive term and taking the limit gives \(b\ge T\chi-T/d\). The same argument works for any finite connected positive cover of degree \(d'\), without assuming it is the universal cover. Each smooth metric here is on a fixed smooth double; no singular compactification theorem is used. The universal Yamabe upper bound also follows by testing in a small normal ball with cutoffs of the rescaled stereographic round conformal factor. [CGY, Corollary B and §8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583) · [Lee–Parker, §§3–5](https://users.math.msu.edu/users/parker/YamabeProblem.pdf)

The double group is the amalgam \(P*_{I}P\), \(P=\pi_1V\), \(I=\operatorname{im}(\pi_1\Sigma\to P)\). The two boundary maps are identical. Consequently their common kernel can be factored out of the pushout. If \(I\ne P\), alternating a representative outside \(I\) in the two factors gives reduced words of unbounded length. Thus finiteness forces \(I=P\) and then \(G=P\). Equivalently in the finite universal cover of \(D\), the lift of the two halves has dual incidence graph a tree (a loop in the graph lifts to a loop in the simply connected cover). The fold retract and deck symmetry give the same surjectivity conclusion.

If \(\chi=1\), \(b<T/2\) implies \(D\cong S^4\): the four-index Weyl integral on \(D\) tends to \(16b< 16\pi^2\chi(D)\). Apply the strict sphere theorem to a smooth positive approximation, and use orientability to exclude \(\mathbb{RP}^4\). The side retracts from the double. If it is a rational homology ball in this splitting, its integral \(H_2\) is zero (it is a summand of the torsion-free \(H_2(\Sigma)\) and has rank zero), and simple connectivity now implies contractibility. For a PSC boundary the Chodosh–Maximo–Mukherjee classification and contractible filling proposition force \(\Sigma\cong S^3\); the proposition identifies the filling topologically. [CGY, Theorem A and (0.4)](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf) · [CMM, Corollary 2.3 and Proposition 4.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574)

## Consequences for the two residual rows

*Free rank zero.* Both halves are rational homology balls. At least one has \(b<T\) in an entering class, so at least one has finite fundamental group generated by its boundary image, equal to its double group. If neither group is finite the budget fails. For either positive double admitting a connected \(d'>1\) cover, \(\chi(\widetilde D)=2d'\), so \(b_2(\widetilde D)=2d'-2+2b_1(\widetilde D)>0\). Its signature is zero (the signature density lifts and the base double has zero signature). Gursky's \(b_2^+\) bound from E03, divided by \(d'\), gives \(b\ge 2T/3\) independently of \(d'\). In particular if the boundary has nontrivial finite first homology then the torsion matching in E03 gives a nontrivial finite abelian quotient on each side and hence each double; each side costs at least \(2T/3\). If its side torsion group has order \(q\), also \(b\ge(1-1/q)T\).

For a single spherical prime, the finite-group case of Hillman's Theorem 5.2 on an **integral homology** four-sphere leaves groups \(1,Q_8,I^*\). The Poincaré sphere cannot bound a smooth integral homology ball: the correction-term argument in CMM's proof of Proposition 4.2 (or the spin \(E_8\) plumbing and Rokhlin). For a smooth embedded integral homology sphere, both halves here are integral homology balls; the same correction-term proof requires equal counts of the two orientations of \(P=S^3/I^*\) in a PSC connected sum. The theorem on a single prime does not apply factor by factor to a reducible boundary. [Hillman, Theorem 5.2(1) and §5.6](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/jonh/embkDec24.pdf) · [CMM, pp. 9–10](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.05574)

There is a quantitative strengthening in the remaining nonempty integral homology-sphere case. Each finite double group is nontrivial, since a trivial one would make the half contractible and CMM would give \(S^3\). Finiteness makes the group a quotient of the free product of the binary icosahedral groups contributed by the boundary primes. Some factor has nontrivial image of order at least 60. Indeed \(I^*/\{\pm1\}=A_5\) is simple and \(I^*\) is perfect. A normal subgroup projecting onto \(A_5\) would have abelian quotient of order at most two, hence would be the whole group. Thus a nontrivial image has order 60 or 120. The finite-cover estimate (or its infinite-group alternative) yields on *each* side \(b\ge (59/60)T\). Hence this residual integral family requires
\[
                         (59/30)T \le e < 2T .
\]
This is an exclusion below a narrow band, not an example in the band.

For \(Q_8\) the boundary first homology is \((\mathbb Z/2)^2\); both halves have \(H_1=\mathbb Z/2\). A side with \(b<T\) therefore has finite group \(\mathbb Z/2\): it is a quotient of \(Q_8\) with this abelianization. Both sides cost at least \(2T/3\). A smooth embedding with the topology of a normal disk bundle of \(\mathbb{RP}^2\) is known in standard \(S^4\). The sibling [ledger return](../../01_chirality_ledger/research-progress/handoffs/TO_SEAM_SPACE_FORM_TESTS_20261127.md) excludes the round class and all conformal classes whose lift is left-invariant in its stated marking. It gives the **different** right-invariant lifted metric \(t=\sqrt3\) on the very same left quotient with \(R=2,\ \eta=-5/12\). It passes the intrinsic and signature tests. A prescribed form on the abstract boundary still needs positive four-dimensional fillings on both marked sides with sufficiently small combined energy. [Ledger, the marked calculation](../../01_chirality_ledger/notes/QUATERNIONIC_MARKING.md)

*Free rank one.* The side with \(\chi=2\) always costs less than \(2T\) under strict entry. Consequently its boundary surjects onto a finite \(\pi_1V=\pi_1D\) of order \(d\),
\[
 e_V\ge\max\{4T/3,(2-1/d)T\},\qquad
 e_{V'}<\min\{2T/3,T/d\}.
\]
For the pure \(S^1\times S^2\) boundary this finite group is a quotient of \(\mathbb Z\) and has zero abelianization by integral Mayer–Vietoris and duality. It is trivial. This gives simple connectivity of the expensive side, not a classification of its smooth filling. The other side has infinite fundamental group since \(b_1=1\).

## Controls and excluded inferences

A round equator realizes the pure sphere row with \(b=b'=0\). A product half \(S^2\times S^2_+\) and \(S^1_{2\pi}\times S^3_+\) have isometric \(S^2\times S^1\) boundaries, both totally geodesic, positive scalar curvature and positive doubles. They glue with the untwisted product marking to \(\partial(D^2\times D^3)\cong S^4\) after rounding the corners. The half-product costs \(4T/3\): on the product of unit two-spheres the Weyl eigenvalues on each chirality are \(2/3,-1/3,-1/3\), the volume of the half is \(8\pi^2\). The half-Hopf side is locally conformally flat and costs zero. Productizing in shrinking collars yields smooth entering representatives with the marked umbilic cut, positive doubles, and energy tending to \(4T/3\); the preservation argument is in the paired filling note. This control proves realization of this pair, not exact attainment by a smooth gluing of the two original second jets. All assertions concern actual side markings or necessary conditions. Torsion or equality of Betti numbers alone cannot supply a filling.

**Integrated refinement (E08B/E11A).** In the displayed smooth Chern–Gauss–Bonnet equality for a cover, the base integral is that of the smooth approximation at the same index. The energy \(4b\) enters only in the limit. At each use of the PSC prime classification intrinsic PSC is assumed or obtained from the entering metric. Correction-term additivity, orientation reversal and vanishing on the boundary of a smooth integral homology ball give the balanced-orientation result; CMM Proposition 4.2 specifically needs a contractible filling. The [source and order check](../reviews/E08B_FINITE_COVERS.md) improves the bound \(59T/30\) above. If a finite side group had order below 120, the boundary-factor argument forces it to be \(A_5\). But \(H_2(\pi_1V;\mathbb Z)=0\), since a \(K(\pi_1V,1)\) is built from the acyclic side by adding cells in dimension at least three. The nonsplit central extension \(2.A_5\to A_5\) gives a nonzero class in \(H^2(A_5;\mathbb Z/2)\). Since \(A_5\) is perfect, the universal-coefficient theorem forces \(H_2(A_5;\mathbb Z)\ne0\). Thus each finite group has order at least 120, and the final necessary interval for a nonempty PSC integral homology-sphere seam is \([119T/60,2T)\). An infinite side group costs at least \(T\).
