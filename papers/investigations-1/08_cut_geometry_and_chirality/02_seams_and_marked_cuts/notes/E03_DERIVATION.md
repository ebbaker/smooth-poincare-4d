## Statement and conventions

Let $M$ be a closed connected oriented smooth Riemannian homotopy four-sphere and $M=V\cup_\Sigma V'$ a decomposition along a connected smooth umbilic hypersurface. Give $\Sigma$ its boundary orientation from $V$. Assume both reflected doubles $DV=V\cup_\Sigma\overline V$ and $DV'$ have positive Yamabe constant in their reflected classes. Work with Hilbert–Schmidt norms of the Weyl blocks acting on $\Lambda^\pm$, $E_\pm(U)=\int_U|W^\pm|^2_{\mathrm{HS}}$, $e_U=(E_+(U)+E_-(U))/2$, $e=e_V+e_{V'}=E_+(M)=E_-(M)$, and $T=4\pi^2$. Here $\Sigma$ is assumed to have positive scalar curvature (PSC), or is known to have it from the parent positivity theorem when the ambient class enters, meaning $Y(M,[g])>0$ and $e<2T$. In the latter case the gauge of §2 has $R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)>0$, and for $B^\circ=0$ the Gauss equation gives $R_\Sigma=R-2\operatorname{Ric}(\nu,\nu)+2H^2/3>0$. A connected oriented closed PSC three-manifold has the form

$$
\Sigma\cong \#_k(S^1\times S^2)\ \#\ (S^3/\Gamma_1)\ \#\ \dots\ \#\ (S^3/\Gamma_m)
$$

with oriented spherical space forms and $k=b_1(\Sigma)$. This is the forward implication of Perelman’s [*Ricci flow with surgery on three-manifolds*, §8.2(a), p. 21](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0303109v1#page=21), since $R>0$ makes $\lambda(-4\Delta+R)>0$. The empty connected sum denotes $S^3$. [Parent §§2, 4 and 5](../../manuscript/cuts_of_subcritical_conformal_classes.tex)

## Integral topology of the sides

Alexander duality or the separation criterion $H^1(M;\mathbb Z_2)=0$ gives two connected sides. In integral Mayer–Vietoris for $M$, the map $H_4(M)\to H_3(\Sigma)$ takes the oriented fundamental class to a generator. Hence $H_3(V)=H_3(V')=0$ and $H_j(\Sigma)\simeq H_j(V)\oplus H_j(V')$ for $j=1,2$, with the two components the inclusion maps up to sign. In particular both inclusion maps in each degree are onto. $H_2(\Sigma)\simeq\mathbb Z^k$ by duality, so both sides have free $H_2$. The map $H_2(V)\to H_2(V,\Sigma)$ vanishes by exactness, so the intersection form of each side is zero and both side signatures vanish.

For $a=b_1(V)$, $b_1(V')=k-a$. The segment of the pair sequence beginning at $H_2(V)\to H_2(V,\Sigma)$ shows that $\partial:H_2(V,\Sigma)\to H_1(\Sigma)$ is injective and has image the kernel of $H_1(\Sigma)\to H_1(V)$. Duality identifies $H_2(V,\Sigma)\simeq H^2(V)$, so by the universal coefficient theorem the rank of this kernel is $b_2(V)=k-a$, and $b_2(V')=a$. For $U=V,V'$, Mayer–Vietoris of $DU$ along a connected boundary gives $H_1(DU;\mathbb Z)\simeq H_1(U;\mathbb Z)$ as the cokernel of the signed diagonal map, using surjectivity from the boundary. The double has Euler characteristic $2\chi(U)$ and signature zero by the orientation-reversing involution. Thus

| Region | $b_1$ | $b_2$ | $b_3$ | $\chi$ | Signature |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| $V$ | $a$ | $k-a$ | $0$ | $1+k-2a$ | $0$ |
| $V'$ | $k-a$ | $a$ | $0$ | $1-k+2a$ | $0$ |
| $DV$ | $a$ | $2(k-a)$ | $a$ | $2(1+k-2a)$ | $0$ |
| $DV'$ | $k-a$ | $2a$ | $k-a$ | $2(1-k+2a)$ | $0$ |

Write $G=\operatorname{Tor}H_1(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$ and let $K_U=\ker(G\to\operatorname{Tor}H_1(U;\mathbb Z))$. Mayer–Vietoris gives $G=K_V\oplus K_{V'}$. Each kernel is isotropic for the nonsingular torsion linking pairing $\lambda:G\times G\to\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z$. To see this for $V$, duality identifies the boundary map of the pair with $H^2(V;\mathbb Z)\to H^2(\Sigma;\mathbb Z)$, up to orientation sign. The latter map is injective on the free quotients because $H_2(\Sigma)\to H_2(V)$ is onto. Thus the Poincaré dual of $x\in K_V$ is the restriction of a torsion class $\alpha\in H^2(V;\mathbb Z)$. Lift $\alpha$ by the Bockstein to $\widetilde\alpha\in H^1(V;\mathbb Q/\mathbb Z)$. For $y\in K_V$ the linking value is, up to sign, $\langle i^*\widetilde\alpha,y\rangle=\langle\widetilde\alpha,i_*y\rangle=0$. The same proof works for the opposite boundary orientation of $V'$. Nonsingularity identifies the two kernels as finite-group duals. Consequently $G\simeq A\oplus A$ and $\operatorname{Tor}H_1(V)\simeq\operatorname{Tor}H_1(V')\simeq A$ for a finite abelian group $A$. The pairing must be hyperbolic, with these two complementary metabolizers, and $H_2(DU)$ has torsion $A$ by closed four-dimensional duality and universal coefficients. A nontrivial cyclic $G$, for instance a single lens space of order greater than one even after adjoining free factors, fails. Square order alone is insufficient. For $\mathbb RP^3\#(-\mathbb RP^3)$ the two nonzero diagonal self-pairings of the generators are $1/2$ mod $1$, so there is only one isotropic line. The restrictions derive from the embedding in an integral homology sphere. Compare [Kawauchi–Kojima, *Algebraic classification of linking pairings on 3-manifolds*, Lemma 6.1](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/kawauchikojima.pdf).

## Double regularity and inequalities

On each side pick a smooth representative $h=e^{2u}g$ with $\partial_\nu u=-H_g/3$ along $\Sigma$, by a collar extension of the specified normal derivative. In the inward Gaussian collar $h=dt^2+h_t$ with $h'_0=0$. The reflected expression $dt^2+h_{|t|}$ is $C^{2,1}$, using bounded one-sided third derivatives and matching second derivatives. It can fail to be $C^3$. The parent’s $C^{1,1}$ designation is valid. Changes of the smooth totally geodesic gauge have vanishing normal derivative for their conformal factor, so the even extension in this collar preserves the rough conformal class. Mollification in smooth double charts and a partition of unity give $C^2$ convergence, and averaging by reflection preserves it and yields smooth reflection-invariant approximants. In particular curvature and Weyl energies converge. For a $C^2$-convergent metric sequence the quadratic Yamabe numerator $Q_j(u)=\int(6|\nabla u|_j^2+R_j u^2)\,dV_j$ changes by at most $o(1)(\|du\|_2^2+\|u\|_4^2)$ in a fixed metric. The denominators $\|u\|_{4,j}^2$ are uniformly multiplicatively comparable with ratio tending to one. The scalar terms are bounded below by $-C\|u\|_4^2$, so normalized near minimizers have bounded Dirichlet integrals uniformly and the Yamabe constants converge by comparison with both sets of test functions. This also proves continuity for $C^1\cap W^{2,2}$ convergence with uniform positivity of the metric, using Hölder for the scalar error in $L^2$. This approximation checks positivity of the approximants whenever the reflected constant is positive. The reflection exchanges the two Weyl blocks, so $E_\pm(DU)=2e_U$.

For a smooth closed oriented positive Yamabe class the two Gursky bounds in operator norm are $E_+\ge2\pi^2(2\chi+3\tau)$ if $b_1>0$, and $E_+\ge(4\pi^2/3)(2\chi+3\tau)$ if $b_2^+>0$. The quoted four-index constants are $8\pi^2$ and $16\pi^2/3$, respectively, and the squared norm divides by four on passing to operators. Sung uses operator norm and reproduces the two bounds in Theorems 1.3(iii) and 1.4(v), respectively. For the former, a surjection to $\mathbb Z$ supplies covers of arbitrary degree. His Theorem 1.2(i)–(ii) and Fu’s Theorems A and C give consistent statements of the original result and its smooth equality cases. Gursky’s original article record was checked, and the JSTOR full text was inaccessible. The rigorous inequalities here use Sung’s primary proof. For the $b_1$ bound alone, CGY’s 2002 Corollary C gives positive Ricci under strict reverse pinching, contradicting $b_1>0$. [Gursky publication record](https://annals.math.princeton.edu/articles/12993) · [Sung, arXiv:2108.03808v1, Theorems 1.2–1.4 and Lemma 2.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808v1#page=4) · [Fu, pp. 80–81, equation (1-1) and Theorems A and C](https://msp.org/pjm/2018/296-1/pjm-v296-n1-p05-s.pdf#page=2) · [Chang–Gursky–Yang 2002, Corollary C, p. 713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583v1#page=5)

Since $\tau(DU)=0$, $b_2^+(DU)=b_2(DU)/2$. Passing the smooth inequalities to the limit proves §5(5.1) with the master’s constants:

$$
 e_U\ge T\chi(U)\quad(b_1(DU)>0),\qquad
 e_U\ge \tfrac23 T\chi(U)\quad(b_2(DU)>0).
$$

Using a unit-volume Yamabe representative of each smooth approximant and dropping its nonnegative $\frac18\int|Z|^2$ gives $e_U\ge T\chi(U)-Y(DU)^2/96$ after passage to the limit, for all signs of $Y(DU)$. The smooth identity giving this is $8\pi^2\chi=E_++E_-+\int(R^2/24-|Z|^2/2)$. The limit proof does not require Yamabe attainment by the reflected metric. An equality classification for a smooth class does not in general follow by taking this limit. [Master doubling proposition](../../../../master/manuscript/claude/umbilic_doubling.tex) · [Yamabe formula and spherical upper bound, Sung §1–2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808v1#page=2)

## Necessary atlas and wall cases

Since both $\chi$'s sum to two, the two side bounds and nonnegativity give the following necessary cases for an entering class. Here $d$ denotes the side with $b_2=1$ and $c$ the side with $b_1=1$ in the $k=1$ row.

| $k,a$ | Sides as $(b_1,b_2,\chi)$ | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $k=0,a=0$ | $(0,0,1)$ on both sides | Both are rational homology balls. The two Betti-number inequalities alone are silent. |
| $k=1,a=0$ or $1$ | $d=(0,1,2)$ and $c=(1,0,0)$, up to exchange | $4T/3\le e_d<2T$, $0\le e_c<2T/3$, and $e_d+e_c<2T$. |
| $k\ge2,0<a<k$ | As in the table above | Both $b_1$'s of the doubles are positive, hence $e\ge2T$. A negative side Euler characteristic improves this to $e>2T$ because the other is greater than two. |
| $k\ge2,a\in\{0,k\}$ | One side $(0,k,1+k)$, the other $(k,0,1-k)$ | $e\ge(2/3)(1+k)T\ge2T$, strict for $k\ge3$. |

Consequently the oriented Euler pairs for a connected entering seam are $(1,1),(2,0),(0,2)$, with *both* sides rational homology balls in $(1,1)$. The Betti-number inequalities depend only on the free factors. Section 6’s opening and the overview’s exterior-gap alternative require a separate restriction to the pure free-factor or specified tube class before concluding that the seam is $S^3$ or $S^1\times S^2$ or that the $b_2=1$ side is a tube. The [inherited arithmetic script](../../../../master/brainstorm/notes/claude/USES_OF_THE_FIRST_ORDER_AND_MARKED_CUT_TOOLS_20261126_checks/check_03_seams_eta_arithmetic.py) checks the pure free-factor table for finite $k$. The inequalities above prove the table for every $k$.

At the ambient wall $e=2T$ with $k\ge2$, the numerical possibilities are the interior pairs with both $\chi\ge0$ and with each $e_U=T\chi(U)$, and the extreme $k=2$ pair with $\chi=3,-1$ and energies $2T,0$, respectively. In interior pairs even $k$ permits only $(1,1)$ and odd $k$ permits only $(2,0),(0,2)$. With smooth reflected classes none of these wall cases is attainable. In the interior pair, smooth $b_1$ equality forces the positive-Euler side to be conformally cylindrical, hence to have $e_U=0$, contrary to $e_U=T\chi(U)>0$. At the extreme $k=2$ pair, $2T=3T-Y(DU)^2/96$ would force $Y(DU)=Y(S^4)=8\sqrt6\pi$. A smooth class attaining this upper bound is round up to conformal equivalence, inconsistent with $\chi(DU)=6$. For a merely $C^{2,1}$ reflected class these equality rigidity deductions are left open here. In the extreme pair the degree bound below nonetheless forces the large-Euler side to be simply connected and the seam to have no torsion. [Sung, Theorem 1.2(ii) and Lemma 2.1, including the smooth round equality case](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808v1#page=4)

## Strict pinching and finite covers on the remaining rows

If $e_U<T\chi(U)$, smooth approximation retains the strict inequality and positivity. CGY 2002, Corollary C supplies a positive-Ricci metric on $DU$, so $\pi_1(DU)$ is finite. Writing $\pi_1(DU)=B*_J B$, where $B=\pi_1(U)$ and $J\le B$ is the image of the connected boundary, the amalgamated product has an alternating reduced normal form of arbitrary length if $J\ne B$. Thus the finite case forces $\pi_1(\Sigma)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_1(U)$ and $\pi_1(DU)\simeq\pi_1(U)$. The expression for the amalgam uses identical inclusion kernels on the two copies of $U$.

If $p:\widetilde{DU}\to DU$ is a connected finite cover of degree $n$, pullbacks of the smooth positive Yamabe approximants have positive Yamabe type, by pulling back their positive scalar curvature representatives. The curvature integrals and Euler characteristic multiply by $n$. The preceding Gauss–Bonnet argument and $0<Y\le 8\sqrt6\pi$ on the covers give

$$
 e_U\ge \left(\chi(U)-\frac1n\right)T .
$$

For a smooth reflected class, equality with $n>1$ is impossible by the equality statement of Sung’s Lemma 2.1. The weak bound is valid for rough doubles by approximation. If $k=0$ and there is a degree $n>1$ cover, then $\chi(\widetilde{DU})=2n$, $\tau(\widetilde{DU})=0$ by signature multiplicativity, and $b_2(\widetilde{DU})=2(n-1)+2b_1(\widetilde{DU})>0$. The chiral bound on the cover gives $e_U\ge2T/3$. For $k=0$ at least one side has $e_U<T$ under strict entry, so its double has finite $\pi_1$. If $A\ne0$, both sides have a nontrivial finite quotient of order $|A|$, and both sides obey $e_U\ge\max(2/3,1-1/|A|)T$. These constraints are still compatible with strict entry. For $k=1$ the $d$ side has $e_d<2T=T\chi(d)$, so $n=|\pi_1(d)|<\infty$ and

$$
 e_d\ge\max(4/3,2-1/n)T,\qquad e_c<\min(2/3,1/n)T .
$$

Here $n$ is divisible by $|A|$, and $e_d=4T/3$ requires $n=1$, so the seam then has no torsion even for a rough double. At $k=2$, $a=0$ or $2$, $e=2T$, apply the finite-group conclusion to the side of Euler characteristic three and the degree bound with its universal cover. Its degree is one, which likewise forces $A=0$. [CGY 2002, Corollary C, p. 713](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0409583v1#page=5) · [Sung, Lemma 2.1 and Theorems 1.3 and 1.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808v1#page=4)

For $k=0$, CGY 2003 Theorem A, with $|W|_{(0,4)}^2=4|W|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2$, identifies $DU\cong S^4$ if $e_U<T/2$: its double has $\chi=2$, total operator Weyl energy $4e_U$, and positive Yamabe constant. Smooth approximation keeps strict pinching, and orientation excludes $\mathbb RP^4$. At $e_U=T/2$ the preceding finite-group and cover arguments force $\pi_1(DU)=1$ even for the rough double. For a smooth reflected conformal class, CGY’s Theorem B also gives $DU\cong S^4$ at this equality: the $\mathbb CP^2$ and covered-product alternatives have the wrong Euler characteristic. With a merely reflected $C^{2,1}$ class the equality argument identifies only a homotopy sphere here. On either side even a standard double does not determine the diffeomorphism type of the half or show that the original cut is free. With $\Sigma=S^3$ the sides are simply connected by van Kampen, but this does not identify a homotopy-ball side as a smooth ball. [CGY 2003, Theorems A and B, Remark 2 and equation (0.4), pp. 106–108](https://numdam.org/item/10.1007/s10240-003-0017-z.pdf#page=2)

## Calibrations and first undecided realization

- The round equator realizes $k=0$ with $e=0$ and two positive round doubles.
- The pure $k=1$ model realizes $e_d=4T/3$ in a smooth entering class, with $e_c>0$ arbitrarily small. Join the half-product $S^2\times D^2_{\rm hemi}$ to $S^1\times D^3$ with their product boundary, giving the rounded boundary of $D^2\times D^3$. The normal warps in gauges with unit $S^2$ are $a(t)=\cos t$ on the cap $t\le0$ and $a(t)=\cosh t$ on the exterior $t\ge0$. The exterior compactifies by the Hopf factor $\operatorname{sech}t$, using $\sin\beta=\tanh t$ to produce $d\beta^2+d\psi^2+\cos^2\beta\,g_{S^2}$. Use the Hopf factor throughout the exterior for the limiting metric. Its value and first derivative match the constant factor on the cap, so the joined metric is $C^{1,1}$ and has positive scalar curvature off the seam ($R=4$ on the cap and $R=6$ on the exterior). The limiting doubles are the smooth product and Hopf representatives. Smooth from the exterior only, taking the warp and factor to be $\cos t$ and $1$ on $0\le t\le\epsilon$ and interpolating to $\cosh t$ and $\operatorname{sech}t$ before $2\epsilon$. Both pairs differ by $O(t^2)$ with matching first jets. A scaled smooth cutoff gives uniformly bounded second derivatives on the shrinking interpolation region and $C^1\cap W^{2,2}$ convergence, including on the exterior double. The ambient and doubles retain positive Yamabe constants, and the ambient metric and both doubles are smooth with the seam totally geodesic. The exterior Weyl energy tends to zero and is positive because it includes a nonzero half-product collar. In the cap the product Weyl block has squared norm $2/3$, and the cap volume is $8\pi^2$, giving $e_d=4T/3$ exactly. [Parent §6, first cap in Proposition 6.2](../../manuscript/cuts_of_subcritical_conformal_classes.tex) · [Master Hopf and half-product models](../../../../master/manuscript/claude/umbilic_doubling.tex)
- For the $k=1$ lower endpoint with a smooth rich-side double, equality in both chiral Gursky bounds implies positive Kähler–Einstein structures in the two orientations. The Einstein metrics are homothetic. Indeed for conformally related Einstein metrics $h$ and $f^{-2}h$ the trace-free Ricci formula gives $(\nabla_h^2 f)^\circ=0$. Taking a divergence and subtracting a constant gives the Obata equation for a nonconstant $f$, which forces the round sphere. The two parallel complex structures commute since they have opposite orientation, and their product has two parallel rank-two eigenspaces. The universal cover splits into equal-curvature round two-spheres by the de Rham splitting and the Einstein condition. The double has $\chi=4$, so this cover has degree one. The doubling involution preserves the Einstein metric by the same uniqueness. An involution exchanging the two factors is orientation-preserving, while the orientation-reversing product involution fixing a hypersurface is identity on one factor and a great-circle reflection on the other. Thus smooth equality has the pure $S^1\times S^2$ seam and half-product cap described in the model. The $C^{2,1}$ proof gives the numerical and trivial-fundamental-group restrictions above. It leaves the corresponding equality rigidity (including exclusion of torsion-free spherical summands at equality) undecided. [Sung, Theorem 1.2(i)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.03808v1#page=3) · [Obata, *Certain conditions for a Riemannian manifold to be isometric with a sphere*, Theorem A, p. 334](https://projecteuclid.org/journals/journal-of-the-mathematical-society-of-japan/volume-14/issue-3/Certain-conditions-for-a-Riemannian-manifold-to-be-isometric-with/10.2969/jmsj/01430333.pdf#page=2)
- A concrete first undecided geometry is $L(3,1)\#(-L(3,1))$ in the $k=0$ row. Its linking form is the orthogonal sum of a cyclic order-three form and its negative. The diagonal and antidiagonal lines are complementary isotropic subgroups, so the integral test permits it. It has an actual smooth embedding in $S^4$: double the punctured $L(3,1)$ as the rounded boundary of its product neighborhood using a fibre of the two-twist-spun trefoil. For this construction the two complementary regions have fundamental group $\mathbb Z/3$. The neighborhood is the product of the fibre with an interval. The other region is the cut-open knot exterior, also a fibre times an interval, joined to the simply connected knot neighborhood $S^2\times D^2$ along $S^2\times I$. The boundary maps onto each region's fundamental group because it includes a fibre face. Thus each double has a degree-three universal cover and the bounds require $2T/3\le e_U<4T/3$ on each side if it enters, with strict lower endpoint for smooth doubles. This is a topological embedding and a nonempty energy range, leaving the geometric question open: can this embedding be made umbilic in a smooth entering class with both reflected Yamabe constants positive? The $k=1$ profiles with spherical summands also remain realization questions once the necessary restrictions above are imposed.
