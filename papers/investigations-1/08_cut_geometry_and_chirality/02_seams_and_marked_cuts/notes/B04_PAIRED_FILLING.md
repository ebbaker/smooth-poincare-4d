## Exact reduction for each surviving marked pair

Fix \(M=V\cup_\phi V'\), a smooth homotopy four-sphere with closed connected seam, with \(\phi:\partial V\to\partial V'\) the actual gluing. For a smooth boundary metric \(h\) on \(\partial V\) define
\[
 F_V(h)=\inf\{e_V(g): g|_{T\partial V}=h,\ B_g=0,\
                 \lambda_{\rm Robin}(V,g)>0\}.
\]
The metrics are smooth up to the boundary. Take \(+\infty\) for an empty collection. The sign in this normalized gauge is the Neumann sign. A boundary conformal factor extends positively with vanishing normal logarithmic derivative; thus \(F_V\) depends only on \([h]\). Transport by a map of a side transports its boundary marking. Write \(\phi_*h=(\phi^{-1})^*h\).

There exists a smooth entering class realizing **this marked seam with both doubles positive** if and only if
\[
                  \inf_{[h]}\{F_V(h)+F_{V'}(\phi_*h)\}<2T.             \tag{1}
\]
If desired one can restrict to positive boundary conformal classes without changing this strict test. In the forward direction take the smooth entering metric and choose the ambient normal conformal jet to kill \(H\). The actual induced datum agrees, both outward forms become zero, and the positive double signs give both Robin signs. The Gauss theorem in the entering gauge supplies intrinsic positivity. In the opposite direction a strict infimum gives one common smooth \(h\) and two actual smooth fillings with energy sum strictly below \(2T\), allowing arbitrarily small errors if the infima are unattained. For the glued \(C^{1,1}\) metric, the closed quadratic form on trace-matched \(H^1\) pairs is the sum of the two \(H^1\)-coercive Neumann forms. It is coercive.

To keep the seam while smoothing, put both sides in outward/inward geodesic collars and on each replace \(h_s\) near \(s=0\) by \(h+\theta(s/\epsilon)(h_s-h)\), where \(\theta=0\) on \([0,1]\) and \(1\) on \([2,\infty)\), smooth. Since \(h_s-h=O(s^2)\), its first radial derivative is \(O(s)\); the same bounds hold after tangential differentiation on the compact seam. Hence the modifications agree exactly with the constant product next to the seam, converge in \(C^1\), and have uniformly bounded second derivatives in a layer of volume \(O(\epsilon)\). They converge in \(W^{2,p}\) for every finite \(p\). The modified manifolds and their reflected doubles are smooth, their Weyl integrals converge, and positivity survives on the closed manifold and both doubles by form convergence as in E04. The resulting common closed chiral energy equals the sum of side half-sums since \(\tau(M)=0\). Productization could change the sign of pointwise scalar curvature in the layer; its effect on the strictly coercive form tends to zero. This is a strict-budget equivalence and supplies no minimizer at equality.

This reduction eliminates all interior coupling other than a single shared conformal metric. In particular, separate unconstrained optimizations at unrelated boundary classes cannot certify the strict inequality. For \(k=0\) it covers both rational balls (including the reducible PSC boundaries), and for \(k=1\) it covers the ordered \(\chi=2,0\) pair. The untwisted sphere and circle-product controls are in E06.

## Independent test of a local obstruction

Suppose a smooth closed three-manifold has a specific metric \(h\) with \(R_h>0\). On a two-sided collar take
\[
  g=ds^2+h+s^2\big(\mathrm{Ric}_h-\tfrac12R_hh\big).
\]
This is a smooth Riemannian metric for small \(|s|\), even in \(s\) and totally geodesic at zero. In the unit-sphere-positive convention the normal curvature form at zero is \(-\tfrac12h_s''=\tfrac12R_hh-\mathrm{Ric}_h\). The tangential Ricci of \(g\) is the intrinsic Ricci plus this form, the normal Ricci is its trace \(R_h/2\), and the mixed Ricci vanishes by Codazzi. Thus \(\mathrm{Ric}_g=(R_h/2)g\) at **every boundary point**, even when \(R_h\) varies. By compactness a smaller collar has \(R_g>0,\ |Z_g|^2<R_g^2/12\). Both chiral energies on a collar of width \(\epsilon\) are \(O(\epsilon)\) for this fixed metric. This verifies all local curvature signs for the actual intrinsic quaternionic example \(R_h=2,\eta=-5/12\). It supplies neither of the global degrees of freedom in (1). The two loose outer faces of an isolated collar cannot be discarded in a closed energy calculation.

## A single projective-plane disk on a specified quaternionic marking

Let \(W\) be the normal disk bundle of the standard projective-plane orbit, with oriented total space and boundary \(Y=Q_8\backslash S^3\). Write the three axes \(i,j,k\), taking the disk circle along \(i\). The corresponding map on \(\pi_1Y=Q_8\) surjects onto \(\pi_1W=\mathbb Z/2\) with kernel \(\langle i\rangle\). This also follows from the circle-fibration exact sequence. An automorphism of the unit quaternions interchanging \(i,j\) and negating \(k\) normalizes \(Q_8\); let \(\phi\) be the resulting orientation-preserving boundary diffeomorphism. Form \(M_\phi=W\cup_\phi(-W)\). The two maps to \(\mathbb Z/2\) have distinct kernels. The images of \(i,j\) kill both generators in the van Kampen pushout, so \(M_\phi\) is simply connected. Integrally \(H_1(Y)\to H_1(W)\oplus H_1(W)\) is an isomorphism, \(H_2(Y)=H_2(W)=H_3(W)=0\), and Mayer–Vietoris gives the homology of \(S^4\). The simply connected homology-sphere criterion (Hurewicz and Whitehead) gives a smooth homotopy four-sphere. This argument needs no smooth recognition theorem.

In the right-invariant frame on the left quotient use \(h_t\) of lengths \((1,1,t)\), with long axis \(k\). The automorphism is an isometry; both collapsing axes in this gluing are *short*. For this prescribed conformal metric (1) becomes the single strict test \(F_W(h_t)<T\). If a smooth class with the given boundary conformal class exists on this split, the sum for its two representatives is \(<2T\); at least one provides a filling of this same metric below \(T\) after boundary conformal normalization. Conversely a single such filling can be used twice. At \(t=\sqrt3\) the known side estimates leave \(2T/3\le F_W(h_t)\); the entering test is whether the upper bound can be made **strictly below \(T\)**. There is no claimed upper bound in this interval. The available biaxial lower bound when the elongated axis is the collapsing axis cannot be transferred to this marking. The right-invariant collar test above gives both local signs here, and the round embedded orbit has scalar-flat induced datum and nonzero bending. The test at this one \(h_t\) could establish an example. A negative result there would leave the other boundary metrics in (1), other rational-ball splittings and the free-rank-one question. No phase of this reduction assumes existence of an exotic four-sphere.
