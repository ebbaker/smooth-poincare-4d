## After the first horizon

The rows above are the first horizon. The directions below serve the components and are listed in the order in which they are likely to matter; the lead may replace them. A statement marked † was derived while this note was written and has not been read independently: it is a first packet for a Sol reader, not an input.

- **Seams the two inequalities do not decide** (S2). † The proofs of Lemma 5.1 and Theorem 5.2 use rational homology only, so the atlas extends at once to every seam on the list of Corollary 4.3 with \(k\) the number of \(S^1\times S^2\) summands, and it says nothing about summands that are spherical space forms: for a seam that is a space form both sides are rational homology balls and both Betti numbers of the doubles vanish. The tools left for those are the eta bound of Corollary 3.3, which needs \(\eta\) of the induced structure and therefore the variation estimate of the [chirality ledger](../../01_chirality_ledger/README.md); † the theorem of Chang, Gursky and Yang applied to the doubled class, which for a rational ball \(V\) with positive double gives \(e_V\ge\tfrac12T\) unless \(\mathcal DV\) is the standard sphere; and the weaker bound with the Yamabe constant quoted at the end of Section 5.
- **A criterion for positive doubles** (S3). † If a side has a representative with \(R>0\) for which the seam is totally geodesic, its doubled metric is \(C^{1,1}\) with \(R>0\) off the seam and the doubled class is positive. The general statement is the sign of the first eigenvalue of the conformal Laplacian with the boundary condition that the reflection induces, in which the two sides see the mean curvature with opposite signs. † For a class invariant under a conformal reflection both doubles are the class itself, and nothing has to be verified.
- **The constant in statement (3)** (S5). † The quadratic form in statement (3) is \(\int(8\lvert\nabla u\rvert^2+(P+\tfrac23H^2)u^2)\), and the term \(H^2u^2\) is the one that the Michael–Simon inequality requires. Applied to \(u^4\) through an isometric embedding of the gauge in a Euclidean space, where the mean curvature changes by a bounded term, and with \(\inf P>0\) to absorb the term in \(u^2\), it should bound the constant below by a number that depends only on the entering gauge, uniformly over closed hypersurfaces. The version of Hoffman and Spruck for a Riemannian ambient space restricts the volume of the support and does not give this directly. If the bound holds, every closed hypersurface whose \(\lVert B^\circ\rVert_{L^3}\) is below that number has positive Yamabe type, Corollary 4.4 holds for such a cut, and in the setting of that corollary on a nonstandard type every entering class has a positive lower bound for the conformally invariant quantity \(\inf\lVert B^\circ\rVert_{L^3}\), taken over hypersurfaces diffeomorphic to the winding-one cut. The sources are not in the shared bibliography and must be read first.
- **The capping cost** (S4). The plan names two sources, Biquard's filling theory near the round datum and an explicit interpolation. The second-order expansion of SEAM-E02 decides whether the cost is comparable to a conformally invariant norm of the datum; Proposition 8.1 then needs the cost to be smaller than the energy that the cap frees.
- **Invariant classes.** † If an entering class is invariant under a reflection with fixed set \(\Sigma\), the two sides have the same energy \(e/2<T\), so by Proposition 8.1(2) the manifold is standard as soon as \(\Sigma\) is a free cut and the capped classes are positive. What remains is to decide the fixed sets that are not round three-spheres, with the atlas, the eta bound and the capping cost. This is the alternative that the [parent overview](../../research-overview/OVERVIEW.md#the-proof-target) names for the first step of its primary route.
- **Certificates.** [Slide filters](../../04_slide_filters/README.md) returns, for the terminal diagrams of 05, the cuts that a representation certifies as not dominated. Each is excluded as an umbilic hypersurface of an entering class by Theorem 4.1, and with the constant above as a nearly umbilic one.
