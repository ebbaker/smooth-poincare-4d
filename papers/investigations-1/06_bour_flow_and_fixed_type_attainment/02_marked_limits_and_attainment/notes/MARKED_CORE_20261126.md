## Input and conclusion

Fix a closed connected oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere \(M\) with smooth Riemannian metrics \(g_i\). Write \(E_\pm(g;U)=\int_U |W^\pm_g|_{\mathrm{HS}}^2\,dV_g\), \(D(g)=\int|\operatorname{Ric}_{0,g}|^2\,dV_g\) and \(S(g)=\int R_g^2\,dV_g\). Weyl uses the curvature-operator Hilbert–Schmidt norm, full Riemann curvature uses the four-index norm, and Ricci uses the ordinary two-tensor norm. Assume
\[
 Y([g_i])\ge y_0>0,\qquad E_\pm(g_i)=e_i\le 8\pi^2-\delta,\qquad
 \delta>0,\qquad \limsup_i D(g_i)\le d_*<32\pi^2 .
\]
Suppose \(\lambda_i^{-2}g_i\) converges smoothly in the pointed sense to a complete connected noncompact nonflat smooth Riemannian \((X,h,p)\) with \(R_h=B_h=0\). The convergence hypothesis means that for each fixed compact domain in \(X\) and all sufficiently large \(i\), an orientation-preserving embedding of a neighborhood of the whole domain into \(M\) gives smooth pullback convergence there. The class inequality and Ricci ceiling are measured on the original sources. [Local normalizations](../../../03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/manuscript/marked_necks_and_chiral_energy.tex#L71)

Then \(X\) has one asymptotically Euclidean end. Its smooth oriented one-point conformal compactification \((N,\bar h)\) is Bach-flat, has \(Y([\bar h])\ge y_0\), and satisfies
\[
 4\pi^2<e_X=E_+(\bar h)=E_-(\bar h)
 \le\min\big\{\liminf_i e_i,\,d_*/4\big\}.
\]
There is an oriented diffeomorphism \(M\to N\) agreeing with the inverse comparison on a fixed normal truncation and its seam germ. Pullback gives a positive Bach-flat class on \(M\), so \(I^+(M)\le e_X\).

## E01 · Compact tests and differentiated ALE structure

Push compactly supported tests forward on an embedded compact neighborhood and extend by zero. The Yamabe quotient is invariant under constant rescaling. Smooth convergence passes the form and denominator to \(h\), giving
\[
 Y_c(X,h)\ge y_0,\qquad 6\|du\|_{2,h}^2\ge y_0\|u\|_{4,h}^2\quad(u\in C_c^\infty(X)).
\]
The scalar curvature vanishes on the limit. The homogeneous Sobolev constant in Tian–Viaclovsky's notation is therefore at most \(\sqrt{6/y_0}\), also for compactly supported Lipschitz tests by density. The two chiral densities and the trace-free Ricci energy are invariant under constant rescaling in dimension four. Smooth convergence on compact domains and exhaustion give \(e_\pm=E_\pm(h)\le\liminf_i e_i\) and
\[
 d=\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_h|^2\,dV_h\le\liminf_i D(g_i)\le d_*.
\]
Here \(d\) is the physical Ricci energy because \(R_h=0\). The four-index decomposition gives \(\int_X|\mathrm{Rm}_h|^2=4(e_++e_-)+2d<\infty\). For the source the same decomposition and closed Gauss–Bonnet give \(S(g_i)/24=16\pi^2-2e_i+D(g_i)/2\) and hence \(\int_M|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_i}|^2=8e_i+2D(g_i)+S(g_i)/6=64\pi^2+4D(g_i)\). [Conformal Laplacian](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/lee-parker.yamabe.pdf)

Tian–Viaclovsky 2005 Theorem 1.1 through (1.7) applies with completeness, noncompactness, \(R=B=0\), finite full \(L^2\) curvature and the homogeneous Sobolev inequality. Its curvature decay \(\sup_{S(r)}|\nabla^j\mathrm{Rm}|=o(r^{-2-j})\) for every \(j\) at distance \(r=d_h(p,\cdot)\) precedes the extra \(b_1(X)\) hypothesis in its ALE conclusion. Apply Tian–Viaclovsky 2008 Theorem 1.1 to \(j=0\) decay and the same Sobolev inequality. Its remaining input in dimension four is \(\int_X|\operatorname{Ric}_{-}|^2<\infty\), which follows from the bound on \(d\). It gives an upper Euclidean volume-growth bound and finitely many ALE ends of order zero. The lower volume bound used in its proof follows from the Sobolev inequality by TV 2005 Lemma 6.1. [TV 2005 v3, Theorem 1.1 and Lemma 6.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0310302v3#page=2) · [TV 2008, Theorem 1.1 and proof](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43177#page=2)

The finite ALE end topology from TV 2008 exhibits \(X\) as a smooth compact core with finitely many product tails, so \(b_1(X)\) is finite. The full conclusion of TV 2005 Theorem 1.1 now supplies order zero in the differentiated sense of (1.3)–(1.4): smooth coordinates with weighted little-oh decay through all derivative orders. This meets Ache–Viaclovsky Definition 1.1. Their Theorem 1.2 applies with \(n=4,k=1\), using the obstruction tensor \(\mathcal O\), which they denote \(\Omega^{(k)}\) for \(k=n/2-1\). In dimension four \(\mathcal O\) is a nonzero multiple of the Bach tensor. The upgrade gives \(D^j(h_{ab}-\delta_{ab})=O(\varrho^{-2-j})\) in exterior coordinates \(x\), \(\varrho=|x|\), through \(j=5\). The exterior radius is comparable with base-point distance. Choose oriented charts, conjugating a finite group by a reflection if needed. The primary theorems give charts on \(X\). The whole-truncation comparison into \(M\) comes from the smooth pointed input. ## E02 · Whole-core embeddings and the end count

Let \(k\ge1\) be the number of ends, with round links \(L_b=S^3/\Gamma_b\). At a large fixed \(R\) let \(K_R\) be the connected compact truncation at \(\varrho=R\) in all ends. Use one comparison \(\Phi_i\) on a larger whole truncation containing \(K_R\) and every buffer \(|t|<\ell+2\), \(t=\log(\varrho/R)\), with \(\ell\ge1\) fixed. On the disjoint collars put
\[
 c_{b,i}=(\lambda_i\varrho)^{-2}\Phi_i^*g_i,\qquad
 c_b^0=dt^2+\gamma_{L_b},\qquad
 \eta_b=\|c_{b,i}-c_b^0\|_{C^2}\le C_\ell R^{-2}+o_i(1;R,\ell).
\]
The estimate follows by writing \(dx^a/\varrho=\omega^a dt+d\omega^a\) and using the weighted ALE derivatives. Extend the positive conformal factor over \(M\) by cutting off its logarithm outside the chosen collar neighborhoods. On \(|t|<1\) interpolate by \(c_b^0+\theta(t)(c_{b,i}-c_b^0)\) with a fixed smooth \(0\le\theta\le1\) equal to zero near \(0\) and one near the ends of the splice. The metric \(\widetilde g_i\) on \(M\) is exactly the product near each slice. The product is conformally flat and the splice has uniformly bounded volume and \(C^2\) error \(O(\eta_b)\). Thus each splice adds at most \(C\eta_b^2\) to each chiral budget. Outside the splices the chiral densities are unchanged by conformal invariance. By choosing \(R\) and then \(i\), obtain
\[
 E_\pm(\widetilde g_i)\le e_i+C\sum_b\eta_b^2<9\pi^2 .
\]
No change of the underlying closed manifold occurs in this product replacement. [Marked-neck buffering and quotient obstruction](../../../03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/manuscript/marked_necks_and_chiral_energy.tex#L174)

Here the quotient obstruction can be checked on each link separately with the same \(\widetilde g_i\). A connected two-sided link separates since a loop of intersection number one would otherwise exist. Both sides have \(H_2=0\) by Mayer–Vietoris, using \(H_2(L_b;\mathbb Z)=H_2(M;\mathbb Z)=0\). Hillman's Theorem 5.2(1) for locally flat finite-group links in integral homology four-spheres leaves \(1,Q_8,I^*\). Smoothness excludes \(S^3/I^*\) because either side is spin with zero signature, and the unique spin structure on the Poincaré sphere permits gluing an oriented \(E_8\) plumbing of signature \(8\) or \(-8\). The resulting smooth closed spin manifold would violate Rokhlin's theorem. [Hillman, Theorem 5.2 and Corollary 4.4.2](https://www.maths.usyd.edu.au/u/jonh/embkDec24.pdf#page=64) · [Kirby–Scharlemann, (1) and (4)](https://web.math.ucsb.edu/~mgscharl/papers/Poincare.pdf) · [Rokhlin, §§1.1–1.2](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/rohlin2.pdf)

For \(Q_8\), the free central involution is \(-\mathrm{Id}\). The anticommuting orthogonal complex structures \(I,J\) from its generators give the basis \(v,Iv,Jv,IJv\), placing the action in its quaternionic form up to orientation. The three order-four cyclic subgroups have equal eta by conjugacy of the quaternion axes, and each has odd signature eta \(-1/2\) in the common quaternion orientation by the cotangent formula for two rotations by \(\pi/2\). The antipodal central element contributes zero. The cyclic average on each order-four subgroup weights its terms by \(1/4\). Thus in the \(Q_8\) average the three cyclic sums give \(8\eta_{\rm sig}=3\cdot4(-1/2)=-6\). Orientation reversal changes the sign. APS I Theorem (4.14) with the exact product hypothesis and \(\tau(P)=0\) on a side \(P\) gives
\[
 E_+(\widetilde g_i;P)-E_-(\widetilde g_i;P)=12\pi^2\eta_{\rm sig}(\partial P)
\]
in the unhalved even-form convention. The two outward boundary orientations are opposite, so one side requires \(E_+\ge9\pi^2\). This contradicts the bound on the closed metric, proving \(\Gamma_b=1\) for every end. [APS I, Theorem (4.14)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps001.pdf#page=24) · [APS II, Proposition (2.12)](https://webhomes.maths.ed.ac.uk/~v1ranick/papers/aps002.pdf#page=8)

For the embedded whole core, now with spherical boundaries, Mayer–Vietoris gives an injection \(H_0(\partial K_R)\to H_0(K_R)\oplus H_0(M\setminus\operatorname{int}\Phi_iK_R)\). Connectedness of \(K_R\) makes the columns for two boundary spheres on the same exterior identical, so each exterior has exactly one boundary. The resulting star decomposition and van Kampen give \(\pi_1(K_R)=0\) and simply connected exteriors. Mayer–Vietoris in degree two gives \(H_2(K_R;\mathbb Z)=0\) and the corresponding result on the exteriors. The pair sequence has \(H_4(K_R,\partial K_R)\to H_3(\partial K_R)\) sending the relative fundamental class to the sum of its oriented boundaries, and \(H_3(K_R,\partial K_R)=H^1(K_R)=0\). Hence \(H_3(K_R)=\mathbb Z^{k-1}\), \(\chi(X)=\chi(K_R)=2-k\) and \(\tau(K_R)=0\). The topology persists for larger truncations by the end products.

Apply Gauss–Bonnet to the original metric \(h\) on larger and larger truncations. Each outgoing Euler boundary contribution to \(8\pi^2\chi\) tends to \(+8\pi^2\), since the rescaled annulus converges in \(C^2\) to the flat Euclidean model and that is the boundary contribution of its unit ball. For signature use product splices taking place inside the boundary in the cylindrical representative. Their new chiral cost is \(O(R^{-4})\) and the \(h\)-energy of removed annuli tends to zero. The round \(S^3\) eta is zero. Exhaustion gives
\[
 e_++e_- -d/2=8\pi^2(2-2k),\qquad e_+-e_-=0,\qquad
 d=2(e_++e_-)+32\pi^2(k-1).
\]
Thus \(d_*<32\pi^2\) forces \(k=1\), and \(d=4e_+=4e_-\). In particular the first-flow ceiling \(D(g_i)\le4I+4\alpha\) suffices for the strict Ricci comparison when \(\alpha<8\pi^2-I\), \(I=I^+(M)\). The bound \(e_i\le e_0/(1-\alpha)\) gives the strict Weyl aperture for \(\alpha<1-e_0/(8\pi^2)\). These checks also require the positive Yamabe bound and the given smooth comparison. ## E03 · Smooth point, positive exterior cap and smooth type

For \(0<\beta<1\), construct a positive factor \(a\) harmonic far out with \(D^j(a-\varrho^{-2})=O(\varrho^{-2-\beta-j})\) through order five. With \(\Delta=\operatorname{div}\nabla\),
\[
 \Delta_h\varrho^{-2}=O(\varrho^{-6}),\qquad
 \Delta_h\varrho^{-2-\beta}=\beta(2+\beta)\varrho^{-4-\beta}
                                  +O(\varrho^{-6-\beta}).
\]
At a sufficiently large inner radius, \(2(\varrho^{-2}-\varrho^{-2-\beta})\) is a positive superharmonic upper barrier and \((\varrho^{-2}+\varrho^{-2-\beta})/2\) a subharmonic lower barrier for Dirichlet data \(\varrho^{-2}\) on the two boundary spheres of expanding annuli. Local estimates give a harmonic limit with two-sided bounds of order \(\varrho^{-2}\). On dilated annuli the interior Schauder estimates give \(D^j a=O(\varrho^{-2-j})\). Cut off the resulting factor to a smooth function on \(\mathbb R^4\) agreeing with it far out. Its Euclidean Laplacian \(f\) is \(O(\varrho^{-6})\) on the tail, by the order-two metric derivatives. The Newtonian potential of \(f\) differs from the cutoff factor by a decaying entire harmonic function, hence by zero. Its leading term \(A\varrho^{-2}\) has error \(O(\varrho^{-2-\beta})\). For \(|z|\le|x|/2\) the kernel difference is \(O(|x|^{-2-\beta}|z|^\beta)\) and \(f\) has finite absolute \(\beta\)-moment. On \(|z|>|x|/2\), the part \(|x-z|\le|x|/4\) is \(O(|x|^{-4})\) by the pointwise tail bound, and the rest is \(O(|x|^{-2-\beta})\) by the tail moment. The lower barrier implies \(A>0\). Scale by \(A^{-1}\) and use dilated Schauder estimates for the equation \(\Delta_h(a-\varrho^{-2})=-\Delta_h\varrho^{-2}\) for the differentiated error. Extend \(a\) positively over the core. [Marked-neck proof of the harmonic factor](../../../03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/manuscript/marked_necks_and_chiral_energy.tex#L640)

In inverted coordinates \(z=x/|x|^2\), the metric \(\bar h=a^2h\) differs from Euclidean by \(O(|z|^\beta)\), with \(j\)-th derivatives \(O(|z|^{\beta-j})\) for \(j\le5\). It is scalar-flat near the puncture by the conformal Laplacian formula and harmonicity, and Bach-flat by conformal covariance. Ache–Viaclovsky Theorem 1.8 thus applies to the punctured constant-scalar obstruction-flat metric, with trivial local group. Definition 1.6 and Remark 7.2 require the weighted smallness through \(2(k+1)+1=5\) for obstruction order \(k=1\). Use the resulting smooth extending chart to define the point of \(N\), with orientation inherited from \(X\). [Ache–Viaclovsky v2, Definition 1.6, Theorem 1.8 and Remark 7.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1249v2#page=5)

Topologically \(N\) fills the whole spherical core by a ball. The core calculation gives \(\pi_1(N)=0=H_2(N;\mathbb Z)\), and duality and Hurewicz–Whitehead give a homotopy sphere. The point has zero \(H^1\) capacity, since cutting a fixed smooth test off on a ball of radius \(s\) has Dirichlet error tending to zero with cutoff energy \(O(s^2)\). Conformal transport and density give \(Y([\bar h])=Y_c(X,h)\ge y_0\). Conformal invariance and the added point's zero measure give \(E_\pm(\bar h)=e_\pm\). These energies are positive since zero would imply \(d=0\) and then \(W=\operatorname{Ric}=R=0\), contradicting nonflatness. On this homotopy sphere the four-index Weyl integral is \(8e_+\). At \(e_+<4\pi^2\), Chang–Gursky–Yang Theorem A recognizes the sphere. At equality their Theorem B excludes the alternatives to the standard sphere by \(H_2=0\) and simple connectivity. On the standard type, Chang–Chen Theorem 2.1 applies with positivity, Bach-flatness and the inclusive four-index limit \(32\pi^2\), forcing zero Weyl. Hence \(e_X>4\pi^2\). Let \(B_s\) be the radius-\(s\) normal ball of the extended metric at the added point, and \(K_s=N\setminus\operatorname{int}B_s\). Write \(\rho=s e^{-t}\) on its collar and use a fixed angular reflection for the orientation of \(t\). The precise collar representative is

 \eta_i=\|c_i-(dt^2+\gamma_{S^3})\|_{C^2}\le C_\ell s^2+o_i(1;s,\ell).
\]
Here \(D^j(\bar h-\delta)=O(\rho^{2-j})\) for \(j\le2\) in normal coordinates, and every derivative conversion to cylindrical coordinates has the matching factor of \(\rho\). The normal chart is connected to the original comparison by the actual smooth transition on \(X\).
