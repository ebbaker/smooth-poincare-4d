## First marking and finiteness of the entire core

The finite-\(b_1\) hypothesis occurs after the decay assertion. The differentiated order-zero charts of the 2005 theorem are therefore available and Ache–Viaclovsky's \(n=4,k=1\) theorem upgrades them to order two. ## Smooth point and the second marking

Use the Laplacian \(\operatorname{div}\nabla\) to construct a harmonic \(a\) in the Euclidean end. For sufficiently large fixed inner radius, solve on expanding annuli with boundary values \(r^{-2}\) at both radii. Since \(\Delta_h r^{-2}=O(r^{-6})\) and \(\Delta_h r^{-2-\beta}=\beta(2+\beta)r^{-4-\beta}+O(r^{-6-\beta})\), small multiples of \(r^{-2}(1+r^{-\beta})\) and large multiples of \(r^{-2}(1-r^{-\beta})\) give lower and upper comparison. Exhaustion and dilated interior estimates give \(D^j a=O(r^{-2-j})\). Cut off in the compact part of the Cartesian chart. The resulting Euclidean Laplacian \(f\) is \(O(r^{-6})\) on the tail. The Newton potential gives the cut-off function because the difference is entire harmonic and tends to zero. For \(|z|\le |x|/2\), the difference of the kernel from its leading term is bounded by \(C|x|^{-2-\beta}|z|^\beta\). For \(|z|>|x|/2\), the contribution near \(x\) is \(O(|x|^{-4})\) by the local integrable kernel and the tail bound. The rest is controlled by the finite absolute \(\beta\)-moment. The lower barrier forces a positive leading coefficient. Normalize it to one and apply dilated estimates to \(a-r^{-2}\) to obtain the differentiated \(O(r^{-2-\beta})\) remainder through order five.

Extend \(a\) positively over the core. The metric \(q=a^2h\) is Bach-flat on \(X\) and scalar-flat near the puncture by \(a\)'s harmonic equation. Inverting the end radius gives Euclidean error \(O(\rho^\beta)\) with its weighted derivatives through five, the required finite number for \(k=1\). Take the extending chart supplied by the removal theorem, with the orientation inherited from \(X\). A radial inversion reverses orientation and admitting a fixed angular reflection is enough when using product coordinates. The point has trivial group, so it gives a smooth manifold \(N\) with \(X=N\setminus\{p\}\), and smoothness carries Bach-flatness through the point. This topological compactification is a homotopy sphere by the core computation. Cutting smooth tests off at a ball of radius \(s\) has Dirichlet cost \(O(s^2)\). By zero capacity and conformal covariance, \(Y([q])=Y_c(X,h)\ge y\). Conformal invariance gives \(E_\pm(q)=E_\pm(h)\) separately. The signature of \(N\) makes them equal, so the open Euler formula now gives \(D_X=4e_X\). ## Two uses of the comparison and the equality

For each late fixed original flow that produces a pointed output, \(I\le e_X\le\liminf_i e(g_i)\) comes from admissibility on the marked \(M\) and exhaustion on the physical slices. At each such fixed index \(j\), the ordered exterior loss, \(i\to\infty\) then \(s\downarrow0\), is bounded per chirality by \(e_{j,0}/(1-\alpha_j)-I\). This upper bound tends to zero as \(j\to\infty\). For a fixed \(j\) the loss can still be positive under the displayed bounds. The supplement follows from preserving the initial Yamabe scalar deficit in the Ricci bound:
\[
 4(e_X-I)+\frac{Y_{\mathrm{rd}}^2-Y_0^2}{12}
 \ \le\ \frac{4(e_0-I)}{\alpha}.
\]
In fact \(D_0=4e_0+(Y_0^2-Y_{\mathrm{rd}}^2)/12\), and \(D_X=4e_X\le D_0+4(1-\alpha)(e_0-I)/\alpha\). Thus \(Y_{\mathrm{rd}}^2-Y_0^2\le48\alpha\) for the initial classes of these actual fixed-\(\alpha\) noncompact outcomes. The quantifiers do not control the starts of bounded original flows or arbitrary minimizing sequences. [Frozen scalar-deficit supplement](../research-progress/handoffs/READING_ADDENDUM_20261126.md)

Use a smooth volume-one physical critical \(q_j\) on \(M\) when the fixed-\(\alpha_j\) output is closed, and use a volume-one Yamabe representative on the identified \(M\) of the compactified pointed output otherwise. Its Ricci bound in the latter gauge follows anew from Gauss–Bonnet and \(Y^2\le Y_{\rm rd}^2\), giving \(D(q_j)\le4e(q_j)\). In both branches \(Y\ge y\), \(e(q_j)\to I\) and there is a strict uniform \(D<32\pi^2\). The normalized representatives have constant scalar \(R_j\in[y,C]\). For \(p_j=\alpha_j\) or \(0\), Bour Proposition 4.4 yields principal term \(\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla E\) with the algebraic terms displayed in the frozen equation. For bounded normalized curvature, the Yamabe cutoff iteration gives uniform ball volume bounds at radius at most one, hence injectivity. In harmonic charts the Ricci identity with bounded Ricci gives \(W^{2,p}\) for the metric for \(p>4\), and the connection-derivative coefficient times the bounded \(E\) is then \(L^p\). The rough Laplacian equation gives \(W^{2,p}\) for \(E\) on smaller balls, followed by alternating interior estimates for \(E\) and the metric.
