## The actual stretch and test construction in smooth geometry

Fix one smooth positive Riemannian source on a closed oriented homotopy four-sphere, one marked separating face, and the endpoint of one joined path with chiral peak $H<2T$, $T=4\pi^2$. Use the exact unit round product on a strip about the given face. A supported conformal exposure of the product, if needed, preserves both four-dimensional chiral curvature measures. Keep the original angular marking and the open core comparison on each side fixed. 1. On a smaller signed product strip $[-b,b]\times S^3$, take a smooth nonnegative bump $B$ supported in $(-b,b)$ with $\int B=1$. At length $L\ge0$ put $s_L(t)=-b+\int_{-b}^t(1+LB(\tau))d\tau$. Its derivative is positive, the inverse is smooth, and it identifies the strip with the product of length $2b+L$. Replace $dt^2$ by $ds_L^2$ and keep the full exterior fixed. The two end collars remain product with the same derivatives. On the retained product pieces use arclength measured from the left or right outer face respectively. In each case the original half-product occupies its original length $b$ inside the longer product and further product length is available beyond its seam. A protected set outside the strip sees the identity map and the metric before stretching. Where a previous interpolation already changed the endpoint metric, its entire change belongs to the endpoint ledger. The product is locally conformal to Euclidean space under $r=e^{-s}$, so both signed energies of the stretched metrics are those of the endpoint. The strict joined ceiling gives the common positive Yamabe floor $\sqrt{48(2T-H)}$ through the scalar-flat wall on the closed source.
2. On each half attach an outward marked copy of $[0,\infty)\times S^3$ with arclength $s$. Write $h$ for the resulting metric. Take a smooth positive factor $\rho(s)$ equal to one near the seam and equal to $e^{-s}$ for all sufficiently large $s$. The metric $q=\rho^2h$ compactifies in the same angular marking with radial coordinate $r=e^{-s}$, inverse $s=-\log r$ on the punctured end, and the Euclidean metric $dr^2+r^2\gamma$ at the center. The marked smooth side has an open overlap described by outward normal arclength and the original angular map, with the normal pointing oppositely on the two original halves. The cap is conformally flat on all of its interior, including the transition in $\rho$ and the center. Thus its two signed energies are the full endpoint masses in the original orientations. This construction preserves the metric on the retained endpoint core. It preserves the source metric wherever the earlier deformation was disjoint from that core.
3. For a smooth cap log-factor $u$, put $w=e^u$. Fix $0<a$ small enough that the ball of radius $2a$ is in the exactly Euclidean cap neighborhood. Let $\eta_a(x)=\eta(|x|/a)$ with $0\le\eta\le1$, $\eta=0$ on $[0,1]$ and $\eta=1$ on $[2,\infty)$, extended by one off the Euclidean chart. It is smooth at the center. The cap factor $w_a=\eta_a w$ satisfies
   $$
   \left|Q_q(w_a)-Q_q(w)\right|\le C_{q,w,\eta}a^2,\qquad
   \left|\int w_a^4\,dV_q-\int w^4\,dV_q\right|\le C_{q,w}a^4,
   $$
   where $Q_q(f)=\int(6|df|_q^2+R_qf^2)dV_q$. The terms with no derivative on $\eta_a$ are bounded on the ball of volume $O(a^4)$. The square of $w\,d\eta_a$ integrates to $O(a^2)$ because $|d\eta_a|\le C/a$. The cross term has size $O(a^3)$ by the same pointwise bound and the bounded derivatives of $w$. The argument uses each fixed test separately.
4. The nonnegative function $\rho w_a$ on $(P\cup[0,\infty)\times S^3,h)$ has compact support and vanishes on an open tail. Four-dimensional conformal covariance gives $Q_h(\rho w_a)=Q_q(w_a)$ and $\int (\rho w_a)^4dV_h=\int w_a^4dV_q$. Take $L$ longer than the support's outward extent, with a positive gap to the opposite outer face, and carry $\rho w_a$ to the stretched source by the product arclength isometry. Extend by zero. This yields a smooth nonnegative function $f$ with the two same integrals on the actual joined metric $h_L$. The two sides can use different lengths and the choice is repeated for every cap test.
5. At this chosen $L$, take $v=\log(f+\delta)$ for small $\delta>0$. The positive-factor numerator and volume of $e^{2v}h_L$ tend to $Q_{h_L}(f)$ and $\int f^4 dV_{h_L}$ as $\delta$ tends to zero at this fixed metric. No uniform estimate in $L$ is needed. Taking $a$ to zero, then selecting the finite length and the regularization tolerance for each $a$, gives genuine joined metrics for every cap log-test. The two transplanted quantities converge separately. The pre-existing Yamabe floor transfer now gives the same strict positive floor for both capped metrics.

The orientation-compatible angular reparametrization of a marked cap is a diffeomorphism of $S^3$. Its component contains an orthogonal map, which can be made the map near the ball center by a collar isotopy. The actual recognition of the opposite cap uses relative disk-neighborhood extension in the standard sphere to match the specified cap ball, followed by the signed open-patch comparison. These are the explicit topology inputs in `MarkedSideReplacement`. The capped homotopy-sphere conclusion and production of these marked maps from a native cut are separate from the analytic transplant. ## Positive scalar curvature and the round control

[PositiveScalarYamabeFour.lean](../../../../SmoothPoincare/Variational/PositiveScalarYamabeFour.lean) applies the pinned Green identity to the conformal scalar numerator and the intrinsic Sobolev inequality at exponent four to its denominator. With scalar curvature at least $\rho>0$, Green gives numerator at least $\min\{\rho,6\}(\|f\|_2^2+\|df\|_2^2)$. The squared $L^4$ norm, equal to the square root of the conformal volume for $f=e^u$, is bounded above by $2C^2(\|f\|_2^2+\|df\|_2^2)$ for a finite metric-dependent Sobolev constant $C$. The ensuing bound $\min\{\rho,6\}/(2C^2+1)>0$ holds uniformly over the smooth log-conformal tests in the infimum. Compactness also supplies $\rho$ from pointwise scalar positivity.

`standardRoundFour_yamabe_pos` uses sectional curvature one to compute scalar curvature $4(4-1)=12$ on the actual unit round model. Its Yamabe sign can be transported to the particular conformal metrics in the round controlled example. The proof does not require normalization of the round Yamabe constant to its sharp value. The open cap problems for general positive subcritical sources still require the joined metric and its native marked transplants.
