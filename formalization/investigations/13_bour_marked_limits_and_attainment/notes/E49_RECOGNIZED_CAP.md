Fix the compact smooth capped source exterior $P$, the connected smooth double $D$ of the removable normal ball, and oriented recognition maps $A:P\to D$ and $K:D\to S^4$. Write $(s,t)$ for the parametrizations from neighborhoods of the closed Euclidean $R$-ball into $(P,D)$. Take $0<r<R$ at the actual seam. The prescribed partial diffeomorphism $\Psi$ is defined on the whole buffered source disk and satisfies $\Psi(s(v))=t(v)$. A single orientation of the model tangent space at $0$ pushes forward to the chosen physical orientations at both cap centers. Either common sign is allowed. The proof works for every such $(r,R)$.

1. The closed buffered image in each compact carrier omits a point. Otherwise the inverse chart would map the entire compact manifold onto a nonempty compact open subset of $\mathbb R^4$. Such a subset would be closed and hence all of connected $\mathbb R^4$, which is noncompact.
2. Work first on the recognized sphere. Align the images of one source exterior point and one target exterior point by a point motion supported away from a third point. The complement of the third point is connected by stereography. The motion fixes a neighborhood of that point, hence its differential there is the identity, which determines its orientation globally. The aligned exterior point $p$ is a stereographic pole for both entire buffered images. A second motion supported away from $p$ aligns the cap centers. Pull the chart and the preliminary diffeomorphism back to $D$ through $K$. Its coordinate target is all of $\mathbb R^4$.
3. At their common center, the differentials of the two parametrizations, one transported through the oriented preliminary map, send the same model orientation to the same tangent orientation. Postcomposition by the common chart preserves their relative sign. Thus the product of their two coordinate determinants is strictly positive, including when both individual signs are negative. The E48 supported isotopy now matches the original closed $R$-disks point by point. This construction never reduces the prescribed radius.
4. The resulting oriented diffeomorphism $F:P\to D$ agrees with $\Psi$ on $s(B_R)$, and $F^{-1}$ agrees with $\Psi^{-1}$ on $t(B_R)$. It identifies the two complements of the open $r$-disks. For the normal-ball double the target complement is the actual deleted normal ball. Its restriction therefore supplies the exterior piece needed for the two-way seam gluing, provided the prescribed outer strip is the inverse of the **same** whole-core comparison.
