## One compact truncation and one orientation

Let \(X\) be the connected complete oriented pointed limit of the original rescaled metrics \(a_i g_i\). Apply the ALE result to this limit once its curvature, homogeneous Sobolev and finite-topology premises have been established. The estimates below use finitely many end maps \(\psi_b:(r_*,\infty)\times S^3/\Gamma_b\to X\) with differentiated ALE control through order two in the finite covers. Each \(\Gamma_b\subset SO(4)\) acts freely on \(S^3\). Increasing radius faces away from the core on every end. [Tian–Viaclovsky, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0310302v3#page=2) · [Tian–Viaclovsky, volume growth, Theorem 1.1](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43177#page=2) · [Ache–Viaclovsky v2, Definition 1.1 and Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1249v2#page=3)

Fix a logarithmic buffer \(\ell>0\), and take \(r\) large enough that the \(k\) annuli \(r e^{-\ell}<s<r e^\ell\) lie in these disjoint end charts. Truncate each end at \(s=r\) to obtain the connected compact \(K_r\). A larger compact \(K_{r e^{2\ell}}\) contains \(K_r\) and the closures of all buffers in its interior. The one comparison on that larger compact gives the markings of the core, the inner and outer buffers, and all boundary links simultaneously at each sufficiently late source index. Take its orientation at the base point and use connectedness for the rest of the domain. ## Normalized jets and induced measure

On the finite cover of each end let \(D_r(y)=ry\) on the fixed annulus \(e^{-\ell}<|y|<e^\ell\), and write \(h_{b,r}=r^{-2}D_r^*\psi_b^*h\). For a chart of positive order \(\tau\), the coefficients of \(h_{b,r}-\delta\) and all derivatives through order two have uniform size \(O_\ell(r^{-\tau})\). In a differentiated order-zero chart the corresponding error is \(o_\ell(1)\). The inverse is uniformly bounded when that error is small. The Christoffel formula bounds \(\nabla^{h_{b,r}}-\nabla^\delta\) by the first-jet error. The curvature formula uses the second-jet error and the quadratic Christoffel term. Conversion to the physical ordered norm uses the bounded inverse metric in all four slots. The Euclidean metric comparison, connection difference, and ordered curvature norm thus share a bound \(\varepsilon_r\to0\). The same three estimates on the composed source marking, at fixed \(r\), use a bound of the form \(\varepsilon_r+o_i(1;r,\ell)\).

On the unit level use \(u(y)=|y|^2/2\). It satisfies \(|du|_\delta=1\) on the level and \(\nabla_\delta^2u=\delta\). For \(k=h_{b,r}\), the outward unit normal is \(\nu=\nabla^k u/|du|_k\). The differential on \(\nu\) equals the positive number \(|du|_k\), and its kernel is the tangent plane. The outward shape there is \(\nabla_k^2 u/|du|_k\). The derivative of the normalizing factor multiplies \(\nabla^k u\), which pairs to zero with tangent vectors. The level of \(u\) and the level of \(|y|\) give the same shape since \(u\) is an increasing function of \(|y|\) near this level.

If the form error and the connection difference have common bound \(\varepsilon\le 1/2\) in the Euclidean reference norm, then
\[
 (1+\varepsilon)^{-1}\le |du|_k^2\le (1-\varepsilon)^{-1},\qquad
 \big||du|_k-1\big|\le 2\varepsilon,\qquad |du|_k\ge \tfrac12 .
\]
The difference of the two actual Hessians is the contraction of \(du\) with minus the connection difference. Each vector of a \(k\)-orthonormal frame has Euclidean length at most \(2\). The Hessian error on a tangent pair and the error incurred by replacing the Euclidean inner product with the physical one are therefore at most \(4\varepsilon\) each. After division by \(|du|_k\), the shape entries differ from the identity by at most \(20\varepsilon\). The entries of the ambient tangential-wedge matrix are bounded by the full **ordered** curvature norm, by comparison of one square to the sum of squares of all entries in a physical orthonormal frame.

In the fixed immersion of the unit link into the normalized annulus, the same quadratic-form comparison holds on the actual tangent images. The induced three-dimensional metrics \(\gamma_0,\gamma_r\) supply the relative density \(J=\sqrt{\det(\gamma_0^{-1}\gamma_r)}\), equal to the Radon–Nikodym factor of their Riemannian measures. The three-dimensional volume comparison bounds \(|J-1|\le3\varepsilon\). This conclusion uses the two induced metrics and the differential of the same immersion. It is invariant under a deck change. The round quotient cover \(q_b:S^3\to S^3/\Gamma_b\) is locally isometric with \(d_b=|\Gamma_b|\) sheets. On each evenly covered measurable set, pushforward sphere area is \(d_b\) times quotient area. Using a finite evenly covered open cover of the compact link and a disjoint measurable partition subordinate to that cover extends the identity to every Borel set. Thus the reference area is \(2\pi^2/d_b\).

## Finite transgression and exhaustion

In the oriented orthonormal frame \((\nu,e_1,e_2,e_3)\) use \(S_{ij}=\langle\nabla_{e_i}\nu,e_j\rangle\). The ambient tangential curvature matrix \(C\) uses cyclic wedges \((e_2\wedge e_3,e_3\wedge e_1,e_1\wedge e_2)\) and has positive diagonal for positive sectional curvature. On the boundary let the split connection have zero mixed component and intrinsic tangential connection. The mixed connection difference relative to this connection is \(\Theta_{0a}=-\sum_b S_{ab}\theta^b\). Along the interpolation to the ambient connection the tangential curvature equals the ambient one plus the quadratic \(S\) terms with coefficient \(1-t^2\). The Pfaffian derivative and the outward-normal-first boundary correction in the \(8\pi^2\chi\) normalization yield the scalar at interpolation parameter \(t\)
\[
 2\sum_{a,b} S_{ab} C_{ab}+6(1-t^2)\det S .
\]
The mixed contribution comes from contracting one mixed connection form with the complementary ambient tangential curvature form. The other terms use the three cyclic choices, each evaluating to \(\det S\). Integration in \(t\in[0,1]\) gives \(2\sum S_{ab}C_{ab}+4\det S\). This uses the smooth compact-domain Chern–Gauss–Bonnet theorem with boundary. The flat ball calculation has value \(4\cdot2\pi^2=8\pi^2\). [Cibotaru–Moroianu, (1.1) and §3](https://www.imar.ro/~sergium/fisiere/pfa.pdf#page=12)

Returning from the normalized annulus to radius \(r\) multiplies the shape by \(r^{-1}\), the orthonormal ambient curvature by \(r^{-2}\), and surface area by \(r^3\). Consequently the measure-weighted polynomial integral is unchanged. The normalized integral has error at most \(412(20\varepsilon_r)(2\pi^2/d_b)\) from \(8\pi^2/d_b\), uniformly across the supplied link. Local outward-adapted frames compute the same scalar. The determinant is conjugation invariant and the tangent Hodge star identifies the cyclic wedge action with the tangent frame action, making the remaining term a Hilbert–Schmidt pairing. The integrand is smooth on a smooth supplied link.

For the scalar-flat pointed limit, apply finite Chern–Gauss–Bonnet to the single \(K_r\), with the orientation and all cut surfaces just fixed. The exact identity is
\[
 E_+(h;K_r)+E_-(h;K_r)-D(h;K_r)/2+\sum_{b=1}^k B_b(r)
 =8\pi^2\chi(K_r).
\]
Here \(D\) is the ordered two-tensor Ricci square integral. Exhaustion gives the finite-end formula once the three physical bulk limits are supplied. The spherical case uses the exact-product splice and the closed-source quotient obstruction to give \(d_b=1\), and the topology of the same marked core to give \(\chi(K_r)=2-k\). Then \(D_X=2(e_++e_-)+32\pi^2(k-1)\), so the strict bound \(D_X<32\pi^2\) gives one end.
