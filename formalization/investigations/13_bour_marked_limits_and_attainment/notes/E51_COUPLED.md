Fix real Euclidean dimension four. Let \(g\) be a positive-class constant-scalar stationary metric for the actual regularized gradient \(G^p\), \(0\le p\le1\). Fix a positive lower Yamabe bound, \(0\le R(g)\le C\), and a bound \(\sqrt{\mathrm{intrinsicCurvatureSq}(h)}\le K\) for \(h=ag\). Assume the quantitative Peters harmonic chart on physical balls under the resulting injectivity lower bound. Fix the standard Euclidean estimate \(\|D^2v\|_{L^6(\mu)}\le C_\Delta\|\Delta v\|_{L^6(\mu)}\) for smooth compactly supported real tests and the same Haar measure. These are the two declared geometric and analytic suppliers. Once the chart has been realized, only the latter enters the estimate. [Peters, Theorem 4.3](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1987__62_1_3_0.pdf#page=10) · [Gilbarg and Trudinger, Chapter 9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-61798-0_9)

One \(r,R,L,A,B\) works before the choice of \(p,g,a,x\). For \(a\ge1\) the chart on \(B_{4r}\) captures the original physical ball of radius \(r/(L\sqrt a)\) in the image of its closed radius-\(r\) ball. On \(B_{3r}\) a single smooth extension \(H\) equals the physical pullback. For every \(i,j\) on \(B_{5r/2}\),
\[
 \|H_{ij}\|_{W^{2,6}}+\|(\mathrm{Ric}_0(H))_{ij}\|_{W^{2,6}}\le B .
\]
Each norm is the sum of the function, full Fréchet derivative and full Fréchet Hessian \(L^6\) norms of the smooth entry. Neither the extension nor the area density away from \(B_{3r}\) enters the estimate. The two mixed cases are \(a=1\) on a bounded curvature subsequence and \(a=Q_j=\max|\mathrm{Rm}(g_j)|\) on a divergent subsequence once \(Q_j\ge1\). The covariant two-tensor \(Z=\mathrm{Ric}_0\) has weight zero. The scalar coefficient is \((2-p)R(g)/(12a)\), bounded by \(C/(6a)\). The curvature ceiling uses the ordered tensor and a normalized first hit has quarter squared norm \(1/4\). The Ricci energy \(D\) uses the ordinary two-tensor norm. To obtain the second estimate, differentiate \(h^{ab}\Gamma^k_{ab}=0\) on the harmonic neighborhood. Commuting the contracted derivative in the rough tensor equation leaves raised Riemann curvature, first metric jets, \(Z\) and its first derivatives. In dimension four the normalized rough source is bounded on \(v,w\) by \((256K^2+\frac83 S K+S^2/6)|v|_h|w|_h\) when \(|R(h)|\le S\). This supplies constants \(F_0,F_1,U\) in \(|Pu_j|\le F_0+F_1\sum_{k,i}|\partial_i u_k|\) and \(|u_j|\le U\) on the chart. The transport of the twice-covariant derivative and its metric trace uses the same local isometry. Scalar constancy or stationarity away from the physical image in the extension is unnecessary. Freeze the inverse Gram coefficients on a radius selected from the common first metric jets and ellipticity. Write \(w_j=\eta^2 u_j\) and \(T=\sum_{ji}\|\eta\partial_i u_j\|_6\). On a freezing patch, \(X_j=\|D^2w_j\|_6\le A_0+B_0T\) and \(Z_{ji}=\|\eta^2\partial^2_{ii}u_j\|_6\le C_0+D_0X_j+F T\). Compactly supported integration by parts yields
\[
 \|\eta\partial_i u_j\|_6\le
 \epsilon Z_{ji}+(24U L_\eta+16U/\epsilon)\mu(K)^{1/6}.
\]
Take \(\epsilon=[2(1+64(D_0B_0+F))]^{-1}\). Absorbing the term in \(T\) gives a common Hessian bound. On an inner ball the cutoff equals one. Reconstruct the full derivative from the fixed basis, restrict the Hessian, and use a finite cover chosen before \(g,p,a\). Two metric extensions agree with every derivative on the open physical ball, so the metric and Ricci inequalities hold on the same \(H\).
