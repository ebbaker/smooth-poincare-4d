## Outward connection and curvature

Use an outward unit normal \(\nu\) and a positive orthonormal frame \((\nu,e_1,e_2,e_3)\) along a boundary patch. For tangent \(X\), split the restricted tangent bundle as \(\mathbb R\nu\oplus T\partial K_r\). Let \(\nabla^0\) preserve the unit section \(\nu\) and use the intrinsic boundary Levi–Civita connection on \(T\partial K_r\). The tangential projection of the ambient connection equals this intrinsic connection by metric compatibility and torsion freeness. Hence \(\Theta=\nabla^h-\nabla^0\) has only mixed blocks, with
\[
 \Theta_X(v)=\langle \nu,v\rangle_h\nabla^h_X\nu
               -\langle\nabla^h_X\nu,v\rangle_h\nu .
\]
This formula gives a smooth skew endomorphism-valued one-form. With \(S_{ai}=\langle\nabla^h_{e_i}\nu,e_a\rangle_h\), it gives \(\Theta_{0a}(e_i)=-S_{ai}\) in the convention \(\omega_{ab}(X)=h(\nabla_X e_b,e_a)\). The actual Gauss normal defect of \(\nabla^h_X e_a\) is \(-h(\nabla^h_X\nu,e_a)\nu\), using the normal unit length and the derivative of metric orthogonality. On a regular level this \(S\) equals the normalized Hessian in E50. In particular \(S=+I\) on the Euclidean unit sphere in the outward orientation.

Use the affine path \(\nabla^t=\nabla^0+t\Theta\) on the same metric bundle. In a local trivialization of the restriction to the boundary, its intrinsic tangential connection is fixed, and the normal defect is multiplied by \(t\). The connection-form Gauss identity at \(t\) and at \(1\) gives
\[
 \Omega^t_{ab}(e_i,e_j)
 =\Omega^1_{ab}(e_i,e_j)
   +(1-t^2)(S_{ai}S_{bj}-S_{aj}S_{bi})
 \qquad (a,b,i,j\in\{1,2,3\}).
\]
In local bundle notation the immersion differential is \(J\), the intrinsic connection form is \(A\), and the pulled-back ambient form is \(C\). The difference \(\Theta_X J(u)\) is the actual normal covariant defect \(D_XJ(u)+C_XJ(u)-J(A_Xu)\). Substituting \(C+(t-1)\Theta\) scales this defect by \(t\), while skewness keeps the metric derivative compatibility. This derives the stated curvature relation using the pinned connection-form Gauss identity, without imposing the curvature relation as a chart hypothesis. [Connection-form Gauss theorem](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry/blob/788efe97894474c032de6dfb1289d515f613d15a/DifferentialGeometry/Geometry/Connection/ConnectionForm/Curvature.lean) · [Cibotaru–Moroianu, §3](https://www.imar.ro/~sergium/fisiere/pfa.pdf#page=11)

## Alternation and the compact-domain formula

In the project normalization the Pfaffian is twice the usual order-two Pfaffian. Its polarized transgression along the connection path is the project alternating wedge of \(\Theta\) and \(\Omega^t\). The outward correction is its negative, since the interior identity is \(E(\nabla^1)-E(\nabla^0)=dT\). Three complementary terms survive on the boundary because the difference is mixed. If \(C_{ai}=\Omega^1_{a+1,a+2}(e_{i+1},e_{i+2})\) in cyclic tangent indices, the signed outward coefficient is
\[
 2\sum_{a,i} S_{ai}C_{ai}+6(1-t^2)\det S.
\]
Indeed each cyclic choice of the first shape row gives the determinant by the alternating two-row minors. Integration over \(0\le t\le1\) gives \(2\sum S_{ai}C_{ai}+4\det S\). The tangential metric at the boundary is the one induced by \(h\). Pointwise changes by an oriented tangent frame preserve the determinant and the Hilbert–Schmidt contraction using the tangent Hodge-star identification of the complementary wedges. [Smooth-boundary normalization and transgression, (1.1), (3.1)–(3.3)](https://www.imar.ro/~sergium/fisiere/pfa.pdf#page=11)

The compact-domain theorem must use **one** \(K_r\), outward-normal-first on each exact component of \(\partial K_r\), and the induced measure on that component. On the scalar-flat interior the physical bulk term is \(E_+(h;K_r)+E_-(h;K_r)-D(h;K_r)/2\), with no signed equality on a piece. The whole marked core and the components enter as one finite sum. In a round four-sphere at sectional curvature one, the boundary of a geodesic ball of radius \(\pi/4\) gives \(S=I\), \(C=I\), and correction scalar \(10\), testing both the cubic term and the nonzero ambient term. For a round shell between radii \(a<b\), the outer normal gives \(S=\cot(b)I\) at the upper link and \(S=-\cot(a)I\) at the lower link. Each enclosing geodesic sphere contributes the physical integral \(12\pi^2\cos r-4\pi^2\cos^3r\) when pointed to increasing radius. Their signed difference is the negative of the shell's Euler bulk integral \(12\pi^2\int_a^b\sin^3r\,dr\). This checks opposite outward directions on the same compact domain without a product assumption.

## Round polar shell verification

The round embedding \((r,y)\mapsto(\cos r,\sin r\,y)\) for \(y\in S^3\) gives the metric \(dr^2+\sin^2r\,g_{S^3}\). Thus the slice area and radial volume density are \(2\pi^2\sin^3r\). Differentiating \(\partial_r\) in each tangent direction gives \(\cot(r)I\) for a normal pointing toward increasing radius. Reversing that normal changes the sign of the shape while preserving the unit-curvature matrix in the complementary wedges.

The Lean shell module encodes these round polar inputs in frames. It evaluates the bulk Pfaffian at the unit sectional curvature tensor and evaluates the boundary path pairing at each signed principal curvature. The two boundary terms are \(P(b)\) and \(-P(a)\), with \(P(r)=12\pi^2\cos r-4\pi^2\cos^3r\). The bulk is \(P(a)-P(b)\) by the fundamental theorem of calculus and \(P'=-12\pi^2\sin^3r\). The sum vanishes for every \(0<a<b<\pi\). The proof uses the round polar data and a single-variable integral, so the general global relative transgression and compact-domain Chern–Gauss–Bonnet inputs above are still required.
