## Normalization and the original flow

Fix one smooth closed connected oriented Riemannian homotopy four-sphere with a positive class of energy below $2T$, where $T=4\pi^2$. Use the common total $e=E_+=E_-$ only on such a closed source. With ordinary Ricci two-tensor square $D$ and scalar square integral $S$, the operator norm square is one quarter of the ordinary four-slot curvature norm square. Its three contributions are $E_++E_-$, $D/2$ and $S/24$. The Euler integral has the opposite Ricci sign, so its topological value $16\pi^2$ yields full curvature integral $16\pi^2+D$. The closed formulae for the functional are $2(1-\alpha)e+\alpha D/2$ and $2e+\alpha S/24-16\alpha\pi^2$. These computations leave the topological characteristic-number inputs explicit. [Bour v1, §§1, 3–4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.0342v1)

For the normalized start, $S_0=Y_0^2$ and $D_0=4e_0-(384\pi^2-Y_0^2)/12$. The start margin is $m=2(8\pi^2-e_0)-\alpha Y_0^2/24$. It converges to a strictly positive number because the supplied starts have $0\le e_0-I\le\alpha^2$ and the round Yamabe bound. A functional sublevel and $D\ge0$ give $e(t)\le 8\pi^2-m/[2(1-\alpha)]$. The closed formula in a Yamabe representative of that slice gives $Y^2\ge 24m/(1-\alpha)$. Positivity requires sign continuation from the starting metric. The same-type comparison $e(t)\ge I$ applies after that step. The sharp Ricci inequality retains the initial deficit:

$$
D(t)\le 4I + 4(e_0-I)/\alpha - (384\pi^2-Y_0^2)/12 .
$$

The scalar bound is $S(t)\le Y_0^2+48(e_0-I)/\alpha$. Both chiral energies, the ordinary Ricci square, and the positivity bound have one common tail with constants valid for every smooth time. The slice scalar square is a physical integral. Its reduction to $Y^2$ uses the volume-one Yamabe gauge at the starting metric. [Bour, Lemma 4.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.0342v1#page=19) · [Flow source audit](../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_BOUR_ATTAINMENT_20261126.md)

For the stated flow speed $-2\operatorname{grad}F$, the energy drop is twice the integrated squared gradient norm. With $g'=\lambda g$ and a covariant two-tensor, the pointwise squared norm scales by $\lambda^{-2}$ and the four-volume by $\lambda^2$. Thus the $L^2$ inner product of fixed variations is invariant. Differentiating scale invariance of the energy in a variation yields $\operatorname{grad} F(g')=\lambda^{-1}\operatorname{grad} F(g)$. The squared $L^2$ gradient norm therefore scales by $\lambda^{-2}$, which cancels the time factor in $s=\lambda^2(t-t_i)$. The analytic passage from small dissipation to a stationary pointed metric needs convergence on common space–time domains through the final time. Fixed-parameter estimates can depend on $\alpha$ at this stage. [Bour, Theorem 1.11 and §8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.0342v1#page=29) · [Bahuaud–Helliwell, Theorem A](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1407.4406v1)

## End count and the order of marked inputs

The decay and ALE results apply in a definite sequence. Finite full curvature, scalar-flat Bach-flatness and the homogeneous Sobolev inequality give differentiated curvature decay. With finite $L^2$ negative Ricci this gives finitely many order-zero ALE ends. Those charts imply finite first Betti number and allow the differentiated charts and order-two upgrade. The end-group argument needs one oriented comparison containing the core and all buffered links, a product replacement on the closed source, and strict $E_+<9\pi^2$ for that replacement. [Tian–Viaclovsky 2005, Theorem 1.1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0310302v3#page=2) · [Tian–Viaclovsky 2008, Theorem 1.1](https://ems.press/content/serial-article-files/43177#page=2) · [Ache–Viaclovsky, Theorem 1.2](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1249v2#page=3) · [Marked-necks quotient obstruction](../../../papers/investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/01_sections_and_connections/01_neck_geometry/manuscript/marked_necks_and_chiral_energy.tex)

After quotient exclusion the capped core has Euler characteristic two. In scalar-flat Gauss–Bonnet, $\chi(X)=2-k$ and the $k$ outgoing Euclidean boundary contributions are $8\pi^2$ each. The result is $D_X=2(E_++E_-)+32\pi^2(k-1)$. With $k\ge1$, nonnegative signed totals and $D_X<32\pi^2$, this proves $k=1$. The arithmetic alone admits $k=2$, $E_+=E_-=0$ and $D_X=32\pi^2$ at equality. The one-end step requires no signature conclusion. The common total and $D_X=4e_X$ follow once a single smooth compactification with zero signature supplies signed conformal comparisons. [Tian–Viaclovsky 2005, §7](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0310302v3#page=51)

Smooth removal uses the positive harmonic factor and the differentiated decay at the inverted puncture. The resulting compactification has $Y\ge y$ by cutoff of tests at a point of zero $H^1$ capacity. Its positive Bach-flat energy exceeds $T$ by the closed critical gap and nonflatness of the scalar-flat model. Fix the normal cap ball only after choosing a buffer that absorbs the Yamabe localization loss. At fixed radius the signed exterior comparison subtracts the full signed core mass from the full source mass and adds the full splice cost. At small radius the strict gap gives a positive cap below $T$. Recognition of that capped exterior supplies a standard sphere, and the inverse comparison on an open seam extends across the specific marked ball by the relative diffeomorphism step. This is the step that identifies the compactification with the original oriented smooth type. [Ache–Viaclovsky, Definition 1.6 and Theorem 1.8](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1106.1249v2#page=5) · [Palais, Theorem C](https://doi.org/10.1090/S0002-9939-1960-0117741-0)

## Stationary estimates and the two uses of extraction

Bour's covariant constant-scalar equation has principal term $\tfrac12\nabla^*\nabla E$, quadratic terms $-\mathring W(E)+E\circ E-|E|^2 g/4$, and scalar term $(2-p)RE/12$. Consequently the Weyl gradient in this normalization is $pRE/12$ on these metrics. The metric-scaled equation has the same form with scalar $R/\lambda$. Among the mixed sequence, the first closed branch keeps its actual constant-scalar critical gauge, while the first pointed branch uses the normalized Yamabe metric of the imported exact compact class. The second Ricci estimate on that branch is recomputed in this new gauge. [Bour, Proposition 4.4](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1012.0342v1#page=17)

At normalized curvature bounded above, setting $v(r)=V(r)/r^4$ converts the cutoff inequality to $v(r)\ge a\sqrt{v(r/2)}$ for $0<r\le1$ and $a=y/[4(24+C)]$. Iteration and the small-ball Euclidean density give $v(r)\ge a^2$. The original unscaled volume one gives a diameter bound by packing balls. A uniform injectivity bound follows at bounded normalized curvature. In harmonic coordinates one uses a fixed integrability exponent $q>4$: the metric's $W^{2,q}$ control makes $(\partial\Gamma)E$ uniformly $L^q$ because $E$ is bounded. Interior estimates then give $W^{2,q}$ for $E$ on smaller balls. Higher estimates alternate the Ricci and $E$ equations. All regularization dependence is in a bounded zeroth-order coefficient. Harmonic coordinate construction, elliptic estimates for the resulting system, and extraction of comparison maps are separate inputs. [Cheeger–Gromov–Taylor, Theorem 4.7](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214436699) · [Peters, Theorem 4.3](https://www.numdam.org/item/CM_1987__62_1_3_0.pdf) · [DeTurck–Kazdan, Lemma 4.1](https://numdam.org/item/10.24033/asens.1405.pdf)

For a first pointed return at fixed $\alpha$, positive marked pullback gives $I\le e_X\le e_0/(1-\alpha)$. The excess can be positive. The sharp Ricci bound there and $D_X=4e_X$ imply $384\pi^2-Y_0^2\le48\alpha$. For a second pointed return the source energies converge to $I$, so the independently applied marked comparison feeds $I\le e_X\le\liminf_j e(q_j)=I$. Only this squeeze gives immediate attainment and zero iterated exterior mass on that extracted sequence. The second closed alternative uses whole oriented comparison diffeomorphisms, convergence of the Yamabe tests and energy, and continuity of the Euler components as the parameter tends to zero.

On a second pointed sequence even the mixed first-branch starts have $Y_{j,0}^2\to384\pi^2$. Indeed $D_X=4I\le\liminf_j D(q_j)$ by Ricci transport on the second pointed domains. For a closed first return the sharp budget bounds the initial deficit by $48\alpha_j+12(4I-D(q_j))$. For a pointed first return the earlier $48\alpha_j$ bound suffices. With the round upper bound both cases are dominated by $48\alpha_j+12\max(4I-D(q_j),0)$, which tends to zero along the extracted sequence. This uses the ordinary Ricci integral, invariant under four-dimensional constant rescaling.
