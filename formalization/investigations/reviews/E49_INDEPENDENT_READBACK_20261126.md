## Closed spatial adjoints and normalization

Write \(K=\dot g\) for the covariant variation and \(L=\operatorname{tr}_g\nabla^2\) for the signed rough Laplacian. The differentiated Christoffel tensor is \(\dot\Gamma^k{}_{ij}=\frac12(\nabla_i K^k{}_j+\nabla_j K^k{}_i-\nabla^kK_{ij})\). The Ricci variation uses \(\nabla_k\dot\Gamma^k{}_{ij}-\nabla_j\dot\Gamma^k{}_{ki}\), and \(\dot\Gamma^k{}_{ki}=\frac12\nabla_i\operatorname{tr}K\). In particular, the crossed derivative with outer slot \(k\) and inner slot \(i\) has the following adjoints against a smooth test tensor \(A\) on the closed slice:

\[
\int A^{ij}\nabla_k\nabla_i K^k{}_j\,dV
=-\int (\nabla_k A^{ij})\nabla_i K^k{}_j\,dV
=\int K^k{}_j\nabla_i\nabla_k A^{ij}\,dV.
\]

The order reverses when both derivatives move. The other crossed term uses the other tensor slot and gives the same pairing for symmetric \(A,K\). The rough term has coefficient \(-1/2\) in the Ricci variation and has adjoint \(-\frac12 L\) for this sign of \(L\). Metric compatibility fixes which slots are raised and allows the first integration by parts with the physical measure. The trace variation contributes \(-\frac12\nabla_j\nabla_i\operatorname{tr}K\), which vanishes for a trace-free speed. [Bour, §3 and Appendix §9.1](https://arxiv.org/html/1012.0342v1#S3) · [Gursky–Viaclovsky, (2.2)–(2.5)](https://arxiv.org/html/1105.4648v2#S2)

In the closed four-dimensional normalization write \(S=\int R^2\,dV\), \(D=\int |\mathrm{Ric}_0|^2\,dV\) and \(F_W=E_++E_-\). Then \(F_p=(1-p)F_W+pD/2=(1-p)8\pi^2\chi+\frac12\int|\mathrm{Ric}|^2\,dV+(p-4)S/24\). The two inverse metric variations in \(|\mathrm{Ric}|^2\) give \(-2\langle\mathrm{Ric}^2,K\rangle\). The volume contribution is \(\frac12|\mathrm{Ric}|^2\operatorname{tr}K\), and \(\dot R=\operatorname{div}\operatorname{div}K-L\operatorname{tr}K-\langle\mathrm{Ric},K\rangle\). The integrated gradient of \(S\) is \(-2R\mathrm{Ric}_0+2\operatorname{Hess}R-2(LR)g\), with trace \(-6LR\). Thus the \(pS/24\) part of \(F_p\) has trace \(-pLR/4\), in agreement with the conformal path speed \(2ug\). The negative twice-gradient flow has derivative \(-2\|G\|_2^2\) and integrated dissipation equal to one half of the energy drop. [Scientific 06, Proposition `prop:budget`](../../../papers/investigations-1/06_bour_flow_and_fixed_type_attainment/manuscript/fixed_type_attainment.tex) · [Bour, §3](https://arxiv.org/html/1012.0342v1#S3)

## Normalized stationary charts

For \(h_j=a_jg_j\), the scalar is \(s_j=R_j/a_j\). In Bour's nonnegative rough convention the stationary Ricci principal coefficient is \(1/2\) and the coefficient of the scalar-Ricci term is \((2-p_j)s_j/12\). If \(0\le p_j\le1\), \(a_j\ge1\) and \(0\le R_j\le C\), this coefficient is in \([0,C/6]\) and tends to zero when \(a_j\to\infty\). The ordered curvature square is four times the operator square, so a normalized operator bound of one means an ordered tensor norm bound of two. A normalization by the ordered tensor maximum instead makes its basepoint value one. Both leave the ordinary Ricci two-tensor norm convention in force. ## Actual ALE link and Euler coefficient

On an oriented four-dimensional end take the outward unit normal \(\nu\) in the direction of increasing Euclidean coordinate radius and write \(S(V)=\nabla_V\nu\). At a point use orthonormal principal tangent directions with eigenvalues \(k_1,k_2,k_3\). In \(8\pi^2\chi\) units the Euler boundary density is \(4\det S+2\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}k_a K^X_{bc}\), with \(K^X_{bc}\) the ambient sectional curvature of the orthogonal tangent plane. The product-to-Levi–Civita interpolation changes the relevant tangent curvature term to \(K^X_{bc}+(1-t^2)k_bk_c\). The mixed connection slot contributes \(k_a\), and integrating \(2(1-t^2)\) gives \(4/3\) per cyclic term. Equivalently the density is \(2\sum_{\mathrm{cyc}}k_a K^{\partial X}_{bc}-2\det S\) by the hypersurface Gauss equation. For a geodesic sphere of a space form with sectional curvature \(\kappa\), radial scale \(f\) satisfying \(f''=-\kappa f\), and \(c=f'\), its boundary integral is \(2\pi^2(6\kappa cf^2+4c^3)\). Its derivative is \(-2\pi^2\cdot6\kappa^2 f^3\), which cancels the derivative of the bulk integral and fixes the sign at the Euclidean limit. The Christoffel correction to the Euclidean Hessian is \(O(r^{-1-\sigma})\), giving \(rS=\mathrm{Id}+O(r^{-\sigma})\). The induced area divided by the model area has density \(1+O(r^{-\sigma})\). The mixed term has integral \(O(r^{-\sigma})\) and the cubic term tends to \(8\pi^2/|\Gamma|\) on the actual quotient because its sphere cover has degree \(|\Gamma|\).
