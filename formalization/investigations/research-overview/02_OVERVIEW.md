Version 01 made equality between the limiting source energy and the sum on a base and its bubbles its principal target. That proof needs embeddings of all the contributing components and a bound for the energy on their entire complement. Its single-cut alternative already saved some of this work by starting from a specified compactified core. The master asks whether one positive-class minimizing sequence on each candidate smooth type has a single marked sphere separating two definite chiral masses. An inexpensive cut on that sequence already contradicts the definition of the infimum. Either full side can retain the original type, and the local interpolation also applies when studying concentration on a marked open domain. A sequence with well-controlled components or spinor fields may still be more accessible to the earlier methods. ## Entry and the variational argument

Let $M$ be a closed connected oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere with smooth Riemannian metrics. Write $E_\pm(g;U)=\int_U |W_g^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2\,dV_g$ for the energy of the Weyl endomorphism on the real self-dual or anti-self-dual two-forms. The full four-index Weyl integral on a region is $4(E_++E_-)$. Signature gives $E_+=E_-$ on the **closed** sphere. Put

$$
T=4\pi^2,\qquad I^+(M)=\inf_{Y([g])>0}E_+(g),\qquad \inf\varnothing=+\infty .
$$

One positive class with $E_+\le T$ gives smooth recognition by Chang–Gursky–Yang, including the equality classification after excluding its other topologies. Universal entry asks for a positive class with $E_+<2T$ on every smooth type. On a nonstandard type satisfying entry, $T\le I^+(M)<2T$, and equality $I^+(M)=T$ can only be unattained. Take unit-volume Yamabe representatives with $e_i=E_+(g_i)\to I^+(M)$ and $e_i\le 2T-\delta$ eventually for a fixed $\delta>0$. They have a uniform positive Yamabe lower bound and bounded physical full curvature energy. Their minimizing property supplies no small source Bach norm. In Lean the real infimum of the empty set equals zero. The primary route carries a positive-class witness on $M$. Any universal formulation must give the empty admissible set its extended value and prove compatibility with the finite witnessed infimum. [Thresholds and physical budget](../../../papers/master/manuscript/chiral_weyl_energy_and_smooth_poincare.tex) · [Recognition sources](../../../references/SOURCE_NOTES_BACH_FLOW_20261123.md)

For one cut let $m_1+m_2=e_i$ be the two **full raw** masses, and let the positive cap energies satisfy $E_j\le m_j+\gamma_j$, $\gamma_j\ge0$. If $e_i+\gamma_1+\gamma_2<2T$, at most one cap reaches $T$. Marked reassembly transfers the smooth type of the possible exceptional cap $s$ to $M$. Thus $m_{3-s}\le e_i-I^+(M)+\gamma_s$. The surcharge belongs to the retained side. Two strictly positive lower mass bounds contradict vanishing cap costs at the infimum. A fixed short spherical chart supplies those costs if its comparison metric is uniformly and strongly $W^{1,4}$-close to Euclidean and its full curvature is bounded in $L^2$ on both splices. The scalar-flat wall at $2T$ keeps the straightening path on the joined manifold positive. Stretching its exact product then supplies the two cap signs. The older marked-core theorem already transports an actual competitor under its own recognition, gluing and residual hypotheses. The immersion theorem offers a separate route to recognition. Every candidate admits a smooth immersion in $\mathbb R^5$. The current manuscript gives three sufficient bounds on one immersion. They are negative Gauss–Kronecker mass $J<8\pi^2/3$, induced chiral energy $E_+\le64\pi^2/9$, or $\|A^\circ\|_{L^4}^2\le16\sqrt{2}\,\pi/3$. The energy test applies throughout that induced conformal class, with either Yamabe sign or a zero class. Its proof combines a lower count of middle handles with a strict integrated Weyl estimate on the part of signature $(2,2)$. Constructing an immersion whose second derivatives give one of these bounds remains open.
