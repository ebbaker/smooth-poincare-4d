Let \(g_c=cg\) for constant \(c>0\) and fix any real parameter \(p\). The Levi–Civita connection is unchanged, so every fixed covariant field has the same uncontracted derivative in the two metrics. Tracing the first two slots in any fixed basis multiplies the result by \(c^{-1}\), because the actual inverse Gram coefficients do. This includes the divergence, and it includes the rough Laplacian with one such trace. The ordinary covariant Ricci tensor is unchanged and \(R_{g_c}=c^{-1}R_g\), so the weighted field \(A_p=\operatorname{Ric}+(p-4)R_g g/12\) is unchanged.

The three terms of the Koszul flux adjoint each have one inverse metric trace. The trace adjoint has two divergences and then a factor of the metric, giving \(c^{-2}c=c^{-1}\). The covariant Ricci square has one inverse trace and therefore scales by \(c^{-1}\). The term \(R_g\operatorname{Ric}\) has the same factor. The intrinsic bulk scalar is \(\tfrac12|\operatorname{Ric}|_g^2+(p-4)R_g^2/24\). Its multiplying tensor metric scales by \(c\) and both scalars in that sum scale by \(c^{-2}\). Symmetrizing in the two covariant slots commutes with scalar multiplication. Together these give
\[
 G_p(cg)=c^{-1}G_p(g).
\]
This derivation retains both scalar-derivative contributions inside the uncommuted adjoints. It uses no constancy of \(R\) and applies at \(p=0\). For example on a flat four-torus take \(h=e^{2\varepsilon\cos x_1}g_{\rm flat}\) with \(\varepsilon>0\). The conformal scalar formula gives values \(6\varepsilon e^{-2\varepsilon}\) at \(x_1=0\) and \(-6\varepsilon e^{2\varepsilon}\) at \(x_1=\pi\). The weight argument applies to this same metric at \(p=0\); the proof never invokes the constant-scalar commuted specialization. [Conformal scalar curvature formula](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry/blob/788efe97894474c032de6dfb1289d515f613d15a/DifferentialGeometry/Geometry/Metric/Conformal/Curvature.lean)

For \(s=c^2(t-t_0)\) and \(\widehat g_s=cg_{s/c^2+t_0}\), the chain rule multiplies the physical covariant speed by \(c^{-1}\). The scaling law therefore identifies the rescaled speed as \(-2G_p(\widehat g_s)\), whenever the same source time is regular and follows the native gradient equation. In four dimensions, the metric contraction, covariant tensor factor and volume give \(\|G_p(\widehat g_s)\|^2_{L^2(\widehat g_s)}=c^{-2}\|G_p(g_t)\|^2_{L^2(g_t)}\). The time change cancels the remaining factor. The exact dissipation identity in the new module therefore uses the gradient computed directly from each actual normalized metric.
