On a positive-parameter stationary pointed four-metric \(h\), the *local* covariant trace computation gives \(p\Delta R=0\). Its proof contracts the two covariant derivatives of Ricci and the curvature action. This works on a noncompact carrier without an integrated Euler or conformal identity. The finite \(L^2\) bound for \(R\) is inherited from the fixed-\(p\) original sequence by compact transfer and exhaustion when those comparisons are supplied. In the mixed stationary curvature-scale branch there is also a different argument, \(R(h_j)=R(g_j)/Q_j\to0\) under the common scalar ceiling. [Trace calculation](E51_LOCAL_TRACE.md) · [Bour, Theorem 1.11](https://arxiv.org/abs/1012.0342)

For a square-integrable smooth harmonic function \(u\), integrate the divergence of the compact vector field \(\eta^2u\nabla u\). The nonnegative square \(|\eta\nabla u+2u\nabla\eta|^2\) gives the physical inequality
\[
 \int\eta^2|\nabla u|^2\,dV \le 4\sup|\nabla\eta|^2\int u^2\,dV .
\]
No global integrability of the unlocalized gradient is assumed. On a connected manifold with compact closed physical balls use smooth radial cutoffs equal to one on \(B(o,r/2)\), supported inside \(B(o,r)\), and \(\sup|\nabla\eta|\le C/r\) for every \(r>0\). Let \(r\to\infty\). Fatou gives zero Dirichlet density almost everywhere. Continuity and positivity of volume on open sets give zero everywhere. Noncompactness and the common positive lower bound on unit-ball volumes let one pack arbitrarily many disjoint unit balls, so the total volume is infinite. The only square-integrable constant is zero. Consequently the fixed-\(p>0\) pointed limit has \(R=0\) when the geometric extraction, noncompactness, unit-ball volume floor and finite physical scalar-square integral have been established on that same limit. The connected closed limit at fixed \(p>0\) has constant scalar curvature. At \(p=0\) the trace is identically zero, so determining the scalar on a zero-parameter member of the mixed sequence uses its Yamabe gauge. This argument supplies no time-dependent compactness estimates, end identification, or exterior collar map.

At \(R=0\) the commuted formula also gives \(G^0=0\) and \(\Delta\mathrm{Ric}+2\mathrm{Rm}^{\circ}(\mathrm{Ric})=|\mathrm{Ric}|^2h/2\). This is the scalar-flat Euler equation for the Weyl functional in Ricci form. The Lean declaration uses the native zero-parameter gradient and physical tensor fields.
