Consider one smooth oriented immersion $F:M^4\to\mathbb R^5$ of a closed smooth homotopy four-sphere. Evaluate the second fundamental form, volume and both Weyl endomorphisms in its induced metric $g=F^*\delta$ on the smooth domain. For principal curvatures $k_1,\ldots,k_4$, form the three pair sums $s_j=k_1k_\ell+k_mk_n$. The Gauss equation gives the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of either chiral block as $\frac1{12}\sum_{j<l}(s_j-s_l)^2$. On signature $(2,2)$ this is at least $\frac83\det A$. Equality at a point forces opposite pairs of equal absolute principal curvatures. The subsequent exclusion of equality on an open set uses Gauss–Codazzi and the smooth principal distributions. [Immersion theorem and its proof](../../../../papers/master/manuscript/immersion_entry.tex) · [Equality audit](../../../../papers/master/reviews/E34_MIDDLE_WEYL_EQUALITY.md)

A second finite calculation gives $|A^\circ|^4/6-|W^\pm|_{\rm HS}^2\ge(8/9)(-\det A)_+$, with equality at $(-1,3,3,3)$. Route 09 supplies the indexed Gauss mass and topological lower count. With those inputs the manuscript rules out $E_+(g)\le64\pi^2/9$ on a nonstandard type, independently of Yamabe sign. Other sufficient recognition tests on one immersion are $\int_M(-\det A)_+\,dV_g<8\pi^2/3$ and $\lVert A^\circ\rVert_{L^4(g)}^2\le16\sqrt2\,\pi/3$. These are targets for Lean. [Sharp deficit](../../../../papers/master/notes/E29_GAUSS_DEFICIT.md) · [Height investigation](../../09_morse_heights_and_handle_recognition/README.md)

The construction arm studies an actual immersed $D^2\times D^2$ with framed incoming $S^1\times D^2$ and outgoing $D^2\times S^1$. The energy of both matching collars and the retained exterior must be computed on the same global immersion. An immersion below $8\pi^2$ can supply the height argument under the stated hypotheses. To pass to intrinsic positive-class descent requires its positive Yamabe sign as well. Existence of an immersion meeting a quantitative recognition or entry bound on an arbitrary smooth type remains open.
