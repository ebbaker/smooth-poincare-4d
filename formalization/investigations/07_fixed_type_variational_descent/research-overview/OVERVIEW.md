Let $M$ be a fixed closed oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere admitting a positive-Yamabe class with $E_+<8\pi^2$. Use positive classes on this same smooth type, an admissible witness, and $e_i\to I=I^+(M)$. On a nonstandard candidate $I\ge4\pi^2$. Route 05 compares each independently repaired ball to this same infimum. At fixed tolerance $\varepsilon$, disjoint marked balls of bounded cardinality give
$$
 e_i \le N_i(e_i-I)+\sum_j\gamma_{ij}+u_i ,
$$
where $u_i$ bounds all uncovered chiral mass. If the two last terms have respective limsup bounds $b(\varepsilon)$ and $\omega(\varepsilon)$ on the same subsequence, then $I\le b(\varepsilon)+\omega(\varepsilon)$. Subsequence and cardinality bound may depend on $\varepsilon$. Sending $\varepsilon$ to zero contradicts the nonstandard lower bound when the two costs vanish. [One-sided replacement and volume reduction](../../../../papers/master/manuscript/chiral_weyl_energy_and_smooth_poincare.tex) · [Joint selection and its residual](../../../../papers/master/manuscript/physical_volume.tex) · [Route 05](../../05_variational_comparison/README.md)

A joint replacement can use the balls **and the region between them**. It supplies one smooth marked positive competitor of energy $c_i$ on $M$. If its removed chiral energy minus the full installation cost exceeds $e_i-I$, then $c_i<I$. The topology, Yamabe sign, changed collar and remaining exterior belong to this same comparison. In the smooth normal-jet calculation a fixed radial flat core can consume the incoming energy at leading order. This motivates testing the joint geometry. When Route 06 supplies closed attainment at positive energy, a smooth nonround minimizer would require rigidity or a genuine lower-energy competitor. A punctured round base can transfer essentially all energy to one exit at the same limiting energy. A termination claim needs an independently controlled secondary quantity.
