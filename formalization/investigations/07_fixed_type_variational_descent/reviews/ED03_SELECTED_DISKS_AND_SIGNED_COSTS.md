## Geometry and fixed acceptance tests

On a fixed closed oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere, use the smooth unit-volume positive Yamabe metric $g_i$ for the two physical chiral measures $\lambda_i^\pm$ and the full ordered-index curvature measure $\nu_i=|\mathrm{Rm}_{g_i}|^2_{\mathrm{tens}}dV_{g_i}$. The volume-one modified representative $q_i$ supplies the probability $\mu_i=dV_{q_i}$ and $R_{q_i}=6L_{q_i}+c_i$ with $c_i\le0$. The smooth positive-Ricci conformal metric $k_i$ supplies comparison distances. The square spectral measure $L^2dV$ transports on the same measurable source regions. The three metric roles matter even when all metrics are conformal. [E58 proof](../../../../papers/master/01_fixed_type_variational_descent/manuscript/subcritical_charge.tex) · [E58 independent check](../../../../papers/master/01_fixed_type_variational_descent/reviews/E58_BAND_CHARGE.md) · [Chen–Zhu, Lemma 2.1 and Theorem 1.7](https://www.intlpress.com/site/pub/files/_fulltext/journals/cag/2014/0022/0005/CAG-2014-0022-0005-a002.pdf)

- **Selection.** Fix the omission tolerance, the physical curvature ceiling and the maximum comparison radius before taking late indices on one subsequence. On the pointed limit the auxiliary volume is absolutely continuous on the Euclidean regular set and vanishes on the removed curvature atoms. Select finitely many small disjoint outer balls at joint continuity radii with a strict ceiling margin. Keep the radii fixed. For $0<\tau<1/6$ the source radii $(1-\tau)r_j$ have separation and the required volume ratio. Cheeger–Colding's A.1.8–A.1.9 give smooth open model embeddings at these comparison balls. Their images contain the closed protected inner balls of radius $(1-3\tau)r_j$ since $(1-3\tau)<(1-\tau)^2$. In an open model ball of radius $R$, the inverse image of the compact inner set has maximum norm $m<R$ after adjoining the center. Taking $r=(m+R)/2$ and precomposing with dilation by $r$ yields a unit disk enclosing the inner set in its interior, with radial room $R/r>1$. An orientation reflection, when needed, preserves the concentric geometry. The original physical curvature ceiling monotone-transfers from the containing comparison ball, and $\mu_i$-coverage monotone-transfers from the union of the protected inner balls. [Finite selection and comparison geometry](../../../../papers/master/manuscript/physical_volume.tex) · [Cheeger–Colding, Appendix A](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214459974)
- **Complete omitted charge.** With $U_i$ the union of the actual marked closed disks, take $R_i=M\setminus U_i$. Write $e_i=\lambda_i^+(M)=\lambda_i^-(M)$, $Y_i=Y([g_i])$, and $\ell_i=\|L_{q_i}\|_{2,q_i}$. If $\mu_i(R_i)\le d$, the Yamabe constant test gives retained first moment at least $Y_i/6-\ell_i\sqrt d$, and Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the retained square moment below by the square of its positive part. The pointwise spectral factor $3/2$ bounds the sum of the two retained chiral masses below. Subtract the opposite total $e_i$ to get either omitted charge at most $2e_i-\tfrac32(Y_i/6-\ell_i\sqrt d)_+^2$. Using $Y_i^2\ge48(2T-e_i)$ with $T=4\pi^2$, this is at most $4(e_i-T)+(Y_i\ell_i/2)\sqrt d$. A single sufficiently small $d$ works at late indices for both raw complements when $I<5T/4$. At equality the leading bound can reach $T$ even at $d=0$. [Finite E58 replay](../research-progress/handoffs/ED03_BAND_CHARGE_TRANSFER.md)
- **Exact width.** For a chart $F$, let $B=F(\overline B_1)$ and use $A=\texttt{selectedDiskTrim}\ F\ k$ on its target. The exterior half is $A\cap B^c$, with smooth seams of zero physical volume and hence zero chiral charge. A chart with parameter radius $b>1$ contains a full open radial trim whenever $1+1/(k+1)\le b$ and $k\ge1$. Strict inequality leaves both closed faces and a further open buffer in the chart. For a finite family one can choose the same large $k$ to satisfy all these width conditions and the individual raw-mass bounds summed at that index. The trim definition alone can be clipped by the chart domain at larger widths. A cost evaluated after changing either chart or width needs its own estimate. [Endpoint trims](../research-progress/handoffs/ED03_SELECTED_DISK_COLLARS.md)
- **Signed repair.** Make $A_j^\mathrm{out}=A_j\cap B_j^c$. On each of $A_j$ and $A_j^\mathrm{out}$ use the source norm $S=\|F_\pm(m)\|_2^2$ and the weighted norm $H=\|(1-\psi)F_\pm(m)\|_2^2$ in the same marked density representation, so $H$ integrates $(1-\psi)^2$ against the original chiral measure. Along the joined path the density differs from the affine leading term $(1-s)F_\pm(m)+s(1-\psi)F_\pm(m)$ by at most $s\beta$ in $L^2$. Its norm is at most $(1-s)\sqrt S+s(\sqrt H+\beta)$. This gives a uniform joined increment $U^\pm=\max\{0,(\sqrt H+\beta)^2-S\}$. At the repaired endpoint the exterior increment is bounded by $V^\pm=(\sqrt{H_\mathrm{out}}+\beta_\mathrm{out})^2-S_\mathrm{out}$ and can be negative. [Weighted estimate and signed bounds](../../../../papers/master/01_fixed_type_variational_descent/manuscript/collar_capacity.tex)
- **Assembly and strictness.** For a single joined patch the total chiral increments agree by signature on the same closed oriented source. Their full complement energies are unchanged, so the common local increment is bounded by $\min_\pm U_j^\pm$. For a single capped patch use the changed set $B_j\cup A_j^\mathrm{out}$ and the equality of its two total net increments, giving the common gain at least $\max_\pm(\lambda_i^\pm(B_j)-V_j^\pm)$. The two exterior surcharges alone need not agree because the raw disk has its own chiral imbalance. With disjoint patches, the strict bound $e_i+\sum_j\min_\pm U_j^\pm<2T$ can enter the actual joined-path, stretching and compactified test argument, with the original angular markings used to identify the caps. The capped metric still needs its positive Yamabe sign and the budget on the whole exterior, including $R_i\setminus\bigcup_j A_j^\mathrm{out}$. The resulting bound $e_i-\sum_j\max_\pm(\lambda_i^\pm(B_j)-V_j^\pm)<I$ is a strict competitor. Equality tests attainment, and a joined ceiling at $2T$ supplies no strict wall margin. ## Which geometric witnesses are available

Liimatainen–Salo's Theorem 1.1 gives ellipticity after the conformal gauge conditions are established. It gives no prescribed global annular marking. Even on a conformally flat round chart, linearly normalizing an annulus of log half-width $b$ to log half-width one gives relative determinant-one eigenvalues $b^{3/2}$ in the radial direction and $b^{-1/2}$ in each tangential direction. Thus small raw Weyl and full-curvature charge in thin annuli is compatible with degenerating normalized ellipticity for that normalization.
