## Topological dependencies

For a smooth Morse function on a closed connected oriented smooth homotopy four-sphere, the two end-body tree reductions preserve the number of index-two points and give odd ranks $\ell,r$ satisfying $\ell+r=n_2$. The rearrangement preserves critical points and indices. The handle decomposition uses an auxiliary ordered function and chosen gradient-like field. A cut of that presentation requires a further comparison before identifying it with a specified physical height level. The smooth elementary cobordism statement is Milnor's *Lectures on the h-Cobordism Theorem*, Theorem 3.14. Theorems 4.7–4.8 supply rearrangement and Theorem 5.4 gives geometric cancellation. His *Morse Theory*, Theorem 3.2, gives the homotopy type of a cell attachment, which does not carry the smooth boundary identifications. [Milnor, *Lectures*](https://www.math.auckland.ac.nz/~hekmati/Books/Milnor2.pdf) · [Milnor, *Morse Theory*](https://pagine.dm.unipi.it/~a019210/Milnor_Morse%20theory.pdf)

- **Zero middle handles.** The two actual balls meet along their full boundaries. The needed smooth extension of a self-diffeomorphism of $S^3$ follows from the components result for $\operatorname{Diff}(S^3)$ recalled by Hatcher. Interpolate the boundary isotopy on a collar and use the orthogonal endpoint on the interior. This permits comparison with the fixed smooth double. The Alexander trick establishes only a topological extension. [Hatcher, p. 553](https://pi.math.cornell.edu/~hatcher/Papers/SmaleConjecture.pdf)
- **One middle handle.** After reversing the presentation if needed, the lower piece is the marked trace of an integer-framed knot $K\subset S^3$ and the upper piece is $S^1\times D^3$. Attaching $D^2\times D^2$ replaces $S^1\times D^2$ in the incoming boundary by $D^2\times S^1$, with the new meridian attached on the slope $\lambda+k\mu$ of the given framing. Here $\mu$ is a meridian and $\lambda$ denotes the preferred longitude. In the knot exterior $H_1=\mathbb Z[\mu]$ and $[\lambda]=0$, so filling gives $\mathbb Z/(k)$ by Mayer–Vietoris. The boundary $S^1\times S^2$ forces $k=0$. Gabai's Corollary 8.3 and Remark 8.5 identify the knot as the unknot. Replacing the full framed tube by the standard zero-framed tube identifies its trace with $S^2\times D^2$. Laudenbach–Poénaru Theorem A at $p=1$ then applies to the actual outgoing boundary identification, regardless of which diffeomorphism occurs. Moussard's Theorem 2.5 explicitly gives extension of every self-diffeomorphism of the boundary over the upper 1-handlebody. [Gabai, pp. 525–526](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214441488) · [Gabai text reproduction](https://www.scribd.com/document/1026013102/2-Gabai) · [Laudenbach–Poénaru, pp. 337, 341–342](https://www.numdam.org/item/10.24033/bsmf.1741.pdf) · [Moussard, Theorem 2.5](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2412.10210v3)

Write $\varphi:\partial X\to\partial Q$ for the actual outward-boundary gluing, and take $a:X'\to X$ and $b:Q'\to Q$ to be changes of piece coordinates. The new mark is $(\partial b)^{-1}\circ\varphi\circ\partial a$. On the incoming side an attaching tube is pulled back pointwise along the inverse boundary identification, including every point in its normal disk. The boundary square is necessary for a map of the quotient and the collar germ controls smoothness. For instance, normal scalings $t\mapsto t$ on one half and $t\mapsto 2t$ on the other have the same boundary restriction and give unequal one-sided derivatives in a signed seam chart. A proof using supplied product-collar maps can check both directions in the signed chart. A proof using side maps without exact product germs needs a separate collar straightening or an equivalent open-seam smoothness argument. These checks are independent of oriented gluing. For the oriented claim, the actual boundary map reverses the two outward-normal-first boundary orientations. An unoriented diffeomorphism to the standard sphere is sufficient for the numerical floor and its orientation can be corrected by reflection.

## Native formal interfaces

The pinned `DifferentialGeometry.Topology.adjunctionHomeomorph` transports a marked topological adjunction. `Handle.handleAdjunctionDiffeomorph` transports a supplied rounded smooth structure. `DifferentialGeometry.Geometry.Boundary.BinaryCollaredGluing` and its `toBoundaryGluing` provide the actual quotient, and `Handle/CollaredGluing` supplies the signed seam and interior pieces. The ordinary `Topology.Double` uses the identity on the boundary of one body. `TwistedSphere.twisted_sphere_homeomorph` uses the topological Alexander trick. `Topology/Diffeomorph/SphereExtension.lean` provides smooth extension in the case of $S^2$, and the general sphere isotopy extension in `SphereIsotopy.lean` starts from a supplied isotopy. The $S^3$ extension remains an external input. [Pinned binary gluing source](https://github.com/qinz1yang/differential-geometry/blob/788efe97894474c032de6dfb1289d515f613d15a/DifferentialGeometry/Geometry/Boundary/Manifold/BinaryGluing.lean)

The one-critical-point smooth attachment construction in `Topology/Morse/Attachment/SmoothHandle.lean` is private and its public attaching-region theorem gives the actual smooth map into an isolated lower level. The reduction to compatible marked ends of an arbitrary closed four-manifold is additional construction. A bounded source scan found no pinned Gabai Property R, Laudenbach–Poénaru extension or arbitrary $S^3$ diffeomorphism extension theorem, and no native Dehn-filling API for the integer-framed knot step. The trace comparison fixes both coordinates of every handle point. The old lower inclusion is applied after $p^{-1}a$, while the new inclusion is applied after $(\partial\alpha)^{-1}p^{-1}a$. **HT-E04d.** The standard product model splits the round sphere in an orthogonal sum at the common squared radius $1/2$. The forward piece maps, inverse coordinate formulas, cover and common face use the same two sphere directions, so the seam mark is the identity in those product coordinates. In the overlap the signed parameter is $s=\lVert x\rVert^2-1/2$ with inverse $((u,v),s)\mapsto(\sqrt{1/2+s}\,u,\sqrt{1/2-s}\,v)$. The target is the open set where neither block vanishes. The two inverse directions are smooth there, and $s\leq0$ on the disk–sphere side while $s\geq0$ on the sphere–disk side. For $(m,n)=(1,1)$ the model gives the two solid tori of the standard $S^3$, with the product longitude of the unknot bounding a meridional disk in its exterior. For $(m,n)=(1,2)$ it gives $D^2\times S^2$ and $S^1\times D^3$ in the round four-sphere. Recognizing the actual integer-framed trace still requires the actual filling identification, Property R and a marked product-trace comparison that handles rounding. Replacing the product seam by an arbitrary boundary diffeomorphism still requires the extension theorem. [Standard model](../../../SmoothPoincare/Heights/StandardSurgeryModel.lean)

**HT-E04g.** `morseCriticalCount` uses the set of actual critical points in each index. Invariance under same-point, same-index transport follows from equality of those sets. **HT-E04h.** The supplied homeomorphism to the split round sphere is assumed smooth in both directions on the two open sides.
