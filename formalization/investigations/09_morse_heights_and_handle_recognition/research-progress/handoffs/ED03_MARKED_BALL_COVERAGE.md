## Covering the fixed target

On a connected compact Hausdorff source, suppose the relative open source patches cover the source, each comparison is a smooth partial diffeomorphism into the fixed smooth unit four-ball, and every forward comparison agrees on its entire source overlap. Relative openness includes the upper boundary in its own manifold topology. Use the full inward half of a matched signed collar including its zero section to supply the boundary patches. To restrict an ambient comparison to these relative open patches, verify its body and side equations.

The forward patches give a local diffeomorphism of the entire source into the ball. It has nonempty open range, and its continuous image of a compact space is closed in the Hausdorff ball. The pinned compact local-homeomorphism theorem actually gives a covering. Connectedness of the source and simple connectedness and local path connectedness of the ball make it one sheet, by uniquely lifting the identity through a chosen starting point. The diffeomorphism uses the literal forward formula on each patch. Global injectivity implies that its inverse agrees with each supplied inverse on the whole patch target, including all target overlaps. Surjectivity then gives that the union of those targets is the whole prescribed ball. This discharges the target-cover and inverse-overlap premises of `markedEdgePatchDiffeomorph` at this step. ## An explicit Euclidean target

Write $(t,z)\in\mathbb R\times\mathbb R^3$ in axial and orthogonal transverse coordinates. The derivative of $q(t,z)=(t^2-1)^2+\lVert z\rVert^2$ is $(4t(t^2-1),2z)$ and its Hessian has axial entry $12t^2-4$ and transverse block $2I_3$. The critical points are the two minima at $(\pm1,0)$ and the index-one saddle at $(0,0)$. Their heights are 0 and 1. The incoming sublevel $q\le b^2$, $0<b<1$, has one lobe in each open sign of $t$. The explicit incoming ball coordinates on each lobe are $((t^2-1)/b,z/b)$, with inverse $(\pm\sqrt{1+b u_0},b u_\perp)$ on a neighborhood of the closed unit ball. Near the saddle, $u=t\sqrt{2-t^2}$ gives the exact model height $1-u^2+\lVert z\rVert^2$. The incoming and saddle formulas are analytical checks of this model. For $a>1$, the ambient diffeomorphism
\[
 D_a(t,z)=\left(\frac{t}{\sqrt{a+1}},\frac{z}{\sqrt{(a+1)(a-1+t^2)}}\right)
\]
sends the entire body $q\le a^2$ to the closed unit four-ball and the entire outgoing level $q=a^2$ to the unit three-sphere. The equality
\[
 q-a^2=(a+1)(a-1+t^2)(\lVert D_a(t,z)\rVert^2-1)
\]
also records the side signs. At any height $c>1$ and width $0<w<c-1$, a smooth partial diffeomorphism maps the entire signed strip $S^3\times(-w,w)$ onto the entire open band $c-w<q<c+w$. Its forward map is $(\omega,s)\mapsto D_{\sqrt{c+s}}^{-1}(\omega)$ and its inverse at $x$ in the band is $(D_{\sqrt{q(x)}}(x),q(x)-c)$. The radius factors stay positive on these domains and vary smoothly in the axial point and height. The height is exactly $c+s$, and both composition equations hold at every point of their whole domains. For $c=4,w=1$, an importing application takes arbitrary angular and transverse parametrizations $u:A\to S^3$, $s:A\to(-1,1)$ and checks the calibrated height, the exact inward-side test $q\le4\Leftrightarrow s\le0$, and recovery of the entire parameter pair $(u,s)$.
