## R46 — Short-time existence

Eric Bahuaud and Dylan Helliwell, *Short-Time Existence for Some Higher-Order Geometric Flows*, *Communications in Partial Differential Equations* **36** (2011), no. 12, 2189–2207. [Published record](https://doi.org/10.1080/03605302.2011.593015) · [Inspected arXiv:1010.4287v1](https://arxiv.org/html/1010.4287v1), 20 October 2010.

Selected preprint Theorem C, the equation following it, and the DeTurck proof description in Sections 1 and 5 were checked. Theorem C starts from a smooth Riemannian metric on a compact manifold of even dimension $n\ge4$ and supplies a smooth short-time solution of the **modified** ambient obstruction equation. The four-dimensional scalar correction is $(\Delta s)g/12$. The introduction applies the theorem to a closed real four-manifold. Its use is local existence, with the conformal and diffeomorphism degeneracies handled by the scalar correction and the proof's gauge adjustment. The journal record supplies publication metadata.

## R47 — Energy dissipation and stability scope

Eric Bahuaud, Christine Guenther, James Isenberg, and Rafe Mazzeo, *Linear and nonlinear stability for the Bach flow, I*, [arXiv:2508.06633v1](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06633v1), 8 August 2025. The inspected copy is the preprint.

The first variation in Section 1, formulas (1.1)–(1.2), and Section 2.3 fix the Bach normalization, the negative sign relating the gradient of the full Weyl energy to $B$, and the fourth-order modified metric equation. In real dimension four its pure-trace scalar term contributes zero to the derivative of the energy, by trace-freeness of $B$. The draft uses that sign and keeps a positive constant for the conversion to the chiral operator norm. Theorem 1.1 treats linear stability of a gauge-modified equation at complete constant-curvature backgrounds. The nonlinear claims inspected in the introduction concern the hyperbolic setting described there. The Bach-flow draft uses the equation and dissipation, with the round calculation in the companion manuscript as a separate local comparison.

## R48 — Continuation and compactness

Christopher Lopez, *Ambient obstruction flow*, *Transactions of the American Mathematical Society* **370** (2018), no. 6, 4111–4145. [Published record](https://doi.org/10.1090/tran/7106) · [Inspected arXiv:1506.01979v1](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1506.01979v1), version posted 5 June 2015. The PDF served by arXiv carries a 15 July 2018 internal date.

Selected preprint equation (1) and Theorems 1.1–1.5 were checked. In dimension four the equation is the modified Bach equation used here. Theorem 1.2 requires a compact solution on its maximal smooth interval and shows that a finite maximal time forces an unbounded supremum of the full curvature tensor. The pointed compactness theorem uses curvature and injectivity-radius control. The high-curvature rescaling theorem assumes a uniform Sobolev constant along the maximal solution and specifies the rescaling sequence. The large-time alternatives require an already global solution with uniformly bounded full curvature. In dimension four the applicable smooth subsequential limits in those alternatives are obstruction-flat. The draft uses the continuation statement and treats compactness and rigidity as separate requirements. The theorem locators refer to the inspected PDF.

## R49 — Yamabe minimizers

Richard Schoen, *Conformal deformation of a Riemannian metric to constant scalar curvature*, *Journal of Differential Geometry* **20** (1984), no. 2, 479–495. [Published record](https://doi.org/10.4310/jdg/1214439291) · [Inspected copy of the published article](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen.yamabe.pdf).

The opening Sobolev-quotient formulation and [Section 3, Theorem 3, pp. 493–495](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen.yamabe.pdf#page=15) were checked for the planning writeup. The solution of the Yamabe problem provides a smooth positive conformal factor attaining the critical quotient on a smooth closed Riemannian manifold of dimension greater than two. In dimension four the volume-one minimizing representative has constant scalar curvature equal to $Y(M,[g])$. This is the input for applying Gauss–Bonnet to the representative of each smooth time slice. The planning estimate uses only its existence and normalization.
