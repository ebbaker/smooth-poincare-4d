The records separate the original hypotheses from the derivations in the [branch notes](../papers/investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/notes/README.md) E48–E50 and the [assessment](../papers/investigations-1/03_first_order_twistor/02_dirac_and_twistor_spinors/reviews/DIR_B23_CORE_CHAMBER_AND_NECKLACE_20261124.md).

**R99 · Variational theory for the total scalar curvature functional for Riemannian metrics and related topics.** Richard M. Schoen. In *Topics in Calculus of Variations* (Montecatini Terme, 1987), Lecture Notes in Mathematics 1365, Springer 1989, pp. 120–154, DOI 10.1007/BFb0089180. Inspected: a university-hosted scan, §2, pp. 132–135, through a text extraction with visible OCR noise. Statements used in E49: a solution singular at $0$ or $\infty$ is singular at both and is radial, by Caffarelli–Gidas–Spruck, so global solutions on the cylinder depend on $t$ only; the ordinary differential equation (2.2); the critical length $T_0=2\pi/\sqrt{n-2}$ below which the product is the only solution; $k$ inequivalent solutions for $T\in((k-1)T_0,kT_0]$; all solutions except the one of fundamental period $T$ are variationally unstable and that one minimizes; its energy tends to $\sigma(S^n)$, so $\sigma(S^1\times S^{n-1})=\sigma(S^n)$, not attained. Schoen states that he has not checked that the period increases with the parameter; E49 checks it numerically for $n=4$. The page numbers come from the extraction and should be confirmed against the printed volume before citation. Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck's paper was not inspected. [Inspected text](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/schoen.totalscalar.pdf)

**R100 · On Yamabe constants of Riemannian products.** Kazuo Akutagawa, Luis A. Florit, Jimmy Petean. arXiv:math/0603486; the version inspected was the one served on this date and its number was not recorded. §3, "Reviewing the circle": attributes to Schoen, with Kobayashi, the description of all solutions on $S^{n-1}\times S^1$ as functions of the circle via Caffarelli–Gidas–Spruck, writes the same equation, and states that the constant is below that of the sphere for every radius and approaches it as the radius grows. Used as a second statement of R99. Journal data not checked. [Inspected text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0603486)

**R101 · Scalar curvature of a metric with unit volume.** Osamu Kobayashi. Math. Ann. 279 (1987), 253–265, DOI 10.1007/BF01461722. Publisher metadata only; the paper was not read. Its connected-sum inequality and the value $\sigma(S^1\times S^{n-1})=\sigma(S^n)$ are used as quoted in R99, R100 and R102. [Publisher record](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01461722)

**R102 · On the scalar curvature of 4-manifolds.** Claude LeBrun. arXiv:2105.10785; version not recorded. Equation (9), $Y(M^n)\le Y(S^n)$, and equation (14), Kobayashi's inequality $Y(M\#N)\ge\min(Y(M),Y(N))$ for nonnegative invariants. The extraction found no statement about homotopy four-spheres and no value for $S^2\times S^2$. [Inspected text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.10785)

**R103 · Low-dimensional surgery and the Yamabe invariant.** Bernd Ammann, Mattias Dahl, Emmanuel Humbert. J. Math. Soc. Japan 67 (2015), 159–182, DOI 10.2969/jmsj/06710159; inspected arXiv:1204.1197, version not recorded. Surgery theorem for $k\le n-3$: $\sigma(N)\ge\min(\sigma(M),\Lambda_{n,k})$. Table 1: $\Lambda_{4,1}>38.9$ against $\mu(S^4)\approx61.56$. $\Lambda_{n,0}=\sigma(S^n)$. §5.3 and Table 2: the bound $t_n$ for 2-connected manifolds of vanishing index is given for $n\ne4$; for $n=4$ the entry is a question mark because it is unknown whether exotic four-spheres exist. Used in the assessment for the Yamabe invariant of homotopy spheres obtained by circle surgery. [Inspected text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1204.1197)

**R104 · On a conformal gap and finiteness theorem for a class of four manifolds.** Sun-Yung Alice Chang, Jie Qing, Paul Yang. arXiv:math/0508621; existing bibliography key `ChangQingYang2006Gap`. Equation (1.2), Chern–Gauss–Bonnet in the form $8\pi^2\chi=\int(\tfrac14|W|^2+\tfrac1{24}R^2-\tfrac12|E|^2)$; (1.5)–(1.6); Theorem 1.1, quoting Chang–Gursky–Yang: positive Yamabe constant and $\int\sigma_2>\int\tfrac14|W|^2$ give $S^4$ or $\mathbb{RP}^4$, with equality only for the Fubini–Study class or a quotient of $S^3\times S^1$; (1.7), $\int\sigma_2\le Y^2/24\le16\pi^2$ at a Yamabe metric; Remark 2, $|W|^2=W_{ijkl}W^{ijkl}$. Used in E50, where the same hypothesis is written as a bound on $\int|{\rm Ric}_0|^2$. The Chang–Gursky–Yang paper itself has an earlier record in the [Bach source notes](SOURCE_NOTES_BACH_FLOW_20261123.md#inherited-background-and-recognition) and was not re-inspected. [Inspected text](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0508621)

**R105 · Decomposing 4-manifolds with positive scalar curvature.** Richard H. Bamler, Chao Li, Christos Mantoulidis. Adv. Math. 430 (2023), 109231; inspected a repository copy of the published article. Theorem 1.1, attributed to Schoen–Yau and Gromov–Lawson: for $n\ge3$, surgeries of dimension $0$ to $n-3$ on a manifold admitting positive scalar curvature give a manifold admitting it. Theorem 1.4 and Questions 1.2 and 1.7 concern decompositions and make no statement about homotopy four-spheres. Used for the surgery theorem only; the original papers were not inspected. [Inspected text](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10437035)

**R106 · Sizes of spaces of triangulations of 4-manifolds and balanced presentations of the trivial group.** arXiv:1610.06130; the authors were not shown in the extraction and are recalled as Boris Lishak and Alexander Nabutovsky, to be confirmed. Passage seen: starting from a connected sum of copies of $S^1\times S^3$, one for each generator, the relators are realized by embedded circles and killed by surgery, and triviality of the group makes the result a homotopy sphere. A second description of the same manifolds, as doubles of the four-dimensional handlebody of a balanced presentation, was seen in Selman Akbulut, *On a homotopy 4-sphere*, arXiv:2006.05835. Used in the assessment to identify the homotopy four-spheres reached from $\#_k(S^1\times S^3)$ by circle surgery. Search snippets only; neither paper was read. [Inspected listing](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.06130)

## Not inspected, quoted from memory in the notes

The Yamabe constant $12\sqrt2\,\pi$ of the Fubini–Study class; the first zero $j_{1,1}\approx3.8317$ of $J_1$ and the Dirichlet eigenvalue of a Euclidean ball; the differential equation, Fourier series and degenerate expansions of the Jacobi elliptic functions, which E49 verifies numerically; Sturm oscillation for periodic problems; the continuity of the Yamabe constant under smooth convergence. Compactness of the full set of constant scalar curvature metrics in a non-round class in dimension four is not used by any proof in E48–E50.

**R107 · Asymptotic symmetry and local behavior of semilinear elliptic equations with critical Sobolev growth.** Luis A. Caffarelli, Basilis Gidas, Joel Spruck. Comm. Pure Appl. Math. 42 (1989), 271–297. Inspected: a university-hosted scan through a text extraction with heavy OCR noise; the title page gives volume XLII, pages 271–297, 1989. Theorem 8.1, as extracted: for a $C^2$ solution $u$ of $-\Delta u=g(u)$ in $\mathbb R^n\setminus\{0\}$, $n\ge3$, with an isolated singularity at the origin, where $g$ is locally Lipschitz, nondecreasing with $g(0)=0$, has $t^{-(n+2)/(n-2)}g(t)$ nonincreasing and satisfies $g(t)\ge ct^p$ for some $p\ge n/(n-2)$ and large $t$: if the origin is a non-removable singularity then $u$ is radially symmetric about the origin, and if it is removable then $u$ is radially symmetric about some point. The exponent in the second condition on $g$ was partly illegible and is read here as the critical one. Used in the manuscript and the supplement with $n=4$ and $g(t)=st^3/6$, which satisfies the three conditions with $p=3$, for the positive function $v=u/|y|$ that is unbounded at the origin. The statements of Theorems 1.1 and 1.2 and of Corollary 8.2 were seen and are not used. [Inspected text](https://math.jhu.edu/~js/Math646/asymmetry.pdf)

The manuscript uses the parameter $m=k^2$ and $m_1=k'^2$. [§22.13](https://dlmf.nist.gov/22.13) · [§19.4](https://dlmf.nist.gov/19.4) · [§19.12](https://dlmf.nist.gov/19.12)
