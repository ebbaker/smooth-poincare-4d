## Estimates for divergence-free and canceling operators in \(L^1\)

**R109 · New estimates for the Laplacian, the div–curl, and related Hodge systems.** Jean Bourgain and Haïm Brezis. C. R. Math. Acad. Sci. Paris 338 (2004), 539–543. Not read. It is named in the abstracts of R111 and R112 as the source of the Sobolev inequality for divergence-free \(L^1\) vector fields, and in R114 as the source of the question about the Lorentz scale. E60 names it for the mechanism of Theorem 5 and imports nothing.

**R110 · Estimates for \(L^1\)-vector fields.** Jean Van Schaftingen. C. R. Math. Acad. Sci. Paris 339 (2004), 181–186, DOI 10.1016/j.crma.2004.05.013. Publisher metadata from a search result only; not read. Named with R109.

**R111 · Limiting Sobolev inequalities for vector fields and canceling linear differential operators.** Jean Van Schaftingen. J. Eur. Math. Soc. 15 (2013), 877–921; arXiv:1104.0192. Read: the journal abstract in a search excerpt, and an excerpt of the arXiv text around Open Problem 8.4. The abstract states that the limiting Sobolev estimate holds exactly when \(A(D)\) is elliptic and canceling. Open Problem 8.4 asks whether \(\|D^{k-n}u\|_{L^\infty}\le C\|A(D)u\|_{L^1}\) holds for elliptic canceling operators of order \(k\ge n\). Volume and pages were not checked against the journal. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1104.0192)

**R112 · Hardy–Sobolev inequalities for vector fields and canceling linear differential operators.** Pierre Bousquet and Jean Van Schaftingen. Indiana Univ. Math. J. 63 (2014), 1419–1445; arXiv:1305.4262, v2 of 10 April 2019 as listed. Read in a fetched extraction of the arXiv PDF: Definition 1.1, an operator is canceling if the images \(A(\xi)[V]\) over \(\xi\ne0\) intersect in zero; Theorem 2, the Hardy inequality for elliptic canceling operators; Theorem 3, for \(A(D)\) homogeneous of order \(k\ge n\) on \(\mathbb R^n\), elliptic and canceling, \(\sup|D^{k-n}u|\le C\int|A(D)u|\) for compactly supported smooth \(u\). The extraction did not show which version was served. Journal data were not checked. Use: E60 states that its amplitude bound is of this type and explains why Theorem 3 is not applied, namely that the linearized Bach operator is not elliptic on all symmetric tensors. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1305.4262)

**R113 · Critical \(L^p\)-differentiability of \(BV^{\mathbb A}\)-maps and canceling operators.** Bogdan Raiță. Trans. Amer. Math. Soc. 372 (2019), 7297–7326; arXiv:1712.01251v3. Abstract only, in search excerpts: for the estimate of R112, Theorem 3, ellipticity is necessary and cancellation is not, and the paper determines the class of elliptic operators for which it holds. Not used. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01251v3)

**R114 · Fractional integration and optimal estimates for elliptic systems.** Felipe Hernandez and Daniel Spector. Calc. Var. Partial Differential Equations 63 (2024), article 117, DOI 10.1007/s00526-024-02722-8; arXiv:2008.05639, v2 of 20 November 2021 as listed. Read in a fetched extraction of the arXiv PDF: Theorem 1.1, for \(d\ge2\) and \(\alpha\in(0,d)\) there is \(C(\alpha,d)\) with \(\|I_\alpha F\|_{L^{d/(d-\alpha),1}(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)}\le C\|F\|_{L^1(\mathbb R^d;\mathbb R^d)}\) for all \(F\in L^1\) with \(\operatorname{div}F=0\) in distributions; Theorem 1.2, the div–curl system in \(\mathbb R^3\); and the remark that the optimal Lorentz inequality for all cocancelling operators is not known. The extraction did not show which version was served. Use: E60 records, as a lead and not as a result, that Theorem 1.1 applied to the rows of \(B'(h)\) with \(d=4\), \(\alpha=2\) gives \(\|D^2h^{TT}\|_{L^{2,1}}\le C\|B'(h)\|_{L^1}\). That deduction was not checked against the paper beyond the statement. A related abstract, F. Hernandez, B. Raiță and D. Spector, *Endpoint \(L^1\) estimates for Hodge systems*, arXiv:2108.06857, states a Besov–Lorentz version for first-order cocancelling constraints; abstract only. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.05639)

## Einstein metrics on the four-sphere

**R115 · A gap theorem for positive Einstein metrics on the four-sphere.** Kazuo Akutagawa, Hisaaki Endo and Harish Seshadri. arXiv:1801.10305, dated "January, 2018; February, 2018 (revised version)" in the fetched text, v2 of 11 February 2018 as listed; published in Math. Ann. 373 (2019), 1329–1339 according to the reference list of R118. Read in a fetched extraction: the introduction and Theorem 1.1, attributed to Gursky: a positive Einstein metric \(g\) on \(S^4\) with \(Y(S^4,[g])\ge Y(S^4,[g_{\mathbb S}])/\sqrt3\) is isometric to the round metric up to scale; the value \(Y(S^4,[g_{\mathbb S}])=8\sqrt6\pi\); and the sentence that the round metric is, to that date, the only known Einstein metric on \(S^4\). Use: the assessment's remark that a non-round positive Einstein metric on \(S^4\) would have \(\tfrac{16}3\pi^2<E_+<8\pi^2\). That inequality is derived in the assessment from Theorem 1.1 and the manuscript's Gauss–Bonnet formula; it is not quoted from the source. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1801.10305)

**R116 · Four-manifolds with \(\delta W^+=0\) and Einstein constants of the sphere.** Matthew J. Gursky. Math. Ann. 318 (2000), 417–431, DOI 10.1007/s002080000130. Not read. The theorem is used as quoted in R115. Bibliographic data from the publisher's DOI record in a search excerpt and from the reference list of R118.

**R117 · Universal principles for Kazdan–Warner and Pohozaev–Schoen type identities.** A. Rod Gover and Bent Ørsted. arXiv:1010.4614v2, 18 November 2010. Abstract and parts of the introduction in search excerpts: for a conformal vector field, scalars that are traces of a locally conserved two-tensor satisfy a Pohozaev–Schoen type identity on manifolds with boundary, and the identity is tied to the invariances of the underlying functionals and to conservation laws. Journal data not checked. Use: named in E61 as related work for the conservation law. E61 does not use it. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/1010.4614v2)

**R118 · Mass and volume of four-dimensional Einstein metrics.** Matthew J. Gursky and Andrea Malchiodi. arXiv:2512.07257v1, 8 December 2025. Abstract and first page in search excerpts: "A long open question is whether the round metric \(g_0\) is the unique Einstein metric on \(S^4\)", and a volume bound for a non-round positive Einstein metric normalized by \(\operatorname{Ric}=3g\). Use: the assessment's statement that uniqueness of the Einstein metric on \(S^4\) is open. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.07257v1)

## Earlier record rechecked

R96, Chang–Chen, arXiv:2109.08208, in the [section source record](SOURCE_NOTES_SEC_20261124.md#fixed-margin-bach-concentration--24-november-2026): a search excerpt of the arXiv text shows Corollary 2.2, that a positive Einstein metric on \(S^4\) with full Weyl integral at most \(32\pi^2\) is conformally round, and the remark after it that existence of another positive Einstein metric on \(S^4\) is open and that Gursky's earlier gap for Einstein metrics is better.
